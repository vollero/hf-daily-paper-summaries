# Qwen-UI-Agent Technical Report: Toward Next-Generation Real-World Centric Foundation GUI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28227) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28227)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors present a 27B foundation GUI agent that unifies real-device and sandbox training, GUI/CLI/API actions, long-horizon reinforcement learning, and cross-platform orchestration, reporting leading mobile results and competitive desktop and web performance, though the supplied source is truncated and several system components lack controlled ablations.

## Problem and motivation

GUI agents could execute tasks across existing applications without requiring dedicated APIs, but the authors argue that current systems remain optimized for simulations and isolated, short, reactive workflows. They identify gaps in real-device reliability, cross-platform state continuity, long-horizon planning and recovery, efficient GUI-plus-CLI execution, scalable capability development, and proactive assistance (Section 1).

These gaps matter because real applications contain changing content, permissions, pop-ups, CAPTCHAs, account state, and network failures that controlled benchmarks do not fully reproduce. Practical workflows may also span phones, browsers, desktops, search tools, and consequential actions requiring user confirmation (Sections 1, 2.2, and 2.5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified GUI-agent system:** A multichannel policy consumes screenshots, CLI results, and API responses, then emits reasoning and either individual or batched GUI, CLI, API, user-interaction, or termination actions (Equations 2–4; Table 1).

- **Realistic, scalable environments:** The infrastructure spans mobile, desktop, browser, and DeepSearch sandboxes, supports up to 10,000 concurrent environments, and adds a runtime with more than 100 physical phones and 150 applications (Sections 2.2.1–2.2.2).

- **Hybrid execution:** GUI and CLI actions can be interleaved within one trajectory, with ordered batches reducing model turns when intermediate observations are unnecessary (Sections 2.1.2 and 2.2.3).

- **Agent-driven data flywheel:** Foundation-model agents synthesize tasks, environment states, verifiers, and training trajectories; diagnose failures; and select targets for subsequent iterations, while humans retain oversight (Section 2.3; Figure 5).

- **Three-stage training:** Domain-conditioned SFT and checkpoint merging are followed by action-level RL for recurring local errors and verifier-guided online GRPO-style RL for full trajectories, using a model-adaptive task curriculum (Sections 2.4.1–2.4.3; Equations 5–6).

- **Harness and evaluation assets:** A stateful harness supports notification-triggered assistance and cross-device workflows, while the paper introduces MobileWorld-Real and AutoJudge for evaluating live-device trajectories (Sections 2.5 and 3.2).

The evidence establishes broad reported performance across multiple interactive settings and supplies targeted before/after behavioral measurements for action RL and online RL. It does not isolate the contribution of every component: the main benchmark gains cannot be attributed separately to real-device data, batching, GUI+CLI execution, the data flywheel, SFT composition, or either RL stage. Some comparisons also use corrected, reproduced, or modified evaluation configurations, so comparability depends on the protocol details reported for each table.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** MobileWorld-Real relies on AutoJudge rather than deterministic verification or universal expert review. Its 92.8% agreement leaves residual uncertainty in reported live-device results (Section 7; Appendix A.1).

- **Author-stated:** Computer-use and DeepSearch training for the 35B-A3B model was incomplete, so corresponding results are absent (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** High-fidelity synthesized environments had not been integrated into the reported training, and their synthesis methodology was only planned for future release (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** The data pipeline is agent-driven rather than autonomous and still requires considerable human monitoring and correction (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Interaction latency remains a practical obstacle, particularly over long GUI trajectories; virtual displays improve parallelism but do not remove per-step perception and inference costs (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** More systematic safety evaluation, safety-oriented objectives, memory provenance, and user control over retained personal information remain future work (Section 7).

- **Analyst inference:** Component attribution is weak because the supplied text provides targeted RL analyses but no comprehensive factorial ablation of the environment, data, action-space, training, and harness components.

- **Analyst inference:** MobileWorld-Real is limited to 409 tasks across 104 apps in the Chinese Android ecosystem and deliberately excludes states that cannot be reliably restored, constraining generalization to other regions, operating systems, and highly state-dependent activities (Section 3.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Harness-enabled proactive and cross-platform claims are supported mainly by qualitative demonstrations rather than a quantitative evaluation of usefulness, safety, interruption cost, or end-to-end reliability (Section 3.4; Figures 11–12).

- **Analyst inference:** WebArena scripts and several general-agent evaluations use modified judges, simulators, task subsets, or runtimes, which may limit direct comparison with official results despite the authors documenting the changes (Sections 3.1 and 3.3.3; Table 9 notes).

## Open research questions

1. **Which system components cause the end-to-end gains?** The paper combines real-device data, hybrid actions, batching, a data flywheel, SFT, action RL, and online RL without a complete component-wise ablation. A next experiment could train matched models that add each component incrementally under equal data and rollout budgets.

2. **How well does real-device performance transfer across ecosystems?** MobileWorld-Real covers Chinese Android applications and excludes irreproducible account-dependent states. A next experiment could use matched task templates across Android/iOS and multiple regions, reporting both success and environment-error rates.

3. **Are proactive services useful and safe over sustained use?** The harness is demonstrated qualitatively, while interruption burden, incorrect initiation, privacy, and personalization quality are not quantified. A longitudinal user study could compare reactive and proactive modes using acceptance, correction, interruption, and harmful-action rates.

4. **Can long-horizon execution become cheaper without losing reliability?** The authors identify accumulated observation and inference latency as unresolved. A next experiment could compare adaptive observation, asynchronous execution, batching, and harness-level decomposition at fixed success targets and report latency, model turns, and compute.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains extensive methods, tables, protocol notes, behavioral analyses, and stated limitations, but it was explicitly truncated: **11,449 characters were omitted from the middle**, so this digest cannot claim complete coverage.

Material missing or uncertain includes the omitted portion of Section 4, full Appendix A and its Table 14 values, detailed model architecture and base-checkpoint specifications, complete training-data volumes and mixing ratios, optimization hyperparameters and compute cost, full ablations, confidence intervals or variance for most benchmark scores, environment-error rates, release and licensing details, and quantitative evaluation of the proactive/cross-platform harness.
