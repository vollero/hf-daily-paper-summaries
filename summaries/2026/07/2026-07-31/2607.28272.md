# MemHarness: Memory Is Reconstructed, Not Replayed

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28272) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28272)


## One-sentence takeaway

MemHarness trains a 7B agent with GRPO to critique and reconstruct retrieved natural-language experiences for the current state, outperforming direct memory replay and standard RL on ALFWorld and WebShop.

## Problem and motivation

Memory-augmented agents commonly insert retrieved experiences verbatim into the action context. The authors argue that semantic relevance does not ensure action-level applicability: an experience formed under a different environment state may mislead the agent. Conversely, encoding experience only in model parameters provides adaptive behavior but makes individual memories difficult to inspect or revise. MemHarness targets the gap between these approaches by retaining explicit memories while adapting them to the current state before action generation (Introduction; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Reconstructive formulation:** Memory-guided decisions are decomposed into observation, retrieval, critique, reconstruction, and action rather than retrieval followed directly by action (Section 1).
- **Unified retrieve–reconstruct–act policy:** Retrieved strategies include their source observations. The policy compares historical and current contexts, then retains, rewrites, or rejects each memory; rejection invokes a self-reasoning fallback (Sections 3.1–3.3; Figure 2).
- **End-to-end learning:** Reconstruction is treated as latent reasoning and jointly optimized with actions using GRPO, sparse task outcomes, and a small formatting bonus, without labeled reconstruction targets (Section 3.4).
- **Online experiential memory:** The policy distills its trajectories into natural-language principles, retrieves the top three using BGE-M3, and applies deduplication and utility-based pruning (Section 4.1; Appendix A.3).
- **Empirical and mechanism analyses:** Evaluation covers main comparisons, inference ablations, ALFWorld distribution shifts, source-state perturbations, counterfactual probes, and training-time memory behavior (Sections 4.2–4.6).

These results support the authors’ claim that state-conditioned reconstruction performs better than the tested replay variants in the two environments. They do not isolate every possible confound: reconstruction training also changes the underlying policy, the ablation tables are incompletely retrieved, and no uncertainty estimates or multi-seed variation are reported in the supplied text. The results therefore do not establish universal superiority across agent architectures or open-ended environments.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Future work is left to larger models and open-ended environments; current experiments use one 7B backbone and two text-based interactive benchmarks (Conclusion).
- **Analyst inference — limited statistical evidence:** The supplied text gives no confidence intervals, significance tests, or number of independent training runs, so the stability of reported differences cannot be assessed.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablation evidence:** Tables 2–4 lack their full numeric bodies in the retrieved text, preventing verification of all summarized component and mechanism claims.
- **Analyst inference — training confound:** The stronger “w/o memory” policy suggests reconstruction-oriented training improves the policy itself; consequently, differences from raw replay may not measure inference-time reconstruction alone (Section 4.3).
- **Analyst inference — externally generated supervision and probes:** GPT-5.1 creates cold-start examples and counterfactual edits. Although it is absent during RL and evaluation, its outputs may shape the learned protocol and the mechanism test (Appendices A.4 and B).
- **Analyst inference — probe-count ambiguity:** Section 4.5 describes 1,000 offline probes, while Appendix B states 2,000 total—1,000 per benchmark.
- **Analyst inference — reward-description inconsistency:** Section 3.4 lists three format criteria, whereas Appendix A.1 appears to enumerate four, making exact reproduction ambiguous.
- **Analyst inference — resource reporting:** Hyperparameters are supplied, but total training duration, hardware count, memory-bank sizes, and end-to-end computational cost are not reported in the provided text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does reconstruction generalize beyond these two environments?** The evaluation covers only ALFWorld and WebShop. A next experiment could train and test the same pipeline on several open-ended or partially observable environments with different action interfaces.

2. **How much gain comes from reconstruction at inference versus representation changes during training?** The memory-disabled policy remains substantially stronger than RL Only. A controlled factorial experiment could cross training with or without reconstruction and inference with reconstruction, replay, or no memory.

3. **Is source-state comparison robust to naturally corrupted memories?** The paper uses missing, random, and LLM-edited counterfactual states. A next study could introduce graded retrieval errors, stale observations, contradictory memories, and adversarially plausible source states while measuring success and calibration.

4. **How does the method scale with model and memory-bank size?** Only a 7B policy with top-3 retrieval is evaluated. A scaling study could vary policy size, bank capacity, retrieval depth, and context budget while tracking accuracy, latency, and memory utility.
