# Beacon: Knowing When and How to Perform Agentic Visual Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28595) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28595)


## One-sentence takeaway

Beacon trains an 8B multimodal model to invoke Python tools according to task necessity and to extract genuine benefit from them, achieving the best reported open-source average across 13 benchmarks while improving tool-enabled accuracy by 1.96 points over its own tool-free mode on a five-benchmark diagnostic suite.

## Problem and motivation

Agentic visual-reasoning models can manipulate images or calculate with external tools, but aggregate accuracy does not reveal whether they call tools only when useful or whether those calls actually expand capability. The authors identify two gaps: existing models often default to one reasoning mode regardless of tool-free difficulty, and gains on hard examples are frequently offset by errors introduced on examples already solvable without tools (Sections 1 and 3.2, Figure 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Diagnostic framework:** Mode Adaptiveness measures avoidance of tools on reliably “text-easy” samples and invocation on “text-hard” samples; Tool Effect separates successful tool-assisted recovery of hard samples from tool-induced errors on easy samples. Difficulty is estimated from five text-only runs, excluding ambiguous 2/5- or 3/5-correct cases (Section 3.1).
- **Beacon training pipeline:** Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, the method applies cold-start SFT on refined, Gemini-generated code trajectories followed by GRPO reinforcement learning (Sections 4.1–4.4).
- **Necessity-Aware Adaptive Reward (NAAR):** Online, group-level outcomes reward correct text-only reasoning most when it succeeds, while preserving partial or full reward for correct tool use depending on necessity (Section 4.4.1, Equation 1).
- **Hint-Guided Capability Expansion (HCE):** When all eight normal rollouts fail, verified answer-free expert hints guide a second rollout; training then removes the hint from the policy input to transfer the discovered behavior to hint-free inference (Sections 4.4.2–4.4.3).
- **Empirical contribution:** The paper evaluates overall accuracy on 13 benchmarks and analyzes adaptiveness and tool effects on five of them, including component ablations (Sections 5.2–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported open-source overall result | Beacon’s 13-benchmark overall accuracy is 58.98 versus 56.91 after SFT alone; the authors report first place among open-source models on 11/13 benchmarks and a 6.07-point average gain over Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. | Section 5.2; Tables 1–2 and 4 |
| Stronger tool-enabled performance | Across HRBench4K, BLINK, BabyVision, MathVista, and TIRBench, Beacon averages 53.53% tool-available accuracy versus 51.57% tool-free accuracy, a +1.96-point difference. | Section 5.3, Table 3 |
| Improved mode adaptiveness | On the same five benchmarks, Beacon averages MA_tool 94.75%, MA_text 22.91%, and MA_mean 58.83%; the authors characterize this as the best average adaptiveness among evaluated models. | Section 5.3, Table 3 |
| Positive net tool effect | Beacon averages 9.29% Tool-Gain and 6.15% Tool-Harm, yielding ΔTE = +3.14 points; MathVista remains negative at −0.38. | Section 5.3, Table 3 |
| NAAR and HCE have complementary effects | GRPO+NAAR reaches the highest ablated MA_mean, 59.68%, while GRPO+HCE raises ΔTE from GRPO’s +1.40 to +2.96; the full model obtains 58.98 overall accuracy and +3.14 ΔTE. | Section 5.4, Table 4 |
| HCE recovers otherwise unusable rollouts | Approximately 40% of initially all-wrong groups become groups with non-zero accuracy advantage after hint-guided rerolling. | Section 5.5, Figure 7 |
| Training scale | RL uses one epoch on 64 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, eight rollouts per prompt, batch size 128 prompt groups, and sequences up to 30,720 tokens. | Appendix C.2 |

These results support the authors’ claim that Beacon improves both aggregate accuracy and measured tool-use behavior within the tested model, prompt, and benchmark settings. They do not establish statistical significance, lower wall-clock or inference cost, robustness beyond Python-based tools, or superiority to the closed-source Gemini 3.1 Pro baseline, which has higher grouped averages in Tables 1–2.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported failed design:** A more aggressive reward using forced-text rollouts caused code-use collapse and degraded perception-heavy performance because the label reflected the best of multiple text attempts rather than single-rollout reliability (Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference — diagnostic scope:** MA and Tool Effect are averaged over only five benchmarks, whereas overall accuracy covers 13; behavior on the remaining benchmarks is therefore not established (Tables 3–4).
- **Analyst inference — excluded cases:** Samples with two or three correct text-only responses out of five are excluded from MA/TE analysis, so the metrics do not characterize uncertain, intermediate-difficulty cases (Section 3.1).
- **Analyst inference — external-model dependence:** Gemini 3.1 Pro generates and refines SFT trajectories and HCE hints, while Gemini models may also adjudicate answers after rule matching fails. This creates teacher-quality and evaluator-dependence risks; no independent-judge comparison is supplied (Sections 4.2 and 4.4.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference — efficiency evidence:** Although avoiding unnecessary computation motivates the work, no latency, token, execution-cost, or energy comparison is reported. Beacon’s diagnostic MA_text is only 22.91%, indicating frequent tool use even on text-easy samples (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference — uncertainty:** The supplied results contain no confidence intervals, significance tests, or sensitivity analysis for sampling thresholds, decoding temperature, retry policy, or semantic judge.
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility cost:** The paper provides substantial training detail, but RL requires 64 H200 GPUs and expert-generated hints; the exact expert-service cost and whether all synthesized training artifacts are released are not established (Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptive tool use remain effective on ambiguous problems?** The current analysis excludes 2/5- and 3/5-correct samples. A next experiment could evaluate calibrated tool-call probability and net tool benefit across all five text-only accuracy levels.
2. **Does adaptiveness translate into real efficiency?** The paper reports invocation ratios but not latency or compute. Measure accuracy–cost frontiers using execution time, generated tokens, tool rounds, and GPU consumption.
3. **How dependent is HCE on the expert model?** Hints come from Gemini 3.1 Pro, and no expert-strength ablation is reported. Compare multiple hint generators, weaker self-generated hints, and verified human hints under equal budgets.
4. **Does Beacon generalize beyond Python-mediated image reasoning?** Only Python visual and numerical operations are demonstrated. Test the same NAAR/HCE framework with search, retrieval, specialized vision models, or embodied actions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method and reported results because the supplied text includes the main sections, tables, and relevant appendices.

Material missing or unclear: statistical uncertainty; per-example diagnostic sample counts after ambiguous-case exclusion; latency and compute comparisons at inference; precise semantic-judge usage rates and agreement; complete released-artifact inventory; expert-generation cost; and evidence for non-Python tools or out-of-suite generalization.
