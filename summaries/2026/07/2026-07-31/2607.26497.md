# BM25 Wins at Scale: A Scaling Study of Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigms

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26497) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497)


## One-sentence takeaway

On a controlled 28-tier enterprise RAG benchmark, raw-file agents lead at small scale, but BM25 overtakes them around 10M corpus tokens and becomes the strongest accuracy–cost default as the corpus grows.

## Problem and motivation

RAG paradigms—lexical, dense, graph-based, and agentic retrieval—are usually evaluated on different benchmarks at a single corpus size, confounding comparisons and obscuring how accuracy and cost change with scale. This matters for growing enterprise knowledge bases, where graph construction and sequential search may become impractical. The authors address the absence of a controlled scaling comparison that fixes questions, relevant evidence, adversarial distractors, reader, judge, and metering protocol while varying only background-corpus size (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled scaling benchmark:** A 28-tier, strictly nested ladder spanning 1,144–511,959 documents and approximately 1.7M–601M tokens, with all gold evidence, traps, and not-found lures present from the smallest tier (Sections 3.1–3.3; Figure 2).
- **Unified cross-paradigm evaluation:** Seven native pipelines covering BM25, DenseRAG, four graph-oriented systems, and a raw File-System Agent, evaluated with a shared reader, judge, retrieval settings where applicable, and token/latency accounting (Section 4.1).
- **Scaling result:** The study identifies a crossover near 10M corpus tokens: raw-file agency has higher early point estimates, while BM25 leads at every larger shared tier (Section 4.2; Figures 1 and 3).
- **Mechanism controls:** Retrieval-swap and graph-access experiments distinguish agent policy from its retrieval substrate, showing that agentic reasoning improves substantially when applied after BM25 candidate ranking (Section 5; Tables 3–5).
- **Reproducibility assets:** The supplement describes scripts, aggregate ledgers, prediction schemas, tier manifests, and artifact-to-claim mappings (Appendices J–M).

The evidence establishes a scale-dependent accuracy–cost crossover under this fixed enterprise benchmark, model, tool budget, and evaluation protocol. It also supports candidate discovery as a mechanism through the two-scale retrieval-swap control. It does not establish a universal 10M-token threshold, prove BM25 superiority for other domains or readers, or provide observed full-scale accuracy for graph indexes that could not be constructed.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Enterprise questions contain precise lexical anchors, while adversarial traps are semantically similar but factually wrong; this setting favors exact lexical matching (Section 6.1).
- **Author-stated graph coverage limitation:** Several graph pipelines stop before full scale. Their full-scale construction figures are fitted projections, and unsupported tiers have no observed accuracy (Sections 4.2–4.3; Appendix D).
- **Author-stated crossover qualification:** The approximately 10M-token crossover is a rounded regime marker, not a fitted threshold or statistical boundary (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated stochasticity limitation:** Each system is run once per question; confidence intervals resample questions rather than capturing variability across repeated model runs (Section 4.1.3; Appendix K).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is uncertain because the study uses one synthetic enterprise benchmark, one reader/policy model, one embedding model, and a fixed 80-call agent budget.
- **Analyst inference:** Some cost comparisons are incomplete at the systems level: token accounting does not fully represent CPU, storage, indexing, or parallel execution costs, and LinearRAG includes a 3% estimate for an unavailable token stream (Section 4.1.2; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Agent+BM25 is evaluated only at two scales on a 150-question subset, so its behavior across the entire 28-tier ladder remains unmeasured (Section 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The retrieved Markdown names tables but omits many table bodies and exact per-tier values, limiting independent inspection of intermediate curves and method-level settings.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the crossover persist in corpora with weak lexical anchors?**  
   The authors identify exact wording and factually wrong semantic traps as advantages for BM25. A next experiment could repeat the nested ladder on paraphrase-heavy, multilingual, or terminology-shifting corpora.

2. **How does Agent+BM25 scale across all 28 tiers?**  
   The retrieval-swap control covers only the bedrock and full corpus. Running the matched agent at every tier would reveal whether its gains emerge gradually and where its extra reasoning cost becomes worthwhile.

3. **Can graph RAG become competitive with cheaper, relation-preserving construction?**  
   Existing graph systems encounter extraction noise, information loss, or prohibitive build costs. A next experiment could compare lightweight relation-preserving indexes under identical construction budgets and full-ladder coverage.

4. **How sensitive are conclusions to model and budget choices?**  
   The study fixes one reader/policy model, top-5 retrieval, and an 80-call agent cap. A factorial experiment varying reader size, retrieval depth, call budget, and latency constraints could test whether the paradigm ordering remains stable.
