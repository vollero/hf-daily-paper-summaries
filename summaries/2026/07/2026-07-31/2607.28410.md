# Can Large Language Models Execute Parent Orders?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28410) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28410)


## One-sentence takeaway

PACE uses LLMs to separate long-horizon allocation from minute-level order adjustment and, in an April 2026 Shenzhen Stock Exchange backtest, beats the strongest reported baseline by 0.65 bps under aggressive and 0.71 bps under passive submission.

## Problem and motivation

Parent-order execution must divide a large buy or sell order over time to reduce market impact and obtain favorable prices. Traditional strategies depend on assumptions about price, volume, or impact, while learned policies require task-specific states, actions, rewards, and retraining. The authors identify an unexplored gap: prior LLM-based finance research focuses on deciding what to trade rather than how to execute an existing order (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **PACE framework:** Plan-Ahead Controlled Execution divides the task into an LLM Planner for long-horizon allocation and an LLM Executor for short-horizon quantity adjustment (§3.3).
- **Controlled deviations from TWAP:** Planner confidence interpolates between uniform TWAP and LLM-preferred slot allocations; the Executor adjusts each minute’s TWAP quantity within a tunable range (Eqs. 5–6).
- **Training-free execution study:** PACE uses pretrained LLMs at inference time without a specified price model or task-specific policy training (§3.3).
- **Evaluation and behavioral analysis:** The authors build a Level-1-data backtester and analyze planning stability, confidence, allocation concentration, time pressure, and recent-return sensitivity (§3.4, §4.6–4.7).

The evidence supports an offline association between PACE and improved execution prices on the sampled market data, with especially clear bootstrap evidence for DS-v4-f. It does not establish live-trading profitability, causal economic explanations for the behavioral patterns, or generalization to other exchanges, periods, asset classes, and liquidity regimes. The annual savings examples are extrapolations from basis-point improvements, not realized fund returns.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The conclusion leaves live trading and richer inputs—news, microstructure data, and cross-asset signals—for future work (§5).
- **Analyst inference — narrow external validity:** Evaluation covers one exchange and one month, with every order starting at 10:30 and quantities limited to 100–10,000 shares (§4.1; Appendix §6.2).
- **Analyst inference — simulated fills:** Results come from a Level-1 backtester. Although depth-limited matching is tested, live latency, queue priority, fees, and broader market impact are not established in the supplied text (§3.4; Table 9).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete numerical reporting:** Retrieved text omits the cells of Tables 1–5 and several figures, preventing verification of absolute wbp values, baseline rankings, ablation magnitudes, and regression coefficients.
- **Analyst inference — tuning comparability:** AC and learning baselines report selecting their best grid-searched parameters, but the supplied text does not specify the selection split or whether PACE hyperparameters received comparable tuning (Appendix §6.4; §4.1).
- **Analyst inference — model dependence:** Only two LLM families are tested, both with default API parameters; GPT-5.4 is described as near the bootstrap significance boundary (Table 8).
- **Analyst inference — observational behavior claims:** Confidence and time-pressure regressions show associations, not that confidence causes better execution or that LLM behavior generally differs from human traders (§4.6–4.7).

## Open research questions

1. **Does PACE retain its gains in live execution?** Backtests cannot capture every fill and impact mechanism. A next experiment could run a prospective paper-trading or tightly risk-limited live trial against TWAP using randomized contemporaneous orders.

2. **How well does it generalize across markets and regimes?** Evidence comes only from April 2026 Shenzhen equities. A next experiment could freeze prompts and hyperparameters, then test multiple exchanges, asset classes, dates, volatility regimes, and intraday start times.

3. **Which information drives the LLM’s advantage?** Module and prompt ablations show both components matter, but exact causal inputs remain unclear. A next experiment could mask or perturb price, volume, spread, Planner rationale, and confidence separately while measuring wbp and completion.

4. **Is self-reported confidence reliably calibratable?** Confidence correlates with performance but is not shown to be calibrated or causal. A next experiment could compare confidence bins with realized gains out of sample and test alternative uncertainty estimators for TWAP–LLM mixing.
