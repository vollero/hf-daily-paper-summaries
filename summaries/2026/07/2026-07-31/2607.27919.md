# Memory Decoder at Scale: A Pretrained, Parametric Long-Term Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27919) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27919)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors scale a retrieval-distilled, standalone language-model memory to 6.9B parameters and 300B training tokens, reporting that large memories paired with frozen small backbones can outperform substantially larger backbone-only models at lower total parameter counts.

## Problem and motivation

Decoder-only language models store long-term knowledge and perform reasoning in the same parameters, so memory capacity cannot be scaled, replaced, or adapted independently. The authors argue that whole-model domain adaptation is expensive and can degrade existing capabilities, while inference-time retrieval systems impose datastore, search, and context-processing costs (§1).

Earlier Memory Decoder experiments reached only 1B parameters and used corpora containing millions of tokens. Consequently, it was unknown whether parametric memory would scale to broad pretraining corpora, transfer across backbones, or offer a better allocation of parameters than backbone scaling (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Standalone parametric memory:** A transformer memory predicts the same next-token vocabulary as a frozen backbone. It is trained with a mixture of KL alignment to offline \(k\)-nearest-neighbor distributions and ordinary next-token cross-entropy; at inference, its distribution is interpolated with the backbone’s output (Eqs. 1–2, §2).

- **Pretraining-scale memory:** The authors train general memories of 1.4B, 2.8B, and 6.9B parameters for 300B tokens, using retrieval supervision constructed over 207B contextual token representations from the deduplicated Pile (§§3.1–3.2, Table 9).

- **Distributed retrieval-supervision pipeline:** OPQ compresses 4,096-dimensional embeddings to 256 dimensions; IVF/HNSW-based index sharding and parallel GPU search construct the offline \(k\)NN targets (Figure 3, §3.1).

- **Storage and loading system:** Sparse target storage retains only non-negligible token probabilities, while memory-mapped distributed streaming reads the rows needed by each batch (§3.2).

- **Modular general and domain memories:** Separate biology, law, and finance memories are evaluated across Qwen3 backbones, and adapted across tokenizer vocabularies to OLMo models (§§3.3, 5.3–5.4).

- **Scaling and diagnostic evidence:** Experiments study parameter allocation, few-shot prompting, training budget, interpolation sensitivity, extractable memorization, and fidelity to retrieval targets (§§5–6; Appendices F, H, I).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that this memory architecture improves aggregate performance across the tested model families and that allocating parameters to memory can outperform backbone-only allocation in the reported Pythia setting. Matched attached-CPT controls also indicate that the training objective contributes beyond the interpolation interface.

It does not establish universally lower deployment cost: parameter count is reported, but measured latency, energy, memory footprint, and end-to-end preprocessing cost are not provided in the retrieved text. Nor do aggregate improvements imply universal task gains—Table 1 includes regressions, such as TruthfulQA at all three matched scales.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Constructing offline \(k\)NN targets still requires costly indexing and retrieval, with preprocessing burden increasing with corpus scale despite compression and sharding (§9).

- **Author-stated:** Inference uses a fixed interpolation coefficient rather than context-dependent weighting; adaptive weighting remains future work (§9). Task-level validation tuning is used when validation data exists (§4, Appendix F).

- **Author-stated:** Joint or staged backbone–memory training is unexplored; it could improve coordination but increase cost and weaken cross-backbone transfer (§9).

- **Analyst inference:** The parameter-efficiency claim is not yet a demonstrated systems-efficiency claim. The components may run in parallel, but the paper only asserts possible latency benefits and reports no direct latency or throughput measurements (§5.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation is limited to text models from Pythia, Qwen3, and OLMo and to the listed general, biology, law, and finance benchmarks. Broader modalities and model families are not tested (§4).

- **Analyst inference:** Some domain averages combine heterogeneous metrics, and BioInst uses a custom deterministic relaxed extractor. The extractor is applied uniformly, but its effect relative to canonical evaluation is not separately ablated (Appendix C.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Retrieval fidelity and memorization probes use 1,024 BioInst training samples, so they establish in-domain training-set behavior rather than fidelity on unseen contexts or comprehensive privacy risk (§§6.6–6.7).

- **Analyst inference:** Domain training and evaluation reuse Biology-Instructions in different roles after reformatting. The text identifies training and benchmark usage but does not provide enough split-overlap analysis to rule out contamination concerns (§4, Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **Can memory contribution be selected per token or context?** Fixed/task-level \(\alpha\) works across broad ranges but varies substantially by task (Appendix F). A next experiment could train a confidence-conditioned gate and compare accuracy, calibration, and transfer against validation-tuned fixed coefficients.

2. **Does parameter efficiency translate into real inference efficiency?** The paper reports total parameters but not observed latency or energy. Benchmarking matched-score backbone-only and backbone-plus-memory systems under identical hardware, batch sizes, and parallel execution would test the deployment claim.

3. **Can retrieval supervision be constructed more cheaply?** Offline indexing over hundreds of billions of contexts remains the principal author-stated burden (§9). A plausible experiment would compare smaller or sampled datastores, alternative approximate indexes, and learned retrieval targets on quality versus preprocessing compute and storage.

4. **How well does memory generalize without increasing unsafe memorization?** The BioInst probes show stronger extraction from training examples (§6.6), but privacy and unseen-context behavior are not evaluated. Future work could measure held-out factual generalization alongside canary extraction, membership inference, and deletion or unlearning performance.
