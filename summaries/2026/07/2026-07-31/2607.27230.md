# Multi-Head Attention Residuals

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27230) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27230)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors claim that splitting attention-residual depth routing into independent feature-subspace heads removes a scale-sensitive single-query bottleneck, improving language-model loss from 100M to 1B parameters while adding no parameters over single-head routing.

## Problem and motivation

Standard Transformers expose each sublayer only to the latest additive residual state, making earlier sublayer outputs no longer individually addressable. Attention residuals address this with learned softmax routing over all prior outputs, but use one depth distribution for the entire hidden width. The authors argue that this forces feature subspaces with different preferred source layers into a shared routing compromise, whose cost grows with model width (§1; §2.1–2.2; Figure 2). They also identify the memory traffic and activation cost of repeatedly reading the depth history as a practical scaling barrier (§2.3; Appendix E).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multi-Head Attention Residuals (MHAR):** Partition the width into \(H\) contiguous subspaces and give each an independent softmax over prior sublayer outputs. The existing \(d\)-element query is reshaped to \((H,d/H)\), so MHAR adds no parameters over single-head attention residuals; \(H=1\) exactly recovers that method (§2.3, Eq. 3; Figure 3; Appendix B).

- **Default head-count rule:** Set routing heads equal to KV heads. Sweeps show this is near-optimal in the grouped-query settings tested, although it is not always the strict grid optimum (§4; Appendix C, Figure 8).

- **Mechanistic evidence:** Probes of trained routing queries associate the widening performance gap with learned disagreement among subspaces’ preferred depth distributions; the probe remains a proxy because it slices one trained query rather than training independent routers (Appendix D, Table 6).

- **Efficient implementation:** Deterministic fused Triton kernels avoid repeated source stacking and fuse normalization, scoring, softmax, and mixing, while the inherent dense-routing traffic remains quadratic in depth (§2.3; Appendix E, Figures 9–10).

- **Pretrained-model conversion:** A zero-gated delta formulation preserves the original 8B model at initialization and enables MHAR to be introduced during continual pretraining (§3.3; Figure 5).

These results support the authors’ claims that MHAR improves the tested language-modeling recipes, that independent depth maps are especially valuable at larger tested widths, and that fused kernels substantially reduce implementation overhead. They do not establish compute-optimal superiority over a plain Transformer: the main comparisons use equal training steps, while MHAR remains 12–45% slower end to end. Nor do they establish generality beyond the tested decoder-only language models, data mixtures, scales, and largely single-run downstream evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** A direct equal-wall-clock or iso-compute comparison against the plain Transformer was not run; the authors leave it to future work (§3.4).

- **Author-stated:** The fixed-step compute-equivalent fit has a flat exponent and yields 1.3–1.5× estimates, whereas the authors estimate that a compute-optimal ladder would imply only about 1.1–1.15× (§3.2).

- **Author-stated:** The pretraining validation stream comes from the same corpus under a different shuffle rather than an explicit held-out split, so overlap is reduced but not eliminated (Appendix I).

- **Author-stated:** Downstream evaluations at 100M–1B are single runs, and broader benchmarks remain future work (§3.2, Table 2).

- **Author-stated:** The 8B result is one schedule-matched pair over roughly 10B tokens; routing gains are significant only on GSM8K and GPQA, while MMLU, MATH, and code are unchanged (§3.3).

- **Author-stated:** The width-disagreement probe slices one trained single-head query, making it an upper-bound proxy rather than a direct measurement of independently trained routers; source collinearity is hypothesized but not cleanly isolated (Appendix D, Table 6).

- **Author-stated:** \(H=\mathrm{KV}\) is near-optimal rather than universally optimal. At 1B the grid minimum is off-diagonal, within reported evaluation noise, and the sweeps are single-seed (Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** Because depth routing inherently moves \(O(L^2BTd)\) data, its relative cost may worsen for substantially deeper models even with fused kernels; the paper evaluates depths only up to 36 layers from scratch and 32 layers in the delta setting.

- **Analyst inference:** The public availability of the implementation, kernels, trained checkpoints, and assembled annealing corpus is not stated in the supplied text, limiting reproducibility assessment.

## Open research questions

1. **Does MHAR remain favorable under equal wall-clock compute?** This remains open because the baseline comparisons use equal steps despite lower MHAR throughput. Train baseline and MHAR for identical accelerator-hours across several depths and report loss-versus-time curves.

2. **Why does the preferred routing-head count track KV heads?** The paper shows a near-optimal empirical rule but no causal account. Factorially vary routing heads, KV heads, width, and grouping while holding parameter count and training tokens fixed, using multiple seeds.

3. **What functions do individual routing heads learn?** Figure 4 shows stable and distinct depth maps but does not connect them to linguistic or computational roles. Intervene on individual heads or source layers and measure changes by token type and downstream task.

4. **How broadly does identity-preserving MHAR conversion transfer?** Only one 8B base model, English-heavy annealing blend, and schedule are tested. Repeat the schedule-matched conversion across model families, languages, corpus compositions, and longer or more aggressive retraining schedules.
