# SpatialCLI: Learning to Reason With Spatial Tools, Then Without Them

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27703) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27703)


## One-sentence takeaway

SpatialCLI trains vision-language models to coordinate specialist spatial-perception tools and then imitate their evidence without tool access, substantially improving both tool-enabled and direct spatial reasoning across the reported benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation

General VLMs can decompose embodied tasks but often miss precise localization, segmentation, metric-depth, and pose details; specialist vision models provide those details but cannot select or combine capabilities into task-level decisions. The authors identify two gaps: existing spatial benchmarks usually test capabilities separately, and prior tool-augmented systems do not establish whether tool evidence can become supervision for tool-free reasoning (Introduction; Sections 1 and 3.4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Call:** Exposes four structured interfaces—Locate, Segment, Depth, and Pose—backed by six specialist models. A ReAct-style agent selects tools, integrates their returns, and operates under a tool-call budget (Section 3.1; Appendix D).

2. **Learn:** Uses filtered teacher trajectories for Cold-Start SFT, followed by outcome-reward GRPO to improve tool selection, arguments, evidence use, and termination (Section 3.2; Appendix B.1).

3. **Internalize:** Converts successful RL trajectories into evidence-grounded reasoning chains through turn-wise consolidation and global verbalization. Dual-view training jointly supervises tool-free reasoning and the original tool-interaction policy (Section 3.3).

4. **Evaluation assets:** Introduces SpatialCLI-Bench, a 516-example, six-choice benchmark in which every item composes at least two spatial-capability groups, plus a 1,000-example held-out Capability Internalization Index suite (Section 3.4; Appendices C and E).

The results establish large improvements relative to the reported checkpoints and ablations under the paper’s evaluation protocol. They also show that tool-free outputs become more similar to specialist-tool outputs as internalization data grows. They do **not** independently prove that the model has acquired the specialists’ underlying perceptual mechanisms: CII references are produced by those same tools, and the task gains could partly reflect learning their output conventions or biases. The supplied text reports three-run means but no confidence intervals or significance tests.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Coverage is limited by the available specialists and their reliability; current internalization targets structured perceptual outputs and perception-centric tasks rather than multimodal generated outputs or joint perception-action control (Conclusion).

- **Author-stated/tool constraints:** Depth is monocular estimated camera-axis depth, pose exposes discretized directions rather than continuous geometry, segmentation omits holes and limits contours to 16 vertices, and Locate fuses predictions using fixed thresholds (Appendix D).

- **Analyst inference:** SpatialCLI-Bench is partly generated and labeled using the same specialist evidence that the evaluated tool framework exposes. Human agreement filters ambiguity, but this construction may favor the tools’ representations and error profile (Appendix C.4–C.5).

- **Analyst inference:** CII measures reproduction of verified specialist outputs, not agreement with independent physical ground truth. Rising CII therefore supports tool imitation/internalization under its operational definition, but cannot by itself establish more accurate real-world perception (Appendix E).

- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark breadth remains limited: 516 English six-choice items, one or two images, and 195 examples derived from MindCube evaluation imagery. The authors state no train/evaluation visual or textual overlap, but the supplied text gives no independently auditable overlap analysis (Appendices B.1 and C).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is constrained by substantial compute—four 16-accelerator nodes for SFT and RL—and by dependence on six specialist services and large teacher/verbalizer models. Code, weights, dataset licenses, and public-release details are not established in the supplied text (Appendices B.3 and D).

- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation uses stochastic decoding across three unfixed-seed runs, yet variance is not reported, limiting assessment of result stability (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does internalization transfer beyond the specialists’ own output distribution?** CII uses specialist-produced references, so an evaluation against independent sensor or human-annotated geometry remains open. A next experiment could compare tool-free models, tools, and their combination against metric-depth sensors, calibrated camera poses, and manually verified masks.

2. **How robust is the method when specialist tools are wrong or disagree?** The paper acknowledges tool reliability as a limitation but does not report controlled corruption tests. A next experiment could inject calibrated noise, missing returns, and conflicting specialist predictions while measuring answer accuracy and reliance on prior visual evidence.

3. **Can internalization handle richer multimodal outputs?** Current supervision verbalizes structured coordinates, polygons, depths, and discrete directions. A next experiment could internalize annotated images or dense feature maps and compare text-only, multimodal, and hybrid trajectory supervision.

4. **Does the learned capability improve embodied action rather than perception-only answers?** The reported scope is perception-centric. A next experiment could place the VLM over a VLA executor in interactive environments and measure task completion, safety failures, tool latency, and recovery from perceptual errors.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, benchmark construction, and reported table values because the retrieved text includes the main paper and extensive appendices.

Material missing information includes public availability of code, weights, and benchmark data; dataset and specialist-model licensing; confidence intervals or per-run variance; independent replication; detailed failure-category analysis; energy or training-time cost; demographic or geographic scene coverage; and evaluation against physical ground truth independent of the specialist tools.
