# ReToken: One Token to Improve Vision-Language Models for Visual Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28627) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28627)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReToken learns one query-conditioned embedding to rank cached visual frames in a VLM’s value space, substantially improving sparse retrieval and downstream QA—especially with many distractors or long videos—while leaving the backbone frozen by default.

## Problem and motivation

Long image collections and videos contain many distractors, degrading VLM answer accuracy and sometimes exceeding GPU memory. Sparse selection is therefore necessary, but conventional attention-based retrieval is poorly aligned with relevance: question-to-key attention achieves only 5.1% average Recall@1 on QAEgo4D-Test-MC and 63.3% in a two-image Visual Haystacks setting (Introduction; §3.1, Fig. 1 and Table 1).

The authors identify two gaps: VLM attention is trained for next-token prediction rather than retrieval, and averaging question tokens produces a noisy, wording-sensitive retrieval query. Their controlled comparison suggests that visual value projections contain a stronger retrieval signal when paired with a precise textual target (§3.1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Value-space retrieval:** Frames are represented by their mean final-layer value vectors rather than attention keys. The paper’s diagnostic experiments show stronger Recall@1 for value–value matching with precise target phrases (§3.1, Table 1).

- **Single learned retrieval token:** ReToken appends one learnable embedding to the question. Its contextualized final-layer representation is projected and compared by cosine similarity with each frame’s mean value vector (§3.2, Eq. 3).

- **Explicit retrieval supervision:** The token and one projection matrix are trained with class-balanced binary cross-entropy over relevant and irrelevant frames; the VLM remains frozen by default (§3.2, Eq. 4; Fig. 3).

- **Cached, two-pass inference:** Visual KV states are encoded once. A retrieval pass selects top-\(K\) frames, followed by answer generation using only their cached states (§3.2, Fig. 4). Long-video retrieval uses an early-layer budget of \(K'=256\) frames by default.

- **Image-to-video transfer:** Training uses filtered multi-image QA examples only, yet the method is evaluated zero-shot on long-video QA (§4.1, §4.3; Appendix B.1).

These results support the authors’ claim that explicitly trained value-space retrieval is more robust than attention retrieval under sparse budgets and large distractor sets, with useful zero-shot transfer to long video. They do not establish universal superiority: several tables lack rendered cells, no uncertainty or significance tests are supplied, and performance weakens when answers require dispersed or temporally combined evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** ReToken requires two forward passes and attends to more visual context during early retrieval layers, increasing memory and response time (Conclusion, Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** Training is restricted to multi-image QA; the method may not adequately learn temporal structure without video training (Conclusion, Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** Independent frame scoring and mean pooling cannot naturally capture evidence emerging across consecutive frames, temporally offset queries, or small within-frame evidence (Conclusion, Future Work).

- **Author-stated:** ReToken hurts LVBench summarization, where relevant evidence is dispersed rather than localized (Table 11).

- **Author-stated hypothesis:** Partial image-based tuning lowers video performance, possibly because of image-to-video domain shift (§4.4, Table 8).

- **Analyst inference:** The early retrieval budget \(K'=256\) gives ReToken more ranking context than the \(K\)-frame answer budget; comparisons should distinguish retrieval-stage compute from answer-stage sparsity (§3.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Training-data filtering depends on frozen Qwen3VL-8B losses and attention scores, which may make the learned-data recipe backbone-dependent; cross-backbone filtering sensitivity is not reported (Appendix B.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text reports greedy decoding and several ablations but no repeated-run variance, confidence intervals, or statistical tests, leaving robustness to training randomness unclear (§4.1–§4.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Can retrieval model temporal combinations rather than isolated frames?** Independent scoring remains inadequate for temporally distributed evidence. A next experiment could compare frame scoring with learned consecutive-window or set scoring on temporally grounded QA.

2. **Would video supervision improve temporal reasoning without sacrificing image retrieval?** The paper trains only on images and reports degradation from partial image tuning on video. A controlled experiment could mix image and video retrieval labels at several ratios and evaluate both domains.

3. **Can token-level retrieval recover small localized evidence?** Frame-level mean pooling may dilute information occupying few tokens. A next experiment could compare mean pooling with token-level top-\(k\), region pooling, or learned aggregation at matched compute.

4. **How sensitive is the method to retrieval-stage context and cache construction?** \(K'=256\), sliding-window encoding, and cross-image cache contamination all affect representations. A factorial ablation over \(K'\), chunk size, window length, and isolated versus fused caches would separate these effects.
