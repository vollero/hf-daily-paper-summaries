# RefCaptioner: Multi-Reference Image-Grounded Video Captioning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28509) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28509)


## One-sentence takeaway

RefCaptioner jointly generates factual video captions and binds local phrases to relevant reference images, outperforming evaluated open-source models on the new MRVBench while retaining strong standard video-captioning performance.

## Problem and motivation

Standard video captioners describe videos but do not explicitly connect individual phrases to multiple reference images. The authors define a task requiring models to select supported references, reject distractors, attach tags to the correct local phrases, and group multiple views of the same entity—all while preserving factual and temporally coherent descriptions (Sections 1 and 3). They argue this grounding is useful for reference-conditioned video generation, editing, and understanding, where incorrect reference–video alignment can exchange identities or attributes.

## Approach and contributions

- **New task and output format:** Multi-reference image-grounded video captioning takes a video plus candidate reference images and produces one caption with each usable `<Image_i>` tag immediately following its corresponding phrase (Section 3).

- **Two-stage RefCaptioner training:** Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, balanced supervised fine-tuning mixes manually checked grounded captions with ordinary detailed captions. HCD-GRPO then optimizes separate factual-caption and reference-grounding rewards (Sections 4.1–4.3).

- **Specialized grounding rewards:** Correctly bound reference coverage rewards useful tags; DAES penalizes distractor use; CRSC rewards grouping different views of the same entity; malformed or invented tags cap the total reward (Section 4.3.2; Appendix B.3).

- **Training and evaluation assets:** The authors construct a 20,000-video, 171,354-reference training corpus and MRVBench, a held-out benchmark of 462 videos, 3,831 candidate references, 5,846 key points, and 2,172 QA pairs (Section 5; Appendix A).

- **Downstream evaluation:** Captions and their cited references condition video reconstruction, followed by blinded human comparison under fixed generation settings (Section 6.7; Appendices E–F).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that their trained model offers a strong balance of caption coverage, local binding, distractor handling, and same-entity grouping among the evaluated open-source systems. The ablations also associate individual reward components with their intended metrics.

It does not establish universal superiority: MRVBench is newly introduced by the same authors, several metrics depend on an MLLM judge, and the human study covers only 40 examples. Reconstruction results measure the combined effect of caption text and selected references, not either contribution independently.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated release limitation:** The 20,000-video training corpus will not be publicly released because redistribution rights vary; only licensable MRVBench test data and annotations are planned for release (Appendix A.2).

- **Author-stated scope of reconstruction evidence:** Reconstruction evaluates captions and their selected reference subsets jointly, so it cannot isolate textual quality from reference selection (Appendix F.2). The conclusion describes this as initial downstream evidence.

- **Analyst inference — evaluator dependence:** Gemini-3.1-Pro judges key-point coverage, QA, binding, and subject consistency. Although all models share the same protocol, dependence on one proprietary judge could introduce systematic bias; no comparison with alternative automated judges is reported (Appendix A.5).

- **Analyst inference — self-judging concern:** Gemini-3.1-Pro is both a benchmarked captioner and the automated evaluator. The supplied text does not analyze whether this affects its scores (Table 1; Appendix A.5).

- **Analyst inference — limited human sample:** Three graduate-level experts evaluate 40 of 462 MRVBench examples. Randomization and anonymization help, but broader annotator populations and confidence intervals are not reported (Appendix F.1).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete significance reporting:** Three repeated base-versus-full runs are said to show statistical significance, but the test, variance, effect distribution, and p-value are absent from the supplied text (Appendix B.5).

- **Analyst inference — compute and reproducibility burden:** GRPO uses 32 H800 80GB GPUs, and the non-released training corpus prevents exact data-level reproduction despite detailed optimization settings (Appendices A.2 and B.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Will the gains persist under independent evaluation?** The main content and grounding metrics use Gemini-3.1-Pro as judge. A next experiment could repeat scoring with multiple independent MLLM judges plus a larger human audit and report inter-rater agreement.

2. **How well does RefCaptioner generalize beyond MRVBench’s construction process?** The benchmark and training corpus arise from the authors’ own annotation pipeline. A next experiment could evaluate on an independently collected dataset with unseen domains, reference-pool construction, and distractor types.

3. **Which output component improves reconstruction most?** Current reconstruction changes both caption text and cited-image subsets. A factorial experiment could cross captions from one model with reference selections from another while fixing the generator, seed, and settings.

4. **Can similar grounding quality be achieved with less computation?** Full GRPO requires 32 H800 GPUs. Experiments varying trainable modules, rollout count, reward approximation, and training-set size could map the quality–compute tradeoff.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated methods, datasets, and reported comparisons because the supplied text includes the main paper and substantial supplementary material.

Material missing or unresolved includes the complete training-data provenance and corpus itself, exact MRVScore aggregation formula, numerical repeated-run variance and significance-test details, confidence intervals for benchmark and human results, inter-annotator agreement, and independent validation of the automated judge.
