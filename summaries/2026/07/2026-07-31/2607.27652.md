# Harness-G: A Graph-Structured Harness for Search Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27652) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27652)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors replace free-form search queries with finite graph-based retrieval actions and add structured non-myopic credit, reporting substantially better QA F1—especially for smaller models and multi-hop tasks—while reducing retrieval aliasing.

## Problem and motivation

RL search agents can generate many distinct query strings that retrieve nearly identical evidence, creating what the authors call “retrieval-equivalence collapse.” In their Search-R1 analysis, the share of rollout groups spanning multiple retrieval-equivalence classes falls from about 86% to below 10% by training step 30 even though query-form diversity remains high (Introduction; Figure 1a).

This matters because group-relative RL requires meaningful differences among same-question rollouts. Nearly equivalent retrieval outcomes can yield vanishing advantages or confound retrieval quality with downstream answer-generation differences. The authors argue that denser rewards alone do not fix the underlying many-to-one mapping from query strings to retrieved evidence (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions

- **Finite graph navigation:** Harness-G constructs a programmatic paragraph–sentence–entity graph and lets the policy choose typed `Select`, `Lookup`, `Answer_With`, or `Answer` actions. The environment validates actions, builds retrieval queries deterministically, filters duplicates, and maintains retrieval state (Method; Appendix J).

- **Interface-level diagnosis:** The paper formalizes retrieval-equivalence classes and identifies collapse between surface query diversity and actual evidence diversity. A transition-matched comparison holds feasible targets and transitions fixed while changing whether targets are selected directly or reached through free text (Introduction; Appendix G).

- **Structured Non-myopic Credit:** SNC compares an executed information-acquisition action with previewed same-state alternatives using a frozen answer scorer, then propagates downstream gains to earlier actions through a provenance DAG (Method, Equations 3–7; Appendix I).

- **Programmatic construction:** Graph construction uses sentence splitting, named-entity processing, normalization, and dense encoding rather than generative-LLM extraction; the graph is cached and reused (Corpus-Induced Tripartite Graph; Appendix E).

The matched interface experiment supports the authors’ claim that exposing finite targets contributes beyond changing the retrieval substrate or training budget. The SNC leave-one-out study separately supports contributions from frontier-relative and enablement credit.

The evidence does not establish universal superiority for all QA settings: Harness-G trails Graph-R1 on NQ at 3B, and the main held-out splits contain only 128 questions per dataset. Published cross-stack results in Table 1 are also not fully matched; the paper says only the RQ2 interface and credit variants share a controlled protocol (Appendix C).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Harness-G is text-only; extending structured actions and SNC to multimodal evidence remains future work (Conclusion).

- **Author-stated:** Each held-out split has only 128 questions, so small per-dataset differences are less reliable than large, consistent trends (Appendix B).

- **Author-demonstrated:** Failures arise when the correct entity is absent from the current menu, when the policy selects the wrong bridge, and when the correct evidence is visible but answer generation still emits the wrong string (Appendix N, Figures 19–21).

- **Analyst inference:** The finite menu improves controllability but makes performance dependent on initial retrieval, entity extraction, normalization, filtering, and candidate caps. The failure traces directly show candidate-coverage risk, though its aggregate frequency is not reported.

- **Analyst inference:** SNC relies on gold-answer aliases and a frozen answerer during training, so its applicability to tasks without reference answers is not established by these experiments.

- **Analyst inference:** Table 1’s baseline comparison mixes published cross-stack references, limiting causal attribution relative to the stronger matched RQ2 comparisons (Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** G-E uses GPT-4o-mini as evaluator, but the supplied text does not report evaluator reliability, variance, or sensitivity analysis (Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **How much performance depends on menu recall?** The failure cases show that gold entities can be absent from available targets. A next experiment could measure gold-sentence and gold-entity recall at each turn while varying candidate caps and retrieval channels.

2. **Can SNC work without gold answers?** Its scorer uses teacher-forced probability of accepted gold aliases. A plausible experiment would compare this with self-supervised evidence-consistency signals or a scorer trained without reference answers.

3. **Does finite navigation remain effective for longer-horizon or live-web research?** Evaluation permits at most six turns over fixed corpora. A next experiment could increase horizon and corpus volatility while measuring retrieval diversity, latency, and error accumulation.

4. **How does the method extend beyond text?** The authors explicitly leave multimodal evidence open. A useful experiment would build typed image–region–entity actions and test whether preview-based and enablement credit remain reliable.
