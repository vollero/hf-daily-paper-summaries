# Filesystem-Based Memory for LLM Agents: Organization, Evolution, and Sustainability

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26637) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26637)


## One-sentence takeaway

Agent-organized filesystem memory consistently reduces retrieval cost on large stores, but within the evaluated horizons its organization does not reliably improve answer quality, while management capability, consumer capability, and tool harness strongly affect outcomes.

## Problem and motivation

LLM agents need persistent memory beyond ephemeral context windows, and deployed systems increasingly use agent-managed directories of markdown files. The authors argue that prior research mostly studies bespoke representations or retrieval over files, leaving two assumptions untested: whether agents can keep filesystem memory organized as information accumulates, conflicts, and becomes stale, and whether that organization repays its curation cost (Abstract; Section 1).

This matters for both declarative memories—facts, events, preferences, and rules—and procedural skills distilled from experience. A poorly maintained store can preserve stale facts, scatter related information, or become increasingly expensive to search (Sections 1 and 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified formalization:** One hierarchical filesystem is shared by a management agent that integrates incoming content, a search agent that returns cited answers or skills, and an optional execution agent that produces trajectories and consumes retrieved guidance (Section 2; Figure 1; Equations 1–3).

- **Controlled design space:** The study varies memory construction—agent-curated, verbatim, reorganized, foldered, chunk-indexed, or absent—along with stream scale, management/search model strength, and tool harness (Sections 2.4 and 3).

- **Cross-setting evaluation:** Declarative memory is tested through conversational question answering on LoCoMo, PersonaMem, and REALTALK; procedural memory is tested through transfer across sequential ALFWorld tasks (Sections 2.3 and 3).

- **Sustainability analysis:** The authors track correctness or task success alongside attribution, tokens, cost, tool effort, store shape, survival of early files, and taxonomy adherence over growth trajectories (Section 3, “Metrics”; Section 4.4).

- **Empirical findings:** Organization mainly buys search economy; memory shape and tool harness strongly change store structure without consistently changing conversational correctness; procedural-memory performance depends on both curation quality and the execution agent consuming it (Sections 4.2–4.5).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that organization has a robust efficiency benefit when raw material is large and that memory usefulness depends on the writer, reader, consumer, and tools. It does not establish that a particular hierarchy intrinsically improves correctness, that results generalize beyond the tested models and benchmarks, or that filesystem memory remains healthy over months-long accumulation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Conversational evaluation uses one LoCoMo conversation, one REALTALK conversation, one PersonaMem 128k conversation, and three PersonaMem 32k conversations; several comparisons therefore rest on 32–158 questions (Sections 3, 4.2, and C.2).

- **Author-stated:** PersonaMem’s 32k and 128k tiers contain different conversations, so their contrast does not isolate scale (Sections 3 and 4.4).

- **Author-stated:** Each store is generally one stochastic build draw. An effectively repeated configuration changed from 2 to 29 files, showing substantial run-to-run structural variation (Section 4.1; Section D.3).

- **Author-stated:** LoCoMo judging varied by about ±1.3 percentage points for a fixed answer cell, and REALTALK was not repeat-judged (Table 1 caption). Four LoCoMo gold answers were also identified as defective, although filtering them did not change ordering (Section D.2).

- **Author-stated:** Growth covers one conversation or 140 tasks, not months-long persistent memory; conversational answer quality was not measured at intermediate growth checkpoints (Section 4.4).

- **Author-stated:** A corrected Curated skills+mem instruction was designed but never evaluated, leaving that condition’s ranking unresolved (Section C.6).

- **Analyst inference:** Because shape, text selection, compression, and wording vary together in agent-curated stores, many comparisons cannot isolate organization from content quality. Foldered sessions provides a cleaner shape-only comparison, but only for one constrained transformation.

- **Analyst inference:** Several headline results depend on one model family and provider-defined high-reasoning behavior, limiting evidence for cross-model generality.

- **Analyst inference:** The evidence tracks file survival and lexical taxonomy properties, but these do not directly prove factual freshness, contradiction resolution, or long-term trustworthiness.

## Open research questions

1. **Does organization improve quality beyond one-conversation horizons?** Current conversational benchmarks show cost benefits but little correctness sensitivity to shape, and no intermediate QA growth curve was measured. A next experiment could stream many conversations over months-equivalent checkpoints and compare curated, flat, and indexed stores on delayed queries and contradiction resolution.

2. **Which organizational properties causally help retrieval?** Agent-curated variants change both content and structure, while static hierarchy metrics are explicitly treated as descriptors. A controlled experiment could hold file text constant and independently perturb folder placement, headings, descriptions, and cross-references.

3. **Can temporal reconciliation prevent stale-preference failures?** PersonaMem 32k curation retained relevant facts but often left old and updated preferences simultaneously active. A next study could compare explicit temporal schemas, contradiction detectors, and co-located update histories with builder-only and searcher-only ablations.

4. **How stable are harness and model effects across repeated builds?** Store shapes varied sharply between runs, and some outcome differences were only suggestive. Repeating every model–harness cell across multiple random build and search draws would estimate variance and separate characteristic behavior from single-run artifacts.
