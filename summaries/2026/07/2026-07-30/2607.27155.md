# OmegaUse-OfficeVal: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Long-Horizon Office-Suite Tasks with Economic Grounding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27155) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27155)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmegaUse-OfficeVal evaluates final deliverables from 100 economically annotated, long-horizon office tasks and finds that tested LLM agents are faster and cheaper than junior workers but substantially worse in artifact quality.

## Problem and motivation

Existing productivity benchmarks often withhold assets, attach economic value only at broad occupational levels, or do not specifically represent office-suite workflows. Office and GUI benchmarks, meanwhile, tend to emphasize bounded interactions, execution trajectories, or environment states rather than long-horizon, usable final deliverables. The authors therefore target a practical question: can an agent produce a correct, editable office artifact, and how much human effort or market value does that work represent? (Sections 1–2.)

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 100 tasks adapted from practitioner-proposed workplace requests. Each provides a natural-language instruction and one or more files, and requires final DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, or PDF artifacts (Sections 3–4; Table 1).
- **Economic grounding:** Every task has measured human labor time and a price proxy. About 20% use practitioner-provided price signals; the rest use consistency-aggregated estimates from three experts (Section 4.2).
- **Artifact-based verification:** Fine-grained executable rubrics assess usability and task completion, including penalties for damage that increases repair effort. Failing any usability check yields zero (Section 4.3; Table 2).
- **Open evaluation asset:** The authors claim to release instructions, inputs, rubrics, verifier code, economic annotations, and metadata (Sections 1 and 2.1).
- **Empirical comparison:** Five LLMs are evaluated under a common programmatic scaffold against a best-of-at-least-two junior-worker baseline, using overall, labor-time-weighted, and price-weighted scores (Section 5; Appendices E–F).

These results establish performance and efficiency differences for five named models under one common, programmatic scaffold. They do not establish general superiority across other models, GUI-based agents, scaffolds, hardware environments, or real deployments. Cost comparisons also combine different concepts: human price proxies versus LLM token costs, rather than matched accounting of total labor and infrastructure.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Reported LLM runtime depends on scaffold implementation, environment, system load, and remote API latency, so it is only a reference measurement (Appendix E.2).
- **Author-stated:** The scaffold excludes GUI-level computer use and evaluates programmatic file interaction only (Appendix E.1).
- **Author-stated:** Human timing quality control involves some expert judgment (Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Only about 20% of tasks have explicit practitioner price signals; most price proxies are expert estimates (Section 4.2.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting the best human submission per task, while apparently using one fixed agent run per task, favors the human baseline and complicates direct reliability comparisons (Section 5.1.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Deterministic verification improves reproducibility but may miss valid artifact qualities that cannot be reduced to executable checks. The paper describes iterative expert alignment but reports no quantitative agreement statistic in the supplied text (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Tasks are reconstructed and privacy-adapted with LLM and human assistance, so fidelity to the original workplace distribution is not directly quantified (Section 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The unbalanced set contains only 100 tasks and is drawn from an unspecified practitioner population; representativeness beyond the collected scenarios is unclear (Sections 3.2 and 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** No scaffold ablation, repeated-run variance, or alternative verifier comparison is reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive are rankings to the agent scaffold?** The paper uses one in-house, programmatic scaffold and excludes GUI control. A next experiment could compare programmatic, GUI, and hybrid scaffolds while holding the model and task set fixed.

2. **How closely do code verifiers match independent human judgment?** The authors describe discrepancy resolution but provide no held-out agreement measurement. A blinded study could report item-level precision/recall and score correlation on unseen agent artifacts.

3. **How stable are results across repeated agent runs?** The supplied text reports no run-to-run variance. Repeating every model-task pair with multiple seeds could estimate failure probability and confidence intervals for model rankings.

4. **Do economic weights reflect realized workplace value?** Most prices are expert estimates, and human price proxies are compared with token costs. A follow-up could collect actual marketplace bids, accepted prices, repair time, and total deployment costs for the same deliverables.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported aggregate results because the supplied text includes methods, formulas, tables, and experimental settings.

Material missing information includes exact domain and intent counts from Figure 3, numerical bucket values from Figure 8, numerical breakdowns from Figures 9–11, verifier–expert agreement statistics, model API and decoding details, repeated-run variance, the full standardized prompt, annotator demographics and compensation, and evidence independently confirming that the claimed assets are accessible and complete.
