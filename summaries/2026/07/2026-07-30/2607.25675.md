# DecoEvo: Score-Decoupled Co-Evolution of Solver and Rubric-Generator Skills in Text Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25675) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25675)


## One-sentence takeaway

DecoEvo improves frozen-LLM text-space optimization by co-evolving solver and rubric-generator skills while evaluating generator edits through coverage and discrimination audits rather than the solver’s aggregate rubric score.

## Problem and motivation

Text-space optimization keeps model weights frozen and edits inspectable natural-language skills, but usually relies on a fixed evaluation rubric. On multidimensional, open-ended tasks, an incomplete rubric may stop revealing useful failure modes once the solver masters its covered criteria. Naively evolving the rubric is also unsafe: selecting rubric updates by the solver’s score can reward criteria that are easier for the current solver rather than criteria that better represent task quality. The authors frame this as score-coupled co-adaptation and an identification problem between genuine solver improvement and an easier proxy (Abstract; Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Score-decoupled co-evolution:** DecoEvo maintains a persistent solver skill and rubric-generator skill while holding the common LLM backbone fixed. Solver edits are selected using criterion-level rubric feedback, whereas generator edits are never selected by changes in aggregate solver score (Sections 3.1–3.3; Figure 2).

- **Dual-view generator auditing:** A task-conditioned structural audit searches for missing or ambiguous requirements, while a rubric-blind contrastive audit examines near-tied responses for quality distinctions that the current rubric misses (Section 3.3).

- **Audit–distill–verify updates:** Example-level diagnoses are distilled into reusable rubric-generation principles. Candidates are evaluated on held-out audit records and accepted through a Pareto-style rule requiring improvement on at least one audit objective without material regression on another (Section 3.3, Equation 10).

- **Black-box evaluation:** No model weights or gold rubrics are used during optimization, and only the final solver skill is needed at deployment (Section 3, Overview).

- **Empirical contribution:** The authors evaluate five benchmarks, three backbones, solver-only and score-coupled baselines, ablations, alternative-explanation controls, training dynamics, and rubric–gold-rubric alignment (Sections 4.1–4.7).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that score-decoupled generator updates outperform the tested fixed-rubric and score-coupled alternatives under official benchmark grading. Ablations and controls also make audit verification, persistent distillation, and evaluation coverage plausible contributors.

It does not establish cross-domain generalization, benefits when roles use different backbones, or correspondence with broad human judgments. Because the supplied text omits the numerical cells of Tables 1–5, many per-task means, standard deviations, costs, and absolute ablation scores cannot be independently checked here.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Every optimization role uses the same backbone, so heterogeneous solver, judge, auditor, and rewriter models remain untested (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** Transfer is confined to medicine and writing and remains within-domain; cross-domain transfer is not established (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** Broader human evaluation is still needed (Section 5).

- **Author-acknowledged selection issue:** Solver and generator validation sets are reused for multiple decisions and are model-selection sets, not unbiased evaluation sets. Final testing is disjoint, which mitigates but does not eliminate the possibility of adapting to gate behavior (Section 3.3).

- **Analyst inference:** All audit roles share the same model, so correlated model biases could affect both proposed rubric changes and their verification. The paper’s official held-out graders provide external outcome evidence, but do not directly validate every audit diagnosis.

- **Analyst inference:** The main ablation and alternative-explanation studies are limited to HealthBench. Their mechanistic conclusions may not transfer to writing or research assistance (Sections 4.3–4.4).

- **Analyst inference:** Criterion-overlap findings depend on an automated GPT-5.5 matcher. Its reported human agreement study is encouraging but covers only 150 balanced, stratified criterion pairs (Section 4.7).

- **Reproducibility risk:** Hyperparameters, rollout counts, schedules, full confidence intervals, and detailed matching procedures are deferred to supplementary material that is absent from the supplied text (Sections 4.1, 4.3, and 4.7).

## Open research questions

1. **Does score decoupling still help when different models fill different roles?**  
   This remains open because all roles share one frozen backbone. A next experiment could factorially vary solver, judge, auditor, and rewriter backbones while holding data and budgets fixed.

2. **Can the learned solver skill transfer across domains?**  
   Only medicine-to-medicine and writing-to-writing transfer is tested. A next experiment could train on one source domain and evaluate unchanged skills on disjoint domains using their official graders.

3. **How well do audit objectives reflect human quality judgments?**  
   The paper evaluates official scores and criterion overlap but calls for broader human evaluation. A blinded study could compare human pairwise preferences with structural adequacy, contrastive resolution, and final response scores.

4. **Which audit components matter outside HealthBench?**  
   Mechanism ablations are reported only for HealthBench. Repeating the full matched ablation matrix on WritingBench and ResearchQA would test whether near-tie auditing and held-out verification remain the dominant components.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text supplies a detailed method, evaluation protocol, aggregate comparisons, intervals, and ablation deltas. However, it does not include the numerical contents of Tables 1–5, supplementary hyperparameters, task split sizes, audit schedules, rollout counts, thresholds, prompts, absolute per-task scores, complete cost figures, or full paired intervals. Code, skill artifacts, and dataset-split release availability are also not established.
