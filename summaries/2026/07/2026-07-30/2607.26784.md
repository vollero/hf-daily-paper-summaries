# SkillRise: Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Cross-Task Skill Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26784)


## One-sentence takeaway

SkillRise trains one LLM policy to solve related tasks and continually rewrite a shared skill document, using later-task rewards to supervise curation, and reports higher success and lower pipeline runtime than the evaluated baselines.

## Problem and motivation

Standard agentic reinforcement learning treats task episodes independently, so useful experience is discarded rather than transferred to related tasks. Existing skill-learning approaches either repeatedly retry the same instance—risking instance-specific lessons and extra interactions—or combine extraction, storage, retrieval, and execution in multi-stage pipelines whose effects and costs are difficult to disentangle. The authors target end-to-end learning of transferable procedural knowledge across distinct but related tasks (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Cross-task formulation:** Distinct instances from an environment-provided task family are grouped and ordered from relatively simple to challenging, making later outcomes a test of whether earlier experience transfers (§3.1).
- **Unified policy and skill state:** One policy alternates between solving a task and rewriting a sequence-local textual skill document. That document—not prior trajectories—is the sole channel to the next task (§3.2; Figure 1).
- **Temporally aligned credit:** Solving receives the current task reward; curation receives a discounted sum of subsequent rewards. Advantages are compared only among trials with the same sequence, position, and role (§3.3, Equations 6–8).
- **Empirical contribution:** The method is evaluated on three text-based interactive benchmarks, including cross-task scaling, repeated-attempt generalization, model-size, curation, discount-sensitivity, and runtime analyses (§§4–5). The authors also report publicly available code (Abstract).

| Gains occur at both tested model sizes | ALFWorld Pass@1 is 78.1% with Qwen3-1.7B and 85.9% with Qwen3-4B, respectively 3.1 and 6.2 points above the strongest baseline; SkillRise gains 7.8 points across sizes | §5.2; Table 3 |

| Discount choice appears moderately robust | For \(\gamma\in\{0.3,0.4,0.6,0.7\}\), ALFWorld training trajectories converge within approximately one percentage point | §5.3; Figure 3 |
| Lower reported pipeline runtime | On ALFWorld, SkillRise obtains 85.9%, matching RetroAgent and exceeding the reproduced SkillRL result by 12.5 points; RetroAgent and SkillRL take 6.0× and 4.3× SkillRise’s runtime under the same GPU configuration | §5.4; Figure 4 |

These results support the authors’ claim that the complete method performs well within the tested text environments and that explicit curation matters beyond merely grouping tasks. The one-attempt sequence analysis is consistent with cross-instance transfer rather than repeated sampling of one instance. It does not, however, isolate which individual document contents cause later success, establish transfer across unrelated task families or environments, or demonstrate scaling beyond 4B parameters. The case studies in Figures 11–12 illustrate plausible updates but explicitly do not establish causal effects.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Sequence construction assumes environment-provided task-family metadata; automatically discovering related tasks remains unresolved (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is limited to models no larger than 4B, three text-based benchmarks, and verifiable outcome rewards (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Multimodal, real-world, and less readily verifiable settings were not evaluated (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** The cross-task scaling experiment is reported only for ALFWorld and \(K\in\{2,4,6\}\), so broader scaling behavior is not established (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The curation ablation removes both curation and inter-task transfer; it supports the value of the combined mechanism but does not separately identify document rewriting, document conditioning, and downstream credit assignment (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Runtime comparisons cover specific reproduced pipelines, including SkillRL with Gemini-2.5-Pro as teacher, so the reported ratios may depend on implementation and teacher choices (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The retrieved text gives training settings and prompt-template locations but not the numerical contents of Tables 1–3 or prompt text, limiting independent inspection of per-baseline and per-task-family results.

## Open research questions

1. **Can related tasks be discovered without family metadata?** This remains an explicit assumption (§8). A next experiment could cluster tasks using instruction, trajectory, or behavioral similarity and compare transfer against metadata-based grouping.
2. **Which component of skill evolution produces the gain?** The no-curation ablation removes multiple mechanisms together (§5.3). A factorial study could independently disable document rewriting, downstream-return credit, and document conditioning.
3. **Does cross-task scaling persist outside ALFWorld and beyond \(K=6\)?** Figure 2 covers one benchmark and three sequence lengths. Testing longer sequences on all three environments, while tracking document length and forgetting, would probe the boundary.
4. **Do learned skills transfer across families or modalities?** Current sequences contain instances from the same family and all benchmarks are text-based (§§3.1, 8). A next experiment could curate on one family or environment and evaluate zero-update transfer to a related but unseen family or multimodal task.
