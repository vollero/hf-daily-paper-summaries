# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-07-30

## Executive summary

- Agent evaluation moves toward consequential outputs: OmegaUse-OfficeVal (`2607.27155`) grades editable office artifacts, while the research-agent study (`2607.27191`) uses complete papers and original-author reviews.
- Embodied systems span opposite ends of the control stack. TurboVLA (`2607.27205`) reports fast, compact manipulation control; HumanCLAW (`2607.27180`) isolates high-level humanoid decision failures, especially self-localization and body placement.
- CAST (`2607.25308`) and SkillRise (`2607.26784`) both improve long-horizon text agents, but through different signals: solver-derived turn credit versus downstream rewards assigned to an evolving skill document.
- Rubrics become optimization inputs rather than final scalar scores. DecoEvo (`2607.25675`) audits and evolves rubric-generating skills; CoRT (`2607.25659`) uses rubric-removal likelihood contrasts for token-level credit.
- Security evaluation separates capability from safe behavior. StealthBench (`2607.26314`) finds frequent operational-security violations among successful offensive-agent runs; GPT-Red (`2607.26115`) reports adaptive prompt-injection attacks and associated defender-training gains.
- TriLayer (`2607.25802`) is the day’s methodological singleton, introducing explicit RGB/RGBA layer supervision for video insertion and decomposition.
- Most headline findings remain setting-specific: several papers omit uncertainty estimates, full result tables, repeated seeds, or broad out-of-distribution evaluation.

## Main results worth noting

- TurboVLA (`2607.27205`) reports 97.7% average LIBERO success across 2,000 rollouts at 31.2 ms per action chunk and 0.9 GB peak inference VRAM on one RTX 4090, batch size one. On 50 clean-setting RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, it reports 60.2% mean success at 43.4 ms, versus 57.0% at 95.6 ms for \(\pi_{0.5}\).

- HumanCLAW (`2607.27180`) reports a steep staged drop for its best tested frozen VLM: 64.9% FindSR, 42.4% NavSR, and 16.8% InteractSR over its simulated benchmark. Among 726 episodes reaching a seat, 71% failed to sit; its deterministic attribution rules assigned 81% of those failures to body placement.

- CAST (`2607.25308`) reaches 62.1% ID and 28.4% unseen Avg@4 across Sokoban, Minesweeper, and Rush Hour with Qwen3-4B, compared with 45.4% and 20.8% for GiGPO’s three-game averages. Its zero-shot ALFWorld/WebShop average was 30.3%, versus 24.7% for the second-best reported method.

- SkillRise (`2607.26784`) reports Qwen3-4B Pass@1 of 85.9% on ALFWorld, 84.4% on WebShop, and 54.6% on ScienceWorld. Its Pass@3 values were 92.2%, 96.1%, and 61.0% when the evolving document persisted across repeated attempts.

- DecoEvo (`2607.25675`) improves five-benchmark mean score over SkillOpt by 3.10 points for GPT-4o, 1.74 for Qwen3-4B, and 1.78 for Qwen3-8B; reported 95% confidence intervals were [2.88, 3.32], [1.42, 2.06], and [1.69, 1.87]. Score-coupled co-evolution fell below SkillOpt in 13 of 15 combinations.

- CoRT (`2607.25659`) improves all five reported Qwen3-14B CSR entries against matched GRPO, including IFEval prompt 87.87→89.17 and MultiDimIF 82.59→83.48. Under sparse AON reward, however, IFEval prompt declined 90.13→89.80 and instruction accuracy 93.94→93.48.

- OmegaUse-OfficeVal (`2607.27155`) reports a human artifact score of 27.79 versus the best tested LLM score of 17.91. Human outputs scored zero on 29% of tasks, while the lowest LLM zero rate was 38%; its cost comparison uses human price proxies and LLM token costs, so it is not matched total-cost accounting.

- The research-agent study (`2607.27191`) reports original-author ratings of 2/6 Reject and 1/6 Strong Reject for its two principal submissions. The agents nevertheless completed hundreds of experiments and full manuscripts; the authors attribute failure mainly to experimental judgment, backtracking, feedback use, resource management, and instruction drift.

- StealthBench (`2607.26314`) reports 527 successful tasks among 771 trajectories, but only 338 trajectories that both solved and passed the stealth panel. Its three judges were unanimous on 87.9% of 770 complete panels, with Fleiss’ \(\kappa=0.834\); this measures model-panel consistency, not agreement with security experts.

- GPT-Red (`2607.26115`) reports GPT-5.6 robustness of 95.9% to generated fake-chain-of-thought direct injections versus 5.2% for GPT-5.1, and below 4% GPT-Red attack success against GPT-5.6 in one held-out indirect prompt-injection exercise. The design does not isolate GPT-Red from other model-generation changes.

- TriLayer (`2607.25802`) reports DBL-Decompose PSNR/LPIPS of 33.09/0.025 on Movies and 38.22/0.021 on Kubric, outperforming listed generation baselines but trailing OmnimatteZero’s reconstruction-oriented 35.11/0.014 and 44.97/0.010. Its perceptual-background FID is stronger, illustrating that pixel fidelity and perceptual plausibility yield different conclusions.

## Open research directions

1. **Test compact embodied control under distribution shift (`2607.27205`).** Train only on clean demonstrations, then evaluate backgrounds, lighting, objects, viewpoints, dynamics, and unseen instructions while measuring success, latency, collisions, and recovery.

2. **Separate missing body state from missing embodied reasoning (`2607.27180`).** Add proprioception, contact, and collision feedback one at a time to the same frozen VLMs; compare termination accuracy, collision rate, and interaction success against RGB-only control.

3. **Compare long-horizon credit signals directly (`2607.25308`, `2607.26784`).** On one shared environment, match rollout and compute budgets across solver-value shaping, downstream skill-document credit, and terminal-only RL; measure transfer and teacher-error sensitivity.

4. **Validate evolving rubrics against independent humans (`2607.25675`, `2607.25659`).** Stratify criteria into local formatting, global semantic, and absence-based requirements; collect human token/span attribution and response-quality judgments, then test both rubric coverage and CoRT localization.

5. **Measure deliverable quality across scaffolds and repeated runs (`2607.27155`, `2607.27191`).** Hold the model and task fixed while varying programmatic, GUI, hybrid, and restart-enabled scaffolds; report expert quality, verifier agreement, variance, cost, and repair time.

6. **Turn research-agent failure diagnosis into controlled interventions (`2607.27191`).** Compare ordinary operation with forced alternative-hypothesis checkpoints, clean-context restart teams, resource dashboards, and explicit stop/restart policies on preregistered unpublished questions.

7. **Join capability and operational safety in offensive-agent evaluation (`2607.26314`, `2607.26115`).** Evaluate adaptive attackers on tasks carrying both exploit-success and human-labeled OPSEC criteria; test whether stopping rules, provenance labels, or execution gates reduce reckless solves without suppressing valid discoveries.

8. **Isolate GPT-Red’s defender-training contribution (`2607.26115`).** Train otherwise matched defenders with and without GPT-Red attacks, then evaluate fresh post-training attackers from several model families under equal query budgets.

9. **Make explicit layered video editing interactive (`2607.25802`).** Compare distillation, cached branch features, reduced-step solvers, and asymmetric branch execution at matched quality, memory, and latency; include difficult contact, reflection, deformation, and occlusion strata.

10. **Test whether proposed cross-paper combinations actually work (`2607.27155`, `2607.25675`, `2607.25659`).** Optimize OfficeVal tasks with evolving rubrics and token-level rubric credit, then evaluate only on held-out artifacts using executable checks plus blinded human usability ratings.
