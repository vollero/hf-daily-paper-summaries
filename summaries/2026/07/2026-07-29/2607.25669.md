# OmniDelta: Skill-Driven Budget Allocation for Token Compression in OmniLLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25669) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25669)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmniDelta is a training-free allocator that improves compressed audio-video QA by redistributing a fixed token budget across modalities and temporal units before token pruning, achieving 44.2% WorldSense accuracy and 1.64× end-to-end speedup at 25% retention with Qwen2.5-Omni-7B.

## Problem and motivation

Omni-modal LLMs incur high memory and prefill costs because audio and video create long token sequences—roughly 10K audio-video tokens for the paper’s 60-second Qwen-Omni example. Existing compressors largely optimize which tokens to retain under predefined modality or temporal budgets.

The authors identify budget placement as the missing preceding decision. Their diagnostics suggest that direct query-to-modality similarity poorly represents the evidence a question requires, while uniform temporal budgets can undersupply informative frames or audio segments even when token selection within each unit is strong (Sections 1, 2.1, and 2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hierarchical fixed-budget allocation:** OmniDelta preserves the requested global retention ratio but reallocates tokens first between audio and video, then among one-second audio segments and individual video frames (Section 3.2; Algorithm 1).

- **Intent-aware modality routing:** Offline-generated audio and video skill pools encode task types such as speech, rhythm, OCR, and motion. At inference, Top-k cosine similarity between query and skill embeddings determines a bounded audio-video budget transfer (Section 3.2.1; Appendix E).

- **Content-aware temporal allocation:** Intra-unit feature diversity estimates complexity, while similarity to the previous unit estimates temporal redundancy. Centered scores increase budgets for complex, less repetitive units and reduce them for redundant units (Equations 7–11).

- **Backend-compatible pruning:** The reported implementation uses audio-encoder attention for audio and interleaved spatio-temporal compression for video, but the authors frame allocation as separable from token selection and potentially compatible with other pruning backends (Section 3.2.3).

- **Exact budget conservation:** Projection and bounded largest-remainder rounding preserve the sample-level retained-token count (Algorithm 1; Appendix B.3, Proposition 3 and Corollary 2).

The results support the authors’ claim that budget placement can improve accuracy at a fixed compressed-token count and yield useful efficiency–accuracy trade-offs. They do not establish model-family generality, superiority over every contemporary compressor, or that the heuristic allocations approximate an optimal information-preserving allocation. The theoretical analysis proves boundedness and conservation, not empirical optimality (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope qualification:** The controlled audio/video diagnostics use visible regions and short intervals as interpretable proxies rather than reproducing the OmniLLM tokenization and pruning pipeline (Appendix D.2).

- **Author-stated theoretical qualification:** Complexity and redundancy are proxies for unknown marginal information loss; the theory does not claim they recover the optimal loss functions (Appendix B.4).

- **Analyst inference — limited model generality:** Evaluation covers only Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and 3B, so transfer to other architectures is unestablished (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference — dataset-dependent routing:** GPT-5.5-xhigh builds the skill pools from WorldSense questions and candidates, and the routing diagnostic also uses WorldSense. Results on broader query distributions or independently constructed skills are not reported (Appendices D.1 and E).

- **Analyst inference — model-mediated diagnostics:** GPT-5.5-xhigh and Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview generate, select, answer, or grade parts of the motivation studies. The supplied text gives prompts but no human-validation or inter-rater analysis (Sections 2.1–2.2; Appendix D).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablation evidence in the retrieved text:** Table 3 is announced as a component ablation, but its numerical contents are absent; the following prose also appears to refer to Table 4 as the ablation despite Table 4 containing efficiency results. Consequently, the claimed complementarity of all three allocation levels cannot be numerically checked here (Section 4.3).

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility gaps:** The skill-bank sizes are reported, but representative entries are absent from the retrieved boxes, and values for routing Top-k, feasible-range projection, and some prior-local-budget details are not fully specified in the supplied text (Sections 3.2.1–3.2.2; Appendices A and E).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the skill pools generalize beyond their construction distribution?** This remains open because they are derived from WorldSense. A next experiment could compare WorldSense-built, cross-dataset-built, and manually curated pools on held-out benchmarks and unseen task families.

2. **Which allocation level contributes most consistently?** The retrieved text omits Table 3’s values. A next experiment should report modality-only, audio-only, video-only, and combined modules across every benchmark, model size, and retention ratio.

3. **Are complexity and adjacent-unit redundancy the best training-free allocation signals?** The paper motivates them but does not establish their optimality. A next experiment could compare them with motion, entropy, longer-range redundancy, and learned-but-lightweight allocators under an identical pruning backend.

4. **How robust are gains across models and pruning backends?** Only two sizes of one model family and one concrete backend are evaluated. A factorial study across OmniLLM architectures and multiple selectors could test whether allocation gains are genuinely backend-independent.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains detailed methods, benchmark tables, diagnostic protocols, and efficiency measurements, but lacks the numerical contents of Table 3, the actual representative skill entries, statistical uncertainty or repeated-run analysis for main benchmark results, human validation of model-mediated diagnostics, full projection/prior implementation details, and evidence of code or asset availability.
