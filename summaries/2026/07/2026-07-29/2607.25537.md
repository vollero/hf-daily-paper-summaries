# Visual prompt engineering for video models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25537) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25537)


## One-sentence takeaway

Transforming task images into model-friendly, often photorealistic variants improves video-model reasoning across several benchmarks and can outperform text-prompt optimization or repeated generation, although gains depend on faithful image editing and are not universal across visual model types.

## Problem and motivation

Video models receive both text and visual prompts, yet prior prompt optimization has largely focused on text while treating the initial task image as fixed. The authors argue that this is a consequential gap because the image specifies much of a visual reasoning problem’s layout, objects, and constraints. They investigate whether changing only a task’s visual presentation—without changing its underlying logic—can improve generated-video solutions (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Visual prompt engineering (VIPE):** An ideator proposes a visual transformation, an image editor generates candidate variants, and an optional scorer selects the candidate judged most faithful to the original task. The resulting image and original text instruction are passed to a video model (Sec. 2; Fig. 1).
- **Automated prompt search:** The paper evaluates open-loop freeform ideation and closed-loop Atomic Concept Editing (ACE), which explores single-concept changes using autorater feedback (Secs. 4.1–4.2).
- **Test-time scaling:** VIPE variants are searched or ensembled alongside repeated video generations, with cost-based comparisons against conventional self-consistency (Sec. 5).
- **Empirical diagnosis:** Experiments isolate a preference for realistic textures, associate increasing realism with better scene consistency, and test whether the effect transfers to native image-generation reasoners (Secs. 3, 7–8).
- **Evaluation contribution:** The study covers physics prediction, mazes, object matching, sorting, conjunctive search, and RushHour, supported by VLM-based and deterministic autoraters with limited human validation (Sec. 4; Apps. A, H).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that visual presentation can materially change measured reasoning performance, especially for video generators given abstract inputs. It does not establish that photorealism is universally optimal, that VIPE improves all tasks or model families, or that gains always reflect deeper reasoning rather than better generation stability, attention, or compatibility with the evaluation pipeline.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** VIPE quality depends on the editor preserving task logic. Artifacts or structural changes can invalidate the comparison, motivating filtering and manual verification (Secs. 3 and 9).
- **Author-stated:** Image editing adds cost, although it was cheaper than video generation under the paper’s API prices (Sec. 9; App. B).
- **Author-stated:** Freeform ideation can be constrained by detailed base prompts and struggles to find one variant that generalizes across task splits (App. E.1).
- **Author-stated:** ACE was expensive and therefore evaluated on only three tasks (Sec. 4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Several automated experiments use only 5–10 source images per task or split, despite many generated videos. This limits diversity at the underlying problem-instance level (App. A; Table 3).
- **Analyst inference:** Best-of-20 and best-per-sample reporting introduces selection advantages; Fig. 3 includes oracle selection that is not directly deployable without a reliable selector.
- **Analyst inference:** VPCT’s sketch and VIPE conditions use different deterministic evaluators, and the VIPE evaluation removes buckets from actual inputs while figures retain them for visualization. These choices complicate attribution of the full performance difference solely to visual realism (Apps. C.1–C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** VLM autoraters may share biases with the VLMs used for ideation and filtering. Human validation is limited, and agreement is only moderate for Maze and RushHour (App. H).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** App. H.1’s prose reports 85.6% agreement and κ=0.711, whereas Table 9 reports 87.0% and κ=0.732; the supplied text does not reconcile these values.
- **Analyst inference:** Cost conclusions depend on specific API prices, an eight-second 720p configuration, and the assumed five-edit loop; broader compute or latency comparisons are not reported (Fig. 5; App. B).

## Open research questions

1. **Do VIPE gains transfer to unseen instances, splits, and model families?** Search often used few source images and task-specific best variants. A next experiment could learn variants on held-out development instances and evaluate them unchanged across unseen splits and multiple video models.

2. **What mechanism matters most: realism, object permanence, attention, or evaluator compatibility?** The texture ablation and consistency study implicate realism, but do not fully separate these factors. A factorial experiment could independently vary texture realism, geometry, contrast, semantic familiarity, and evaluator while measuring both task accuracy and temporal consistency.

3. **Can a non-oracle selector predict the best variant without generating many complete videos?** Best-per-sample results are strong but require knowing which variant succeeds. A next experiment could train or calibrate a lightweight selector on image-level features and test its out-of-sample selection regret against random and oracle choices.

4. **Can training reduce realism bias rather than compensate for it at inference time?** The paper treats VIPE mainly as test-time optimization. Fine-tuning on paired abstract/realistic scenes, followed by evaluation on both styles and new abstractions, could test whether representation robustness improves without sacrificing realistic-video performance.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental design and headline numerical results because the supplied text includes the full paper and appendices.

Material gaps remain: exact confidence-interval values and statistical tests; complete per-variant/per-task scores behind several plots; independent human verification of edit faithfulness beyond VPCT; training-data information needed to test the representation-gap hypothesis; latency and hardware measurements; and an explicit code, prompt, or transformed-dataset release statement.
