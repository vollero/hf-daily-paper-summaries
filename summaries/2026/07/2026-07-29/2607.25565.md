# ReDesign: Recovering Editable Design Structures from Images via Agentic Decomposition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25565) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25565)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReDesign reconstructs raster designs as editable layer hierarchies by combining specialized tools through VLM-guided tree expansion and locally verifying each expansion, achieving the authors’ best reported editability while enabling up to 7.1× parallel-execution speedup.

## Problem and motivation

Raster exports and screenshots discard the text, geometry, grouping, and layer-order information needed for precise editing, forcing designers to recreate assets manually. Recovering this information is an ill-posed inverse problem: visually identical images may arise from different underlying structures.

The authors argue that existing OCR, vectorization, and layer-decomposition methods solve only parts of the problem. Straightforward serial composition is unreliable because early tool errors propagate, while flat RGBA layers do not necessarily recover semantic object identities, parameters, or hierarchy (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hierarchical reconstruction:** ReDesign represents the full raster image as the root of a tree. A VLM controller progressively expands nodes into atomic text, vector, or raster elements and exports the result as an editable JSON hierarchy (Sections 3.1–3.3; Figure 2).

- **Modular tool composition:** Its actions combine OCR, segmentation, inpainting, layered decomposition, connected-component labeling, object detection, vectorization, and font/property fitting. The action space can accommodate new tools (Section 3.2).

- **Graceful verification:** Every parent-to-children proposal is accepted, partially pruned, or retried according to parent coverage, hallucination, and sibling-redundancy checks. Repair remains local to the failing branch (Sections 3.4–3.5; Figure 19).

- **Parallel tree execution:** Nodes retain only their root-to-node history, allowing independent frontier branches to expand concurrently without sibling-state coordination (Sections 3.3 and 3.5).

- **Editability benchmark:** The authors introduce the Figma Edit Replay Benchmark, comprising 909 raw design files and 14,796 controlled edits covering layout, color, and text (Section 4.1; Figure 3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** There is no universally correct decomposition granularity; the desired structure depends on the intended edit. ReDesign’s layers may therefore fail to align one-to-one with Figma or Crello annotations even when the rendering is accurate (Section 5.3).

- **Author-stated operational concern:** Reconstruction uses commercial VLM APIs and several specialized tools. Local accelerated components were evaluated on an NVIDIA A6000, although the authors state that optimized tools can run below 8 GB of VRAM (Section D.2).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete quantitative evidence:** Tables 1–5 and figures containing metric values are not transcribed. Claims of superiority cannot be independently quantified from the supplied text.

- **Analyst inference — protocol ambiguity:** Section 4.3 describes matching an edit target through highest IoU, whereas Appendix D.1 specifies minimum-cost many-to-many matching with containment candidates and Hungarian assignment. The relationship between these descriptions is unclear.

- **Analyst inference — benchmark scope:** Evaluation centers on Figma and Crello graphic designs. The text does not establish performance on other authoring tools, document types, photographs, languages, or accessibility-focused edits.

- **Analyst inference — dependence on imperfect tools:** Appendix C.2 reports that most actions other than connected-component labeling frequently require retry or pruning. The source does not provide the numeric intervention rates needed to judge robustness.

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility:** Prompts and tool defaults are described, but API versions, matching thresholds, cost weights, complete latency/cost figures, and explicit artifact-release details are not available in the supplied text.

- **Ethical risk identified by the authors:** Editable reconstruction can facilitate unauthorized copying or modification of copyrighted and branded material; they recommend usage policies, provenance, auditing, and disclosure that outputs are approximate (Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does ReDesign generalize beyond the evaluated design distributions?**  
   Only Figma and Crello results are reported. A next experiment could evaluate fixed configurations on multilingual posters, presentation slides, web screenshots, and designs containing photographic or textured effects.

2. **Which verification checks contribute most to editability and efficiency?**  
   The paper reports intervention patterns and broad ablations but supplies no isolated numerical comparison of hallucination, redundancy, and coverage checks. A factorial ablation could remove each check and measure edit-replay scores, retries, runtime, and hard failures.

3. **How should decomposition granularity be selected for a user’s intended edits?**  
   The authors acknowledge that granularity is task-dependent and show prompt-based adjustment, but do not quantify its reliability. A next experiment could condition reconstruction on known edit sets and compare automatically selected granularity with designer preferences and edit success.

4. **Does the speed advantage persist under equal compute and API budgets?**  
   The reported maximum is 7.1× over serial execution, but detailed resource-normalized measurements are missing. A controlled study could equalize hardware, concurrency, tool-call limits, and VLM tokens while reporting latency, cost, variance, and accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives a detailed method, evaluation protocol, and qualitative conclusions, but omits the numerical contents of Tables 1–5 and most figures. Material missing information includes exact metric values and baseline margins, sample splits, uncertainty or significance estimates, verification thresholds and matching weights, per-action retry rates, full latency and monetary cost, detailed ablation results, API/version information, and confirmed release or licensing terms for the benchmark and implementation.
