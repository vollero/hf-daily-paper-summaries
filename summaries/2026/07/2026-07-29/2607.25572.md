# Mapping CVEs to MITRE ATT&amp;CK Techniques: A Curated Gold-Set Classifier and the Limits of LLM-Assisted Label Expansion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25572) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25572)


## One-sentence takeaway

A multi-label classifier trained on 1,207 expert-mapped CVEs substantially outperforms zero-shot similarity ranking, while adding up to 984 LLM-labeled CVEs at roughly 0.39 expert agreement yields no reliable ranking gain and harms rare-technique performance.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses automatic mapping from free-text CVE descriptions to Enterprise MITRE ATT&CK techniques, enabling vulnerability feeds to support detection coverage, threat-informed prioritization, and risk assessment. Manual mapping cannot scale, while automation is difficult because mappings are multi-label, long-tailed, partly subjective, and connect descriptions of software flaws to descriptions of attacker behavior.

The authors identify two gaps: deterministic CWE→CAPEC→ATT&CK mappings contain severe table-expansion artifacts and are unsuitable as training labels, while it was unclear whether inexpensive LLM-generated labels could safely enlarge a small expert-curated dataset (Sections 1, 3.3, and 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Provenance-tiered dataset:** The authors normalize and merge two existing CTID expert-mapping sources into 1,207 CVEs, keeping expert labels separate from automatically derived and LLM-generated labels (Sections 3.1–3.2).
- **Supervised classifier:** They fine-tune `roberta-base` on CVE titles and descriptions using a sigmoid multi-label head, weighted binary cross-entropy, parent-technique normalization, and a 57-technique working vocabulary (Section 4).
- **Derived-label audit:** They quantify the fan-out and implausible frequency patterns produced by the CWE→CAPEC→ATT&CK chain on 39,156 CVEs (Section 3.3; Figure 2).
- **LLM-expansion study:** They validate a local LLM labeler against expert mappings, then test 100–984 added labels across five seeds and two encoders (Sections 6 and 7).
- **Evaluation correction and artifacts:** They diagnose test-set checkpoint selection as a source of unstable conclusions, replace it with validation-split selection, and release datasets, models, code, and logs (Sections 6.3, 6.5; Reproducibility and Artifacts).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Supervised learning beats zero-shot similarity ranking. | On the identical gold test split and 57-technique vocabulary, single-run recall@5 rises from 0.322 to 0.686 and MRR from 0.397 to 0.620. These figures used the pre-correction protocol. | Table 1; Section 5 |
| Corrected gold-only performance remains strong. | With validation-split checkpoint selection over five seeds: recall@5 \(0.673\pm0.019\), recall@3 \(0.536\pm0.032\), micro-F1 \(0.410\pm0.006\), and macro-F1 \(0.177\pm0.014\). | Section 6.3, “The mechanism: evaluation noise” |
| The selected LLM remains below the classifier’s expert agreement. | On all 121 gold test CVEs, the 122B model reaches 0.392 micro-F1 versus the classifier reference of 0.407; its 30-CVE estimate was 0.465. | Table 2; Section 6.2 |
| LLM expansion provides no ranking gain under the corrected protocol. | Across five seeds, recall@5 falls from 0.673 gold-only to 0.655 with 297 rows and 0.651 with 984 rows. | Table 6 |
| Large-scale expansion harms rare-technique performance. | With 984 LLM rows, macro-F1 falls from 0.177 to 0.151 under the corrected protocol, approximately 2.9 SEM; the micro-F1 increase of 0.017 remains unresolved at approximately 1.3 SEM. | Table 6 |
| The negative tail effect transfers to another encoder. | On ModernBERT-base, adding 984 rows lowers macro-F1 from 0.180 to 0.152, approximately 3.1 SEM; recall@5 gains are not reliable. | Table 7; Section 7 |
| Curated labels continue to improve performance. | From 243 to 972 gold training rows, recall@5 increases from 0.556 to 0.673 and macro-F1 from 0.114 to 0.177, using a frozen vocabulary and test set. | Figure 4; Section 6.3, “Gold labels scale” |
| Derived mappings have high coverage but severe fan-out. | Among 39,156 CVEs, 88.3% receive labels; labeled CVEs have median 8 and mean 13.3 techniques, while 7,381 CVEs receive at least 20. | Figure 2; Section 3.3 |
| Test-based checkpoint selection causes substantial instability. | Three nominally identical fixed-seed runs report recall@5 of 0.636, 0.683, and 0.685—a spread of 0.048—when selecting among 40 checkpoints on 119 test examples. | Section 6.3, “The mechanism: evaluation noise” |

The evidence supports the authors’ claims that expert supervision improves ranking over their chosen zero-shot baseline, that unreviewed labels at the tested agreement level do not improve the classifier, and that test-set checkpoint selection can overwhelm small effects. It does not establish that all LLM annotation is ineffective, that the results generalize beyond the tested labelers, encoders, ATT&CK domain, and dataset distribution, or that macro-F1 perfectly measures rare-technique utility.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The 1,207-CVE gold set is small and biased toward vulnerabilities exploited in the wild, especially remote server exploitation; transfer to never-exploited CVEs is uncertain (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Filtering labels with fewer than five training examples prevents the classifier from emitting much of the ATT&CK matrix (Sections 4 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** CTID labels are an operational gold standard, not necessarily exhaustive ground truth; missing techniques are not guaranteed true negatives (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** ATT&CK version and schema drift cannot be fully resolved by identifier replacement, particularly after semantic splits or merges (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** The expansion verdict covers one local model family and prompt, at most 984 added CVEs, and five seeds; stronger or human-reviewed labeling remains untested (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Expansion can alter the label vocabulary, so gold-only and expanded models are not evaluated under a fully controlled label space (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Tables 1 and 3–5 used test-set checkpoint selection and therefore carry select-on-test optimism; the decisive comparison was subsequently rerun under the corrected protocol (Sections 6.3 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** Expansion quality is measured indirectly through performance on the old gold test split, not through expert review of the newly labeled CVEs (Section 7).
- **Analyst inference:** The main baseline comparison is limited to one general-purpose embedding ranker, so superiority over other supervised, retrieval, or hybrid approaches is not established.
- **Analyst inference:** Macro-F1 is treated as a rare-technique proxy, but the supplied text does not report per-technique utility or analyst-centered evaluation by frequency band.

## Open research questions

1. **What annotation quality is sufficient for beneficial expansion?** The paper tests labels near 0.39 expert agreement but does not locate a success threshold. A next experiment could create controlled mixtures of expert-reviewed and noisy labels at several measured agreement levels.

2. **Can confidence filtering or rare-technique targeting avoid tail degradation?** Randomly sampled LLM labels strengthen frequent techniques while weakening rare ones. A next experiment could compare random expansion with high-confidence-slot filtering and technique-stratified sampling under a frozen vocabulary.

3. **How well does the classifier generalize beyond CTID/KEV-like vulnerabilities?** The gold set is exploitation-biased. A prospective evaluation could have analysts annotate a temporally newer, stratified sample including never-exploited CVEs.

4. **Would richer security-specific inputs improve the ceiling?** The current model uses only titles and descriptions, and the authors leave source changes and patches for future work. A next experiment could compare text-only classification with a controlled multi-input model using advisory text, commit messages, and safe patch representations.
