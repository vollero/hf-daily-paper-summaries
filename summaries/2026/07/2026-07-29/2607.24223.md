# A New Role for Relevance: Guiding Corpus Interaction in Agentic Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24223) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24223)


## One-sentence takeaway

RARG uses document- and excerpt-level relevance as an execution prior for grep-style corpus exploration, improving the reported accuracy–tool-use trade-off in fixed-corpus question answering while showing task-dependent benefits in reasoning-intensive retrieval.

## Problem and motivation

Retrieval agents usually use relevance to select a top-\(k\) context, which can omit decisive spans and cannot by itself localize, connect, or verify multi-document evidence. Direct Corpus Interaction (DCI) offers finer-grained grep and file-reading operations, but searches without corpus-level prioritization, so useful evidence may appear late or be lost through output truncation.

The authors identify a gap in intermediate systems such as RISE and DR-DCI: relevance helps construct a bounded workspace but does not guide the order in which grep traverses documents or determine which matches remain visible within the agent’s observation budget (§1; §2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Execution-prior framing:** The paper proposes using relevance during corpus interaction, rather than only to filter its inputs (§1).

- **RARG:** An embedding retriever writes up to 10,000 ranked document paths into a scope file. Ripgrep scans these paths sequentially using an automatically inserted single-threaded `-j1` option, exposing matches from higher-ranked documents first (§3.2).

- **RARG+:** The agent is initialized with the ten most query-relevant paragraphs selected from top-ranked scope documents, providing an early search anchor (§3.3).

- **RARG++:** Up to 500 grep matches are reranked using a constructed query that combines the global scope query with local grep terms; only the top 30 or 60 matches are shown, depending on the benchmark (§3.4; §4.1).

- **Empirical contribution:** The variants are compared with retrieval agents, unrestricted DCI, and retrieval-constructed interaction spaces on BrowseComp-Plus, a 1M-document scaling condition, and four BRIGHT domains (§4).

The results support the authors’ claim that relevance-guided traversal can improve the empirical accuracy–efficiency frontier in the tested fixed-corpus settings. The variant reversal on BRIGHT also indicates that aggressive local reranking is not uniformly beneficial: it favors rapid convergence in QA but may suppress the breadth needed for ranked retrieval.

The evidence does not establish comparable gains for open-web search, other model families, or arbitrary domains. It also does not isolate every component under controlled computational cost, and the single case study is illustrative rather than proof of general superiority.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Performance depends on embedding quality, with long-document ranking and short-match reranking potentially requiring different models. BRIGHT uses NV for documents but Qwen3-Embedding for matches.
- Sequential `rg -j1` and match reranking add latency; tool-call counts therefore do not fully represent wall-clock or computational cost.
- The protocol is sensitive to instruction following: GPT-5.4-nano used relevance scopes less consistently.
- Long noisy documents create incidental matches; RARG++ only partly mitigated the degradation at 1M documents.
- The generative reranking-query variant exhibited a suspected train–evaluation mismatch.
- Evaluation is limited to BrowseComp-Plus, four BRIGHT subsets, and GPT-5.x models.

**Analyst inferences**

- Accuracy is judged by GPT-5.1 on BrowseComp-Plus, but the supplied text gives no human-validation rate or judge uncertainty, creating a potential measurement risk (§4.1).
- The 1M-document corpus uses one specific distractor construction—900K long FineWeb-Edu documents—so it does not establish robustness to other corpus-growth patterns (§4.1; §4.3).
- RARG changes several factors across variants, while the supplied experiments do not report isolated ablations for scope size, paragraph count, reranking pool size, or `-j1`.
- Tool calls are explicitly non-comparable across interfaces on BRIGHT because tools return different amounts and forms of content (§4.4).
- Some baseline figures are cited from RISE rather than rerun, and the GPT-5.4 RISE thinking effort is unspecified in Table 1.
- The retrieved text omits the detailed rows of Tables 2–4 and the plotted numerical values in Figures 3–4, limiting independent scrutiny of those analyses.

## Open research questions

1. **Can relevance-guided interaction remain effective without single-threaded scanning?**  
   The authors acknowledge a latency trade-off from `rg -j1`. A next experiment could compare sequential scanning with parallel scanning followed by streaming, relevance-aware match reordering, measuring accuracy, wall-clock latency, and compute.

2. **How should local reranking adapt to task objectives?**  
   RARG++ leads on QA but trails RARG+ on BRIGHT, suggesting a convergence–recall trade-off. A next experiment could dynamically vary reranking breadth based on whether the target is one verified answer or a ranked document list.

3. **Can one relevance model handle both long documents and short matches?**  
   The BRIGHT setup required different embedding models at the two granularities. A next experiment could train or calibrate a multi-granularity retriever and compare it with the two-model pipeline under equal compute.

4. **How robust is RARG to different kinds of corpus noise and scale?**  
   The scaling test uses long FineWeb-Edu distractors and shows reduced grep coverage. A next experiment could separately vary document count, document length, lexical overlap, and adversarial distractors to identify the main failure source.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods and the complete BrowseComp-Plus Table 1, but material evidence is missing or incomplete: full numerical Tables 2–4, exact Figure 3–4 values, per-domain BRIGHT results, run-to-run variance or significance tests, wall-clock latency, embedding and LLM cost, judge validation, full early embedding-baseline results, and evidence of a public RARG code release.
