# PerceptionBench: Evaluating Atomic Visual Perception in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24957) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24957)


## One-sentence takeaway

PerceptionBench isolates ten failure-derived visual capabilities in 3,000 verified questions and finds that none of sixteen evaluated frontier MLLMs exceeds 60% accuracy, with hallucination resistance and per-sample consistency emerging as major weaknesses.

## Problem and motivation

Existing holistic MLLM benchmarks entangle perception with reasoning and external knowledge, making errors difficult to attribute, while application-specific benchmarks cover narrow, designer-defined slices of perception. Because missed or hallucinated visual information can undermine downstream reasoning, the authors seek a diagnostic evaluation that isolates individual perceptual capabilities and derives its coverage from observed model failures rather than predefined task categories (Introduction; Sections 2 and 2.1.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Failure-derived taxonomy:** The authors collect model failures from 42 existing benchmarks, use a stronger frontier model to identify the earliest erroneous step, cluster open-vocabulary labels, and obtain 22 error types across five classes. The perception branch defines ten capabilities: localization, attributes, counting, relations, depth/3D, OCR, comparison, fine-grained recognition, context integration, and hallucination (Section 2.1.1; Tables 3–4).

2. **Atomic benchmark construction:** Source failures are decomposed into perception-only subquestions, supplemented with newly authored questions, filtered by an ensemble of four models, stratified by difficulty, and checked for image duplication. Automated verification and independent human review assess capability alignment, visual grounding, and answer clarity (Sections 2.1.2–2.2).

3. **Released evaluation asset:** PerceptionBench contains 3,000 verified samples—1,800 decomposed questions and 1,200 newly authored ones—drawn from an in-house pool exceeding 17,000 samples (Sections 2.1.2 and 2.3; Figure 5).

4. **Capability-level evaluation:** Sixteen proprietary and open-source frontier MLLMs are compared using unified prompts, official settings where available, and short-answer automatic grading (Sections 3.1–3.5).

These results establish performance differences under PerceptionBench’s perception-focused, short-answer protocol. They do not establish human-level difficulty, causal reasons for model errors, or direct improvements in downstream task performance. The proposed link between aggressive answer commitment and hallucination is explicitly an author hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal mechanism (Section 3.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The taxonomy reflects failures of the current model generation and source-benchmark coverage; it may need re-induction as models improve.
- Attribution depends on a stronger analyzer model, so attribution errors can propagate into capability labels.
- First-error labels may shift when future models fail at different stages on the same item.
- Perception-only scores do not directly predict end-to-end task performance and should complement task-oriented benchmarks (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- Difficulty calibration is model-relative: both selection and screening use a four-model frontier ensemble, potentially emphasizing weaknesses specific to that ensemble (Sections 2.1.2 and Limitations).
- Public representativeness evidence is limited to a per-capability correlation of \(r=0.84\); the supplied text does not report confidence intervals, per-model correlations, or rank-correlation statistics (Section 3.4).
- Repeated-run reliability is reported for only three models, so sample-level instability is not quantified across all sixteen systems (Table 2).
- Automatic-judge validation uses 300 predictions, but the sampling composition and error case are not described in the supplied text (Section 3.1).
- Claude-Fable-5 uses Claude-Opus-4.8 for 1.1% of answers refused by its safety filter, making that row a partially mixed-system result (Section 3.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How stable is the taxonomy across model generations?** The authors acknowledge that it is conditioned on current failures. A next experiment could independently induce taxonomies from older, current, and future model cohorts and measure category alignment and item-label migration.

2. **Does targeted improvement on an atomic capability transfer downstream?** PerceptionBench intentionally does not measure end-to-end performance. A controlled intervention could improve one capability, such as OCR or localization, then test matched downstream tasks while holding reasoning components fixed.

3. **What causes the large pass@4–pass4 gap?** Four-run results show intermittent success but do not distinguish visual uncertainty from decoding or answer-format variability. A next experiment could vary sampling temperature and reasoning budget while recording intermediate visual evidence.

4. **How robust are labels to the attribution model and first-error rule?** Residual analyzer errors are an acknowledged risk. Multiple independent analyzer models plus blinded human attribution could quantify agreement and identify systematically ambiguous categories.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported headline results because the supplied text includes the construction procedure, category definitions, benchmark statistics, full leaderboard, and repeated-run results.

Material missing information includes confidence intervals or significance tests for model comparisons; detailed human-annotation agreement statistics; the identities of the four construction ensemble models and stronger attribution/verifier models; per-source composition of the released set; the deduplication corpus coverage and resulting discard counts; full-pool per-model results; and reliability measurements for thirteen of the sixteen evaluated models.
