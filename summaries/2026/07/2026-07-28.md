# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-07-28

## Executive summary

- Persistent, inspectable state is a recurring agent-design theme: StateAct (2607.22798) reports better long-horizon desktop performance and lower cost through program-state access, while JarvisHub (2607.23588) applies editable artifact graphs to creative workflows. The latter is supported only by qualitative demonstrations.
- Two papers expose hidden failure modes in distillation. MAPD (2607.24280) turns proprietary search traces into structured training-only protocols, while PDM (2607.24731) prevents compensating errors between diffusion guidance branches when the teacher has privileged conditioning.
- Data composition is treated at complementary levels: Data Pyramid (2607.24744) surveys embodied-data sources and their trade-offs; DecoupleMix (2607.24516) reports gains from separately optimizing capability budgets and within-class VLM dataset allocations.
- Evidence quality receives unusually explicit treatment. ClinFusion (2607.24743) evaluates matched, missed, and hallucinated medical findings; quote-based document attribution (2607.24651) substantially improves evidence localization over coordinate generation, though its GRPO variant trades precision for recall.
- OmniVAE (2607.23855) and dRAE (2607.22148) both restructure autoencoder representations. The former improves audio-video synchronization at some reconstruction and motion-quality cost; the latter improves same-budget image reconstruction over Euclidean vector quantization.
- New assets broaden evaluation coverage: FilmBench (2607.24241) targets cinematic craft, DriveDNA (2607.23822) exposes driving-identity confounds, and IndicTalk (2607.23242) supplies synthetic Indic code-mixed dialogue. Each has important validation limits.
- Kimi K3 (2607.24653) reports broad open-weight capability at 2.78T total parameters, but comparisons mix harnesses and tool settings, several evaluations are internal, and the model does not consistently lead proprietary systems.
- Application-focused generation spans controllable head geometry (2607.23687) and open-world virtual try-on (2607.21694). Both report favorable results, but GNM’s numerical comparison cells are missing and Oxygen-TryOn’s strongest deployment-oriented evidence partly relies on internal data and model judges.
- Galahad (2607.23806) reports exact zero-token reuse for previously verified task families, but this is not evidence of frontier-level cold-start reasoning: implementation details are proprietary and no frontier model was run on the same battery.
- Two singleton studies round out the collection: a robotics progress-reward survey (2607.21655) cautions that control utility is not reward fidelity, while ARI (2607.21936) improves Hanja restoration with retrieval and chronological context but degrades under temporal shift.

## Main results worth noting

- **Long-horizon agents — 2607.22798:** On OSWorld 2.0’s 108 tasks with Claude Opus 4.8, StateAct improves binary success from 20.6% to 26.9% and mean partial score from 54.8% to 61.6% versus the reference harness. Estimated cost falls from about $72 to $7.8 per task. The state-first ablation causes the largest measured loss, 61.6%→51.3% partial score. These gains do not imply reliable verification: 68 of 76 non-perfect gated tasks were incorrectly accepted.

- **Agentic search distillation — 2607.24280:** MAPD reaches 39.4% and 44.4% macro-average exact-match success across seven QA benchmarks for Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B. Relative improvements over SDAR are 4.8% and 3.3%; reported relative gains are larger on four multi-hop tasks than three single-hop tasks. The setting uses a static Wikipedia corpus, at most four inference turns, and ground-truth-assisted offline protocol repair.

- **VLM mixture optimization — 2607.24516:** Against size-proportional stacking on the same 4B model, DecoupleMix improves the 16-benchmark average by 1.6 points at 2.5B tokens, 2.0 at 5B, and 1.4 at 10B. Reusing the 2.5B-token recipe without retuning gives 62.7 versus 61.5 on a 32B model. This supports transfer within the tested regime, not universal scale invariance.

- **Audio-video representation learning — 2607.23855:** On the 21-way VGS-Sp offset task, frozen A@1/A@5 improves from 6.4/31.5% for reconstruction-only training to 20.2/50.1% with OmniVAE. On Verse-Bench at CFG=5, DeSync improves 0.884→0.570 and text-audio similarity 0.201→0.246. UCF-101 reconstruction PSNR falls 36.93→36.27 and rFVD worsens 2.40→2.81, while Motion Score falls 0.556→0.456.

- **Branch-aware diffusion distillation — 2607.24731:** PDM’s strongest support is mechanistic: matching only a CFG-composed output leaves branch errors under-identified, and naive matching exhibits negative-branch error growth with privileged reference conditioning. On 1,018 held-out OCR prompts at inference CFG=1, teacher, naive, and PDM rewards are 75.24%, 73.87%, and 74.48%. Most video effect sizes are unavailable.

- **Document attribution — 2607.24651:** Across six open VLMs on 719 verified CiteVQA questions, quote attribution raises IoU-0.5 recall from at most 8.1% under coordinates to 25.9–46.9%, and reduces Attribution Hallucination from 82.1–97.0% to 38.6–65.4%. For Qwen3-VL-8B, GRPO raises recall 39.9→51.3 and SAA 22.4→33.8, but citations per response rise 2.1→4.6 and precision falls 37.6→26.5.

- **Medical multimodal evaluation — 2607.24743:** ClinFusion-8B report F1 is 37.8 versus Hulu-Med-7B’s 31.9 on CheXpert-Plus and 57.3 versus 46.5 on IU-XRAY under the RoI-grounded judge. In a reduced ablation, removing native 3D features lowers average 3D VQA 65.8→62.8 and report F1 15.7→11.7. ClinFusion does not lead every task: on AMOS Report, its 32B model scores 16.1 F1 versus Gemini-3-Flash’s 23.4.

- **Visual-token geometry — 2607.22148:** With 16,384 codes and 1,152-dimensional latents on ImageNet-1K validation, dRAE improves rFID 1.31→0.69, PSNR 22.33→24.03, and SSIM 0.67→0.70 over VQRAE. Scaling to 131,072 codes reaches rFID 0.42, PSNR 24.52, and SSIM 0.72. Most downstream understanding and generation table values are unavailable.

- **Cinematic evaluation — 2607.24241:** FilmBench’s evaluator reproduces expert model rankings with Spearman ρ=0.95 for T2V and 0.96 for R2V across 2,878 model–prompt pairs. Multi-shot T2V averages 81.3 versus 89.2 for single-shot prompts, with the largest macro drops in editing fluency (−29.8), focus (−23.0), and camera movement (−21.9). Agreement is weaker for editing and audio criteria.

- **Driving confounds — 2607.23822:** Supervised-contrastive telemetry embeddings reach AUROC .935±.005 for unseen-driver re-identification with five-minute enrollment, versus .707 for classical descriptors. Under 14,868 condition-matched pairs, AUROC remains .811±.006. Video-only AUROC drops .937→.675 after matching and shows strong route and vehicle leakage, supporting the authors’ caution against equating re-identification with style.

- **Virtual try-on — 2607.21694:** On the 985-sample human study, Oxygen-TryOn scores 3.5502 overall versus GPT-Image-2’s 3.5375 and leads subject consistency 3.7492→3.6365; GPT-Image-2 leads item consistency 3.2951→3.2250 and aesthetics 3.8240→3.8025. Multi-item results cover two to four references, and item fidelity does not lead all proprietary systems.

- **Historical restoration — 2607.21936:** For Qwen3-32B validation, BM25 retrieval plus deduplication raises named-entity-mask accuracy from 5.63% with metadata alone to 27.85%, and random-mask accuracy from 14.83% to 61.45%. On 100 genuinely damaged documents judged by three experts, ARI-32B reaches Accuracy@1 .383 versus .207 for BERT-Res and .273 for Gemini-2.5-Pro.

- **Verified reuse — 2607.23806:** The report claims 180/180 correct fresh parameter instances across nine stored task families and four frozen models, with zero generation tokens per reused answer. Its empty-memory control solves none, supporting attribution to stored executable methods. The evidence is confined to known families, and the undisclosed implementation and tasks prevent independent assessment.

- **Foundation-model scale — 2607.24653:** Kimi K3 reports 93.5% on GPQA Diamond, 43.5%/56.0% on HLE-Full without/with tools, 77.8% on ProgramBench, and 91.2% on BrowseComp with context compaction. These settings are not uniform across compared systems, and the report acknowledges gaps on several proprietary-model benchmarks.

- **Survey findings — 2607.21655 and 2607.24744:** The progress-reward survey’s principal result is a validity distinction: calibration, ordering, retrieval, and policy utility measure different properties. Data Pyramid’s principal result is a five-layer taxonomy—real robot, UMI-style, human video, simulation, and general data—not an experimentally validated ranking.

- **Qualitative or partial-result papers — 2607.23588 and 2607.23687:** JarvisHub establishes feasibility through three creative-workflow demonstrations but no comparative metric. GNM reportedly outperforms FLAME on 15,000 held-out scans and 2,000 synthetic images, but the numerical table cells are missing.

- **Corpus evidence — 2607.23242:** IndicTalk contains 1,328,604 conversations and 10,691,164 turns. Human overall-quality ratings range 3.0–4.4/5 across 180 sampled conversations. This does not establish downstream training utility, factual faithfulness, or resemblance to natural bilingual dialogue.

## Open research directions

1. **Independent verification of agent work (2607.22798, 2607.23588, 2607.23806).** Compare structural checking, heterogeneous-model re-derivation, task-specific executable tests, and human review on the same persisted artifacts. Measure false acceptance, false rejection, repair success, and cost.

2. **Controlled state-representation experiments (2607.22798, 2607.23588).** Hold the model and tools fixed while comparing chat history, flat files, program objects, and typed artifact graphs across matched long-horizon tasks. Report success, recovery, context loss, and edit effort.

3. **Distillation under systematically varied privileged information (2607.24280, 2607.24731).** Factorially vary which teacher branches receive protocols, references, or control signals, then track aggregate loss and component-level errors. This would test whether privileged conditioning is necessary or merely sufficient for instability.

4. **Embodied mixture optimization with real transfer outcomes (2607.24744, 2607.24516).** Apply fixed-budget mixture search to real-robot, UMI, human-video, simulation, and general data while holding architecture and compute constant. Evaluate real-robot success, perturbation recovery, and collection cost.

5. **Representation benefits under matched budgets (2607.23855, 2607.22148).** Train aligned/quantized and conventional autoencoders with identical data, steps, decoder capacity, and downstream generators. Measure reconstruction, task quality, utilization entropy, latency, and sensitivity to scale.

6. **Evidence recall without citation inflation (2607.24651, 2607.24743).** Add citation-cost or redundancy penalties and compare recall, precision, F1, hallucination, and expert judgment at fixed citation budgets. For medical reports, repeat with context independent of the reference report.

7. **External validation of judge-heavy benchmarks (2607.24241, 2607.21694, 2607.23242, 2607.24743).** Freeze generated outputs and rescore them with several model judges plus blinded domain experts. Report sample-level agreement, rank stability, subgroup effects, and sensitivity to rubric wording.

8. **Confound-resistant multimodal identity modeling (2607.23822).** Collect the same drivers across shared physical vehicles and routes, then compare ordinary, route-balanced, and adversarially deconfounded training. Jointly measure personalization utility and leakage.

9. **Generalization outside curated or internal distributions (2607.24653, 2607.24241, 2607.21694, 2607.21936).** Use unseen harnesses, non-cinematic video, external try-on sets, and period-matched versus period-shifted archives. Predefine evaluation protocols and avoid tuning on target distributions.

10. **Downstream utility for released representations and corpora (2607.23687, 2607.23242, 2607.21655).** Test whether GNM improves animation or neural rendering, whether IndicTalk improves held-out conversational models, and whether progress-reward fidelity predicts policy utility under a shared robotics protocol.

## Coverage and uncertainty

All 19 supplied current papers were analyzed and appear in the taxonomy, at-a-glance table, results discussion, and Sources.

Taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical connections were supplied and checked against the paper analyses. Twelve historical edges were retained after checking evidence at both endpoints. They describe technical similarity, complementarity, or an experiment opportunity—not publication order, priority, citation, or influence.

Input limitations materially affect confidence:

- Hugging Face Markdown collection failed for 2607.24653, 2607.23855, 2607.23806, and 2607.23242. Their analyses remained available from other collected paper text, but the failed source path is recorded.
- Current-paper sources were substantially truncated for 2607.24744, 2607.24653, and 2607.24743.
- Numerical tables were incomplete for 2607.24731, 2607.23687, and 2607.22148; only values present in the analyses were used.
- JarvisHub (2607.23588) supplies qualitative demonstrations rather than comparative empirical evidence.
- The Galahad report (2607.23806) withholds implementation and task details, so its exact-reuse results are not independently reproducible from the supplied material.
- IndicTalk (2607.23242) is synthetic and only sparsely human-evaluated; no downstream experiment is reported.
- Several retained historical analyses were truncated by the service. Edges were kept only where the visible portions gave concrete evidence at both endpoints.
- Low-confidence or especially qualified conclusions include GNM’s magnitude of improvement, PDM’s video effect sizes, Kimi K3’s cross-system ranking, and any generalization from internal or judge-based evaluations.
