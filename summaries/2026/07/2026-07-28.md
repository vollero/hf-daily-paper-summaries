# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-07-28

## Executive summary

- Agent research emphasized persistent, inspectable state: JarvisHub (`2607.23588`) uses editable artifact graphs, while StateAct (`2607.22798`) makes program state the primary action and verification interface.
- On-policy distillation appeared in both production-scale and controlled settings. MAPD (`2607.24280`) improved seven-benchmark search QA, while the planning gym (`2607.24720`) found that transfer depends strongly on student support and teacher compatibility.
- Diffusion work split between controllability and efficiency: PDM (`2607.24731`) addresses classifier-free-guidance branch errors, TILT (`2607.21606`) adds intrinsic compositional guidance, and Sol-Attn (`2607.24027`) sparsifies attention during inference.
- Representation papers redesigned generative latents: OmniVAE (`2607.23855`) aligns independently decodable audio/video spaces, while dRAE (`2607.22148`) uses hyper-spherical visual codes.
- Evaluation became more explicit and inspectable. PAJAMA (`2607.22561`) compiles judging rubrics into programs; FilmBench (`2607.24241`) validates cinematic criteria against professionals; evidence attribution (`2607.24651`) replaces coordinate citations with retrievable quotes.
- Embodied-AI contributions ranged from surveys of rewards and data (`2607.21655`, `2607.24744`) to a unified world/action diffusion policy (`2607.23909`) and a confound-aware driving dataset (`2607.23822`).
- Several strong numerical claims require narrow interpretation: Kimi K3 (`2607.24653`) used heterogeneous harnesses and effort settings; the zero-token memory report (`2607.23806`) covers previously verified task families; Bitcoin forecasting (`2607.23370`) contains claims contradicted by its own tables.
- Many papers lack repeated-run uncertainty, complete ablations, public artifacts, or independently reproducible evaluation. Six collection attempts also failed to obtain Hugging Face Markdown, although alternate paper evidence was available.

## Main results worth noting

- StateAct (`2607.22798`) improved Claude Opus 4.8 on 108 OSWorld 2.0 tasks from 20.6% to 26.9% binary success and from 54.8% to 61.6% mean partial score, while estimated cost fell from about $72 to $7.8 per task. Its finish gate nevertheless passed 68 of 76 non-perfect gated tasks incorrectly.

- MAPD (`2607.24280`) reached 39.4% and 44.4% macro-average exact-match success across seven Wikipedia-backed QA benchmarks for Qwen3-1.7B and 4B. Relative improvement over SDAR was 4.8% and 3.3%; five evaluation seeds came from one training run.

- The planning gym (`2607.24720`) showed that short-only training did not compose reliably: Pass@8 was 93.12% on short tasks, 0.83% on middle tasks, and 0% on long tasks. Adding 5% middle-horizon data raised middle Pass@8 to 51.88%.

- Sol-Attn (`2607.24027`) reported 2.02× end-to-end speedup on Wan2.1-14B at 85.12% sparsity, with aggregate VBench 76.13 versus 75.90 for dense FA3. On Ideogram 4, however, its 1.56× speedup accompanied a Qwen-Image-Bench decline from 59.24 to 55.31.

- OmniVAE (`2607.23855`) improved frozen temporal-offset probing from 6.4/31.5/16.2% to 20.2/50.1/37.2% on A@1/A@5/A@1-tolerance. This came with modest reconstruction regressions relative to its reconstruction-only variant.

- dRAE (`2607.22148`) improved ImageNet-1K reconstruction at 16,384 codes from VQRAE’s rFID 1.31, PSNR 22.33, and SSIM 0.67 to 0.69, 24.03, and 0.70. Scaling to 131,072 codes produced rFID 0.42, though broader downstream claims lack retained tables.

- ReDe (`2607.22098`) improved all 32 reported combinations of two 8B models, four tasks, and four detector families. The clearest example is Qwen3-8B TruthfulQA CCS AUROC, 68.63% before filtering and 87.32% after filtering.

- PAJAMA (`2607.22561`) averaged 78.11% pairwise accuracy at 439.41 samples/s on five filtered preference datasets, versus 79.70% at 9.30 samples/s for OLMo-2-13B. The tasks exclude ambiguous pairs, mathematics, and coding.

- FilmBench (`2607.24241`) obtained model-level Spearman correlations of 0.95 for T2V and 0.96 for R2V against 90 professional raters over 2,878 model–prompt pairs. Component-level correlation was lower for editing (0.60) and audio quality/coherence (0.68 each).

- Quote-based evidence attribution (`2607.24651`) raised IoU-0.5 recall from at most 8.1% under coordinate generation to 25.9–46.9% across six VLMs. GRPO increased Qwen3-VL-8B recall from 39.9 to 51.3 but reduced precision from 37.6 to 26.5.

- DriveDNA (`2607.23822`) reported condition-matched driver-verification AUROC of 0.811±0.006, versus 0.550 for classical descriptors. Video-only AUROC fell from 0.937 unmatched to 0.675 after matching, exposing substantial route and context leakage.

- The Bitcoin study (`2607.23370`) should not be summarized as a winning forecaster: RAML’s three-hour AUC was 0.5084 and six-hour AUC 0.4902. Static concatenation had the higher six-hour F1 and AUC, contradicting broader superiority claims in the paper.

## Open research directions

1. **Verify long-horizon completion semantically, not just structurally** (`2607.22798`, `2607.23588`). Compare independent source re-derivation, heterogeneous verifier models, and task-specific checks on workflows with planted value errors.

2. **Map OPD compatibility before costly training** (`2607.24280`, `2607.24720`, `2607.24653`). Measure student–teacher support overlap and teacher-pattern conflict before training, then test whether those diagnostics predict gains or forgetting across model scales.

3. **Separate latent alignment gains from reconstruction trade-offs** (`2607.23855`, `2607.22148`). Train matched tokenizers and generators under identical examples, steps, latent budgets, and decoders; jointly report reconstruction, utilization, synchronization, and generation.

4. **Make sparse inference policies portable** (`2607.24027`, `2607.23373`). Repeat operator and architecture selection across several GPU/NPU stacks, reporting absolute latency, energy, memory, and quality under identical workloads.

5. **Improve citation recall without citation flooding** (`2607.24651`, `2607.22098`). Add citation-cost and redundancy penalties, then compare recall, precision, attribution hallucination, and answer accuracy at fixed citation budgets.

6. **Validate automated judges at sample level** (`2607.22561`, `2607.24241`, `2607.24743`). Use blinded expert ratings on stratified individual outputs, not only model-level rankings, and test sensitivity to judge, rubric, and aggregation weights.

7. **Test embodied data and supervision mixtures causally** (`2607.21655`, `2607.24744`, `2607.23909`). Hold policy architecture and compute fixed while varying source layers, progress rewards, failure/recovery data, and future-visual supervision; evaluate held-out and real-robot success.

8. **Audit synthetic corpora for downstream value and source fidelity** (`2607.23242`, `2607.21936`, `2607.24516`). Compare matched models with and without each corpus or mixture, while measuring source entailment, duplication, subgroup quality, and contamination.

9. **Test specialized systems outside their curated regimes** (`2607.21694`, `2607.23518`, `2607.23687`, `2607.23806`). Use higher reference counts, laboratory binding assays, independent demographic scan sets, and structurally novel task families, respectively.

10. **Use evaluation designs that expose shortcuts** (`2607.23370`, `2607.23822`, `2607.22639`). Apply rolling-origin splits, shared-vehicle crossover data, and open catalogs with controlled rule coverage; report uncertainty and matched metrics rather than incomparable retrieval regimes.

## Coverage and uncertainty

All 31 supplied current papers were analyzed and appear in the taxonomy or singleton notes, the at-a-glance table, and the source list.

Taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical connections were available. They were treated as editorial aids and checked against the supplied endpoint evidence. Twelve historical edges were retained; all use cached analyses at the historical endpoint. No edge relies solely on a listing summary.

Collection failures affected Hugging Face Markdown retrieval for `2607.24653`, `2607.24027`, `2607.23855`, `2607.23806`, `2607.23242`, and `2607.22682`. Alternate analyses were supplied, so these papers remain covered. The analysis for `2607.24653` was itself truncated; `2607.24744` and `2607.24743` also had substantial source truncation. Numerous other analyses lacked numerical table bodies or figure values, especially `2607.24731`, `2607.22148`, `2607.23687`, `2607.23373`, and `2607.21606`.

Low-confidence conclusions include comparative claims without retained table values; judge-based results without independent human validation; internal or proprietary benchmarks; single-run or single-seed studies; and claims based on qualitative demonstrations. The strongest explicit inconsistency is in `2607.23370`, whose tables do not support its broader best-F1 or best-AUC claims.

No cross-paper numerical ranking was attempted across incompatible tasks, metrics, hardware, models, or evaluation protocols.
