# IDEAgent: Agentic Quality-Diversity Search for Research Idea Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22375) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22375)


## One-sentence takeaway

IDEAgent combines lineage-aware multi-agent repair and refinement with memory-based diversification, producing more mutually distinct ideas that pass quality thresholds than the tested baselines across 32 computer-science topics, according to LLM judges.

## Problem and motivation

LLM ideation systems typically optimize ideas independently for quality or diversity, leading either to clusters of near-duplicates or to diverse but trivial, unclear, or unsound proposals. The authors argue that useful scientific ideation instead requires a portfolio of individually strong and mutually distinct ideas under a fixed generation budget (Sections 1 and 3.1). Existing quality and diversity metrics evaluated separately do not measure this conjunction.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Quality–diversity formulation:** Scientific ideation is framed as maximizing the number of high-quality, mutually distinct proposals under a fixed seed budget (Section 3.1).

2. **IDEAgent framework:** An Ideator, Stenographer, Critic, quality evaluator, five-member soundness panel, and diversity judge manage ideas as lineages. Compact signatures and active, historical, repair, and rejected-pattern memories guide diversification; targeted repair and refinement improve near-misses and accepted ideas (Sections 3.2–3.3; Figure 1).

3. **Yield metric:** After filtering ideas by non-obviousness, soundness, and clarity thresholds, `Yield` finds the maximum-cardinality subset satisfying pairwise diversity constraints (Section 5; Appendix B, Algorithm 2).

4. **Evaluation assets:** The authors construct 32 topics across eight computer-science domains and report that the framework and experimental code are open-sourced (Sections 4 and 8; Appendix A, Table 3).

These results establish stronger LLM-judged quality–diversity performance than the included generation baselines under the paper’s topic construction, thresholds, models, and budgets. They do not establish that the generated ideas are correct, novel relative to the full literature, publishable, or effective after implementation. The retrieved text also omits the numeric contents of Tables 1 and 12–14, preventing independent checking of several summaries and significance annotations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** All central assessments depend on LLM judges. Soundness has particularly low absolute-score agreement (\(\kappa=0.26\)), despite using two external judges and five internal soundness samples (Sections 7 and 9).

- **Author-stated:** No ideas are implemented or experimentally validated. Feasibility, exact correctness, practical effectiveness, publishability, and novelty against comprehensive prior art remain unverified (Section 9).

- **Author-stated:** Proprietary models are used throughout the main experiment, limiting accessibility and leaving transfer to open models untested. Preliminary open-model trials reportedly exposed substantial soundness weaknesses (Sections 6.1 and 9).

- **Author-stated:** Compute and cost constrain each lineage to one repair and at most two refinements; the weighting, thresholds, and other hyperparameters were not rigorously optimized (Sections 3.3 and 9).

- **Analyst inference:** Because IDEAgent receives more generator calls per seed than most primary baselines, causal attribution to lineage management versus additional computation is incomplete. Appendix D’s scaled-baseline analysis helps, but Sequential Memory scaling covers only four topics.

- **Analyst inference:** The component evidence is not a full controlled ablation: repair and refinement outcomes are reported, but the supplied text does not show matched variants independently removing archives, lineage tracking, rejected-pattern memory, the soundness panel, or each revision stage.

- **Analyst inference:** The same broad class of proprietary LLM systems generates and evaluates the ideas, so correlated model preferences may remain even when generator and judge families differ.

## Open research questions

1. **Do higher Yield scores predict real scientific value?** The paper performs no implementation or expert validation. A next experiment could have domain experts blindly select ideas across methods, implement a preregistered subset, and compare technical success with predicted Yield.

2. **Which IDEAgent components cause the gains?** Archives, signatures, repair, refinement, and multi-judge evaluation are bundled. A factorial ablation with matched generator-call and token budgets could isolate each component’s contribution.

3. **How robust is Yield to judges and thresholds?** Judge agreement is weak for soundness, and Yield uses hard gates. A next study could compare multiple LLM and human juries while reporting threshold-sensitivity and confidence intervals for each idea’s eligibility.

4. **Can open models reproduce the results economically?** Main experiments depend on proprietary models, while preliminary open-model generations had soundness problems. A controlled model-scale study could hold prompts, budgets, and judges fixed while measuring Yield, repair frequency, latency, and cost.
