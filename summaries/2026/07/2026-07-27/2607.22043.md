# Scaling Native Multimodal Pre-Training From Scratch

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22043) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22043)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors find that native vision-language pre-training has predictable but modality-dependent compute-optimal laws: language allocation is largely mixture-invariant, multimodal allocation is mixture-sensitive, and sufficiently large models gain spatial transfer and multimodal few-shot learning.

## Problem and motivation

Native multimodal models train vision and language jointly from scratch, avoiding the representational and optimization asymmetry of attaching a separately trained vision encoder to an LLM. However, it was unclear how a fixed compute budget should be divided among parameters, training tokens, and the text–multimodal mixture—or whether language and multimodal objectives favor compatible allocations. The paper addresses this gap by estimating separate and joint compute-optimal scaling laws and examining their downstream implications (Sections 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Objective-specific scaling analysis:** The authors separate language and multimodal losses and estimate optimal model size and token count under \(C=6ND\). They cross-check IsoFLOP profiles with training-curve envelopes (Sections 2.1–2.2).
- **Composition-aware allocation laws:** They find language allocation approximately invariant to multimodal ratio \(r\), while multimodal allocation shifts toward more data as \(r\) increases (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figures 3 and 6).
- **Joint efficiency frontier:** A mixture-dependent Pareto frontier maps total compute and \(r\) to proposed optimal parameter, text-token, and multimodal-token configurations (Section 3.3; Figure 7).
- **Downstream findings:** Across models from 71M to 3B activated parameters, multimodal pre-training preserves aggregate text performance, improves text-only spatial reasoning at larger scales, and produces scale- and data-dependent multimodal in-context learning (Sections 4.2–4.3).

The paper explicitly frames its novelty as scaling analysis rather than a new architecture (Section 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Language allocation is largely invariant to mixture composition. | IsoFLOP and training-envelope slopes vary minimally and show no shared monotonic trend with \(r\). Numerical exponents are not supplied in the retrieved text. | Section 3.1; Figure 3 |
| Multimodal allocation becomes more data-heavy as \(r\) rises. | Both estimators show a declining optimal model-size exponent from \(r=0.1\) to \(0.3\). | Section 3.2; Figure 6 |
| Joint optimal parameter scaling changes with mixture. | Under total compute, \(N_{\mathrm{opt}}\propto C_{\mathrm{total}}^{0.69}\) at \(r=0.1\), versus \(C_{\mathrm{total}}^{0.66}\) at \(r=0.3\); the latter assigns token scaling \(D_{\mathrm{opt}}\propto C_{\mathrm{total}}^{0.34}\). | Section 3.3; Figure 7 |
| Aggregate language performance is preserved. | With 250B text tokens, 0–75B multimodal tokens, and models from 71M to 3B, average accuracy across 16 text benchmarks differs by under one percentage point at every scale. | Multimodal training improves text-only spatial reasoning most clearly at larger scales. | On SpatialEval’s MazeNav and SpatialMap subsets, \(r=0.3\) consistently exceeds \(r=0\), with a widening gap toward 3B; the retrieved text does not provide subset-level numerical gains. | Section 4.2; Figure 9 |
| Multimodal few-shot gains emerge with scale. | Across 21 multimodal benchmarks, the author-reported few-shot gain rises from near zero at A71M to +1.80 points at A874M and +2.43 points at A3B; models use 250B text and 75B multimodal tokens. | Section 4.3; Figure 10 |
| Few-shot benefits are task-dependent. | Gains are largest on spatial reasoning, modest on aggregate and vision-knowledge tasks, and absent or negative on OCR/recognition tasks. | Section 4.3; Figure 12 |

The evidence supports empirical regularities within the studied architecture, data family, mixture range, and scale. Agreement between two scaling estimators strengthens the allocation findings, although the joint Pareto analysis uses only IsoFLOP estimates. It does not establish universality across architectures, larger models, other modalities, or other dataset compositions, nor does it validate extrapolated loss laws with held-out large-scale runs.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Experiments stop at 3B activated parameters, use one image-text data family, and rely on loss-based scaling proxies (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** Smoothed training loss substitutes for test loss because the authors report no solid multimodal validation metric (Section 2.1).
- **Author-stated:** Images are capped at 1,536 tokens, or 512 in few-shot evaluations, to fit a 4K-token context, limiting visual resolution and example capacity (Section 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that multimodal training causes spatial transfer is supported by text-only SpatialEval improvements, but no intervention isolates visual spatial structure from other differences in the added multimodal corpus.
- **Analyst inference:** The Pareto frontier’s large-compute recommendations remain extrapolations; the supplied text does not report prospective validation at those expanded budgets.
- **Analyst inference:** No comparison against late-fusion models is reported in the supplied experimental results, so the evidence does not show that native pre-training is more compute-efficient than that alternative.
- **Analyst inference:** Dataset construction, filtering, duplication controls, and public availability are insufficiently specified in the supplied text, limiting reproducibility.
- **Analyst inference:** Variance across seeds or confidence intervals is not reported, making it difficult to assess small differences such as the under-one-point language-performance variation.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the allocation exponents persist beyond 3B active parameters?** The paper extrapolates from models no larger than 3B. A next experiment could train several larger models around predicted IsoFLOP minima and compare realized with forecast loss.

2. **How dependent are the laws on multimodal corpus composition?** Only one image-text data family is studied. Repeating the scaling sweep with separately controlled paired, interleaved, OCR-heavy, spatial, video, or audio mixtures would test whether \(a(r)\) is transferable.

3. **What produces the text-only spatial gain?** The current evidence does not isolate visual grounding from corpus-content or extra-compute effects. A controlled experiment could match total compute and textual content while varying whether spatial information is supplied visually, verbally, or not at all.

4. **Can validation performance replace training loss when choosing allocations?** The frontier uses smoothed training loss as a proxy. A next experiment could fit laws using held-out modality-specific losses and downstream utility, then test whether they recommend the same parameter–token mixtures.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental scope and headline findings because the retrieved text includes the methods, model settings, benchmark protocol, figures, and extensive result tables.

Material missing information includes exact fitted loss-law parameters and most allocation exponents, uncertainty estimates and seed counts, detailed corpus construction and filtering, hardware or wall-clock costs, prospective validation of extrapolations, subset-level SpatialEval scores underlying Figure 9, and any confirmed release of models, data, or code.
