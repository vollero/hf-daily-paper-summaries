# DecoupleMix: Decoupled Ratio Search and Convex Allocation for Scalable VLM Data Recipes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24516) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24516)


## One-sentence takeaway

DecoupleMix improves vision–language pretraining mixtures by separately searching capability-level budgets and optimizing dataset-level allocations, outperforming heuristic stacking across tested token scales and transferring its proxy recipe to a 32B model without retuning.

## Problem and motivation

Open VLM pretraining recipes commonly stack quality-filtered datasets and choose cross-domain sampling ratios heuristically. The authors argue that this neglects data difficulty, diversity, and redundancy while making the effect of admitting a new dataset hard to attribute.

They formulate recipe construction as two more tractable problems: allocating a finite token budget across capability classes and distributing each class’s budget among its constituent datasets (Introduction; §2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Automated dataset assessment:** An LLM judge evaluates 150 samples per dataset to assign capability labels and aggregate quality and difficulty scores (§2.1; Appendix B).

- **Decoupled optimization:** A coordinate-style, single-variable search selects inter-class ratios. Within each class, a constrained convex program allocates tokens using quality, difficulty, and an entropy diversity term, subject to dataset availability and lower bounds (§2.2, Eqs. 1–3).

- **Attributable admission protocol:** New datasets enter under fixed total budget, inter-class ratios, and allocation rules, isolating admission as a single intervention that includes budget-preserving within-class reallocation (§2.3, Eqs. 4–5).

- **Proxy-to-scale reuse:** Ratios searched at 2.5B training tokens are reused at 5B and 10B tokens and on a 32B-parameter model without retuning (§4.3).

The controlled stacking comparisons support better mixture performance within the tested architecture, budgets, and evaluation protocol. The scale-transfer experiments show reuse across the tested 2.5B–10B token range and at 32B parameters, not universal scale invariance. Table 1 establishes end-to-end competitiveness but not a causal advantage from DecoupleMix because the external systems differ in training data and other design choices, as the authors acknowledge.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Token-scale experiments cover 2.5B–10B tokens; behavior at frontier-scale budgets is not reported (§6).

- **Author-stated:** Coordinate search and the convex objective are tractable approximations and may be inferior to joint search or richer objectives (§6).

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is limited to vision–language models; extension to broader omni-modal systems is untested (§6).

- **Author-stated:** Assessment depends on a frozen judge, while dataset-level atomic scoring cannot exploit within-dataset variation (§6).

- **Analyst inference:** The human-alignment evidence is preliminary because it contains only 10 instances, despite spanning the capability categories (Appendix B.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The dataset-admission claim is supported by one OCR source on the proxy model; admission effects for other classes, sources, and larger models are not reported (§4.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The text does not give the searched candidate ratios, final mixture weights, scoring coefficients, bounds, candidate-dataset inventory, or number of ratio-search runs. Appendix C supplies training hyperparameters and aggregate GPU cost, but these omissions limit recipe reproduction.

- **Analyst inference:** Table 6’s prose reports domain aggregates that are not displayed in the supplied table and includes a Math & Logic value of 27.0 that cannot be directly reconciled with the listed per-benchmark entries; the table’s overall averages remain explicit.

## Open research questions

1. **Does proxy-recipe transfer persist at much larger token budgets?** The study stops at 10B tokens for its controlled data-scale comparison. A next experiment could hold architecture and data pool fixed while applying the 2.5B recipe unchanged at several substantially larger budgets.

2. **Would joint optimization improve on the decoupled search?** The authors identify coordinate search and the convex allocator as potentially suboptimal. A matched-compute experiment could compare DecoupleMix with joint Bayesian, regression-based, or differentiable mixture optimization.

3. **How robust is attributable admission across dataset types?** Only one OCR dataset is tested. A preregistered series of admissions across general, reasoning, document, and video classes could compare off-target variation and downstream gains under the same fixed-budget protocol.

4. **Does sample-level assessment justify its extra cost?** Dataset-level atomic scoring sacrifices within-source selectivity. A controlled experiment could compare dataset-level, clustered-subset, and sample-level allocation at matched assessment and training budgets.
