# Evidence Attribution in Visual Document Understanding without Coordinates or Region Labels

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24651) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24651)


## One-sentence takeaway

Replacing coordinate generation with verbatim evidence quotes resolved to document regions substantially improves attribution across six open VLMs, while region-label-free GRPO further improves recall but does so partly by producing more citations.

## Problem and motivation

Visual document QA requires answers to be traceable to supporting page regions, especially in high-stakes domains. Existing systems usually ask VLMs to generate bounding-box coordinates, but models often answer correctly while citing the wrong region—“Attribution Hallucination.”

The authors investigate whether this reflects a fundamental grounding deficit or an interface mismatch: models may recognize and describe evidence in language without reliably emitting precise coordinate tokens. They also address the cost of existing training approaches, which depend on page- or region-level evidence annotations that are expensive for long documents (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Controlled interface comparison.** The same six open VLMs, document pages, questions, and scoring protocol are evaluated with either direct coordinate generation or language-based evidence attribution (Sections 1 and 4).

2. **Quote-and-retrieve attribution.** A model emits an answer plus verbatim supporting quotes. A layout parser proposes paragraph, table, figure, and caption blocks; a multimodal encoder matches quotes to rendered block crops; and Hungarian assignment converts matches into page-region citations (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figure 2).

3. **Region-label-free reinforcement learning.** GRPO trains the quoting policy using a vision-language judge that scores answer correctness, evidence relevance, and evidence coverage from the gold answer and retrieved region crops. The reward uses no region labels, although evidence-page annotations are used to construct training windows (Section 3.3; Equation 2; Appendix A).

4. **Diagnostic controls.** Coordinate snapping, question-only and question-plus-answer retrieval, lexical/BM25 resolvers, and an independent parser test separate the effects of output language, retrieval, block geometry, and parser choice (Section 5.3; Appendices E–F).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Language attribution strongly outperforms coordinates. | Across six open VLMs on the verified CiteVQA set, IoU-0.5 recall rises from at most **8.1%** to **25.9–46.9%**, precision from at most **8.6%** to **22.9–52.7%**, and AH falls from **82.1–97.0%** to **38.6–65.4%**. | Section 5.1; Table 1 |
| Answer quality is generally stable. | Five of six backbones differ by no more than **2.3 answer-score points**; Qwen3-VL-8B drops **6.2 points** under the language interface. | Section 5.1; Table 1 |
| Coordinate failure becomes severe at element-level overlap. | For Qwen3-VL-8B, coordinate recall falls from **30.7% page-level** to **5.8% at IoU 0.1** and **0.0% at IoU 0.7**; language recall remains **42.3% at IoU 0.1** and **39.2% at IoU 0.7**. | Section 5.2; Figure 3 |
| The parser imposes a high but non-perfect ceiling. | The main parser’s macro recall ceiling is **88.2% at IoU 0.5**; an independent parser reaches **87.8%**, while producing language-interface recall 3–6 points below the main parser. | Section 5.2; Appendix E |
| Retrieval machinery alone does not explain the full gain. | Question-only retrieval remains below quote retrieval for every backbone; lexical and BM25 quote resolution lose **5–14 recall points** relative to multimodal crop retrieval. | Section 5.3; Table 2; Appendix F |
| Region-label-free GRPO improves attributed accuracy. | For Qwen3-VL-8B, recall rises **39.9→51.3**, SAA **22.4→33.8**, same-page recall **63.2→73.8**, and AH falls **41.2→28.4**. | Section 5.4; Table 1 |
| GRPO’s recall gain trades off against citation selectivity. | Citations per response rise **2.1→4.6**, precision falls **37.6→26.5**, and box F1 falls **32.2→28.8**. | Section 5.4; Appendix H |
| Independent judging supports—but does not prove—the value of extra citations. | Under SAA requiring answer and judged relevance without the recall disjunct, performance rises **19.1→27.4**; McNemar **p=6.0×10⁻⁷**. | Section 6.3; Appendix D |
| Training primarily improves evidence coverage. | Of the **11.9-point** gain in recovered evidence elements, **10.4 points** come from reducing cases where the relevant evidence was not retrieved from any quote. | Appendix H; Figure 7 |

These results establish a controlled association between the evidence interface and substantially better attribution for the tested open VLMs and verified bilingual subset. Ablations support the authors’ claim that generated language contains useful location information and that multimodal resolution adds value.

They do not establish that coordinate attribution is inherently impossible, that the language pipeline generalizes to multi-document or heavily scanned collections, or that GRPO improves precision. The method also delegates final localization to a parser and retriever, so the experiments do not show that the VLM itself learns coordinate-level localization.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The study covers single-document attribution only; multi-document retrieval is untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Localization is not end-to-end—the VLM writes quotes, while a parser and retriever produce regions. Performance is bounded by parser coverage (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Purely graphical, uncaptioned evidence cannot be quoted through the proposed interface (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Every quote is assigned a block, so hallucinated quotes can receive plausible-looking citations unless similarity-based abstention is added (Limitations; Appendix I).
- **Author-stated:** Only 719 of 987 CiteVQA questions remain after PDF and annotation verification; performance on heavily scanned collections is untested (Section 4; Appendix B; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Results use single greedy-decoding runs; GRPO has one seed and one 8B backbone (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Both training and evaluation depend on VLM judges and may inherit their biases, although distinct models are used for training and evaluation (Section 4; Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Calling the training “region-label-free” is accurate for the reward, but the training-data construction uses evidence-page annotations to select windows. Thus the full data pipeline is not wholly free of localization metadata (Appendix A, “Training data construction”).
- **Analyst inference:** The GRPO improvement cannot be summarized as uniformly better geometric attribution because recall and SAA improve while citation precision and box F1 decline (Section 5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that the main deficit is the coordinate interface is well supported for the evaluated open models, but the supplied closed-source reference point shows that coordinate emission can work with different training and scale; it is not a universal interface limitation (Section 6.1; Appendix Table 5).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the pipeline abstain reliably on unsupported quotes?** Assignment currently forces every quote onto a block. A next experiment could calibrate similarity thresholds on held-out documents and report precision–coverage and AH trade-offs (Limitations; Appendix I).

2. **Can training raise recall without increasing citation volume?** GRPO improves coverage but reduces precision and F1. A controlled experiment could add a citation-cost or redundancy penalty and compare recall, precision, F1, SAA, and judged relevance at matched citation budgets (Section 5.4; Appendix H).

3. **How well does the method handle scanned and uncaptioned graphical documents?** These cases are not adequately tested and are structurally difficult for quote-based attribution. A next experiment could stratify evaluation by OCR quality, text-layer availability, and caption presence (Limitations; Appendix B).

4. **Can language supervision produce an end-to-end region-localizing model?** The present VLM does not directly output regions. Retrieved blocks could be used as pseudo-labels to train direct block selection or coordinate prediction, followed by comparison against the original pipeline under identical evaluation (Limitations).
