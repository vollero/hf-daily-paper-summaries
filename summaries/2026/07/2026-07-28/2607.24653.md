# Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24653) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653)


## One-sentence takeaway
Kimi K3 is an open-weight, native-multimodal 2.8T-parameter sparse model that combines hybrid long-context attention, depth-wise attention residuals, stabilized MoE routing, multi-domain agentic reinforcement learning, and specialized infrastructure to approach—but not consistently surpass—the strongest evaluated proprietary systems.

## Problem and motivation
The authors address a perceived imbalance in open-model development: test-time reasoning and agentic methods have advanced, while open pre-trained foundations have remained near the 1T-parameter regime, risking a widening gap with proprietary systems. Their goal is to scale both axes simultaneously—pre-training to 3T-class capacity and post-training to million-token, long-horizon reasoning and tool use—while keeping training and serving computationally feasible (§1, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Large native-multimodal foundation:** A 2.78T-parameter MoE with 104.2B activated parameters, a one-million-token context window, and a jointly trained vision pathway (Table 1; §§2–3).

- **Architecture:** A 3:1 hybrid of Kimi Delta Attention and global Gated MLA; Attention Residuals for selective access across depth; and Stable LatentMoE with 896 routed experts, 16 active per token, SiTU-GLU, normalization, and Quantile Balancing (§§2.1–2.4; Fig. 2).

- **Multi-effort agentic post-training:** SFT followed by RL for general tasks, general agents, and coding agents at low, high, and max reasoning effort, producing nine specialist teachers consolidated through Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (§4.1).

- **Long-horizon environments:** Configurable agent harnesses and environments for search, professional workflows, visual reasoning, kernel optimization, personal assistance, autonomous execution, and web development (§4.2).

- **Systems co-design:** KDA kernels and context parallelism, perfectly balanced MoonEP training, memory-saving techniques, persistent KV and sandbox state for long RL trajectories, and hybrid-cache-aware serving (§5).

- **Release:** The authors state that they release the full Kimi K3 model weights (§1, p. 2).

The results support broad capability across coding, agents, reasoning, and vision under the authors’ evaluation configurations. They do not establish uniform superiority: Kimi K3 trails Claude Fable 5 or GPT-5.6 Sol on several major benchmarks, including HLE-Full, DeepSWE, GDPval-AA v2, and multiple vision tasks (Table 2). They also do not isolate how much each architectural, data, RL, or infrastructure component contributes to final benchmark performance.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated performance gap:** The authors acknowledge that Kimi K3 remains behind the strongest proprietary systems overall and identify research-level reasoning as a continuing weakness (§§1, 6.1.4, 8).

- **Author-stated cybersecurity gap:** Kernel exploitation and completion of hardened exploit chains remain weak; the authors characterize their cyber evaluation as a lower bound conditioned on the current model and evaluation coverage (§6.2.2).

- **Comparison heterogeneity:** Models sometimes use different agent harnesses, Fable 5 results may include fallbacks, GPT-5.6 Sol results may include cyberguards, and GPT-5.5 uses xhigh rather than max effort (§§6.1.2–6.1.3). These conditions weaken strict model-only attribution.

- **Internal-evaluation risk:** Several highlighted agent and web-development results use frequently refreshed in-house tasks or internal judges, limiting independent reproducibility from the supplied text (§6.2.1).

- **Analyst inference — bundled improvements:** The 2.5× scaling claim combines architectural, data, and training changes; the supplied material lacks component-wise loss or downstream ablations sufficient to assign causal credit (§3.2, Fig. 7).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete reproducibility:** Corpus sizes, training-token totals, compute budgets, many RL hyperparameters, and full task-generation details are not provided in the available text, preventing exact reconstruction.

- **Source truncation:** The collector omitted 8,642 characters from the middle of the report. This analysis therefore cannot claim complete coverage, particularly of material between the cybersecurity discussion and later case studies.

## Open research questions

1. **Which components drive the reported 2.5× scaling gain?** The comparison bundles KDA, AttnRes, Stable LatentMoE, data, and optimization changes. A plausible next experiment is a controlled factorial ablation at matched FLOPs and tokens, reporting validation loss and downstream scores.

2. **Does the one-million-token window produce reliable long-range retrieval and reasoning throughout the full context?** The report describes the training curriculum and agentic use, but the supplied evaluation results do not provide a position-stratified long-context analysis. A next experiment could vary evidence position and dependency distance from 8K to 1M tokens while measuring accuracy and compute.

3. **How robust is agent performance across unseen harnesses?** Training deliberately varies harness components, yet evaluation still uses model-specific harness assignments on several tasks. A cross-product evaluation of every model with every compatible harness would separate model capability from scaffold effects.

4. **Can research-level reasoning improve without sacrificing agent efficiency?** Kimi K3 is strong on many agentic tasks but trails leading systems on HLE-Full and CritPt. A next experiment could extend reasoning-domain RL with verifiable research tasks and evaluate accuracy, token use, and tool calls across all effort levels.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied pages give detailed architecture, training, systems, and evaluation descriptions, but the source is truncated. Materially unavailable or incomplete information includes the omitted middle section, complete training-data composition and quantities, total pre-training compute and token count, many hyperparameters, comprehensive component ablations, contamination controls, model and code licensing details, and full independent reproducibility evidence.
