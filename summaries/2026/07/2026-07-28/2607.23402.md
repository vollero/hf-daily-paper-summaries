# Characterizing Warp Divergence from Pascal to Blackwell

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23402) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23402)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across the tested Pascal, Ampere, Hopper, and Blackwell GPUs, warp-divergence cost remains predictable linear path serialization, although compiler-emitted reconvergence mechanisms change substantially across generations.

## Problem and motivation

Warp divergence reduces GPU SIMD utilization because paths taken by different lanes must execute separately. Although Volta introduced Independent Thread Scheduling (ITS), its implementation is proprietary and informally documented, leaving unclear whether divergence behavior measured on one post-Volta architecture transfers to later generations. The authors identify a lack of cross-generational divergence studies—particularly for Blackwell—and distinguish two questions: whether programmer-visible costs changed and whether the underlying compiler/ISA machinery evolved (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions

- **Dynamic characterization:** Cycle-accurate microbenchmarks vary the number of divergent paths and occupancy across Pascal, Ampere, Hopper, and two Blackwell variants. Measurements use the median of 201 launches and hardware counters where available (Sections III–IV).
- **Cross-checking genuine divergence:** Disassembly verifies real reconvergence barriers, while active-thread counters distinguish divergent execution from compiler predication (Section III).
- **Static reconvergence study:** The authors compile a 38-kernel control-flow corpus for architectures from `sm_80` through `sm_120`, separately analyzing Pascal `sm_61`, reconstruct control-flow graphs, compute immediate post-dominators, and classify compiler-selected reconvergence points (Section V).
- **Blackwell ISA reverse engineering:** Binary inspection and controlled bit flips characterize its two barrier classes, uniform branches, and partial-mask synchronization (Sections V-C–V-D).
- **Principal contribution:** The study separates an apparently stable divergence cost model from an evolving reconvergence ISA and compiler strategy (Sections IV–VI).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Divergence cost scales linearly with path count. | Balanced divergent regions from \(k=1\) to \(32\); per-path slopes are 54.1k cycles on Ampere, 58.1k on Hopper, 46.1k on both Blackwell variants, and 70.1k on Pascal. A 32-way split costs 31.7–31.9× one path. | Section IV-A; Figure 1 |
| Warp execution efficiency follows approximately \(32/k\). | Post-ITS active-thread counts are 31.9, 16.2, 8.3, 4.25, 2.23, and 1.21 for \(k=1,2,4,8,16,32\); Pascal counters were unavailable. | Section IV-B; Figure 2 |
| Predication removes the measured two-path serialization. | Replacing a two-way branch with branch-free predication reduces cost from 2.00× to 1× on post-ITS GPUs, with ratios within 0.5% of 2.00; Pascal’s branch cost is 1.94×. | Section IV-C |
| Occupancy does not hide a 32-way divergence penalty. | Across light through oversubscribed occupancy, the penalty remains 28–31×; Pascal spans 29.9–30.9×. | Section IV-D; Figure 3 |
| Deferred reconvergence becomes uncommon on later architectures. | Later-than-IPDom branches fall from 29 on Ampere to 7 on Hopper and 2 on Blackwell; exact-IPDom rates are 72.7%, 90.8%, and 83.2%, respectively. | Sections V-B–V-C; Figure 4; Table II |
| Reconvergence machinery changes by generation. | Pascal uses SSY/SYNC and no BSSY/BSYNC/BREAK; Ampere and Hopper use bare barrier registers; Blackwell adds `.RECONVERGENT` and `.RELIABLE`. | Sections V-A–V-C |
| Blackwell adds further static control-flow features. | The corpus contains 23 `BRA.U` occurrences on Blackwell versus 0 on Ampere/Hopper; partial-mask `WARPSYNC` appears only on Blackwell; maximum observed barrier depth is 3 versus 4. | Section V-D; Table II |
| Barrier-class mutations show no observed runtime effect in tested cases. | Flipping the 2-bit field leaves output, completion, tested forward progress, partner exchange, and cycle counts unchanged, including mismatched barrier classes; tests are on single-warp `sm_110`. | Section V-C; limitations in Section VI |
| Tested post-ITS paths make forward progress but execute structurally. | Lock probes terminate on all tested generations; consumers see a producer result after at most two spin iterations even when the producer performs \(10^7\) iterations. | Section IV-E |

Together, these results support the authors’ claim that isolated divergence has a stable, issue-count-driven cost model across the tested hardware while static reconvergence representation evolves. They do not establish identical behavior for all GPU workloads, other vendors, untested architectures, memory-divergent code, or every possible synchronization pattern. The `.RELIABLE` experiments show no effect within their tested executions, not that the field can never affect runtime behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The study does not execute the unbounded intra-warp peer-wait needed to reproduce classical stack deadlock, so the Pascal comparison relies on prior analysis (Section VI).
- **Author-stated:** Barrier-field mutation is limited to single-warp experiments on `sm_110`; multi-warp scheduling corners and profiler/debugger use of the field remain untested (Section VI).
- **Author-stated:** Microbenchmarks isolate path serialization, whereas real applications may combine control-flow and memory divergence (Section VI).
- **Analyst inference:** Pascal lacks the hardware-counter efficiency result because access was unavailable, so its lost-utilization interpretation rests on timing and occupancy evidence rather than the same direct counter used post-ITS (Section IV-B).
- **Analyst inference:** The static conclusions depend on one 38-kernel corpus and compiler-generated code. The text provides corpus categories but not enough detail to assess representativeness or compiler-version sensitivity fully (Sections III and V).
- **Analyst inference:** No artifact-release statement is supplied, limiting assessment of independent reproducibility.

## Open research questions

1. **Does Blackwell’s barrier-class field matter under multi-warp contention or tooling?** Single-warp mutations found no effect, but the authors explicitly leave multi-warp and profiler/debugger behavior untested. A next experiment could mutate the field in multi-warp synchronization workloads while collecting scheduler and tool-visible traces.

2. **Does the linear cost law hold when control-flow and memory divergence interact?** The current kernels intentionally isolate path serialization. A factorial benchmark could vary path count, memory coalescing, cache behavior, and occupancy independently.

3. **How robust are reconvergence-placement trends across compilers and applications?** The static analysis uses a 38-kernel corpus, but compiler-version sensitivity is not reported. Compiling larger application-derived corpora under multiple toolchain versions would test whether the Ampere-to-Blackwell trend persists.

4. **What scheduling conditions produce fine-grained interleaving of divergent paths?** The producer/consumer probe suggests structural, run-to-completion ordering absent contended synchronization. Experiments varying synchronization primitives, path lengths, and dependency structures could map when ITS changes that ordering.
