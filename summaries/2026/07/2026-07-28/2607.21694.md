# Oxygen-TryOn: Fashion-Native Foundation Model for Any-item Virtual Try-On

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21694) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21694)


## One-sentence takeaway

Oxygen-TryOn reframes mask-free virtual try-on as multi-reference conditional generation and reports leading single- and multi-item performance, although its strongest real-world results rely on an internal benchmark and model-based judges.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses image-based virtual try-on from heterogeneous references: clean product images or photographs of people already wearing the desired items. The intended system must preserve the target subject and fine item details while handling clothing, footwear, bags, and accessories in full-body, half-body, and local-region views.

The authors argue that prior specialized systems generally assume one garment category, studio imagery, flat-lay references, or garment masks, while general image editors can alter identity, hallucinate item details, and struggle with layering and multiple references (Section 1). This leaves a gap between constrained academic try-on and practical, open-world outfit composition.

## Approach and contributions

- **Understanding-driven formulation:** The output is generated jointly from one subject image, a variable-size reference set, and an optional instruction, without a garment-agnostic mask. Semantic understanding determines item identity, body-region placement, layering, and optional edits (Sections 1 and 3.1).

- **Multi-reference generative architecture:** Starting from JoyAI-Image-Edit, the model combines Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, Wan-2.1-VAE, and a 16B-parameter MMDiT. Subject and reference latents occupy distinct temporal-position intervals, while natural-language picture indices bind items to their intended roles (Sections 3.1.2–3.1.4; Figure 6).

- **Fashion-specific data engine:** The pipeline collects, filters, annotates, and pairs e-commerce, open-domain, and synthetic images into single- and multi-item try-on triplets spanning diverse products, subjects, and scenes (Section 2; Figures 4–5).

- **CPT–SFT–RL training:** Continued pre-training uses a 1:1 mixture of general and try-on data; SFT learns item transfer and composition; RL combines an in-house try-on reward model with a rubric-guided Gemini judge (Section 3.2).

- **Broader try-on capability:** The resulting model supports product-to-person and person-to-person transfer, multiple item categories, optional editing instructions, and qualitative cross-domain use on illustrated or artistic subjects (Sections 4.4 and 5).

These results support the authors’ claim that the model is highly competitive across constrained, multi-reference, and in-the-wild settings. They do not establish universal superiority: some official baseline values could not be reproduced, multi-item item fidelity trails several proprietary systems, and the deployment-oriented benchmark uses internal data and an automated judge.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The underlying foundation was pretrained for at most four reference images. Five or more references increase item confusion, omissions, and implausible layering (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Multi-step diffusion inference limits throughput for interactive or large-scale deployment; an accelerated distilled model is future work (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** On multi-item TStars-VTON, Oxygen-TryOn’s item fidelity is below Nano Banana Pro, Seedream5 Lite, and GPT-Image-2 (Table 4).

- **Analyst inference:** Claims of arbitrary reference cardinality are qualified by the stated four-reference primary capability range and the 2–4-reference quantitative evaluation.

- **Analyst inference:** Automated comparisons are sensitive to judge versions. The authors report substantial differences between original and reproduced TStars-VTON scores, possibly from Gemini API changes (Table 3).

- **Analyst inference:** Oxygen-TryOn Bench is internal and judged by GPT-5, limiting independent verification. Dataset sampling procedures beyond broad composition and the judge’s exact rubric are not fully reported (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The stage ablation establishes checkpoint-level gains but does not isolate architectural choices, data sources, reference-position encoding, reward components, or the 1:1 CPT mixture (Table 6).

- **Analyst inference:** The text promises or plans releases but does not establish that model weights, training data, benchmark samples, reward models, or executable training code are currently available.

## Open research questions

1. **Can fidelity remain stable beyond four references?** The paper identifies omissions and layering failures at higher cardinality. A next experiment could evaluate controlled outfits with 1–8 references while separately measuring item recall, per-item fidelity, and layering correctness.

2. **Which parts of the hybrid RL reward cause the gains?** Table 6 compares SFT with the combined RL system but not the in-house and Gemini rewards separately. A factorial ablation could compare each reward alone, their weighted combination, and alternative aggregation rules.

3. **How robust are conclusions to evaluator choice and version drift?** Reproduced TStars scores differ substantially from official values. A next study could evaluate the same fixed outputs with multiple frozen VLM judges and blinded human raters, reporting agreement and rank stability.

4. **How much does each data source contribute to open-world generalization?** Real e-commerce, open-domain, and synthetic samples are pooled without source-level performance ablations. Training matched models with individual sources removed could test effects on long-tail items, worn-on references, and cross-domain subjects.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, training stages, metrics, and numerical results because the supplied text is extensive and includes the main tables.
