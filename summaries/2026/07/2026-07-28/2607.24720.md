# The Physics of Multi-Turn Long-Horizon Planning: From Pre-training to Post-training via Single- and Multi-Teacher On-Policy Agentic Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24720) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24720)


## One-sentence takeaway

In a controlled synthetic planning gym, the authors find that long-horizon planning depends on explicit world-model reasoning and exposure to high-quality long trajectories, while on-policy distillation primarily reshapes existing shared planning patterns and can fail or cause forgetting when teacher knowledge or patterns conflict.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks how multi-turn, long-horizon planning is acquired during pre-training, shaped during reinforcement-learning post-training, and integrated from multiple teachers. The authors argue that opaque Internet training corpora prevent controlled attribution of planning gains: it is unclear whether post-training creates planning abilities or merely activates patterns already learned during pre-training (Sections 1 and 4).

To isolate these factors, they construct synthetic environments in which task length, trajectory quality, procedural knowledge, and reasoning-pattern distributions can be varied independently (Section 3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Controlled planning gym.** Hierarchical AND/OR skill graphs are instantiated in three synthetic domains—Fantasy Alchemy, Livestock Farming, and Electronic Assembly—with held-out entity mappings and short, middle, and long task partitions (Section 3; Tables 10–11).

2. **Pre-training analysis.** Experiments vary whether trajectories include explicit chain-of-thought state-transition modeling, which horizon lengths occur in training, and the ratio of optimal to suboptimal patterns (Section 4).

3. **Single-teacher post-training analysis.** The paper separates reusable planning patterns from task-specific procedural knowledge using a mutual-information framing, then compares outcome-reward GRPO with top-100 on-policy distillation, or OPD (Section 5).

4. **Multi-teacher integration analysis.** Cascaded multi-teacher OPD, or MOPD, is tested under shared-compatible, partially shared/conflicting, and fully non-shared/conflicting pattern configurations (Section 6).

5. **Mechanistic diagnostics.** SVD-based update-direction similarity, token-level KL dynamics, pattern-frequency analysis, PCA, parameter distances, and update-direction cosine similarities supplement success-rate evaluation (Sections 5.2.2, 5.3.2, and 6.4).

The main conceptual contribution is an empirical map of when post-training can select existing general patterns, when task-specific knowledge transfer fails, and when sequential teacher consolidation generalizes or forgets. These boundaries are demonstrated only within the paper’s constructed environment.

The evidence supports causal comparisons inside a deliberately controlled synthetic setup, including the effects of trajectory format, horizon distribution, pattern quality, and teacher compatibility. It does not establish that the same numerical boundaries or failure modes hold for large production models, natural environments, or real-world agent tasks. Several mechanistic explanations—such as error accumulation, mode seeking, and insufficient RL update magnitude—are author interpretations supported by diagnostic correlations, not formal proofs.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated boundary:** OPD’s broader effective region assumes an ideal and correct teacher; OPD suppresses diversity and may inherit the teacher’s preferred pattern (Section 5.2.1).

- **Author-stated boundary:** OPD cannot reliably create planning patterns absent from the target environment’s pre-training or SFT support (Section 6.2).

- **Author-stated boundary:** Gold trajectories provide very few successful recoveries from incorrect intermediate states, motivating explicit error-correction data (Section 5.3.2).

- **Analyst inference—external validity:** All main experiments use a randomly initialized approximately 100M-parameter Qwen2.5-style model and synthetic crafting graphs. Transfer to pretrained foundation models and less structured environments is untested (Section 4.1; Table 8).

- **Analyst inference—benchmark breadth:** Evaluation covers three semantically different renderings of the same general graph-based synthesis formulation, so domain diversity may overstate structural diversity (Sections 3.1–3.2).

- **Analyst inference—metric ambiguity:** The paper itself argues that Pass@k can mischaracterize latent capability at long horizons. This complicates claims about capability ceilings derived from Avg@8 and Pass@8 (Section 5.2.1).

- **Analyst inference—causal interpretation:** Gradient-subspace similarity, PCA, KL dynamics, and pattern frequencies are diagnostic associations; the supplied text does not report interventions proving that these quantities cause the observed performance changes.

- **Analyst inference—reporting inconsistency:** Section 6.3’s prose says the “Livestock Farming” score crashes from 90.00 to 15.62, but Table 7 assigns those values to Fantasy Alchemy. The table supports forgetting, but the domain named in the prose appears inconsistent.

- **Analyst inference—missing robustness checks:** The supplied text does not establish results across model scales, architectures, teacher qualities, OPD top-\(k\) choices, teacher orders, or more than three teachers.

## Open research questions

1. **Do these applicability regions persist at realistic model scales?** The experiments use a 100M model trained from scratch, leaving scale dependence unresolved. A next experiment could repeat the pre-training-quality × horizon grid on several pretrained model sizes while holding the gym and data fixed.

2. **Can mismatched procedural knowledge be transferred without overwriting existing knowledge?** Direct OPD from Recipe B damages a Recipe A student. A next experiment could compare replay, explicit path identifiers, modular adapters, and a staged SFT-plus-OPD curriculum on both A and B test graphs.

3. **How sensitive is MOPD to teacher order and shared-pattern frequency?** Only the FA→LF→EA sequence is reported in the main MOPD tables. A full permutation study could measure final performance, forgetting, and pattern distributions for every teacher order and several shared-pattern proportions.

4. **Can recovery data improve long-horizon robustness?** The KL analysis finds very few correct trajectories containing incorrect intermediate steps. A next experiment could inject controlled error-state prefixes with recovery demonstrations and evaluate success under forced action errors at different depths.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported synthetic-environment findings and numerical table entries; **medium** for the paper’s broader mechanistic conclusions.

Material missing or insufficiently established in the supplied text includes statistical uncertainty or repeated-seed variation, significance tests, complete hyperparameters for GRPO/OPD/MOPD, teacher-order ablations, model-scale and architecture ablations, sensitivity to OPD’s top-\(k=100\), wall-clock and inference costs, evaluation on natural agent benchmarks, and independent validation of the released code, datasets, and checkpoints.
