# Leveraging External Knowledge for Historical Document Restoration via Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21936) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21936)


## One-sentence takeaway

ARI combines domain fine-tuning, chronological metadata, and deduplicated BM25 retrieval to improve Hanja document restoration—especially named entities—while expert evaluation and temporal-shift tests reveal practical value but limited robustness outside the training period.

## Problem and motivation

Damaged historical documents contain illegible characters that obstruct OCR, expert interpretation, and downstream historical analysis; the JRS alone contains about 41.9K damaged characters across 11.1K documents (Table 1). Existing masked-language-model restoration relies mainly on local document context, which is often insufficient for proper nouns requiring historical knowledge. The authors further identify a mismatch between fixed training-mask rates and real corruption patterns (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **External-knowledge restoration:** The framework supplies a causal LLM with chronological metadata and related documents retrieved from the training corpus (§4; Figure 4).
- **ARI models:** ARI-32B and ARI-8B fine-tune Qwen3 models on Hanja restoration prompts with dynamic masking; 25% of training samples prioritize named-entity masking (§4.3; Table 7).
- **Retrieval design:** The final system uses 20 BM25-retrieved documents and removes near-duplicates above an 80% similarity threshold (§4.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Evaluation assets:** The authors construct random-mask, named-entity-mask, and held-out real-damage evaluations from AJD and JRS, supplemented by blinded assessment from three domain experts (§3; §5.2).
- **Candidate generation:** Byte-level constrained decoding produces ranked character candidates despite multi-byte tokenization of uncommon Hanja characters (Appendix C).
- **Availability:** The paper states that model code and models are publicly available (§1), although the supplied text does not enumerate the released files or licenses.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant retrieval substantially improved Qwen3-32B restoration. | On the validation datasets, metadata-only accuracy was 5.63% on D_NE and 14.83% on D_Rand; BM25 retrieval raised these to 24.28% and 60.36%, and deduplication further raised them to 27.85% and 61.45%. | Table 4; §4.2 |
| BM25 outperformed dense and random retrieval. | With dynamic shots, D_NE/D_Rand accuracy was 8.55%/26.18% for random documents, 19.88%/59.91% for embedding retrieval, and 24.28%/60.36% for BM25. | Table 4 |
| Named-entity-focused training produced a favorable balance at a 25% share. | With 100K training samples, a 75:25 D_Rand:D_NE mixture achieved 45.41% NE and 76.13% random-mask accuracy, versus 42.75% and 75.67% with random-only data. | Table 7; Appendix A |
| ARI-32B led the expert evaluation on genuine damage. | Across 100 D_RD documents assessed blindly by three experts, ARI-32B achieved Accuracy@1 0.383, Accuracy@10 0.583, nDCG@10 0.477, and a 46.0% win ratio. BERT-Res obtained 0.207, 0.403, 0.296, and 24.0%; Gemini-2.5-Pro obtained Accuracy@1 0.273 and a 30.0% win ratio, with ranking metrics unavailable. | Table 5; §5.2 |
| Real damage was concentrated in named entities. | Gemini-2.5-Pro classified approximately 44.8% of about 42K damaged characters as named entities; manual inspection of 200 sampled documents found four classification errors, reported as 0.7%. | Figure 3; §3 |
| Retrieval effectiveness depended on temporal proximity. | On Goryeosa, ARI-32B reportedly remained robust under moderate shifts but degraded as the gap from the AJD/JRS reference period widened; exact values are absent from the supplied text. | Figure 8; §5.4 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that relevant, diverse retrieved context helps restoration and that ARI-32B is more useful than the evaluated alternatives in the small expert study. It does not isolate every component of the final ARI model, establish statistical significance, or demonstrate generalization beyond the evaluated Korean Hanja corpora. Exact synthetic-test scores for the final ARI models and most values underlying Figures 1, 7, and 8 are not present in the retrieved text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Synthetic corruptions may not reproduce the intrinsic characteristics of genuine document damage, despite matching observed corruption statistics and adding a real-damage expert evaluation (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The 4,096-token maximum excludes approximately 1.7% of the data (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The system is text-only even though original document images are available; visual evidence could aid restoration (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Performance declines as the temporal gap from the retrieval corpora grows (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The real-damage evaluation is narrow—100 documents and three experts—and the text does not report inter-annotator agreement or uncertainty estimates (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Some dataset labels and categories depend on Gemini-2.5-Pro. A 200-document audit supports the damage-category proxy, but validation of the separate 7,707-document entity-type evaluation is not reported (§3; §5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 4 evaluates a sequence of prompting and retrieval changes rather than a full factorial ablation, so interactions among metadata, examples, retrieval, and deduplication are not completely isolated.
- **Analyst inference:** Training ARI-32B required approximately 1,500 H200 GPU-hours, which limits accessibility and replication (§4.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can image evidence improve restoration over text-only ARI?** The paper excludes manuscript images. A next experiment could compare text-only, image-only, and multimodal models on the same genuine damaged spans.
2. **How can retrieval remain reliable across historical periods?** Figure 8 shows degradation with widening temporal gaps. A next experiment could index period-matched corpora and compare temporal filtering, adaptive retrieval, and the current AJD/JRS-only index.
3. **Which ARI component contributes most after fine-tuning?** The reported ablations do not fully cross metadata, retrieval, deduplication, dynamic masking, and fine-tuning. A controlled factorial ablation on identical splits would quantify their individual and interacting effects.
4. **How well does the method transfer to other scripts and archives?** The broader low-resource applicability asserted in §6 is not evaluated. A next experiment could fine-tune and test the framework on a non-Hanja historical corpus while preserving the same random, entity, and real-damage protocols.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methodological and evaluation detail, but omits exact final synthetic-test results for ARI-32B/ARI-8B and numerical values from several figures. Also unavailable are statistical significance tests, uncertainty intervals, inter-annotator agreement, complete release contents and licensing, detailed split composition by corpus, retrieval latency, inference cost, and full evidence for cross-language generalization.
