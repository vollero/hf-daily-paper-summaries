# TRACE: Business Rule-Grounded Reasoning Curriculum for Knowledge-Preserving Parametric Tool Retrieval in Enterprise LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22639) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22639)


## One-sentence takeaway

TRACE uses a two-stage memorization-and-reasoning curriculum grounded in expert business rules to preserve an LLM’s tool knowledge while retrieving from an 8,283-tool enterprise catalog with single-beam decoding.

## Problem and motivation

Enterprise copilots must select the correct API from thousands of tools with terse, overlapping descriptions. The authors report that embedding retrieval caused about 60% of incorrect retrievals in a production copilot serving roughly 39,000 monthly users; among 609 sampled sessions, 81.7% of failures involved more than ten semantically overlapping tools requiring business rules for disambiguation (§1).

Parametric retrieval can internalize a catalog, but conventional query-to-token training with constrained beam search reportedly destroys learned tool knowledge and is too slow for production. TRACE addresses this claimed dissociation between retrieval performance, knowledge retention, and deployment latency (§§1, 4.2, 4.6).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Two-stage curriculum.** Stage 1 uses LoRA-based, multi-format SFT to bind tool descriptions, names, and virtual tokens. Stage 2 trains the model to generate a deliberative trace followed by a JSON list of tool tokens (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix A).

2. **Business-rule-grounded training data.** Stage 2 combines ToolSense-style realistic retrieval pairs with queries targeting 123 expert-authored rules over confusable tools. Generated traces compare candidates, apply relevant rules, and substitute tool names with virtual tokens to reinforce description-token associations (§§3.1, 3.3; Figure 1; Appendix B).

3. **Quality-controlled synthesis.** Deterministic checks enforce grounding, non-leakage, JSON consistency, and rule attribution; an LLM judge assesses naturalness, correctness, and trace faithfulness, with up to five regeneration attempts (Appendix B).

4. **Greedy parametric retrieval.** At inference, the model generates reasoning and tool-token JSON with one unconstrained beam, avoiding prefix-trie search (Appendix D).

5. **Enterprise evaluation.** The study covers 8,283 proprietary HR and Finance tools, three token formats, knowledge probes, production retrieval queries, rule-density sweeps, and H200 latency measurements (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| TRACE retrieves substantially better than the embedding baseline. | Wrapped-hierarchical TRACE with rule grounding and single-beam decoding obtains 85.5% R@gen on Domain A and 60.2% on Domain B; text-embedding-3-large obtains 27.5% and 52.7% R@10, respectively. The metrics use different output regimes, so this is not a strictly matched comparison. | §4.3; Tables 2–3 |
| Reasoning avoids the knowledge collapse associated with non-reasoning retrieval. | On MCQ_expert, the wrapped-hierarchical Stage-1 checkpoint scores 69.2%, non-reasoning retrieval 33.7%, reasoning retrieval 61.5%, and rule-grounded reasoning 56.4%; random accuracy is 29.3%. | §4.2 |
| The pattern holds across token formats. | Non-reasoning MCQ_expert accuracy is 34.8%, 30.0%, and 33.7% for flat, bare-hierarchical, and wrapped-hierarchical tokens; reasoning recovers 59.5%, 50.9%, and 61.5%. | §4.5; Table 4 |
| Increasing rule-targeted data improves Domain-A retrieval but costs some general-domain accuracy. | For flat tokens, Domain-A R@gen rises from 55.7% at zero queries per rule to 73.3% at 12 and 86.3% with replacement; MCQ_expert falls from 59.5% to 55.7%. Domain B remains within its baseline confidence interval. | §4.4; Figure 3; Table 6 |
| Correct retrieval is associated with explicit rule citation. | On Domain-A PRB for the headline rule-grounded model, citation rises from 0% to 71%; recall is 94.6% for traces citing a rule versus 63.2% for traces that do not. | §4.4 |
| Greedy decoding greatly reduces serving cost. | On one H200 and 100 Domain-A PRB queries, single-user latency is approximately 1.9 seconds versus 19 seconds for constrained beam-10; at concurrency 32, throughput is 11.2 versus 0.05 queries/s, approximately a 200× gap. | §4.6; Figure 4 |
| Unconstrained output is usually, but not always, valid. | The reported off-vocabulary tool-token rate is 3% for TRACE `(c,m,r+R)`. | Appendix D |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that reasoning-augmented training can retain substantially more probed knowledge than non-reasoning retrieval while enabling lower-latency generation in this enterprise setting. Rule-density sweeps also provide evidence that expert rules help where catalog coverage exists.

It does not establish generalization beyond two proprietary domains, one base-model size, or expert-curated rules. The embedding comparison mixes R@10 with generated-set recall, while the latency comparison changes both decoding strategy and whether reasoning is emitted. Rule citation is correlational evidence rather than proof that the generated rationale causally produced the answer.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers only HR and Finance at one model size; behavior in other verticals and at larger scales remains unknown (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The proprietary catalogs and benchmarks cannot be released, restricting direct replication (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Expert rule curation is costly, automatic extraction is not evaluated, and sufficient rule density lacks a principled criterion (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Domain B has incomplete rule coverage, and greedy decoding still trails constrained-beam retrieval on its 7,365-tool catalog (§4.3; Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** The teacher and judge model identities are not supplied in the retrieved text, limiting reproducibility and leaving sensitivity to synthetic-label or judge errors unclear (§3.3; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** PRB is small—131 and 123 queries—and proprietary, so representativeness and contamination cannot be independently checked (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The rule-citation analysis conditions on model behavior; cited-rule traces may simply be easier cases. A controlled intervention on citation or rule access is needed to establish causality (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported summary gains are inconsistent across the supplied text: the abstract says Stage-2 gains of +3.2 and +9 percentage points over Stage 1, while the conclusion reports +7.6 and +4.5. The underlying table values needed to reconcile these statements are absent from the retrieved rendering.
- **Analyst inference:** A 3% off-vocabulary rate may matter operationally, but the paper does not report how invalid outputs affect end-to-end retrieval or whether a fallback is used (Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Can business rules be extracted or maintained automatically?** The paper relies on 123 expert-authored rules and does not evaluate automatic extraction. A next experiment could compare expert rules with rules mined from documentation and corrected through limited expert review.

2. **What rule coverage is sufficient at large catalog scale?** Domain B’s weaker result is attributed to incomplete coverage, while “at least six queries per rule” is only a practical recipe. A controlled experiment could vary both the fraction of confusable clusters covered and the number of examples per rule.

3. **Do reasoning traces causally preserve knowledge and improve retrieval?** The citation analysis is observational. An intervention could hold query-answer pairs fixed while ablating rule text, candidate comparison, token substitution, and visible reasoning independently.

4. **Does TRACE generalize across models and domains?** Only one model size and two enterprise domains are tested. A factorial evaluation across multiple model sizes, open catalogs, and additional verticals would test whether the knowledge-retention and latency trade-offs persist.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methodological and evaluation detail, but several result tables are represented only by captions rather than their numeric contents.

Material missing information includes the full values and confidence intervals from Tables 2–8; training-set size and synthesis yield statistics; teacher and judge identities; sentence encoder and hard-negative `K`; exact constrained-retrieval baseline scores; business-rule coverage by confusable cluster; latency output lengths; error handling for invalid tokens; and sufficiently detailed public assets for independent reproduction.
