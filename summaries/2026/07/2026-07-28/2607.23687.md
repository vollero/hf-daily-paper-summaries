# GNM Head: A Generative aNthropometric Model of the human head

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23687) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23687)


## One-sentence takeaway

GNM is a publicly released, controllable 3D morphable head model that jointly represents external facial geometry, eyes, teeth, tongue, and localized expressions, with reported lower reconstruction error than FLAME although the retrieved text omits the numerical results.

## Problem and motivation

Existing public 3D morphable models generally represent the head as an external shell, omitting detailed oral and ocular anatomy. The authors argue that this limits realistic reconstruction, animation, generative conditioning, and precise control of speech-related mouth and tongue motion. They target the gap between controllable linear models and anatomically broader but less interpretable neural representations (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Holistic parametric model:** GNM combines a linear identity basis, regional expression bases, identity-dependent joint locations, four-joint skeletal articulation, and linear blend skinning. Its 17,821-vertex topology includes skin, neck, eyeballs, teeth, gums, and tongue (Sections 3.3–3.9).
- **Composite anatomical spaces:** Identity is partitioned into head, eye, and teeth components; expression is partitioned into left eye, right eye, lower face, tongue, and pupil components. This is intended to reduce unwanted coupling and enable localized control (Sections 3.4–3.7; Figures 2 and 8).
- **Hybrid data foundation:** The model combines approximately 150,000 expressive samples from over 5,000 captured people with artist-authored assets, including 5,000 synthetic dental shapes, approximately 2,500 tongue samples, 10,000 sampled eye cross-sections, and 200 sculpted heads for cranial completion (Sections 3.1, 3.2, and 3.7).
- **Registration and fitting pipelines:** The authors develop multi-view registration using image, normal, semantic, landmark, geometric, and collision losses, plus image fitting from dense 2D landmarks with anatomical and optional temporal constraints (Sections 3.2 and 4.2).
- **Semantic control:** A dual-CVAE sampler maps demographic categories and 20 expression classes into GNM coefficients, with conditional mixup supporting blended controls (Section 4.3).
- **Release:** The authors state that the complete framework is publicly available for academic and commercial use; dense tracking landmarks are described only as planned future releases (Introduction; Section 5).

It does not reveal the magnitude or statistical significance of the improvements because the retrieved table cells and plotted values are missing. It also does not establish downstream gains in animation, generative-image conditioning, telepresence, synthetic-data training, or neural rendering.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated population limitations:** The Semantic Sampler recognizes only binary gender categories and four broad ethnicity categories. The authors explicitly warn that these categories are neither exhaustive nor sufficiently granular and may create fairness and representation concerns (Section 4.3.2).
- **Author-stated capture limitation:** Hair occlusion prevents empirical reconstruction of the cranium, which is instead inferred using a latent regression model trained from 200 artist-sculpted heads (Section 3.2).
- **Modeling approximations:** Teeth and tongue placement under identity change is approximated by lip displacement; their lower-jaw motion is inferred from a rigid transform of the lower lip and chin. Eye shape assumes a constant scleral radius and shared parameters between both eyes (Sections 3.7.3 and 3.8).
- **Analyst inference — comparison scope:** FLAME is the only experimentally evaluated competing model, so superiority over neural or other anatomically complete head models is not established (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference — dataset dependence:** Held-out scans were registered using the authors’ GNM-based pipeline, potentially favoring GNM despite the subsequent ICP refinement intended to reduce mapping bias (Sections 3.2 and 4.1.1–4.1.2).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete quantitative evidence:** The retrieved text omits Table 1 dimensions, Table 2 and Table 3 metric values, and numerical values from Figures 10–13, preventing independent assessment of effect sizes.
- **Analyst inference — missing ablations:** No reported ablation isolates the effects of regional bases, internal-anatomy components, collision constraints, dataset scale, artist-made data, or the Semantic Sampler’s mixup and KL schedule.
- **Analyst inference — qualitative real-world validation:** Multi-view and in-the-wild results are visual only, with no real-image ground-truth geometry, landmark-baseline sensitivity, runtime, or failure analysis supplied (Sections 4.2.2–4.2.3).
- **Reproducibility risk:** The framework is claimed to be public, but dense landmarks are only planned for release; exact model-region dimensions and several fitting weights are unavailable in the supplied text (Table 1; Sections 4.2 and 5).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does each anatomical or regional component contribute?** The paper reports no component-level ablation. A next experiment could remove the teeth, tongue, ocular, and regional-expression bases individually and measure scan-to-mesh error plus collision frequency.

2. **Does GNM improve downstream generation and animation?** These are motivated applications but not directly evaluated. A controlled study could substitute GNM and FLAME as priors in the same renderer or generative pipeline and measure geometry consistency, temporal stability, and perceptual quality.

3. **How well does the model generalize beyond its demographic labels and capture distribution?** The authors acknowledge coarse demographic categories but provide no out-of-distribution evaluation. A next experiment could use independently collected scans with finer ancestry, age, and gender annotations and report group-disaggregated errors.

4. **How robust is landmark-based fitting under occlusion or detector error?** Synthetic evaluation uses ground-truth landmarks, while real-image evidence is qualitative. An experiment could perturb or occlude landmarks systematically and compare reconstruction error, failure rate, and internal-anatomy collisions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text provides detailed model and evaluation procedures, but omits the actual entries of Tables 1–3 and numerical plot values. Materially missing information includes exact basis dimensions, quantitative reconstruction and specificity scores, uncertainty or significance estimates, detailed demographic counts, data licensing and consent procedures, fitting-loss weights, runtime and compute requirements, failure cases, ablation results, and quantitative multi-view or in-the-wild evaluation.
