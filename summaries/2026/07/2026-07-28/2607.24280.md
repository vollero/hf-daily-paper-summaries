# From Proprietary to Open-Source: Bridging the Distribution Gap via Multi-Agent Protocol Distillation in Agentic Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24280) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24280)


## One-sentence takeaway

MAPD converts proprietary-model search traces into quality-checked, style-normalized JSON protocols and uses them as privileged information for joint self-distillation and reinforcement learning, outperforming the evaluated baselines across seven QA benchmarks without adding inference-time teacher overhead.

## Problem and motivation

Agentic search requires planning, retrieval, and reasoning across long trajectories, but exact-match outcome rewards provide only sparse supervision. Token-level policy distillation could provide denser guidance, yet proprietary teachers expose neither logits nor compatible token distributions, while imitating their free-form trajectories can transfer verbosity and stylistic artifacts instead of reasoning strategies.

The authors identify a missing bridge: an intermediate representation that extracts useful black-box teacher behavior while remaining compatible with an open-source student’s token space and native response style (Abstract; Sections 1 and 2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured protocol:** Each offline exploration trace becomes a JSON object encoding task type, reasoning plan, extractive grounding facts, optional partial findings, and answer verification. This is intended to separate reasoning strategy from teacher-specific prose (Section 3.1).

- **Multi-agent synthesis:** An orchestrator decomposes questions, searchers retrieve from a local Wikipedia corpus, a repair agent diagnoses failures using ground truth offline, and a protocolizer creates either a succeeded or evidence-only protocol (Section 3.2).

- **Quality control:** Protocols undergo schema, exact-match consistency, grounding, and leakage checks; rejected examples fall back to a self-rollout baseline (Section 3.2, Stage C).

- **Joint learning:** A protocol-conditioned privileged branch and an unconditioned student branch share the same policy. Reverse-KL self-distillation supplies dense token-level supervision alongside sparse GRPO rewards (Equation 4, Section 3.3).

- **Cross-teacher evaluation:** The same pipeline is tested with three proprietary teacher backbones without teacher-specific retuning (Section 4.2, Table 3 and Figure 4).

The evidence supports improved exact-match performance over the listed baselines in the paper’s Wikipedia-backed QA environment and shows useful component, teacher, and loss-weight comparisons. It does not establish generalization to non-QA agent tasks, live web search, other student families, or inference conditions beyond the reported setup. Because the supplied text omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–3, many per-dataset results, standard deviations, and exact ablation values cannot be independently reconstructed.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Training uses only NQ and HotpotQA, evaluation is limited to seven knowledge-intensive QA benchmarks, and retrieval uses the fixed wiki-18 corpus (Section 4.1).

- **Teacher and synthesis cost:** Protocol creation depends on proprietary APIs and, in the Gemini example, costs approximately $1,454 for 25,600 instances, despite adding no inference-time overhead (Section 4.2).

- **Ground-truth dependence:** Repair, exact-match checks, and succeeded-protocol construction use ground-truth answers offline. This limits direct applicability to tasks without verifiable labels, even though the authors state that ground truth is hidden from the student and retrieval queries (Section 3.2).

- **Analyst inference — statistical uncertainty:** Results are evaluated across five seeds from a single training run, but the supplied text lacks standard deviations and significance tests. Consequently, the reliability of small differences—such as 44.4% versus 44.6% across teachers—cannot be assessed.

- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablation visibility:** Table 2’s body is absent. The prose supports the direction and selected magnitudes of component effects, but not every configuration or interaction.

- **Analyst inference — limited model coverage:** Only two sizes from one student family are reported. Claims about broad open-source-model transfer therefore remain untested in the supplied evidence.

- **Analyst inference — protocol review details:** The paper reports 99.33% manual-review accuracy, but the supplied text does not describe sampling, annotators, agreement, or error categories.

## Open research questions

1. **Does MAPD transfer beyond Qwen3?** The paper evaluates only Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B. A next experiment could repeat the same protocol corpus and hyperparameter sweep across several unrelated open-source model families.

2. **Can protocols be synthesized without ground-truth-assisted repair?** Ground truth is used offline to diagnose failures and validate answers. An experiment could compare the current pipeline against confidence-based or evidence-consistency repair on unlabeled queries.

3. **How well does the method handle changing or open-web evidence?** Evaluation uses a static local Wikipedia corpus. A next experiment could use a timestamped live-web environment and measure both answer accuracy and citation-grounding fidelity.

4. **Which protocol fields cause the gains?** The supplied ablations separate structured protocols from MAS generation but do not report field-level removals. A factorial ablation could remove reasoning plans, grounding facts, partial findings, and verification independently.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.**
