# Sol-Attn: Accelerating Video Generation Inference via On-the-Fly Attention Sparsification

> [← Daily summary](../2026-07-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24027) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24027)


## One-sentence takeaway

Sol-Attn is a training-free block-sparse attention operator that performs dynamic threshold routing and approximates skipped blocks inside one online-softmax pass, improving inference speed while remaining close to dense-attention quality across the evaluated visual diffusion models.

## Problem and motivation

Long token sequences make quadratic self-attention a major inference bottleneck in diffusion transformers for high-resolution and long-duration video. Existing training-free block-sparse methods typically use top-\(k\) routing, which imposes a fixed per-query budget, or top-\(p\) routing, whose dynamic budgets can be highly imbalanced; both also materialize proxy scores and routing indices in high-bandwidth memory. Their keep-or-drop treatment of unselected blocks can further degrade accuracy at aggressive sparsity (Section 1, pp. 2–3; Figure 2).

The authors target a better accuracy–efficiency trade-off: dynamic but controllable routing without a separate proxy-map pass, plus inexpensive recovery of information from skipped blocks.

## Approach and contributions

- **Query-dependent threshold routing:** For each query block, Sol-Attn selects key blocks whose proxy logits exceed \(\mu_i+\beta\sigma_i\). The shared \(\beta\) controls average density, while each query retains a variable block count. The required moments can be computed without constructing the full proxy-score map (Section 3.1, Equations 4–5; Figure 3).

- **On-the-fly fused execution:** Proxy scores are generated chunk by chunk inside online softmax, compared with the threshold on chip, and immediately used to dispatch selected blocks to an exact sparse-attention inner loop. This avoids storing the full proxy map or routing indices (Section 3.2, Equations 6–7; Figure 4 and Algorithm 1).

- **Approximate correction for skipped blocks:** Rather than discarding unselected blocks, the method uses a zeroth-order Taylor approximation based on pooled keys and summed values. Exact and approximate contributions share one online-softmax state (Equations 8–11).

- **Hardware-oriented implementation and evaluation:** Custom GPU kernels are evaluated across text-to-video, video editing, video refinement, and 2K text-to-image generation, including H100, B200, and RTX 5090 settings (Sections 4.1–4.4).

These results support the authors’ claim that Sol-Attn improves the measured quality–speed trade-off over the selected training-free sparse baselines across several visual-generation pipelines. They do not establish statistical equivalence to dense attention: the text reports no confidence intervals, significance tests, evaluation-set sizes, or run-to-run variance. “Without quality degradation” should therefore be read as the authors’ interpretation of reported benchmark scores and qualitative samples, not a formal equivalence result.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The B200 kernel does not yet fully exploit Blackwell hardware.
- The implementation supports forward inference only.
- Evaluation is limited to bidirectional diffusion-based visual generation and excludes autoregressive video generation.
- Backward support and a trainable implementation remain future work (Section 6).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- Generalization beyond the tested NVIDIA H100, B200, and RTX 5090 platforms is not established.
- The approximation analysis assumes dense, noncausal keys within each block; causal or semantic masks require modified centering and valid-key multiplicities (Appendix B).
- Several pipelines preserve dense attention during early denoising, in first layers, for cross-attention, or for text sink tokens. Reported system speedups therefore depend on model-specific deployment policies rather than universal all-layer sparsification (Section 4.1; Appendix A).
- Baseline configurations were adapted for models not covered by their original releases. The supplied text does not report the full adapted hyperparameters, making exact reproduction and sensitivity assessment difficult (Section 4.1).
- Figure 9 supports the correction mechanism qualitatively, but exact plotted error values are absent from the retrieved text. No ablation isolates the exact versus diagonal covariance threshold estimator described in Appendix B.
- Sample counts, prompt sets, variance across runs, and statistical uncertainty are not supplied, limiting assessment of benchmark robustness.

## Open research questions

1. **Does threshold calibration remain reliable for causal or autoregressive generation?** The evaluated attention is bidirectional and the error analysis assumes noncausal blocks. A next experiment could implement valid-key-aware thresholding and correction in an autoregressive video model, measuring density calibration, quality, and latency across generation positions.

2. **How robust is the method across hardware and kernel implementations?** Results cover three NVIDIA GPU generations, while the B200 implementation is explicitly under-optimized. Profiling optimized kernels on additional accelerators at matched sequence lengths and sparsities would test portability.

3. **When does zeroth-order correction cease to be accurate?** Appendix B ties residual error to within-block score spread and value alignment, but experiments do not stratify blocks by these quantities. A targeted study could bin skipped blocks by score variance and value heterogeneity, then compare zeroth-, first-, and higher-order corrections.

4. **Can dense warm-up and exact-token policies be selected automatically?** Current policies vary by model and keep some steps, layers, cross-attention, or text tokens dense. An ablation or controller that jointly chooses sparsity, warm-up duration, and exact-token sets could measure whether similar quality is attainable with less manual configuration.
