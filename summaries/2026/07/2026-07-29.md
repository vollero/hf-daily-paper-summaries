# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-07-29

## Executive summary

- The day’s clearest shared theme is selective information routing: relevance-guided corpus traversal (`2607.24223`), persistent-memory surfacing (`2607.24368`), codec-driven video sparsity (`2607.24904`), and hierarchical audio-video budget allocation (`2607.25669`) all ask what information should remain visible under a constrained context or token budget.
- Controlled diagnostics expose failures hidden by aggregate evaluation. InMind (`2607.24368`) separates storage from retrieval and application, while PerceptionBench (`2607.24957`) isolates ten atomic visual capabilities and finds no evaluated model above 60% overall accuracy.
- HiFi-UMI (`2607.25895`) reports approximate aggregate parity between robot-free UMI and task-specific teleoperation post-training across three policy backbones, but uses roughly ten times as many UMI trajectories and does not provide a formal equivalence test.
- ReDesign (`2607.25565`) treats flattened-design recovery as locally verified tree decomposition. It reports the best tested editability and up to 7.1× parallel-execution speedup, although most metric values are unavailable in the supplied analysis.
- Mage-VL (`2607.24904`) and OmniDelta (`2607.25669`) demonstrate different efficiency mechanisms: codec-native patch selection for streaming video versus fixed-budget allocation across audio, video, and time. Both retain meaningful quality at reduced perceptual-token cost.
- Wonder (`2607.26037`) combines camera-aligned conditioning, sparse full-resolution history retrieval, and four-step generation, reporting 16 FPS and improved VBench and estimated camera-pose results on its I2V and V2V evaluations. Long-duration memory evidence remains mostly qualitative.
- Visual prompt engineering (`2607.25537`) shows that changing an initial task image can materially alter video-model task accuracy. Benefits are task- and model-dependent, and faithful preservation of the original problem is a central validity condition.
- Safety and security classification produced contrasting lessons that should not be treated as a contradiction: synthetic contrastive data substantially improved Shieldstral’s (`2607.25857`) unseen-policy validation, whereas the tested LLM-generated ATT&CK labels in `2607.25572` did not improve ranking and degraded rare-technique macro-F1.
- The small-model PPO study (`2607.25091`) attributes several failures to engineering and numerical defects rather than PPO alone, but its positive evidence is confined to short, single-turn runs with synthetic preferences and reward-model-based evaluation.

## Main results worth noting

- `2607.25895`: Across four bimanual tasks and 160 rollouts per source within each backbone, UMI versus teleoperation success was 51.3% versus 53.8% for StarVLA-QwenPI, 77.5% versus 74.4% for OpenPI-\(\pi_{0.5}\), and 56.9% versus 57.5% for LingBot-VA. These are practical pipeline comparisons with unequal demonstration counts, not evidence of equal sample efficiency or formal statistical equivalence.

- `2607.24223`: On 100-query, 100K-document BrowseComp-Plus, RARG++ with GPT-5.4-mini reached 84% accuracy and 23.9 tool calls, compared with 78% and 28.7 for RISE and 78% and 99.1 for DCI. On four BRIGHT domains, however, RARG+ led the supplied variants at 53.36 nDCG@10, while RARG++ reached 50.55, indicating task-dependent value from aggressive excerpt reranking.

- `2607.25565`: ReDesign is author-reported as best on all tested edit types and Figma reconstruction metrics, but those numerical cells are unavailable. The auditable quantitative headline is a maximum 7.1× parallel speedup over serial tool execution; hardware- and workload-specific timing values are missing.

- `2607.24368`: With the target fact directly visible, GPT-5-mini applied it in 84.0% of 125 tasks, with a 95% Wilson interval of [76.6, 89.4]. Query-time memory configurations achieved only 3.2%–16.0% application and 0.8%–12.0% target recall, while the always-visible diagnostic reached 68.8% application. A human audit found the context-aware application judge only 85.0% accurate, with all 15 errors being false positives.

- `2607.24904`: On NExT-QA, Mage-VL-tc8 scored 80.8 in 415 seconds against Qwen3-VL-4B’s 79.8 in 1,460 seconds on an 8×B200 node. Mage timing includes video loading, while Qwen timing excludes estimated loading, so the 3.5× wall-clock ratio should not be attributed solely to codec pruning.

- `2607.26037`: On the authors’ 1,000-image I2V benchmark, Wonder reached 0.8558 average VBench, 0.0132 translational RPE, and 0.0784 rotational RPE; the best listed comparator values were 0.8415, 0.0174, and 0.1155. RPE uses two learned pose estimators followed by Umeyama alignment.

- `2607.24957`: Across 3,000 questions and sixteen MLLMs, the highest overall PerceptionBench score was 59.7%. Mean hallucination accuracy was 36.7%. For GPT-5.6-Sol, four-run aggregate SD was only 0.17 points, yet pass@4 was 72.9% and pass4 was 46.9%, exposing substantial item-level inconsistency beneath a stable aggregate.

- `2607.25857`: Shieldstral’s reported averages were 84.9% text F1, 83.8% multimodal F1, and 91.3% adaptability F1. The multimodal comparator OmniGuard scored 77.6%, while the adaptability comparator GPT-OSS-Safeguard-20B scored higher at 94.1%. Generated taxonomy data raised unseen-policy validation F1 from 61.1% to 84.4% before model merging.

- `2607.25537`: On VPCT, photorealistic VIPE changed Veo 3.1 accuracy from 41.3% to 59.3% and Omni Flash from 56.3% to 67.5%, with each bar averaging ten full-dataset runs. For Veo 3.1, 20-run self-consistency reached 50.0%, one engineered-image sample reached 59.3%, and VIPE plus 20 samples reached 68.0%. Native image-generation reasoners did not improve consistently.

- `2607.25091`: Pythia-410M on TinyStories recorded reward delta +1.355, analytical win rate 59.9%, 95% CI [0.61, 2.10], and \(p<0.001\) over 200 held-out prompts. The same model on Wikitext-103 regressed by −1.043 with \(p<0.001\). All gains above +0.2 occurred below supervised perplexity 20 in the tested grid, but the authors describe this as empirical rather than universal.

- `2607.25669`: With Qwen2.5-Omni-7B on WorldSense at 25% retention, OmniDelta scored 44.2%, versus 43.2% for OmniZip and 46.8% with full tokens. Relative to full tokens, memory fell from 30.0 to 23.4 GB, TTFT from 3,006 to 1,776 ms, and end-to-end latency from 3.15 to 1.92 seconds.

- `2607.25572`: Under corrected five-seed validation-based checkpoint selection, gold-only recall@5 was \(0.673\pm0.019\) and macro-F1 \(0.177\pm0.014\). Adding 984 LLM-labeled rows reduced recall@5 to 0.651 and macro-F1 to 0.151; ModernBERT reproduced the macro-F1 decline, from 0.180 to 0.152. This result concerns the tested labeler, prompt, data distribution, and expansion size—not LLM annotation in general.

## Open research directions

1. **Measure manipulation-data fidelity causally (`2607.25895`).** Independently degrade pose accuracy, relative-hand geometry, synchronization, and field of view while holding episode count and scene coverage fixed; then measure real-robot success and action error.

2. **Test relevance routing under realistic latency budgets (`2607.24223`, `2607.24368`).** Compare sequential scanning, parallel relevance-aware streaming, knowledge-bridge generation, and oracle routing under equal wall-clock, token, and privacy budgets. Report evidence-set recall and answer correctness separately.

3. **Validate editable reconstruction under equal resources (`2607.25565`).** Run serial and parallel agents with matched hardware, API tokens, tool-call caps, and concurrency costs; report edit-replay success, latency, monetary cost, retries, and catastrophic branch failures.

4. **Separate token allocation from token selection (`2607.24904`, `2607.25669`).** Cross OmniDelta-style allocation with codec, uniform, random, and learned-saliency selectors under identical models, training data, token counts, and pruning backends. This would identify whether gains arise from budget placement, selection, or both.

5. **Quantify persistent video-world memory (`2607.26037`).** Revisit multiple locations after controlled delays and trajectory changes using rendered scenes with ground-truth cameras and geometry. Measure pose, appearance consistency, state persistence, cache size, and latency as rollout duration grows.

6. **Test whether atomic capability gains transfer (`2607.24957`, `2607.24368`).** Improve one isolated component—such as visual localization or memory target recall—while holding the downstream reasoner fixed, then measure end-to-end gains and new failure modes.

7. **Evaluate policy and label adaptation outside synthetic pipelines (`2607.25857`, `2607.25572`).** Use independently authored, multilingual policies and expert-reviewed security labels at controlled agreement levels. Measure calibration, rare-label performance, paraphrase robustness, and abstention or analyst-review utility.

8. **Disentangle visual realism from task preservation (`2607.25537`).** Factorially vary texture realism, geometry, contrast, object familiarity, and evaluator choice using executable semantic states and answer-preserving render controls. Evaluate unchanged variants on held-out instances and model families.

9. **Validate small-model PPO beyond reward-model feedback (`2607.25091`).** Repeat the stabilization-factor ablation across several model–corpus pairs and seeds, then add blinded human judgments, downstream task success, longer training, and multi-turn tool environments.

10. **Measure reliability, not only aggregate efficiency (`2607.24904`, `2607.24957`, `2607.25669`).** Repeat benchmark runs across seeds and input perturbations, reporting per-example consistency, confidence intervals, and quality–latency–memory Pareto curves rather than only mean accuracy.
