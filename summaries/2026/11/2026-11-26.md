# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-26

## Executive summary

- **Passing a check can hide a defect.** NOPO uses rejections from partial verifiers as training evidence, the text-to-SQL study audits rewards on hidden database variants, and AIChipBench checks committed hardware state before errors reach the output (`2611.17287`, `2611.34363`, `2611.33093`). The SQL audit found clearer gains in grading reliability than in policy training.
- **Agent benchmarks are measuring the path as well as the outcome.** DetourBench distinguishes efficient success, recoverable failure, and irreversible error. ROAM-Bench separately checks whether a spatial answer was backed by required observations. EgoUseBench finds a large final-state success gap between first-person browser tasks and their resolved-text versions (`2611.20844`, `2611.33143`, `2611.34466`).
- **When an assistant acts is an evidence question.** A cooking assistant trained on time-matched examples exceeded video-blind baselines under the official per-step protocol. AIRIS found that models could recognize social cues yet commit to an intent prematurely (`2611.30895`, `2611.34347`).
- **Methods that revise agents change different parts of the system.** Elite evolves a coding scaffold without downstream task scores in its selection loop. SEEK carries forward robot programs and measured action effects. RoboEvo² uses verified repairs to update both the harness and the learned controller (`2611.21056`, `2611.33915`, `2611.34675`).
- **Robot-video studies are testing whether forecasts respond to actions.** SimPWM changes the object motion supplied to a renderer while replaying the arm motion. WorldReforge trains on perturbed grasps. DemoFoundry asks whether rendered observations improve physical policy learning when action trajectories are held fixed (`2611.32786`, `2611.33925`, `2611.34671`).
- **Visual artifact production benefits from inspecting what was rendered.** SlidesWeaver preserves editable slide elements, VibeVideoAgent reports perceptual gains across all three tested coding models, and Play2Evolve improves some game-quality measures while strict success on GameASG-Bench stays flat after refinement (`2611.28010`, `2611.34237`, `2611.33457`).
- **Efficiency results depend on what was timed or matched.** DyLoMo-DiT matches average forward compute, MoLT matches input tokens in its language-quality comparison, and LiveVAE times the encoder and decoder on one H100. These settings answer different questions about the cost of inference (`2611.30844`, `2611.34923`, `2611.34912`).

## Main results worth noting

- **Reward quality and policy quality can diverge.** In `2611.34363`, hidden-copy grading reduced full credit for buggy SQL and constructed hacks versus single-database grading in a synthetic audit. In the controlled GRPO comparison with three seeds per arm, the mean counterfactual reward produced no in-domain accuracy gain over the original-database reward, and the exploratory Spider transfer interval spans zero.
- **Resolved instructions made a larger difference than input delivery timing in the matched browser comparison.** In `2611.34466`, across six models on 200 matched tasks, mean final-state success was 63.4% with observations and requests arriving during execution, 64.2% with prerecorded context upfront, and 86.1% with resolved text. The reported means describe the gap without establishing the precision of the online–offline difference.
- **The coding scaffold and robot policy results use different evaluation units.** `2611.21056` raised mean DeepSeek-V4-Flash task success from 60.0% to 85.6% on a fixed 60-task SWE-bench Pro subset, relative to its initial scaffold. `2611.34675` raised standalone manipulation success from 11.0% to 84.1% relative to its starting policy on 800 unseen LIBERO-PRO episodes after eight repair rounds without additional human demonstrations.
- **Direct comparisons give a clearer view of what changes with extra training.** `2611.30965` averaged a 4.79-point accuracy gain over TTPO with self-consistency at matched generated-token budgets across five competition-math benchmarks and three Qwen3 sizes. `2611.33925` reduced mean object trajectory error by 28.2% versus pretrained Cosmos3 Nano on Reforge-Eval, including a 35.02% reduction on objects unseen during post-training.
- **Two imaging results show why the evaluation setting belongs beside the metric.** `2611.32002` improved PSNR in 56/60 matched reduced-resolution backbone–sensor comparisons versus the interpolated multispectral base, with less consistent full-resolution gains. `2611.34912` cut median H3 VAE encoder latency by factors of 5.2–6.2 and decoder latency by 3.0–3.3 versus the original VAE at 480p–2160p on one H100, while reconstruction PSNR improved and LPIPS worsened.
- **Aggregation can obscure the task people care about.** `2611.34575` raised the base model’s mean score from 52.24% to 58.26% across 31 public multimodal benchmarks, while whole-case clinical workflows remained difficult. `2611.33457` improved GameCraft-Bench scores and human clip ratings over initialization and adapted Play2Code, while strict GameASG-Bench success stayed flat after refinement.

## Open research directions

1. **Measure when more reliable grading improves learning.** Compare the original-database reward, mean and strict hidden-copy rewards, and a rejection-only objective on the same SQL training tasks. Prespecify held-out semantic accuracy and false acceptance, then repeat across independent seeds. (`2611.17287`, `2611.34363`)
2. **Score evidence at the moment of commitment.** Vary when a decisive observation arrives while keeping task goals fixed. Measure premature commitment, justified success, clarification cost, and eventual success separately in replay and live interaction. (`2611.30895`, `2611.33143`, `2611.34347`, `2611.34466`)
3. **Test confidence gates where people can decline or ignore their output.** Freeze thresholds fitted on one language or conversation split, then measure match quality, coverage, and unwanted interruptions on held-out languages and ordinary turns with and without a subsequent edit request. (`2611.21155`, `2611.33307`)
4. **Isolate the value of recoverable agent history.** At equal active context, token, and wall-clock budgets, compare expandable summaries, summaries with archived records disabled, and recent-history truncation. Include tasks that require an older fact after an intervening revision. (`2611.28231`, `2611.28244`, `2611.34147`)
5. **Validate robot forecasts against matched physical interventions.** Execute paired nominal and perturbed grasps from matched resets. Score contact, object trajectory, and completion in the physical scene, simulator, and generated video, then test whether forecast errors predict downstream policy failures. (`2611.32786`, `2611.33925`, `2611.34671`)
6. **Measure when robot improvement pays for its development cost.** Repeat entire development runs with matched initial policies and budgets, then test unseen layouts, held-out tasks, and repeated hardware trials. Report cumulative development compute and per-deployment success and latency. (`2611.33144`, `2611.33915`, `2611.34675`, `2611.34750`)
7. **Compare loop schedules at equal training compute and deployment load.** Within each model family, sweep shared traversal counts at equal training FLOPs. Report task quality and measured latency, throughput, and peak memory across batch sizes on the same hardware. (`2611.30844`, `2611.34923`, `2610.26030`)
8. **Measure video continuity and latency in the same run.** Vary camera overlap, revisit gaps, and shot deadlines. Record time to first output, tail latency and playback stalls alongside identity, location, and object-motion consistency. Test an H3 VAE swap only after verifying interface compatibility. (`2611.32473`, `2611.34064`, `2611.34907`, `2611.34912`)
