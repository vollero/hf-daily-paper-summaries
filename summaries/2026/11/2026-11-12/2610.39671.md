# Incentivizing Self-Learning in Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39671) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39671)


## One-sentence takeaway
RL combining exploration incentives and round-level tool credit raises textbook-assisted proof accuracy from 51.5% to 79.5% on the authors’ evaluation, with smaller, selective gains on external tasks (§§3.2–3.5).

## Problem and motivation
The authors study how LLMs can autonomously acquire and apply knowledge from long reference materials while solving unfamiliar problems. They identify a gap between retrieving relevant facts or reasoning over supplied context and deciding what to study as a derivation evolves. Textbook exercises provide a setting where definitions and general results remain available, but exercise solutions are withheld (§§1–2.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Textbook-grounded task collection:** A pipeline separates exposition, exercises, and reference solutions, producing 5,183 proof problems from 158 mathematics textbooks (§§2.2, 3.1).
- **Interactive study interface:** BookNavigator provides table-of-contents access, keyword search, and full-section reading, allowing study to alternate with reasoning (§2.3).
- **Fine-grained RL:** GRPO receives a correctness-gated, annealed bonus for successful textbook access. A frozen initial-policy judge identifies harmful interactions; centered round-level credit redistributes advantages while preserving their token-weighted mean. Useful and neutral interactions receive identical numerical scores (§§2.4, 3.1).
- **Transfer and attribution experiments:** Held-out books, held-out subjects, reference interventions, and external benchmarks test whether gains extend beyond familiar textbook content (§§3.2–3.5).

## Evidence and main results

Mathematics results use LLM-judged proof accuracy, averaged over four independently sampled solutions per problem; acceptance requires all eight GLM-5.2 reviews to approve (§3.1; Appendix C.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large overall improvement | Self-Learning increases from **51.5% to 79.5%** overall proof accuracy; trained Top-k Sections reaches **73.1%**, a **6.4 percentage-point** gap. | §3.2, Table 1 discussion |
| Transfer across books and subjects | Trained Self-Learning reaches **85.2% OOD-Book** and **86.7% OOD-Subject**, exceeding trained Top-k Sections by **4.2** and **5.4 points**, respectively. | §3.2, Table 1 discussion |
| Gains include stronger unaided reasoning | The Self-Learning-trained policy improves No-Book accuracy from **55.7% to 71.7%**; paired-textbook access reaches **79.5%**, adding **7.8 points**. | §3.3, Table 2 discussion |
| Relevant reference content matters | Trained-policy accuracy is **75.2%** with a topic-matched book, **74.6%** after source-neighborhood removal, and **79.8%** after matched-count non-source removal. | §3.3, Table 2 discussion |
| External transfer is modest and selective | BrowseComp-Plus score reaches **51.9%**, **+1.7 points** over Base, using BM25 retrieval; CL-bench reaches **17.1%**, **+2.3 points**, using supplied context without tools. All RL variants decline on CL-bench Procedural Task Execution. | §3.4, Table 3 discussion |
| Correctness alone suppresses tool use | Correctness-only RL raises proof accuracy from **51.5% to 74.9%**, while tool-using trajectories fall from **36.9% to 6.7%** and calls per trajectory from **1.5 to 0.3**. | §3.5, Table 4 discussion |
| Exploration bonus improves OOD performance | Bonus-only RL produces **79.9%** tool-using trajectories and improves OOD-Book/OOD-Subject accuracy by **2.0/2.9 points** over correctness-only RL; the full combination performs best on every mathematics split. | §3.5, Table 4 discussion |

The results support improved textbook-assisted solving and some transfer under the tested settings. Reference interventions strengthen the case that external information contributes beyond unaided reasoning. They do not establish a universally domain-agnostic learning mechanism: external gains are selective, and the authors’ explanations of learned study strategies remain interpretations rather than direct measurements.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed boundary:** Every RL variant underperforms Base on procedural execution. The proposed mismatch with proof-oriented training is a hypothesis (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited generality:** All training runs start from one base model, and held-out subjects are probability and statistics; broader model and domain generality remains untested (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** Proof correctness relies on GLM-5.2 rather than reported human or formal verification. Eight-review unanimity is conservative, but judge accuracy is not validated in the supplied text (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confounding:** Main protocol comparisons vary both interaction format and reward design. The within-protocol ablation helps isolate reward components, but a fixed-retrieval baseline with matched process supervision is not reported (§§3.1, 3.5).
- **Analyst inference—resource comparability:** Interactive trajectories have a total token cap, while fixed-retrieval baselines have separate input and output allowances. Actual compute, latency, and judging costs are unavailable (§3.1).
- **Reporting gaps:** Table captions and narrative results are present, but table bodies are absent. Confidence intervals, training-seed variability, release details, and pretraining-overlap checks are not supplied.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the method generalize across base models?** Only one initialization is tested. Repeat the reward ablation across model families and sizes with matched budgets.
2. **How reliable is the proof-accuracy signal?** Judge agreement with independent verification is unreported. Audit a stratified sample using expert graders or formal verification where feasible.
3. **Which interactions cause the gains?** Reference interventions show content dependence but do not isolate adaptive reading from all training differences. Compare adaptive and fixed retrieval with matched content, tokens, and supervision.
4. **Can procedural reliability improve alongside knowledge acquisition?** Procedural execution declines despite aggregate transfer gains. Test mixed proof-and-procedure training and evaluate both CL-bench category scores and mathematics retention.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods and narrative results support the central findings, but complete numerical tables, uncertainty estimates, independent judge validation, compute costs, pretraining-contamination analysis, and public artifact availability are missing.
