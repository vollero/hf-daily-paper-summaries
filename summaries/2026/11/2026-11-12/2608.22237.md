# Read Less, Solve More: Token-Efficient Sparse Reading of External Content for AI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22237) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22237)


## One-sentence takeaway
SparseRead controls which external evidence enters an agent’s context, reporting median token savings of 61.4% and wall-time savings of 53.7% versus native agents, with task scores maintained or improved in 29 of 30 model–scenario settings (§6.2).

## Problem and motivation
Agents often receive entire documents, files, or tool responses when their next decision needs only a few passages. Unnecessary content can persist across later calls, increasing cost and potentially obscuring relevant evidence (§§1–2).

The authors identify a gap between retrieval or compression and coordinated reading control: agents need a common interface for changing evidence needs, inspected-source coverage, cumulative exposure budgets, and full-view fallback across different artifact types (§3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Stateful Read Intent Protocol:** Each access specifies current evidence needs. An EvidencePack returns anchored evidence, processed scope, exposure size, and unresolved coverage slots. Artifact fingerprints invalidate stale evidence; the agent retains responsibility for sufficiency and stopping (§4.1).
- **Interchangeable Reader Backends:** A common interface supports passage retrieval, structured projections, compact full views, and exact source operations. Configuration selection minimizes estimated cost subject to calibrated evidence reliability (§4.2; Appendix E).
- **Budgeted Read Gate:** Sparse payload is checked before delivery against a fixed episode budget. If another intact batch would exceed it, the system supplies a faithful full view. This bounds unweighted artifact exposure, rather than total inference cost (§4.3).
- **Framework integration and evaluation:** A shared runtime connects to NanoBot, OpenCode, and OpenClaw through adapters without model retraining (§§5–6).

The distinctive contribution is the combined reading-control contract; retrieval and compression remain replaceable acquisition components.

## Evidence and main results

All empirical findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad efficiency gains with occasional quality loss | NanoBot, six models × five scenarios: tokens reduced 15.7–92.9%, wall time 23.0–89.0%; medians 61.4% and 53.7%. Scores maintained or improved in 29/30 settings; GLM-5.1 regressed on scientific scripting. | §6.2; Fig. 6 |
| Web-search gains include higher scores | All six models versus Naive: tokens reduced 31.0–43.0%, time 23.0–37.0%, and scores increased 5–14 percentage points. | §6.2; Fig. 6 |
| Competitive rather than uniformly cheapest | Across 15 settings covering three scenarios and five models, SparseRead maintained or improved score versus Naive; it had the fewest tokens in six settings and shortest time in nine among six methods. Static RAG was faster in every Web setting. | §6.3; Fig. 7 |
| Portability includes quality regressions | Across three frameworks and six models, token use and time fell in all 18 settings; scores were maintained or improved in 15. Three score decreases ranged from 0.013 to 0.031. | §6.4; Table 1 |
| Explicit intents improve reading diagnostics | Combining session and access intents versus no intent reduced repeated reads from 4.8 to 1.5 per task and missing-evidence answer failures from 25% to 5%. | §6.5; Fig. 8(a) |
| Hybrid retrieval improves evidence hits | On 47 LooGLE documents and 741 questions, Hybrid achieved 65.3% hit rate versus BM25’s 60.1%, at median exposures of 1,490 versus 1,497 characters per collect. A hit required at least 80% annotated-evidence coverage. | §6.5; Fig. 8(b) |
| Gate bounds raw exposure under specified conditions | For a fixed-version episode and fixed full view of length \(L\), cumulative artifact exposure is at most \(2L\), excluding separately bounded previews and protocol metadata. | §4.3, Proposition 4.1; Appendix A |

The evidence supports substantial savings across the evaluated systems, with frequent quality preservation or improvement. It does not establish universal quality preservation, minimum cost against every baseline, or a bound on billed tokens, latency, context replay, or whole-session exposure.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated ablation gap:** The Reader experiment compares location methods; it does not isolate adaptive configuration selection against fixed configurations (§6.5; Appendix E).
- **Author-stated guarantee boundaries:** The exposure bound applies per episode and excludes previews and protocol metadata. Fallback may also reduce prefix-cache reuse (§§4, 4.3).
- **Evaluation scope:** BrowseComp-Plus uses a fixed corpus. Scientific evaluation covers adapted LAMMPS script generation, without the complete simulation-execution and physical-validation evaluation (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Baseline comparisons evaluate complete systems with different reading contracts and backend capabilities, so they cannot attribute all gains specifically to the protocol (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Main scenario task counts, score-checker details, and statistical uncertainty are unavailable. Related questions can share source documents, limiting any assumption of independent observations (Appendix B).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Figure 9’s caption names DeepSeek-V4-Pro, while Appendix C describes DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Figure 7’s caption also gives inconsistent comparison dimensions. Appendix C evaluates an earlier Gate policy, distinct from current budget enforcement.

## Open research questions
- **Does adaptive selection outperform a strong fixed Reader?** The paper explicitly leaves this unisolated. Compare adaptive and fixed configurations with identical protocols, Gates, artifacts, and budgets.
- **What causes the quality regressions?** Aggregate results identify losses without explaining their mechanisms. Audit failed trajectories and replay them with controlled evidence expansion.
- **How do exposure limits translate into production cost?** The guarantee excludes replay, billing, and latency. Measure cache-aware costs over long sessions with multiple episodes and source updates.
- **Do scientific gains preserve executable correctness?** The adaptation omits full physical validation. Evaluate generated scripts through simulation execution and the omitted validation stages.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the mechanism and aggregate findings, but does not provide the numerical contents of Table 1 or plotted figures, main evaluation task counts, detailed scoring procedures, main-run variability, complete calibration-group counts and overhead constants, or a confirmed public implementation release. Caption inconsistencies further limit precise interpretation.
