# Bits or permutations? Spending a fixed inference budget on weight precision or on option-order averaging in small local language models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14413) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14413)


## One-sentence takeaway
On two small Qwen3 models under simulated quantization and a weight-traffic cost proxy, retaining 6-bit weights before adding cyclic option-order averages gives the best tested budget trade-off, but this does not establish a deployment latency advantage (§§10–14).

## Problem and motivation
Weight quantization saves storage, while averaging answers across reordered options can reduce positional bias. The authors ask whether quantization increases order sensitivity beyond ordinary accuracy degradation, and whether a fixed inference budget is better spent on additional weight precision or additional reordered passes (§1).

Their identified gap is the joint measurement and pricing of these interventions. Their novelty search covered titles in one paper index, so priority remains tentative (§13).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint diagnostic study:** Evaluate Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B with fp16 and simulated 8-, 6-, 4-, and 3-bit round-to-nearest quantization. Each model receives 300 items from each of four benchmarks under eight option orders. Measurements separate answer flips, answer-slot skew, accuracy, and calibration (§2).
- **Controls for degradation:** Compare against an accuracy-matched independent-error baseline and Gaussian weight perturbations matched to quantization error power per weight group. These test different explanations for increased sensitivity (§6).
- **Budget comparison:** Price a configuration by weight bytes read multiplied by sequential passes, construct accuracy-cost frontiers, and cross-fit a precision/order decision table (§§10–11).

The authors explicitly identify the joint diagnostics and budget comparison as their proposed contributions; quantization and permutation averaging themselves are established methods (§13).

## Evidence and main results

Unless stated otherwise, results pool four equally weighted tasks: 1,200 items per model. Reported intervals are paired item-bootstrap 95% intervals (§2.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Six-bit RTN preserves measured one-pass accuracy | Versus fp16: **+1.8 points** [1.0, 2.5] for 0.6B and **+0.9** [0.3, 1.5] for 1.7B. Authors leave the gains unexplained and interpret them as “no loss.” | §4 |
| Four-bit RTN increases slot skew, especially in the smaller model | Slot-skew total variation: **0.146→0.526** for 0.6B and **0.066→0.125** for 1.7B, fp16→4-bit. Matched-noise skews are **0.147/0.372** across two 0.6B draws and **0.186** for one full 1.7B draw. | Table 2; §6.3 |
| Increased flips do not exceed the chosen degradation baseline | At 4 bits, observed minus accuracy-matched baseline flip rates: **−5.1 points** [−6.2, −4.0] and **−5.3** [−7.5, −2.9], respectively. Baseline errors are independent and uniform. | Table 3 |
| Four-order averaging recovers accuracy but leaves residual sensitivity | At 4 bits, mean-probability averaging adds **12.6 points** [10.5, 14.7] for 0.6B and **4.9** [3.8, 6.1] for 1.7B. Residual cross-orbit flip rates remain **0.310/0.187**, versus **0.193/0.153** for averaged fp16. | §4; Table 2 |
| Moderate quantization plus averaging beats a single fp16 pass under the proxy | Six-bit, two-order averaging costs **0.78** normalized fp16 passes and gains **4.1 points** [3.2, 5.1] for 0.6B and **3.2** [2.5, 4.0] for 1.7B versus fp16 once. | Table 6 |
| Averaging improves measured calibration | At 4 bits, ECE with ten equal-width bins falls from **0.332→0.087** for 0.6B and **0.284→0.086** for 1.7B, single order→four-order mean. | Table 5 |
| Cross-fitted budget choices favor six bits once affordable | Both item halves select the same configuration at every tested budget: six bits with increasing order counts when affordable; four bits once at the lowest tested budget. | Table 8 |

The evidence supports a trade-off within this model, quantizer, prompt, and benchmark grid. It does **not** isolate a rounding-specific cause of slot bias, demonstrate real hardware savings, or establish that six bits is generally optimal. Lower flip rates also do not necessarily mean more correct answers (§§6, 10–14).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors:** Only two sizes from one model family, one fixed prompt without a chat template or thinking phase, and four-option questions are evaluated. Cyclic shifts and reversals leave other ordering effects untested (§14).
- **Authors:** Quantization is simulated with a simple quantizer; no packed weights or low-bit kernels run. Activations, embeddings, and the output matrix remain fp16 (§§2.2, 14).
- **Authors:** The cost proxy omits activation traffic, dequantization, prefix sharing, and batching. Counting the full fp16 embedding/output matrix changes a six-bit pass from approximately **0.39** to **0.55/0.50** fp16-pass units for the two models (§14).
- **Authors:** Few noise draws, fixed item samples, and multiple comparisons limit causal and statistical interpretation. The six-bit gain is unexplained; five-bit weights were not evaluated (§§4, 11, 14).
- **Analyst inference:** The blanket advice to use four bits below the six-bit threshold needs an affordability boundary: Table 1 prices a four-bit pass at **0.266**, so it cannot fit every smaller budget.
- **Reproducibility:** Scripts, seeds, revisions, and cache filenames are described, but the supplied text provides no public artifact location (§15).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the precision–averaging crossover survive stronger quantizers?** Only RTN is tested. Repeat the same grid with alternative quantizers and an evaluated five-bit configuration (§§11, 14).
2. **Which configuration wins on actual hardware?** No latency or energy measurements support the proxy. Benchmark packed inference with sequential and batched orders, including output-matrix and activation costs (§14).
3. **Is slot skew driven by perturbation structure or overall damage?** Matched-noise results vary across draws and models. Run more perturbation draws and compare skew at matched accuracy as well as matched SNR (§6.3).
4. **Does the rule generalize across prompting and ordering protocols?** Text generation, chat templates, and other permutations are untested. Repeat the comparison across those protocols and additional model families (§14).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the within-protocol findings clearly, but deployment and broader generalization remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes public code/cache access, actual packed-inference performance, evaluated five-bit results, broader model and prompt coverage, sufficient noise repetitions, and an explanation of the six-bit accuracy gain.
