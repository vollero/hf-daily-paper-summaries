# GTAGym: Real AAA Game Tasks Environment for Evaluation and Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13028) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13028)


## One-sentence takeaway
GTAGym combines automatically scored, GTA V mission-derived tasks with configurable RL environment generation, showing navigation gains from targeted training while exposing persistent weaknesses in dynamic control and real-time interaction.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a gap between realistic commercial-game interaction, automatic outcome verification, and generation of executable training tasks. Existing approaches provide these capabilities separately; GTAGym aims to combine them for evaluating screenshot-driven agents over extended interactions (§1–2).

The motivation is to measure whether agents actually complete goals requiring spatial reasoning, coordinated control, and adaptation to moving actors, rather than merely answer questions about gameplay.

## Approach and contributions
- **Mission-derived environment and benchmark:** The authors manually decompose 75 GTA V main-story missions and reconstruct selected objectives in Babylon.js. GTAVBench contains 103 interactive 3D tasks and 47 image-based shooting/sniping tasks. Online outcomes use engine-state predicates; image tasks use annotated hit regions (§3.1–3.2, Appendix A.2).
- **Two evaluation protocols:** GTAVBench provides textual instructions and bounded screenshot history. GTAVBench-AGI places the objective only in the initial screenshot and uses Codex history compression. Both share tasks, actions, and scoring (§3.3).
- **Parameterized training environments:** Fixed templates expose constrained parameters such as routes, movement modes, and actor speeds, saving resolved configurations and seeds. Shooting generation samples existing image–target pairs and aiming offsets; sniping remains evaluation-only (§3.4, Appendix B).
- **RL demonstration:** Qwen3.8-27B receives GRPO training through LoRA on generated on-foot tasks, followed by evaluation on held-out benchmark tasks and native GTA V (§4.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best model remains below experienced humans | Best configuration achieves 70.9% overall accuracy over 150 tasks versus 89.8% mean accuracy for three experienced players: an 18.9 percentage-point gap. | §4.2; Appendix A.5; Table 2 caption, with cells unavailable |
| AGI protocol improves aggregate performance | Across six models on the same tasks, overall accuracy increases by 0.9–10.0 percentage points relative to GTAVBench; aiming-category changes are mixed. | §4.2 |
| On-foot RL improves held-out navigation and overall accuracy | Qwen3.8-27B trained on 50 generated on-foot instances: held-out on-foot accuracy rises from 54.2% to 72.9%, and overall accuracy from 34.2% to 38.9%, averaged across three seeds. | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Transfer across categories is uneven | OOD-category accuracy rises from 31.8% to 34.8%; road driving remains 3.3%, aerial navigation and interception remain 0%, while shooting declines from 73.6% to 68.1%. | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Native-game transfer is positive but limited | On 100 native GTA V navigation/interaction tasks, recorded success increases from 15% to 23% after RL; inference is paused and videos are manually verified. | §4.3; Appendix B.3 |
| Control-related errors dominate annotated first errors | Calibration and Regulation comprise 63.2% of 565 categorized first errors drawn from 900 JSON-protocol trajectories. | §5.2; Appendix C.1 |
| Shorter actions improve accuracy at higher interaction cost | Sol with Codex on 103 online tasks: reducing the action cap from 1 s to 0.5 s raises accuracy from 62.1% to 66.3%, while mean rounds rise from 62.6 to 121.2. | §5.4 |
| Inference-time pausing materially changes results | For Sol with Codex on those 103 tasks at a 1 s cap, unpaused accuracy falls from 62.1% to 39.8%; following, interception, and evasion reach zero success. | §5.4; Figure 7 |

These results support the authors’ claims that generated navigation training can improve task completion and that dynamic control remains difficult. They do not establish broad competence across commercial games or physical-world transfer. The protocol comparison changes instruction presentation, history management, and harness together, so its gains cannot be attributed specifically to compression.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described scope:** Online benchmark tasks reconstruct selected mission requirements with adapted geometry and predefined actor behavior; they are not native GTA V missions (§3.2).
- **Author-observed weaknesses:** RL gains do not resolve driving, aerial control, or interception, and shooting performance declines (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Training and benchmark routes are spatially separated but share the city and controllers. The experiment tests instance generalization more directly than generalization to new environment mechanics (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—timing dependence:** Default inference-time pausing removes environmental evolution during model deliberation. The unpaused ablation shows that default accuracy can substantially overstate real-time capability (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Humans use direct gameplay controls, whereas models use timed screenshot–action turns; the human gap therefore includes interface differences (Appendix A.3, A.5).
- **Annotation uncertainty:** Recovery analysis counts truncated and inconclusive cases as unrecovered. Annotator agreement is not reported in the supplied text (Appendix C.2).
- **Incomplete reporting in retrieval:** Many tables appear only as captions, preventing inspection of complete scores, deviations, configurations, and task distributions.

## Open research questions
- **Which protocol component produces the gains?** Instruction modality, memory policy, and harness change jointly. A factorial experiment holding two components fixed while varying the third could isolate their effects (§3.3, §5.3).
- **Can training improve dynamic control without harming aiming?** Navigation-only RL leaves several control categories unchanged and reduces shooting accuracy. Compare navigation-only training against a mixed navigation, driving, interception, and aiming curriculum (§4.3).
- **Do improvements survive new layouts and controllers?** Current generated training shares the benchmark city and controllers. Evaluate trained policies in held-out cities with altered vehicle dynamics and actor policies (Appendix B.1).
- **Can faster decision cycles restore unpaused performance?** The timing ablation implicates latency but does not isolate it experimentally. Sweep controlled inference delays and action caps on the same dynamic tasks (§5.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the central methods and results, but numerous table cells and plotted values are absent.

Material missing information includes full per-model results and uncertainty, complete reasoning-effort configurations, RL update count and total training cost, reward-component ablations, native-game evaluation uncertainty and reviewer agreement, and confirmed public access to code, benchmark instances, and trained adapters.
