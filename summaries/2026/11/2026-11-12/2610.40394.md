# Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40394) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40394)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that textbook-derived proof supervision improves mathematical reasoning on a shared Qwen backbone, with controlled experiments supporting additional benefits from explicit proof-gap targets, structured retrieval, curriculum order, and broader source coverage (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Most mathematical instruction corpora organize supervision as isolated problem–solution pairs, losing textbook progression and proposition dependencies. The authors seek to preserve these signals while making omitted intermediate arguments explicit, enabling both proof training and structure-aware retrieval. Their claimed contribution combines broad textbook coverage with local proof obligations and retained chapter/reference metadata; preserving proof references alone is already represented in prior work (§2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured corpus construction:** TOME-Math contains 380,292 QA records from 2,118 textbooks across 29 subject categories: 205,861 Proof QA and 174,431 Gap QA records. OCR and model-assisted parsing recover chapters, separate exercises from exposition, extract propositions and proofs, and resolve dependencies (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Explicit local proof obligations:** A proof-gap audit identifies omitted arguments, which are rewritten into self-contained propositions with completed proofs. Structural checks and source-conditioned quality judgments assess the resulting records (§3.1; Appendix A.4).
- **Evaluation assets:** TOME-LongForm provides book-held-out, proof-heavy exercises; TOME-Objective evaluates final-answer correctness. Public proof benchmarks are evaluated as natural-language proof tasks, rather than through formal verification (§4.1; Appendix D).
- **Uses of retained structure:** Experiments test dependency-guided retrieval, forward chapter curricula with Gap-first ordering, matched-budget source breadth, and cross-subject transfer (§5). These controlled studies are more informative about structural effects than the heterogeneous full-corpus comparison.

The supplied text describes these assets but does not establish their public availability.

## Evidence and main results

All performance figures below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full-corpus SFT improves aggregate performance | On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, one epoch over the 364,631-example training split raises the unweighted mean of six benchmark pass@1 scores from 29.17% to 45.83%, a 16.66 percentage-point gain. Authors report best performance on five benchmarks and second on AIME2026. | §4.2; Table 2 description; Appendix E.3 |
| Structured retrieval improves frozen-model performance | On 300 TOME-LongForm problems, pass@1 is 13.25% closed-book, 14.00% random same-book, 14.71% local-chapter, 15.54% proposition-only, and 17.38% propositions plus gaps. Context-augmented policies share a token budget; each problem has 16 responses. | §5.1; Table 3 description; Appendix F |
| Curriculum order helps under matched supervision | Across six subjects, at respective per-subject target-token budgets of 0.75M, 1.8M, and 2.4M, macro pass@16 is 41.04%, 42.52%, and 43.21% for Shuffle versus 45.38%, 48.98%, and 50.93% for Forward–Gap. Each subject–scale cell uses identical examples and training settings. | §5.2; Figure 4; Appendix G |
| Broader sources help at fixed budget | Distributing the same 0.75M-token Forward–Gap budget across 16 rather than eight books improves pass@16 by 1.6–4.8 percentage points across subjects, averaging 3.3 points. Scores average five evaluation runs per checkpoint. | §5.3; Figure 5(a); Appendix G |
| Cross-subject performance approaches target specialists | For subject-specific 0.75M-token checkpoints, mean target-normalized pass@16 is 0.92 across 30 ordered subject pairs; eight ratios exceed 1.0. Each target set contains 50 held-out exercises. This is a ratio, not an absolute correctness rate. | §5.3; Figure 5(b); Appendix H |
| Gap QA contributes to the full recipe | Removing Gap QA lowers pass@1 by 5.05–6.77 percentage points across the four proof benchmarks. Replacing Gap-first with within-stage mixing costs 3.74 points on TOME-LongForm and 2.59 on TOME-Objective. | §5.4; Table 4 description |
| Source traceability remains weak in reported audits | Groundedness and evidence alignment each pass below 50% of records and have a 28.6% fail rate; the “no verbatim evidence” review flag covers 48.4% of the corpus. These measure traceability concerns, not directly mathematical incorrectness. | Appendix B.3; Figures 11–12 |

The matched-example curriculum and matched-budget retrieval/breadth studies support benefits within their tested settings. The full-corpus comparison does not isolate structure causally: corpora differ in content and scale, and one epoch does not equalize training tokens or computation. Moreover, benchmark tables are supplied only as captions, so individual scores and uncertainty estimates cannot be inspected.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-acknowledged confounding:** The full-corpus comparison cannot by itself attribute gains to textbook structure (§4.2).
- **Reported data-quality concerns:** Source-grounding audits identify substantial traceability problems, while full-corpus training retains all quality tiers (Appendices B.3, E.1). **Analyst inference:** downstream gains do not establish that individual records are reliably source-grounded.
- **Verifier validity remains unresolved:** Appendix D.4 describes human calibration, but the supplied Table 8 has no numerical results. False acceptance, false rejection, and agreement with human model rankings therefore remain unavailable.
- **Limited demonstrated scope:** All experiments use one backbone, and controlled textbook studies cover six subjects (§§4.1, 5). **Analyst inference:** generalization across architectures, sizes, languages, and less represented subjects is unestablished.
- **Formal correctness is not demonstrated:** MiniF2F and ProofNet scores assess generated natural-language proofs using an LLM judge (Appendix D.1).
- **Construction provenance is unclear:** Model assignments differ between §3.1 and §3.2/Appendix B.4. Appendix A.5 acknowledges versioned allocation changes, but does not fully reconcile the final stream-level assignments.
- **Ordering effects depend on setting:** Chapter direction dominates the subject-specific study (§5.2), whereas Gap-first dominates the reported full-corpus ordering ablation (§5.4). Neither result establishes a universal hierarchy of curriculum components.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the reported rankings survive human assessment?** Calibration outcomes are missing. A next experiment would publish the weighted human audit and compare model rankings under human and automatic judgments.
2. **How much benefit comes from Gap QA content versus its generation process?** Removing Gap QA changes supervision content, and the streams have different generator assignments. A next experiment would match tokens, subjects, and generators while replacing gaps with additional proposition proofs.
3. **When does chapter direction matter more than Gap-first ordering?** The subject-specific and full-corpus studies emphasize different components. A next experiment would run a matched-budget factorial ordering study across single-subject and multidisciplinary training.
4. **Does structure help beyond this backbone and source-aware retrieval setting?** Only one backbone is tested, and retrieval knows the source book. A next experiment would repeat the controls on another backbone and retrieve across books without source identity.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main reported trends and experimental controls, but independent assessment is limited by missing table contents.

Material unavailable information includes individual benchmark scores and confidence intervals, numerical human-verifier calibration results, complete optimizer/decoding settings from caption-only tables, exact retrieval ranking and leakage-filter rules, reconciled final generator provenance, and public access/licensing details for TOME-Math and its evaluation assets.
