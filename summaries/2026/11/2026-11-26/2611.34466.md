# EgoUseBench: Benchmarking Computer-Use Agents under Evolving First-Person Contexts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34466) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34466)


## One-sentence takeaway

On 200 matched browser tasks, six models averaged 63.4% final-state success with first-person observations and requests delivered during execution, 64.2% with prerecorded context supplied upfront, and 86.1% with resolved text instructions (§4.3, Appendix C.2).

## Problem and motivation

Wearable cameras let users refer to objects and activities they can see without describing them in full. The wearer’s viewpoint can leave the intended target ambiguous, and later observations or requests can invalidate browser actions whose effects persist. The authors identify a gap in evaluating whether agents can complete browser tasks while both first-person evidence and user requirements evolve (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Interaction framework.** The authors define *Ego Use* and distinguish instruction-only Computer-Use, upfront Offline Ego Use, and Online Ego Use with observations and requests delivered during execution (§1, Fig. 1).
- **Executable benchmark.** EgoUseBench builds 693 task versions from over 74 hours of real-world first-person recordings, including 200 matched versions per setting and 93 additional Online composite tasks. It tracks observations, requirements, and website state separately. **Graft** changes the request, **Refresh** supplies new visual evidence, and **Clarify** returns a reviewed reply when the agent asks a question (§1, §§3.1–3.3, Appendix A.1).
- **Evaluation protocol.** The checker compares terminal website state against verified acceptable outcomes. Matched comparisons and execution-trace audits examine when agents resolve references and whether they remove obsolete selections (§3.4, §§4.2–4.6, Appendices C.4 and E).

The matched comparison establishes a performance gap under the tested input protocols. The Computer-Use baseline supplies resolved final requirements, so the gap combines the demands of visual grounding, ambiguity resolution, and information timing. The paired outcomes show that near-equal Offline and Online averages can mask substantial task-level changes. Final-state success alone cannot establish that the agent resolved the referent before acting or avoided intermediate mistakes (§3.4, §4.3, Appendices C.2 and C.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-described measurement limits.** Clarify returns a fixed answer regardless of question wording, so it measures whether an agent asks and leaves question quality untested. Terminal scoring gives full credit for corrected mistakes and for correct selections made while the referent is still ambiguous (§§3.3–3.4, Appendix C.4).
- **Author-described comparison limits.** Wake-up leaves fewer decisions after delivery despite the same overall cap. Cue comparisons depend partly on whether the agent stays active long enough to receive the update. Most conditions use one run per task, and the repeated request-count experiment shows reversals across runs (§4.1, Appendices C.3 and C.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Frozen sites, step-driven replay, and scripted replies limit extrapolation to live wearable use. Place bookmarking appears only once in the matched set, and the composite evaluation covers shopping and tutorials, so aggregate results offer little evidence about place-bookmarking performance (§3.3, Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The failure breakdown is diagnostic for the labeled subset. Figure 6 covers 604 of 807 failures from four models and relies on model-judge attribution with human fallback, so its category proportions may differ from those of all failures (§4.6, Appendix E.2–E.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can explicit website-state reconciliation improve revision?** Traces show agents finding valid replacements while retaining obsolete selections. Compare the same agent with and without a post-update comparison of the current website state against revised requirements. Measure both final success and preservation of still-valid selections (Appendices C.3 and E.4).
2. **How much of the wake-up gap comes from fewer remaining decisions?** The reported comparison changes delivery mode and the remaining action budget together. Hold the pre-update website state and post-update budget fixed while varying whether the episode resumes from standby (Appendix C.3).
3. **Can agents ask questions that reliably resolve the intended referent?** Fixed replies leave question specificity untested. Compare fixed replies with human answers that depend on what the agent asks, and score whether the referent is resolved before selection alongside final-state success (§3.3, Appendix C.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark design and headline results stated explicitly in the supplied text. Several cited tables and figures appear only as captions or prose summaries. Missing details include complete model-by-subtype success rates, paired confidence intervals and adaptation rates for many contrasts, and the full task-source breakdown. The supplied text also does not establish public access, licensing, or setup instructions for the executable benchmark.
