# GOLD-SLAM: Gaussian-splatting Omnidirectional LiDAR-Depth SLAM

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32335) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32335)


## One-sentence takeaway

GOLD-SLAM fuses panoramic vision, LiDAR, and inertial measurements in a spherical Gaussian-splatting SLAM system that reduces mean radial-depth error relative to panoramic baselines on shared front views, while geometric accuracy in the LiDAR blind zone remains unmeasured.

## Problem and motivation

Ground and aerial robots need accurate poses and dense maps. Panoramic cameras increase visual overlap, but equirectangular images stretch near the poles, and LiDAR covers only part of the panorama. This leaves large regions without measured depth for map initialization. Existing Gaussians can also have enough opacity to block insertion on newly observed nearer surfaces. The authors identify a separate evaluation gap: common LiDAR–vision benchmarks underrepresent UAV motion. (§1, Fig. 1)

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified spherical SLAM.** GOLD-SLAM uses a common projection model for panoramic pose updates and Gaussian rendering. It builds on FAST-LIVO2’s filtering framework and adapts the Gaussian-LIC2 mapping backend. (Fig. 2, §§3.1–3.3)
- **Visual updates tailored to LiDAR coverage.** In blind zones, the frontend triangulates tracked features with sufficient parallax and adds uncertainty-weighted bearing residuals. In LiDAR-supported regions, Patch Brightness Uncertainty (PBU) models patch-wide gain and offset changes during direct visual updates. (§3.2, Appendices D–E)
- **Depth-guided incremental mapping.** The backend initializes Gaussians from measured LiDAR depth and LiDAR-aligned predictions from the existing Depth Any Panoramas model. Depth-Aware Map Extension and Pruning (DAEP) inserts nearer surfaces even where existing opacity is high, gives depth-conflict insertions stronger updates, and prunes Gaussians that conflict with locally consistent depth. (§3.3, Fig. 4)
- **WHOLE dataset.** The authors collected 20 indoor and outdoor campus sequences with panoramic images, LiDAR, and IMU, covering weak geometry, rapid motion, omnidirectional coverage, low light, and easy conditions. (§4.1, Fig. 5)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive localization | GOLD-SLAM had the lowest mean ATE RMSE on the selected Hilti-Oxford and M3DGR sequences, ranked second on Oxford Spires, and was comparable on M2DGR. On M3DGR Corridor02, endpoint drift was 0.274 m versus 1.319 m for FAST-LIVO2. On WHOLE, mean endpoint drift was 34% lower in the weak-geometry group and 43% lower in the high-dynamic group than for FAST-LIVO2. | §4.3, Tables 3–4 and 12–13 |
| Lower depth error and higher mean PSNR against panoramic baselines | On shared 97° front views for in-sequence novel-view evaluation, mean PSNR rose by 9.10 dB versus ODGS-SLAM and 5.56 dB versus PanoGS-SLAM. Mean Depth-L1, the radial-depth MAE against LiDAR, fell by 71.9% and 83.9%, respectively. Both baselines received GOLD-SLAM’s fused depth. Across the evaluated WHOLE groups, mean Depth-L1 was 20–33% lower than for Gaussian-LIC2. | §§4.2, 4.4, Table 5, Appendix J |
| Frontend and mapping components helped on Exp18 | On Hilti-Oxford Exp18, removing blind-zone visual updates or PBU raised ATE RMSE from 0.100 m to 0.161 m or 0.158 m, respectively. With frontend poses fixed, prediction-only mapping raised Depth-L1 from 0.450 m to 1.223 m, while removing DAEP raised LPIPS from 0.189 to 0.214. | §4.5, Table 7 |
| Depth error increased as backend LiDAR coverage shrank | On Oxford Spires Keble03 with the frontend unchanged, retaining 50% of the original backend LiDAR solid angle instead of 100% raised Depth-L1 from 0.242 m to 0.390 m and reduced PSNR by 0.33 dB. With no backend LiDAR support, Depth-L1 reached 9.797 m. | §4.5, Fig. 8 |
| Embedded replay traded image quality for feasibility | On the 16 GB Jetson Orin NX at recorded sensor speed, 4 of 37 sequences failed due to memory exhaustion. The authors report mean PSNR of 19.51 dB for the embedded configuration versus 25.31 dB for the main configuration, and mean endpoint drift of 0.333 m across the 17 completed WHOLE sequences. | Appendix A, Table 8 |

The results support better LiDAR-referenced depth consistency for the tested system configurations. Rendering quality varies by sequence, and the shared-front-view metrics leave full-panorama performance against the baselines unquantified. The panoramas in Fig. 7 provide qualitative evidence beyond the forward view. The Exp18 ablations isolate individual changes on that sequence, while the headline baseline comparisons measure the combined effect of different sensor inputs, tracking, and mapping.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Geometric evaluation is restricted to regions with LiDAR reference depth. The authors also identify panoramic stitching and temporal alignment as sources of tracking and mapping error through multi-camera parallax and synchronization errors. (§5)
- **Analyst inference.** WHOLE’s endpoint reference poses leave errors along the trajectory unmeasured. The core component ablation uses Exp18, and the backend coverage sweep uses Keble03, so the size of those effects across other scenes and sensor layouts remains uncertain. (§4.1, §4.5)
- **Analyst inference.** The baseline comparisons use method-specific camera and sensor configurations, and ODGS-SLAM has out-of-memory entries. The reported averages therefore warrant checking the matched-sequence subset and aggregation rule before comparing effect sizes. (§4.2, Table 2, Appendix J)
- **Analyst inference.** The embedded experiment establishes feasibility for recorded-data replay on completed sequences. Live onboard operation and robustness to memory exhaustion remain open. (Appendix A)

## Open research questions

1. **How accurate is geometry inside the LiDAR blind zone?** The paper lacks dense reference geometry there. A next experiment could use simulated sequences with dense ground truth and report radial-depth and surface error separately inside and outside the blind zone.
2. **How much of the localization gain comes from panoramic coverage, blind-zone bearing updates, or PBU?** The cross-system comparisons vary sensor inputs, and the individual frontend ablations use Exp18. A next experiment could vary camera coverage and toggle each update within the same frontend across weak-geometry, rapid-motion, and low-light sequences.
3. **Which DAEP operation contributes most when a nearer surface enters view?** The reported ablation removes DAEP as a bundle. A next experiment could toggle insertion, adaptive updates, and pruning independently on sequences with newly revealed surfaces, measuring surface recovery and floating artifacts.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and the reported comparisons within their stated settings. The supplied text omits several aggregate table bodies, including the detailed embedded processing rates. It also leaves unclear how failed runs enter the headline averages, whether the embedded PSNR comparison uses identical sequence subsets, and whether WHOLE or the GOLD-SLAM implementation is publicly available. Section 4.1 describes UAV-platform acquisition, while the conclusion also mentions handheld motion without specifying which sequences used it.
