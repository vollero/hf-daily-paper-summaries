# Speak When You See It: Proactive Task Assistants Should Wait for Evidence, Not the Clock

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30895) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30895)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under the official per-step protocol on two cooking benchmarks, Evidence-Contrastive Training moved a 2B video–language model beyond the paper’s video-blind frontier in all four seeds, with fewer false alarms driving most of the completion gain in a QICD replay (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation

Proactive assistants must decide when to confirm a step or flag a mistake. Early confirmations can advance users before they are ready, and false alarms can interrupt correct work. The authors show that benchmark timing rules can score well without seeing the video: annotated step starts, scoring tolerance windows, and feedback clustered at step boundaries give simple policies a shortcut (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Video-blind frontier.** The authors build an upper convex hull of completion accuracy and mistake F1 attained by fixed timing rules, instruction-based priors, and gradient-boosted policies. The frontier margin measures how much mistake F1 a model gains over the blind frontier at equal or higher completion accuracy (§3, Appendix B).
- **Evidence-Contrastive Training (ECT).** The authors fine-tune Qwen3-VL-2B with LoRA and answer-token cross-entropy on the official benchmark questions. Positive checks are paired with unfinished or mistake-free checks from another step of the same recording at equal elapsed time. This controls the clock within each pair while allowing the instruction to differ (§4, Appendix J).
- **Behavioral diagnostics.** Time-matched ranking, timestamp rescaling, recording swaps, and decision replay probe visual dependence and help explain how paired negatives change the balance between alarms and confirmations (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Blind timing can score well, and the two metrics must be read together | On Ego-MC-Bench, alarming at 25 s and confirming at 30 s yielded **0.373 mistake F1 and 0% IC-Acc**, versus a published F1 maximum of **0.27**. The paper’s frontier contained the published Ego-MC-Bench per-step results and all **12** QICD zero-shot models tested. | §3, Fig. 2, Appendix B |
| ECT exceeded the paper’s frontier on both benchmarks | Under greedy per-step decoding, all **4 seeds** crossed it. Mean IC-Acc and mistake F1 were **40.8 ± 1.3% and 0.247 ± 0.033** on QICD Advanced Planning and **59.0 ± 5.7% and 0.246 ± 0.034** on Ego-MC-Bench without adaptation (mean ± SD). Same-backbone zero-shot IC-Acc was **10.5%** and **14.4%**, respectively. | §5, Table 1, Table S6 |
| Transfer improved completion over a published same-backbone model | On Ego-MC-Bench, ECT scored **59.0% IC-Acc / 0.246 F1** on **39** released sessions. The published synthetic-plus-real-data model scored **30.4% / 0.16** on **40** sessions, with F1 revised to **0.20**. | §5, Appendix B |
| Video mattered at equal elapsed time | Across **931** QICD test pairs, ECT ranked completion pairs correctly **90–92%** of the time and mistake pairs **75–77%**, versus **65%** and **54%** for the instruction-duration heuristic. Swapping recordings on the QICD test split reduced seed 0 IC-Acc from **41.2% to 13.4%** while retaining instructions and timing. Timestamp rescaling had little effect on seed 0 in a fixed validation probe. | §6, Fig. 1, Appendices E, H |
| Paired negatives shifted the alarm–completion trade-off | In a thresholded QICD test replay using seed 0 of each variant, **7.3%** of checks on mistake-free steps raised false alarms with ECT versus **18.3%** without paired negatives. Substituting ECT’s mistake scores raised the ablation’s IC-Acc from **31.1% to 40.8%**, near ECT’s **42.0%**. In greedy Ego-MC-Bench transfer, adding paired negatives raised IC-Acc from **48.3% to 59.0%** while mistake F1 fell from **0.337 to 0.246**. | §§5–6, Table 2, Appendix F |

The results support visual dependence and better-timed completion under the tested protocol. Instruction-duration cues still predict some decisions, and stronger blind policies narrow the QICD margin. Better ranking on mistake pairs can coexist with missed errors or incorrect feedback (§7, Appendices B, E, G–H).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** The main evidence comes from cooking and an annotated-start per-step protocol. Adding plan position and earlier step durations to the blind learner moves greedy ECT’s QICD Advanced Planning margins to **−0.028 to +0.029**. In unanchored QICD streaming, ECT had higher completion accuracy and lower mistake F1 than LiveMamba, whose training data differ (§§5, 7, Appendix B).
- **Authors’ limitations.** ECT’s mistake recall was **0.18** on Ego-MC-Bench. In a manual audit of **25** credited QICD detections from one thresholded seed, **9** fully identified the annotated mistake, **3** identified it partly, and **13** described a different mistake. Scoring credits alarms by timing (§7, Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference.** The ablations support using same-recording negatives. Exact elapsed-time matching remains an open design choice because random-moment negatives also crossed the frontier and their effects varied by seed. Several mechanism analyses were retrospective, and greedy decoding was designated the main setting after both frozen decodings had run on the test sets (Appendices C–D).

## Open research questions

- **Does the gain persist in unanchored guidance outside cooking?** The primary evaluation uses cooking and annotated starts, while unanchored streaming showed a mixed metric trade-off. Evaluate matched training variants on continuous recordings from another procedural domain under the same streaming protocol (§§5, 7).
- **When does exact time matching help?** The same-recording random-moment controls produced mixed same-seed differences. Compare matched and random negatives across more seeds with equal sample counts and a fixed validation alarm budget (Appendices C–D).
- **Can ECT retain its completion gains while catching and naming more mistakes?** Timing-based F1 and the small content audit leave feedback accuracy unresolved. Collect a held-out set with independently labeled mistake onset and identity, then measure timely recall, correct identification, and completion accuracy at matched alarm budgets (§7, Appendix G).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported per-step results and the direction of the video-dependence findings. Generalization to live use remains uncertain. The supplied package contains no datasets, raw prediction logs, model weights, or evaluation code, and it provides no confirmation that the announced release occurred. It also lacks a rerun of the published Ego-MC-Bench models on the same released sessions and evidence from a live deployment with users (Appendices B, K).
