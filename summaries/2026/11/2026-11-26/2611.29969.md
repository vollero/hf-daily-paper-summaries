# CARE-3D: Reinforcement Learning for Evidence Grounded Agentic Reasoning in Situated 3D Scenes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29969) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29969)


## One-sentence takeaway

CARE-3D rewards grounding evidence that increases the likelihood of the correct answer and samples underused tools during training, improving situated 3D question answering on MSQA and Beacon3D over supervised fine-tuning and, in the Qwen3-VL-4B ablation, answer-and-format-only reinforcement learning (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Situated 3D question answering requires an agent to identify relevant objects and interpret spatial relations from a specified observer position and orientation. The authors argue that supervised traces teach a prescribed grounding call, while final-answer rewards give little credit to intermediate tool choices and can reward answers driven by textual priors. They also identify a risk that on-policy sampling drops infrequently used tools before their utility can be tested (§§1, 3–4, Figures 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Adaptive grounding policy.** SceneCOT-style supervised tuning initializes a two-turn interaction. The agent chooses a referential query and one of four tools that return ranked object matches, global geometry, viewer-relative geometry, or a highlighted crop. GRPO then updates only the agent’s LoRA adapters, with the vision-language backbone and grounding module frozen (§§4.1–4.3, 4.6–4.7).
- **Counterfactual Attribution of Returned Evidence (CARE).** The rollout policy scores the same reference answer with the returned evidence present and with it replaced by a fixed placeholder. Scoring occurs before the sampled reasoning trace. The mean token log-likelihood difference is normalized across executable rollouts for the question and added to the format and answer rewards (§§4.5–4.6, Appendix A.4).
- **Group Coverage Sampling (GCS).** Training supplements six freely sampled rollouts with one rollout for each of the two least-used tools in that group. The supplied tool identifiers are masked from the policy loss, while the coverage rollouts still contribute to group-relative advantages. At inference, the policy selects its own tool (§4.4, Appendix A.5).

## Evidence and main results

Benchmark scores below use a 0–100 scale. Beacon3D Case scores individual questions, and Obj. requires all questions for a target object to be answered correctly (§5.1, Appendix A.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Gains over matching supervised baselines across all four backbones | CARE-3D improves MSQA Overall by 3.7–7.9 points, Beacon3D Case by 4.1–5.2 points, and Beacon3D Obj. by 3.0–4.5 points. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Both components improve aggregate scores in the Qwen3-VL-4B ablation | MSQA Overall / Beacon3D Case / Obj. were 62.6 / 61.6 / 22.9 for Answer+Format GRPO, 63.2 / 62.6 / 25.4 with CARE alone, 64.7 / 62.4 / 23.7 with GCS alone, and 65.7 / 64.5 / 25.7 with both. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Least-covered tool selection beats random coverage at the same rollout budget | With six free and two coverage rollouts per prompt, selecting the least-covered tools beats random selection by 4.0 points on MSQA Overall and by 3.2 and 2.9 points on Beacon3D Case and Obj. | §5.4, Table 3 |
| Fixed evidence masking is the strongest tested scoring counterfactual | MSQA Overall / Beacon3D Case / Obj. were 65.7 / 64.5 / 25.7 with fixed masking, 62.2 / 56.5 / 13.9 with random tokens, and 21.2 / 14.5 / 2.1 with alternative-tool evidence under the same initialization and training configuration. | §5.5, Table 4 |
| CARE-3D recovers some evidence-invariant SFT failures | On the fixed MSQA subset where SFT gave the same incorrect answer with and without evidence, CARE-3D corrected 47 of 103 cases (45.6%). | §5.5, Table 5 |
| Accuracy holds up better when relevant evidence changes the correct answer | On 1,369 validation samples, accuracy under original / relevant-edited / irrelevant-edited evidence was 49.7% / 43.4% / 49.5% for SFT and 56.8% / 55.9% / 56.8% for CARE-3D, scored against the answer for each condition. | §5.5, Table 6, Appendix A.6 |

The reported scores support gains on both benchmarks, including Beacon3D, which was reserved for evaluation. The authors interpret the intervention results as stronger reliance on grounded evidence. The aggregate accuracies support that interpretation but leave open how often a prediction changed to the new correct answer on the same item. Table 6 compares full CARE-3D with SFT, so it does not isolate CARE’s contribution to intervention performance.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** A single grounding call prevents query refinement and multi-tool composition. A fixed placeholder may create distribution shift, and reference-answer phrasing can affect likelihood scores. Paired scoring adds training computation. The authors also flag incorrect grounding as a risk in embodied deployment (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** Benchmark gains span four backbones, but the controlled component ablation uses Qwen3-VL-4B, and reinforcement post-training uses only MSQA. The component effects therefore have narrower evidence than the SFT comparisons. The evaluation covers scene question answering, including navigation questions, and leaves physical execution untested (§5.1, Tables 1–2).
- **Analyst inference.** The answer-reward judge comparison holds the final GPT-Score evaluator fixed. It tests sensitivity to the training judge, leaving agreement with human judgments unmeasured (Appendices A.3, A.7).

## Open research questions

- **Does iterative grounding help when one tool returns incomplete evidence?** The current policy makes one call. Compare it with a multi-call policy under matched inference budgets on questions requiring both appearance and geometry (Limitations).
- **Is Evidence Gain stable across equivalent answer phrasings and realistic counterfactuals?** CARE scores a fixed reference answer against a placeholder. Compare paraphrase-averaged scoring and format-preserving evidence edits against the original reward, using paired answer-changing interventions (§4.5, Limitations).
- **Which component helps the policy follow answer-changing evidence?** The intervention study compares only SFT and full CARE-3D. Evaluate all Table 2 variants on the same edited items and report how often an originally correct prediction changes to the new correct answer (Tables 2, 6).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied methods and tables support the reported benchmark comparisons. The text does not provide run-to-run variance or confidence intervals, a detailed evidence-edit construction and validation protocol, human agreement with GPT-Score, or measured training overhead for CARE’s paired scoring.
