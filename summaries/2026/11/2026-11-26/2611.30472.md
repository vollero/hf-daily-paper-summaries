# Target-Part Preference Optimization for Part-Aware 3D Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30472) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30472)


## One-sentence takeaway

Fine-tuning PartCrafter with metric-ranked seed pairs and a target-part preference loss raised matched-part F-score by about 5% over pretrained PartCrafter on held-out multi-part Objaverse, with less consistent gains on ABO and ShapeNet (§§5.2–5.3, 7).

## Problem and motivation

A globally plausible 3D mesh can still contain missing, fused, or fragmented parts, limiting component editing, recomposition, and articulation. Fine-grained 3D annotations are scarce, and the authors identify a gap in how existing preference methods supervise a single semantic part in a generator that produces several parts jointly (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Seed-pair construction.** Rank frozen PartCrafter samples offline using a composite geometry score that includes ground-truth object and target-part metrics. Retain object–part examples with large across-seed score gaps. Each ordered seed pair generates two Gaussian noise tensors at a shared timestep for the same clean ground-truth compositional latent (§§4.1–4.2, S2).
- **Target-block objective.** Adapt FlowDPO to favor the winner’s reduction in target-block flow error relative to a frozen, stage-initial reference. After a reconstruction-only warm-up, combine this comparison with target-part and all-part ground-truth flow matching on the winner. Only the shared diffusion transformer is updated (§4.3, Eqs. 3–5).
- **Training sets and controls.** Construct three metric-ranked subsets from PartObjaverse-Tiny and test visual ranking, synthetic loser crops, label reversal, random-pair augmentation, and matched reconstruction-only objectives (§§5.1, 5.3–5.6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Medium setting led the tested scales on matched-part metrics | On held-out Objaverse with 2–8 parts, the medium model achieved about 5% higher matched-part F-score than pretrained PartCrafter and about 4% higher than the reported direct supervised fine-tuning baseline. | Table 2 (B2), §§5.2–5.3, 7 |
| Large setting improved F-score across part-count groups | On held-out Objaverse, the continued large model improved object and matched-part F-score over pretrained PartCrafter in every reported group. Direct supervised fine-tuning led the single-part matched-part metrics. | Table 1, §5.2 |
| The pairwise term improved both matched controls | On held-out 2–8-part Objaverse, adding the term improved all four metrics at medium and large scales. In the separate medium rerun, object Chamfer distance fell about 27% relative to reconstruction-only training. Paired bootstrap intervals over 200 shared inputs favored the full objective on all four metrics. | Table 4, §5.6, §S5 |
| Pair construction affected results | On held-out multi-part Objaverse, reversing pair labels reduced quality, synthetic crops failed to beat pretrained PartCrafter, and the screened human-ranked pilot improved on pretrained while the larger human-ranked set degraded. | Table 2, §§5.4–5.5 |
| Part gains transferred unevenly | The authors report improved global shape on ABO and inconsistent matched-part gains on ABO and ShapeNet. On a 59-object multi-part ShapeNet subset, the medium model raised F-score for the part selected as weakest by pretrained scores from 0.182 to 0.427. Remaining-part F-score fell by 0.046 versus pretrained, with a 95% bootstrap interval of [−0.110, +0.018]. | §§5.2–5.3, Tables S1–S3 |

The matched controls isolate the benefit of adding the pairwise term within the tested Objaverse configurations. The direct supervised fine-tuning comparison also changes data organization, objective, and learning rate, so its reported gap cannot be attributed to preference optimization alone (§5.2, §S2). The ShapeNet interval leaves the effect on remaining parts uncertain.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Fine-tuning still requires ground-truth part geometry. Offline preferences depend on candidate coverage, ordering reliability, and the selection criterion. Gains transfer unevenly, and updating a shared transformer can shift whole-object geometry even when the preference loss targets one block (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** The scale study cannot isolate dataset size because the large checkpoint continues training from a first-stage model with refreshed pairs, while smaller checkpoints start from pretrained weights (§5.3, §S2).
- **Analyst inference.** The part averages omit extra predictions, and aggregate metrics exclude failed evaluations and examples with very high part Chamfer distance. The reported gains therefore leave end-to-end failure rates and overproduction unclear (§S2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much do pair count and coverage each contribute?** The large run changes both training stage and seed pairs (§5.3). Train all sizes from the same checkpoint for a fixed update budget, with nested subsets and stratified resampling to test coverage separately.
2. **Which ranking signals yield useful preferences under distribution shift?** The score mixes object and part geometry, and transfer varies across datasets (§§5.2–5.4, S2). Hold candidate pools and pair counts fixed, vary the score components, and compare matched-part and object metrics on all three held-out lists.
3. **Can stronger local alignment preserve the remaining parts?** The ShapeNet loss-scale sweep shows a local–global trade-off, and the remaining-part estimate is uncertain (§5.6, §S6). Compare fixed and part-adaptive loss weights under the same budget and report weakest-part, remaining-part, and whole-object outcomes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The objective and matched-control design are explicit, but the supplied text contains table captions and narrative summaries without the numerical table cells. Exact baseline and checkpoint scores, full per-dataset and part-count breakdowns, and exclusion counts by method are unavailable. The text also leaves the per-input candidate count, exact reconstruction warm-up duration, and release status of code, pair files, and fine-tuned checkpoints unspecified.
