# DocSEAM: Bidirectional Cross-Page Encoding for Multi-Page Document Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34420) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34420)


## One-sentence takeaway

DocSEAM enriches each page’s visual tokens with context from its immediate neighbors before language-model processing, improving multi-page question answering, cross-page span localization, and robustness to artificial page breaks while preserving the language model’s visual token count (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Document vision-language models typically encode pages independently, even when a paragraph, table, or section continues across a page break. The language model must then connect evidence from visual tokens formed in isolation. The authors identify sensitivity to page-break placement as a symptom of this design and show that cutting through tables and paragraphs lowers baseline accuracy even when the document content and page count are held fixed (§1, Fig. 1, §4.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Post-encoder cross-page attention.** DocSEAM forms a triplet from the previous, current, and next pages. A center-anchored mask blocks direct attention between the two outer pages, while directional send and receive gates modulate cross-page links using token features and distances to content boundaries. The module starts as an identity and returns only the enriched center-page tokens (§§3.1–3.3, Fig. 2).
- **Boundary-focused training.** A supervised warm-up trains the module on consecutive-page alignment tasks and single-page controls, with an auxiliary gate loss. Downstream fine-tuning freezes DocSEAM and adapts the language model with LoRA and the visual patch merger (§3.4, Appendix A).
- **Controlled pagination diagnostic.** The authors compare cuts at whitespace with cuts inside paragraphs or tables, holding pixel content and page count fixed across the two variants (§4.3, Appendix G).

The capacity and training controls support contributions from both real neighboring pages and boundary-focused supervision on the two ablation benchmarks. LongDocURL gains were largest for adjacent two-page evidence, consistent with the local window. That stratification and the attention visualizations leave the causal role of specific token routes unresolved (§4.2, Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Gains vary by backbone and task. Qwen3.5-4B loses accuracy on SlideVQA relative to matched SFT and remains below zero-shot on MMLongBench-Doc. Larger backbones were unevaluated, and rule-based parsing pipelines retain a substantial lead on truncated-table merging (Appendix F, Table 10, Appendix E, Table 6).
- **Author-reported.** The tested post-encoder reconstruction warm-up performed poorly. The observed gate collapse is consistent with a same-page shortcut, and other self-supervised masking strategies remain open (Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference.** The re-pagination experiment supports robustness to the specified vertical cuts on two benchmarks. Its scope leaves robustness to broader layout changes uncertain. The SVE comparison also changes insertion point, directionality, and training schedule together, limiting attribution of the gap to any single choice (Appendices G and N).

## Open research questions

1. **Can a wider context window help with distant evidence?** DocSEAM directly fuses only immediate neighbors, and gains were smaller for non-adjacent-only evidence on LongDocURL. Compare immediate-neighbor, wider-window, and sparse distant-page variants under a matched compute budget, stratified by evidence distance (Appendix D).
2. **Does freezing DocSEAM preserve the gains better than continued adaptation?** The reported controls vary warm-up data and objectives, leaving the effect of freezing unresolved. Start from the same warmed-up checkpoint and compare frozen and trainable DocSEAM during otherwise identical downstream fine-tuning (§3.4, Appendix F).
3. **Can self-supervised warm-up learn useful cross-page links?** Post-encoder masking admits a plausible same-page shortcut. Compare masking before and after the vision encoder at a matched training budget, and measure span localization with real and shuffled neighbors (Appendix I).
