# When Does Reasoning Help Entity Disambiguation? Small LLMs In-Domain and Under Shift

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30334) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30334)


## One-sentence takeaway

For the small LLMs tested, pointwise reranking led at 4B on supervised ZELDA, reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards led after few-shot adaptation, and off-policy reasoning distillation led among the main recipes under true zero-shot shift.

## Problem and motivation

Entity disambiguation maps a marked mention in context to a knowledge-base entry. It supports entity linking, information extraction, and knowledge-based question answering. Prior reasoning-based selectors transferred across domains and knowledge bases using large LLMs, but their long generations raised inference cost. The authors ask whether smaller models can learn this capability and when it pays off relative to simpler selection methods (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled selector comparison.** With candidate retrieval fixed, compare independent pointwise scoring, direct multiple-choice selection over the reranker’s top 10, and explicit reasoning with a vote over 10 sampled completions (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Training comparison across supervision settings.** Evaluate 0.6–4B Qwen3 and Gemma-4 models under classical, few-shot, and true zero-shot conditions. Compare correctness-reward GRPO with on- and off-policy distillation, then cross trajectory source with KL direction to isolate their effects in one configuration (§§3.3–4.5, Table 6).
- **Analysis of when reasoning helps.** Use the reranker’s top-two score margin to study where the two systems differ in correctness, then standardize margin distributions across settings to test how much uncertainty explains the gains (§4.5, Fig. 4, App. J).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reranking leads at 4B with abundant in-domain supervision | On classical ZELDA, 4B pointwise reranking scored **81.0%** average accuracy versus **80.5%** for the best 4B reasoning configuration in the main table. At 0.6B, off-policy distillation scored **79.4%** versus **78.7%** for pointwise reranking. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Off-policy distillation transfers best among the main recipes | On true zero-shot ZESHEL, 4B off-policy distillation reached **83.69%** macro normalized accuracy versus **77.64%** for 4B reranking and **84.93%** for LELA with the 26B teacher. It led the main comparison in every tested size group. | §4.3, Table 3 |
| Few-shot RLVR improves target accuracy and increases source forgetting | With 100 sampled mentions per ZESHEL domain, including 10 held out for validation, 4B distilled initialization followed by RLVR reached **85.92%** macro normalized accuracy versus **84.90%** for continued off-policy distillation and **83.77%** for adapted reranking. On source-domain AIDA, 4B RLVR lost **2.0–5.8 percentage points**, while both distillation variants lost at most **1.4 points**. | §4.4, Table 4, App. M, Table 17 |
| Uncertainty predicts disagreement better than it identifies the better selector | At 4B on shortlist-eligible mentions, true zero-shot reasoning repaired **9.2%** and broke **3.2%** of reranker predictions. In the margin analysis, reweighting to the classical margin distribution reduced disagreement from **12.40%** to **5.40%**, while repairs still occurred about twice as often as breaks. Test-optimized margin routing added just **0.29 accuracy points** over the stronger standalone method in true zero-shot. | §4.5, Tables 5 and 16, Fig. 4, App. J |
| Forward KL helps with either trajectory source | On true zero-shot ZESHEL with the same 4B student and teacher, forward versus reverse KL yielded **83.69% vs. 82.68%** macro normalized accuracy on teacher trajectories and **83.76% vs. 77.85%** on student trajectories. | §4.5, Table 6 |
| Biomedical transfer preserves the trained-recipe ordering | On true zero-shot GLADIS, 4B off-policy distillation reached **53.35%** accuracy versus **30.61%** for 4B reranking, **53.51%** for prompted Qwen3-4B with LELA, and **65.74%** for LELA with the 26B teacher. | App. I, Table 15 |
| Extra samples have different value across recipes | In the 4B true zero-shot ZESHEL analysis, RLVR majority-vote accuracy was nearly flat as sample coverage rose. Distillation converted more of that coverage into vote accuracy and generated longer outputs. | Fig. 5, Apps. E and K, Fig. 7 |

The experiments establish which **configured selection pipelines** performed best under the tested conditions. ZESHEL normalized accuracy conditions on the gold entity being present among the retrieved candidates, and the reported inference FLOPs exclude retrieval. The margin reweighting supports the authors’ claim that lower margins explain much of the increase in disagreement, with a residual advantage for reasoning under shift. The design leaves the causal contribution of explicit reasoning unresolved because reasoning, supervision, and generation budget change together.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The experiments cover English and two small-model families. The main shift benchmark uses four self-contained fictional domains. GLADIS broadens coverage while also changing the task and removing entity descriptions (Limitations, App. I).
- **Author-stated training and statistical limits.** Each configuration uses one training run. The study uses LoRA, tests GRPO as its RLVR algorithm, and leaves intermediate distillation settings unexplored. Hyperparameters were chosen for comparability across paradigms, leaving per-recipe optima uncertain (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The classical-to-true-zero-shot comparison also changes benchmarks and candidate construction. Margin standardization controls the observed margin distribution, leaving other benchmark differences as possible explanations for part of the residual advantage (§§4.2–4.3, App. J).
- **Analyst inference.** The few-shot listwise selectors use an unadapted reranker, while the standalone pointwise baseline is adapted. The reported ranking applies to those pipelines. A shared adapted shortlist could change the comparison (§4.4). Routing thresholds were also selected on the evaluation splits, so the reported gains are optimistic for deployment (App. J).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gain comes from explicit reasoning?** The selectors differ in training and decoding as well as output format. Compare answer-only and rationale-trained variants with the same backbone, shortlist, training mentions, and inference budget.
2. **Can a router save compute on unseen domains while retaining the reasoning gains?** The margin-only router was tuned on the evaluation splits and showed limited headroom. Fit a router using margins and candidate ambiguity on development domains, then measure accuracy and FLOPs on held-out domains.
3. **At what amount of target supervision does RLVR overtake distillation, and when does forgetting emerge?** The main few-shot study uses one target-data budget. Sweep the number of labeled mentions and repeat sampling across seeds, measuring both target accuracy and source retention.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the headline benchmark results and reported comparisons. The supplied text leaves seed-to-seed variance, performance across intermediate target-data budgets, and a matched-budget answer-only control unavailable. The inference-cost accounting is also ambiguous for the voting configuration. Appendix D.2 describes output tokens averaged across samples, while the main evaluation votes over all 10 completions, leaving it unclear how Figure 3 charges the full decoding cost.
