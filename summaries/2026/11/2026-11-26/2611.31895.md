# Loupe: Reinforcing Knowledge-Intensive Video Generation Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31895) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31895)


## One-sentence takeaway

Loupe trains an agent to retrieve and verify evidence and turn it into temporal video plans without rendering videos during reinforcement learning, improving all four Search-VBench dimensions over direct generation across three tested generators (Tables 2 and 5).

## Problem and motivation

Short video requests often omit the facts, distinctive appearances, and event sequences needed for an accurate depiction. The authors argue that rendering quality alone cannot fill these gaps, manually designed search workflows limit adaptation, and final-video rewards are costly and make it hard to distinguish good planning from good rendering. Trajectory-level rewards also obscure which search or planning decisions helped (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Loupe** uses a ReAct loop with text search, image search, and page browsing to identify and verify missing information. It turns that evidence into a temporally ordered prompt and selects reference images for a downstream video generator (§3.1, Fig. 2).
- **Rubric-Based Credit (RBC)** derives factual, visual, and temporal requirements from each request without requiring annotated answers or target videos. A judge scores each assistant turn against the rubric and the evidence available at that point. RBC-SFT weights assistant-token loss by turn credit. RBC-GRPO compares credits at corresponding turn positions across rollouts and adds a final-plan reward at the terminal turn (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Search-VBench** evaluates faithfulness, visual correctness, temporal dynamics, and aesthetics on 150 held-out prompts across seven categories, with 339 reference-image entries and 566 rubric items (§4.1, Fig. 5, App. B.1).

The matched-renderer comparisons support a benefit from adding Loupe under the reported settings. Human preferences corroborate the direction of the benchmark gains. The ablations support RBC’s contribution for MiniMax-H3 on Search-VBench. The separate effects of retrieved text, reference images, and temporal planning remain unmeasured in the reported ablations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Loupe depends on available, reliable retrieval evidence. Downstream renderers can still misrepresent grounded plans, and rubric judges can miss aspects of decision quality. The authors also report that VideoGen-Agent’s closed implementation prevented a direct comparison (§4.1, Apps. D.2 and F).
- **Analyst inference.** The RBC ablations cover one renderer, and the long-video comparison also covers one renderer on a sampled subset. Their results leave cross-renderer transfer of the training gains and long-video gains open (§4.4, Table 4, App. B.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The human study measures relative preference under forced choice. Its agreement analysis uses the same direct-versus-Loupe pairs, so validation of the judge on closer comparisons, such as between RBC variants, remains open (App. D.4).

## Open research questions

- **Which inputs drive the gains?** The reported ablations vary training and rewards. A factorial test could independently vary retrieved text and reference images while holding the renderer and planning protocol fixed.
- **Can the agent recover from misleading early evidence?** The paper illustrates a failure where an incorrect hypothesis persists despite contrary evidence, but gives no aggregate recovery rate. A controlled test could inject conflicting results and compare the default agent with one required to verify contradictory evidence (App. E, Fig. S9).
- **How much does rendering capacity limit the benefit of a grounded plan?** The matched comparisons measure the whole pipeline. An experiment could feed the same independently verified plans and images to each renderer and score which individual requirements survive rendering (Tables 2–3, App. F).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and benchmark comparisons. The supplied text lacks the verbatim prompt templates referenced in Appendix G, confidence intervals for the headline comparisons, results across multiple training seeds, and confirmed public availability of the Loupe checkpoint, code, or Search-VBench files.
