# HOG-WAM: Improving Visual Robustness through Future Gradient Prediction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34811) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34811)


## One-sentence takeaway

Adding a training-only future-gradient prediction branch to FastWAM improved reported manipulation success under sensor noise in RoboTwin-Plus, LIBERO Plus, and four Franka tasks while preserving FastWAM’s inference architecture.

## Problem and motivation

World-action models use future observation prediction to train features for robot control, but RGB prediction supervises object boundaries alongside color, texture, and lighting. The authors argue that changes in appearance can disrupt a policy even when the manipulation goal stays fixed. They seek a way to emphasize task-relevant boundaries while retaining the RGB cues needed to identify objects from language instructions (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Future-gradient targets.** The authors convert clean demonstration frames into three-channel rasters that summarize local gradient magnitude and orientation. The same frozen video encoder processes RGB and HOG rasters. The targets are deterministic and require no additional sensors, annotations, or learned target generator (§3.2, Fig. 2).
- **Removable auxiliary predictor.** A HOG diffusion transformer reads RGB features through asymmetric attention. Joint flow-matching training lets its loss update the RGB backbone used for control. Action queries use observed RGB features, so the HOG branch and future visual tokens can be dropped at inference (§§3.3–3.4, Fig. 3).
- **Controlled robustness evaluation.** The authors extend RoboTwin with four RGB corruptions and compare auxiliary HOG supervision with replacing RGB inputs and prediction targets on LIBERO Plus (§§4.2, 4.5).

The authors attribute the noise gains to features that aggregate local boundary evidence. The experiments support a task-success benefit in the tested settings, with the clearest uncertainty result for LIBERO Plus sensor noise at fixed checkpoints. The proposed feature mechanism remains unverified, and robustness across retraining, corruption severities, and unseen physical setups remains open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported scope.** Evaluation used one checkpoint per model and benchmark. RoboTwin scenes were unpaired across policies. LIBERO Plus used one rollout per variant, and its bootstrap intervals were pointwise and unadjusted for multiple comparisons. Franka trials used one synthetic noise setting, lacked matched reset identifiers, and had no documented randomized model ordering (Appendices B.3, B.6–B.7, D).
- **Author-reported comparison differences.** The released RoboTwin baselines used different execution horizons and retained their native camera layouts. The π₀.₅ comparison used measured joint states because the release’s original state preprocessing was unavailable (§4.1, Appendices B.2, B.4–B.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The auxiliary branch increases training compute and parameter count. The ablation changes both inputs and targets for RGB-to-HOG and lacks a compute-matched auxiliary control, so it cannot isolate the contributions of gradient content, future prediction, and added training capacity (§4.5, Appendices B.1, E.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does predicting future gradients matter?** The ablation leaves timing and extra training capacity unresolved. Compare future HOG, current-frame HOG, and future RGB auxiliary targets with matched branches and training compute.
2. **How sensitive are the gains to noise type and severity?** RoboTwin-Plus and Franka use a single prescribed mixed-noise severity. Sweep noise strengths and evaluate naturally degraded camera observations on the same physical tasks.
3. **Do the gains survive independent retraining?** The reported uncertainty conditions on fixed checkpoints. Train both methods across multiple seeds and evaluate them on matched simulation scenes and randomized, matched physical resets.
