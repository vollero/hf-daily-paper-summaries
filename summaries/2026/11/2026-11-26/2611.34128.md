# FoundSurface: Feedforward Ray-Based 3D Scene Surface Reconstruction from Unposed Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34128) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34128)


## One-sentence takeaway

FoundSurface turns unposed RGB images into a continuous, queryable surface using VGGT geometry and coarse-to-fine ray regression, achieving the lowest ray-distance error by ADE among the Table 1 baselines on every listed dataset (§§3–4.1).

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses detailed 3D scene reconstruction from images with unknown camera poses and intrinsics. The authors identify a gap between feedforward pointmaps, which recover geometry from unposed images but leave discrete samples, and ray-based methods, which recover finer surfaces but typically require accurate poses or a point cloud. They motivate continuous geometry for applications such as robot manipulation, augmented reality, and scene editing (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Query-specific reference selection.** Pretrained VGGT estimates source camera parameters, pointmaps, and visual features. FoundSurface projects the pointmaps into the target camera and ranks source views by patch-level depth agreement with a z-buffered pseudo-depth map (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Pointmap-to-ray fusion.** A Transformer uses target Plücker ray tokens to attend to ray-conditioned reference features in target-camera coordinates. It predicts a coarse surface intersection along each query ray (§3.3).
- **Local surface refinement.** FoundSurface adapts RayletDF’s raylet formulation by initializing raylets at the coarse intersections and using nearby sparse-convolution features to predict distance corrections. The resulting field supports analytical normals. One model is trained across nine datasets using depth supervision in two stages (§§3.4–3.5, §4, Appendix A.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest ray-distance ADE against the main baselines | With 50 unposed source views, FoundSurface achieved the lowest ADE on all six Table 1 datasets. | Strongest reported surface normals | FoundSurface led all reported normal metrics on ScanNet++ and Replica. Mean angular error was 29.83° versus 39.87° for the strongest external baseline, RayletDF, on ScanNet++, and 20.72° versus 28.96° on Replica. | §4.2, Table 2 |

| Better mesh F1 under the reported fusion protocol | On ScanNet++, FoundSurface at voxel setting 0.01 scored 0.792 F1 at 5 cm and 0.910 at 10 cm, versus 0.787 and 0.897 for VGGT + Poisson, the strongest external baseline on both scores. | §4.7, Table 9 |

The evidence supports lower ADE across the listed benchmarks and better normals on the two datasets evaluated for that task. Refinement lowers ADE across Table 1, with some regressions on other metrics. The mesh results apply to ScanNet++ under the reported fusion protocol, and the single-view results apply to adjacent-frame queries.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitations.** The pretrained backbone makes the model large. The authors also show reconstruction failures on mirrors and transparent glass, where VGGT produces inaccurate pointmaps (§5, Appendix A.21).
- **Analyst inference from the sensitivity results.** Errors rise across the pointmap and camera-error bins, qualifying the authors’ robustness claim. The bins group naturally occurring errors and leave the independent effects of pose and pointmap errors unresolved (§4.6, Tables 7–8).
- **Analyst inference on evaluation scope.** The main component ablations use ScanNet-only training because of compute constraints. Their effect sizes may differ for the full training mixture. The single-view protocol leaves wide-baseline performance open (§§4.4–4.5).

## Open research questions

- **How far can a single-image query move before geometry degrades?** The paper tests adjacent frames. A follow-up could vary camera translation, rotation, and visible overlap while keeping the source image fixed (§4.4).
- **How much error comes from source poses versus pointmaps?** The reported bins leave these effects entangled. A follow-up could perturb each prior independently on the same scenes and measure ray-distance and mesh errors (§4.6).
- **Can a smaller backbone retain the accuracy gains?** The paper reports A800 measurements and identifies mobile deployment as future work. A follow-up could train a compact variant and measure end-to-end latency, peak memory, and reconstruction error on mobile hardware (§5, Appendix A.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method summary and reported comparisons. The exact target-camera alignment procedure at evaluation and the aggregation protocol for Table 12’s standard deviations remain unclear in the supplied text. Table 1 includes Replica, but §4.1 omits it from the list explicitly excluded from both training pipelines, leaving its strict out-of-domain status unclear. Actual code, data, and checkpoint availability also remain unverified because the abstract and checklist give conflicting release statements.
