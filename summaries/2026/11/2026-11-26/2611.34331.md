# Puzzling-Bench: Evaluating Language Models on 23,777 Human-Written Puzzles with Community-Verified Answers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34331) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34331)


## One-sentence takeaway

Puzzling-Bench collects 23,777 human-written puzzles with asker-accepted answers, and GPT-6.1 Sol leads the shared text-only pilot at 74.8% accuracy on non-errored runs (§3.2, §5.1, Table 4).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that puzzles test how models combine world knowledge, lateral thinking, and multi-step reasoning. They identify a gap between broad reasoning suites and puzzle benchmarks focused on a single genre or answer format. Puzzling Stack Exchange offers free-form puzzles across genres, with asker-confirmed solutions, spoiler hints where available, and timestamps for comparing performance by posting date (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark construction.** Filter the Puzzling Stack Exchange archive to open questions whose accepted answer is present in the dump. Preserve the full answer, tags, dates, available hints, images, and attribution. Release spoiler-based short references across the corpus and LLM-extracted references for the two evaluation pilots (§§3.1–3.6, §9).
- **Free-form scoring.** Apply normalized exact match first, then use an LLM to judge answer equivalence against the short reference and full accepted answer. Check how scores change across judges and reference versions (§4.3, §§6.1–6.2).
- **Diagnostic evaluation.** Compare nine solvers on a frozen text pilot, ablate hints and images, and analyze posting-period effects, shared failures, oracle coverage, and the effect of excluding errored runs (§§4.2, 4.5, 5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-6.1 Sol leads the text pilot | On Pilot-255 with available hints shown and locked references, GPT-6.1 Sol scored 74.8%, Opus 5.5 66.1%, and Opus 4.8 62.9%. Accuracy excludes each solver’s errored rows and uses the Opus 4.8 judge after exact match. | §5.1, Table 4 |
| Scores depend on semantic grading and reference quality | Exact match accounted for 45–60% of answers ultimately marked correct, depending on the solver. Across 1,540 Tier 2 rows, judge pairs agreed on 95.3–96.9% of verdicts, with solver-level κ ranging from 0.82 to 0.96. Switching judges moved accuracy by at most 1.6 percentage points. Replacing spoiler-based references with final extracted references moved it by up to 2.8 points and preserved the ranking. The authors detected no own-family preference with the locked references. | §4.3, §§6.1–6.2, Table 4 |
| Solver failures overlap, with some complementary coverage | On the 193 Pilot-255 puzzles completed by every solver, the nine-model oracle solved 91.2%, and 17 puzzles defeated all nine. After GPT-6.1 Sol, Opus 4.8 added 18 solves to the greedy union. Opus 5.5 joined fifth and added one. | §6.3, Table 6, Appendix D |
| Hints and images improve paired accuracy | On hinted Pilot-255 puzzles completed in both runs, withholding hints cost GPT-6.1 Sol 10.5 points and Opus 5.5 15.0. On Img-100 puzzles completed in both conditions, withholding the image cost the same models 35 and 24 points, respectively. Both ablations used the Opus 4.8 judge. | §4.2, Table 3, §5.4, Table 5 |
| The newest posting bucket scored lower | All nine solvers scored lower on the 56 Pilot-255 puzzles in the designated cutoff bucket than on puzzles posted in 2020 or earlier. For example, GPT-6.1 Sol fell from 76.7% to 66.7%; the bucket postdates the vendor-stated cutoff for six solvers. | §§3.6, 5.2, Figure 2 |
| Recovering failed runs reduced reported accuracy | With spoiler-based references on Pilot-255, recovering 12 Kimi K3 rows reduced accuracy from 65.0% to 62.9%, and recovering 26 Grok 4.7 rows reduced it from 55.0% to 50.6%. | §6.4 |

The paired ablations show the value of hints and images for the tested models and items. Judge agreement establishes consistency across the tested graders, while absolute grading accuracy against independently adjudicated solver answers remains unknown. Oracle coverage measures the benefit of perfect answer selection, so achievable routing accuracy remains open. The posting-date comparison leaves training exposure and changes in puzzle difficulty unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Accepted answers can be wrong or omit valid alternatives, and judges can share grading errors. In the 50-reference human review, 46 extractions were correct, two were corrected for including explanatory text, and two were unverifiable (§§3.2–3.3, 8).
- **Author-stated.** The pilots are small. The date slice overlaps three solvers’ stated training cutoffs and does not control for reposted variants. Harnesses differ in injected prompts and reasoning settings, and the requested completion budget was unenforced. Img-100 requires text references, tags are overlapping and uneven, and transfer to general reasoning is unestablished (§§4.1, 4.4, 5.4, 8).
- **Analyst inference.** Corpus-wide model rankings remain uncertain because Pilot-255 applies length filters and deliberately includes all eligible recent items. The partial strict text-only runs offer mixed evidence for extrapolation. Likewise, the all-model-completion filter can select against difficult or slow items, so the oracle result applies to the common subset (§§4.5, 5.3, 6.3–6.4).

## Open research questions

- **How much of the posting-date gap reflects training exposure?** Posting period and difficulty can vary together. Test on a larger refresh that postdates every tested model’s stated cutoff, screen for earlier variants, and match recent and older items on genre and human-rated difficulty (§§3.6, 5.2, 8).
- **How often does the scorer reject valid alternatives or misgrade visual solutions?** Cross-judge agreement leaves shared errors unresolved, and the image pilot uses text references. Have independent human reviewers grade a stratified sample of solver outputs with access to full solutions and images, then compare text and vision-capable judges against those verdicts (§§5.4, 6.1, 8).
- **How much oracle coverage can a practical router recover?** The greedy union uses known correctness. Test a router and answer selector on held-out puzzles under a fixed call budget, counting timeouts as failures and comparing with a single-model baseline (§§6.3–6.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark design and headline results reported in the prose. The supplied text includes captions but omits table bodies and figure contents, leaving some exact values unavailable, including per-model confidence intervals and complete per-condition counts. It also lacks broad human adjudication of solver outputs, verified training exposure, and a complete run of the nine solvers on the strict text-only corpus.
