# SIA: Selective Image Analogy

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33894) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33894)


## One-sentence takeaway

SIA uses a text-token mask to transfer selected edits from a fixed before/after image pair to a new image, leading the tested visual-analogy baselines on retained-edit accuracy and recall across three vision-language judges (Tables 1 and 11).

## Problem and motivation

A before/after pair can convey an edit’s exact appearance, placement, and scale, which can be difficult to specify in text. The authors identify a gap in visual analogy methods that transfer the demonstrated changes together. When a user wants only some of those changes, omitting the unwanted edits from the prompt can still leave the model free to copy them from the edited reference (§§1, 2.2–2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Selective transfer formulation.** A fixed before/after pair, a query image, and text identifying the desired subset define the task. The authors claim this is the first image analogy setting to support subset selection from a single multi-edit pair (§§1, 4).
- **Mask-aligned suppression.** AnaLoRA learns full transfer on FLUX.2-Klein. ExLoRA learns to suppress excluded edits by gating its updates to their text tokens during denoising. Before denoising, the Concept Suppression Vector (CSV) uses the same mask to weaken those token embeddings along directions estimated from contrastive prompts (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Multi-Edit Analogy Dataset.** The authors curate full and partial targets for same-recipe subject pairs, combining augmented RelationAdapter examples with sequentially synthesized edits and vision-language filtering (§4.1, App. E).

The strongest evidence is for retained-edit fidelity on the authors’ benchmark, supported by model judges and small human studies. Qwen3-VL preservation favored several visual-analogy baselines, and the main comparison table omits a direct suppression score across methods. The ablation gives direct evidence for suppression within SIA, while the human rankings assess suppression jointly with fidelity and preservation. The separate Qwen-Image-2.1 protocol leaves relative backbone performance unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** SIA may struggle when the before and after images differ substantially. The dataset covers a limited range of scenes and omits operations such as artifact removal and editing complex layouts (§6).
- **Analyst inference on subset coverage.** §4.1 describes targets for all retained subsets, while Apps. E.1 and E.5 specify that several categories have targets only for subsets reachable through sequential synthesis. The dataset therefore provides uneven supervision across edit combinations. App. E.3 also describes a single four-edit recipe, while Table 8 lists five four-edit settings. Their relationship is unclear.
- **Author-reported evaluation caveats.** The visual ablation uses selected training-subject examples, a shorter run, one seed, and a different CSV implementation. The second-backbone checkpoint was selected using half the test set, and its curated figures omit several failures discussed in the text, including generic accessories, partial style application, and reframing (Apps. F and I).
- **Analyst inference on metric sensitivity.** VLM score calibration differs by judge, and the paper reports visible failures that the second-backbone judge penalizes weakly. Aggregate scores can therefore miss mismatches in the precise visual form of an edit (Apps. G.1 and I).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does SIA generalize to unseen edit combinations?** Higher-order training targets cover only certain subsets, and the reported split does not establish recipe-disjoint performance. Hold out entire recipes and score every nonempty subset, broken down by whether its edit combination appeared in training (App. E.5).
2. **When does CSV add value after ExLoRA is trained?** Its gain is small in the main ablation and declines with longer ExLoRA training in the development run. Compare several ExLoRA checkpoints with CSV on and off on the same held-out tasks and seeds (Table 2, App. F).
3. **Can selected edits be combined across different demonstration pairs?** The current setup uses one pair. Test two-pair transfer with overlapping and conflicting edits, measuring fidelity to each reference, leakage of excluded edits, and query preservation (§6).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and reported comparisons. Material gaps include uncertainty intervals for the main benchmark and human-study results, explicit recipe-disjoint split information, a rule for choosing CSV strength per edit, and confirmation that the dataset, model weights, or promised training configurations and evaluation scripts are publicly available.
