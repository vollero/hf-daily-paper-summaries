# Beyond Code and Pixels: Co-Generating Editable and Visually Rich Presentations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28010) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28010)


## One-sentence takeaway

SlidesWeaver uses diffusion to propose slide compositions and specialized agents to turn them into editable PowerPoint decks, with one of its two generation routes earning the best score in each of 15 model–metric comparisons on the authors’ test set (§§3–4.2).

## Problem and motivation

Presentation generators must coordinate wording, visual hierarchy, and placement while preserving elements that users can revise. The authors identify a gap between programmatic systems, whose editable primitives can lead to repetitive layouts, and raster-first systems, whose rich compositions can be difficult to edit. Combining them creates a coordination problem because committing text or geometry too early constrains later design choices (§§1–3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Staged multimodal commitment.** Specialized agents share a deck-level narrative, style, and reusable assets, then generate slides in parallel. Each slide progressively commits its wording, composition, geometry, and appearance (§§3.1–3.4, Fig. 3).
- **Two generation routes.** Topology First grounds a diffusion proposal into a relational layout and regional capacity constraints before writing the copy. Text First commits generated copy upfront, uses diffusion as composition guidance, and refines a browser-rendered HTML plan to fix geometry while preserving the wording (§3.3).
- **Diffusion for planning and synthesis.** Lightweight local diffusion explores composition, and remote image generation realizes the visuals. Topology First synthesizes a joint canvas and can adjust placement afterward. Text First extracts transparent raster layers and places them in validated slots. Both assemble native editable text in PPTX (§3.4, Apps. B–C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| SlidesWeaver led the reported quality comparisons | Across 1,728 generations covering 72 requests and 24 configurations, one of the two routes led all 15 language-model–metric comparisons against three baseline systems. Text First, averaged over its six configurations, improved over the strongest baseline for each metric by 14.2% in visual expressiveness, 15.9% in instruction following, 17.7% in content quality, 20.3% in Layout, and 22.9% in Topo-Vendi. | §§4.1–4.2, Table 1 |
| Both routes increased within-deck topology diversity | Mean normalized Topo-Vendi was 0.845 for Topology First and 0.840 for Text First, versus 0.683 for the strongest baseline. Text First also averaged 0.950 on Layout, the fraction of successfully rendered slides free of severe overlap, clipping, and out-of-bounds elements. | §§4.1–4.2, Table 1 |
| Topology First offered the faster operating point | Against image-enabled DeepPresenter, configuration-averaged Topology First cost $0.64 per deck and took 556.8 seconds, 37.3% cheaper and 57.7% faster. Text First cost $0.88 and took 1,279.9 seconds, 13.7% cheaper and 2.9% faster. First-pass success was 97.69% and 96.99%, respectively. SlidesWeaver accounted for six of eight non-dominated configurations on quality, cost, and latency. | §4.2, Fig. 5, App. E |
| Diffusion and layout judging affected different metrics | In Text First ablations that reused upstream outputs, removing the diffusion proposal reduced mean Topo-Vendi from 0.84 to 0.54 and visual expressiveness from 3.15 to 2.83 on the five-point scale, while Layout moved from 0.95 to 0.94. Removing the layout judge reduced Layout from 0.95 to 0.85, with little change in Topo-Vendi. | §4.3, Table 2 |
| Layered synthesis improved layout validity in both routes | With earlier stages held fixed, switching Topology First from inpainting to layered generation raised mean Layout from 0.72 to 0.87. Switching Text First from layered generation to inpainting lowered Layout from 0.95 to 0.79 and mean visual expressiveness from 3.15 to 3.01 on the five-point scale. | §4.3, Table 3 |

The comparisons support a quality advantage under the authors’ benchmark and scoring protocol. The matched Text First ablations provide more direct evidence that diffusion proposals increased measured diversity and that layout judging reduced severe defects. Editability was an output requirement for all evaluated systems. Comparative editing effort, exact wording retention, and agreement with human reviewers remain unmeasured in the supplied evaluation (§4.1, App. E).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors.** Topology First is sensitive to inpainting drift and weak mask adherence. Text density varies with the language backbone, and resource constraints left system-level post-training for future work. The authors also flag possible factual errors, bias, inappropriate content, and resemblance to protected material (Apps. D and F, Ethical Considerations).
- **Analyst inference.** A single blinded VLM supplies subjective scores, defect flags, and topology graphs. Those measurements could share judge-specific biases, and the supplied evaluation reports no human agreement study. Layout excludes slides that remain failed after recovery, so it should be read alongside first-pass success (App. E).
- **Analyst inference.** Disabling DeepPresenter’s image search limits the comparison to the tested visual-tool configuration. The diffusion ablation applies to Text First, since Topology First depends on diffusion for planning. Its isolated effect in Topology First remains unmeasured (§4.3, App. A).

## Open research questions

1. **Does editable structure make revision faster or more accurate?** The study treats editability as a requirement. A timed user study could compare text corrections, object moves, and layout changes across the generated PPTX files (§4.1).
2. **Do the quality rankings hold under human review?** The reported protocol uses one frozen VLM judge. A blinded study with presentation authors and intended audience members could measure agreement with the judge and compare system rankings (App. E).
3. **Can tighter spatial control improve Topology First without sacrificing scene integration?** The synthesis ablation improves Layout through layering, while visual expressiveness varies by model. A controlled comparison could hold topology and text fixed, vary mask adherence and synthesis strategy, and measure both geometric drift and human-rated visual coherence (§4.3, Apps. D and F).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and the aggregate results explicitly reported in the supplied text. The text omits the numerical rows of Tables 1–3, the full example composition prompt and evaluation judge prompt referenced in Apps. C and E, and per-case results or uncertainty estimates. It links to code, but availability of the benchmark requests and generated decks is unconfirmed.
