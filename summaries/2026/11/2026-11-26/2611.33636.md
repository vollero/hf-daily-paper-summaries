# What Is Redundant at High Resolution? Efficient Attention and Feed-Forward Design for All-in-One Image Restoration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33636) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33636)


## One-sentence takeaway

HiGLR uses local mixing at full resolution, shared-routing hybrid attention at intermediate scales, and compact spatial feed-forward layers to improve reported PSNR on three restoration benchmarks while reducing inference cost against the compared methods.

## Problem and motivation

All-in-one image restoration uses one model to recover detail across different degradations, including combinations of degradations. Multiscale networks use coarse features for global context and fine features for local detail, but dense spatial attention becomes expensive as token count grows, and convolutional feed-forward networks incur memory traffic by materializing expanded high-resolution activations. The authors ask which computations can be reduced at each scale while preserving restoration quality. (§1)

## Approach and contributions

- **Resolution-aware architecture.** The authors measure attention concentration and readout redundancy by scale. HiGLR uses learned local convolution kernels at full resolution, hybrid attention at the two intermediate scales, and dense self-attention at the coarsest scale. (§§3.1–3.2, Fig. 2)
- **Block-Routed Hybrid Attention (BRHA).** The coarse branch attends densely to learned summaries of all blocks. The fine branch attends to token-level deviations from those summaries within the query’s own block and selected remote blocks. Neighboring queries share the selected blocks while retaining their own attention weights. Degradation descriptors condition routing. (§3.3)
- **Dual-Space Feed-Forward Network (DSFFN).** Motivated by a rank-reduction study, DSFFN confines depthwise spatial mixing to a compact path and injects its output into an expanded, gated pointwise path. Kernel fusion avoids writing full-image expanded intermediates to GPU memory during inference. (§3.4, §10.4)

The benchmark comparisons support a quality–efficiency advantage for HiGLR in the measured settings. The ablations support specific design choices, including shared routing and fine-branch residual values. The readout statistic measures spatial sharing within an image, and the rank study probes pretrained outputs. Those measurements alone cannot show that attention is input-independent or predict the best spatial width for another model. (§§7–9)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated caveat.** Spatially shared readouts do not imply input independence or lossless replacement. The authors test replacement separately in an architecture that retains lower-resolution attention. (§§7–8)
- **Author-reported scope.** Most CDD-11 ablations use 120 epochs, half the full schedule. The authors also report that replacing dense attention with BRHA at the four-stage bottleneck lowers PSNR. (§4.3, §14.2)
- **Analyst inference.** The readout probes use variants of one backbone family, and the rank intervention uses two pretrained models on CDD-11 crops. The evidence leaves open how well either diagnostic transfers to other backbones or degradation distributions. (§§7, 9)
- **Analyst inference.** Cross-GPU latency evidence covers batch-one inference at two resolutions, and the training-cost comparison uses different patch sizes across methods. Throughput at larger batches and training efficiency under matched patch sizes remain uncertain. (§§15–16)

## Open research questions

1. **When does full-resolution global attention improve restoration?** The replacement study keeps coarser global context in place. Cross the choice of full-resolution mixer with the amount of coarse global context and report quality by degradation condition. (§8, §14.2)
2. **Can BRHA allocate its remote-block budget per image or region?** The paper sweeps fixed budgets and shows that selected regions vary with degradation. Compare adaptive routing with fixed budgets at matched measured latency, reporting PSNR by condition and tail latency. (§4.3, §§14.3–14.4)
3. **Can output-rank sensitivity predict the right DSFFN spatial width?** The PCA study compresses pretrained outputs, while the width ablation varies only Stage 1. Use PCA measurements to choose widths across stages, then compare against an independently tuned width sweep at matched inference cost. (§4.3, §9)
