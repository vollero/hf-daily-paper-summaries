# DetourBench: Trajectory-Level Evaluation of Interactive Agents with Exact Minimum-Action Bounds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20844) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20844)


## One-sentence takeaway

DetourBench uses exact full-information access bounds to distinguish successful agents by interaction cost and failed agents by remaining work or irreversible error on deterministic, partially observable tasks.

## Problem and motivation

Endpoint success gives the same score to direct and circuitous successes, and to near-misses and irreversible mistakes. The authors argue that authored solutions demonstrate solvability without certifying a minimum, making it hard to compare how much interaction agents used or how close failed runs came to finishing (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Exact access and residual bounds.** Breadth-first search over each finite transition graph computes the minimum number of charged accesses and produces a replayable shortest path. The same graph gives the minimum remaining accesses from a recoverable terminal state. The evaluator reports success, attainment on successful runs, residual progress, normalized detour, and irreversible failures separately (§3).
- **Two suites with a shared validation protocol.** Controlled pairs vary a single information cue while preserving the graph, correct answer, and oracle bound. Integrated tasks combine evidence sources, prerequisites, and terminal decisions. Both suites use witness replay, provenance and structural audits, and versioned artifacts (§4).
- **Diagnostic evaluation.** A controlled cue-removal experiment tests discovery behavior, and a separate integrated evaluation shows how access costs and residual work distinguish runs with identical binary outcomes (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Withholding a cue reduced discovery and success | Across 40 paired Currency problems, four models, and three repeats per arm, removing the sentence naming the point-in-time view reduced view use by 63 percentage points and success by 41 percentage points, while increasing out-of-scope reads by 2.9 per episode. The view remained available in both arms. | §5.1, Table 1 |
| The cue also affected access efficiency when both runs succeeded | In 176 matched model–problem–repeat pairs that succeeded in both arms, mean conditional attainment fell from 0.763 to 0.637 without the cue. The 95% problem-cluster bootstrap interval for the change was [−0.156, −0.099]. | §5.1 |
| Success and attainment yielded different model orderings | In 375 integrated episodes across 25 tasks, five endpoints, and three repeats, 276 runs succeeded. Endpoint success ranged from 58.7% to 96.0% and mean attainment on successful runs from 0.803 to 0.890. GLM had the lowest success rate but higher attainment than Qwen and DeepSeek, including on the 13 tasks solved by all models. | Abstract, §§5.2–5.3, Table 2 |
| Equal success concealed different access costs | All five models succeeded on every repeat of eight integrated tasks. Within-task spreads in mean attainment ranged from 0.074 to 0.190. On task 3, whose oracle required 15 accesses, model means ranged from 17.0 to 21.7 accesses, and both cost extremes had zero invalid or duplicate accesses. | §5.4, Appendices D and G |
| Residual work distinguished failed runs | Of 99 integrated failures, 95 exhausted the budget, two stopped while recoverable, and two committed irreversibly. Of the 95 budget-exhausted runs, 47 finished within two accesses of success. On task 18, the six failures included two one-access voluntary stops, three budget exhaustions at 0.85–0.95 residual progress, and one absorbing wrong commitment. | §§3.5, 5.5, Appendices E–F |
| Exact search was tractable on the evaluated graphs | Single-threaded CPython search reproduced all 25 frozen integrated bounds. The most expensive instance generated 13,541 states, with a median runtime of 273 ms across five runs and peak memory of 24 MiB measured in a separate run. | Appendix I, Table 8 |

The controlled comparison supports an effect of the signposting sentence on the audited Currency pairs. The integrated results establish that the metrics can distinguish runs with the same endpoint outcome. Their frequency in natural workflows remains unknown because task selection favored separation. Model-specific success and failure subsets also limit aggregate efficiency and residual comparisons. The oracle measures excess relative to full information, leaving open how much exploration an agent could reasonably avoid.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Model behavior informed task construction and selection. Three repeats limit estimates of stochastic reliability, and only the Currency mechanism yielded pairs suitable for the controlled comparison (§§4.1, 5.6, 6).
- **Author-stated.** The oracle can select the correct lookalike first. Exactness applies under authored, unit-cost semantics, and executed semantic counterfactuals cover only four integrated tasks. The budget multiplier was fixed at 1.5, leaving budget sensitivity unmeasured (§§3.1, 3.4–3.5, 6, Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference.** The reported HY4-Preview run cannot isolate an endpoint effect across the main suite because it used the later prompt throughout and omitted task 25. The released harness supports reruns, while independent analysis of the original access sequences is limited by the absence of public trajectories and the use of provider-default sampling settings (Appendices G–H).

## Open research questions

- **How much detour reflects necessary discovery?** The full-information oracle has a lookalike-selection advantage (§3.4). Compare agents with a reference policy restricted to the same observations, varying cue strength and candidate ambiguity while holding the graph and oracle bound fixed.
- **How often does access cost separate agents on unseen workflows?** The integrated suite was partly selected for separation (§5.6, Appendix B). Preregister a held-out task collection before any model runs, then measure the frequency and size of matched-success cost gaps with more repeats.
- **How sensitive are failure categories to access budgets?** The integrated evaluation fixes the budget multiplier, and most failures exhaust it (§3.5). Rerun the same model–task grid at several preregistered multipliers and track changes in success, residual work, and irreversible commitments.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method and reported within-suite findings. The supplied text contains captions and narrative summaries for several tables but omits most of their numeric cells, including complete per-endpoint results for the controlled and integrated evaluations and per-model residual summaries. Results for an observation-limited reference policy, alternative budget multipliers, human baselines, or a preregistered held-out suite are also unavailable.
