# A Mesh for Every Moment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34939) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34939)


## One-sentence takeaway

MEMO reconstructs per-frame meshes of unmasked dynamic scenes from synchronized, calibrated multi-view video and achieves the best reported joint geometry F-scores on Blender-Moments while remaining competitive at novel-view synthesis (§§4–5, Table 1, Appendix E).

## Problem and motivation

Dynamic Gaussian splatting can render convincing novel views while yielding noisy or incomplete meshes, limiting uses that depend on accurate surfaces, such as relighting and AR/VR scene composition. The authors identify a gap in reconstructing complete scenes without foreground masks. Prior geometry-focused methods largely target isolated objects, and fixed-grid extraction can lose fine detail. Large, fast motion also makes it difficult to recover trajectories from a poor initialization (§§1–2, 4.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Persistent motion and direct mesh extraction.** MEMO learns separate temporal Gaussian kernel expansions for each Gaussian’s position and rotation, carrying its oriented normal along with it. It adapts Gaussian Wrapping’s vacancy field and marching tetrahedra to extract a mesh at each frame directly from the moving Gaussians (§4.1, Fig. 2, Appendix B.2).
- **Track-based initialization.** MEMO links sparse per-frame 3D points with Kalman tracking and fits initial position trajectories in closed form using Gaussian-process regression. Training then refines motion and appearance with photometric and geometric losses (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Blender-Moments benchmark.** The authors introduce nine synthetic multi-view scenes with ground-truth meshes exportable at any frame. The reported geometry evaluation uses ten evenly spaced frames per scene (§5.1, Appendix D.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| MEMO led on the primary geometry metric | On ten evenly spaced frames per Blender-Moments scene, MEMO had the best joint F-scores across all scenes and the best normal Chamfer distance on all but one. Other methods led point Chamfer on several scenes. The joint F-score requires position error below 1% of the bounding-box diagonal and normal error below 20°, evaluated on surface samples and held-out renders. | §5.2, Table 1(a), Table S5, Appendices D.4 and E |
| Both the learned representation and extraction contributed | On Blender-Moments, MEMO with TSDF still led all baselines on normal Chamfer and joint F-score. The reported comparisons support a geometry advantage on the sampled Blender-Moments frames, with the same-model extraction comparison helping isolate the contribution of meshing. Real-scene mesh evidence is qualitative because neither real dataset has ground-truth meshes in this evaluation. Full-frame image scores can obscure failures on moving regions, and training-time rankings leave end-to-end preprocessing and mesh-extraction costs unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Independent extraction gives no guarantee of shared vertices or faces across frames. Missed tracks can smear or erase thin, fast-moving parts. Lighting and texture boundaries can create false relief, and narrow camera baselines can leave depth weakly constrained (§6, Appendix A, Fig. S1).
- **Author-stated.** MEMO has the largest models among the compared methods, averaging 46% larger than FreeTimeGS, the next largest (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference.** Geometry rankings may depend on capture coverage and frame sampling. Quantitative tests use an upper-hemisphere synthetic camera rig and ten mesh frames per scene, leaving accuracy on sparsely viewed real scenes and short-lived motions uncertain (§5.1, Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference.** Reproducing the real-data comparison depends partly on shared preprocessing, including dense background clouds and manual cleanup for Neural 3D Video. The evaluation leaves sensitivity to those inputs unresolved (Appendix D.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can MEMO preserve vertex correspondence without sacrificing surface accuracy?** Independent extraction leaves correspondence unresolved. Compare a temporally coupled extraction variant with per-frame meshing on animations with known correspondences, measuring trajectory error and joint F-score.
2. **How much of the thin, fast-motion failure comes from tracking?** Appendix A identifies missed tracks but leaves the recovery ceiling unknown. Compare the current initializer with ground-truth tracks on synthetic sequences and score the moving thin parts separately.
3. **How does geometry accuracy change as camera coverage shrinks?** The narrow-baseline failures are qualitative. Vary camera count and angular coverage on the same Blender-Moments scenes and compare depth error and joint F-score.
