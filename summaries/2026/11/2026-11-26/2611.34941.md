# Improving and Expanding Video Editing Capabilities Without Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34941) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34941)


## One-sentence takeaway

I2VT uses a frozen image editor to supervise single-frame denoising rollouts, improving instruction-following on full clips across three pretrained video editors while retaining broadly comparable temporal quality on OpenVE++ (§§2.3, 3.3–3.4, 4.3).

## Problem and motivation

Instruction-guided video editors struggle with basic edits and with production tasks such as changing a performance or relighting a scene. The authors identify a data bottleneck: a paired target must carry out the requested change throughout the clip while preserving unrelated content. They ask whether a capable image editor can teach those edits to a pretrained video editor using only still images during fine-tuning (§1, Appendix A.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **I2VT.** The video editor treats each source image as a one-frame clip and rolls out its own denoising trajectory. A frozen JoyAI-Image-Edit teacher evaluates the same noisy states in a shared Wan 2.1 VAE latent space. The student’s LoRA learns to match the teacher’s velocity predictions at each step. At inference, the student edits full clips with its original sampler and settings (§§2.1–2.3, 3.1, Appendix B).
- **I2VT-17K.** The authors construct source-only training pools from first frames of OpenVE-3M clips. Each frame carries two instructions for the same edit, worded for the image teacher and video student respectively (§2.4, Appendix C).
- **OpenVE++.** The benchmark adds action changes, expression changes, whole-body movements, and scene relighting to OpenVE-Bench. It contains 207 single-edit video–instruction pairs with source clips disjoint from the training pools (§3.2, Appendix G).

The matched base-model comparisons support transfer from single-frame supervision to full clips in the tested setups. The temporal evidence supports comparable aggregate quality, with individual metric declines. The authors’ state-of-the-art claim applies to the reported comparison sets. The leading OpenVE++ model differs by judge, and the three-group transfer experiment leaves relighting to the joint evaluation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Appendix A.1 omits direct comparisons with two closely related image-only methods, citing missing code for ImVideoEdit and missing trained weights for ViFeEdit. Table 3 also combines published and newly run OpenVE-Bench scores, while OpenVE++ comparisons were rerun by the authors (§3.4, Appendix I).
- **Author-reported.** OpenVE++ evaluates single edits, non-relighting training examples require one person with a visible face and upper body, and the external IVEBench evaluation uses its short-video subset (§4.4, Appendices C and G).
- **Analyst inference.** One teacher and a shared latent representation across all tested students leave portability to other teacher–student pairings unresolved. The offline comparison and design ablations use only Bernini-R-1.3B, so their findings have narrower support than the base-to-I2VT gains (§§3.1, 3.5, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The human study supports the direction of the Bernini gains within a small acquaintance-recruited sample. Its overall-preference votes and omission of VideoCoF limit conclusions about individual quality dimensions and all three editors (Appendix H).

## Open research questions

1. **Does I2VT transfer across different VAE latent spaces?** All tested pairs share the Wan representation (§3.1). Compare direct shared-latent supervision with a latent-alignment variant under the same task and training budget.
2. **Can still-image supervision teach when an action should happen?** OpenVE++ evaluates single edits, and training provides no multi-frame supervision (§2.3, Appendix G). Test instructions requiring delayed, ordered, or repeated actions and score their timing and preservation of unrelated motion.
3. **Does online distillation retain its advantage at equal compute across editors?** The offline comparison uses one student and matches supervised states in its default configuration (§3.5). Repeat it across all three students with equal end-to-end GPU budgets that include offline target generation.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and the direction of the reported benchmark gains. The supplied text provides captions and selected values, while full table cells, run-to-run variability, per-clip scores, and independent replication are unavailable. It also does not establish checkpoint availability or confirm that the announced OpenVE++ release is accessible.
