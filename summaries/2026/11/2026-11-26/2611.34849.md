# Real2SimLab: A Unified Platform for Real-to-Simulation Transfer

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34849) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34849)


## One-sentence takeaway

Real2SimLab uses executable simulation programs as a common interface for evaluating video-to-simulation methods across existing benchmarks and for generating reward-annotated training examples.

## Problem and motivation

Real-to-simulation methods turn recordings into simulations that researchers can replay and inspect. Benchmarks differ in their inputs, required outputs, and scoring environments, so evaluating a method across benchmarks can require a separate integration for each pairing. The authors also identify a training-data leakage risk when different datasets contain copies of the same recording (§1, Fig. 2; §5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Shared evaluation interface.** Method adapters receive the visible task data and return a runnable deliverable. Benchmark adapters define tasks and scoring. The platform freezes deliverables and generally re-runs them offline, retaining official scoring where applicable and reporting separate session and re-run variants where needed (§3, Fig. 4).
- **Benchmark integrations and synthetic checks.** The authors report support for 47 coding agents and 5 non-agent reference methods across six existing benchmarks, plus a synthetic suite with exact ground truth (§4, Fig. 5).
- **Reward annotation and overlap filtering.** The pipeline compares frame thumbnails across time offsets to screen for reused recordings. It scores generated programs with the evaluation code and exports programs, rewards, and redacted agent logs (§5, Fig. 6).
- **Research map.** The authors report releasing Awesome-Real2Sim, an open-source catalog of nearly 300 papers organized by what they deliver or ask for (§§1–2, Fig. 3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer adapter integrations by design | The shared interface requires \(N+M\) integrations for \(N\) coding agents and \(M\) benchmarks, versus \(N\times M\) pairwise integrations. | §§1, 3.2; Fig. 2 |
| Most adapted benchmarks retain official scoring | Five of six use official scorers. IRIS uses trajectory reproduction scoring for ball drop, one of its eight classes. | §§3.4, 4; Fig. 5 |
| Selected adapter checks reproduce reference results | Physics-IQ scores matched the official pipeline on the same videos. Video2World reference packages reproduced recorded scores on Push-T. The synthetic data-generating program scored 1 on reproduction skill, indicating exact agreement with the reference. | §§3.4, 4 |
| Overlap detection caught most tested copies | The filter detected 36 of 40 transformed copies of synthetic evaluation videos, missing the most heavily compressed copies. It detected all 27 Physics-IQ clips identified as reused by PhysCodeGym. | §5, “Filtering out evaluation recordings” |

These checks support selected adapter results and show that the overlap filter caught known reuse in the tested settings. The integration count follows from the interface design. The paper provides no measured integration-time savings, systematic method rankings, training gains, or general overlap-detection rates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The authors defer systematic comparison of the connected methods and report no model training. All six adapted benchmarks are reserved for evaluation, and synthetic scenes are the main current source of training tasks (§§1, 5).
- **Author-stated scoring and filtering limits.** The IRIS adapter covers ball drop and measures motion reproduction, so its scores cannot be compared directly with official IRIS parameter-recovery scores. The overlap filter missed heavily compressed copies (§§2, 4–5).
- **Analyst inference.** The overlap tests establish detection on known copies. The text gives no false-positive rate on distinct recordings, so the cost of excluding valid tasks is unclear. The replay checks are specified, but aggregate pass rates across methods are unavailable (§§3.3, 5).

## Open research questions

1. **How often does scoring a frozen re-run change method rankings?** The platform reports session and re-run variants separately, but the paper gives no paired comparison. Run the same methods on both variants of Physics-IQ and PhysCodeGym and measure score shifts, rank changes, and replay pass rates (§3.3).
2. **Does reward-annotated synthetic training improve performance on real recordings?** The authors report no training runs. Fine-tune on high-reward synthetic programs and compare with the same starting model on screened, held-out real-video tasks (§5).
3. **What is the trade-off between missed copies and wrongly excluded tasks?** The paper reports detections on known copies but gives no false-positive rate. Sweep the matching threshold on labeled duplicate and distinct recording pairs, including heavily compressed copies and visually similar independent recordings (§5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the platform description and the reported checks. The supplied text does not provide a cross-method scorecard, detailed parity results for every adapter, aggregate replay pass rates, overlap false-positive rates, or downstream training results.
