# VibeVideoAgent: Closing the Perceptual Loop for Code-Driven Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34237) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34237)


## One-sentence takeaway

VibeVideoAgent gives coding agents audiovisual feedback during video production and raises mean perceptual quality for all three tested coding models on VibeVideoBench, though technical and rubric compliance vary by model (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

A rendering program can exit successfully while its video contains overlapping labels, poor camera framing, or mistimed narration. Code-driven production gives creators precise control and an editable project, but the authors identify a gap in how coding agents inspect what their programs render. Repeated raw-media inspection can inflate the coding context, while waiting for a final render can turn an early design error into substantial rework (§1, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Perception during production.** A training-free framework sends bounded media excerpts to a short-lived multimodal worker and returns compact, time- and space-grounded Findings. It adapts VideoNexus’s separation of perception and reasoning to drafts that change as code is edited (§§1–3.1).
- **Verification across drafts.** A common representation maps evidence among source, excerpt, and film clocks and records hashes for excerpts and declared source files. Seven tools support media exploration, checks that require evidence from specified modalities, reference and revision comparisons, and local stream measurements (§§3.2–3.3, Tables 4–5).
- **Staged production.** The workflow ties requirements quoted from the brief to production beats, tests the highest-risk requirement on a minimal preview, and guides rough-cut checks, targeted repair, and final delivery (§3.4).
- **Evaluation across coding agents.** The authors compare three coding models with their respective native agents and use component removals and trajectory audits to examine verification and repair behavior (§4).

The paired benchmark supports a perceptual-quality gain for the full package in the tested setting, with mixed compliance outcomes. The largest reported gains concern a combined audio-quality and synchronization score, so the contribution of synchronization alone remains unknown. The ablations support the tested removals on the diagnostic subset. The payload audit measures what enters the coding context, leaving end-to-end cost savings and the isolated effect of context separation unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Bounded sampling and selective windows can miss brief or uninspected defects. The perception model can misread dense text or fine depth ordering. Hash-bound comparisons show a visible change after an edit, while regressions outside inspected windows can remain. Intermediate renders add compute and latency, and provider updates may change behavior (§5).
- **Analyst inference.** The main results cover one benchmark, one perception model, and automated judges, one of which is also the perception model. The separate Argon results support some gains, but human agreement and transfer to other perception models remain unmeasured (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The ablations use one coding model on a deliberately stratified subset. They remove three tools individually and bundle the planning and preview changes. An otherwise matched raw-media-in-context comparison is absent. The link between local edits and better compliance comes from trajectory observations, so its causal role remains open (§§4.2–4.3, Table 3).
- **Analyst inference.** The paper’s broad description of a media-free coding context has a documented exception: `inspect_segment` can attach evidence frames. The payload audit disabled them, and the main evaluation does not report their frequency (Appendices A–B, G).

## Open research questions

1. **Do smaller patches preserve rubric compliance?** The trajectory audit associates coarse rewrites with lost details, but edit size was not assigned experimentally. Randomize patch-locality guidance within the same coding model and compare L2, L3, and regressions outside the edited shot (§4.2).
2. **What does context separation save end to end?** The audit measures payload compression without a matched coupled run. Give coupled and decoupled agents the same excerpts and tool budget, then compare total input tokens, wall time, and delivery quality (Appendix G).
3. **Do the gains survive a perception-model swap and human review?** Perception is fixed and evaluation uses model judges. Repeat paired tasks with alternative perception models and blinded human ratings of synchronization, legibility, and overall quality (§4.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and reported benchmark comparisons. The supplied text does not establish public implementation availability, repeated-run variability or confidence intervals for the main comparisons, full per-task and per-judge results, aggregate wall-time or cost deltas against native agents, or human ratings.
