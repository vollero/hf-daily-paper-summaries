# AIRIS: Benchmarking Social Embodied Intelligence in Egocentric Streaming Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34347) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34347)


## One-sentence takeaway

AIRIS shows that models can score highly on social cue grounding while still committing to intentions too early and rarely getting all parts of a proposed response right in the same episode.

## Problem and motivation

An embodied agent needs to recognize social cues, judge when they support a specific intent, and decide whether and how to respond without disrupting the interaction. The authors argue that prior benchmarks often isolate these skills or give models access to completed videos, making it hard to locate failures within an unfolding exchange (§1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **A shared timeline for four skills.** AIRIS links Attention, Intention, Reaction, and Interaction within each episode using expert-annotated anchors for cue onset, earliest inferable intent, response onset, and resolution. Each question uses observations available by its designated time, and the stages are scored independently (§2.1, Figure 1, §C.1).
- **An expert-curated benchmark.** AIRIS contains 8,000 closed-set questions across 357 egocentric clips, 11 contexts, and 36 capabilities. Attention is annotated manually. The other stages use model proposals, human annotation, and expert adjudication, followed by item-level review (§§2.2–2.4, Figure 2).
- **Diagnostics for waiting and response composition.** The Intent Anticipation Score rewards early, stable intent recovery once evidence is sufficient and discounts premature guesses. Reaction Joint-EM requires the engagement decision, action domain, subaction, and applicable speech function to match together. The authors use these metrics in a zero-shot evaluation of 45 models (§§2.3, 3.1–3.2, §D.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest headline result | In the zero-shot AIRIS evaluation, Gemini-3.7-Flash led the main table with 0.726 Overall, the mean of four stage scores, and led Attention at 0.936 and Intention at 0.569. | Table 2, §§2.3, 3.2 |
| Intent anticipation remained weak | Before the expert-defined decidability point, mean false-commitment rates across model categories ranged from 0.26 to 0.51. Gemini-3.7-Flash had the best Intent Anticipation Score at 0.237 and still committed on 0.421 of its early queries. | §3.2, Figure 4c, Table 8 |
| Joint scoring reordered the Reaction leaderboard | HY-Embodied-VLM-1.0 led aggregate Reaction at 0.688 but scored 0.028 on same-event Joint-EM. RynnBrain-30B-A3B led Joint-EM at 0.147 with an aggregate Reaction score of 0.410. | §3.2, Table 9 |
| Compliance and violation scores differed | Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct led resolved-episode Compliance at 0.923 and the Violation composite, which includes online and episode-level probes, at 0.589. | §C.2, Table 10 |
| Explicit reasoning had mixed effects | With identical streaming inputs and thinking-aware answer parsing, the reported Overall changes versus no-think were +0.1 points for Qwen3.6-27B, −6.4 for Qwen3.6-35B, and +2.9 for Qwen3-Omni. Online intent and compliance scores fell for all three. | §3.2, Figure 5 |

The evidence establishes that model rankings depend on whether AIRIS credits individual answers, timely intent recovery, or jointly correct response choices. Reaction Joint-EM measures agreement with reference choices within Reaction. The benchmark scores the four stages separately, so the results leave open how errors propagate through a single acting policy and whether its choices would succeed with a human partner (§2.1, §4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-identified scope.** The authors leave robot-in-the-loop testing, broader social contexts, and open-ended generation to future work (§4). The think/no-think analysis also acknowledges that harder items tend to elicit longer traces, complicating interpretation of the association between trace length and accuracy (§3.2, Figure 5).
- **Analyst inference on label validity.** Expert adjudication gives AIRIS a consistent decision rule, but the supplied audit description gives no agreement statistics for the earliest inferable intent. Disagreement near that anchor could affect both the false-commitment rate and the Intent Anticipation Score (§2.3, §C.4).
- **Analyst inference on model comparisons.** The harness supplies raw audio to omni-modal models and transcripts to standard VLMs. The reported comparisons change both model and input representation, leaving the effect of native audio or streaming architecture unresolved (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Analyst observation on reporting.** Table 2’s grounding column and Table 7’s spatial-localization column differ for some models without a clear reconciliation in the supplied text. For Gemini-3.7-Flash, the reported zero-shot Attention values are 0.948 and 0.911, respectively. Those columns should be compared within their respective tables.

## Open research questions

1. **How stable is the earliest-inferable-intent annotation?** The paper describes adjudication but gives no agreement distribution (§C.4). Have independent raters mark the anchor from progressively revealed prefixes, then recompute model scores across plausible anchor placements.
2. **Can a model reduce premature commitment and still recover intent promptly?** Table 8 shows that low false commitment can coexist with poor anticipation. Compare an explicit abstention rule with the same model’s default behavior on held-out episodes, reporting false commitment, anticipation score, and time to stable correctness together.
3. **Does joint response scoring predict success in live interaction?** AIRIS uses fixed recordings and reference choices (§2.1, §4). Evaluate the same policies on matched robot-in-the-loop tasks and test whether Joint-EM predicts intervention timing, task completion, and partner-rated appropriateness.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the benchmark formulation and the central anticipation and Reaction findings, which are supported by explicit metric definitions and per-model tables. The supplied text does not establish dataset or evaluation-code access and licensing, annotator agreement, a human performance baseline, uncertainty intervals for model comparisons, or the relationship between the two Attention grounding columns.
