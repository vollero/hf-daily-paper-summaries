# Beyond Self-Consistency: Recursive Self-Improvement at Test Time

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30965) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965)


## One-sentence takeaway

On five competition-math benchmarks across three Qwen3 model sizes, TT-RSI combined sequential solution refinement with label-free self-distillation and averaged 4.79 percentage points higher accuracy than TTPO with self-consistency at a matched generated-token budget (Table 1, §4.2, Appendix D).

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks how a deployed language model can improve on unlabeled questions using its own outputs. The authors identify two weaknesses in majority-based self-consistency. Independent samples discard reasoning from earlier attempts, and training on the most frequent answer can reinforce an error when that answer is wrong (§1, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **In-Context RSI.** The model repeatedly re-solves a question with the latest full visible derivation and answer as a neutral reference. It can reuse, revise, or discard that reasoning, and the chain returns the final attempt’s answer (§§3.1–3.2, Appendix B).
- **In-Weight RSI.** The evolving model generates an initial solution and its immediate refinement. A frozen initial-model teacher uses these references to provide token-level guidance on the student’s own rollouts. Recursive Distillation trains both answering and refinement. Accelerated Distillation also teaches the first attempt from the refined reference, emphasizing tokens where the two teacher predictions differ most (§3.3, Figure 2).
- **Conditional theory.** The authors show how exact distributional backups can make trained attempts match later attempts of the initial model in distribution. Convergence to the initial refinement chain’s stationary distribution requires a mixing condition (§3.3, Appendix A).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Weight updates improved first attempts | In-Weight RSI increased mean first-attempt avg@12 accuracy by 5.79 percentage points over Base across the same model sizes and benchmarks. | Table 1, §4.2 |

The main results establish gains when each method can adapt on the unlabeled questions it will be evaluated on. The transfer experiment extends the evidence to new questions within competition math. The wrong-majority result shows recovery on a small subset, and the generated-token comparison leaves end-to-end inference latency unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning and Qwen3 models up to 8B parameters. Open-ended tasks and tool-using agents are proposed extensions (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The matched-budget comparison counts output tokens and treats reference-input processing as negligible. Sequential dependencies and input processing could change the comparison under a latency budget (§4.4, Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference.** The theory uses exact sequence-level backups, while the implementation uses clipped, weighted token-level losses with shared parameters. The proof therefore gives a conditional account of the refinement mechanism, and convergence of the implemented training remains an empirical question (Appendix A.2–A.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does TT-RSI transfer beyond Qwen3 competition math?** The reported transfer stays within one model family and one domain. Adapt another model family on unlabeled open-ended tasks and evaluate held-out outputs with an independent rubric.
2. **Does the efficiency advantage hold at matched latency?** The inference comparison matches generated tokens. Benchmark both methods on the same hardware at matched wall-clock budgets, including reference processing and parallel sampling.
3. **When do additional updates or refinements start to hurt?** The theory relies on fixed transition rates or mixing conditions, and the refinement ablation pools rates across attempts. Measure fix and break rates by chain depth and training checkpoint, then test whether they predict held-out accuracy and a useful stopping point.
