# 3D CoCa v3: Contrastive Learners with Scene Graphs for Urban Scene Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33835) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33835)


## One-sentence takeaway

3D CoCa v3 combines multi-view RGB and geometry with scene-graph-derived captions, improving overall top-1 accuracy over 3D CoCa v2 by 20.88 percentage points on City-3DQA’s sentence-wise split and 3.57 points on its city-wise split (§5.3).

## Problem and motivation

Urban 3D question answering requires both fine geometric detail and relationships spanning objects and viewpoints. The authors argue that existing 3D vision-language methods emphasize global alignment or individual objects, leaving explicit spatial relationships underused in frameworks that combine contrastive learning with generation (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multi-view geometric pre-training.** Scene-CLIP fuses aligned RGB and XYZ point maps, pools view embeddings into a scene embedding, and aligns both levels with visual and language targets. Within-scene caption matching and Chamfer-based neighborhood consistency add view-level supervision (§4.2).
- **Graph-derived language supervision and data.** City3D-9.6K extends 193 City-3DQA scenes with 9,650 aligned RGB views, point maps, and view captions, plus multi-view scene graphs (MSG) and graph-informed scene captions. It retains the original QA pairs (§3, Fig. 2).
- **Question-conditioned decoding.** A rule-based retriever selects a relevant scene caption when one matches the question. A LoRA-adapted Qwen decoder generates the answer using gated cross-attention to frozen visual features. The final reported configuration uses the CoCa-style decoder (§4.3, Table S1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher sentence-wise top-1 accuracy | City-3DQA overall Acc@1 was **88.35%**, up **20.88 percentage points** over 3D CoCa v2. Multi-hop Acc@1 rose from **55.89% to 80.97%**. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Smaller gain on disjoint cities | City-3DQA city-wise overall Acc@1 was **64.79%**, ahead of 3D CoCa v2 by **3.57 points** and Sg-CityU by **1.03 points**. Multi-hop Acc@1 was **55.02%**, up **5.06 points** over v2. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Best on seven of eight reported GeoEval3D metrics | On GoogleEarth, spatial reasoning and comparison accuracy reached **75.56%** and **82.34%**, versus GeoProg3D’s **64.00%** and **59.73%**. | Alignment, fusion, and retrieval helped in city-wise ablations | On City-3DQA, removing view-level alignment reduced overall Acc@1 from **64.79% to 62.05%**. The evidence supports stronger top-ranked answers under the reported protocols. The authors report that several baselines still lead on City-3DQA Acc@10. The MSG ablation is mixed across decoders, and the GeoEval3D results in Table 3 cover a subset of the benchmark tasks, with grounding scores absent (§§5.1, 5.3–5.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The model targets static scenes, City3D-9.6K covers a limited range of urban environments, and fine-grained measurement and answer ranking remain weaknesses (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference.** City3D-9.6K derives from the same scenes as City-3DQA. Although the QA split holds out cities, the supplied method does not specify whether those cities were also excluded from contrastive pre-training. The result therefore leaves open how well the full pipeline transfers to cities unseen at every training stage (§§3, 5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The available ablations leave the independent contributions of graph-derived supervision during pre-training and graph-derived captions during decoding unresolved. The CoCa MSG comparison also reports gains for both hop categories alongside a slight decline overall, with the aggregation unexplained in the supplied prose (§5.4, Table 5).

## Open research questions

1. **How large is the city-wise gain when held-out cities are excluded from every training stage?** Pre-training exclusions are unspecified. Rebuild City3D-9.6K for each city-wise split using training cities only and compare with the reported setup (§§3, 5.1–5.2).
2. **Where does graph-derived language contribute most?** MSG effects vary by decoder, and the available results leave its two uses unresolved. Independently toggle graph-derived captions during pre-training and decoding with a fixed backbone and caption budget (§§4.2–4.3, 5.4).
3. **Can temporal relations improve answers about moving objects?** The framework models static scenes. Compare static and temporal variants on matched urban sequences with questions about motion and changing spatial relationships (Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The headline City-3DQA results are explicit, and Table 3 provides the GeoEval3D comparisons. The supplied text lacks full entries for Tables 2 and 4–7, run-to-run uncertainty, split-specific pre-training membership, detailed caption retrieval rules, and dataset or checkpoint release details. The method also leaves the named Pyramid Vision Encoder’s pyramid structure unspecified. The main text and supplement differ on the downstream view count and pre-training learning rate, while the decoder training curve covers fewer epochs than the main training description (§§4.2–4.3, 5.2; Appendices A–C).
