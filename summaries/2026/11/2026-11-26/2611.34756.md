# Collect for Coverage, Replay with Evidence: Adaptive and Efficient On-Policy Distillation for Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34756) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34756)


## One-sentence takeaway

CARE-OPD combines student-seeded histories, teacher-confidence filtering, and hindsight turn selection to reach 59.5% task success on SWE-bench Verified while cutting end-to-end training time by 45% and environment actions by 44% relative to asynchronous one-turn on-policy distillation (§§4.2–4.3, §5).

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon on-policy distillation (OPD) requires fresh environment rollouts, so slow tool calls and variable trajectory lengths can dominate training time. Prefix replay decouples collection from training, but teacher-only histories omit states reached through student actions, and fixed position decay can suppress useful late decisions. The authors identify a joint design problem: broaden history coverage while preserving reliable teacher supervision, then allocate replay to turns whose value is supported by execution evidence (§§1, 3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Replay decomposition.** The authors separate trajectory sampling, turn allocation, current-student response sampling, and response acceptance, clarifying where collection and training decisions affect the updates (§3.2, Eq. 3).
2. **Student-seeded collection with confidence gating.** A frozen student starts a subset of rollouts and the teacher completes them. Only teacher continuation turns are eligible for replay. At training time, the current student generates a fresh response from a stored prefix, and a minibatch-relative gate filters responses with low mean teacher top-token probability before the reverse-KL update (§§3.1, 3.3, Algorithm 1).
3. **Hindsight turn allocation.** After a verified rollout, the rollout teacher uses downstream observations and the outcome to select a fixed fraction of eligible turns. The selection query reuses the trajectory KV cache, and training uses the original prefix without future evidence (§§3.4–3.5, Algorithm 1).

The matched efficiency comparison supports a quality and cost improvement over asynchronous one-turn OPD in the reported SWE setting. The allocation ablation supports the value of model-based selection over random selection at the same subset size. It leaves open how much of the gain specifically requires later execution evidence, since the reported comparison does not include a selector with that evidence withheld.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors.** The replay pool uses a frozen initial student and bounded roll-ins, so it can become less representative as the student learns. Replayed responses are never executed, leaving their validity and downstream effects unobserved. The teacher-confidence score measures certainty and can diverge from correctness (§5).
- **Analyst inference.** The detailed efficiency and component comparisons are reported for SWE. Deep Research is evaluated with one model family because the authors omitted Qwen3 training there after observing a small teacher–student accuracy gap. Transfer of the cost savings and component gains to other settings remains uncertain (§§4.1, 4.3–4.5, Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference.** The supplied text includes table captions and selected results in prose, but omits the numerical cells needed to inspect all baseline scores and seed variability. The reported rankings therefore cannot be checked against the full score distributions (§4.1, Tables 1–4).

## Open research questions

1. **When is replay-pool refresh worth its environment cost?** The method uses a fixed pool, and §5 identifies drift as a risk. Compare fixed and periodically refreshed pools under the same total environment-action budget.
2. **Does teacher confidence predict whether a replayed response will work?** The training loop leaves response outcomes unobserved (§5). Execute a stratified sample across confidence levels, then compare confidence-only and execution-aware gates under a matched update budget.
3. **How much does downstream evidence contribute to turn selection?** The reported baselines vary the selection rule without isolating access to future observations (§4.5). Hold the selector, candidate turns, and selection budget fixed, then mask later observations and outcomes in a comparison run.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The method and headline SWE results are explicit. The supplied text omits the full per-method scores and sample standard deviations, including the absolute CARE-OPD BrowseComp-Plus accuracy, as well as the exact hindsight selection prompt. It also does not establish whether code, trained checkpoints, or the curated training pool were released.
