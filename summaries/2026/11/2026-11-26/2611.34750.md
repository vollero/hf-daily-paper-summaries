# SOLVE: Sparse, Ordered Keyposes Make LLM Agents Versatile and Efficient Robot Manipulator

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34750) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34750)


## One-sentence takeaway

SOLVE uses a frozen multimodal LLM to issue sparse, visually grounded sequences of end-effector poses, outperforming reported simulation baselines and completing 75 of 85 zero-shot trials on a real UR12e (§4.2, Tables 1, 3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Step-wise LLM controllers incur a model call for each small move, while code-as-policy systems depend on predefined primitives and may receive feedback too late to recover within a sequence. The authors ask whether an agent can issue enough motion per call to reduce inference cost while still observing outcomes often enough to correct errors (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Sparse action interface.** The authors quantify how much teleoperated motion compresses, then use that finding to design a direct policy. The frozen LLM chooses the spacing, speed, and order of absolute 6-DoF end-effector targets. A planner executes the segments, gripper commands take effect on arrival, and the agent receives visual and execution feedback after each sequence (§§3.1–3.2, Figures 2–3).
- **Sparse metric grounding.** The agent selects task-relevant pixels and receives world coordinates tagged with their measurement source. Simulation uses epipolar matching with depth-estimation and table-plane fallbacks. Hardware uses wrist stereo, cross-camera triangulation, and a table-plane fallback (§3.3, Appendices C.2 and E.2).
- **Rollout-derived rules.** The main simulation evaluations use bounded rules distilled from independent rollout sets on layouts disjoint from evaluation, without updating model weights. RoboDojo also uses demonstration-derived guides where available. The hardware evaluation uses neither guides nor experience notes (§§3.4, 4.1, Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Teleoperated trajectories compress substantially | At 2 cm and 10° tolerance, a grasp or release takes 4.9 keyposes in RoboDojo and 6.0 in DROID, compared with medians of 56 and 96 recorded actions, respectively. | §3.2, Figure 3, Appendix C.1 |
| RoboDojo leaderboard gain | GPT-6 Astra SOLVE with experience notes reaches 74.5% under the official dimension-averaged metric over 2,100 episodes, 18.1 percentage points ahead of GO-3 Nano Preview. The reported same-backbone RoboProbe result is 22.5%. | §4.2, Table 1, Appendix B |
| RoboLab gain with experience notes | Across 600 episodes, success rises from 79.8% without notes to 84.5% with notes on the same evaluation layouts. The latter is 27.4 points ahead of SenseNova-RoboRSI. | §4.2, Table 3, Appendix B |
| Lower inference cost and fewer calls | GPT-6 Astra SOLVE averages $1.70 per episode at list prices across RoboDojo and RoboLab. The cited GPT-6 Astra Direct averages $21.10 on its public 50-episode RoboDojo set. On the reported RoboDojo comparison, SOLVE uses 36 versus 154 model calls per episode and achieves 74% versus 26% success. | §4.2, Appendix B |
| Zero-shot hardware performance | SOLVE completes 75 of 85 UR12e episodes (88%) and succeeds at least once on each of 16 tasks, without guides or notes. Episodes with full logs average 21.9 calls and 6.6 minutes. | §4.2, Table 4, Appendix E.2 |
| Component and batching effects | In the standard-split RoboDojo ablation, average success moves from 22.9% step-wise to 49.5% with keyposes and their executor, 62.2% with metric queries, and 68.3% with merged notes. In a separate cap sweep, allowing one versus five keyposes per move lowers calls from 56.6 to 33.7 per episode, with success of 69.8% versus 68.2%. | §§4.3–4.4, Table 5, Figure 6, Appendix B |
| RoboTwin robustness under scene randomization | With one demonstration-derived guide per task and notes carried across episodes, SOLVE achieves 94.6% Easy and 95.0% Hard success over 1,000 episodes. The best leaderboard results are 95.3% Easy and 84.8% Hard under a different trial and demonstration protocol. | Appendix E.3, Table 11 |

The cap sweep gives the clearest evidence that batching reduces model calls with little observed change in success on that seed. The larger ablation gain bundles the keypose interface with its executor, leaving their individual effects unresolved. The compression study establishes geometric fidelity within tolerance. Its demonstrations lack object-state records, so successful execution of the compressed trajectories remains untested (Appendix C.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Contact-rich tasks can require force or continuous feedback. Inference latency confines the tested system to quasi-static scenes, and hardware failures include slipping fabric, incomplete insertion, and inadequate wrist-camera clearance (§5, Appendix E.2).
- **Author-reported.** Continuously updated notes can overwrite earlier lessons and fluctuate in effectiveness. RoboTwin comparisons also differ in trial counts, demonstration use, and when experience notes are available (§4.4, Appendices C.3 and E.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Hardware transfer is demonstrated on one physical platform, with human success judgments and only one RoboProbe attempt per task. The results leave comparative reliability across hardware and operators uncertain (Appendix E.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The headline cost ratio compares averages from different episode pools. The supplied text gives per-episode API pricing and describes offline rule generation, while the cost of that offline work and local compute remains unspecified (§4.2, Appendix B).

## Open research questions

- **Does force feedback improve contact-rich execution?** Insertion and folding failures persist on hardware. Compare the same keypose policy with and without a force-aware low-level controller on repeated trials, recording success and protective stops (§5, Appendix E.2).
- **When should the agent interrupt a keypose sequence for feedback?** The cap sweep tests fixed limits, while hardware drawing uses a larger cap. Compare fixed limits with interruptions triggered by contact or perceptual uncertainty, measuring success, calls, and elapsed time (§4.4, Appendix E.2).
- **Do rollout-derived rules transfer to new tasks?** The main notes evaluation holds out layouts within tasks. Evaluate general rules alone on held-out tasks and compare with a no-notes policy under matched rollout budgets (§3.4, Appendix B).
