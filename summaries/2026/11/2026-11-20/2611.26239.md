# Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models to Stop Safely

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26239) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26239)


## One-sentence takeaway

On selected LIBERO tasks where scripted holding passed a precheck, State-Aware Fallback Imitation reduced OpenVLA-OFT’s prohibited-task success after takeover from 99.7% to 1.3%, with no recorded physical incidents or episodes meeting the action-saturation criterion (§4.3, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

A robot policy can suppress a prohibited task from its initial state yet finish it when handed control partway through. It can also leave the task unfinished while causing a physical incident, such as dropping a held bottle. The authors use stove activation and bottle placement to show why an evaluation must examine both intermediate execution states and the physical response after takeover (§2, Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Intermediate-state takeover audit.** The original policy starts the task, then each edited policy takes over from matched restored states. The audit measures target attainment and physical events over the observed rollout (§§2, 4.1, C.2–C.3).
- **Fallback supervision across execution states.** After verifying that scripted holding is appropriate, SAFI relabels action chunks throughout prohibited-task demonstrations. It requests no arm motion and repeats each chunk’s first demonstrated gripper command, preserving the observations and instruction (§3.1, Appendix B.1).
- **Joint adaptation to preserve permitted skills.** Starting from OpenVLA-OFT, SAFI updates language-model LoRA adapters and the action head using equally weighted imitation losses for fallback targets and original retained-task actions (§§3.2, 4.2).

The matched-state results support the authors’ claim that SAFI can suppress ongoing execution while largely maintaining the intended hold on eligible benchmark tasks. The incident counts cover defined events within finite observation windows. Retained success measures task performance from normal starts, and the longer-window result shows that suppression can weaken as the rollout continues.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated limitations.** Evaluation used one VLA architecture, selected benchmark tasks, and a fixed training seed. The authors identify physical robots, additional embodiments, and repeated training runs as open validation settings. SAFI learns holding, which can be unsuitable for unstable objects or ongoing contact (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** Requiring scripted holding to pass before task inclusion limits the reported rates to tasks favorable to that fallback. The observation windows also vary by setting, and some Goal placement rollouts end at success, limiting comparisons of later physical events (Appendices C.1–C.3, D.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The coverage ablation changes temporal coverage and associated gripper labels together, so it cannot isolate their individual effects. The paper also reports that its OFT implementation of VLA-Forget changes action outputs after checkpoint reload, which qualifies comparisons with that baseline (Appendices D.2, C.5).

## Open research questions

- **How well does SAFI stop from unfamiliar intermediate states?** The reported takeovers come from successful donor trajectories at specified boundaries. Evaluate the same checkpoints at additional takeover times and from perturbed manipulation states (Appendix C.2).
- **Can the method learn a fallback when holding is unsuitable?** The applicability check excludes such cases. Compare supervised controlled placement or withdrawal against scripted references on tasks where holding fails the precheck (Limitations, §3.1).
- **How stable are the gains across training runs and hardware?** The fixed-seed OpenVLA-OFT evaluation leaves both sources of variation open. Repeat the adaptation across seeds, then run matched takeover audits on a physical robot under varied sensing conditions (Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information
