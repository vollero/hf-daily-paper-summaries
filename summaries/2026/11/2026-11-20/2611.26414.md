# ZeroVLN: Learning to Navigate without Supervised Fine-Tuning of the VLM Brain

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26414) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26414)


## One-sentence takeaway

ZeroVLN jointly trains a vision-language model and a continuous-action expert through closed-loop reinforcement learning, achieving the highest reported success rates among matched-backbone, matched-episode baselines on three simulated navigation benchmarks while largely retaining the base model’s visual benchmark accuracy and avoiding navigation-domain supervised fine-tuning of the VLM.

## Problem and motivation

Vision-language navigation in continuous environments requires an agent to interpret instructions and visual observations, execute precise movements, and recover when movement fails. The authors report that adapting a general-purpose VLM to produce pixel goals or numerical motion commands can degrade its performance on general visual tasks. They ask whether a VLM can use its existing language and tool-use capabilities to direct a trainable controller and improve through interaction. (§1, Fig. 1)

## Approach and contributions

1. **Semantic interface for slow–fast navigation.** The VLM uses instructions, RGB observations, tool history, and the executed trajectory to request movement in natural language or to turn or stop. A flow-matching action expert executes a short prefix of each predicted action sequence, then replans from fresh RGB observations. (§2, Figs. 2–3)
2. **Supervision grounded in the VLM’s own requests.** With the VLM frozen, a privileged planner uses mesh-derived semantics and geometry to produce and verify reference trajectories for its sampled requests. The expert’s supervised loss covers the action prefix that will actually be executed before replanning. (§3, App. B.2–B.3)
3. **Joint reinforcement learning with shared trajectory credit.** Both policies learn from the same leave-one-out trajectory advantage through separate gradient paths. Rewards cover terminal success, first arrival, distance progress, and discovery of task-relevant entities. The authors claim priority for this joint slow–fast optimization in VLN-CE and for a slow–fast VLN framework without navigation-domain VLM SFT. (Abstract, §4, App. A)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Joint RL improves navigation success | On official evaluation sets with a 200-round limit, SR rose from 36.9% to 51.3% on SAGE-3D, 17.0% to 52.7% on R2R, and 18.7% to 51.4% on RxR. Final SR was highest among the matched-backbone, matched-source-episode comparisons on all three. | Table 1, §§5.1–5.2.2 |
| More goal visits, lower path efficiency | On official R2R and RxR evaluations, ZeroVLN’s OSR was 66.7% and 64.6% versus matched NaVILA’s 51.8% and 55.6%. SPL was 29.2% and 28.1% versus NaVILA’s 41.8% and 43.0%, respectively. | Table 1, §5.2.1 |
| Smaller VLM changes and stronger visual benchmark retention | Relative VLM weight displacement from the shared base was 0.01497% for ZeroVLN, 3.21% for DualVLN, and 7.67% for NaVILA. Across twelve visual and spatial benchmarks, free-form mean scores were 59.16% for ZeroVLN, 59.47% for the base, and 0% for both SFT baselines. In a separate next-token option readout on 100 questions from each of seven benchmarks, ZeroVLN scored 62.00% versus the base’s 62.86%. | Figs. 7–8, App. D.5 and E.1 |
| Ablations support prefix supervision and joint updates | Prefix-only supervision outperformed full-sequence supervision with identical reference trajectories and a frozen VLM. On the fixed navigation subsets, joint updates sustained gains beyond either policy alone. Trajectory-level credit improved SR and SPL over the tested round-level estimator, which yielded higher OSR. | Figs. 9, 11–13, App. D.1 |

The evidence supports higher navigation success and near-base visual benchmark scores for this configuration. SAGE-3D credits arrival, while R2R and RxR also require stopping in the goal region, so the success rates measure different completion criteria. The option-token readout weakens a formatting-only explanation for the SFT baselines’ low scores. The matched comparisons hold the backbone and source episodes fixed while varying action interfaces, supervision pipelines, and training exposure, leaving the independent effect of omitting VLM SFT unresolved. (§5.1, App. D.4–E.1)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ reported trade-off.** ZeroVLN visits the goal region more often than matched NaVILA on R2R and RxR but has lower SPL. The authors attribute the trade-off to additional exploration and recovery. (§5.2.1)
- **Analyst inference.** Training depends on privileged mesh-derived maps, execution verification, and simulator feedback. The reported evaluation establishes performance in simulation, leaving physical transfer and training with weaker scene information unresolved. (§§3–5, App. B.2)
- **Analyst inference.** Request-grounded and fixed-route anchoring were compared with different episode subsets, contexts, executors, training exposure, and rollout budgets. The augmentation runs used one training seed and different exposure. These comparisons motivate request grounding but leave its isolated effect uncertain. (App. D.2)
- **Analyst inference.** The trajectory-consistency check uses accepted requests from SAGE-3D training episodes and evaluates only the first execution prefix. It leaves execution fidelity on unseen scenes and across entire movement calls uncertain. (§5.4, App. E.2)

## Open research questions

1. **How much does pairing each request with its own trajectory improve navigation?** The anchoring comparison varies several pipeline and evaluation choices. Train both variants with matched contexts, executors, supervision budgets, and seeds, then evaluate them on identical episodes and rollout budgets. (App. D.2)
2. **Can ZeroVLN retain its goal-reaching advantage while improving path efficiency?** R2R and RxR show higher OSR and lower SPL than matched NaVILA. Test a path-cost reward across several weights and report SR, OSR, and SPL together on the official sets. (§5.2.1)
3. **How much privileged scene information is needed during training?** The planner uses mesh-derived semantics and geometry. Compare reference trajectories generated from full meshes with those generated from observation-built maps, holding the policy architecture and episode pool fixed. (§3, App. B.2)
