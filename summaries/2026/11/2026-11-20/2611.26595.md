# Coda-DaVinci: Reimagining Video Generation with Code2Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26595) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26595)


## One-sentence takeaway

Coda-DaVinci selectively uses executable 3D scene proxies to improve instruction following and scene consistency in the reported video benchmarks, with task-dependent trade-offs in motion naturalness and weaker transitions than direct generation in the long-video study.

## Problem and motivation

Video generators can produce realistic footage while missing detailed instructions, losing scene state after a camera revisit, or depicting implausible interactions. Executable 3D proxies give explicit control over layout, motion, and state, but errors in proxy geometry or animation can carry through to the finished video. The authors identify a gap in knowing when to use a proxy, how much detail to model, and how to coordinate simulation and prompting with the rendered scene (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled component study.** The authors vary proxy granularity, Blender simulation requirements, and prompt enhancement, then use blinded human ratings to identify task-dependent trade-offs (§3; Appendix B.1).
- **Adaptive generation workflow.** Coda-DaVinci routes tasks that need precise structure or motion control to 3D modeling and can route natural body movement directly to synthesis when explicit control is unnecessary. On the 3D route, it uses medium detail for environments and structured objects, coarse proxies for people, and simulation for supported interactions. The coding agent inspects the render before writing a prompt that binds proxy entities to appearance references and describes fine actions (§§4.1–4.2; Figures 1 and 5).
- **Persistent workspace and repair loop.** A lead agent integrates editable assets and preserves scene state across revisions and continuations. Visual inspection helps locate errors in the proxy or synthesis, and an independent acceptance agent checks candidates against task requirements. Experiments probe component effects, model pairings, rendering engines, and long-video continuation (§§4.3, 5.3–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

The matched human study supports task-dependent effects of proxy design, and the shared-generator comparisons support the reported benchmark rankings. The evidence leaves open whether those rankings generalize to other generators, how large most margins are, and how much of the prompting gain comes from the text versus the identity cues in the render.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Detailed proxies can make articulated motion stiff, and video synthesis can deviate even when proxy actions or camera movements are correct. Long-video transition stability and setting adherence remain weaker than direct continuation on their respective benchmarks (§§3.3, 5.3–5.4, 6).
- **Analyst inference.** The matched component sample excludes inputs that fail to return a valid output in any configuration, partly because of Seedance’s privacy policy. Quality rankings therefore apply to retained cases, and the effect of filtering on the rankings is unknown (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The agent-aligned prompting intervention changes both prompt content and proxy identity colors. The simulation contrast tests required versus optional simulation, since optional runs could still use a solver. These comparisons leave the separate effects of identity colors and actual solver use unresolved (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The component ablations use one automated judge on a small diagnostic subset. Their agreement with blinded human judgments on the same ablations is unreported (§5.3).

## Open research questions

1. **When does adaptive routing select the better output?** The fixed-3D ablation reports aggregate scores, leaving task-level routing accuracy open. Generate both routes for each held-out task and compare the selected route with blinded human preferences under matched generation budgets (§5.3).
2. **How much do identity colors contribute to the prompting gain?** The existing intervention changes both the proxy and the prompt. Cross neutral versus color-coded proxies with matched prompts that include or omit explicit entity bindings, keeping geometry and simulation fixed (Appendix B.1).
3. **Can scene reuse preserve state without weakening clip boundaries?** The continuation study reports state and structure gains alongside weaker transitions or setting adherence. Compare tail-frame and overlapping-clip conditioning on matched sequences and score event fulfillment, boundary continuity, and setting adherence separately (§5.4).
