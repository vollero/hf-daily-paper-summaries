# Probing the &quot;Conscious&quot; Mind: Selective Access and Global Workspace in Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26590) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26590)


## One-sentence takeaway

MC-Lens reveals query-dependent changes in which visual concepts can be read from vision-language models, and interventions on the identified activation subspaces can redirect answers in direct, one-hop, and multi-hop visual question answering (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation

A vision-language model’s answer gives limited insight into where visual concepts are represented, how the question selects among them, or whether readable information contributes to the answer. The authors extend the language-model Jacobian Lens to study those questions across vision and language components, using global workspace theory to motivate tests of selective access and downstream use (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multimodal Concept Lens (MC-Lens).** V-Lens and L-Lens use layerwise averaged Jacobians and the decoder’s output head to rank vocabulary tokens from intermediate visual and language states. L-Lens gives equal weight to image, text, and template token categories. Visual readouts can subtract a reference mean computed from ordinary photographs (§§2.1–2.2, Fig. 3).
- **Concept direction sets and interventions.** The authors expand single-name-token probes using either word forms or vocabulary directions selected by greedy state reconstruction. Their subspace replacement operator, SubRep, copies the projection of a matched target activation into the source activation (§§2.3–2.4, Figs. 4–6).
- **Alignment diagnostics and cross-model evaluation.** The authors introduce J-state cosine and reconstruction coverage (ReconCov) to track image–text concept alignment across training checkpoints. They also compare concept readout, selectivity, and intervention effects across Qwen3-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, and Gemma 3 (§§6–7.1).

The interventions establish that editing the selected activation components can change generated answers in the tested settings. The authors interpret query selectivity and cross-task effects as functional evidence consistent with global workspace theory. Subjective experience falls outside the study’s scope, and the experiments leave open whether the same mechanism explains these effects across model families (opening note, §§5–6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Workspace boundaries were estimated manually. Readout and selection profiles used to place those boundaries cannot independently validate them (App. A.4).
- **Author-reported scope.** Material labels were scattered in the illustrated patch maps, and the autoregressive evidence came from two case studies (§§3.3, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The causal experiments rely heavily on flag-based geographic questions, and several edit all decoder layers. They establish control over answers in that setting, while transfer to other domains and localization to a particular layer remain open. Shorter edit windows were tested, with one-hop control benefiting from wider windows (§§5.1–5.4, App. B.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Necessity and sufficiency rates are conditional on cases where the original image yields a correct answer and the grey image yields an incorrect one. Those rates cannot be extrapolated to all VQA inputs from the supplied evidence (§5.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can independently chosen workspace boundaries predict where causal edits work?** The reported boundaries partly use readout and selection profiles. Estimate the intervals on separate data, then compare equal-width edits inside and outside them on held-out questions (App. A.4, App. B.3).
2. **Do the same concept directions transfer beyond geographic facts?** The cross-task and multi-hop intervention suites use flags. Fit directions for natural-image objects, then test whether they redirect answers to held-out questions about attributes or relations (§§5.3–5.4).
3. **Does concept readability reliably develop before selectivity under other training regimes?** The visible evidence describes one frozen-backbone setup and gives a qualitative ordering. Repeat the checkpoint analysis across seeds and compare connector training with decoder fine-tuning (§1(iii), §7, App. A.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text describes the method and main readout and intervention comparisons, but the source was truncated mid-§7.1 and coverage is partial. Missing information includes the detailed checkpoint results and metric comparisons, the full discussion and author-stated limitations, exact values for many plotted comparisons, and whether code, fitted lenses, or constructed evaluation cases were released.
