# Depth Pro 2: Sharp and Temporally Consistent Metric Monocular Depth

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26603) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26603)


## One-sentence takeaway

Depth Pro 2 uses one native-resolution transformer for monocular images and video, and its H+ variant achieves the best average ranks among evaluated methods for zero-shot metric depth accuracy by δ₁, boundary sharpness by F1, and temporal consistency by track jitter (Tables 2–5).

## Problem and motivation

Dense monocular depth estimation needs to recover physical distances, preserve fine occlusion boundaries, and keep predictions stable across video frames. The authors identify a benchmark trade-off: single-image methods often preserve detail but fluctuate across frames, while multi-frame methods tend to be steadier and smooth over fine structure. Depth-guided bokeh illustrates why both properties matter, since errors around a moving subject’s silhouette can make the rendered blur unstable (§1, Figs. 1–2, Appendix D.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified native-resolution depth model.** A DINOv3-initialized vision transformer processes one or more frames, alternating within-frame and cross-frame attention in its later blocks without adding attention parameters or encoding frame order. A DPT decoder predicts focal-normalized inverse depth per frame (§3.1, Fig. 3).
- **Joint image and video training.** Training mixes single images, video, and multi-view clips of up to 32 frames and avoids interpolating depth labels. The proposed consistency loss uses ground-truth camera poses to reproject predictions between frames, masking correspondences that violate static-scene geometry using ground-truth depth (§3.2, Appendix A.1).
- **Calibration and streaming.** Metric conversion uses supplied focal length or a lightweight field-of-view estimator shared by all four variants. Video inference supports overlapping buffered clips and causal unbuffered processing with cached attention keys and values (§§3.1, 3.3–3.4, Table 1).

The reported comparisons support the authors’ claim that one model can lead on aggregate metric accuracy, boundary sharpness, and temporal stability under the stated protocols. The ordering changes with the metric and dataset. UniK3D-L leads H+ on mean single-image AbsRel rank with predicted intrinsics, and VeloDepth leads H+ on mean temporal alignment error rank (Tables 8 and 12). Track jitter measures changes in depth error after video-level alignment, so absolute metric accuracy is established separately by the metric-depth evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The primary jitter benchmark requires dense ground-truth tracks and covers synthetic Sintel and Spring. The authors note that temporal alignment error assumes static geometry and can favor blurred predictions when labels are sparse or noisy. The quantitative bokeh benchmark uses still images, with video bokeh assessed qualitatively (Table 5, Appendices B.3, D.1, F.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Baselines retain their official inference resolutions, and average ranks can cover different dataset subsets when training overlap excludes entries. The rankings therefore summarize performance under the reported configurations and eligibility rules (Appendices F.2–F.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Reproducing the training recipe depends in part on access to internal real and synthetic data and substantial compute. The external-only variant’s substantially lower metric δ₁ on held-out ARKitScenes shows that omitting internal data can materially change results in at least one setting. Most controlled training variants were run once, leaving their seed-to-seed variability unknown (Appendix A.1, Table 7, Appendix E.1, Table 19).

## Open research questions

- **How stable is unbuffered metric depth during real camera zoom?** Multi-view focal aggregation assumes a constant focal length, and the long-video evaluation supplies true intrinsics. Compare constant, per-frame, and temporally smoothed focal estimates on calibrated zoom sequences, measuring unaligned metric error and track jitter (§3.1, Appendix E.3).
- **Does the jitter advantage transfer to real dynamic video?** The primary track-based comparison uses synthetic sequences. Benchmark on real clips with measured depth and verified point tracks, reporting results separately for moving objects and static backgrounds (Table 5, Appendix F.1).
- **Can public training data recover the indoor generalization gains associated with the internal mixture?** The external-only ablation loses accuracy on ARKitScenes, but it does not identify which properties of the internal data drive that result. Run matched-compute substitutions with public posed indoor clips and evaluate across several unseen indoor datasets (Appendix E.1, Table 19).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and tabulated comparisons under the stated protocols. The supplied text does not identify which individual baseline entries were gray in tables that rely on shading to mark training overlap. It also does not provide a direct release location or license, establish access to the internal training data, or report seed-to-seed variability for most training ablations.
