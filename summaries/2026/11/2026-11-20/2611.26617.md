# One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26617) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617)


## One-sentence takeaway

O₂LaF initializes inverse and forward dynamics from the same pretrained video Diffusion Transformer and learns latent actions from unlabeled video that improve few-shot control decoding and zero-shot motion transfer in the tested navigation and manipulation settings (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Videos show how a scene changes, but rarely label the actions that caused the change. Recovering those actions could turn unlabeled video into training data for controllable world models. The authors identify two gaps in prior work: inverse models often learn transition inference from scratch or on frozen features, and pretrained forward generators can reconstruct partially visible targets while paying little attention to the inferred action (§1, Fig. 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Repurpose one checkpoint for two roles.** O₂LaF independently adapts two copies of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B with LoRA. The inverse model uses read-only action queries to extract one continuous Gaussian latent action per transition in a single pass. The forward model uses the clean initial state and aligned actions to jointly predict noisy future states under frame-causal attention (§§3.2–3.4, Figs. 4–5).
- **Control what each model can see.** The models use independent noise schedules, with clean inverse inputs and a forward schedule that emphasizes high noise in the main experiments. Joint flow matching trains both models through a compact, KL-regularized action bottleneck. The paper tests how adaptation, temporal factorization, and noise affect action decodability (§§3.4–3.5, 4.1, 5.1–5.2).
- **Test the representations beyond probing.** The authors compare inverse backbones and measure both action decoding and downstream video control. They also show that the jointly trained causal forward model can initialize a higher-fidelity bidirectional world model while keeping the action encoder frozen (§§5.3–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

The results support label-efficient decoding and motion transfer in the tested video settings. Navigation fidelity is measured from estimated camera trajectories, and manipulation cycle scores measure round-trip reconstruction. Accurate one-way robot motion and physical task success remain unverified. The policy-verification examples are qualitative, and the authors report that the disputed grasp was never executed on the robot (Appendix C.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Individual latent actions remain difficult to interpret without inspecting their source videos. Video diffusion rollouts are computationally costly, and the model exposes neither executable actions nor task rewards directly. Efficient planning and closed-loop control remain future work (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated.** Causal prediction and high-noise training favor action learning at a cost to generation quality. The higher-fidelity setup requires a separate bidirectional training stage (§5.4, Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The GR-1 experiments support transfer to the tested robot embodiment. Broader embodiment generalization remains uncertain, and aggregate reconstruction scores can conceal failures on individual tasks (§4.3.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Manipulation probing uses released baselines with different training histories. The comparison measures performance under a shared probing protocol, but cannot isolate the effect of the initialization strategy alone (§4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How faithfully does motion transfer in one direction across embodiments?** The paired demonstrations are unaligned, so the paper quantifies round-trip reconstruction. Collect synchronized demonstrations with wrist and object trajectories, then score one-way transfer against those trajectories (§4.3.2).
2. **Can the latent actions retain motion while carrying less source appearance?** Color probing and transfer examples reveal leakage, but do not establish which training changes would remove it. Train with controlled appearance variations for the same motion and measure both source-color decodability and transfer fidelity (§5.3).
3. **Can video-derived actions improve closed-loop control at a practical rollout cost?** The policy-verification examples lack physical execution outcomes, and the model has no direct action or reward interface. Fit a compact dynamics model over the learned latents and compare task success and planning latency against direct video rollouts in matched robot trials (Appendices C.2 and D).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and reported benchmark comparisons. The supplied text does not establish the exact baseline scores at every Figure 6 label budget, the budget tied to the headline GR-1 R² pair, uncertainty across runs for the main comparisons, quantitative one-way robot transfer accuracy, physical outcomes for the policy-verification examples, or whether O₂LaF code and checkpoints were released.
