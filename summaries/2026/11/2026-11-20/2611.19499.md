# Do Probing and Steering Favor the Same Directions Within and Across Language Models?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19499) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19499)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across three instruction-tuned language models, truthfulness and sentiment steering favored directions closer to the class mean difference than the best linear probes at matched neutral-text perturbation, and transferred probes generally retained more of their target-model performance than transferred mean-difference steering vectors (§4.1, Figs. 2–3).

## Problem and motivation

A hidden-state direction can predict a concept without being the most effective direction for changing model behavior. Earlier work compared fixed endpoints, typically a probe and a class mean difference. The authors ask where probing and steering peak between those endpoints when directions are compared at equal measured side effect, and whether the two roles transfer differently across models (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Within-model comparison.** Sweep a closed-form family of regularized discriminants from strongly covariance-adjusted directions toward the class mean difference. Score each direction by held-out AUROC and by its additive steering effect at a shared next-token KL budget on neutral text. Check the result across layers, with matched texts, and with gated rotation (§§2.1, 3.2, 4.1; Apps. F–H, M).
- **Cross-model comparison.** Fit separate affine ridge maps in both directions on paired neutral-text activations. Transfer steering vectors with the forward map and probes with the transpose of the reverse map. The contribution is the comparison of these existing transfer laws on the same ordered model pairs against target-native directions (§§2.2, 3.3, 4.1).
- **Abstention application.** Adapt a sampling-based error taxonomy to PopQA, test a pre-answer reachability probe against first-token entropy, and evaluate ungated and probe-gated steering toward abstention by error type (§§2.4, 3.4, 4.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Probing and steering favored different regions. | Across the six main TruthfulQA and Yelp model–concept settings, held-out AUROC peaked near the average within-class variance scale \(\tau\) at 0.96–0.98, versus 0.60–0.89 for the mean difference. At matched neutral-text KL, the strongest additive steering directions lay at \(10\tau\)–\(316\tau\), measured by answer-margin or sentiment log-odds change. | §4.1, Fig. 2 |
| The directional shift survived several controls. | The steering plateau shifted toward the mean difference in all six matched-text settings, in 59 of 60 layer–model–concept settings on the coarser sweep grid, and in all four gated-rotation settings where the budget comparison was defined. Of the 59 passing layer settings, 55 had disjoint plateaus. | Apps. F–H, M; Tables 2–3, 14 |
| Gains over the mean difference depended on the setup. | At the main layers and matched KL, an intermediate direction selected on one split yielded 27–56% more sentiment log-odds change than the mean difference on the other split. With matched texts the gain disappeared in LLaMA and Gemma and shrank in Qwen. Truthfulness met the prespecified gain criterion in none of the three main additive settings. | §4.1; App. F, Table 2 |
| Probes generally retained more under transfer. | Across 12 ordered model–concept comparisons, transferred probes retained 81–91% of target-native above-chance AUROC. Transferred mean differences retained 44–93% of native steering effect at the target KL budget, with lower retention in 11 of 12 comparisons. Nine were inconclusive under the prespecified categories. Re-expressing probe retention on the \(d'\) scale reduced the count to 9 of 12. | §4.1, Figs. 3, 8; App. N.2 |
| Abstention steering primarily affected uncertain errors. | On PopQA at matched KL versus an unsteered rerun, mean-difference steering raised abstention by 18–44 percentage points on uncertain errors and 2–5 on confident errors. Random directions raised uncertain-error abstention by 1–9 points. Probe gating passed the prespecified benefit–cost rule only in Qwen at the mean-difference anchor strength. | §4.2, Fig. 4; App. J, Tables 7–8 |
| Reachability and error confidence favored different predictors. | On PopQA, the probe outperformed first-token entropy on reachability among wrong answers in LLaMA and Gemma, and in all three models when abstentions were included. For uncertain versus confident errors, entropy had higher AUROC in all three models, with significant differences in LLaMA and Gemma. | §4.2; App. N.3, Fig. 11 |

The interventions establish that changing the tested residual-stream directions changes the measured outputs, and the controls support the within-family shift toward the mean difference. The exact steering optimum varies with the setup. The main TruthfulQA and Yelp steering metrics measure answer margins and word log-odds, leaving accuracy and broader generation quality open. The cross-model result is a descriptive pattern under the tested maps and retention metrics. PopQA reachability is defined by the sampled outputs, so the abstention results leave factual knowledge and error recognition unresolved (§§2.4, 4, 6; App. A).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The study covers three binary concepts and three instruction-tuned checkpoints, using one direction at a time. PopQA steering was evaluated at one layer per model. Truthfulness directions were estimated and tested on separate splits of TruthfulQA, leaving cross-dataset generalization untested. The singular sample covariance also means the small-regularization limit differs from the classical Fisher discriminant (Apps. A–B, M).
- **Author-stated comparison limits.** Neutral next-token KL controls perturbation on the calibration sentences. The rotation gate opens differently on task text, and an ungated rotation was not run. The matched-text control changes several prompt properties at once. Transfer comparisons reuse the same models, use different probe and steering metrics, and rely on maps that fit concept text worse than neutral text (Apps. A, F–G, N.2).
- **Author-stated PopQA limits.** Five sampled answers can miss a reachable correct answer, leaving small correction groups and wide intervals. Greedy abstentions dominate the class used to estimate the “unknown” direction, and the authors did not fit a direction from wrong answers alone (§§3.4, 4.2; Apps. A, I).
- **Analyst inference.** The transfer gap may depend partly on where each direction is chosen along the family. The primary comparison uses a probe at the target-selected regularization level and a mean-difference steering vector. Same-regularization comparisons show that steering retention varies along the family (Eq. 4; App. N.2, Fig. 9).

## Open research questions

- **Does the directional split survive a task-level perturbation budget?** The reported budget uses neutral next-token predictions. Repeat the sweep with KL measured on task prompts and continuations, and report both the original steering metrics and answer accuracy (§2.1; App. A).
- **How much of the transfer gap comes from map quality?** Neutral-trained maps fit concept texts worse, and the matched-text control was within-model only. Compare neutral-trained and task-domain maps fitted without labels on disjoint alignment data, then evaluate both roles on the same held-out concept examples at matched regularization (Apps. F, N.2).
- **Does abstention steering still preferentially affect uncertain errors under more reliable reachability labels?** Limited sampling can miss rare correct answers, and the estimated direction is dominated by baseline abstentions. Increase the number of samples per question, measure how often categories change, then compare the original direction with one fitted only on initially wrong answers (§3.4; App. I).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported within-family directional split for the tested settings. The exact steering optimum, relative transferability, and abstention-gating benefit are more sensitive to the evaluation setup.

The supplied text gives captions and selected values for several figures and tables without all underlying cells or item-level outputs.
