# DailySafe: Benchmarking Dynamic Safety Awareness in Everyday Multi-Turn Multimodal Interactions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26298) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26298)


## One-sentence takeaway

DailySafe tests whether multimodal assistants can use earlier actions and images to handle emerging risks while helping users pursue benign goals, and finds wide performance gaps across 16 models and uneven gains from existing safety enhancements on two tested backbones (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation

An ordinary request can become risky because of something the user did or showed earlier in the conversation. An assistant needs to recognize when that earlier evidence changes the safety of the next step and still help the user make progress. The authors identify a gap between single-turn situational safety tests, multi-turn tests focused on adversarial prompts or changing intent, and everyday interactions where the goal stays benign while the task state changes (§1, Table 5).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Evaluation target.** The authors define *Dynamic Safety Awareness* as tracking evolving task conditions, recognizing implicit risks, and giving safe, helpful guidance when enough evidence is available (§§1, 2.1).
2. **Benchmark and scoring protocol.** DailySafe contains 195 curated cases across 10 domains and 42 domain–category pairs. The authors seed scenarios with topics from real user dialogues and documented hazards, synthesize task progressions, retrieve or lightly edit photographs, and screen cases with human reviewers. Each case has a designated critical turn and a safety note withheld from the tested model. A model judge scores the full trajectory for safety and helpfulness separately, using the evidence available at each turn (§§2.1–3.2, Table 3).
3. **Empirical comparisons.** The authors compare 16 models, test image removal and replacement, consolidate dialogue into a single request, vary an earlier task fact in matched pairs, and test transfer of five existing safety enhancements on two backbones (§§3–4, Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Models vary widely in both metrics | In the original multi-turn multimodal setting, three-run mean SR ranged from 2.91% to 92.65%, and mean HR from 3.93% to 94.53%, across 16 models. Claude Opus 5.5 led SR and tied GPT-6 Sol on HR. Kimi-K3 exceeded Claude Sonnet 5 on both metrics. | §3.3, Table 1 |
| Images matter, and descriptions often help | Relative to Full, removing images lowered mean SR for all 16 models, by up to 41.4 percentage points. Human-written image descriptions improved mean SR over Text Only for all 16 and over Full for 14 of 16. | §4.1, Fig. 4 |
| Consolidation has mixed effects | Combining the original user inputs into one chronological request lowered mean SR by 1.21 percentage points and HR by 2.07 points, averaged across 16 models relative to Full. SR rose for seven models and fell for nine. | §4.2, Fig. 5, Table 8 |
| Earlier task facts change final decisions | Across four models on 60 matched cases, clearing the risk by editing an earlier message increased the final-reply proceed rate by 64.4–72.2 percentage points over the risk-present condition. Final requests, images, and prior assistant replies were held fixed. | Appendix E.1, Table 7 |
| Existing enhancements transfer unevenly | In the original protocol, EchoSafe raised three-run mean SR by 4.96 points on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and 6.84 points on Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking relative to their unmodified backbones. The best enhanced SR was 12.99%. Some methods reduced HR. | §4.3, Table 2 |
| The automated judge broadly agrees with manual review | On 300 randomly sampled trajectories from the main evaluation, Claude Opus 5.5 matched manual judgments on 89.7% of SR decisions, 88.0% of HR decisions, and 87.0% of both decisions jointly. | Appendix C.3, Table 6 |

The results establish sensitivity to evidence presentation on DailySafe. The matched-history test isolates the effect of an earlier task fact for the selected cases and models. The image-description comparison leaves open how much of the gain comes from easier visual interpretation versus human descriptions making relevant details more salient. Consolidation also removes intermediate opportunities to intervene, so its results cannot isolate the effect of distributing information across turns. SR and HR are separate benchmark rates, and neither measures real-world outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated qualifications.** Consolidation changes the response protocol as well as the input format (§4.2). The safety-enhancement conclusions are limited to two Qwen3-VL-8B variants (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Fixed, short, human-screened interactions give a controlled comparison, with uncertain generalization to longer conversations or users who change course in response to model advice (§§2.2–2.3, 3.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Claude Opus 5.5 is both the judge and a tested model. The aggregate human audit gives an overall agreement estimate, while the supplied breakdown leaves model-specific scoring bias and its effect on close rankings unresolved (§3.2, Tables 1 and 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Which part of the image-description advantage comes from highlighting relevant details?** Section 4.1 changes both the modality and how evidence is presented. Compare original images with neutral descriptions and descriptions that emphasize hazard-relevant details, holding the dialogue and scoring fixed.
2. **How well do models track hazards in longer, adaptive conversations?** The benchmark fixes user messages and uses three or four turns per case (§§2.3, 3.2). Extend matched cases with intervening steps and let follow-up messages depend on the assistant’s earlier advice.
3. **Do safety enhancements transfer to stronger or unrelated backbones while preserving helpfulness?** Table 2 covers two variants of one 8B model family. Apply the same enhancements to additional backbone families and compare both rates against each unmodified model under the original protocol.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark description and reported comparisons, with generalization and judge-bias questions still open. The supplied text lacks the full judge prompt announced in Appendix C.2, model-level human–judge agreement, uncertainty estimates for the reported ablation and transfer differences, and the complete per-model numerical breakdown for the image variants.
