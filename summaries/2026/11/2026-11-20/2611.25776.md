# Suppressing the Shortcut Hidden Behind the Gains of VideoLLM Fine-Tuning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25776) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25776)


## One-sentence takeaway

VETO projects LoRA updates away from directions identified using text-only activations, reducing the text-only accuracy gains of standard fine-tuning while retaining similar video-plus-text gains on filtered SSv2 and outperforming it on six filtered benchmarks under distribution shift (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Video language models can improve on multiple-choice video question answering by learning textual cues that predict the answer even when the video is absent. The authors show that this behavior emerges during supervised fine-tuning (SFT) even after they filter items the base model answers consistently from text alone. Full-input accuracy can mask the shortcut in distribution, while under shift the model can gain text-only accuracy without improving its full-input score. The authors target the fine-tuning update so they can intervene without specifying the textual cues in advance (§§1–2, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Diagnose shortcut formation.** The authors turn SSv2 clips into multiple-choice questions with temporally related distractors, then track the increase in text-only accuracy over the base model across epochs, training-set sizes, and backbones (§2, Figs. 2–3).
2. **Constrain the update with VETO.** Once per backbone, VETO uses a held-out, unlabeled text-only calibration set to find the leading eigenvectors of each adapted module’s uncentered input-activation second moment. After each optimizer step, it projects the LoRA input factor onto the orthogonal complement. The adapter contributes exactly zero along the retained directions at that module. The main recipe uses 400 stratified prompts and retains directions accounting for at least 95% of calibration activation energy (§3, Fig. 4, Apps. C–D).
3. **Test which directions and when to project.** The authors compare the calibrated projection with a same-rank random projection and with projection applied only at initialization or after training. They also probe how removing video or blocking attention to it changes predictions (§4.5, Tables 5–6, App. E).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| VETO retains in-distribution gains across backbones. | Across seven VideoLLMs on filtered SSv2, VETO gives similar full-input accuracy gains over the base models to standard SFT, with smaller text-only gains on every backbone. | §4.2, Fig. 5 |
| On Perception Test, VETO improves full-input accuracy and reduces the text-only gain. | On the filtered subset with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, full-input accuracy was 61.1% for base, 61.1 ± 0.7% for SFT, and 64.3 ± 0.7% for VETO. Text-only gains over base were +17.0 ± 1.1 percentage points for SFT and +4.9 ± 2.2 for VETO. The fine-tuned results are means ± standard deviations over three seeds. | §4.3, Table 2, Apps. B and D |
| The shift result extends to five further benchmarks. | For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the authors report higher full-input accuracy and smaller text-only gains for VETO than for SFT on every additional filtered benchmark. The supplied text omits the individual scores. | §4.3, Table 3 |
| Calibration and projection timing matter. | On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, a same-rank random subspace gave results close to SFT. Projection only at initialization allowed the shortcut to reappear, while projection only after training reduced it at an accuracy cost. | §4.5, Tables 5–6, App. D |
| The probes support greater reliance on video. | For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VETO’s text-only hidden states stayed closer to the base model’s, and adding video reduced answer uncertainty more than under SFT. The controlled SFT comparison shows that the measured text-only gain can be reduced while retaining similar full-input gains on filtered SSv2. The probes support the authors’ interpretation that VETO increases reliance on video in the tested setting. The mathematical guarantee applies to calibrated input directions at individual modules. Faithful use of the relevant video evidence for each answer remains unverified.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors:** The shortcut measure is established for multiple-choice QA. Free-form evaluation and extensions beyond LoRA remain open. The main experiments train on SSv2, with supplementary NExT-QA and STAR runs limited to Qwen2.5-VL-7B (§7, App. F).
- **Authors:** Shortcut components can fall outside the calibrated span. Within it, the projection blocks valid new uses of text along with shortcuts. The method also inherits the base model’s biases (§3.2, ethics statement).
- **Analyst inference:** Filtering evaluation items by base-model behavior makes the findings conditional on those selected subsets. The supplied results leave performance on the original, unfiltered benchmarks unknown (§4.1, App. B).
- **Analyst inference:** Removing video tests reliance on the modality broadly. The reported probes leave open whether the model uses the specific temporal event that determines the correct answer (§4.4, App. E).

## Open research questions

1. **Does VETO improve grounding in free-form video QA?** The paper measures shortcut gains through option scoring. Compare base, SFT, and VETO on open-ended questions with and without video, using human judgments of answer correctness and support from the clip (§7).
2. **Does VETO respond to answer-changing temporal evidence?** Video removal and attention blocking test access to video as a whole. Hold the question fixed and test matched clips whose motion direction or event order changes the correct answer (§4.4, Apps. A and E).
3. **Do the shift gains persist on unfiltered benchmarks?** All reported shift results use blind-filtered subsets. Evaluate the same checkpoints on the original benchmark splits and report scores separately for retained and discarded items (§4.1, App. B).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and the direction of the reported effects within the tested settings. The supplied text omits several referenced table bodies and figure values, including exact per-backbone SSv2 gains, per-benchmark scores for the five additional shift tests, detailed ablation and probe results, and measured training costs. The abstract links to code, while the reproducibility statement describes the split manifests as planned. Their contents and availability are unverified here.
