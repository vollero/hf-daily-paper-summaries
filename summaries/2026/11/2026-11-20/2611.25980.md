# Learning What Actions Change: Action Effect Matching for Dynamics-Faithful World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25980) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25980)


## One-sentence takeaway

AFFECT trains robot video world models to match recorded responses to small action perturbations, improving agreement with real or simulated policy success rates across all tested backbone–robot combinations and improving DreamDojo-based planning in two settings (Tables 1–2, Figure 3).

## Problem and motivation

A robot world model can generate convincing video while missing whether a small change in the gripper’s approach causes a grasp, a miss, or a displaced object. Those errors can distort policy comparisons and compound in closed-loop rollouts, where the policy acts on the model’s predictions. The authors identify a gap in training objectives that reward visual fidelity without directly supervising how outcomes change with actions (§§1–2, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Perturbation play.** A policy trained on task-agnostic human teleoperation collects trajectories with temporally correlated noise added to its action chunks. Each window logs the executed commands, pre-clipping noise, noise parameters, and observed video. One recorded future per window suffices, with no task-outcome labels required for this training objective (§3.1, Appendix A.1).
2. **Action-effect matching.** AFFECT adds an auxiliary loss to rectified-flow training. It uses frozen features to match the context-averaged covariance between centered perturbation features and video features in recorded and generated futures. The loss is a finite-feature maximum mean discrepancy, estimated across independent episodes with a paired U-statistic. Generated futures come from fresh noise via a differentiable sampler, and the backbone architecture stays the same (§§3.2–3.3, Appendices A.3 and A.6–A.9).

The matched-backbone comparisons support an effect of adding the loss on the reported metrics, and the fixed planning setup shows that the trained world model can improve proposal selection in the tested tasks. Pearson correlation measures agreement across checkpoint–task success rates. Absolute calibration and same-state counterfactual accuracy remain untested by that metric. The ablations measure video fidelity, so their implications for policy evaluation and planning are indirect.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ caveats.** The loss constrains only responses detectable by the chosen features. Averaging before squaring allows overestimation in some contexts to cancel underestimation in others. The play policy also sees more cameras and proprioceptive information than the world model, so the auxiliary and flow objectives can favor different optima (Appendices A.3, B.1–B.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Policy evaluation covers two tasks per embodiment, generated-video success depends on human judgments and object visibility, and planning tests only DreamDojo with a fixed proposal ensemble and scorer. Transfer to other tasks, planners, or perturbations remains uncertain. Gripper commands were excluded from perturbation play (§§4.1–4.2, Appendix A.1).
- **Comparison caveats.** CoCo was reproduced using only its MSC2 component because the authors judged ASC2 unsuitable for their joint-space actions. The training comparison matches sample exposure, while AFFECT requires extra forward passes, leaving compute-matched gains unresolved (§4.1, Appendix A.9).

## Open research questions

1. **Does AFFECT recover the response to alternative actions from the same state?** The evaluation uses aggregate success rates and unpaired training rollouts. In resettable simulation, execute multiple perturbations from each held-out state and compare predicted and observed frequencies of grasps, misses, and object displacements.
2. **Which feature families drive policy and planning gains?** Table 3 varies the overall objective and reports video metrics. Ablate signed, magnitude, and video feature families under matched training budgets, then measure policy-success correlation and planning success.
3. **Does the benefit extend to direct gripper perturbations?** The collection protocol holds gripper commands fixed. Compare arm-only training with training that also varies gripper timing in simulation, then evaluate grasp and release outcomes on held-out tasks.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and tabulated comparisons. The supplied text leaves material details unavailable:

- Confidence intervals, training-seed variability, and agreement among human success raters.
- Absolute ALOHA-2 planning success rates and planning trial counts.
- The embodiment used in Table 3 and measured training-time overhead.
- Public availability of AFFECT code, trained checkpoints, and perturbation-play corpora.
