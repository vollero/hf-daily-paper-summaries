# PAD: Policy-Aware Data Valuation for Autonomous Driving

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25604) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25604)


## One-sentence takeaway

PAD ranks synthetic driving demonstrations using held-out performance and training gradients from the target planner, improving DiffusionDrive’s EPDMS over real-only training by 3.5 points on NavTest and 5.0 points on NavHard while retaining the top quarter of the candidate pool (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Adding driving demonstrations can improve a planner’s average performance while degrading individual scenes, and the same demonstration can affect different planners differently. The authors argue that rarity, difficulty, training loss, and uncertainty provide indirect signals of downstream utility. They identify a gap for scalable, per-demonstration valuation tied to the planner that will train on the data (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Policy-specific valuation.** PAD scores candidates around a real-only checkpoint. It uses standardized scene-level EPDMS from a held-out real calibration set to weight gradients of the planner’s supervised trajectory loss. Scores estimate local influence on this outcome-weighted surrogate (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Scalable estimation and selection.** PAD Gaussian-projects trajectory-head gradients and uses a damped gradient second moment to approximate local training geometry. One linear solve yields a direction for scoring all candidates. PAD selects the top-scoring subset within the budget, then retrains the full model on real and selected synthetic data (§3.4, Appendix A).
- **Empirical validation.** The authors test whether score-ranked groups predict removal effects, compare rankings across three planner families, and measure aggregate performance and scene-level regressions after retraining (§§4.2–4.5).

| The same pool receives different rankings | Across DiffusionDrive, LTF, and MeanFuser on 208,413 synthetic demonstrations, pairwise Spearman correlations were 0.026–0.107 and top-quarter Jaccard overlaps were 0.146–0.155. | §4.4, Fig. 3 |

On NavHard, the best tested budgets improved over full-pool training by 1.6 and 2.4 EPDMS points for DiffusionDrive and LTF, respectively, and tied for MeanFuser. | Tables 3 and 6, Appendix B.2 |

The removal experiment supports directional ranking for groups under one retraining protocol. Accuracy for individual demonstrations and effect magnitudes remains unverified. The benchmark results support aggregate improvements in the tested setting, with component trade-offs such as lower Stage 2 extended comfort on NavHard at each planner’s best tested budget (Appendix B.2). Real-world safety remains untested.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** The first-order estimator omits interactions among candidates and scores them at a single real-only checkpoint. Evaluation uses synthetic candidates within NAVSIM. The authors also show persistent time-to-collision, traffic-rule, and drivable-area failures after curation (§§3.4, 5, Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The weak cross-planner rank agreement establishes that the scores differ. The paper does not test whether applying one planner’s selected subset to another actually reduces performance (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference.** Budget-matched random selection tests the combined PAD method. The reported experiments do not isolate how much of the gain comes from EPDMS weighting or gradient preconditioning, and the fixed training seed leaves run-to-run variability unquantified (§4, Appendix C.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity.** The DiffusionDrive SimScale row in Table 3 matches the PDM-only row in Table 1, although §4.4 describes the comparison as full-pool. Table 2’s claim of the strongest result among controlled baselines excludes AutoScale, which has a higher reported NavHard score and is an external reference (Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions

- **Does each planner benefit most from its own ranking?** The paper compares rankings and retrains planners on their own selections. A matched-budget experiment could train each planner on every planner’s selected subset across multiple seeds.
- **Which parts of the estimator drive the gains?** The reported comparisons evaluate PAD as a package. A fixed-budget ablation could compare full PAD with unweighted calibration-loss influence and with an identity preconditioner.
- **Does rescoring during training improve selection?** PAD computes values once at the real-only checkpoint. An equal-compute experiment could compare fixed rankings with rankings refreshed at intermediate checkpoints.
- **Do gains transfer to real candidates and more reactive evaluation?** The candidates are synthetic and evaluation is pseudo-closed-loop. A follow-up could curate a heterogeneous real demonstration pool and measure aggregate performance and scene-level regressions in an independent reactive closed-loop benchmark.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text describes the estimator and benchmark protocol in detail. The conflicting SimScale labels limit confidence in the exact cross-planner full-pool margins. Material gaps include multi-seed variability for the leaderboard gains, estimator ablations, valuation runtime and memory measurements, direct cross-planner subset transfer, and confirmation that the saved score files and allowlists are publicly available.
