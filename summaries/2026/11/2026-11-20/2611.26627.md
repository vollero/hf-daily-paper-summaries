# RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26627) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627)


## One-sentence takeaway

Prompt-independent activation shifts in frozen diffusion transformers recover most of the measured policy-gradient post-training gains on six image and two video checkpoints, and training only those shifts with RL exceeds the OCR and PickScore gains of the corresponding SD3.5-M checkpoints.

## Problem and motivation

Policy-gradient post-training improves visual generation on tasks whose requirements vary by prompt, such as rendering a requested phrase or composing several objects. Weight updates can change activations differently for each token. The authors ask how much of the measured reward improvement actually requires that flexibility, and how a fixed shift can produce prompt-specific changes. (§1)

## Approach and contributions

- **Velocity matching.** Freeze the base and RL-tuned models and fit additive block-output vectors by regressing onto the tuned model’s velocity predictions on re-noised teacher samples. The vectors are shared across prompts, tokens, and seeds, with separate vectors by block, stream, guidance pass, and sampling-step bin where applicable. The fitting loss uses no reward. (§§3.2–3.3)
- **Cross-checkpoint comparison.** Test the intervention on public LoRA and full fine-tunes. Velocity matching consistently recovers more reward gain than averaging activation differences, and the paper also tests a parameter-matched LoRA. (§§4.1–4.5)
- **Direct RL and mechanism.** Train zero-initialized vectors with Flow-GRPO on frozen SD3.5-M, then use restricted fits and attention interventions to trace how steering and weight-based RL affect generated images. (§§4.6, 5)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed shifts recover most image reward gains | Across six SD3.5-M and FLUX.1-dev checkpoints, fitted vectors recover **90.0–96.3%** of the corresponding RL gains over base on PickScore, OCR, GenEval, or HPSv2.1 test evaluations. Mean-difference steering recovers **12.2–79.0%** across the same settings. The SD3.5-M vectors use **0.29M** parameters versus **18.8M** in the rank-32 RL LoRA. | Table 1, §§3.2, 4.1–4.3 |
| Video recovery depends on the checkpoint and metric | Wan2.1 recovers **111.9%** of the HPSv3 gain on VBench prompts (paired 95% interval **106.5–118.0%**), using vectors fitted on 33-frame clips and evaluated on 81-frame clips. VBVR-Pro recovers **89.8%** of the in-domain rule-score gain (**76.2–105.6%**). | Table 1, §4.2 |
| Direct RL on vectors exceeds both weight-tuned point estimates | On SD3.5-M test sets at two sampling seeds, vector-only Flow-GRPO reaches **104.6%** of the OCR gain and **103.6%** of the PickScore gain. In pairwise judgments over **50 test prompts and 25 raters**, fitted and RL-trained steering win **60–65%** against base and **50–52%** against the RL-tuned models, counting ties as half-wins. The latter rates did not differ significantly from chance. | §1, §4.6, Table 3 caption, Appendix G |
| SD3.5-M gains depend strongly on what image tokens read from text | Restoring the base model’s text keys and values for image queries leaves **20.5% OCR / 10.5% PickScore** of the fitted steering gains and **26.4% / 24.5%** of the corresponding RL-tuned gains. | §5, Fig. 9 |
| Parameter-matched LoRA helps more in the supervised subject-learning control | For six fictional DreamBooth subjects, steering recovers **68–83%** of the ArcFace identity-similarity gain over FLUX.1-dev versus **81–91%** for matched LoRA on **25 held-out prompts at four sampling seeds**. The matched LoRA shows no detected advantage on the reported RL comparisons and trails steering on Wan2.1. | §4.5, Table S5, Appendix E |
| Steering also reproduces side effects | On SD3.5-M PickScore evaluations, fitted steering reproduces the tuned models’ reductions in color energy and within-prompt DINOv3 diversity. For the checkpoint trained with KL, the fitted vectors also improve HPSv2.1 and ImageReward by roughly as much as the tuned model on PickScore test prompts. | §4.4, Table 2 caption, Appendix C |

The experiments establish that fixed shifts are sufficient to reproduce most measured reward gains under the tested conditions. The attention intervention identifies a route that both steering and weight-based RL rely on for the tested SD3.5-M objectives. The authors hypothesize that policy-gradient RL primarily steers capabilities already present in the base model. Reward recovery and the shared attention route leave open how the original weight updates produce their gains and whether input-dependent adaptations could improve them further. (§§5–6, Appendix H)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The study establishes sufficiency for the tested rewards. The necessity of these shifts and the rank of the original weight updates remain undetermined. Preference optimization and reward backpropagation were untested. The video recovery claim covers the two of six evaluated video models whose measured gains had intervals above zero. (§§4.1, 6, Appendix H)
- **Analyst inference.** Human preference evidence is limited to the SD3.5-M image study. The absence of a detected difference against the RL-tuned images leaves the size of any preference difference uncertain. (§4.6, Appendix G)
- **Analyst inference.** Reward-free fitting applies to the regression objective. Because checkpoint selection used validation reward, a pipeline that never accesses rewards has more limited support. Appendix A compares reward-based and loss-based checkpoint selection on SD3.5-M validation data. (§4.1, Appendix A)

## Open research questions

1. **Do fixed shifts recover gains from other post-training objectives?** All eight headline checkpoints used policy gradients. A next experiment would apply preference optimization and reward backpropagation to the same base model, then compare fixed-shift recovery on common held-out prompts. (§6)
2. **When does input dependence improve policy-gradient post-training?** The main matched-size comparison fits both parameterizations to teacher velocities, while direct vector RL covers only two SD3.5-M rewards. A next experiment would train fixed vectors and matched-budget input-dependent adapters directly on the same rewards with matched compute and multiple training seeds. (§§4.5–4.6)
3. **Does the attention route generalize across architectures?** The strongest replacement evidence comes from SD3.5-M joint attention. A next experiment would separately replace text keys and values in joint- and cross-attention models and measure reward retained on matched prompts and seeds. (§5, Appendix I)

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark recovery and the SD3.5-M intervention results. Broader claims about what policy-gradient RL learns remain hypotheses. The supplied text omits the data rows for Tables 2, 3, S1, S2, and S8, so exact cross-scorer results, direct-RL confidence intervals, per-model training settings, and criterion-specific user-study breakdowns are unavailable. A release of the fitted vectors or implementation code is not established.
