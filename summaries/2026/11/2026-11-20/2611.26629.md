# Posterior Matching Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26629) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26629)


## One-sentence takeaway

Posterior Matching Distillation (PMD) trains a stochastic student to match a diffusion teacher’s tilted posterior means, which identify the full conditional posterior under ideal assumptions, and the authors report higher intra-prompt diversity and the best FID among compared four-step methods on SDXL and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B (§§4.1–4.2, 5).

## Problem and motivation

Diffusion distillation cuts inference cost by replacing iterative denoising with a few network evaluations. A noisy state can correspond to many plausible clean samples. The authors argue that matching the overall output distribution can miss this conditional structure, and that approximate reverse-KL objectives can favor dominant modes. They also identify a gap in prior Moment Matching, which compares conditional means at re-noised student predictions. PMD instead supervises the distribution of clean predictions at the student’s original noisy input (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Direct posterior supervision.** PMD compares an exponentially weighted mean of stochastic student predictions at a fixed on-policy noisy state with a frozen teacher target. Under Gaussian corruption, the target for each tilt is available from a single teacher evaluation at a shifted input at the same noise level. The method uses the Gaussian identity to train without an auxiliary student-score network (§§2, 4.1–4.4, Algorithm 1).
- **Posterior identification.** The authors prove that matching tilted means throughout an open neighborhood of zero identifies the full conditional distribution at a fixed noisy state, including its mode probabilities, given an exact teacher and finite student exponential moments there (§4.2, Appendix A.2).
- **Flexible sampling and practical training.** The same student can use different budgets on its trained timestep grid by alternating clean predictions with Gaussian re-noising. Training uses a jackknife and cross-fitting to reduce finite-sample error, plus an adversarial loss to improve visual quality (§4.5, Appendices C.1 and E.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** PMD requires multiple student draws during training. The finite-sample correction can retain bias at nonzero tilt, large tilts can concentrate weights on a few samples, and the reported system uses adversarial supervision to reach competitive visual quality. The identification theorem targets the unguided posterior under an exact teacher (§6, Appendices C.1, D.2 and E.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The spatially coarse training probes leave it unclear whether the practical tilt distribution covers the full-dimensional neighborhood needed for the identification guarantee. The finite-draw, GAN-augmented objective also leaves open how closely the trained student matches the ideal posterior (Appendices A.2, B.3 and C.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The video comparison covers one backbone, and the externally reported PDD result was evaluated on a different prompt set. The FLUX.2 prose also names PMD as leading HPSv3 and PickScore among four-step methods while listing RTDMD scores without the full table rows, leaving the ranking and comparison budget unclear from the supplied text (§5, Table 7 note, Appendix H).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does PMD recover mode probabilities from a fixed noisy state?** The diversity benchmarks vary seeds across complete runs. Test a synthetic multimodal distribution with an exact Gaussian-corruption posterior and measure errors in mode probabilities across tilt strategies and student draw counts.
2. **How much quality and diversity carry over across inference budgets?** The supplied two-step comparison is qualitative. Evaluate the same checkpoint on matched prompts and seeds across supported timestep subsets, reporting FID, prompt adherence, and diversity at each budget.
3. **Can another auxiliary objective preserve more of the unguided posterior while improving fidelity?** The component ablation shows a quality–diversity trade-off for the reported GAN setup. Sweep the GAN weight and compare a non-adversarial alignment objective under matched training budgets and a fixed student architecture.
