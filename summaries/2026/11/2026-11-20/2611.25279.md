# Accord: Universal Online Metric Localization and Dense Mapping from one Camera and an IMU

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25279) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25279)


## One-sentence takeaway

Accord uses visual–inertial poses to anchor learned depth and bidirectional agreement between overlapping views to correct per-image scale, achieving the highest mean map F-score at 5 cm on all four public benchmarks with reference scans under the authors’ whole-map evaluation (§§4–5.1).

## Problem and motivation

Visual–inertial odometry (VIO) provides metric motion but only sparse landmarks, while learned depth models can retain image-dependent scale errors even when conditioned on metric poses. Those errors can put the same surface at different depths across mapping windows. Accord targets this inconsistency so that a dense map can be used at the scale and placement supplied by the system’s trajectory, without fitting it to a reference scan (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Metric anchor.** Building on DAVIO, Accord conditions Depth Anything 3 (DA3) on VIO poses and camera intrinsics. It estimates a robust scale offset for each keyframe window from sparse points triangulated across overlapping views (§§3, 4.1).
- **Cross-window scale correction.** Bidirectional depth reprojection estimates relative scale errors between images that see the same surfaces. An asynchronous solver corrects per-image depth scales while preserving the geometric mean correction at one within each connected component. A factor graph fuses the submaps (§§3, 4.2).
- **Context and evaluation.** Earlier covisible keyframes provide extra context to the depth model. The authors test one mapper configuration across lab, survey, and consumer devices using whole-map placement from the estimated trajectory, and introduce a campus phone-rig dataset with a LiDAR-phone reference (§§4.3, 5, Appendix G).

The evidence supports cross-platform mapping gains under the tested protocol, which penalizes both geometry errors and trajectory drift. TUM-VI supplies trajectory evidence only, and the GoPro walk is qualitative. The published ranking includes two comparison exceptions: VGGT-SLAM 2.0 receives reference scale, and ScaRF-SLAM’s mapper receives Accord’s poses (§5, Appendices B, D, G).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Absolute map scale inherits VIO bias. The filter drifted during blank-wall stops, failed on three phone recordings, and loop closure worsened the trajectory on one Oxford Spires sequence. The 20 m depth cutoff limits coverage of large outdoor spaces (Appendices D, E, G, J).
- **Author-reported.** The mapper uses fixed settings, but Hilti-Oxford uses platform-specific IMU noise settings and an update at rest. ORI was replayed at 0.3× sensor rate, and the final asynchronous corrections arrived 13–40 seconds after sequence end (Appendices A, B, D).
- **Analyst inference.** The phone results support comparisons against a shared reference, but the LiDAR-phone map and unmeasured lever arms limit absolute accuracy claims. The supplied ablation prose quantifies anchoring and context, while the missing table cells prevent a complete quantitative assessment of the bidirectional correction alone (§5.3, Appendices A, C, G).

## Open research questions

1. **How does the correction behave as overlap falls or VIO scale bias grows?** The solver needs cross-window overlap and preserves the scale inherited from VIO. Replay scanned sequences with controlled overlap and pose-scale perturbations, then measure absolute depth bias and map F-score (§4.2, Appendix A).
2. **How much of the map improvement comes from bidirectional correction alone?** The supplied prose quantifies the anchor and context increments and illustrates the correction on one live map. Run paired replays with identical poses and keyframes, toggling only the correction, and report per-sequence F-score and live-map lag (§5.3, Fig. 9, Appendix C).
3. **How well does mapping transfer to consumer hardware at full sensor rate?** Consumer devices supply the recordings, while the reported system runs on an RTX 4090 and ORI uses slowed playback. Replay the same recordings at sensor rate on lower-power hardware and measure dropped windows, correction latency, and map quality (§5, Appendices B, I).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text describes the method, evaluation protocol, and headline rankings directly. Several result tables are present only as captions, leaving the full baseline scores, ablation values, and speed and memory comparisons unavailable. The paper links source code and announces the phone dataset release, but the supplied text does not establish current dataset availability.
