# Twice-Told Critiques: Claim-Level Homogenization of Peer Review in the LLM Era

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26286) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26286)


## One-sentence takeaway

Measured critique overlap at ICLR rose from 12.0% to 21.3% between comparable discussion-phase snapshots, and pairs of reviews flagged by an LLM-text detector overlapped more within the same paper, an association whose cause remains unresolved (§4, Figs. 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Peer review benefits when different readers uncover different problems. Whole-review similarity blends wording and substance, while earlier studies of synthetic reviews leave open how often reviews actually submitted to venues repeat the same criticism. The paper measures that repetition at the level of individual problems, both over time and within papers (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Claim-level measurement.** GPT-6 Luna first extracts atomic critiques with supporting quotes, then groups claims across a paper’s reviews when they identify the same problem in the same paper element. Pairwise overlap divides the shared-problem count by the size of the smaller critique set. The study also measures how many new problems a third review adds (§§3.2–3.4, Table 4).
- **Evidence from submitted reviews.** The study tracks overlap across three ML conferences and five journal sources, compares pairs with two MELD flags against pairs with zero flags within the same paper, and checks sensitivity to alternative models, detectors, overlap measures, and rewrites intended to preserve critique content (§4, Apps. B–E).
- **Reusable artifacts.** The paper reports releasing code, collected reviews, paper metadata, extracted claims, problem groups, and per-review MELD scores (App. A, p. 9).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| ICLR critique overlap rose sharply | Mean within-paper overlap rose from 12.0% in 2026 to 21.3% in 2027, up 9.3 percentage points. Both snapshots came from the discussion phase. Across reported sensitivity checks, the rise ranged from 8.9 to 10.4 points. | §4 RQ1, Fig. 1, Table 14 |
| A third ICLR review added fewer problems | Across three-review subsets, newly contributed problems per third review fell from 6.12 to 5.46 and distinct problems per subset fell from 19.92 to 18.98, while claims per review rose from 7.20 to 7.31, from 2026 to 2027. | Table 4 |
| Overlap also rose at other venues | Mean overlap rose by 1.1 points at NeurIPS from 2024 to 2025 and 3.2 points at ICML from 2025 to 2026. Relative to the 2019–2023 mean, 2026 overlap rose by 5.8 points at F1000 and 2.5 at PLOS. | Fig. 1, Table 9 |
| Two flagged conference reviews overlapped more | Within papers with both pair types, two MELD-flagged reviews exceeded unflagged pairs by 3.0 points at ICLR 2024–2025 and 4.5 at ICLR 2026–2027, by 4.7 at NeurIPS 2024–2025, and by 4.0 at ICML 2025–2026. GigaCheck yielded positive gaps with 95% intervals excluding zero in all four settings. | Fig. 2, Table 11 |
| The pooled journal gap was positive | A paper fixed-effects regression across five journal sources from 2024 onward estimated a 5.4-point gap between two-flagged and unflagged pairs. Source-level intervals included zero for BMC Medicine and SciPost. | §4 RQ3, Fig. 4, App. C |
| Rewriting produced smaller shifts | On 600 pre-2023 ICLR papers, Luna’s fixed-effects estimates were +0.5 points after polishing and +0.9 after rewriting from outlines, compared with the observed ICLR flagged-pair gaps of 3.0 and 4.5 points in the later periods. | App. E RQ4, Table 12 |
| Grouping checks supported the direction of results | Against a three-model majority vote on cross-review claim pairs in 507 validation panels, Luna achieved 83.6% precision and 78.4% recall. All four end-to-end pipelines agreed on the direction of the tested contrasts, with different absolute overlap levels. | §3.3, Tables 3 and 8 |

The author interprets the findings as evidence of declining diversity in the problems reviewers raise. Fixed-size shared-problem counts support that interpretation, and the rewrite controls suggest that the tested wording changes explain only part of the flagged-pair gap. Overlap also rose among unflagged ICLR pairs, from 10.8% to 14.5% between 2026 and 2027. The study leaves the causal contribution of LLM use unresolved because detector flags are imperfect proxies and within-paper comparisons can still reflect differences among reviewers (§4 RQ2, Apps. C–E).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Human labels validate extraction only. Grouping checks rely on other LLMs that may share errors, and Luna missed more links in panels with no flagged reviews (Limitations, App. B).
- **Author-stated.** MELD’s training sources are undisclosed. Its flags cannot distinguish polishing from generation, and an unflagged review may still incorporate LLM help. The rewrite checks cover only the tested models, prompts, and older reviews (Limitations, Apps. D–E).
- **Author-stated.** Earlier ICLR snapshots contain final reviews, while 2026–2027 snapshots come from discussion. Accepted-paper selection, English-only coverage, changes in review forms and policies, and possible exposure to earlier reviews at open journals limit generalization and cross-venue comparisons. The pooled journal regression assumes a common pair-type effect across papers (Limitations, App. A).
- **Analyst inference.** Fewer distinct critiques establish narrower measured coverage, but the study does not assess whether the repeated or omitted critiques differ in correctness, importance, or usefulness to editors (§4, Table 4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the ICLR 2027 increase persist after discussion?** The study observes 2027 before decisions, and the public record lacks final versions for the 2026 comparison cohort. Follow a future cohort from initial reviews through final revisions and compare overlap on the same papers (Conclusion, App. A).
2. **Does LLM ideation increase overlap when reviewers write the prose themselves?** The rewrite controls preserve existing points and leave LLM-suggested critiques untested. Randomly assign reviewers of the same papers to unaided review, LLM-assisted ideation, or language polishing, record actual use, and have blinded experts match critiques (App. E RQ5).
3. **Would human grouping reproduce the main contrasts?** Pass 2 uses a model-vote reference. Have independent experts annotate complete problem groups in panels stratified by venue, year, and flag status, then recompute the temporal and within-paper gaps (App. B).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported descriptive comparisons within the sampled reviews and measurement pipeline. Material information unavailable includes verified LLM use and assistance type for submitted reviews, detector precision on naturally submitted reviews, human reference groups for cross-review matching, final-stage evidence for the 2027 increase, and direct measures of critique quality or decision impact.
