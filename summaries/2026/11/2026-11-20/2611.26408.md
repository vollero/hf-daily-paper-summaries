# Training Vision Models for LLM-Driven Weight-Space Programming

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26408) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26408)


## One-sentence takeaway

Edit-Ready Training (ERT) yielded the highest mean held-out net edit score for GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 across nine image-classification settings, with the largest gains where standard classifiers were hard to edit and a persistent cost to overall accuracy (§§5.1, 5.3).

## Problem and motivation

Deployed classifiers sometimes need a targeted change, such as relabeling a bird species or correcting a recurring class confusion, while preserving other predictions. Fine-tuning can require additional data and alter behavior beyond the requested fix. LLM agents can directly inspect and edit weights, but the authors identify an open question: whether classifiers trained for accuracy are easy for these agents to edit, and whether dedicated training improves edit quality (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Edit-Ready Training.** ERT rehearses two closed-form classifier-head edits based on the contrast between support and reference features. One changes a sparse set of features, and the other changes the readout along a feature direction. Training rewards the requested prediction on separate query images and penalizes changes to reference predictions. Gradients pass through the temporary edit into the classifier, and the temporary weights are discarded (§3.2).
- **Benchmark and edit analysis.** WeightLab gives blinded LLM agents a fixed tool budget to inspect activations, read and write weights, and probe predictions without gradients. The study compares four training methods with five editors, scores edits on held-out images, and analyzes submitted weight changes and intermediate steps. The authors describe a planned release of the benchmark, code, and edit records (§§3.1, 4–5, Appendix H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| ERT had the highest mean net score for both main editors. | Across nine settings on held-out test images, mean net scores for ERT / ENN / label smoothing / ERM were **0.74 / 0.71 / 0.63 / 0.63** for Astra and **0.72 / 0.68 / 0.65 / 0.60** for Opus. The reported paired ERT gain over ERM was **+0.12** for each editor (*p* < 0.001). ERT’s mean lead over ENN was +0.03 for Astra (*p* = 0.10) and +0.04 for Opus (*p* = 0.07). | §5.1, Table 1, Appendices C–D |
| Gains concentrated in difficult settings. | For Entity-30 confusion correction with ResNet-50, ERM → ERT net score rose from **0.46 to 0.79** for Astra and **0.44 to 0.74** for Opus. For Waterbirds relabeling with ViT-B/16, it rose from **0.50 to 0.74** and **0.33 to 0.76**, respectively. | §1, §5.1, Table 6 |
| Open-weight editor gains were uncertain. | Across eight settings per editor, excluding Entity-30, ERT’s mean paired net-score change versus ERM was −0.01 for GLM-5.3-Flash (*p* = 0.90), +0.06 for Gemma-4-26B (*p* = 0.30), and +0.03 for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (*p* = 0.44). | Appendix E |
| The main editors usually changed the classifier head, and final edits still cost accuracy. | Astra changed head weights in **291 of 292** main-grid processes and Opus in **296 of 308**. Averaged across the nine settings for the main editors, final full-test accuracy for each training method was **3–4 percentage points below** its unedited network. Relabeling accuracy uses the original labels. | §5.3, Figs. 3 and 5, Table 7 caption |

The paired evidence supports an ERT benefit over ERM for Astra and Opus on the tested settings. When settings serve as the units of analysis, the paper reports that Opus’s ERT gain over ERM remains significant (*p* = 0.047). Superiority over ENN on average and reliable gains for the open-weight editors remain unestablished. Net score measures target success minus side effects under task-specific definitions, so it should be read alongside full-test accuracy (§4.2, §5.1, Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Evaluation covers image classification on four datasets and three backbones. The 8–16 paired processes per cell give limited power to detect small differences. Hyperparameters and baseline selection relied on scripted edits on ResNet-18, and all training methods still incurred post-edit accuracy loss (§6, Appendix B).
- **Author-reported design.** Most cells rely on a single trained network. Bootstrap intervals primarily reflect variation across edits conditional on those networks. The seed ablation found little between-network variation in the subset it tested (§4.1, §5.2, Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference.** Most ERM and label-smoothing runs were added later than the ENN and ERT runs, so run timing is a possible confound. Editor comparisons also combine model identity with harness differences, including reasoning effort, turn limits, and continuation reminders. The supplied results do not isolate those effects (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The concept holdout tests transfer to concepts excluded from edit rehearsal. Those concepts remained in ordinary classifier training, which limits what the result says about editing concepts unseen during training (Appendix B, §F.2).

## Open research questions

- **Can ERT preserve overall accuracy through repeated edits?** The study evaluates one request per run and reports residual accuracy loss. Compare default ERT with training that rehearses edit sequences, then measure whether earlier edits persist and track accuracy outside all target groups (§§5.3, 6).
- **Are open-weight editor outcomes driven by editing capability or harness differences?** The reported gains are uncertain and the harnesses differ. Rerun the same requests with matched turn limits, reminder rules, and tool-output handling, then compare submission rates and paired net scores (Appendices A.4, E).
- **When does rehearsing backbone edits help?** The tested backbone rule showed no reliable benefit for Opus on Waterbirds with ResNet-18. Compare head-only and backbone-inclusive rehearsal across multiple backbones and editors on requests where pilot runs frequently change backbone weights (§5.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported main comparisons within the evaluated protocol.
