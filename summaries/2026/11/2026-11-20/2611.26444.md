# Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26444) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444)


## One-sentence takeaway

Mind2Act World evaluates history-dependent reasoning, physical execution, and their closed-loop combination in simulation, where the leading agent’s final success rate fell from 52.7% on Mind and 39.0% on Act to 12.0% on Mind2Act across difficulty levels and scene settings (Table 4).

## Problem and motivation

A robot can fail a manipulation task by losing track of what it needs to do, executing the right action poorly, or choosing its next action without checking what physically happened. The authors argue that aggregate success rates obscure these distinctions and that separate reasoning and execution tests rarely show whether a robot can use physical outcomes to guide later decisions (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Three-view benchmark.** Thirty simulated bimanual tasks span five application domains. Mind keeps manipulation routine while testing decisions from earlier observations. Act specifies the operation and stresses precision or timing. Mind2Act chains operations whose physical outcomes determine what comes next. Three difficulty levels and paired *Clean* and *Random* scenes yield 180 configurations (§3, Fig. 1).
- **Shared evaluation and training assets.** The evaluator uses simulator states and events to verify completion and score partial progress. The authors provide 4,500 scripted expert demonstrations for policy fine-tuning and report separate leaderboards for tool-using agents and end-to-end policies (§§3.4–4.4, Table 4).
- **Episode-level diagnosis.** Rules, an LLM judge, and human adjudication identify the first unrecoverable error. Profile cards summarize the diagnosed failures of three representative systems (§6.1, Appendix E.4).

The results establish a workflow completion gap under the reported protocols. The authors report that many episodes ended before a feedback-dependent decision, especially on the hardest-to-complete workflows (§5.2, Appendix E.2). Because the views use different tasks and Mind2Act workflows are longer, the gap and the Mind × Act reference cannot isolate a causal composition deficit. The diagnostic shares describe the first observable breakdown and leave later failure modes unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The tasks are tabletop abstractions of real workflows. The simulator pauses during inference, so timed success measures action selection and execution in simulation time. The authors leave matched interventions to isolate composition for future work (§§3.1, 4.2, 5.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Cross-family rankings reflect differences in training, action interfaces, physics budgets, and evaluation sample sizes alongside model capability (§§4.1–4.2, 5.1, Table 3).
- **Analyst inference.** Failure-category shares depend on the evidence available to each review layer. Table 17 shows that rules alone assign a larger share of the failures they settle to Mind for the two profiled policies than the full review assigns across all failures. Scene-randomization results likewise measure task success and leave the internal memory mechanism unresolved (§5.2, Table 17).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the workflow gap comes from using feedback, independent of workflow length?** The views differ in tasks and horizons (§3.1). Test matched variants with identical motions and step counts, varying only whether the next action must be inferred from the preceding physical outcome or is supplied as a cue.
2. **Would more frequent observations improve sustained contact?** The authors link contact failures to the open-loop intervals between observations (§5.2). Vary observation and replanning frequency while holding the model and task instances fixed, then measure contact failures and final success on pen tracing and domino placement.
3. **Which timed-task results survive a continuously running clock?** The reported protocol pauses physics during inference (§4.2). Rerun conveyor pickup and light-cued response on paired instances with physics advancing during inference, and compare deadline misses and final success.
