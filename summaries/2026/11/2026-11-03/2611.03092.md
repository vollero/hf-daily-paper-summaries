# Avatar Intelligence: Amplifying Human Agency Through Persistent Personal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03092) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03092)


## One-sentence takeaway
This position paper proposes persistent, user-aligned agents and an oversight-adjusted evaluation framework, but provides no empirical evidence that they improve delegation or reduce human effort.

## Problem and motivation
Agents may automate execution while leaving users responsible for specifying goals, resolving ambiguity, coordinating workflows, and verifying results. The authors argue that progress should instead reflect reliable decision delegation with less routine supervision and continued user control. They identify persistent representation of user goals, values, and constraints across tasks and time as a central gap (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The contributions are conceptual proposals and formalizations rather than an implemented, evaluated system:

- **Architecture and roadmap:** A persistent user model and memory support perception, planning, decision, and execution, with task-specialized clones sharing an identity. Levels L0–L3 describe increasing delegation within specified domains; users retain revocation authority (§§2.1–2.2; Figures 2–3).
- **Agentic Oversight Index (AOI):** The proposed score is \(AOI=U-\lambda P\), where \(U=\tanh((V-C_{\mathrm{sys}})/s_\tau)\) represents normalized delivered value minus operational cost, and \(P\) represents human oversight relative to a human-only baseline (§2.3; Appendix B).
- **Personalization formalization:** User identity combines relatively stable decision structure with user-specific knowledge, subject to proposed consistency and stability constraints (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Research agenda:** Rehearsal Engineering uses user-specific traces, staged scenarios, feedback, and policy updates; safety combines runtime controls with contractual compensation for covered losses (§§4.2–4.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| AOI penalizes additional oversight at fixed net value. | By definition, \(\partial AOI/\partial P=-\lambda<0\), with \(\lambda>0\); a mathematical property, not an experimental finding. | §2.3, Eqs. 1–2 |
| AOI remains defined without oversight and permits arbitrarily negative scores. | As \(P\to0\), AOI approaches \(U\); unbounded oversight produces no finite lower bound. | §2.3; Appendix B |
| Oversight normalization identifies workflows that increase human effort. | \(P=H/H_{\mathrm{human}}\): \(P=1\) equals the human-only burden and \(P>1\) exceeds it; no measured values supplied. | Appendix B, Eqs. 4–5 |
| Personalization is framed as prediction under identity constraints. | A likelihood objective is constrained by cross-context inconsistency and a separate stability condition; no solution procedure or validation is reported. | Appendix A, Eq. 3 and stability condition |
| Practical benefits remain untested. | Architecture, scenarios, and adaptation loops are described without experiments, participant studies, or baseline comparisons. | §§2–4; Appendices A–B |

The text establishes definitions and their algebraic properties. It does not establish reduced oversight, improved user alignment, successful transfer across tasks, or effective safety and compensation mechanisms. Industry examples in §3.1 provide context, not comparative evaluation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated open challenges:** Stable yet updateable identity modeling, effective user-specific feedback, safe autonomy, and accountable delegation remain unresolved (§4).
- **Analyst inference—empirical validity:** Without experiments or user studies, the proposed architecture and rehearsal loop have no demonstrated advantage over existing personalization or agent workflows.
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** AOI requires choices of utility, cost conversion, normalization scale, oversight weights, and \(\lambda\). The illustrative formulation supplies no calibrated values or sensitivity analysis, leaving practical comparability unestablished (§2.3; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—operationalization:** Appendix A does not specify how identity inconsistency, identity distance, or the user’s underlying identity would be measured.
- **Authors’ scope boundary:** Insurance covers contractually specified losses and cannot replace technical controls or reverse every harm (§4.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does identity modeling improve transfer across tasks?** Appendix A proposes this objective without testing it. Compare the proposed representation with a memory-based personalization baseline on held-out task contexts, measuring user-endorsed decisions and oversight.
- **Does rehearsal reduce total human effort?** §4.2 proposes adaptation without measuring its costs or benefits. Compare agents with and without rehearsal, including user effort spent supplying feedback and correcting subsequent actions.
- **Are AOI rankings robust to evaluator choices?** The paper leaves scales and weights configurable. Measure utility, cost, and oversight in matched workflows, then test ranking sensitivity to those choices.
- **Can agents reduce checkpoints without losing user control?** The roadmap proposes this transition without validating it. Vary escalation policies in bounded tasks containing preference conflicts and authority limits, measuring violations and intervention burden.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** that this is a conceptual research agenda with a proposed metric, rather than an empirical demonstration.

Missing information includes an implemented system, released code or datasets, participant and task distributions, measured outcomes, baseline comparisons, AOI calibration, operational identity measures, and validation of rehearsal or safety mechanisms.
