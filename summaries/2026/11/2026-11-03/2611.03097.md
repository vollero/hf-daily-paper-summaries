# DR Pixel: Page-Native Deep Research with Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03097) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03097)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that retrieving and reasoning over document page images improves scientific question answering over OCR pipelines, while step-weighted supervised training raises Qwen3.6-27B accuracy from 40.1% to 52.1% on DRPixelBench (§§4.2–5.2).

## Problem and motivation
OCR-based research agents can lose figures, distort tables and equations, and propagate parsing errors into retrieval and reasoning. The authors investigate whether iterative research can operate directly over rendered pages while preserving relationships between visual evidence and surrounding text (§§1, 4.1).

They also identify an evaluation gap: existing benchmarks largely separate complex text-based search from multimodal questions requiring relatively shallow retrieval (§3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Page-native research agent:** DR Pixel uses a ReAct loop with text queries, a visual embedding index, and a VLM that reads retrieved page images. Each search returns three pages, and all observations remain in context (§4.1).
- **Counterfactual credit reweighting:** Weighted SFT suppresses low-value teacher steps. Reasoning credit measures the gold-answer likelihood change after deleting a reasoning block; search credit measures the likelihood gain between consecutive histories. Positive credits become ranked weights, with additional reasoning–action coupling and final-answer weighting (§5.1). Thus, the search calculation is an incremental likelihood measure rather than the same deletion test used for reasoning.
- **DRPixelBench:** The released benchmark contains 500 human-reviewed questions, each grounded in exactly one page, over 99,055 papers and 1,062,153 rendered pages. Questions target text, tables, figures, and equations (§3; Appendices A.2, A.5).
- **Trained agent:** Qwen3.6-27B receives LoRA training on 2,500 correct GPT-5.4 trajectories from visual questions, with training-supporting papers disjoint from evaluation-supporting papers (§5.1; Appendix C.1).

These reported results support page-native inference and weighted training in the tested settings. They do not isolate the benefit of visual reading from changes in retrieval representation and retrieval unit. The authors approximately match retrieved text volume, but this does not make the two pipelines identical (Appendix B.2). Matching GPT-5.4’s reported accuracy also does not establish statistical equivalence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described training mismatch:** Correctness filtering ensures retained training trajectories end with the answer in context; evaluation provides no such guarantee (Appendix C.1).
- **Author-described residual weakness:** The trained agent still trails GPT-5.4 when answering from gold pages retrieved earlier in a trajectory, despite stronger retrieval (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—restricted task coverage:** Every benchmark question has sufficient evidence on one page and a short answer. This limits conclusions about multi-document synthesis and long-form research reports (§3; Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference—limited visual transfer evidence:** Only 1.7% of ViDoRe-V3 questions reportedly require visual content, weakening its ability to test transfer of visual reasoning specifically (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement uncertainty:** Accuracy uses GPT-5.4-mini majority grading. Human calibration of that judge and statistical uncertainty are unavailable in the supplied text (§4.2; Appendix E).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Many tables appear only as captions, and figure references are inconsistent in the corpus-size ablation. Detailed comparisons therefore cannot always be checked against displayed values.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the method handle evidence distributed across documents?** DRPixelBench enforces single-page sufficiency. Next experiment: evaluate questions requiring evidence from several pages and papers, measuring evidence coverage and answer correctness.
2. **Which part of page-native processing drives the gain?** Retrieval representation and reader input change together. Next experiment: cross text/page retrieval with text/image reading under matched evidence budgets.
3. **Does likelihood-based credit identify causally useful steps?** The method scores fixed histories without rerunning subsequent actions (§5.1). Next experiment: compare its scores with deletion-and-rerollout answer outcomes.
4. **Can earlier visual evidence be retained more reliably?** A reading deficit remains for pages retrieved earlier (Appendix C.2). Next experiment: compare full-history retention with explicit evidence selection under matched context budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are clearly described, but most numerical table bodies and plotted values are absent. Material missing information includes per-benchmark score breakdowns, uncertainty estimates, full annotation statistics, judge-validation results, actual prompt contents, and total offline credit-computation cost. The supplied code/data links indicate intended assets; their contents and availability were not verified.
