# Order-Only On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02112) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02112)


## One-sentence takeaway
O³PD transfers teacher token rankings instead of probabilities on student-generated prefixes, improving average mathematical reasoning accuracy over the paper’s OPD baseline across all six tested Qwen3 teacher–student pairs (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation trains students on their own generated contexts, addressing the mismatch between teacher-generated training prefixes and student-generated inference prefixes. Conventional OPD also requires students to match teacher probability values.

The authors argue that these values combine useful token preferences with teacher-specific confidence that smaller students may struggle to reproduce. They identify ranking-only supervision as an intermediate option between imitating one token and matching an entire distribution, avoiding teacher-temperature selection (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Ranking-only on-policy supervision:** At each student-generated prefix, a frozen teacher supplies its ordered top-K tokens. Teacher probability magnitudes never enter the training loss (§3).
- **Adjacent logistic objective:** The student learns to score each teacher-ranked token above its successor. These adjacent constraints determine the complete order through transitivity, although the loss continues to favor larger student margins (§3.2).
- **Temperature-invariant supervision:** Positive teacher-temperature scaling preserves candidate identities and ordering at a fixed prefix. More generally, strictly increasing transformations of teacher scores leave the objective unchanged (§3.1).
- **Empirical comparison of ordinal objectives:** All-pairs logistic, top-weighted logistic, and ListMLE also improve over the tested OPD baseline, supporting the broader supervision principle rather than a uniquely effective loss (§4.3).

These results establish that ranking-only supervision is competitive and often stronger under the tested setup. They do not establish universal sufficiency of rankings, superiority over fully temperature-tuned OPD, or that confidence matching causally explains the accuracy gap. The authors’ capacity-and-calibration explanation remains an interpretation of the observed results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments cover mathematical reasoning only; safety alignment and open-ended generation behavior are not evaluated (Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Main comparisons use one model family, LoRA updates, and short training runs. Ranking-loss and candidate-size ablations cover only the 8B→1.7B pair (§4.3; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—baseline tuning:** Main OPD results fix teacher temperature at 1. The temperature sweep evaluates only HMMT25 after three steps, leaving full-training, temperature-tuned comparisons unresolved (§4.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and selection:** One training seed is listed. Evaluation uses three sampling seeds, but no confidence intervals or repeated-training variability are reported. Checkpoints are selected using the same benchmark average being reported (§4.1; Table 5).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Teacher-agreement measurements use student-generated responses; the text does not establish comparison on identical fixed prefixes across methods. Consequently, trajectory differences may affect these diagnostics (§5.1).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The objective and hyperparameters are specified, but a code/checkpoint release, training-subset size, and measured compute costs are not supplied.

## Open research questions
1. **Does O³PD beat fully tuned distribution matching?** The temperature sweep is brief and single-benchmark. Run full-length OPD temperature searches with separate validation selection and held-out evaluation.
2. **Does the advantage generalize beyond this setting?** Evidence is limited to Qwen3 mathematics with LoRA. Compare both objectives across model families, non-mathematical tasks, and full-parameter training.
3. **What drives the gains: ordering, entropy, or optimization behavior?** Current diagnostics are observational. Evaluate identical fixed prefixes and compare ranking loss against entropy-controlled OPD while tracking both alignment and task accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported within-setting results; **medium** in the proposed mechanism and broader conclusions.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, repeated training runs, fully tuned OPD results, independent checkpoint-selection data, exact training-subset size, release artifacts, measured resource costs, and evaluations beyond mathematical reasoning.
