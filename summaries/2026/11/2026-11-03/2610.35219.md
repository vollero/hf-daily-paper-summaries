# Multi-Agent Relay Thinking

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35219)


## One-sentence takeaway
Macr transfers an evolving reasoning trajectory among heterogeneous vision-language models using standardized local confidence, reporting higher accuracy than tested single-model strategies and substantially lower token usage than multi-agent comparators (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Single models can encounter intermediate reasoning bottlenecks. The authors argue that prevalent multi-agent methods generate separate reasoning trajectories and then aggregate them, overlooking collaboration through sequential refinement of one shared trajectory. Their proposed alternative requires deciding when to transfer reasoning ownership and which model should continue; raw confidence differs across models and cannot be directly used for this selection (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Relay collaboration:** Agents inherit and extend a shared reasoning context through dynamic ownership transfers rather than independently completing answers (§§1–2).
- **Macr scheduling:** Offline, the method standardizes each model’s sliding-window mean token log-probability using historical model-specific statistics. Online, candidate models generate short continuations; the highest standardized confidence selects the initial model and subsequent handoffs. Low confidence triggers bidding (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Empirical analyses:** Transition patterns, generation survival, trigger locations, and ablations support the usefulness of heterogeneous models and adaptive scheduling in the tested setting (§3.4).

The implementation uses a 16-token window and threshold −0.3, selected on 600 RealWorldQA instances disjoint from evaluation sets (§3.1). The authors announce source-code availability in the abstract.

These results support the tested relay framework’s accuracy–token tradeoff and the usefulness of its scheduling components. They do not establish universal superiority, statistical equivalence to multi-agent baselines, or lower wall-clock and hardware costs. The authors report evaluation across three seeds, but the supplied text omits the promised main-table standard deviations (§3.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed failure:** Macr can still answer incorrectly despite integrating more cues; qualitative examples indicate limits imposed by the model pool (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation covers four multimodal benchmarks and one three-model pool. Transfer to other model sizes, pools, or modalities remains unestablished (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—confidence validity:** Reduced ANOVA F and qualitative correctness separation do not alone demonstrate that standardized confidence reliably ranks the best continuation at every handoff. No direct routing-oracle comparison or calibration-removal ablation is reported (§§2.1, 3.4.4).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic claims:** Transition frequencies and survival patterns support scheduling diversity, but do not independently prove that the chosen model is optimal or that sentence-boundary triggering causes hallucination accumulation (§§3.4.1–3.4.4).
- **Reporting limits:** Tables 1–3 have captions but no cells in the supplied retrieval. Token accounting, hardware, latency, and statistical significance are not specified; ablations are confined to sampled MMStar instances.

## Open research questions
- **Does confidence identify the best successor?** Current evidence is indirect. Compare confidence-selected, random, uncalibrated, and oracle successors at identical handoff contexts (§§2.1, 3.4).
- **How robust is calibration under distribution shift?** Historical statistics and separately tuned parameters are used, but transfer robustness is not reported. Freeze them and evaluate new domains and model pools (§§2.1, 3.1).
- **Do token savings produce deployment savings?** Latency and memory are unreported despite bidding and separate KV caches. Measure end-to-end latency, peak memory, and total compute against the same baselines (§§2.2, 3.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and selected results are explicit, but complete table contents, seed variability, exact main-result comparator configurations, evaluation splits and sizes, detailed token accounting, decoding settings, hardware costs, and calibration-transfer evidence are unavailable in the supplied text.
