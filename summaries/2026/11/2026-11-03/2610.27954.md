# Lost in Recovery: Demystifying Recovery Behavior of Computer-Use Agents via Goal-Oriented Action Replay

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27954) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27954)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReTrace finds that explicit recovery instructions elicit restoration abilities rarely used spontaneously by computer-use agents, but end-to-end benefits depend on recovery quality and can become losses when restoration fails (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation

Computer-use agents can compound mistakes and leave unintended changes during long workflows. The authors argue that forward reflection does not necessarily undo these changes, while environment resets, replay from scratch, and browser-specific rollback have practical restrictions. They identify a gap in systematically measuring whether agents can restore earlier states themselves and whether that ability improves task completion (§1).

Recovery also requires more than a matching screenshot: hidden files or configuration may differ despite an identical interface (§5.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Recovery criterion:** Transition-Consistent Recovery defines restoration through matching downstream transitions, combined with observation agreement (§5.1).
- **Evaluation framework:** ReTrace collects successful on-policy trajectories, retains those succeeding in repeated replay, injects errors, and evaluates recovery by replaying the original remaining actions and checking task success. Its implemented observation check uses SSIM (§5.2; Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Controlled behavioral study:** The authors compare implicit continuation with explicit recovery using target observations and several history/context variants across OSWorld and WindowsAgentArena (§§5.3–5.4).
- **Downstream assessment and assets:** They test ideal restoration and practical recovery during task execution, and report releasing code and a dataset. ReTrace is an evaluation contribution; recovery-aware training is proposed rather than developed (§§4, 6; Abstract; Limitations).

The evidence supports a distinction between spontaneous recovery, explicitly elicited recovery, and successful continuation afterward. It does not establish universal gains or statistically significant improvements: few independent end-to-end runs are reported, and the meaning of the reported ± values is unspecified.

The human study measures performance on evaluator-disagreement cases, rather than overall oracle accuracy. Likewise, replaying one saved continuation successfully tests compatibility with that continuation and task oracle; **analyst inference:** it cannot establish equivalence of every hidden state component or every possible future action sequence.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Explicit prompting only elicits existing abilities; it cannot repair underlying GUI understanding, state tracking, or planning deficiencies. Weak recovery can introduce additional drift. Training better recovery policies is outside the paper’s scope (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—selection and comparison:** Recovery sets contain successful, replay-stable trajectories with filtered perturbations. Model-specific subsets differ in size and application composition, limiting direct attribution of cross-model differences to recovery ability alone (Appendix A.1; Tables 6–7).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** Experiments use isolated environments, exclude tasks requiring proxy or Google Drive configuration, and remove infrastructure or verification failures. Generalization to live, less controllable environments remains untested (Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Analyst inference—resource comparison:** End-to-end recovery adds separately budgeted recovery actions and per-step detector calls. The results do not isolate recovery benefits under matched total action, token, latency, or monetary budgets (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal interpretation:** The suggestion that forward-only post-training weakens recovery comes from comparisons among different models; no controlled before/after training intervention establishes that cause (§5.4.2; Appendix C).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 3 includes Sonnet, but Appendix B.2 describes only the three open-source forward models. Exact prompt contents and several main numerical plots are represented only by captions in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does recovery-aware training improve restoration without harming forward performance?** The authors propose it but do not train such models. A next experiment could compare the same backbone trained with forward-only versus mixed forward-and-recovery trajectories (Limitations).
2. **How much hidden-state damage does the oracle miss?** Current verification uses a saved continuation and task-success evaluator. A next experiment could inject known hidden changes and compare ReTrace with instrumented state checks and multiple alternative continuations (§5.2).
3. **When should an agent attempt recovery under a fixed resource budget?** Weak recovery sometimes reduces success, while recovery adds actions and detector calls. A next experiment could compare confidence-gated recovery, unconditional recovery, and restarting under equal total budgets (§6.2; Appendix B.2).
4. **Do injected-error findings transfer to naturally occurring failures?** Recovery datasets use prompted perturbations and stability filtering. A next experiment could evaluate matched natural and injected failures on common tasks across models (Appendix A.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and broad findings, but limits precise quantitative and causal conclusions.

Material missing information includes numerical values from several main figures, actual prompt and guideline contents, definitions of reported uncertainty, significance tests, end-to-end task counts, detector accuracy, and total resource costs. Code and dataset availability is author-reported and was not independently checked.
