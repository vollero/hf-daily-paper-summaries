# Self-Improvement with Auxiliary Rewards: Learning from Verified Traces and Evaluating Transfer

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41296) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41296)


## One-sentence takeaway
UID-Conj entropy-trajectory weighting improves learning from verified correct responses, with the clearest evidence being a 1.93-percentage-point macro-average gain over matched uniform SFT, while online and transfer benefits remain setting-dependent (Abstract; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Answer-level feedback treats all correct solutions alike, although their reasoning quality and usefulness as training examples may differ. The authors ask whether preferences derived from a model’s predictive distributions can extract additional learning value without new problems or step-level annotations. The gap is between selecting apparently good traces and demonstrating that training on those preferences improves downstream performance (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Compare two trajectory preferences.** Self-Certainty (SC) rewards predictive confidence; UID-Conj multiplies calibrated scores for global entropy variation and local smoothness. Both use renormalized top-25 token distributions. Online and offline implementations differ in feature normalization and calibration (§4.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Integrate preferences into three training objectives.** Online TTRL adds a bounded, centered auxiliary reward only among majority-agreeing responses. Offline DPO prefers higher-scoring over lower-scoring correct responses; weighted SFT emphasizes higher-scoring examples within a fixed correct-response pool (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Separate trace quality from training utility.** Blinded judging compares verifier-correct traces, while matched controls test downstream learning. Evaluation also examines transfer from mathematical training to verdict prediction, scientific question answering, and instruction following (§§3–5).

The contribution is this controlled assessment and integration of auxiliary preferences; SC itself is prior work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted SFT provides the strongest positive evidence | Qwen3-1.7B UID-Conj SFT reaches **29.49%** accuracy macro-averaged over AIME2026, HMMT2026, MinervaMath, and GPQA-D, **+1.93 pp** over uniform SFT on identical responses; paired prompt-bootstrap 95% CI **[0.44, 3.44]**. Accuracy averages five responses per question. | Abstract; §5.2, Table 4; §6; Appendix D.4 |
| The SFT conclusion depends on the aggregation target | Weighting benchmarks by prompt count gives **+0.98 pp**, 95% CI **[−0.30, 2.29]**, for the same UID-Conj versus uniform-SFT comparison. | Appendix D.4, Table 13 |
| Online gains are positive point estimates | Following adaptation on 30 AIME2026 problems, Qwen3-1.7B four-benchmark mean-sample-accuracy macro-average rises from **25.51%** for TTRL to **27.54%** with SC and **27.59%** with UID-Conj. Reported online held-out comparison intervals include zero. | §5.2, Table 3; §6; Appendix D.4 |
| Correct–correct DPO preferences improve the aggregate | UID-Conj DPO reaches **29.03%** four-benchmark macro-average mean sampled accuracy, **+1.25 pp** over random correct–correct DPO; the reported comparison interval includes zero. | §5.2, Table 4; §6 |
| Judged trace quality favors SC | Against random correct selection, net judge-score win rates are **+13.2/+11.7 pp** for SC and **−7.7/−0.5 pp** for UID-Conj on Qwen3-1.7B/4B, respectively. Comparisons condition on eligible correct traces and average budgets within questions. | §3.3, Figure 4; Appendix A.2 |
| Critique transfer improves relative to TTRL | UID-Conj reaches CriticBench verdict accuracy **68.00%** and incorrect-class F1 **63.23%** on the balanced diagnostic subset, averaging five sample-index scores. Both comparisons against TTRL have positive bootstrap intervals; comparisons against Random-TTRL include zero. | §5.2, Table 5; §6; Appendix C.2 |
| Trajectory-specific online effects are mixed | Permuting realized auxiliary rewards preserves their distribution while breaking trace association. UID-Conj does better on AIME2026 and CriticBench metrics, but slightly worse on GPQA-D and MinervaMath; numerical table cells are unavailable. | Appendix D.3, Table 12 |

The evidence supports additional learning value from auxiliary weighting under the tested offline SFT setting. It does not establish universal superiority, reliable autonomous scientific reasoning, or that individually lower-rated traces teach better: diagnostic and training cohorts differ (§5.2). Online AIME2026 measures adaptation on the training problems, whereas the other benchmarks assess transfer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** experiments center on Qwen3-1.7B, with additional online Qwen3-4B evaluation and limited mathematical supervision. Majority agreement can reinforce shared errors (Limitations; Broader Impact).
- **Online attribution:** small raw reward adjustments can produce substantial normalized advantages in unanimous groups. The reported permutation control helps isolate trace association, but its outcomes remain mixed (Appendices B.2.1, D.3).
- **Evaluation validity:** trace quality uses one model judge and includes calibration/development questions; LabBench2 uses an evidence-conditioned text subset and model judging. CriticBench measures verdict correctness, not critique quality (Appendices A.2, C.2).
- **Uncertainty:** bootstrap intervals condition on fixed checkpoints and generations, exclude training-seed variation and repeated judging, and lack multiple-comparison adjustment. The positive SFT interval does not persist under prompt weighting (Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference:** online/offline outcome differences cannot isolate the training objective’s effect because scoring definitions and data subsets also differ (§4.1; Appendix B.3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix C.4 defines a three-mathematics-benchmark macro-average, while the main results use four benchmarks including GPQA-D. Many tables are supplied only as captions, preventing independent reconstruction.

## Open research questions
- **Does entropy structure add value beyond confidence, length, and judged quality?** Appendix E leaves this mechanism unresolved. A next experiment could match correct traces on those properties before comparing UID-weighted training.
- **How much online benefit comes from trace-specific preferences?** Permutation-control results are mixed. Repeat UID-Conj, permuted UID-Conj, and random controls across training seeds, stratifying updates by unanimous versus mixed-agreement groups.
- **Does the effect generalize across models and evaluation populations?** Current model coverage and scientific subsets are narrow. Test additional model families and expert-validated scientific tasks using fixed supervision budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main methods and strongest SFT result, but full numerical contents of most result tables are missing. Public code/model/data release status, numerical permutation-control effects, complete uncertainty intervals, training-seed robustness, and net computational savings are unavailable or not established.
