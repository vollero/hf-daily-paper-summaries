# Can Coding Agents Sustain Continual Evolution without Forgetting?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03219)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the authors’ sampled coding tasks, sequentially evolving a fixed-model agent’s harness often severely reduces earlier-task performance, with task-specific verification constraints accounting for most manually classified failures (§§4.1–4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Harness self-evolution changes an agent’s prompts, execution logic, or tools while keeping model parameters fixed. The authors identify a gap: prior work mainly evaluates improvement on one task type, whereas coding agents must move between repair, test generation, and feature implementation. They investigate whether adaptation to a new task preserves earlier capabilities (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Problem formulation:** Define continual harness self-evolution and measure forgetting through absolute and relative declines on previously encountered datasets (§2).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Adapt AHE and Meta-Harness to Mini-SWE-Agent and evaluate all six directed transitions between three task datasets. Each experiment starts from the best harness evolved on the source dataset, then continues evolution on the target (§§3–4.1).
- **Failure analysis:** Inspect harness changes and 520 execution trajectories, identify verification, workflow, and budget conflicts, and test representative modifications individually (§4.2).

The contribution is an empirical diagnosis and evaluation setting; the paper does not introduce or validate a method that prevents forgetting.

## Evidence and main results
All results below use DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 with Mini-SWE-Agent-derived harnesses. The sampled datasets contain 50 SWE-bench Verified tasks, 50 SWT-Bench tasks, and 30 FeatureBench tasks (§3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Single-task evolution improves sampled repair performance | SWE-Bench score rises from 82.0% to 96.0% with AHE after nine rounds; Meta-Harness finishes at 94.0%. | §4.1; Fig. 2 |
| Single-task test generation reaches the sampled ceiling | Both methods improve SWT-Bench performance from 82.0% to 100.0% on the sampled 50 tasks. | §4.1; Fig. 2 |
| Test-generation adaptation can nearly erase repair performance | After five SWE→SWT evolution rounds, reported relative SWE-Bench performance decreases are 100% for AHE and 98% for Meta-Harness. | §4.1; Fig. 3 |
| Feature adaptation also damages repair retention | Meta-Harness’s SWE-Bench score falls from 94.0% to 32.0% after five FeatureBench evolution rounds, a reported 66% relative decrease. AHE’s reported relative decrease in this direction is 29%. | Abstract; §4.1; Fig. 3 |
| Verification conflicts dominate classified regressions | Among pass-to-fail cases identified through analysis of 520 trajectories, 87.1% are associated with verification conflicts, 8.2% with workflow conflicts, and 0.6% with budget conflicts. | §4.2.1 |
| A representative artifact constraint blocks repair | Adding a test-only submission constraint from SWE→SWT evolution to the SWE-evolved AHE harness reduces SWE-Bench performance to 0.0%. | §4.2.2; Table 1 discussion |
| Representative workflow changes damage accuracy and efficiency | FeatureBench-derived workflow modifications reduce SWE-Bench performance from 96.0% to approximately 66.0% and increase average execution time by 235%. | §4.2.2; Table 1 discussion |
| Budget changes mainly affect execution time in the reported intervention | Adding the FeatureBench-derived budget strategy increases execution time by 79%; the authors describe little performance impact without providing an exact score in the supplied prose. | §4.2.2; Table 1 discussion |

These results establish substantial harness-level interference in the tested settings despite fixed model parameters. Representative interventions support specific failure mechanisms, particularly incompatible artifact requirements. They do not establish that forgetting is inevitable, that the conflict proportions generalize, or that longer task sequences behave identically. The authors’ task-relatedness explanation is suggestive rather than experimentally isolated (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated resource constraint:** Evaluation uses small randomly sampled subsets rather than the complete benchmarks (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited generality:** One foundation model, one seed harness, two adapted evolution methods, and two-stage sequences limit conclusions about other systems and longer histories. The authors assert generalization to longer sequences but do not test it (§§3–4.1).
- **Analyst inference—unclear held-out evaluation:** The text does not clearly distinguish evolution tasks from an independent evaluation split. Consequently, sampled-task gains do not establish unseen-task generalization.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and attribution:** Independent-run variability, confidence intervals, annotation agreement, and the exact pass-to-fail denominator are unavailable. Representative interventions cover a narrow setting and do not isolate every change within a conflict category (§4.2).
- **Prompt-setting sensitivity:** Appendix B reports template-based task routing with original SWT-Bench prompts and its absence after paraphrasing. This shows that prompt presentation affects evolution behavior; it does not quantify retention across diverse natural prompts.
- **Evidence availability:** Figures and tables are largely represented by captions and prose, leaving their complete numerical contents unavailable.

## Open research questions
1. **Can semantic task routing preserve artifact requirements?** Appendix B observes template-dependent routing, but robust routing remains untested. Compare semantic routing, template routing, and unconditional submission gates on unseen paraphrases.
2. **Can retention-aware evolution balance old and new tasks?** No forgetting-prevention method is evaluated. Add source-task regression checks or replay during evolution and measure both target improvement and source retention.
3. **Do these effects persist across longer histories and different systems?** Only two-stage transitions with one model and seed harness are studied. Test longer task sequences across additional models and harnesses with independent runs.
4. **How separable are the proposed conflict mechanisms?** The workflow intervention bundles procedural changes with network-command restrictions. Ablate those changes separately and jointly to measure their individual effects.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly supports the central finding and several large effects, but broader generalization and precise causal attribution remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes complete figure and table values, sampled instance identities and seeds, an explicit evolution/evaluation split, independent-run uncertainty, detailed annotation procedures, and the full reproducibility configuration referenced in Appendix A.
