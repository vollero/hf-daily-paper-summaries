# IdeaTaste: Do LLMs Share the Research Taste of Human Researchers?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03424) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03424)


## One-sentence takeaway

IdeaTaste finds that LLM proposal populations anticipate only a small fraction of subsequent community research choices, with the largest practical weakness in concrete approaches and execution prospects rather than presentation or broad topic selection (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

As AI scientists choose research directions, their judgment about what to investigate matters alongside their ability to execute experiments. The authors identify a gap between evaluating individual ideas or population diversity and measuring whether AI research choices resemble those of a human research community (§1).

Their operational definition is descriptive: agreement with research subsequently released by a community, rather than scientific merit itself (Appendix A.5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Community-level benchmark:** Freeze literature through 2025, generate proposals from that corpus, and compare them with January–August 2026 papers in machine learning systems, code generation, world models, and automated research (§3.1).
- **Two-level evaluation:** Measure citation matching/diversity and idea matching/diversity. Citation matching uses bidirectional nearest-neighbor citation-set Jaccard similarity; idea matching uses retrieved candidates and model judgments over five abstract-derived slots, weighted by their rarity among human papers (§3.2).
- **Controlled agent comparisons:** Compare six models using four context conditions: unrestricted exploration, assigned topics, a field landscape, and access to earlier proposal rounds. Main populations contain 300 proposals with 20 steps per agent (§§3.3–4).
- **Diagnostic evidence:** Combine slot decomposition, direction coverage, population scaling, and blinded expert review to investigate why proposals diverge from community research (§§4–5). The authors announce an open, continuously updated leaderboard (§1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Low agreement with subsequent research | Across six models, four methods, and four areas, authors report average Match-Cite at about **26%** and Match-Idea at **18%** of human self-comparison levels; no evaluated population reaches half the human level on either metric. | §4; Table 2 |
| Citation diversity does not ensure idea diversity | Under topic/frontier, four models attain citation diversity up to roughly **3×** the human level, while every population remains below human idea diversity. | §4; Table 2 |

These results establish substantial disagreement under the benchmark’s operational measures and lower expert expectations for the sampled proposals. They do **not** establish actual experimental failure or equate community agreement with scientific value. Appendix A.5 explicitly limits matching to descriptive agreement; the expert study supports, but cannot prove, the authors’ interpretation that unmatched proposals are weaker directions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated reference limits:** Reference papers include preprints and model-assisted work; they represent human release decisions, not uniformly peer-reviewed or purely human research (Appendix A.4).
- **Author-stated collection gaps:** Indexing delays, missing older abstracts, incomplete reference lists, and uncited recent preprints leave coverage incomplete (Appendix A.7). Survey-reference coverage ranges from **79.0% to 94.0%**, depending on area (Table 11).
- **Analyst inference—measurement dependence:** Abstract extraction, five-candidate retrieval, rarity weighting, and binary model judgments can affect measured agreement. The supplied text says judge validation exists, but its results are omitted (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—unequal information and maturity:** Agents use a frozen corpus, whereas reference papers can incorporate subsequent developments; proposals also differ from completed papers. The authors acknowledge cutoff information asymmetry (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited diagnostic generality:** Direction coverage uses one model in automated research, and scaling uses two models and two methods (§5). These analyses do not establish universal causes across all settings.
- **Source limitation:** The supplied source is truncated; substantial evaluation, implementation, and robustness details are unavailable.

## Open research questions

1. **Does matching predict realized research success?** Expert expectations are not execution outcomes. Execute matched and unmatched proposals under comparable resources and assess reproducibility and contribution.
2. **How sensitive are rankings to the evaluation pipeline?** Detailed judge validation is missing from the supplied text. Repeat scoring with independent judges, larger retrieval pools, and human-adjudicated slot matches.
3. **Can execution feedback improve concrete approaches?** Richer context and population scaling do not resolve the gap in the tested settings. Compare the same agents with and without pilot experiments and revision under matched budgets.
4. **Do unmatched ideas appear later?** The reference ends during August 2026. Rescore the same proposals against later publication windows and separately review persistently unmatched ideas.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Main methods and reported results are clear, but coverage is incomplete because the source was truncated.

Material missing information includes judge-validation statistics, reviewer agreement and uncertainty estimates, detailed prompts/configurations beyond the supplied excerpts, numerical scaling endpoints, omitted robustness analyses, and independently verified release contents.
