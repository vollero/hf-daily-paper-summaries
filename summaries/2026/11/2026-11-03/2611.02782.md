# History Forcing: Distilling History-Dependent Continuations for Long-Horizon Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02782) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02782)


## One-sentence takeaway
History Forcing adds a teacher’s history-dependent prediction residual to distillation targets, improving reported long-video consistency and camera-revisit quality while remaining limited by the teacher’s temporal modeling ability.

## Problem and motivation
Autoregressive video generators accumulate appearance and scene drift, especially when objects disappear from view and later return. The authors identify **history–quality coupling**: distilling an entire history-conditioned autoregressive teacher prediction transfers its visual-quality weaknesses alongside its historical constraints. They propose strengthening historical supervision separately from the reference model’s generative supervision. (§1)

## Approach and contributions
- **Residual-based historical supervision:** Predict the same noisy student chunk with and without additional history, keeping recent context fixed. Add the weighted score difference to a reference score: \(s_{\mathrm{HF}}=s_{\mathrm{ref}}+\lambda(s_{\mathrm{AR}}^{+}-s_{\mathrm{AR}}^{-})\). The authors interpret this as history-dependent distribution reweighting. (§4.1, Eqs. 4–7)
- **Long-video distillation:** Use consecutive earlier student-generated chunks as additional history, correcting a bidirectional teacher’s DMD target during on-policy training. (§4.2)
- **Out-of-sight refinement:** Generate fixed-camera teacher rollouts from the student’s initial image, then use their history to correct EMA velocity targets during revisit chunks. This supplies supervision for unseen scene evolution. (§4.3, Eqs. 10–11)

The proposed contribution is the explicit historical residual and its integration into these training objectives; the underlying DMD and self-distillation frameworks are adopted from prior work.

The matched DMD comparison and history-construction ablations support the usefulness of the correction in the tested setup. They do not establish general physical correctness or reliable performance at arbitrary horizons. Full numerical table bodies are absent, and several in-text table references conflict with the supplied captions; section-level prose is therefore the clearest evidence for many results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Experiments cover 1.3B long-video teachers/students and one 5B world-model backbone. Historical supervision remains constrained by their temporal modeling capabilities. (§6)
- **Author-stated:** Fixed-view teacher rollouts can contain temporal errors and insufficient scene evolution; appearance and scene drift remain after refinement. (§6; Appendix B)
- **Analyst inference:** World-model findings have limited demonstrated scope: training uses 200 initial images with four leave-and-return trajectories each, and evaluation uses preset trajectories on two subsets. Generalization to broader exploration patterns is unestablished. (§5.1; Appendix A.2)
- **Analyst inference:** Greater consistency can trade off against motion: increasing the long-video history weight improves consistency but reduces dynamic degree. (§5.3)
- **Evidence limitations:** The supplied text lacks complete result tables and uncertainty estimates. Minute-long competitor results are taken from LongLive-2.0 rather than all being reevaluated here. PlayWorld scoring uses a VLM, without scorer-validation evidence in the supplied text. (Table 2 caption; Appendix A.2)

## Open research questions
1. **Does better teacher dynamics improve unseen-state evolution?** The authors identify inaccurate or static teacher rollouts as a bottleneck. A next experiment could compare teachers with differing temporal accuracy while holding the student and training budget fixed.
2. **How far does temporal extrapolation extend?** Evaluation reaches minute-long videos, leaving longer horizons unestablished. Test progressively longer rollouts and track consistency, motion, and failure frequency.
3. **Can guidance adapt to history reliability?** Fixed weights show a consistency–motion tradeoff, while teacher history can be erroneous. Compare fixed guidance with confidence-dependent weights under deliberately corrupted or stale history.
4. **Does revisit refinement generalize to unfamiliar camera paths?** Training and evaluation use prescribed leave-and-return trajectories. Evaluate held-out paths with multiple revisits and longer observation gaps.

## Confidence and missing information
