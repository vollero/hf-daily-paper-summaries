# Library of Layers (LoL): Adaptively Ordering Transformer Layers at Runtime

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03526) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03526)


## One-sentence takeaway
LoL dynamically selects and reuses transformer blocks per token window, achieving broadly competitive language-model performance while enabling routing interventions that control solution strategies on synthetic puzzles.

## Problem and motivation
Fixed layer order may require transformers to duplicate useful computation across depths. The authors identify a gap in existing sparse, recurrent, and layer-skipping architectures: they change parameter use or computation depth while retaining execution-order constraints (§1–2).

Dynamic ordering introduces an attention problem: independently routed tokens can compare queries and keys produced by different experts. The authors report instability with this setup and seek input-adaptive routing that preserves causality (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Global block library:** A top-1 router selects a complete transformer block at each routed depth for each window. Library size controls available parameters; core depth controls block applications. Blocks can be reused or left unused, between fixed prelude and coda stacks (§3.1).
- **Causal window routing:** Tokens within a window share an expert and attend causally within that window. Boundaries shift between depths to propagate information across windows; routing uses earlier windows, with a special first-window rule (§3.2; Figure 2).
- **Interpretability and control:** Routing features distinguish languages and harmfulness labels. Forced strategy-specific paths control sudoku deductions and maze exploration traces (§4.1).
- **Unified empirical comparison:** Varying library size supports comparisons with dense, looped, and sparse MoE models. Progressive depth growth reduces training computation (§3.3; §4.2–4.3).

The library size is the central reuse/sparsity control, but the implementation also requires depth, window, stride, and training choices.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

These results support dynamic routing as a viable architecture and a control mechanism in the tested synthetic settings. They do not establish general superiority, deployment speedups, or safety interventions. The supplied text includes several table captions without their numerical contents, limiting independent assessment of the authors’ competitiveness claims.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Safety labels come from another classifier and are noisy; probe results measure agreement with those labels. Sequence labels may also mischaracterize individual windows (§4.1.2; Appendix C.2).
- **Author-stated:** LoL exhibits greater seed variance than looped baselines; router initialization sensitivity is offered as a hypothesis (§4.3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Forced “paths” fix both expert choices and residual weights. The intervention therefore does not isolate block ordering alone as the cause of strategy control (§4.1.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Puzzle control relies on training with router-visible strategy cues and restricted generated tasks. Transfer to unsupervised strategy discovery or harder distributions remains unestablished (§4.1; Appendix C.3–C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Natural-language experiments use a 1024-token context, and no measured latency, throughput, or memory results are supplied. FLOPs comparisons alone do not establish practical serving efficiency (Appendix A; §4.2–4.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does ordering itself drive control?** Expert selection and residual weights change together. A next experiment could independently vary ordering, expert composition, and weights while measuring strategy adherence (§4.1.3).
- **Can safety routing support behavioral intervention?** The paper demonstrates label prediction and explicitly leaves chat interventions open. Test routing interventions on held-out harmful requests while measuring both harmful behavior and benign-task performance (§5).
- **Does greater inference depth improve reasoning?** Extra test-time depth is proposed without reported results. Sweep inference depth beyond training depth and measure accuracy, stability, and computation (§5).
- **Does routing remain useful at longer contexts?** Current natural-language contexts are short. Train matched models with longer contexts and vary window/stride settings, measuring quality and serving throughput (Appendix A).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and experimental settings are well described, but important numerical evidence is unavailable in the supplied rendering: benchmark table entries and standard errors, safety-control AUROCs, exact puzzle accuracies and evaluation counts, and most plotted loss values. Hardware performance measurements and confirmation of released checkpoints or datasets are also unavailable.
