# BYOD: Build Your Own DLM - Efficient Autoregressive-to-Diffusion Language Model Conversion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41574) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41574)


## One-sentence takeaway
BYOD converts four instruction-tuned autoregressive model families into bidirectional masked generators using frozen backbones and single-GPU LoRA training, but generation quality remains below their autoregressive parents and LLaDA (Abstract; §§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
Diffusion language models support parallel token prediction and bidirectional context, but training them from scratch or converting autoregressive (AR) models through full-model updates is expensive. The authors seek a reproducible conversion recipe that works across model families with modest additional compute. Their gap is cross-family, standalone diffusion conversion using a simple masked objective and LoRA alone—not the first use of LoRA for diffusion generation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared conversion recipe:** Replace causal attention with full bidirectional attention and train rank-1024 LoRA adapters on every query/value projection, keeping all original parameters frozen. Remove the AR label shift to reconstruct tokens at their original positions (§3.2).
- **Simple masked objective:** Independently mask response and EOS-padding tokens while leaving instructions visible; optimize noise-weighted reconstruction with separate answer/padding weights. Inference starts from a fully masked answer block and progressively retains confident predictions (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Cross-family evaluation:** Apply the recipe to Gemma 2 9B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen2.5 7B, and Ministral 8B; compare each conversion with its exact AR parent and rerun LLaDA in the same harness. The authors claim the first reported Ministral conversion, qualified by their literature search (§§1–2, 4).
- **Experimental assets:** Report a standalone implementation, pinned training presets, evaluation harness, training-mixture revision, public generation prompts, and four interactive demos (Appendices C–D; Reproducibility Statement).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Low incremental conversion cost | Each primary conversion uses 25,000 updates, at most 102.4M processed tokens, and 3.1–4.0 hours on one RTX PRO 6000; adapters constitute 4.1–5.8% of final parameters. | Abstract; §3.2; Appendix C |
| Best BYOD benchmark average approaches LLaDA | BYOD-Gemma averages 50.6% versus LLaDA’s 51.9% across nine tasks in the matched harness, using the same fixed 125-example subset per task; AR parents perform better on average. | Abstract; §5.1; Table 3 caption; Appendix D |
| Longer Llama training yields limited gains | Increasing from 25,000 to 50,000 updates raises the nine-task average by 0.5 percentage points: five tasks improve and four decline. The longer run also changes the learning-rate schedule. | §5.1; Appendix C |
| Open-generation quality remains weaker | On 100 questions with a 128-token canvas and 64 denoising evaluations, Phi-4-scored perplexity is 8.3–11.1 for BYOD, 4.1 for LLaDA, and 2.2–3.0 for AR parents. | Abstract; §4.3; Appendix F |
| Right-context information affects predictions | Adding a later clue changes greedy predictions on every test item for every conversion and LLaDA. Exact copying ranges from 20% for BYOD-Qwen to 100% for BYOD-Gemma and BYOD-Ministral; without clues, all score 0%. | §5.4; Appendix G |
| Syntax errors contribute to the code gap | On paired HumanEval/MBPP subsets, BYOD-Llama-50k has parser-failure rates of 28.0%/8.8%, versus 0%/0% for Llama AR after standard code extraction. | §5.1; Appendix E |

These reported results support feasible cross-family conversion and functional use of right context. They do not establish inference acceleration, parity with AR generation quality, or general infilling competence. Most numerical table bodies are absent from the supplied text, so detailed per-task and per-model results cannot be independently inspected here.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Efficiency concerns conversion training; wall-clock inference latency was not measured. Each diffusion evaluation processes the full answer block (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Cross-family results confound architecture, parent training, model size, tokenizer, and adapter parameter budget. The fixed rank is not established as optimal (§§6–7).
- **Author-stated:** External perplexity and Distinct-n do not adequately measure factuality, grammar, coherence, or human preference. Only LLaDA receives a matched external comparison (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Resource estimates exclude original AR pretraining and are not measured environmental footprints; total development and evaluation required hundreds of GPU-hours (§6; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed benchmark subsets limit how confidently small average differences generalize; the supplied text provides no uncertainty intervals (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** The longer-training comparison does not isolate update count because its learning-rate schedule also differs (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **Which adapter budget and placement best preserve quality?** Rank and target modules are fixed rather than systematically ablated. Next experiment: compare ranks and query/value versus expanded adapter targets under matched training budgets (§6).
- **Can low-step generation improve without sacrificing capabilities?** Additional Llama training gives uneven task gains and leaves a fluency gap. Next experiment: compare alternative denoising objectives or partial fine-tuning from merged checkpoints (§§5.3, 6, 8).
- **Does conversion enable practical inference speedups?** NFE is reported without latency. Next experiment: measure wall-clock latency and memory at matched output lengths and quality levels against AR parents (§7).
- **Do automatic quality trends match human judgments?** Current proxies omit important text qualities. Next experiment: blind human evaluation of coherence, grammaticality, and factuality across denoising budgets (§§6–7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly supports the method and headline findings, but most table bodies and plotted values are unavailable. Material gaps include complete task scores, model-specific parameter counts and timings, task-specific inference budgets, exact diversity values, copy-test sample count, uncertainty estimates, and measured inference latency.
