# Video Generation Meets Agent Harness: Agentic Video Generation, Agentic World Modeling and Recursive Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40191) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40191)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey organizes video production, interactive world modeling, and model development around an executable agent harness, while finding no surveyed demonstration that revising the development procedure improves subsequent generator-development rounds (§§1.2, 5).

## Problem and motivation
A video generator does not itself manage shot planning, persistent references, inspection, editing, or continuing interaction. These decisions depend on earlier outcomes, and local repairs can disrupt later consistency. The authors identify a gap in existing surveys: the runtime surrounding the generator has not been their common unit of analysis across production, interaction, and development (§§1.1–1.2).

The supplied source is truncated; this analysis covers the available text and cannot establish complete coverage.

## Approach and contributions
This is a survey and conceptual framework, rather than a new generator or experimentally validated production system.

- **Harness-centered framework:** Defines a shared execution loop covering observation, context construction, tools, execution, memory, feedback, and operational constraints; applies it to production and world interaction (§2.2, Eq. 8; §§3–4).
- **Improvement taxonomy:** Separates current-output repair, retained harness changes, generator/evaluator updates, and changes to the procedure governing future updates. Only independently validated benefits from the last category meet its RSI criterion (§2.3, Eq. 10; §5).
- **Evaluation requirements:** Distinguishes visual quality from task fulfillment, interaction reliability, persistent capability, and improvement of development procedures; recommends matched resources and independent assessment (§§4.4, 5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ assessment:** Isolated evidence for world-model harness interventions is thin (§4.2).
- **Authors’ assessment:** Explicit programs preserve represented state, but leave appearance consistency, rendering adherence, and events outside programmed rules unresolved (§4.3).
- **Authors’ assessment:** Tester capability and world-model reliability can confound each other; independent environments and paired comparisons are needed (§4.4).
- **Authors’ assessment:** Repeated optimization can overfit tasks or judges, motivating held-out requests and independent evaluation (§5.3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The heterogeneous settings summarized here cannot establish a general harness-performance effect without matched generators, controllers, controls, and budgets.
- **Coverage limitation:** The source cuts off during §5.3.2 and omits later substantive discussion. Detailed later evaluation guidance and challenges cannot be assessed.

## Open research questions
- **Does harness revision improve later generator-development rounds?** The authors find this unestablished (§5). A next experiment could compare fixed and revisable development procedures across successive rounds under equal budgets and independent held-out evaluation.
- **Which external memory interventions actually preserve world state?** Stored records help only if the generator consumes them (§4.2). Ablate tracking, retrieval, and feedback correction with a frozen generator on controlled revisit tasks.
- **How reliably do verified programs constrain final pixels?** Proxy checks do not validate rendered outcomes (§4.3). Measure object, event, and trajectory adherence after rendering across proxy representations and revisits.

## Confidence and missing information
