# ProVe: Proposer-Verifier Decoding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27815) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27815)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProVe reverses speculative decoding’s model roles by having a large model propose token blocks and a verifier check them, reporting 2.54× average throughput for algorithmically exact greedy decoding across six benchmarks, with additional speed–accuracy tradeoffs from smaller verifiers.

## Problem and motivation
Autoregressive generation requires serial model calls. Conventional speculative decoding reduces large-model calls but still drafts tokens serially with a smaller model. The authors ask whether block-capable large models make this role assignment unnecessary.

Their motivating observation is that block proposals contain usable tokens that confidence-only acceptance misses: on GSM8K, the average greedy-AR-matching prefix is 6.6 tokens, versus 3.0 committed by the default MARS confidence rule (§3.1). Verification could recover that unused prefix if its additional latency is sufficiently small.

## Approach and contributions
- **Reverse the roles:** Building on MARS rather than introducing block prediction itself, ProVe uses a fine-tuned large model to propose a block from placeholders in one pass. A smaller AR verifier scores the realized tokens in another pass (§§2–3).
- **Combine complementary signals:** ProVe accepts the leading prefix whose proposer-confidence × verifier-probability exceeds a threshold, always committing the first token. The verifier scores actual preceding proposals rather than placeholders (§3.2).
- **Self-verification for exact greedy decoding:** ProVe-lossless uses the proposer as verifier, accepts matching tokens, and adds the greedy correction or next token. The authors give an induction argument for reproducing the fine-tuned proposer’s greedy trajectory (§3.2).
- **Isolate verification’s value:** Comparisons with MARS hold the proposer and candidate blocks fixed, measuring whether an additional verification pass improves acceptance enough to repay its cost (§§3.1, 4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Confidence-only acceptance leaves matching tokens unused | On 300 GSM8K continuations with block size 16: average AR-matching prefix 6.6 tokens; MARS at τ=0.95 commits 3.0. | §3.1 |
| Combined scores improve token discrimination | On 26,407 GSM8K blocks with a 3B verifier: AUC 0.909 for proposer confidence, 0.960 for verifier probability, and 0.964 for their product, evaluated on the population encountered by a sequential gate. | Appendix C, Table 7 |
| Self-verification accelerates the fine-tuned proposer | Across six instruction benchmarks: ProVe-lossless averages 2.54× AR throughput, ranging from 1.43× on IFEval to 3.98× on BBH. Public and proposer-fine-tuned EAGLE-3 trees average 2.32× and 2.58×. | Table 1 |
| Smaller verifiers offer lossy operating points | Six-task averages: stock 3B, θ=0.01 gives 2.72× throughput and −2.6 percentage points; stock 7B, θ=0.05 gives 2.30× and approximately −0.3 points. Table 1 prints −0.2 for the latter, while prose and absolute-score averages support −0.3. | §4.1; Tables 1, 5 |
| Reversing roles increases speed but changes exactness | Stock 3B: ProVe at θ=0.10 averages 2.28×, versus 1.63× for exact speculative decoding with k=5. ProVe’s mean score loss is approximately 1.2 points; Table 1 prints −1.1. | §4.1; Tables 1, 5 |
| Reasoning results show the same tradeoff | Qwen3-32B with a stock 0.6B verifier on MATH-500: ProVe-lossless reaches 2.79×; ProVe at θ=0.10 reaches 3.16× with −0.2 points versus AR. | Table 2 |
| Concurrency reduces acceleration | GSM8K concurrency experiment: from 1 to 32 concurrent requests, AR-relative throughput falls from 3.2× to 1.6× for ProVe-lossless, and from 3.3× to 1.3× for stock-3B ProVe at θ=0.01. | Appendix D, Figure 8 |

The evidence supports acceleration in the tested serving configurations and improved acceptance from verification. Smaller-verifier comparisons include output changes, so they do not establish faster *exact* decoding.

“Lossless” describes the algorithm: bf16 execution produces trajectory differences in practice. For the reasoning proposer, measured GSM8K scores are 94.0 for ProVe-lossless versus 94.9 for AR (Table 6; Appendix D). Small task-score differences also require caution given the reported evaluation uncertainty.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Block-specific fine-tuning is required; this is not a drop-in accelerator. Experiments cover only Qwen models at one proposer scale, and all decoding is greedy (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** Scheduling, cache management, verifier calls, and memory-pool allocation affect realized speed. Gains narrow under concurrency (Appendices D–E).
- **Author-stated:** Small-verifier ProVe is not distribution-exact; its accuracy effects depend on task, verifier, and threshold. Fine-tuning changes probability calibration, so equal thresholds need not represent equal conservativeness (§4.1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Exactness relative to the final proposer does not imply preservation of the starting checkpoint’s quality. Block training and preceding SFT change scores and response behavior (Appendix A, Table 3).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Several rounded averages differ between prose and tables; Appendix A also gives different per-task speculative throughputs from Table 1. Appendix D says its MATH-500 confidence half-width covers all reported differences, although Table 2 includes a larger loss. These discrepancies limit precise interpretation.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text does not identify exact GPU models, fully specify training data and hyperparameters, or establish downloadable code/checkpoint availability.

## Open research questions
- **Does the role reversal generalize across families and scales?** Only Qwen 32B proposers are tested. Repeat matched proposer-training and serving comparisons across other families and model sizes.
- **Can sampling retain exactness and useful speedups?** The proposed sampling extension is unimplemented. Implement the correction rule, test distributional fidelity, and measure acceptance and throughput across sampling settings.
- **Can adaptive gates improve the accuracy–speed frontier?** Fixed thresholds shift meaning after verifier fine-tuning, while learned scalar gating shows modest offline headroom. Evaluate held-out calibration or adaptive thresholds using online task scores and latency.
- **How much performance survives production workloads?** Concurrency narrows gains and the serving loop remains research-oriented. Test an integrated implementation under mixed request lengths, sustained arrivals, and matched resource budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and principal results are well documented, but numerical inconsistencies and incomplete implementation details constrain precision. Material missing information includes exact hardware, complete training recipes and data provenance, artifact availability, sampling results, and evaluations beyond Qwen 32B.
