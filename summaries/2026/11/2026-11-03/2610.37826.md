# DeFT-RAG: Decomposing Multi-Hop Questions into Self-Contained Sub-Questions with Reusable Answers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37826) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37826)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeFT-RAG combines incremental sub-question generation, explicit QA memory, a lightweight decision model, and answer caching to improve multi-hop QA accuracy and reduce backbone input tokens, while increasing latency with its locally served decision model (§§3–4; Appendix E).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-hop QA requires finding intermediate entities absent from the original question. The authors argue that single-pass graph retrieval often misses these evidence chains, while iterative RAG burdens the LLM with planning, dependency management, rewriting, and tracking facts. Fixed-size retrieval also mixes missing evidence with distracting passages, and discarded intermediate answers prevent reuse across questions (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Incremental decomposition:** Generate the next sub-question from the original question and accumulated QA pairs. Each sub-question should explicitly identify its entities and be answerable independently; future steps are generated after earlier answers arrive (§3.1).
- **External state and decisions:** Maintain the QA table through rules, and use a LoRA-tuned StartLux-Decision-4B model for passage relevance, direct-answer routing, and stopping. The backbone generates sub-questions, passage-grounded answers, and the final answer (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Cross-question answer reuse:** Cache intermediate QA pairs, matching new sub-questions through normalized wording or semantic-equivalence decisions before retrieving again (§3.4).
- **Empirical evaluation and artifacts:** Compare against thirteen baselines across three benchmarks and two backbones. The authors report releasing code and prompts; prompts are also included in Appendix B (§§1, 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Largest accuracy gains occur on MuSiQue | On 1,000 questions without caching, LLM-Acc is 0.629 versus Hi-Q’s 0.577 with gpt-4o-mini, and 0.636 versus 0.583 with Qwen3.6; both differences reported significant. | §4.3; Table 2 discussion |
| Highest reported scores do not imply significant wins everywhere | On 2Wiki with gpt-4o-mini, LLM-Acc is 0.852 versus Self-Ask’s 0.849; on HotpotQA with Qwen3.6, 0.871 versus Hi-Q’s 0.864. Authors describe these comparisons as on par. | §4.3 |
| Stronger three-hop coverage with fewer passage words | MuSiQue, gpt-4o-mini: full-chain coverage is 46% for three-hop questions versus Self-Ask’s 41%; average passage words per question are 390 versus 2,590. Four-hop coverage is 18%, below LinearRAG’s 19%. | Table 3 |
| Passage-selection configuration improves accuracy | On 300 questions per benchmark with gpt-4o-mini, replacing selection from top ten with unfiltered top five reduces LLM-Acc by 2.3, 3.7, and 5.3 percentage points on MuSiQue, 2Wiki, and HotpotQA; HotpotQA p=0.002. | §4.4; Table 4 discussion |
| Decision-model fine-tuning improves end-to-end accuracy | First 300 MuSiQue questions, gpt-4o-mini: LLM-Acc rises from 0.587 with the base decision model to 0.623 after fine-tuning. | §4.5 |
| Caching saves backbone calls in one tested sequence | Across 1,000 ordered MuSiQue questions with gpt-4o-mini, 23.1% of sub-questions use cached answers and LLM calls decrease 12.9%; LLM-Acc is 0.642 versus 0.629 without reuse, p=0.18. | §4.6 |
| Backbone token savings accompany higher latency | With gpt-4o-mini, averaged across three benchmarks, DeFT-RAG uses 1,569 input tokens per question versus 1,926–13,928 for other iterative methods. Its reported median latency is highest at 9.9 seconds. | Appendix E; Table 7 |

The evidence supports competitive accuracy and lower backbone token use in the evaluated settings. It does not establish universal superiority, statistically demonstrated cache non-inferiority, or lower total computational cost: decision-model work adds substantial latency. Numerical bodies of several tables are absent from the supplied extraction, so some results above rely on accompanying prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Observed generation and stopping failures:** The authors document nested sub-questions, selection of the wrong fact, and premature or delayed stopping. On 2Wiki bridge-comparison questions, 38% of nested sub-questions receive no answer versus 4% of single-relation sub-questions; 75 of 235 questions reach the step limit or repeat themselves (Appendix F).
- **Test-informed threshold choice:** Appendix A states that the direct-answer threshold was selected using stored answers from some evaluation questions. **Analyst inference:** this weakens evaluation independence, despite reported low threshold sensitivity and separate decision-model training data.
- **Incomplete component isolation:** Passage selection and decision-model fine-tuning are ablated, but separate controlled ablations of incremental decomposition and the QA table are not supplied. The selection comparison also changes the retrieval candidate pool, so it measures the combined configuration (§§4.4–4.5).
- **Narrow reuse evidence:** Caching is evaluated with one backbone, one benchmark, and one question order. Error propagation, corpus updates, and order sensitivity are not established (§4.6).
- **Limited deployment evidence:** Evaluation uses bounded benchmark corpora and two backbones; local decision-model serving dominates much of the runtime. Reduced backbone tokens therefore do not establish lower overall cost (§4.1; Appendix E).
- **Claims exceed some observations:** Self-contained generation is a design objective rather than a guarantee, as Appendix F shows. Table 3 also gives LinearRAG higher four-hop coverage, qualifying broader claims of best coverage beyond two hops.

## Open research questions
- **Can self-contained generation be enforced reliably?** Nested questions remain a documented failure. Next experiment: add an entity-resolution validator and compare nested-question frequency, accuracy, and additional calls on bridge-comparison questions.
- **Which state and decomposition choices cause the gains?** Their individual effects are not isolated. Next experiment: factorially vary incremental generation and QA-table memory while holding retrieval and decision models fixed.
- **How robust is answer reuse across workloads?** One ordered MuSiQue run cannot establish general cache safety. Next experiment: randomize question order, test all benchmarks and both backbones, and audit incorrect cache matches.
- **Can decision-model serving preserve quality while reducing latency?** Local inference is the main measured bottleneck. Next experiment: benchmark batching or parallel passage decisions with end-to-end latency, accuracy, and total compute accounting.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and main findings are described clearly, but the supplied text omits numerical bodies of Tables 1, 2, 4, and 5. Other material gaps include independent decomposition/memory ablations, multi-order cache evaluations, human validation of LLM judgments, and total compute or monetary cost including decision-model training and inference. The claimed code release was not independently inspected.
