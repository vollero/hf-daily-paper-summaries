# DAMI: Dual-Axis Memory Initialization for Enhanced Long-Context Retrieval in Gated Linear Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41994) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41994)


## One-sentence takeaway

DAMI jointly initializes memory timescales across heads and gate responses across layers, improving reported synthetic retrieval accuracy across five recurrent language architectures, while broader capability gains remain limited (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Fixed-size recurrent states make linear attention efficient but can lose distant information amid distractors. The authors identify a coordination gap: realized decay depends jointly on `A_log`, `dt_bias`, and input activations, whereas native initialization samples the parameters without activation calibration (§§1, 3.1).

Strong correlations between initial and trained GDN memory parameters motivate treating initialization as consequential, although correlation alone does not establish the retrieval mechanism (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Joint behavioral initialization:** DAMI combines geometrically spaced head-wise timescales with a low–high–low layer-wise sensitivity profile. Geometric spectra build on prior work; the contribution is coordinating timescale and response targets (§§2, 3.3–3.4).
- **Activation-based calibration:** For GDN-family models, calibration proceeds layer by layer without gradients. Bisection sets `dt_bias` to match mean sigmoid sensitivity; `A_log` then compensates for the resulting mean Softplus factor to match the target timescale (§3.5, Equations 10–11).
- **Architecture extensions and evidence:** The authors adapt the design to KDA/GDN2, Mamba-2, and RWKV-7 without additional trainable parameters or changed state-update operators. GDN ablations test parameter coupling, timescale allocation, and sensitivity profiles (§§3.5, 4.3; Appendices C–D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** DAMI addresses decay-side initialization while leaving native write mechanisms unchanged; interactions between writing and forgetting remain unresolved (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** The text reports 15 main checkpoints and no multi-seed uncertainty estimates. RWKV-7 uses different seeds for DAMI and its controls, limiting attribution of its gains solely to initialization (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Results concern a PoST-style spectral control, not the full method with position-adaptive scaling (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic scope:** The retention envelope explicitly sets subsequent-input interference aside, and calibration substitutes sample-average decay. It does not model complete retrieval dynamics (§3.3; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Detailed ablations center on GDN; larger models, longer training, and substantially longer practical retrieval contexts are not established (§§3.1, 4).
- **Reproducibility gap:** The supplied text does not specify calibration sample size/source, training corpus, full optimizer recipe, or exact decoding settings.

## Open research questions

1. **Do gains persist across seeds and scale?** Main results lack repeated-seed uncertainty and cover approximately 100M-parameter models. Run matched-seed repetitions and larger-model experiments (§4.1).
2. **How much does calibration data choice matter?** Sample-average activation statistics define the targets, but calibration-set details and robustness are unavailable. Vary sample size and domain, then measure target matching and retrieval (§3.5).
3. **Can joint write–decay initialization improve practical retrieval?** Writing is left native, and real-world gains are limited. Factorially vary write and decay initialization on longer real-world retrieval tasks (Limitations; §4.2).
4. **How does DAMI compare with full PoST?** The evaluated control omits position-adaptive scaling. Compare both full methods under matched training and evaluation budgets (§4.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported aggregate findings are clear, but missing table bodies, uncertainty estimates, calibration details, and training/evaluation specifications limit independent assessment. Code availability is asserted by the authors and was not verified.
