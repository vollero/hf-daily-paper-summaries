# Where Do Vision Language Models Fail at 3D Reasoning?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39639) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39639)


## One-sentence takeaway
BedBench finds that success on individual 3D judgments often fails to extend to exact, consistent answers, even when trained probes can recover the required counts from frozen visual features (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Standard spatial-reasoning accuracy can obscure whether a VLM recovered the complete object structure, used related properties consistently, or exploited answer priors. The authors distinguish individual judgments—such as counting distinct colors—from exact structured answers, such as correctly pairing every color with its block count. Their identified gap is a controlled evaluation that combines these demands with consistency checks, image-removal controls, and representation probes on the same objects (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **BedBench:** thirteen procedurally generated tasks covering counting, geometric decisions, part relations, and cross-view matching. Voxel objects have exact generator-derived labels and ambiguity checks; related questions share object ground truth (§3).
- **Combined diagnostic evaluation:** exact-match scoring, numerical error metrics, linked-answer agreement, image removal, answer-format controls, and frozen-feature probes distinguish different failure sources. The authors claim novelty in this combination rather than in each diagnostic individually (Table 1; §§2–4).
- **Behavioral and representation findings:** comparisons across twelve open-weight models, four frontier models, and a human reference, plus selected reasoning, rendering, fine-tuning, and probing interventions, expose gaps between recovering counts and using them consistently (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. Default evaluation uses 100 instances per task; Projection and Mixture-of-Shapes instead use 300 questions over 100 objects/scenes (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Exact per-color counting shows a large human–model gap. | Color Distribution requires every color count to be correct: best evaluated VLM **7.2% EM**, humans **93.3% accuracy**. Human reference: 30 volunteers, each answering one random instance per task. | §5.1; Figure 2; §4 |
| Simplifying output does not resolve counting errors. | Across twelve open-weight models, present-color exact-count accuracy changes from **32.5%** under joint prediction to **33.2%** with independent single-color queries; merged full-distribution EM is **0.0%**. | §5.1; Figure 3 |
| A matched 2D presentation substantially improves counting. | Qwen3.5-27B Color Distribution EM rises from **3.0% in 3D to 52.4% in 2D**; difference **49.4 pp**, 95% CI **[39.8, 59.0]**. Per-color MAE falls from **0.50 to 0.11 blocks**. | §5.1; cited Table 7 |
| Fine-tuning improves exact counts without ensuring related visual judgments. | Language-model-only LoRA on **800 in-distribution examples** raises Qwen3.5-27B counting EM from **3.0% to 68.6%**. Dominant-color accuracy is **35.0% from images**, versus **74.2% given its own counts as text**. | §5.1; cited Tables 8–9 |
| Thinking benefits selected judgments while exact counts remain near floor. | At the default **1024-token** Thinking budget, average gains are **13.8 pp** for Packaging and **13.9 pp** for Color Count; Color Distribution accuracy changes only from **0.7% to 1.0%**. Thinking also changes decomposition prompts. | §§4, 5.2; cited Table 12 |
| Exact counts are recoverable from frozen features with a trained readout. | Attention probes trained on **800 objects**, tested on **100 benchmark objects**, obtain **65.0% EM** from Qwen3.5-27B connector outputs, versus **3.0%** for the VLM. | §5.3; cited Table 20 |
| High accuracy can arise without images. | In a deliberately fixed-layout, five-option Volume control, Gemma4-31B achieves **100% without images** by always selecting the third option, which is always correct. | §5.4; cited Tables 21–22 |

The evidence supports separate failures in exact recovery, consistency, and visual dependence within this controlled benchmark. The authors interpret probe recovery and language-model-only fine-tuning as evidence of difficulty using available visual information (§5.3). **Analyst inference:** these results support that interpretation for the tested settings, but do not uniquely identify an internal causal mechanism or establish the same bottleneck across all models.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** synthetic voxel objects and primitives do not establish generalization to natural or embodied scenes; the human cohort is small and serves as a solvability reference (Limitations; Ethical Considerations).
- **Author-stated:** deeper probes and training interventions cover selected tasks and representative models rather than the full suite (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** the 2D control changes visibility, view integration, and layout together, so their individual effects remain unresolved (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Thinking comparisons combine reasoning mode with decomposition prompting, preventing attribution solely to additional reasoning tokens (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** frontier models receive one evaluation with provider-dependent budgets, limiting comparability with repeated open-weight conditions (§4).
- **Coverage limitation:** the source is truncated, omitting much of the supplementary evidence. Several cited tables and exact prompt bodies are unavailable here; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions
- **Which part of 3D presentation causes counting errors?** The tile control conflates several changes (§5.1). Next experiment: vary occlusion, camera arrangement, and layout independently on matched objects.
- **Can training enforce consistency across linked tasks?** Counting fine-tuning leaves dominant-color judgments unreliable (§5.1). Next experiment: compare counting-only training with multitask training that penalizes disagreement between entailed answers.
- **Where does decodable count information become unusable?** Connector probes recover counts, but downstream causal evidence is missing (§5.3). Next experiment: probe successive language-model layers and test interventions on count-relevant representations.
- **Do these diagnostics transfer to realistic scenes?** The authors explicitly leave this unresolved (Limitations). Next experiment: introduce controlled texture, lighting, and geometry changes while retaining exact labels.
