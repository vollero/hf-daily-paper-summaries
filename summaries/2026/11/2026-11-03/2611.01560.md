# Democratizing Robot-Use Agents: A Unified Harness with Open Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01560) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01560)


## One-sentence takeaway
uni-robot-agent enables an unmodified open-weight Qwen model to exceed a published proprietary-model RoboDojo result through shared robot tools and structured feedback, although performance remains limited and real-world comparisons use unequal sensor inputs (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Robot-use agents apply general-purpose models to manipulation through tools, but proprietary weights restrict further training and model-level optimization. The authors identify a missing foundation: a reusable harness that establishes what open-weight models can already accomplish before adding privileged information or task-specific solutions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified robot interface:** Shared motion, perception, skill, and episode tools connect interchangeable model runtimes to simulation and physical robots through environment adapters. Common interaction records are intended to support future training (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Reliable, overlapping execution:** Resource queues preserve action order while background calls allow model reasoning during motion. Immutable observation snapshots, duplicate-request protection, monitored movement, diagnostic feedback, and visual-history management support consistent interaction (§§2.3–2.5; Appendix D).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors compare open and proprietary models, alternative harnesses, geometric inputs, and skill access in simulation, alongside an in-house physical benchmark (§§3–6).
- **Capability analysis:** Trajectory failures and benchmark associations motivate jointly developing visual understanding, spatial grounding, geometric coding, and embodied reasoning. This is an empirical hypothesis; the paper does not demonstrate a new training procedure (§4).

The evidence supports competitive open-weight robot use within this harness and shows that depth and skill libraries are unnecessary for its primary simulation result. It does not establish general superiority over proprietary models: Astra with uni-robot-agent performs better (§§3.1.2, 6.1). Cross-model capability associations do not establish causality, and physical rubric scores cannot be interpreted as full-task success percentages.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Precise manipulation and reliable long-horizon execution remain difficult; bimanual coordination also exposes embodied-reasoning weaknesses (§4.3; §8).
- **Comparison risk—analyst inference:** Physical agents’ additional depth input prevents attributing their advantage solely to model or harness quality (§3.2.1).
- **Sampling risk—analyst inference:** Additional model comparisons use one seed/layout, and pipelining uses one episode per configuration on selected tasks, limiting conclusions about variability (§4.1; §6.2).
- **Measurement scope:** Embodied reasoning is assessed through pick-and-place grasp proposals judged by Astra, which the authors acknowledge captures only a narrow part of the capability (§4.3).
- **Causal uncertainty—analyst inference:** Different generations, model families, and reasoning budgets confound the suggestion that training distribution matters more than size; no controlled training intervention establishes this explanation (§4.2; Appendix A).
- **Ablation coverage:** The supplied text reports depth, skill, and pipeline comparisons, but does not isolate each reliability or context-management design choice (§§2.5, 5–6).

## Open research questions
- **Does targeted training improve the proposed four capabilities and robot success?** The paper provides associations, not training evidence (§4). Compare capability-specific and joint training against action-only training under matched data and compute.
- **How much of the physical advantage comes from depth?** Physical sensing differs across methods (§3.2.1). Repeat the benchmark with matched RGB-only inputs and a separate depth-enabled condition.
- **Does pipelining consistently improve speed while preserving task outcomes?** The timing study has single runs on selected tasks (§6.2). Run paired repeated evaluations across the full task suite, measuring duration, success, and recovery behavior.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main findings and evaluation settings are explicit, but the retrieved text omits numerical bodies of Tables 1, 2, 4, and 5 and most plotted values. Material gaps include complete baseline and per-task results, physical trial counts and uncertainty estimates, exact capability correlations, failure-label validation, full execution budgets/configurations, and confirmed code/data release details.
