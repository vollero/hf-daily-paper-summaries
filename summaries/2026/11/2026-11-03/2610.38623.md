# Cross-Primitive Consistency in Jev

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38623) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38623)


## One-sentence takeaway
PrimaCons exposes inconsistent decisions across semantically matched Jev query formats, while the proposed frozen-backbone residual calibrator improves consistency and accuracy in the reported evaluations, including one held-out domain (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Jev produces structured decisions through typed primitives such as binary Noul queries and multiple-option Choice queries. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that test each primitive separately: they do not establish whether equivalent questions produce compatible decisions across interfaces. Such contradictions can reveal reliability problems that accuracy alone misses (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **PrimaCons benchmark:** Four axes test complement consistency, label hierarchy consistency, abstention alignment, and label-description consistency using controlled transformations of existing datasets (§2).
- **Empirical characterization:** Evaluations of JevK5-2B, -4B, and -9B identify disagreements associated with label granularity, query primitive, and descriptive augmentation; complement disagreement also predicts errors (§3).
- **Cprc calibrator:** A lightweight MLP uses PCA-projected penultimate-layer states, raw probabilities, and metadata to predict bounded residual logit corrections. The backbone stays frozen; training combines cross-entropy and axis-specific consistency penalties (§4).

These results support measured interface sensitivity and improvements over the uncalibrated backbone in the reported settings. They do not establish that consistency guarantees correctness, that Cprc outperforms alternative calibrators, or that transfer holds across arbitrary domains. Baseline diagnostics use calibration splits (§3); Cprc results use separate test splits (§4).

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no dedicated author-stated limitations section. The following are analyst observations:

- **Narrow transfer evidence:** Although called LODO, the described experiment holds out only Axis IV. It does not demonstrate all possible held-out-axis rotations (§4).
- **Equivalence assumptions:** Label descriptions, answer-option sets, and normalized category-specific binary probabilities are assumed to preserve the intended decision distribution. Independent validation of these assumptions is not supplied (§§2–3).
- **Restricted population:** Axis II retains only unsafe examples, limiting conclusions about general safety classification (§2.2).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations:** No supplied results isolate the consistency penalty, hidden-state features, or residual architecture, or compare against alternative calibration methods (§4).
- **Internal reporting inconsistencies:** The held-out accuracy change **84.90% → 87.42%** is **2.52 percentage points**, while §4 labels it **+2.97%**, apparently a relative gain. The introduction also pairs an Axis I gap reduction with an accuracy gain later assigned to Axis II. In Axis III, the reported opposite-quadrant shares total **29.19%**, differing from the stated **26.69%** conflict rate; the text does not reconcile these quantities (§§1, 3.2, 4).
- **Incomplete numerical access:** Tables 1–7 appear as captions without their full numerical bodies. This prevents checking model-specific results and resolving ambiguities.

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer work in every direction?** Only Axis IV is explicitly held out. Train and evaluate all held-out-axis rotations, reporting both accuracy and consistency.
- **Which Cprc components drive the gains?** Component ablations are absent. Compare cross-entropy-only training, consistency-only training, and versions without hidden-state features against the joint method.
- **How sensitive are findings to semantic construction?** Equivalence is asserted rather than independently validated. Repeat evaluations with reviewed paraphrases, alternative descriptions, and varied distractor sets.
- **Does disagreement predict errors beyond Axis I?** The reported error-ranking result concerns complement residuals. Evaluate held-out error detection across the other axes and model sizes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark design and selected results are explicit, but material gaps include full result tables, appendices, exact prompts and descriptions, hierarchy mappings, calibrator dimensions and hyperparameters, uncertainty estimates, alternative-calibrator comparisons, latency measurement details, and verified repository contents.
