# SIRIS: Can LLMs Tell When Simulation Errors Matter in AI-Driven Systems Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10329) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10329)


## One-sentence takeaway
Siris shows that detecting a simulator limitation does not ensure a correct design choice: evaluated LLMs sometimes reject recommendations that remain valid, and review can both correct decisions and introduce errors (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Simulation errors can change predicted performance without changing the best design—or can reverse the ranking. LLM agents conducting systems research must therefore judge whether an error matters for the particular decision. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that measure this distinction separately from detecting limitations or completing research tasks (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired evaluation framework:** Siris injects one controlled computation change into a simulator and constructs two workloads sharing candidates, limitation, metric, and presented recommendation. The reference simulator supports that recommendation on one workload (“upheld”) and rejects it on the other (“overturned”) (§3.1).
- **Decision-focused measurement:** Judgment is \(J=(U+O)/2\), where \(U\) and \(O\) are upheld and overturned accuracy; disposition is \(D=(U-O)/2\). Limitation identification is annotated separately and earns no decision credit (§3.3).
- **Evidence and review experiments:** Models select fixed designs using read-only files under no-simulator, perturbed-simulator, and reference-simulator conditions, then review their choice without new evidence or correctness feedback. The serving instantiation evaluates thirteen models on 58 problems forming 29 pairs, with three repetitions per condition (§§3.2–4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Perturbed evidence distinguishes decision ability. | Every model improves over its no-simulator judgment; GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol approach their reference-condition judgment. Always following the perturbed recommendation yields \(J=50\%\), \(D=+50\) percentage points. | §5.1; Fig. 4 |
| Similar judgment can hide different error profiles. | Final perturbed-condition \(J\) is 66% for GPT-6 Sol and 67% for Grok 4.7, but \(D\) is +29 and +10 percentage points, respectively. | §5.1; Fig. 4 |
| Strong pair performance is concentrated in two models. | GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol solve both workloads in 79 and 65 of 87 pair attempts, respectively; every other model solves fewer than half. | §5.2; Table 9 referenced |
| Identification does not ensure a correct choice. | About one-quarter of limitation-identifying responses still choose incorrectly. Among eight models with loss measurements, 151 of 162 identifying-but-incorrect choices have reference-simulator p95 latency at least 5% above the best design. | §5.2; §B.2 |
| Wrong choices can have substantial simulated cost. | For those eight models, median per-problem relative p95 losses are 14% for abandoning valid recommendations, 24% for retaining invalid recommendations, and 47% for choosing a third design. | §B.2 |
| Simulator outputs can reduce judgment. | For GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Sol, adding either execution records or predictions to code, cost parameters, and configuration lowers judgment relative to that bundle alone. | §5.1; Fig. 5 |
| Review produces uneven net gains. | Under perturbed evidence, GPT-5.6 Sol gains 12 judgment points through 31 corrections and 10 new errors; GPT-5.6 Terra gains only 1 point through 17 corrections and 15 new errors. | §5.3; Table 4 referenced |

The evidence supports a distinction between recognizing an error and assessing its effect on candidate rankings. It also shows that additional evidence and review do not uniformly improve decisions. Correctness and selection losses are defined by the reference simulator; these results do not establish real-hardware benefits, general model rankings, or the reasoning process behind a choice.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** Evaluation covers one domain, one simulator family, and selected single perturbations. Perturbation forms are tied to particular design problems, preventing separation of their effects. Repetitions reuse the same situations (Limitations; §A.1).
- **Authors’ reference-validity limits:** The reference simulator is imperfect, and top-choice labels omit lower-rank changes and decision margins. The hardware illustration contains no case where an LLM improves real performance by overturning an invalid recommendation (Limitations; Appendix C).
- **Authors’ causal limits:** Review combines reconsideration, extra computation, newly opened files, and attention directed toward limitations. No matched continuation or independent second-attempt control was run. Inspection instructions also suggest a possible specification mismatch (§5.2; Limitations).
- **Authors’ measurement limits:** Evidence-component experiments cover two models and bundle code with numerical system information. LLM annotation may retain shared biases, and requested reasoning effort does not equalize computation across providers (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Near-chance no-simulator performance cannot by itself establish weak unaided systems understanding, because validation explicitly does not ensure the problem description alone suffices (§3.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does performance transfer to natural simulator limitations and other domains?** Only selected serving perturbations were evaluated. A next experiment could construct matched pairs from documented limitations in another simulator, using an independent reference (§6; Limitations).
2. **What causes review gains?** Reconsideration is confounded with additional reading, computation, and prompt wording. Compare standard review against equal-budget continuation and independent second attempts with matched file access (Limitations).
3. **Can models judge interacting limitations?** Each problem contains one perturbation. Inject combinations whose effects compound or cancel, then measure pair accuracy and reference-defined selection loss (§6).
4. **Can targeted measurements improve decisions efficiently?** Current agents cannot execute experiments. Permit budgeted measurement requests and compare final design quality and measurement cost against fixed evidence packets (§6; §B.3).
