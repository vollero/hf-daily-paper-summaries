# Horizon-Scalable Q-Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10987) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10987)


## One-sentence takeaway

HQL adaptively extends TD backups through logged actions that pass a density-free critic comparison, improving reported long-horizon benchmark success while relying on the critic’s ranking accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

One-step off-policy TD learning can accumulate small Bellman residuals across long horizons, leaving substantial value error despite low training loss. Fixed multi-step returns propagate information faster but introduce bias when they follow suboptimal logged actions. The authors identify a need for adaptive off-policy correction compatible with flow and diffusion policies that provide samples without tractable action densities (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Adaptive critic targets:** HQL continues a backup when the target critic ranks the logged action at least as highly as the best of sampled behavior-policy actions; otherwise it uses a one-step bootstrap. Targets are computed backward at every position in a trajectory window (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Density-free correction argument:** The authors derive the hard comparison as a limiting case of clipped importance-ratio gates for exponentially tilted behavior policies. The behavior density cancels, leaving only sampling and value comparisons (§4.2).
- **Error analysis:** A deterministic-chain decomposition separates residual accumulation from behavior-action bias, explaining why neither one-step nor fixed multi-step backups always suffice. The scaling laws depend on explicit residual assumptions (§3.1; Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Chunked-HQL:** The same gate operates over action chunks, combining adaptive backups with temporally extended decisions (§4.3).

The evidence supports improved benchmark control performance and more accurate values in the controlled chain experiment. It does not establish universally unbiased deep value learning or an unrestricted horizon-scaling guarantee. Most headline control results include online fine-tuning; Table 1 separately reports substantially lower offline scores. Several prose figure references are inconsistent with the supplied captions, so locations above prioritize section content and caption descriptions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The gate depends on the critic’s own ranking, so inaccurate rankings can admit poor continuations or reject useful ones (§6).
- **Theory scope:** The error law is conditional, schematic, and derived on deterministic chains. Appendix A.2 explicitly ignores residual–bias cross terms. The fixed-point discussion concerns tabular valid gates; **analyst inference:** it does not establish exact validity for estimated critics and finite sampled normalizers (§4.2).
- **Limited replication:** OCBench and the hard-versus-soft gate comparison use one seed. Table 2 does not provide uncertainty estimates for policy-extraction comparisons (Table 6; Appendix A.7; Table 2).
- **Confounded ablations:** Gate-width experiments also change acting width. The soft-versus-hard comparison uses different sampling widths, so its results do not isolate gate form alone (Appendices A.7–A.8).
- **Budget interpretation:** Matching critic-target counts does not match transitions read or prove equal wall-clock cost; Appendix A.5 explicitly notes different transition consumption.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The trace histogram’s precise aggregation population is unspecified. Appendix A.7 also describes a different task set from its table, limiting interpretation of that comparison.

## Open research questions

- **How robust is gating to ranking errors?** The authors identify ranking dependence but do not quantify its consequences. A next experiment could perturb critic rankings or compare learned gates against exact-value gates on corrupted combination locks.
- **What is the independent effect of gate width?** Existing width ablations change acting width simultaneously. Hold actor and bootstrap widths fixed while varying only gate candidates, reporting success and accepted-trace lengths.
- **Do OCBench gains persist across seeds?** Its reported comparison uses one seed. Repeat the matched-target experiment across seeds, measuring both offline and final online success with uncertainty.
- **Can HQL extend to hierarchical subgoals?** The authors propose this direction without evaluating it (§6). Compare gated and ungated subgoal backups under a shared hierarchy and training budget.
