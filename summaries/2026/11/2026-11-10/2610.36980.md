# AutoVend: Benchmarking LLM Agents on Long-Horizon Autonomous Vending Operations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36980) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36980)


## One-sentence takeaway
AutoVend tests sustained vending-network management under shared inventory and paid replenishment, finding that only one of five evaluated LLM agents consistently beats its rule-based baseline in a single simulated environment (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Seller-side agents must turn repeated assortment, pricing, procurement, and replenishment decisions into sustained economic returns. Online decisions incur physical execution costs, and allocating inventory to one site constrains others. The authors identify a benchmark gap: evaluating shared inventory across heterogeneous physical retail sites where replenishment itself costs money and requires scheduling (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and interface:** One agent manages 20 machines across five site types for 180 simulated days, using 18 MCP tools and persistent memory. The objective is terminal cash plus unexpired inventory valued at cost (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Data-grounded simulator:** Commercial vending records calibrate product popularity, site preferences, sales scale, and demand variability. The simulator combines a shared warehouse, procurement lead times, inventory aging, recurring charges, and paid site visits; some economic and demand-response parameters are assumed benchmark settings (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Evaluation and diagnostics:** Five LLM agents are compared with a newsvendor/order-up-to baseline, using economic outcomes, operational metrics, site-reassignment diagnostics, temporal comparisons, and inspected failure cases (§§4–5). The contribution is the integrated evaluation setting, rather than a new agent-training method.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Most agents trail the baseline | Same 20-machine, 180-day setting; three runs per LLM in one frozen world. All three GPT-6.1 Sol runs beat the baseline; none of the other 12 runs do. All 15 finish above starting capital. | §§4.1–4.2, 5.2 |
| Terminal returns differ substantially | Mean terminal assets: GPT-6.1 Sol 309.35 thousand CNY, GLM-5.3-Flash 148.43 thousand, Kimi K3 91.52 thousand. Evaluation starts with 40 thousand CNY. | §§3.1, 5.1 |
| Return and inference efficiency rank models differently | GPT leads terminal assets; DeepSeek leads net-asset growth per estimated API dollar. GPT’s mean API expenditure is approximately 80× DeepSeek’s for approximately 2.9× its asset growth. | §4.2 |
| Demand replay improves over references, with residual error | Held-out eight-week evaluation on 67 machines, averaged over 100 seeds: machine-week WAPE 0.473 versus 0.606 naive and 0.561 persistence; subcategory TVD 0.153 versus 0.206 naive. | §3.3; Fig. 3 |
| Strong returns coexist with imperfect site matching | Across six 30-day checkpoints, GPT’s existing-assortment assignment falls 7.05% below the restricted reassignment optimum, versus 2.61% for the baseline. | §5.3; Fig. 5b |
| Feedback accumulation does not show consistent economic improvement | Daily asset growth declines between days 16–45 and 136–165 for all five model means and 13 of 15 executions. | §5.5; Fig. 6 |
| Low waste can accompany poor availability | Whole-episode Claude results combine 0.16% waste with empty shelves on 25.21% of site-days; DeepSeek records 1.85% waste and 1.67% empty site-days. | §5.1 |

These author-reported results support differences within the evaluated simulator and show why isolated operational metrics can misrepresent economic effectiveness. They do not establish real-world performance, general model rankings across operating environments, or a causal failure to learn from feedback.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Three runs per model in one environment; the conclusion calls for varied conditions and controlled interventions (§6).
- **Author-stated simulator limits:** Price and environmental responses remain unvalidated. Margins, shelf lives, elasticities, environmental effects, and costs include assumed settings (§3.3).
- **Author-stated diagnostic limits:** Temporal comparisons confound feedback with changing environment and inventory states. Inspected failure cases establish neither prevalence nor causality (§§5.5–5.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Unlimited negative cash without interest weakens applicability to businesses facing financing or insolvency constraints (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Valuing all unexpired terminal inventory at acquisition cost may obscure difficulty selling remaining stock; the objective does not measure its eventual liquidation value (§3.1).
- **Reporting gaps:** The supplied retrieval includes Table 2’s caption but not its numerical body. Full per-model results, uncertainty, and absolute API expenditures cannot be checked from it.

## Open research questions
- **Do rankings survive different operating conditions?** The main evaluation uses one frozen world. Next experiment: repeat agents and baseline across demand seeds, network compositions, and execution-cost settings (§§4.1, 6).
- **Can explicit state verification prevent infeasible allocations economically?** Trajectories show repeated stock-check failures, without a controlled intervention. Next experiment: add product-level pre-action checks and measure rejected actions, assets, and API cost (§5.6).
- **Does persistent policy revision improve later decisions?** Repeated local repairs do not establish durable learning. Next experiment: compare structured policy memory against the existing notebook under matched conditions (§§3.4, 5.5–5.6).
- **How sensitive are outcomes to assumed demand responses?** Pricing and environmental responses are unvalidated. Next experiment: validate these responses on additional observations, then rerun model comparisons under the revised simulator (§3.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the benchmark and headline findings, but lacks the numerical bodies of Tables 1–2 and plotted figure values, exact daily interaction budgets and compaction configuration, detailed API rate calculations, and a verifiable release location. Raw commercial records are explicitly withheld (Appendix C).
