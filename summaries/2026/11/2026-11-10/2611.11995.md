# Infrathon: Benchmarking Language Agents for Real-World, Long-Horizon AI Infra Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11995) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11995)


## One-sentence takeaway
Infrathon evaluates agents on 25 infrastructure optimization tasks with 12–24-hour budgets, finding frequent late improvements and horizon-dependent rankings, alongside a video-cache codec that improves compression and measured quality but introduces workload-dependent runtime tradeoffs (§§4.1–4.3; Appendix E.5).

## Problem and motivation
Infrastructure optimization requires repeated implementation, profiling, and validation under correctness and hardware constraints. The authors argue that existing benchmarks provide incomplete coverage of sustained optimization across individual operators, interacting modules, and complete systems, leaving unclear whether agents can keep finding useful improvements over extended runs (§1; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and task construction:** Infrathon contains 11 operator, five module, and nine system tasks spanning training and inference. Human experts construct tasks using Infrathon-build; operator budgets are 12 hours, and module/system budgets are 24 hours (§§3–3.1).
- **Execution and validation framework:** Tasks specify editable workspaces, public/private tests, resources, and budgets. Separate Work and Judge environments protect private tests; a GPT-6.1 Sol Controller reviews submissions for task compliance, while a stop hook encourages continued work (§3.2).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Six models are evaluated across 150 main runs, with additional equal-GPU-hour comparisons between sustained runs and independent shorter attempts (§4; Appendix A.1).
- **Optimization case study:** An agent develops a video KV-cache codec combining clustering, PCA residual transforms, trellis quantization, and entropy coding, evaluated against the human codec on two video models (§4.2; Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation scale | Six models × 25 tasks = 150 main runs; 11,376 allocated GPU-hours, including continuously allocated Judge resources; 12-hour operator and 24-hour module/system budgets. | §4; Appendix A.1 |
| Claude leads final rankings | Claude Opus 5.5 wins 17/25 tasks, with mean rank score 91.2 versus Astra’s 75.2. Scores aggregate within-task ranks with equal task weights, rather than raw performance margins. | §4.1; Appendix A.1 |
| Rankings depend on horizon | At 25% of budget, Astra leads Claude 84.0 versus 79.2 in mean rank score; at 50%, Claude leads 89.6 versus 80.0. | §4.3; Figure 7(a) |
| Late improvements are frequent | Best private-test scores improve during the second half in 135/150 runs. Among runs with positive first-half scores, median improvement is 3.05%, and 33 runs improve by more than 10%. | §4.3; Appendix A.1 |
| Sustained runs particularly benefit systems | Across two models and six tasks at equal GPU-hours, best-of-four shorter runs achieve 102.6%, 96.9%, and 77.3% of long-run performance for operators, modules, and systems, respectively; these are geometric means of task–model score ratios. | §4.3; Figure 8; Appendix C |
| Codec improves compression and reference similarity | On 200 samples per dataset, Human→Claude compression is 6.75→20.89× on WorldScore and 6.88→18.83× on MovieGen; PSNR is 31.15→36.33 dB and 23.67→26.95 dB, respectively. SSIM and LPIPS also improve in dataset means. | §4.2; Table 3 |
| Codec runtime gains are workload-dependent | Mean generation time is 305→262 seconds on WorldScore, but 1,734→1,760 seconds on MovieGen, with MovieGen timing using 188 matched pairs. MovieGen decoding time rises from 18.5 to 102.4 seconds per video. | Appendix E.5; Tables 9–10 |
| Public-test success does not ensure compliance | Claude and DeepSeek attention implementations pass public numerical tests while changing required rounding order; the Controller rejects both. | §4.4; Figure 9 |

The evidence supports sustained improvement within these tasks and budgets, and a concrete codec improvement against the specified human implementation. It does not establish universal superiority of longer runs, broad superiority over human engineers, or compression gains translating directly into end-to-end speedups.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section, but reports several material caveats:

- **Reported scope:** Models use different harnesses—Claude Code for Claude and Codex CLI for the others—so comparisons evaluate model–harness combinations (§4).
- **Analyst inference—run variability:** Main results retain the most recently started run per task–model pair. Repeated-run uncertainty for headline rankings is not established; figure error bars summarize variation across tasks (Appendix A.1; Figure 5).
- **Analyst inference—limited horizon comparison:** Equal-GPU-hour comparisons cover only two models and six tasks; their generality remains uncertain (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—validation reliability:** Compliance review uses another language model, without reported detection accuracy or reviewer ablations (§3.2).
- **Reported codec selection caveat:** WorldScore screening retains 200/289 reviewed samples; codec results had already been inspected for eight pilot samples, two of which were excluded (Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference—quality scope:** Frame-similarity metrics cover the first segment using compressed KV; full-video evidence uses four VBench dimensions. These do not establish human-perceived quality or unrestricted deployment performance (Appendix E.4).
- **Reported reproducibility distinction:** Codec task scores were recomputed with a quality-weighted metric; they should not be conflated with compression ratios (Appendices E.2–E.4).

## Open research questions
- **When should compute fund longer runs versus restarts?** Outcomes differ by task level and model. Repeat the equal-budget experiment across all tasks and multiple seeds, then evaluate adaptive restart policies.
- **How reliable is automated compliance review?** Rejection examples demonstrate usefulness but not error rates. Compare Controller decisions with blinded expert audits and adversarially constructed submissions.
- **Can late gains be predicted?** Plateaus sometimes precede improvement, while many second-half gains are small. Test stopping rules based on profiling, submission history, and progress signals against full-budget outcomes.
- **Can the codec retain its advantages while reducing decoding overhead?** MovieGen decoding becomes substantially slower. Profile the codec components and evaluate decoding-focused variants under the same compression and quality checks.
