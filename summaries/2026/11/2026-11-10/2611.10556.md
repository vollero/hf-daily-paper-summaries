# onóna: Twin Embeddings with Tied Unembeddings

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10556) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10556)


## One-sentence takeaway
onóna’s two tied vocabulary tables improve loss and perplexity in 11 of 12 matched-token comparisons, but add computation and lose to a roughly resource-matched deeper baseline at 330M (§§3.2, 4.3).

## Problem and motivation
A conventional tied language model shares one table between input embeddings and vocabulary prediction, saving parameters but retaining the expressivity restriction of a single-softmax head. The authors seek greater output capacity while preserving input–output sharing: their untied-head and two-component mixture-of-softmaxes controls do not recover onóna’s gains under the tested training recipe (§§1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Twin tied tables:** Two vocabulary tables, A and B, provide averaged input embeddings to an unchanged decoder. The same hidden state is projected through each table; their separately normalized output distributions are mixed (§2.2).
- **Token-only gate:** A learned gate reads the current token’s averaged embedding. Input averages and per-token gate values can be precomputed for serving. Exact scoring and greedy decoding still require both vocabulary projections (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Expressivity and empirical analysis:** A constructive example shows that the head can exceed the single-softmax rank bound; architecture comparisons and factorial ablations test the joint input/output design. The rank construction does not establish the mechanism behind trained-model gains (Appendix A; §§3–4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Smaller-model external perplexity improves consistently | Across all eight 142M/330M pairs, LAMBADA last-word perplexity falls 0.99–9.18% and WikiText-2 word perplexity falls 0.67–3.62%. | §3.2 |

The reported results support a useful vocabulary-head modification under the tested recipe, with evidence of complementary input and output roles. They do not establish superior compute efficiency, statistically reliable small accuracy gains, or rank expansion as the cause of improvement. Control groups have separate baselines and should not be pooled indiscriminately.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Primary comparisons lack replicate seeds and uncertainty estimates. Training tokens are matched, but parameters and FLOPs are not; the approximate resource control exists only at 330M. Hardware, software, and GPU layouts vary, and some separate evaluations use unequal validation-window counts. Baseline and twin inputs have different initialization variance, without an isolating control (Appendices C–E).

The authors also leave the 1B MoE loss/accuracy disagreement unexplained. Larger vocabularies, longer contexts, and gate assignments by token type are untested. Their custom downstream scoring and truncated MMLU prompts prevent direct comparison with published harness scores (Appendices B, E).

**Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond the tested corpus, tokenizer, and context length remains uncertain. The factorial evidence establishes complementarity in one Dense setting, rather than across all evaluated architectures.

## Open research questions
- **Does onóna improve quality per unit compute at larger scales?** The depth control favors Standard at 330M, and no corresponding 1B control exists. Next experiment: replicated parameter- and FLOP-matched depth/width comparisons at 1B.
- **How much improvement comes from initialization scale?** Averaging independently initialized tables changes input variance. Next experiment: match input variance across Standard and twin-table models while retaining the factorial design.
- **Why does 1B MoE worsen on perplexity but improve downstream accuracy?** The paper offers no explanation. Next experiment: repeat paired runs with shared validation windows and inspect task-level uncertainty and routing behavior.
- **Does trained-head expressivity explain the gains?** Appendix A proves possibility, not realized rank expansion. Next experiment: measure log-probability rank, component divergence, and gate behavior across matched checkpoints.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose gives explicit protocols and substantial results, but the referenced tables contain captions without their numerical bodies. Complete per-task results, full model/hyperparameter tables, primary-run uncertainty, trained-rank measurements, and code/checkpoint release availability are unavailable in the supplied text.
