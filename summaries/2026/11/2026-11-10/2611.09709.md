# RidgeFill: Training-Free Late-Layer Cache Fill for Faster Prefill

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09709) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09709)


## One-sentence takeaway
RidgeFill uses closed-form ridge maps to approximate late-layer prompt caches, reporting 1.25–1.60× faster prefill in two serving deployments while accepting model-dependent quality losses that retrieval scores alone can conceal (§§4–6, 8).

## Problem and motivation
Long-prompt prefill delays the first response because every prompt token normally traverses every layer. The authors target the upper layers’ computation while retaining every token’s cache entry.

Their identified gap is a retrofit that improves on identity-based cache filling without iterative training or backbone changes. They also argue that average token loss obscures damage to distant-context retrieval, motivating evaluation specifically on filled-region needles and tokens that benefit from the prefix (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Closed-form cache fill:** Run lower layers exactly, then predict each late layer’s input as \(\hat{x}_\ell=A_\ell x_S+b_\ell\). Fit each map by ridge regression on captured calibration activations; retain the layer’s original normalization, projections, and hybrid recurrent updates. Backbone weights remain unchanged (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Selective exact computation:** Run an exact tail, selected prefix rows, and all decoding steps through the full model. Deployment-specific selectors use attention, token classes and rarity, or a late-layer attention probe. Optional corrections rescale values or adjust recurrent state (§§2.3–2.4). These computations still read approximate caches, so their outputs need not match exact prefill.
- **Serving implementations and diagnostic evaluation:** Implement the method in llama.cpp and vLLM, compare fitted maps with identity fill and SwiftKV, and use matched-row controls and cache-component splices to identify failures (§§3–6). The authors report releasing maps, reference code, results, and reproduction scripts (§9).

“Training-free” here means no iterative training: calibration capture, closed-form fitting, and held-out hyperparameter selection are still required (§2.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

The evidence supports useful speed–quality tradeoffs in the tested configurations, not lossless acceleration or universal non-inferiority. Near-perfect needle retrieval does not establish preservation of context-dependent prediction: even selected-row configurations retain measurable key-token loss (§8). The two serving speedups also reflect different hardware, engines, and memory placement and should not be directly ranked.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Quality failures are material and model-dependent:** Shallow Llama configurations can perform worse than no fill; selected rows hurt Qwen3-8B multi-needle retrieval without a tested explanation (§§6.5–6.6, 8).
- **Selector transfer and cache reuse remain uncertain:** Selectors were developed separately for each deployment. Two depend on the current question; the second-question experiment largely reuses already-protected needle tokens and does not establish performance on unrelated questions (§8; Appendix D).
- **Long-context coverage is uneven:** Calibration positions stop around 30K tokens. On the 27B, retrieval and agreement evaluations stop at 32K; longer-context quality evidence is limited to LongBench v2’s long group (§8).
- **Important ablations are absent:** Final runs do not isolate the 27B value rescale or Flash-Next’s terminal correction from protected rows. The 27B probe window and budget were not swept (§8).
- **Speed evidence is narrow:** Only the two quantized hybrid serving models were timed. Timings have few repetitions and no intervals; Flash-Next uses prefixes of one document. Its expert offloading confounds saved arithmetic with saved PCIe traffic (§§3.4, 5.3, 8).
- **Memory and reproducibility costs remain:** Cache memory is retained and maps add memory. Flash-Next lacks an upstream-equivalence check, its plain run lacks a fill-off check, and fork patches are unpublished. Calibration/evaluation texts are represented by hashes rather than released text (§§8–9).
- **Analyst inference:** Because serving deployments omit identity-fill comparisons, they do not isolate how much of the measured serving benefit specifically requires fitted maps rather than the surrounding skip-and-select pipeline (§3.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does longer calibration improve extrapolation?** Maps never see positions beyond roughly 30K, and longer calibration was not tested (§8). Fit matched maps with short versus long calibration documents, then compare filled-region retrieval and key-token loss at longer contexts.
2. **Why do selected exact rows hurt Qwen3-8B?** Random rows avoid the same decline, while partial key/value splices also destabilize results (§§6.5–6.6). Vary whether complete needles, individual rows, or paired key/value components become exact.
3. **Which corrections independently help?** Final configurations bundle corrections with other changes (§8). Run a factorial ablation of protected rows and terminal-state correction on Flash-Next, plus value-rescale on/off on the 27B.
4. **Can one filled cache serve unrelated questions reliably?** The existing second-question test has substantial protected-token overlap (Appendix D). Test unrelated questions against a once-filled cache, comparing query-aware selection, question-independent selection, and deeper splits.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative gives detailed settings, controls, and explicit caveats, but most referenced tables contain captions without their cells.

Material information unavailable here includes complete per-task scores and intervals, full timing/memory tables, several calibration counts and model revisions, raw experimental records, and the unpublished vLLM patches. Reported releases and reproduction checks have not been independently verified.
