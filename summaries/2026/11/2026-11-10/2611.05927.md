# Modality-Aware Expert Pruning for MoE-Based Multimodal Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05927) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05927)


## One-sentence takeaway
MAEP uses separate text-preservation and low-attention vision-pruning signals to remove routed experts without training, reporting stronger multimodal performance retention than tested compression baselines while remaining sensitive to calibration coverage (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Sparse expert activation reduces computation but leaves expert weights resident in memory. The authors report that routed experts dominate the weight footprint of the evaluated MoE vision-language models. Existing compression methods often aggregate importance across tokens, obscuring differences between text and vision expert roles (§§1–2).

The identified gap is permanent expert removal for memory reduction using modality-aware importance, complementing approaches that skip experts during inference while retaining their parameters (§2.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Redistribution-aware importance:** Delta-H Output measures squared MoE-output change under an expert-removal counterfactual. Cached routing weights and expert outputs enable scoring in one forward pass per calibration sample, without gradients. The implemented approximation omits the promoted replacement expert’s output (§5.1).
- **Asymmetric modality scoring:** Text importance protects experts; importance on the bottom 2% of vision tokens by text-to-vision attention favors removal. The authors support this choice with ranking-stability, saliency, and output-perturbation analyses (§5.2; Appendices G and I.1).
- **Global selection:** Log-transformed, layer-normalized modality scores are combined using a model-specific correlation-based weight, then ranked across layers. The theoretical bound motivates within-layer Delta-H scoring; global normalization and modality combination rely on empirical validation (§§5.1.1–5.3).

These results support modality-aware scoring in the tested settings and establish weight reduction more directly than universal acceleration. They do not establish domain invariance, unchanged language capability, or deployment on a physical 40 GB GPU: the memory-capped measurements use an A100 80GB. Missing numerical table bodies prevent independent assessment of most benchmark margins.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fixed masks do not model joint removal of mutually supporting experts or input-dependent sparsity. A two-stage pruning experiment performs worse but explicitly does not isolate interaction effects (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Calibration under-coverage can sacrifice specialized capabilities. The modality-weight rule is empirical, and layer-wise weighting, fine-tuning recovery, and compression combinations remain future work (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Ranking stability across two calibration sources supports a limited transfer claim, rather than general dataset stability. Several mechanism checks focus on InternVL3.5 (Appendices G–I).
- **Analyst inference:** The KL bound does not guarantee faithful importance ranking or justify global allocation. Shallow-layer proxy weakness and unestimated layer-dependent constants limit its explanatory reach (§5.1.1).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** InternVL3.5’s rounded operating weight is 0.8, while the sensitivity discussion describes a swept stable region ending at 0.7 and collapse at 0.9. Missing Table 3 cells prevent resolving this precisely (§6.3.3).

## Open research questions
- **How can pruning account for expert interactions?** One-shot scoring omits joint-removal effects, and the staged experiment is confounded by rescoring after redistribution. Compare pairwise-aware selection against one-shot MAEP at matched budgets (Appendix A).
- **How broadly does low-attention stability generalize?** Detailed stability evidence centers on limited models and calibration sources. Repeat matched-size selector tests across additional document, OCR, and visual domains (Appendix G).
- **Can layer-specific modality weights improve preservation?** The method uses one weight per model despite acknowledged layer variation. Compare fixed and calibration-derived layer-wise weights on held-out multimodal and text tasks (§6.3.3).
- **What runtime gains survive other serving implementations?** Measurements use non-fused kernels, with fused-engine benefits only hypothesized. Benchmark resident and memory-capped serving across fused kernels, context lengths, and batch sizes (Appendix C.1).
