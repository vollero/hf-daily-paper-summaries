# Auriga Technical Report: A Task-Agnostic Agent Harness for Zero-Shot Embodied Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01112) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01112)


## One-sentence takeaway
Auriga combines geometric perception, motion previews, execution checks, and within-episode recovery to improve a frozen vision-language agent’s zero-shot manipulation performance on two simulation benchmarks, although real-world effectiveness remains unestablished.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a gap between selecting sensible manipulation actions and executing them reliably: small grasp errors, slippage, and unintended contact can invalidate otherwise reasonable plans. In their diagnostic, bypassing robot-arm execution substantially improves success (§2.2).

They argue that existing step-wise interfaces reveal failures too late, while generated programs can finish normally despite failed physical actions. Auriga targets unseen-task execution within one episode, without preparatory target-task trials, demonstrations, or resets (§§2.1–2.3).

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is an integrated agent harness rather than a newly trained robot policy:

- **Explicit task state:** A persistent model session maintains a compact plan, uncertainty list, and premise ledger, backed by retrievable interaction records (§3).
- **Unified motion admission:** Direct calls and generated Python programs share a preview–execute–verify gateway. Trajectories are compiled before execution; stage deviations trigger stops and measured feedback for model-directed recovery. Preview review is the default but can be skipped (§4).
- **Geometric perception and expressive control:** Calibrated localization, learned depth and segmentation, triangulation, and in-hand measurements support finger-referenced goals and coordinated arm timelines (§5).
- **Development/evaluation separation:** The authors describe cross-task capability development followed by a frozen evaluation harness; plans and recovery evolve only within each evaluated episode (§§2.1, 2.3).

The supplied evidence supports these architectural contributions, but does not independently establish their novelty relative to every prior system.

All results below are author-reported.

The evidence supports improved performance for the evaluated system and useful contributions from several mechanisms on selected subsets. It does not establish real-world reliability or isolate every component’s effect. The privileged-motion diagnostic changes the action space, so its success rate is an operational proxy for planning capability rather than a pure measurement of reasoning.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Reported weaknesses:** Precision and long-horizon performance remain below the leading comparator. RoboCasa failures include request-budget exhaustion and the model ceasing to act; coupled-object recovery can consume the remaining budget without completing the task (§§6.2–6.3; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** Both evaluations are simulated, and deliberation pauses the world. These results do not establish behavior under continuous real-world dynamics or latency (§§3.1, 6.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** RoboProbe uses lower reasoning effort. RoboCasa baselines differ in VLA use and preparatory memory, and learned-policy entries evaluated in pretraining kitchens are not directly matched to target-kitchen agents (§6.1).
- **Analyst inference—information boundaries:** RoboCasa supplies rendered-depth coordinate maps, success-check source, and live predicates, unlike RoboDojo’s restricted observations. Its results therefore do not demonstrate the same perception-only information boundary (§6.1).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Ablations cover selected subsets, and some remove multiple capabilities together. For example, “No triangulation” also removes object estimates and projection, preventing attribution solely to triangulation (§6.4).
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated, and many tables retain captions without their cells; complete coverage is impossible.

## Open research questions

- **Does the approach transfer to physical robots?** Paused simulation avoids scene changes during deliberation. A next experiment could evaluate physical manipulation with measured latency and moving-scene disturbances.
- **How much improvement comes from the harness versus reasoning effort?** The reference comparison uses unequal effort. Run both harnesses on matched cases with identical model settings and budgets.
- **Which state-management mechanisms matter independently?** The supplied ablations do not isolate task belief and evidence retrieval. Remove each separately on matched memory and long-horizon tasks.
- **Can recovery become more economical?** Failed episodes consume substantial calls and motion budgets. Compare the current recovery strategy with budget-aware stopping and replanning under equal total budgets.
