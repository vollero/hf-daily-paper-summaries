# WorldFoundry: Towards Unified Infrastructure for World Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10771) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10771)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorldFoundry proposes a functional definition of world models and shared infrastructure for executing heterogeneous models with traceable evaluation artifacts, but the supplied text reports integration claims rather than measured world-model performance.

## Problem and motivation
Visually convincing generation does not establish persistent state, physical dynamics, or consistent responses to interventions. The authors argue that incompatible representations, runtimes, and evaluation protocols make these capabilities difficult to compare reproducibly. They identify a need for both an operational definition of world modeling and infrastructure that preserves model-specific semantics while organizing execution and evidence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Functional definition:** State inference, forward simulation, and interactive response describe model operations. Qualification as a world model additionally requires persistent state: interventions must constrain later predictions, and new observations must revise that state (§1).
- **Capability framework:** The authors organize systems around perception, modeling, manipulation, dynamics, and interaction, allowing partial capability profiles without requiring every system to implement all operations (§1).
- **Unified infrastructure:** WorldFoundry connects discovery, dependency and asset preparation, execution, artifact management, evaluation, and visualization. Modality-specific adapters retain native inputs, outputs, and evaluation semantics; manifests, typed artifact references, and scorecards make claims traceable to execution evidence (Abstract; §1; Fig. 1).
- **Operational validation:** The authors claim documented reference-workflow execution for every integrated model runner. This establishes claimed readiness under tested configurations, rather than demonstrated world-model capability (§1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Reference-workflow validation supports operational readiness only under tested configurations; the infrastructure distinguishes readiness from validated performance (Abstract; §1).
- **Analyst inference:** A successful reference run cannot establish broad runtime reliability or world-model qualification. The supplied text gives no per-run configurations, failures, or capability measurements.
- **Analyst inference:** Preserving native evaluation protocols leaves cross-model comparability unresolved without details on shared inputs, settings, and scoring.
- **Missing evidence:** No ablations, runtime/resource measurements, comparative results, or detailed implementation protocols are available. This limits assessment of the infrastructure’s benefits, rather than proving those benefits absent.

## Open research questions
1. **How should loop closure be measured?** The paper defines intervention persistence and observation-driven revision but supplies no concrete test. A next experiment could evaluate controlled intervention-and-feedback sequences with explicit state-consistency scoring.
2. **How robust is operational readiness across configurations?** Validation is restricted to documented reference workflows. A next experiment could repeat runs across supported dependency versions, hardware, and input settings while recording failures.
3. **Can native protocols support fair cross-system comparisons?** The infrastructure preserves heterogeneous semantics without showing comparative results here. A next experiment could evaluate overlapping capabilities under matched inputs and disclose protocol-dependent ranking changes.
