# Click3R: Interactive Stereo 3D Reconstruction with Sparse Correspondence Clicks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12875) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12875)


## One-sentence takeaway
Click3R conditions a jointly trained stereo reconstruction model on sparse cross-view correspondence clicks, improving difficult-case geometry and pose accuracy, although its strongest single-click results require oracle selection (§§3, 5.2–5.4).

## Problem and motivation
Feed-forward reconstruction models can produce incorrect geometry without a simple way for users to correct it. The authors identify repetitive textures, symmetry, limited overlap, and large viewpoint changes as recurring challenges; automatic correspondences can also fail in these settings. Click3R addresses the gap between fully automatic prediction and expensive test-time refinement by accepting sparse human-specified matches (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Correspondence-conditioned reconstruction:** Click3R adds view-specific attention adapters before DUSt3R’s decoder. Correspondence tokens combine Fourier position encodings, view identity, and shared match identity; bidirectional attention exchanges information between prompts and image features. The backbone and adapters train jointly using geometry-derived prompts. Without prompts, the adapters are bypassed (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Hard3DBench:** A diagnostic benchmark contains 404 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution stereo pairs, plus 400 and 300 five-view tuples, respectively. Samples are selected using pretrained DUSt3R pose failures, with a geometric failure taxonomy (§4; Appendix C).
- **Interactive evaluation:** Experiments cover oracle, human, automatic, perturbed, and incorrect correspondences, plus controlled transfer to MASt3R (§5; Appendix E). The authors claim novelty specifically for sparse correspondence prompting of feed-forward stereo reconstruction, rather than correspondence-based reconstruction generally (§§1–2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Improvements extend across hard settings | Authors report higher mAA@30° and lower median pointmap error across both view counts and both distribution splits; largest reported pose gain is +9.9 points on five-view ID. | Human clicks improve difficult OOD pairs | On 100 pairs, one annotator supplies 3.8 matches on average, ranging from 1–7. Median rotation error falls from 17.30° to 8.78° and mAA@30 rises from 36.2 to 56.2 versus the unprompted model. | §5.4; Table 3 caption |
| Benefits span failure categories | With eight GT matches on Hard3DBench stereo pairs, aggregate mAA@30° rises from 54.6 to 61.7 versus retrained DUSt3R; large-viewpoint-change pairs gain 12.1 points. Categories overlap. | Appendix D; Table 7 caption |
| Joint adaptation performs better than freezing | Averaged across three standard datasets and two Hard3DBench stereo splits with one GT match, mAA@30 is 75.1% from CroCo v2 initialization, 72.3% with DUSt3R fine-tuning, and 71.2% with frozen DUSt3R. | §5.5; Appendix E.1 |

The evidence supports correspondence conditioning as a useful correction mechanism, including with human and automatically generated matches. Oracle results demonstrate achievable headroom rather than deployable click-selection performance. Five-view results use an iterative global alignment stage, so the absence of test-time optimization applies to the prompted stereo network, not the entire multi-view pipeline.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** End-to-end backbone training is costly; the adapter is not an effective plug-in correction module for a frozen model. Training is limited to stereo inputs at 224-pixel resolution (Appendix J).
- **Author-stated:** Attention provides soft conditioning rather than enforcing exact geometry. Failures persist with negligible overlap, extreme rotations, large scenes, and ambiguous matches (Appendices H–J).
- **Author-stated:** Incorrect correspondences can make performance worse than using no prompts (Appendix E.5).
- **Analyst inference:** A study with one annotator on 100 selected hard pairs does not establish usability across users or annotation-time efficiency (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting Hard3DBench through pretrained DUSt3R failures limits population-wide interpretation. The authors partly address model specificity by demonstrating difficulty for VGGT and Pi3 (Appendix C).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The human subset’s mAA@30 values differ between §5.4 (36.2→56.2) and Appendix E.6 (35.6 baseline; 56.8 human). The supplied text does not explain the discrepancy. Most numerical table bodies are also absent.

## Open research questions
1. **Can useful click locations be selected without ground truth?** Oracle gains depend strongly on placement (§5.2). Compare learned or uncertainty-guided selection against free human selection under equal click and time budgets.
2. **Can geometric constraints be enforced more reliably?** Soft conditioning still permits distorted reconstructions (Appendices H–J). Test multiple decoder injection points or explicit geometric modules against the current adapter using identical prompts.
3. **Does interaction scale to native multi-view reconstruction?** Current five-view evaluation combines stereo predictions through global alignment (Table 2). Train a multi-view model with correspondence trajectories and evaluate accuracy, latency, and annotation effort.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied method description and experiments support the central finding, but missing numerical table bodies limit comparison auditing. Material gaps include statistical uncertainty, multiple training runs, annotation time and inter-annotator variation, confirmed code/checkpoint/benchmark availability, and an explanation of the inconsistent human-subset scores.
