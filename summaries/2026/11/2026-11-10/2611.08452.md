# ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research Artifacts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08452) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08452)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReproBridge preserves recovered execution procedures in reusable artifacts, raising reported mean coding-agent reproduction success from 76.4% to 92.6% across four models while reducing downstream API spending by 19.7% on average (Abstract; §4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Released ML code often leaves dependencies, configurations, repairs, and score aggregation implicit. Successful reproducers recover this information, but their decisions can remain buried in session histories, forcing subsequent users to repeat the work. The authors ask whether reproduction depends on artifact quality or an agent’s ability to compensate for missing information, and whether preserving recovered procedures improves independent reuse (§1).

Here, reproduction means re-executing author-released materials to obtain numerically compatible measurements; it does not mean independent reimplementation or scientific validation (§§1, 5).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Standardized artifact design.** A refined reproducible artifact contains repaired code, environment setup, experiment launchers, and a reproduction guide with justified decisions and a change record. It preserves both executable steps and their rationale (§3.2).
2. **Construction workflow.** ReproBridge organizes agents into Specify, Execute, and Preserve stages, with separate review contexts, persistent execution evidence, and clean-shell delivery checks. Humans define the workflow and standards; agents construct, review, and package artifacts (§3.1; Appendix B).
3. **Benchmark and evaluation protocol.** ReproBridge-Bench contains 1,029 eligible measurements from 387 experiments across 36 papers, with five human-verified reference observations per task. Success combines numerical compatibility with an independent execution-integrity audit (§2; §4.1).

Numerical compatibility uses a reference-based Student-t prediction interval with a 1% tolerance floor. Fresh downstream sessions receive either raw code or refined artifacts, without construction conversations or hidden reference observations (§§2.3, 4.1).

The evidence supports improved coding-agent reuse under the tested workflow, tasks, budgets, and scoring rules. It does not isolate documentation formatting from upstream investigation and repair, establish human productivity gains, or demonstrate full economic break-even. Several referenced tables contain captions but no numerical bodies in the supplied text; the results above use numbers explicitly available in prose.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Selection and eligibility:** The evaluation covers accessible, resource-feasible papers and only tasks whose verified references are compatible with published scores. The authors explicitly reject interpreting it as a conference-wide reproduction rate (§§2.2–2.3, 6.3).
- **Reference uncertainty:** Five observations may inadequately characterize variability. The interval construction assumes faithful, independent references and approximate normality; numerical agreement alone cannot certify fidelity (§2.3; Appendix A.4; §6.3).
- **Bundled intervention:** Construction includes investigation, repair, review, and packaging. Ordinary-output controls come from separate upstream runs, and ablations measure component losses conditional on retained information (§§4.5, 6.3).
- **Incorrect knowledge can persist:** Refined artifacts can preserve wrong assumptions or introduce experimental choices that later agents inherit; reversals are documented (§4.4; Appendix D.3).
- **Evaluation boundaries:** Vendor harnesses differ, making cross-model comparisons descriptive. Human reuse, maintenance, and total costs remain unmeasured (§§4.1, 6.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Because tasks can share executions and audit evidence, task counts should not be read as equally many independent confirmations; this dependence is explicit for human audit validation (Appendix C.4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the handoff improve human reproduction?** Only coding-agent reuse is measured (§6.1). A randomized researcher study could compare completion, time, and comprehension using raw versus refined artifacts.
2. **What does packaging contribute beyond identical repaired outputs?** Separate upstream runs confound this distinction (§6.3). Freeze one execution directory and randomize standardized documentation and launcher packaging.
3. **How robust are compatibility decisions to reference sampling?** Five references do not guarantee adequate uncertainty estimation (§6.3). Collect additional independent observations and compare eligibility and success labels across reference subsets.
4. **When does reuse repay total costs?** Current crossover calculations omit GPU execution, auditing, maintenance, and human work (Appendix E.1). Track those costs during repeated reuse and dependency updates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly supports the workflow and headline findings, but many table bodies and figure contents are absent. Material gaps include per-model main-result means and standard deviations, exact component-ablation losses, external-benchmark success rates, complete harness settings, underlying execution records, and verified public asset availability. Human-use outcomes and full lifecycle costs are not measured.
