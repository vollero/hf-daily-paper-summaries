# InfraRSI: Harnessing Recursive Self-Improvement for Training Infrastructure

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11558) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11558)


## One-sentence takeaway
InfraRSI jointly searches training configurations and source patches under verification gates, reporting a 1.83× geometric-mean speedup across four LLM workloads, although its short-window checks do not establish long-run training equivalence (§6.1; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
Distributed training performance depends on interacting launch settings, kernels, communication schedules, and memory limits. Optimizations that work individually may fail when combined, while coding agents can exploit timing boundaries or change numerical behavior instead of improving equivalent work. The authors identify a gap between isolated kernel optimization and reliable, coordinated optimization of complete training systems (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Execution and verification harness:** A five-stage workflow—init, doctor, run, report, apply—fixes the task contract, measures cluster noise, searches, audits results, and applies promoted changes. It combines receipt checks, source review, fresh baseline comparisons, and accounting for failed trials (§3).
- **Compositional search:** Diverse starting configurations feed global Bayesian execution-time and censored peak-memory models. These guide prior revision, memory-saving bundles, conflict recovery, exploration beyond local optima, and verified patch stacking (§4).
- **Experience transfer:** Structured environment, observation, and effect records initialize configurations and model priors on later hardware. Target measurements subsequently update those priors (§5).

The contribution is the integrated controller and verification workflow; the paper orchestrates existing compiler and kernel primitives (§7).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher aggregate throughput than compared agents/tuner | Across four dense/MoE workloads, geometric-mean speedup over reference configurations: InfraRSI 1.83×; RE-Bench 1.37×; Auto-Research 1.34×; OptiCo 1.23×, under identical task contracts. | §6.1; Table 4 and Figure 2 references |
| Higher training utilization | Across the same workloads, mean MFU rises from 17.4% to 30.0%, using active-model FLOPs and non-sparse BF16 peak; timing uses updates 11–30. | §6.1 |
| Coordinated changes deliver workload-specific gains | Gemma-4-26B-A4B, 8×H200: 2.75× speedup, 17.6% MFU. Two-node Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 16×H100: 1.51×, 13.2%. Dense Qwen3.8-27B, 8×H200: 1.55×, 45.4%; Qwen3.5-9B, 8×H100: 1.73×, 43.8%. | §6.1, coordinated discoveries |
| Cross-SKU experience helps in one controlled task | Qwen3.5-9B, 8×H100: final speedup 1.82× versus 1.70× for both cold start and all eligible packs. Only cross-SKU transfer reaches the common 90% peak-log-speedup threshold within budget: trial 9, 2.97 GPU-hours. | §6.2; Table 5 reference |
| Verifier detects tested violations | Receipt suite accepts 12 benign cases and rejects 28 perturbed cases, matching all 40 expected verdicts. Offline rejudging rejects 400/1,212 executions; 39 require numerical checks despite passing non-numerical checks. | §6.3; Table 6A |
| Limited sensitivity to tested tolerance/noise choices | Qwen3.5-9B, 8×H100: loss tolerances of 0.10%, 3%, and 50%, plus a fixed 2% noise-gate arm, yield 1.70–1.75× final speedups and reach the common 90% peak-log-speedup threshold within 10–11 trials. Reported search costs span 6.02–7.05 GPU-hours. | §6.3; Table 6B |

These results support throughput gains in the tested campaigns and useful transfer in one target setting. They do not establish long-run convergence equivalence, universal protection against reward hacking, or the separate causal contribution of each search component. Most referenced table bodies and figure data are absent from the supplied rendering, although surrounding prose supplies the headline results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-defined scope:** Numerical verification concerns short-horizon loss consistency; timing measures a post-warmup window. Full training-quality preservation is not demonstrated (§§2.1, 6.1).
- **Analyst inference—verification coverage is conditional:** Appendix A permits unchecked data identity when digests are absent; gradient norms were unchecked in every historical run; precision metadata is informational. Precision-menu enforcement therefore also depends on source auditing (§3.3; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—tolerance strength varies:** Appendix A allows loss tolerance to widen without a cap under specified conditions. “Verified” does not necessarily mean the nominal tolerance was enforced unchanged.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization and attribution:** End-to-end evaluation covers four workloads on H100/H200, while controlled transfer and sensitivity studies use one target workload. No component-removal study isolates memory modeling, initialization, prior revision, or patch stacking (§6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Identical contracts and cost logging are stated, but the supplied text does not establish matched search budgets, proposal-model identities, repeated-campaign uncertainty, or complete baseline configurations (§§2.2, 6).

## Open research questions
- **Do promoted patches preserve long-run training quality?** Short-window loss agreement leaves this unresolved. Next experiment: train reference and promoted configurations for matched token budgets and compare convergence and held-out evaluation.
- **Which controller components drive the gains?** Existing ablations vary tolerance and noise gates, not core search mechanisms. Next experiment: remove memory modeling, multi-start initialization, prior revision, and patch stacking separately under matched budgets.
- **How broadly does experience transfer help?** Controlled evidence covers one target workload, and broader eligible retrieval underperforms filtered transfer. Next experiment: repeat filtered, unfiltered, and cold-start comparisons across workloads, hardware, and topology changes.
- **How robust is verification when telemetry is incomplete?** Some checks are unchecked or informational in the historical audit. Next experiment: test adversarial code changes with and without mandatory data digests, precision enforcement, and checkpoint-resume checks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and headline outcomes, but lacks training dataset identities, complete task configurations and budgets, proposal-model details, repeated-run uncertainty, most table bodies and figure data, long-run quality results, and independent confirmation of released artifact completeness.
