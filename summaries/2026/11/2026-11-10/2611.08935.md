# SkinMind: Equipping Agentic RAG with Human-Inspired Graph Probing for Reliable Dermatology AI

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08935) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08935)


## One-sentence takeaway
SkinMind improves dermatology VQA scores across six benchmarks and two policy models through adaptive multimodal graph probing, while using less retrieved evidence but more total tokens than LightRAG (Table 3; §5.5).

## Problem and motivation
The authors target dermatology assistants that produce incorrect, outdated, or insufficiently traceable answers. They argue that one-shot text retrieval cannot adapt to emerging information needs, while conventional graph retrieval often leaves the model unable to explore relations interactively. Their proposed gap is adaptive access to connected textual, relational, and visual clinical evidence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interactive graph retrieval:** SkinMind lets an orchestrator construct a query-specific evidence subgraph using eight tools for node search, node inspection, source reading, relation and path exploration, and image retrieval. Progressive disclosure separates compact navigation results from detailed evidence (§§3.3–3.4; Table 1).
- **Multimodal knowledge graph:** SkinMind-KG combines source-linked guideline and textbook knowledge with disease-linked images. Construction uses a dermatologist-informed schema, ontology initialization, entity merging, and search-assisted conflict resolution (§4; Appendix A).
- **Integrated inference workflow and evaluation:** A fast–slow router selects direct answering or iterative retrieval; context compression controls history growth. Experiments compare the complete system against shared-backend RAG baselines and ablate three tool capabilities (§§3.1–3.3, 5).

The claimed methodological contribution is the integration of interactive graph exploration, multimodal retrieval, and selective evidence disclosure; the paper builds on existing graph-RAG, agentic retrieval, ontologies, and encoders (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher average score with GPT-5.4-mini | SkinMind **67.90**, Direct Answer **59.20**, LightRAG **62.15**: gains of **8.70** and **5.75** points. Average is the unweighted mean of five accuracy scores and the Morph-R judge score. | Table 3; §§5.1–5.2 |
| Higher average score with Gemini-2.5-Flash | SkinMind **69.42**, Direct Answer **60.53**, LightRAG **65.82**: gains of **8.89** and **3.60** points under the same six-benchmark protocol. | Table 3 |
| Consistent within-policy benchmark wins | SkinMind has the highest reported score on every benchmark against all evaluated alternatives within each policy-model block. Standalone reference models are explicitly outside the shared comparison protocol. | Table 3 and caption |
| Graph exploration contributes | With GPT-5.4-mini, disabling relation, neighbor, and path tools reduces DDI accuracy from **65.09 to 62.65** and SNU134-4 from **85.00 to 82.50**. | Table 4; §5.3 |
| Images and disclosure contribute | On SNU134-4 with GPT-5.4-mini, removing image retrieval reduces accuracy from **85.00 to 79.17**; removing progressive disclosure reduces it to **80.83**. | Table 4 |
| Compact evidence does not mean lower total usage | On Morph-R with Gemini-2.5-Flash, SkinMind averages **1,380 retrieved evidence tokens/question**, versus LightRAG’s **7,901**; aggregate token usage is approximately **26.1k versus 12.1k**. | §5.5; Figure 5 |
| Large source-linked retrieval asset | SkinMind-KG contains **1,006,079 nodes**, **2,038,419 edges**, and **423,016 image nodes**, of which **301,561** link to disease nodes. | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Conflict processing is largely automated | Of **1,375 detected description conflicts**, **1,366 (99.3%)** were automatically reconciled and nine referred to experts; correctness of those resolutions is not quantified. | §4.1; Appendix A.4 |

These results support improved benchmark answering under the authors’ shared corpus, backend, and policy settings. The ablations support contributions from the tested components, but do not fully isolate why the complete system wins. They do not establish clinical safety, citation faithfulness, or prospective patient benefit.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified retrieval failures:** Two analyzed errors show unverified distinguishing features and exploration concentrated on a favored diagnosis. The authors propose improving tool-use strategy through training (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—limited component coverage:** Ablations use one policy model and two benchmarks. Routing, context compression, conflict resolution, and individual graph tools are not separately evaluated (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty is unquantified:** No confidence intervals, repeated-run variation, or significance tests are reported, despite generation temperature being set to 1.0 (§5.1; Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Morph-R uses a judge without access to the input image; the other benchmarks measure multiple-choice accuracy. Neither directly validates real-world treatment advice or evidence attribution (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference—residual data-quality risk:** Exact and high-similarity image filtering addresses image overlap, but broader contamination audits and quantitative extraction, alignment, and conflict-resolution accuracy are not reported (§§4.1, 5.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency tradeoff:** Lower evidence volume accompanies higher total token usage; latency and monetary cost are unavailable (§5.5).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Prompts and implementation details are supplied, but the extraction few-shot example is omitted, and release access for SkinMind code, KG, and full source inventory is not established (Appendices A–D).

## Open research questions
1. **Can comparative exploration reduce diagnostic anchoring?** Appendix C shows favored-candidate exploration. Compare the existing policy with a policy required to retrieve distinguishing evidence for competing diagnoses.
2. **Which components generalize across settings?** Current ablations cover only one policy and two benchmarks. Run routing, compression, and tool ablations across both policies and all benchmarks.
3. **Are retrieved claims and final citations clinically faithful?** Source links and conflict processing are described without a quantitative correctness audit. Have dermatologists independently assess sampled graph claims, reconciliations, and answer citations.
4. **What accuracy–cost tradeoff is achievable?** SkinMind uses more total tokens despite compact evidence. Sweep retrieval budgets and stopping rules while measuring accuracy, latency, and cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive summary of the supplied methods and reported results; clinical reliability remains unestablished.

Material missing information includes benchmark sample counts, run variability, subgroup performance, clinical validation, attribution accuracy, graph-quality audits, routing behavior, latency and monetary costs, and confirmed public access to the system and KG.
