# Stochastic Deep Supervision for Gallery-Consistent Early-Exit Face Recognition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09987) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09987)


## One-sentence takeaway
SD-ViT trains intermediate ViT exits through a shared identity head and classifier, enabling cheaper probes to match a full-depth gallery while retaining strong recognition accuracy, although fixed-threshold performance deteriorates at the shallowest exits (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Face recognition compares embeddings of previously unseen identities, so early exits must produce representations compatible with enrolled gallery embeddings. Independently trained exit heads can create incompatible spaces, requiring gallery recomputation or adaptation layers. The authors identify this **gallery consistency** requirement as the gap between conventional early-exit classification and practical face recognition (§1).

The motivation is to serve multiple compute budgets with one model and one gallery, rather than train separate networks for each budget.

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared identity supervision across depths.** Each training step supervises the final block and one uniformly sampled intermediate block through the same embedding head and identity prototypes. Both receive margin-based classification losses; the default weights favor the final exit. No teacher or feature-matching objective is required (§3.2).
- **Efficient coverage of all exits.** Sampling approximates deterministic supervision of every intermediate exit while evaluating only one auxiliary head and classifier per step. The contribution of sampling is training efficiency; the recognition gains primarily come from shared-classifier deep supervision (§4.3).
- **Gallery-fixed evaluation and threshold testing.** The authors test shallow probes against full-depth references and separately examine whether a threshold calibrated at full depth transfers across exits (§4.4).

Deep supervision and shared output heads have precedents acknowledged by the authors; the proposed distinction is their use for gallery-compatible, open-set face embeddings (§§1–2). Inference depth is selected by budget, not by an input-adaptive stopping policy (§3.3).

## Evidence and main results

Unless otherwise specified, results below use ViT-S/12 trained on WebFace4M with AdaFace. “Self” embeds both sides at the same exit; “gallery-fixed” uses full-depth references.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large early-exit gains with a small full-depth loss | Self IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴: SD-ViT versus final-only baseline is **87.01% vs. 11.05%** at exit 3, **95.11% vs. 42.22%** at exit 6, and **96.51% vs. 96.89%** at exit 12. Costs are respectively 4.70, 9.26, and 18.37 GFLOPs. | §4.2; Table 4 |
| Shallow probes can use the full-depth gallery | Gallery-fixed IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴: **93.06% vs. 0.02%** at exit 3 and **95.84% vs. 34.63%** at exit 6, SD-ViT versus baseline. SD-ViT exit-3 TinyFace Rank-1 is **59.31%**. | Table 3; §4.4; Table S17 caption |
| Dedicated models remain stronger at their own budgets | Self IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴: scratch-trained truncated models versus SD-ViT give **93.78% vs. 87.01%** at 4.70 GFLOPs and **95.94% vs. 95.11%** at 9.26 GFLOPs. | §4.2 |
| Sampling approximates all-exit supervision more cheaply | Self IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴ at exits 3/6: sampled **87.01%/95.11%**, deterministic **86.54%/95.15%**. Training takes **68.6 vs. 88.3 A100 GPU-hours**, against a 65.5-hour baseline. | Table 3; §4.3 |
| Auxiliary identity loss outperforms tested feature matching | At exit 3, self IJB-C TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴ is **87.01%** with SD-ViT versus **55.49%** when the auxiliary label loss is replaced by feature-MSE through the shared head. | Table 3 |
| Threshold transfer has a depth-dependent cost | Gallery-fixed IJB-C, held-out pair half, full-depth threshold **τ=0.382** calibrated at target FAR=10⁻⁴: TAR losses versus per-exit calibration are **0.64 points at exit 6** and **3.97 at exit 3**. At exit 1, fixed-threshold TAR is **15.66% vs. 63.81%** with per-exit calibration. | §4.4; Table 6 discussion |

These comparisons support useful cross-depth matching within a jointly trained model and a favorable compute–accuracy tradeoff. They do not establish compatibility with another model’s existing gallery, equal accuracy to dedicated small networks, or reliable fixed-threshold operation at every exit. The evidence also distinguishes ROC-level recognition results from deployment at one unchanged threshold.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Constant token dimensions are required; gallery and probe must come from the same network. Input-adaptive exiting remains future work (§3.3).
- **Calibration coverage:** Threshold transfer was tested only on ViT-S/12. The shallowest exit loses substantial TAR, and quarter-depth operation still incurs a meaningful calibration penalty (§4.4).
- **Selection bias—analyst inference:** The authors explicitly selected the loss weight on reporting benchmarks, reducing independence between tuning and evaluation (§4.1).
- **Unresolved mechanism:** Per-exit-head comparisons also change prototypes and training regime, so they do not isolate head sharing. Separate intermediate prototypes perform nearly as well, qualifying claims that shared prototypes are essential (§4.3; Appendix S13).
- **Run uncertainty:** The Table S23 caption explicitly acknowledges one run per configuration and says small sampled-versus-deterministic differences cannot be resolved.
- **Text inconsistency:** The distillation discussion says external-teacher results are below 22% at quarter depth, but Table 4 reports 40.26% for teacher-to-intermediate Feature-MSE. Individual rows provide the clearer evidence (§4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can per-image exit selection preserve gallery consistency and calibration?** Only fixed-budget exits are evaluated. A next experiment could compare learned stopping policies against fixed exits at matched average compute (§3.3).
2. **Does threshold transfer generalize across architectures and data shifts?** Calibration is limited to ViT-S/12. Repeat development/test calibration across ViT-B and KP-RPE, including shifted probe conditions (§4.4).
3. **How much alignment comes specifically from the shared head?** Existing per-exit-head controls change multiple factors. Train separate-head variants from scratch while holding prototypes, losses, and schedules fixed (§4.3; Appendix S13).
4. **Are small differences between supervision strategies reproducible?** Single runs cannot resolve near ties. Repeat sampled and deterministic supervision across seeds with independent hyperparameter selection (Table S23 caption; §4.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central method and major comparisons, but many tables—including most supplementary results—contain captions without numerical bodies.

Unavailable information includes complete per-exit results, exact measured latency and memory values, full realized-FAR calibration tables, multi-seed uncertainty, demographic subgroup analyses, and independently verified code or checkpoint availability.
