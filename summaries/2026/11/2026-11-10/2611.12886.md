# Reframe: Observation-Aligned Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Visual Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12886) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12886)


## One-sentence takeaway
Reframe combines grounding-aware specialist training with annotation-guided repair of student tool actions, improving reported image and video benchmark averages over vanilla multi-teacher on-policy distillation while increasing measured use of tool-returned evidence (Tables 2–4).

## Problem and motivation
Visual agents can invoke tools without using the returned observations, which the authors call **observation neglect**. Conversely, poorly grounded tool actions can leave subsequent reasoning—and teacher supervision—without relevant evidence. The authors argue that existing distillation methods mainly improve supervision while accepting the student’s observation state as given (§§1–2).

The goal is to consolidate image and video specialists into one model while addressing both evidence acquisition and evidence use, without a task-specific supervised fine-tuning cold start.

## Approach and contributions
- **Ground-Drop-GRPO (GD-GRPO):** Trains separate image and video specialists using answer rewards, grounding rewards, temporary tool-call incentives, and validity penalties. After the first successful tool call, the original visual input is removed with probability 0.5; advantages are normalized separately within preassigned drop/keep groups (§3.2).
- **Observation-Gated-MOPD (OG-MOPD):** Replaces missing, invalid, or insufficiently grounded student actions with annotation-derived actions, then resumes student generation from the repaired observation. Student-generated tokens receive specialist distillation; injected action tokens receive a separate negative log-likelihood loss. Repair and teachers are absent at inference (§3.3).
- **Reframe-100K:** Contains 63K image and 37K video question–answer pairs with grounding-derived tool metadata, evidence auditing, and context-expanded annotations. Both training stages use this corpus (§3.1).

The distinctive methodological contribution is intervention on the observation state during distillation, paired with specialist training intended to make that observation useful. The authors’ claim of being the first such unified framework is not independently established by the supplied text.

These results support improved benchmark performance and sensitivity to useful returned evidence in the tested settings. Modality means combine different benchmark metrics: video scores include accuracy, Non-Lin Score, M-Avg, accuracy/MRA, and scaled mAM, so their differences are **benchmark-score points**, not uniform accuracy gains (Table 2; Appendix C.1).

The observation probe isolates returned-image content under a fixed injected action; it does not alone establish successful autonomous evidence acquisition. Tool-gain results provide complementary evidence, although tool-free evaluation also changes the prompt and removes tool definitions (Appendix C).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged trade-off:** Repair changes the student’s history, reducing how fully on-policy training remains. Selective intervention limits this departure but does not eliminate it (§§3.3, 4.3.2).
- **Annotation dependence:** The authors emphasize that unreliable grounding can undermine both rewards and repair. Their auditing pipeline addresses this concern, but no residual annotation-error estimate is supplied (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The reported GD-GRPO comparison changes grounding rewards, dropout, and subgroup handling together. OG-MOPD adds repair and injected-action NLL together. Separate component effects are not established by the supplied ablations.
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Experiments use one base model, two tools, and frozen visual components. Observation dependence is tested only on the 191-question V∗ image benchmark; corresponding video dependence is not established (Appendices B, C.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and uncertainty:** Shared visual budgets do not establish matched external-model training or compute. External interfaces include adaptations and restrictions. No repeated-seed variability or confidence intervals are reported; “best-performing” checkpoint selection is described without its selection criterion (Appendices B–C).

## Open research questions
- **Which GD-GRPO component drives observation use?** The bundled comparison cannot separate rewards, dropout, and subgroup normalization. A factorial ablation could measure their individual and interactive effects on performance, dependence, and tool gain.
- **Does repair help beyond action imitation?** Repair and injected-action NLL are enabled together. Compare repair-only, NLL-only, and combined training under matched rollout budgets.
- **Does stronger evidence dependence extend to video?** The controlled probe covers image crops only. Repeat it with relevant, irrelevant, and blank selected frames while keeping action history fixed.
- **How robust is annotation-guided training to imperfect evidence?** Annotation quality is central but not quantitatively stress-tested. Introduce controlled spatial/temporal annotation noise and evaluate downstream acquisition and reasoning.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods, tables, and appendices support the reported comparisons, but causal attribution and broader generalization remain limited.

Material missing information includes component-level ablations, seed variability, checkpoint-selection criteria, GPU type and elapsed training cost, detailed source-data composition and contamination checks, residual annotation-error rates, and independently verified release contents. Several prompt texts referenced in Appendix A are also absent from the retrieved rendering.
