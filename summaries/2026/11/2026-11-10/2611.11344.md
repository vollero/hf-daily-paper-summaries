# Spatial Harness: Grounding Spatial Reasoning in Executable World States with Physics Engines

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11344) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11344)


## One-sentence takeaway
Spatial Harness improves spatial question answering by compiling image estimates and textual constraints into executable scene states, reaching 71.14% end-to-end MindCube accuracy with Qwen3.5-27B versus 52.70% for direct reasoning (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Direct VLM reasoning combines scene interpretation and imagined spatial transformations, making perceptual mistakes difficult to distinguish from reasoning errors. The authors argue that explicit scene descriptions alone leave transformations unchecked: an executable representation lets an engine compute consequences consistently with the assumed geometry (§1).

The central gap is therefore independently inspectable perception and spatial computation, while acknowledging that consistent execution cannot correct an inaccurate scene automatically.

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable world-state representation:** A shared object-and-camera layout, per-view observations, and question-derived constraints separate estimated scene facts from their use in reasoning (§3.1).
- **Structured Perceiver:** Specialized detection, depth, orientation, and camera-pose estimates are fused with VLM descriptions and textual constraints. Projection and deterministic layout refinement reconcile observations in a shared frame (§3.2; Appendix C.2).
- **Interactive Reasoner:** MuJoCo instantiates labeled proxies; a VLM issues Python calls for camera movement, measurements, and rendering. Unsupported or incomplete states route to direct image-based answering (§3.3; Appendix C.3).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Matched annotated states isolate reasoning from state estimation; additional ablations examine perception channels and feedback types (§4.3).

The engine supports static geometric computation here, rather than contact dynamics or physical-parameter estimation (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Execution improves reasoning with identical scene information | MindCube, Qwen3.5-27B, same annotated map: Spatial Harness **98.25%**, direct Base **71.81%** accuracy. Overall gains occur across all seven evaluated backbones. | Table 1 |
| Stronger end-to-end MindCube performance | Qwen3.5-27B: **71.14%** versus Base **52.70%** and pySpatial **63.62%**; Spatial Harness has the highest overall accuracy for all four tested backbones. | Table 2 |
| Transfer gains vary by benchmark and backbone | Qwen3.5-27B accuracy: SAT **83.20%** versus Base **81.80%**; MMSI-Bench **39.50%** versus **33.40%**. Qwen2.5-VL-72B declines on MMSI-Bench: **29.00%** versus Base **29.60%** and pySpatial **33.20%**. | Table 3 |
| Large quantitative gains | SpatialRGPT-Bench, Qwen3.5-27B: **83.31%** success versus Base **48.20%**. Success requires metric predictions within **±25%** of reference or direction within **one clock hour**. | Table 3; Appendix C.6 |
| Structured perception matters | MindCube, fixed Qwen3.5-27B Reasoner: full Perceiver **71.14%**, VLM-generated map **41.33%**, without visual channel **52.95%**, without text channel **68.38%** accuracy. | Table 4 |
| Computed feedback contributes beyond self-review | MindCube, Qwen3.5-27B, fixed GT maps: Base **71.81%**, self-review **77.81%**, numerical-only **95.62%**, rendered-only **93.71%**, combined **98.25%** accuracy. | Table 5 |
| State errors remain consequential | Corrupting one camera yaw by **(90°, 180°]** reduces MindCube accuracy from **98.25%** to **76.67%**, averaged over three corruption seeds with Qwen3.5-27B. | Table 10; Appendix D.6 |

These results support separating state construction from execution and using computed feedback on the tested tasks. They do not establish accurate perception, guaranteed answer correctness, or a unique benefit from a physics engine over a simpler geometric executor. The numerical-only ablation shows that much of the matched-state gain is achievable without rendered images.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** States describe a single static moment; temporal scene changes remain unsupported (§5). Appearance and occlusion questions route away from the engine (Appendix C.3).
- **Feedback can be ignored:** A documented failure answers incorrectly despite correct engine observations, so execution consistency does not guarantee faithful interpretation or answer selection (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** SpatialRGPT-Bench supplies predicted metric measurements directly as text as well as through engine geometry. No reported measurement-only comparison isolates the engine’s incremental contribution there (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation breadth:** Detailed construction and feedback ablations use one backbone on MindCube; the other three benchmarks use only two backbones (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and scoring:** Run variation is reported for GT-map experiments, but comparable end-to-end variation is unavailable. LLM answer extraction and map-name alignment introduce evaluation dependencies without a reported human audit (Appendices C.6, D.5, D.7).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency:** Hardware and turn limits are disclosed, but runtime, total compute, and cost-matched comparisons are absent.

## Open research questions
1. **Is MuJoCo necessary for the measured gains?** No alternative executor is tested. Compare identical states, APIs, prompts, and budgets using MuJoCo versus a lightweight geometric implementation.
2. **Can execution feedback improve perception itself?** Render-to-input checking is proposed but not evaluated (§5). Test iterative state correction against the current one-pass Perceiver.
3. **How can models reliably follow computed evidence?** Appendix E shows feedback being overridden. Evaluate explicit reference-frame checks and answer-verification rules on such failures.
4. **Does the representation extend to temporal reasoning?** The authors defer temporal states (§5). Add frame-indexed poses and evaluate motion and event-order questions with separate tracking and reasoning assessments.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark findings and method description; lower in broader generalization and component-specific causal claims.

Material missing information includes end-to-end run variability, measured latency and cost, a non-physics executor comparison, quantitative measurement-only ablations, human validation of judge decisions, and temporal evaluation. Several prompt figures are represented only by captions or descriptions in the supplied text; the stated code release was not independently inspected.
