# How Much Training-Inference Mismatch Can On-Policy Distillation Tolerate?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11450) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11450)


## One-sentence takeaway

In the tested math-distillation setting, OPD tolerates substantial but model-specific rollout mismatch, enabling FP8 plus asynchronous generation to reach the synchronous BF16 baseline’s final held-out teacher KL 1.62× sooner, without establishing a universal safety threshold (§5.4; Appendix I).

## Problem and motivation

On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its own generated responses using token-level teacher feedback. Quantization and asynchronous generation reduce rollout costs but change the sampling distribution relative to the student being trained. Such mismatch can destabilize RL; the authors identify a gap in understanding its severity, mechanisms, and practical limits for OPD (§§1–2).

The practical question is whether configurations can be screened cheaply before committing to full training, rather than treating bit width or weight staleness as reliable indicators of safety.

## Approach and contributions

- **Common measurement and empirical budget.** Measure rollout-to-trainer mismatch as sampled per-token KL, averaged over training. Bracket tolerance between the highest passing and lowest failing tested configurations, using held-out student-to-teacher KL against synchronous BF16 (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Calibrated admission check.** Generate a small response set with a candidate engine, rescore identical tokens using standard BF16, and compare the estimated mismatch—with a safety margin—to a calibrated admission line. Candidate screening needs no teacher scoring, but initial calibration requires training runs (§3.3).
- **Controlled characterization and efficiency evaluation.** Test quantization across three student–teacher pairs; study staleness, fixed-direction Gaussian weight noise, and combined configurations primarily on the 7B pair (§§4–5).
- **Mechanism analysis.** Derive teacher-matching stationarity under fixed-engine sampling, distinguish token-level error from changed prefix visitation, and test RL-style corrections. The stationarity result requires trainer-computed advantages and does not bound convergence or tolerance (§6; Appendices D.2–D.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial mismatch can preserve OPD quality | On the 7B pair, all twelve tested configurations with mean mismatch ≤0.184 nats/token passed; final held-out teacher KL changed by −5.0% to +6.4% against standard BF16, whose mismatch was 0.0008 nats/token. | §5.1; Figure 3; Table 6 |
| The observed budget is a bracket | At approximately 0.39 nats/token, both noise runs had approximately 23% higher final held-out KL and failed. The empirical bracket is [0.184, 0.39] nats/token under the 20% degradation rule with a paired confidence-interval condition. | §§4.3, 5.1; Table 6 |
| Quantization sensitivity depends strongly on model and component | On 7B, keeping first-layer KV cache in BF16 reduced the offline FP8-cache estimate from 0.52 to 0.0037 nats/token. Every-layer Model Optimizer FP8 on Llama-3.2-3B instead produced approximately 0.01 in-training mismatch and +5.6% final held-out KL, passing the rule. | §5.2; Figure 5; Table 7 |
| Admission predictions succeeded on separated cases | All eight decisions fixed before full training matched outcomes; five used offline checks. Six offline checks took 24 minutes on one GPU. No prediction tested the admission boundary closely. | §5.3; Appendix B.4; Table 7 |
| Calibrated FP8 provides capacity; combined execution improves time to quality | First-layer-protected FP8 cache held 1.93× BF16’s KV-cache tokens. On eight GPUs, protected Model Optimizer FP8 with up to eight-version staleness reached the synchronous run’s final held-out KL in 3.69 hours, 1.62× sooner; final accuracy was 60.46% versus 61.54%. | §5.4; Appendix E; Tables 9, 11 |
| Tested uncorrected RL was more fragile | Across three engines tolerated by OPD, REINFORCE broke in all three and GRPO in two, assessed against each method’s own BF16 baseline over 200 steps. Neither standard RL baseline passed the authors’ learning test. | §5.5; Figure 8; Appendix B.2 |
| Prefix changes contribute beyond token-level error | At the untrained 7B student, increasing noise from the passing to failing level increased prefix-shift error 5.3× and token-level error 2.1× under the main decomposition. | §6; Appendix D.3 |

These results support configuration screening and efficiency gains within this experimental setup. They do not establish that every engine below a scalar mismatch threshold is safe. “Passing” means avoiding the predefined material KL degradation; it does not mean identical quality. The budget’s failing noise runs also did not fail the secondary accuracy rule (Appendix B.1; Table 6).

The mechanism evidence establishes that matching the teacher remains stationary under the stated fixed-prefix assumptions. It does not establish convergence under changing rollout distributions or explain every observed failure (Appendix D.2; Appendix I).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors: limited replication and scope.** Most configurations have one run; confidence intervals resample problems rather than training runs. Full budget characterization centers on one pair, math tasks, reverse-KL OPD, and runs lasting at most 200 steps (Appendix I).
- **Authors: admission is incompletely validated.** No run occupies the main budget bracket, and no prediction tests its edge. In-training mismatch exceeded the pilot safety margin for admitted engines; a noise run below the pilot admission line failed later (§5.3).
- **Authors: qualified RL comparison.** Standard GRPO and REINFORCE did not pass the learning test, which was dropped before mismatch experiments. The comparison establishes relative failure sensitivity under these recipes, rather than robustness during demonstrated successful RL learning (Appendices B.2, B.7).
- **Authors: hardware and timing constraints.** NVFP4 is emulated for quality only. Speed measurements have sparse replication; some runs used a newer driver without a control. Time-to-quality results depend on checkpoint resolution and draft-head settings (Appendices E, I).
- **Authors: incomplete mechanism coverage.** Prefix-shift sizes are reported only for weight noise; fixed prefixes may exaggerate error reduction during training. Why corrections fail to help OPD remains unresolved (Appendices D.3, I).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported held-out KL includes problems flagged as overlapping training, so it is not a fully uncontaminated generalization measure; the text does not quantify the overlap’s effect (Appendix G).
- **Reproducibility:** Code is reported available, but plans, logs, analyzer outputs, and figure-generation scripts are deferred until after review; per-run tables were copied manually (Appendix G).

## Open research questions

1. **How reliable is admission near the boundary?** Tested candidates were well separated. Next experiment: prospectively screen densely spaced intermediate configurations using conservative calibration and multiple training seeds (§5.3).
2. **How do budgets change with longer training and more distant teachers?** One noise configuration changed from passing to failing between evaluation stages, and teachers are close to their students. Next experiment: extend training and vary teacher distance while holding the student and rollout recipe fixed (Appendix I).
3. **Does prefix shift explain practical quantization failures?** Reported decomposition sizes cover weight noise only. Next experiment: repeat the decomposition across protected and every-layer FP8 recipes, measuring its ability to predict later KL degradation (Appendix D.3).
4. **Where is OPD’s actual staleness limit?** Tested stale-weight runs remained far below the observed failure range. Next experiment: increase staleness until comparable mismatch levels arise, then compare outcomes with noise and quantization at matched measured KL (§5.1; Appendix I).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed protocols and numerical results, but generalization and prediction reliability remain provisional.

Material unavailable information includes complete contents of several caption-only tables, the deferred raw logs and preregistration records, full pilot results, replicated near-boundary outcomes, longer-horizon budgets, and quantified effects of held-out overlap. Some main-text table references also disagree with the appendix’s displayed numbering.
