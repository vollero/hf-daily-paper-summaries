# InstantHMR : Real-Time Human Mesh Recovery on Your Phone

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09195) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09195)


## One-sentence takeaway
InstantHMR achieves the best reported accuracy among the evaluated models meeting the phone real-time threshold, producing a posed mesh in 5.49 ms on a Galaxy S23 NPU with 48.8 mm EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE, excluding detection and cropping (Tables 1–2; §4).

## Problem and motivation
Single-image human mesh recovery must balance reconstruction accuracy with latency on resource-constrained phones. The authors identify a gap between accurate GPU-oriented models that run slowly or fail on phone NPUs and faster mobile-compatible methods with substantially higher reconstruction errors. They also argue that inconsistent boxes, camera settings and body-model conversions obscure comparisons (§§1–2).

Low latency could support rehabilitation, AR/VR, robotics and exergames, but these applications are motivations rather than validated deployments.

## Approach and contributions
- **Phone-oriented model family:** Four model sizes combine an HGNetV2 convolutional backbone with a small transformer decoder, camera conditioning and heads for MHR parameters, camera translation and auxiliary 2D keypoints. Forward kinematics and skinning generate a mesh within the exported graph (§3; Figure 2).
- **Training with released annotations:** Models use 4.2 million person crops with SAM 3D Body’s released human-annotated MHR fits. Geometry, parameter and image-space losses supervise training; crop-framing augmentation provides the largest reported ablation gain (§3.2; Table 4).
- **Comparison and deployment evidence:** The authors re-evaluate eleven competitors with a largely matched protocol and measure latency across eight targets, reporting reproduction discrepancies explicitly (§4; Tables 1–2).

The contribution is the mobile-compatible integration and evaluation of these components; HGNetV2, MHR, CLIFF conditioning and SimCC are attributed to prior work.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Fast mesh generation on measured hardware | InstantHMR-M: **5.49 ms** on Galaxy S23 NPU fp16, **4.01 ms** on S24 NPU fp16 and **0.98 ms** on RTX 4070 TensorRT fp16; batch one, crop to posed 6,890-vertex mesh, excluding detection/cropping. | Table 1; §4 |
| Competitive accuracy with a mobile tradeoff | PA-MPJPE: **48.8 mm** EMDB-1 J24, **40.0 mm** 3DPW J14 and **41.7 mm** RICH J24, under the stated benchmark protocol and official MHR-to-SMPL conversion. | Table 2; §4 |
| More accurate phone-compatible competitors are slower | NLF-S/L achieve **45.0/39.3 mm** EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE at **67.97/125 ms** on S23 NPU, versus InstantHMR-M’s **48.8 mm at 5.49 ms**. SMPLer-X-S32 achieves **71.0 mm at 11.12 ms**. | Tables 1–2 |
| Larger backbone improves accuracy with little measured NPU cost | Nano→medium: EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE **61.7→48.8 mm**, S23 NPU latency **4.60→5.49 ms**; S23 CPU fp32 latency increases **31.5→93.0 ms**. | Table 3 |
| Crop augmentation materially improves accuracy | Standard crops alone versus 75% standard plus alternative framings: EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE **58.8→48.8 mm**, 3DPW **42.7→40.0 mm**, medium backbone at 288 px. | Table 4 |
| Commercial variant loses substantial accuracy | Same medium architecture and latency, but restricted training corpus: M-C achieves **72.2 mm** EMDB-1 and **50.7 mm** 3DPW PA-MPJPE, versus **48.8/40.0 mm** for M. | Table 3; §5 |
| Evaluation choices affect ranking-scale differences | Changing boxes, focal length and conversion gives InstantHMR-M **46.9–50.1 mm** EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE; the cited learned conversion gives **65.9 mm**. Underlying breakdown is deferred to supplementary material. | §4 |

These results support a strong accuracy–latency tradeoff on the tested Qualcomm NPUs. They do not establish end-to-end camera-pipeline latency, sustained operating performance or downstream application quality. Comparisons are substantially harmonized, but NLF retains its own detector, training-data exposure differs, and some competitors do not reproduce their published accuracy (§§4–5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors:** Hands receive only whole-person image features; there is no dedicated hand branch. Hand-specific accuracy is not reported (§6).
- **Authors:** Phone-NPU tests cover Galaxy S23/S24 Qualcomm hardware; Apple Neural Engine performance is untested (§6).
- **Authors:** No temporal jitter or acceleration evaluation is provided, including for the released demo’s filter (§6).
- **Authors:** Main weights have research-use restrictions; the commercial variant is substantially less accurate (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Calibrated-camera benchmark results do not establish accuracy with the deployment focal-length default (§§3.1, 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported medians from three phone jobs do not establish sustained thermal behavior or energy consumption (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 4 isolates crop framing and input size, but the supplied text does not isolate the decoder, auxiliary keypoint head or individual loss contributions. Commercial-variant results also confound training-set size and composition.

## Open research questions
1. **Can a lightweight hand branch improve hand recovery within the mobile latency budget?** The current architecture lacks dedicated hand processing; compare hand-specific errors and complete-graph NPU latency with and without such a branch (§6).
2. **How stable are predictions during live movement?** Temporal metrics are absent; evaluate jitter, acceleration error and motion lag on sequences, with and without the demo filter (§6).
3. **Does performance transfer to other phone hardware and sustained use?** Qualcomm measurements leave portability and prolonged operation open; benchmark complete pipelines on Apple hardware and during extended sessions (§§4, 6).
4. **Can permissively licensed data close the commercial model’s accuracy gap?** The smaller corpus changes both data quantity and composition; run controlled data-scaling experiments with permissively licensed real or synthetic images (Table 3; §6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this summary of the supplied results; broader deployment conclusions remain limited.

Material missing information includes the referenced supplementary loss weights and protocol breakdowns, training variability and uncertainty estimates, hand-specific and temporal metrics, sustained power/thermal measurements, end-to-end latency, and verification of the claimed released artifacts.
