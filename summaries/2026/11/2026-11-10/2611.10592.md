# MotionGraphicsBench: Evaluating Motion Graphics Generation with Programmatic Verification

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10592) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10592)


## One-sentence takeaway
MotionGraphicsBench evaluates instruction-following in SVG animation using prompt-specific deterministic programs that achieve 93.3% agreement with held-out human judgments, while tested models reach at most 67.7% pass@1 (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Motion requests combine spatial, appearance, and temporal constraints, and an animation can satisfy some while missing others. The authors argue that reference-animation similarity penalizes valid alternatives, while VLM judges offer less deterministic and inspectable decisions. Their editing setup supplies all graphic elements in a static SVG, isolating motion generation from graphic creation (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Curated benchmark:** 100 static-SVG/prompt instances derived from distinct Lottie animations, with human-reviewed prompts spanning 17 overlapping requirement types. Every prompt combines spatial and temporal requirements (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Prompt-specific verification:** Python programs inspect geometry, appearance, and temporal relationships in rendered SVG-frame measurements. Success requires every check to pass without execution errors; no reference-trajectory match is required (§4.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Verifier construction pipeline:** A GPT-6 Astra writer and test-case synthesizer iteratively construct programs using source animations, human-labeled construction outputs, and transformed positive/negative examples. Programs are frozen before held-out validation (§4.2).
- **Validation and model study:** The paper compares verifier decisions with human judgments and a VLM baseline, then evaluates model success, repeated sampling, and check-derived failure patterns (§§5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Verifiers outperform the tested VLM judge | On 180 held-out animations covering all 100 instances, balanced between 90 approvals and 90 rejections: agreement **93.3% vs. 83.3%**, approval recall **90.0% vs. 82.2%**, rejection recall **96.7% vs. 84.4%**. Baseline: GPT-6 Astra, maximum effort, up to 64 timestamped frames. | §5; Table 2 caption; Appendix C.2 |
| Single-attempt performance remains limited | Across 47 configurations from 32 models and 12 providers, pass@1 ranges **6.0–67.7%**, averaging **30.3%** with equal configuration weights. All use the same 100-instance GSAP task. | Abstract; §6.1; Figure 3 |
| Independent sampling substantially improves success | For 15 highest-available-effort configurations with 32 attempts per instance, mean pass@1 rises from **36.7%** to **73.7% pass@32**; pass@32 ranges **38.0–95.0%**. | Abstract; §6.1; Figure 4 |
| Many unsuccessful outputs partially satisfy prompts | Among **29,632** failed animations with complete, error-free reports in the 15-model cohort, **93.6%** pass at least one check and **31.6%** fail exactly one. | §6.1; Appendix D.2 |
| Pose, trajectory, and coordination failures recur | Reference-pose mismatch: **41.5%** failure, **94.3%** coverage; trajectory mismatch: **38.8%**, **88.6%**; event coordination mismatch: **32.4%**, **93.0%**. Rates average equally across configurations and relevant instances; unknown cases remain in denominators. | §6.2; Figure 5; Appendix D.3 |

The evidence supports strong agreement with human decisions on this held-out output set and substantial remaining difficulty under the specified generation protocol. It does not establish universal verifier correctness, superiority over all judging methods, or performance across animation mechanisms. Pass@32 measures success among independent candidates, rather than an evaluated process for selecting or repairing an animation. Failing one check also need not mean missing exactly one requirement (Appendix D.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Detailed prompts omit underspecified, conversational design workflows; experiments use GSAP; the benchmark excludes complex deformation, large scenes, and highly interactive behavior (§7).
- **Observed verifier errors:** Held-out disagreements include nine false rejections and three false approvals. False approvals miss relative sub-element motion; most false rejections enforce geometry or timing too strictly (§5; Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Held-out animations cover existing benchmark instances, rather than unseen prompts or scenes. Validation therefore tests new outputs on these tasks, not automatic verifier construction for new tasks (§5).
- **Analyst inference—construction evidence:** Synthetic labels are agent claims, and no construction-component ablation is reported in the supplied text. Their independent contribution remains uncertain (§4.2).
- **Annotation uncertainty:** Initial inter-annotator agreement cannot be reconstructed because initial decisions were overwritten. Consensus labels consequently lack a reported initial agreement measure (Appendix C.1).
- **Comparison limits:** Effort labels are provider-specific, and the paper cautions against interpreting release comparisons as controlled training effects (Appendices D.1–D.2).

## Open research questions

- **Does synthesized testing improve verifier validity?** Its contribution is not isolated. Rebuild verifiers with and without synthesized cases and compare errors on fresh human-labeled outputs (§4.2).
- **Do scores transfer across animation libraries?** Only GSAP generation is tested. Repeat matched tasks using CSS or SMIL while retaining the SVG-frame verifier (§7).
- **Can check-level feedback improve complete success?** Generation receives no verification-based revision. Compare independent sampling with iterative repair under matched generation budgets (Appendix D.1; §7).
- **Can verification support ambiguous, multi-turn requests?** Detailed single-turn prompts leave this untested. Collect short requests and clarification dialogues, then assess agreement and task success (§7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported task, aggregate results, and stated scope; **medium** in broader validity.

The supplied retrieval omits table bodies and most plotted values, preventing reconstruction of full model rankings, coverage counts, sampling settings, and disagreement details.
