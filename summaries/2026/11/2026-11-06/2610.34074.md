# Identically Trained Language Models Break on Different Jailbreaks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34074) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34074)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the tested small language models with fixed post-training, jailbreak vulnerability patterns track the particular pretraining run much more strongly than the data recipe, limiting what one checkpoint’s evaluation establishes (§3.1–3.3).

## Problem and motivation
Jailbreak evaluations commonly test one checkpoint, but the authors ask whether another full pretraining run using the same data and safety training would fail on the same prompts. They identify a gap between studying randomness after pretraining and comparing independent, complete pretraining runs (§1).

This matters for interpreting attack rankings, comparing pretraining corpora, and deciding whether red-teaming findings transfer to another checkpoint.

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled comparison of training runs:** The main grid contains 150 models: 25 DataDecide data recipes × three 1B pretraining runs × two post-training seeds. All receive the same SFT and DPO pipeline. Additional experiments cover smaller models, same-seed reruns, and replacement SFT safety data (§2–3).
- **Prompt-level measurement:** Each model produces 20 responses per prompt. Vulnerability is non-refusal among coherent samples; profiles are compared using Pearson correlation corrected for sampling noise. The “ancestry gap” compares models sharing a base checkpoint with models sharing only a data recipe (§2).
- **Empirical findings and assets:** The authors demonstrate run-specific vulnerability patterns, unstable attack rankings, and behavioral identification of pretraining runs. They report releasing code, post-trained weights, and per-prompt judge labels; generated responses are excluded (§3.3; Impact statement).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

The ancestry gap is **0.173**, 95% CI **[0.167, 0.179]** over recipes; its prompt-bootstrap interval excludes zero in every recipe. | §3.1; Table 1; Fig. 2 |
| The gap appears at smaller sizes. | On DCLM-Baseline, ancestry gaps are **0.204 [0.189, 0.218]** at 150M and **0.211 [0.198, 0.224]** at 300M, versus **0.171 [0.160, 0.183]** at 1B; intervals resample prompts. | Table 1; Table G.8 |

Template scores average their own, differing prompt sets. | §3.3; Appendix H.1 |
| Vulnerability profiles identify pretraining runs. | Matching post-training-seed-2 queries against seed-1 references identifies **75/75** runs using 3,211 prompts. With 50 random prompts, mean accuracy is **98.1%** across 200 subsets. | §3.3; Fig. 4b; Appendix H.1 |
| One checkpoint can reverse data-recipe comparisons. | Single-model comparisons disagree with six-model recipe-mean ordering in **23.4% [17.6%, 29.3%]** of comparisons; the interval resamples recipes. | §3.3; Appendix H.1 |
| Changing safety data materially limits the main interpretation. | On the main recipe, replacement SFT safety data preserves an ancestry gap of **0.178 [0.165, 0.194]**. However, same-run correlation **across** safety datasets is only **0.558 [0.537, 0.577]**, below same-recipe correlation under fixed safety data. | Appendix G, pp. 59–60; Table G.14 |

The evidence supports a substantial run-specific component of **which prompts elicit non-refusal under fixed post-training**. It does not establish that pretraining runs dominate changes in safety training: the cross-safety-data experiment explicitly narrows that claim. Nor is non-refusal equivalent to harmful compliance—the paper’s human rubric includes both harmful and non-harmful compliance (Appendix D.1).

Data recipes can still matter for aggregate vulnerability, even when they contribute comparatively little to prompt-by-prompt similarity (§3.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Models come from one pretraining family, with the largest at 1B parameters, and principally use one SFT–DPO pipeline. Smaller-size and replacement-safety-data checks have narrower coverage (§5).
- **Judge error:** WildGuard overcalls non-refusal. The authors report mean vulnerability falling from **0.528** to an audit-adjusted **0.278 [0.230, 0.324]**. Human labels support the correlation gap, but cover only four models from one recipe (§5).
- **Rerun uncertainty:** Same-seed results use few base runs; their confidence intervals reflect prompt variation rather than uncertainty across new training runs. Partial-rerun comparisons also cross hardware/software stacks (§3.2).
- **Training comparability:** Smaller-model checkpoints may have differing learning-rate histories; released optimizer states were unavailable to check this (Appendix C.1).
- **Registration departures:** The authors disclose failed reproduction tolerances, an overridden post-training diagnostic stop, an omitted intermediate-checkpoint comparison, and exploratory analyses. These qualify claims of a wholly preregistered workflow (Appendices B, C.4, H.2–H.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Best-template retention reflects both template behavior and the difficulty of each template’s own prompt set, limiting interpretation as a pure template-effect comparison (§3.3).
- **Incomplete source:** A substantial middle portion was truncated. This analysis cannot assess all appendix methods or supporting evidence.

## Open research questions
- **Does the ancestry gap persist beyond this family and scale?** Coverage stops at 1B within DataDecide. A next experiment could repeat the nested run/post-training design in larger models and another architecture.
- **Which training operations generate the divergence?** Same-seed weights diverge from the first step, but the probe does not isolate a kernel. Compare deterministic and standard training while changing one operation at a time (Appendix B.2).
- **How robust are run fingerprints across broader post-training changes?** Replacement SFT safety data reduces similarity substantially. Test identification across independently varied SFT, DPO, and later training stages with held-out runs (Table G.14).
- **How much of the effect survives direct harmful-compliance assessment?** The main metric measures non-refusal and the judge has substantial error. Expand blinded human annotation across recipes and compare refusal-based with harmful-compliance profiles (Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied main results are explicit and supported by several checks, but coverage is incomplete.

Material unavailable information includes much of the detailed judge-audit methodology, mechanistic experiments, partial-rerun checks, and robustness analyses omitted by truncation. Release contents and reproducibility were not independently verified.
