# Open-HumanScale: An Open Recipe for Scaling Human and Robot Experience for Embodied Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06817) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06817)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that curated, action-aligned human-video pretraining improves robot generalization and adaptation efficiency, while adding human experience to robot pretraining improves visual-goal-conditioned zero-shot manipulation.

## Problem and motivation
Robot manipulation needs diverse experience, but collecting teleoperated robot demonstrations is costly. Human egocentric video offers greater scale, yet heterogeneous motion annotations, embodiment differences, and inconsistent training recipes complicate transfer. The authors identify a gap in reproducible evidence connecting human-data scale and diversity to **closed-loop robot success**, beyond prediction losses (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Curated corpus and action interface.** Open-HumanScale-50K combines five egocentric datasets, filtering poor visibility, unstable motion, and inactivity. Camera frames, wrist axes, and hand openness are aligned into a shared bimanual end-effector representation (§2; Table 1).
- **Controlled scaling studies.** Human-only pretraining is followed by standardized RoboTwin adaptation. Experiments examine duration, task/scene diversity, demonstration efficiency, action alignment, motion supervision, and camera-view transfer (§3).
- **HumanWAM-Vanilla.** A video–action model uses future-video-conditioned action prediction, absolute camera-frame actions, and human multi-view mid-training before robot post-training (§4.1).
- **HumanWAM-Goal.** Hybrid human–robot training adds visual goals, a masked multi-embodiment action interface, and joint video–action sampling. This model uses a different backbone from Vanilla, so their performance difference does not isolate hybrid data alone (§§5.2–5.4).
- **Claimed open release.** The authors state that they release the corpus, pipelines, checkpoints, inference code, and simulation evaluation resources; availability is not independently verified here (§1).

## Evidence and main results

All numbers below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large curated human corpus | Filtering reduces 104,289.41 source hours to 50,000 hours across five datasets; the retained corpus yields approximately 35.9 million continuous clips. | §2.1; Table 1 |
| Human-only scaling improves generalization | With isolated attention, scaling from 100 to 10,000 hours raises RoboTwin success from 71.2%→80.0% clean, 25.2%→58.1% scene-ood, and 8.4%→28.7% random after common robot post-training. No-Ego scores are 69.5%, 16.2%, and 2.1%, respectively. | §3.2; Fig. 5 |
| Scaling trend persists under another configuration | With IDM attention and absolute actions, 100→50,000 hours raises scene-ood success from 49.1%→73.2% and random from 21.5%→42.9%; clean stays within 87.4–89.0%. OOD log-linear fits have R²=0.969 and 0.955. | §3.2; Fig. 6 |
| Diversity and motion supervision matter | Expanding task/scene coverage produces steeper fitted scaling curves than fixed coverage. On the same 1,000-hour video corpus at 20,000 steps, approximately 50% motion supervision versus video-only training raises scene-ood success from 13.0%→34.8% and random from 3.6%→16.3%. | §3.3, Fig. 7; §3.6, Fig. 11 |
| Larger pretraining reduces adaptation needs | A 5,000-hour model with 25 demonstrations/task exceeds a 500-hour model with 50: scene-ood 42.0% versus 37.9%; random 21.8% versus 16.2%. | §3.4; Fig. 8 |
| Vanilla is competitive after robot adaptation | RoboTwin: 92.08% Clean2Clean, 68.88% Clean2Random, 80.48% average, using 50 clean demonstrations/task and 100 evaluation episodes/task/condition. GO-3 Nano Preview reports a higher 85.01% average in the comparison table. | §4.2; Table 7 |
| RoboDojo performance remains uneven | After adaptation on 3,500 demonstrations, Vanilla obtains 14.17% success / 19.00 progress, averaged equally over six groups. Precision and Long-Horizon success reach 20.50% and 33.00%, but Memory and Open reach only 6.00% and 1.50%. | §4.2; Table 8 |
| Physical visual robustness improves | Across four Cobot Magic tasks, with 50 adaptation demonstrations/task and 10 trials/task/model/setting, mean success rises from 57.5%→87.5% in distribution and 2.5%→67.5% under tablecloth/lighting shifts versus No-Ego. | §4.3; Table 9 |
| Human data improves hybrid zero-shot transfer | Holding robot data at 14,618 hours, adding 45,938 human hours raises RoboTwin zero-shot mean success from 11.48%→13.94% and single-checkpoint coverage from 18→22 of 50 tasks. Evaluation uses 50 episodes/task/condition; coverage requires ≥2% mean success. | §5.6.1; Table 13; Fig. 17 |

Detailed result sections are omitted from the supplied source. | Abstract; §1 |

The strongest visible evidence supports better generalization and adaptation within the evaluated manipulation settings. The scaling fits are empirical relationships over tested budgets, not guarantees beyond them. Zero-shot transfer is demonstrated on an embodiment excluded from pretraining (§5.5), but aggregate success remains modest. The claimed state-of-the-art hybrid result and embodied in-context learning have less inspectable support because their detailed sections are truncated.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Human-only pretraining still requires robot adaptation; the initial shared action space excludes locomotion (§§2.3, 5).
- **Analyst inference—scaling confounds:** Global batch size changes with corpus size, and checkpoints are selected separately for each evaluation setting. The curves therefore do not isolate data hours under fixed compute or represent one universally selected policy (§3.1; Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—diversity interpretation:** Both diversity series converge to the same parent corpus. Steeper slopes alone do not establish that growing-diversity subsets outperform broader fixed-coverage subsets at matched hours (§3.3; Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Vanilla baselines come from leaderboard or published results rather than uniformly rerun experiments. Zero-shot deployment errors are missing evaluations, not measured failures (§4.2; Table 13).
- **Analyst inference—goal-interface dependence:** Goal generation uses manually authored task references, public task descriptions, external models, and repeated review. “Zero-shot” excludes target-task demonstrations and parameter updates, while retaining this task-specific specification support (§5.4; Appendix 9).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** The physical Vanilla study has few trials, and the supplied results do not report confidence intervals or repeated-training variability (§4.3). Truncation prevents assessing the full limitations discussion.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the scaling relationship survive fixed-compute, independently selected evaluation?** Batch sizes and checkpoint selection vary here. A next experiment could match compute across scales, select checkpoints on separate validation scenes, and report repeated-seed uncertainty.
2. **Which diversity dimensions drive transfer?** Task and scene coverage change together (§3.3). A factorial experiment could vary task diversity and scene diversity independently at fixed duration.
3. **How much does goal construction contribute to zero-shot success?** The visible results do not isolate the manually guided synthesis pipeline. Compare language-only execution, directly captured goals, and generated goals with varying annotation support under the same checkpoint.
4. **How broadly does the shared action interface transfer?** Visible evaluation centers on bimanual manipulation. Test additional held-out embodiments and camera geometries using unchanged normalization, while separately measuring control-mapping failures and task failures.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods and tables support the main human-pretraining findings, but the source is substantially truncated.

Missing material includes detailed hybrid post-training results and augmentation settings, most physical hybrid/in-context-learning experiments, full failure analyses, repeated-run uncertainty, hardware and compute costs, exact curation thresholds, and independently verified release contents. This analysis does not provide complete paper coverage.
