# TailSteer: Trajectory-Tail Steering for Drift-Resistant Multi-turn Image Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06741) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06741)


## One-sentence takeaway
TailSteer reduces cumulative image-editing drift by training preservation corrections near the denoising trajectory’s end while matching earlier base-model dynamics, although editing success remains dependent on the backbone and steering onset (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Repeatedly feeding edited images back into an editor accumulates unintended changes to textures, identities, colors, and backgrounds. Strong single-turn editing therefore does not ensure reliable iterative editing.

The authors identify a gap between improving preservation through external constraints, representations, or memory and improving the editor itself. They propose refining denoising dynamics without curated target editing pairs, cross-turn training sequences, or additional inference components (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Preservation-aware targets:** Generate identity and local edits with a frozen editor, filter unreliable trajectories, and construct targets that restore unchanged source regions while retaining the model’s chosen edit elsewhere. Local preservation masks come from source–output differences (§3.1; Fig. 3).
- **Tail-focused, on-policy training:** Match the frozen model’s velocity during early denoising, then gradually blend toward endpoint-directed preservation supervision. Training states come from the refined model’s rollouts starting at cached anchors; gradients apply only at the sampled prediction. The resulting model performs the entire inference trajectory alone (§3.3; Fig. 5).
- **Controlled drift evaluation:** Introduce **Add & Preserve**, separating outside-region preservation from cumulative object-edit completion, alongside evaluations across four backbones and existing generation/editing benchmarks (§4; Table 2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong preservation gains on Klein | Add & Preserve, turn 10, onset 46: base → TailSteer PSNR **13.85 → 19.96**, SSIM **0.289 → 0.621**, LPIPS **0.524 → 0.290**; COA **31.02% → 31.84%**. | Table 2 |
| Gains also occur with high edit completion | SenseNova, onset 42: PSNR@10 **11.23 → 18.22**, SSIM@10 **0.166 → 0.489**, LPIPS@10 **0.607 → 0.417**; COA **94.93% → 94.78%**. | Table 2 |
| Preservation superiority does not imply overall editing superiority | Klein onset 46 exceeds GPT-Image-2.5 Sunburst API on all three outside-box preservation metrics, but COA is **31.84% versus 98.29%**. | Table 2 |
| General multi-turn results are mixed | Klein onset 46: MSE-Bench SR@5 **40% → 33%**, EdiVal IF@3 **32.69% → 30.24%**, CC@3 **85.24% → 87.57%**. SenseNova onset 42: SR@5 **50% → 55%**. | Table 2 |
| Single-turn capability retention has exceptions | Klein onset 46: GEdit-EN **7.47 → 7.52**, ImgEdit **4.20 → 4.19**, RISE accuracy **14.17% → 13.61%**. Qwen onset 30: RISE **20.56% → 18.06%**. | Table 1 |
| On-policy training helps some capabilities more than preservation | Klein onset 46, full versus without on-policy training: RISE **13.61% versus 11.39%**, PSNR@10 **19.96 versus 19.81**, COA **31.84% versus 31.94%**. | Table 3 |
| Longer-horizon evidence is mainly qualitative | Selected examples show improved preservation through 15–20 turns; controlled Add & Preserve measurements cover 10 turns. | Figs. 1, 4, 6; §4.3 |

The evidence supports substantial preservation improvements across the tested backbones, with generally smaller changes to single-turn scores. It does **not** establish universal gains in instruction following, overall multi-turn success, or superiority to the proprietary comparator. Ablations support the combined training design, but do not show every component improving every metric.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed trade-off:** Earlier steering can improve preservation while reducing editing success; onset selection materially affects results (Table 2).
- **Author-observed residual drift:** Repeated VAE reconstruction still causes color shifts. Latent feedback provides complementary improvements in a Klein experiment (§4.3; Figs. 8–9).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Add & Preserve isolates drift using boxed object additions/removals on natural images. It cannot alone establish preservation under unrestricted global transformations or identity changes; those settings receive qualitative demonstrations (§4.3; Figs. 1, 7).
- **Analyst inference—supervision dependence:** Targets depend on filtered model outputs and inferred preservation masks. Incorrectly accepted edits could supply incorrect preservation supervision; filtering thresholds and mask-construction details are unavailable (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and scaling:** Tables provide no confidence intervals or repeated-run variability. Data-scale experiments train for approximately one epoch, so larger datasets also entail more training exposure, limiting attribution to data diversity alone (§4.4; Table 4).

## Open research questions
1. **Can onset selection preserve editing success more consistently?** Table 2 shows backbone-dependent trade-offs. Test an onset-selection rule on held-out tasks, then evaluate preservation and instruction following jointly.
2. **Do quantitative gains persist beyond the controlled horizon?** Longer sequences are demonstrated qualitatively. Extend unrestricted and boxed-edit evaluations to longer sequences, tracking both retained edits and source preservation.
3. **How sensitive is training to preservation-mask errors?** Supervision relies on inferred masks without a reported quality ablation. Compare automated masks with audited masks and controlled mask corruption.
4. **How much benefit comes from data diversity versus additional optimization?** Approximately one-epoch scaling changes both. Compare dataset sizes and trajectory mixtures under matched update counts and compute budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the framework and reports consistent preservation gains, but leaves material reproducibility and uncertainty details unavailable:

- Optimizer, learning rate, exact velocity-blending schedule, and filtering thresholds.
- Detailed mask construction, benchmark instruction generation, and COA scoring.
- Per-backbone accepted trajectory counts, training time, and repeated-run uncertainty.
- Confirmed code, weights, and benchmark release availability.
- Quantitative unrestricted editing results over the longest demonstrated horizons.
