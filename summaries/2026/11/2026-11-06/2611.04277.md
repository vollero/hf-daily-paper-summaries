# Harness-S: Learning from Sibling Harnesses for Generalizable Agent Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04277) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04277)


## One-sentence takeaway
Harness-S improves agent performance under the original execution harness by training on fresh, selectively routed rescue attempts from complementary sibling harnesses, with gains reported across three benchmarks and two model sizes (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Agent performance depends on both model weights and the surrounding harness: prompts, tools, memory, context handling, and execution rules. The authors identify three properties of harness changes: repairs are local, different variants complement one another, and their usefulness changes as the policy learns (Section 1; Figure 1).

These properties complicate building one universally better harness or repeatedly co-evolving harnesses and weights. The paper asks whether specialized execution support can instead produce training experience whose benefits persist after returning to the original harness, \(H_0\).

## Approach and contributions
- **Directional harness evolution:** Diagnose related failure causes, branch into specialized repairs, and select future parents by joint validation coverage. Retain every executable, protocol-compliant child in a pool that stays fixed during policy training (Section 3.2).
- **Selective rescue training:** Generate eight initial attempts under \(H_0\). Only when all fail, generate eight fresh attempts using distinct auxiliary harnesses and replace the failed group for shared-policy GRPO training. The same current policy generates both groups; auxiliary instructions and tool observations are excluded from the token loss (Section 3.3; Appendix B–C).
- **Adaptive routing:** Match tasks to harness behavior profiles using multiple LLM rankings. Periodically rebuild profiles from paired current-policy validation executions to track changing harness utility (Section 3.4).
- **Transfer evaluation:** Evaluate the trained policy under \(H_0\), task-specific Rank-1 routing, and external harnesses to distinguish retained policy gains from additional execution support (Sections 4.2–4.7).

The results establish reported performance improvements after auxiliary support is removed, alongside stronger reward contrasts and changed workflows. These findings support the authors’ capability-transfer interpretation. They do not isolate which individual rescue experiences caused which improvements; behavioral frequencies and two held-out cases provide supporting evidence rather than definitive causal attribution.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Lasting transfer, efficient pool reuse across policies, state exchange between independently developed harnesses, and scaling repair diversity remain future work (Section 5).
- **Analyst inference—bounded generalization:** Main evaluation covers two sizes of one model family and three benchmarks. External-harness evaluation covers only the 4B policy on AppWorld and SWE-Gym (Sections 4.1, 4.7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Rescue controls match the trigger and per-task rescue allocation, but total sampling varies with rescue frequency. Other adaptation baselines also use different objectives and search organizations, limiting attribution to a single component (Section 4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—transfer evidence:** Behavior labels are LLM-generated, and the supplied text gives no labeling reliability assessment. Two transfer cases illustrate retained behavior but do not quantify its prevalence (Section 4.5).
- **Evidence availability:** Several tables appear only as captions, preventing inspection of complete split counts, rescue ablations, search settings, and resource accounting. Baseline variability and formal significance tests are not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer persist through later training?** Section 5 explicitly leaves lasting transfer open. A next experiment could continue training without rescue and track \(H_0\) success and acquired behaviors over time.
- **Can a pool transfer efficiently between policies?** Pool reuse across policies remains future work. Freeze a pool evolved with one policy, recalibrate it for another, and compare against evolving a new pool at matched total cost.
- **How much repair diversity is useful?** The paper demonstrates complementarity but leaves harness scaling open. Vary pool size and repair diversity separately while measuring \(H_0\) gains, rescue coverage, and calibration cost.
- **Can heterogeneous harnesses support the same rescue mechanism?** Current siblings share execution foundations and interfaces. Test adapters between independently developed harnesses while holding tasks, verifiers, and rescue budgets fixed.
