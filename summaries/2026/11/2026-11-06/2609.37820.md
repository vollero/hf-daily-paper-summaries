# Information Games: Strategic Crowding and Firm Repositioning in Language-Model Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37820) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37820)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors derive a restricted mechanism linking competitive capacity adjustment to news-distribution movements and find a small positive historical association with returns, but reporting changes alone can generate the same pattern (§§3.4, 4.2, 5.1).

## Problem and motivation
Distributions of corporate-news embeddings can describe firms’ reported economic footprints, but their movement does not explain the operating decisions behind repositioning. The authors address this gap by linking competition, costly capacity adjustment, financial valuation, and news production. This matters because changing news composition may reflect either business changes or changing attention (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Economic model:** Extend proportional-share multi-contest competition to capacity allocation across overlapping activities, establish equilibrium uniqueness and comparative statics, and introduce sequential Nash adjustment with quadratic adjustment costs (§§3.1–3.3; Appendices B–C).
- **Directional prediction:** Derive a nonnegative expected local movement–return covariance in a restricted two-opportunity economy under common reporting intensity and specified timing and error restrictions (§3.4, Proposition 2; Appendix A).
- **Measurement method:** Compare annual news distributions with fixed reference profiles using squared Wasserstein distance, then relate distance decreases to reference firms’ article-timing-weighted excess returns after centering within target-years (§3.5).
- **Validation and application:** Use simulations to separate operating signals, article noise, and reporting distortion; apply the estimator to Nasdaq firms and separately audit reconstruction of news movements (§§4–5; Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results

Numerical values below are rounded from the supplied text.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

The evidence establishes a sufficient theoretical mechanism, measurement difficulties in the specified simulations, and a descriptive historical correspondence. It does not identify causal operating adjustment, distinguish capacity changes from reporting changes, or establish a corporate-population confidence interval. The reconstruction audit evaluates news geometry separately from the financial association (§§3.6, 5.2; Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope restriction:** The sign theorem is local and depends on special exposure ordering and observation assumptions; general response matrices need not preserve that ordering (Appendix A).
- **Authors’ identification limitation:** Capacity and reporting intensity jointly determine observed article mixtures. Historical news and returns do not separately identify their contributions (§§3.4, 5.2).
- **Authors’ modeling simplifications:** Firms optimize current stage payoffs without continuation values; valuation uses constant discounting and does not feed back into investment (§3.3).
- **Authors’ inference boundary:** Article redraws condition on firms, returns, and years. The archive has no established independent-firm-history sampling design, and simulation containment does not prove general bootstrap validity (Appendix E).
- **Authors’ empirical boundaries:** Financial influence is uneven, reconstruction is weak, and the embedding model postdates the article sample (§§4.4–5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** External validity remains uncertain because the historical application covers one roster and period, while direct operating measures are absent. Raw inputs are not redistributed, and the replication package is promised for the version of record (Data availability statement).

## Open research questions
1. **Can operating adjustment be separated from reporting attention?** The historical estimator cannot distinguish them. A next experiment could combine dated investment or production measures with news and returns (§5.2).
2. **When does directional alignment survive richer competition?** The theorem supplies a restricted sufficient mechanism. A next experiment could vary activity overlap, firm scale, and reporting heterogeneity in jointly solved economies (Appendices A, D.2).
3. **How much coverage is needed for useful recovery?** Tested coverage increases still leave large relative error. A next experiment could extend the article-budget ladder across different operating-shock magnitudes (§4.2; Appendix D).
4. **Does the association generalize across environments?** The historical sign is stable within the observed sample but influence is concentrated. A next experiment could repeat the fixed-reference design on additional firm cohorts and periods (§5.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative clearly supports the central findings and their descriptive interpretation, but several tables appear only as captions.

Material missing information includes complete table cells for alternative specifications and diagnostics, the firm roster and detailed corpus-selection rules, precise market-data provider and excess-return benchmark definitions, and an accessible replication-package location. Direct capacity and reporting-intensity measurements are unavailable.
