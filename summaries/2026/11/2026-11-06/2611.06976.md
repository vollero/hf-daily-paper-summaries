# On the Opportunities and Challenges of Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-Model Transfer

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06976) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06976)


## One-sentence takeaway
PLatour improves generation through reward-trained bridges between frozen language models, but its controls show that many gains can arise from receiver adaptation rather than useful sender information (§§4–7).

## Problem and motivation
Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-model Transfer (PACT) lets a large sender encode a prompt while a smaller receiver generates the response independently, aiming to combine stronger representations with cheaper decoding. The authors identify two gaps: limited open support for studying this collaboration across architectures and scales, and insufficient evidence that benchmark improvements actually require information from the sender (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **PLatour:** Independent, zero-initialized, bias-free linear projections inject sender hidden states into the receiver’s residual stream during prompt processing, excluding the final prompt token. Both backbones stay frozen; GRPO trains only the bridges using task rewards. Relative-depth layer matching includes attention-type constraints for hybrid architectures (§4).
- **Empirical baseline and ablations:** The authors evaluate full-attention Qwen3 and hybrid-attention Qwen3.5 systems, comparing interfaces, training objectives, initialization, and layer matching (§§6–7; Appendices E–F).
- **PACT-Check and PACT-Score:** Dummy and irrelevant transfer intervene on a trained system; separately trained self-communication and receiver-only baselines test adaptation alternatives. PACT-Score is system accuracy minus the strongest applicable control’s accuracy (§5).

The evidence supports a strong task-trained bridge and a material adaptation confound. Positive PACT-Scores provide evidence beyond the tested controls; they do not identify the transmitted information or prove a shared representation hypothesis. Conversely, non-positive scores show that a control matches or exceeds performance, without proving that no information is transferred (§§5, 8).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limitation:** Latent-transfer experiments remain within Qwen families; cross-family generalization is open (§8).
- **Comparison limitation:** Mostik’s undisclosed training data and uncertain general versus domain-specific bridge design prevent a controlled superiority claim (§7.1).
- **Contamination:** The authors identify training-pool overlaps in the full AIME set and report a separate cleaned subset. Its earliest yearly subsets have very small denominators (§6; Appendix B.5).
- **Training instability:** Larger-sender failures limit scaling conclusions. Clipping accompanies disruptions, but the authors explicitly do not establish causation (Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference:** PACT-Score depends on the included controls and their training quality; it is evidence relative to those alternatives, rather than a complete causal decomposition (§5).
- **Evidence availability:** Many main-result and control tables appear only as captions and narrative summaries in the supplied text, preventing verification of exact margins or uncertainty.

## Open research questions
- **Can training explicitly encourage sender dependence?** Reward optimization permits adaptation-only gains (§8). A next experiment could compare standard GRPO with an objective rewarding improvement over matched sender-independent controls.
- **Does useful transfer generalize across model families?** Current latent experiments do not establish this (§8). Test heterogeneous sender–receiver pairs under the same training budget and full PACT-Check.
- **What causes larger-sender instability?** Observed clipping correlations are inconclusive (Appendix I). Vary rollout limits and optimization settings independently across repeated runs.
- **Can cached shared-prefix representations retain useful transfer?** The authors propose amortizing sender computation but provide no experiment (§8). Compare shared-prefix-only and question-specific transfer on accuracy, PACT-Score, and total latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture, protocol, and qualitative findings are clearly described, with some numerical ablations available. Material missing information includes most main/control table cells, numerical latency measurements and measurement hardware, repeated-run variability and uncertainty for central PACT comparisons, and direct mechanistic evidence of what the bridges transmit.
