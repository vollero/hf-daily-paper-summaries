# SLA: Scaling Spatial-Language-Action Model via Continual Pre-training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08373) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08373)


## One-sentence takeaway
SLA improves manipulation robustness by aligning a pretrained robot policy’s visual features with a frozen geometry teacher during continual pre-training and downstream adaptation, with controlled evidence strongest on RoboTwin.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that VLA policies inherit strong semantics from image–text pre-training but insufficient spatial representations for precise manipulation. Explicit geometric inputs introduce sensor and calibration dependencies, while prior implicit spatial alignment primarily targets downstream adaptation. SLA addresses the gap of developing transferable spatial representations across heterogeneous robot experience before task-specific training (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Spatial alignment across training stages:** Starting from π₀.₅, SLA combines action prediction with cosine alignment between intermediate visual features and frozen OmniVGGT features. Alignment continues during downstream post-training. This extends the authors’ prior post-training-only Spatial Forcing approach (§§3.2.1, 4.3).
- **Adaptive supervision:** Shared, feature-dependent gates aggregate projected features across transformer layers at token and channel level. The teacher accepts RGB plus whatever depth and calibration annotations are available (§§3.2.2–3.2.3).
- **Heterogeneous data preparation:** Six public sources are filtered and subsampled, with robot-centered end-effector states and chunk-relative actions standardized across embodiments. Rotations use SO(3) composition and rotation-vector targets (§3.3).
- **Supporting training recipe:** Probabilistic removal of state tokens encourages visual reliance. Teacher, projector, and gates are discarded at deployment; RoboDojo separately retains historical-image inputs for memory (§3.2.4; Appendix B).

The central contribution is scaling spatial alignment through continual pre-training; neither the base policy nor the geometry teacher is introduced by this paper.

The evidence supports the complete recipe across several evaluated settings, particularly randomized-scene manipulation. The stage ablation strengthens the case for spatial supervision beyond additional data alone. It does not establish universal spatial reasoning or isolate geometry as the sole cause of every benchmark gain. Several leading margins are small, and uncertainty estimates are absent.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described challenges:** Randomized layouts, full multistage completion, and tasks without demonstrations remain difficult on RoboDojo-Sim; performance also varies substantially by task and embodiment (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited scope:** Filtering excludes dexterous hands and base motion, so transfer to those control settings is untested (§3.3.2).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** RoboDojo adds historical-image memory without a reported memory ablation. Its gains cannot be attributed solely to spatial alignment; additional image inputs also qualify the broad inference-overhead claim (§4.3.4; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Tables report no confidence intervals or training-seed variability. RoboCasa demonstration budgets differ across methods; RoboDojo-Sim compares locally evaluated SLA against leaderboard results (Tables 4, 15).
- **Internal reporting inconsistency:** Table 5 and Table 14 disagree on some embodiment-level RoboDojo-Real results, although both give the same overall score and SR.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Table 1 describes source totals before selection, not the final training corpus. The supplied text omits the final retained counts, exact mixture weights, alignment coefficient α, and depth-availability breakdown (§§3.2.1, 3.3).
- **Resource demand:** Continual pre-training reportedly takes approximately **110 hours on 48 H100 GPUs**; no lower-resource scaling study is supplied (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How do alignment and state dropout interact?** The dropout ablation changes only post-training dropout from one checkpoint. A factorial experiment varying alignment and dropout in both stages could separate their contributions (Tables 6, 8).
2. **How much do historical images explain RoboDojo gains?** Memory is retained at inference without a matched ablation. Compare SLA and π₀.₅ with identical memory configurations, alongside memory-free variants (Appendix B).
3. **Does the recipe transfer beyond the tested backbone and control scope?** Only π₀.₅ and filtered stationary-base manipulation are demonstrated. Repeat controlled alignment experiments on another VLA backbone and datasets containing mobile manipulation or dexterous hands (§§3.3.2, 4.2).
4. **Can spatial supervision improve multistage and undemonstrated tasks?** RoboDojo-Sim exposes persistent failures in these settings. Evaluate targeted temporal spatial supervision against the current method with matched data and memory budgets (Appendix A.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, protocols, results, and useful ablations, but causal attribution and statistical reliability remain incomplete.

Material missing information includes final processed-corpus statistics and sampling weights, α and some training defaults, annotation coverage, training-seed variability and confidence intervals, matched memory controls, verified checkpoint/data release status, and reconciliation of the RoboDojo-Real embodiment results.
