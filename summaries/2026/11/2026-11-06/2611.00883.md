# Random Is Almost Enough? Stable and Low-Cost Benchmark Compression via BestRand

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00883) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00883)


## One-sentence takeaway
BestRand selects random benchmark subsets using cached probe-model scores, empirically reducing estimation error and high-error risk while its theory explains a shared source of sampling instability (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation
Benchmark compression estimates full-benchmark model scores from fewer test items. Random sampling is inexpensive and often accurate, but occasionally selects unusually easy or difficult subsets, producing large errors. The authors identify insufficient understanding of this error distribution and expensive construction requirements in existing response-based methods as key gaps (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Distributional analysis:** Exact sampling moments and a Gaussian approximation relate compression error to subset size, item difficulty variation, and model correlation. Under an equal-variance, equal-correlation model, shared errors persist as the evaluated model panel grows (§2.1; Appendices F.4–F.6).
- **BestRand selection:** Sample a candidate pool, score it with probe models, and select the random subset whose mean probe scores most closely match the pool mean. Defaults are five probes, 128 candidates, a pool of up to four times the subset size, and L2 matching. Candidate search reuses cached scores (§2.2; Appendix A.4).
- **Empirical validation and extension:** Evaluate score accuracy, tail risk, rankings, construction cost, distribution shifts, and joint matching of multiple metrics, including driving benchmarks (§§3–4; Appendix C).

The theoretical contribution supports a common-shift reduction mechanism; it does not guarantee contraction of the complete MAE distribution for the implemented finite-pool method (Appendix F.7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lower mean score error | Across 44 text, 16 multimodal, and 10 agent benchmarks at six retention ratios from 2% to 50%, authors report 16.4% average MAE reduction versus Random, ZipBench, and TinyBenchmarks. | §3; Table 1 |
| Concrete accuracy comparison | At 20% retention, category-average MAE (%) for BestRand versus Random is 1.73 versus 2.11 for text, 1.71 versus 1.96 for multimodal, and 3.14 versus 3.65 for agents. | Table 1 |
| Reduced high-error risk | Authors report 50.2% average reduction versus the same three baselines, using exceedance of Random’s mean and 70th-percentile MAE as risk thresholds across benchmark categories. | §3; Figure 3 |
| Fewer construction records | BestRand uses 51.3% of data-dependent baselines’ construction records on average, and 8.0% at 2% retention; cost counts observed model-item scores. | §3; §4(4) |
| Stronger OOD score estimation | At 20% retention, aggregate MAE (%) is 1.69 for BestRand, 1.95 for Random, 2.19 for ZipBench, and 2.36 for TinyBenchmarks; the final aggregate weights the three shift types equally. | Table 2; Appendix A.4 |
| Ranking gains are limited | Across 5–50% retention, mean Spearman correlation is BestRand/Random: text 0.922/0.921, multimodal 0.917/0.919, agents 0.870/0.870. | Table 8 |
| Multi-metric driving extension | On Bench2Drive at 50% removal, average error is 1.47 with four-metric matching, 1.89 with success-rate-only matching, and 1.99 with Random; this average combines differently scaled errors. | Table 6; Appendix C.2 |

These results support improved average score estimation and reduced empirical tail risk under the reported protocols. They do not establish universal superiority on individual benchmarks or improved rankings everywhere. Construction-record savings also do not directly measure end-to-end compute savings.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Estimation error remains nonzero, probe inference costs persist, and compressed evaluation cannot fully replace comprehensive final evaluation (Appendix D.1).
- **Theory scope:** The Gaussian approximation requires an appropriate asymptotic regime and covariance stability. Equal variances and correlations are simplifying approximations; “long-tailed” denotes observed right skew, not a heavy-tailed asymptotic law (Appendices F.4–F.6).
- **Selection guarantee:** Residual-variance preservation relies on a joint Gaussian, independent-candidate model with a full-benchmark reference. Finite-pool selection lacks a general contraction or per-model unbiasedness guarantee; reducing a tail lower bound does not prove reduced actual MAE tail probability (Appendix F.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Unequal trial counts weaken precision comparisons for stochastic baselines: main experiments use 10,000 trials for Random/BestRand and three for others. A ten-seed check supports similar risk patterns on six benchmarks, but does not resolve this across the complete suite (Appendices A.4, B; Figure 7).
- **Reproducibility:** Historical response collection costs cannot be reliably estimated. The abstract claims public code/data, while later statements promise future release; availability is unverified in the supplied text (p. 1; pp. 10, 30).
- **Coverage:** The source is truncated, omitting part of the additional comparisons and theoretical material; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions

- **When do probes fail to transfer?** OOD tests show gains, but do not establish reliability when probes explain little target difficulty variation. Next experiment: vary probe–target similarity and measure explained variance alongside MAE tail risk.
- **Can finite-pool selection receive stronger guarantees?** Current contraction results concern an idealized selection model. Next experiment: systematically vary pool size and candidate count while measuring reference error, residual variance, and actual tail probabilities.
- **Can selection preserve close model rankings better?** Table 8 shows little aggregate ranking improvement despite lower MAE. Next experiment: evaluate pairwise ranking reversals among models with small full-score differences.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied sections clearly support the procedure and main empirical findings, but the source is truncated. Material gaps include omitted comparison results, portions of assumption validation, exact model identities and OOD benchmark membership, verified code/data availability, and original response-generation costs.
