# VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08449) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449)


## One-sentence takeaway
VFlash jointly decodes complete visual primitives without requiring autoregressive token agreement, reporting lower robot-trajectory errors and substantially faster planning, although dense-motion accuracy is mixed and downstream comparisons require care.

## Problem and motivation
Vision-language models generate visual plans—boxes, points, and trajectories—to guide physical actions. Autoregressive decoding produces coordinates sequentially, so richer plans increase latency and limit update frequency. The authors identify a mismatch in conventional speculative decoding: exact token agreement with a target model can reject geometrically useful predictions and restrict parallel throughput (§§1, 2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Primitive-aligned parallel decoding:** Flash Primitive Decoding uses a known schema specifying type, dimensions, and length to predict an entire primitive in one drafter pass. It extends fixed-size box decoding to variable-length trajectories and dense motion (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Spatial supervision and direct acceptance:** A shallow drafter learns annotated coordinates using frozen target-VLM features. Valid primitives are accepted without target-token agreement; boxes additionally use a confidence guard and AR fallback. Ordinary text retains verified speculative decoding (§§2.4–2.5).
- **Multiple coordinate representations and action interfaces:** Special coordinate tokens and fixed-width digit layouts support different target formats. The target processes accepted plans to produce hidden states for downstream action models (§§2.3, 3; Appendices A–D).

The contribution builds on DFlash’s drafting architecture and LocateAnything’s box decoding rather than introducing those components (§4).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; the supplied text contains table captions and narrative summaries but omits the numerical table bodies.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lower 2D trajectory errors | Against the same AR target on ShareRobot-Bench and VABench-V: HD decreases 7%; DFD and RMSE decrease 3–6%. Evaluation uses 200 and 300 queries, respectively. | §3.1; Table 2 caption; Appendix F.1 |
| Substantial 3D trajectory gains | On InternA1, AgiBot, and MolmoBot, ADE decreases 18–49% for 3D-only outputs and 28–61% for joint 2D+3D outputs versus their respective AR targets. On AgiBot, waypoint accuracy within 5 cm rises from 50% to 73% and from 40% to 74%, respectively. | §3.1; Table 4; Appendix F.2 |
| Box speed depends on fallback | On COCO and Dense200, confidence-guarded AR fallback matches AR mean F1 while generating boxes 2.6–2.8× faster. Direct acceptance without fallback loses about 20 mean-F1 points on Dense200. | §3.1; Table 3; Appendix F.4 |
| Dense-motion quality is mixed | Versus MolmoMotion-AR on PointMotionBench: DAVIS ADE/FDE decrease 27%/19%; HOT3D FDE decreases 8%; WorldTrack FDE increases 15%; PWT is reported 7–12% lower across the three datasets. | §3.1; Table 5 |
| Preliminary dense-motion acceleration | One rollout per method on one H100, batch size one: HOT3D 3.21 vs 79.8 s; WorldTrack 3.86 vs 90.6 s; DAVIS 3.78 vs 99.6 s—24.8×, 23.5×, and 26.4× faster. | Appendix F.3 |
| Better MolmoSpaces success | Across four suites and 3,915 simulated episodes, VFlash-conditioned WAM averages 70.2% success versus 62.6% with its original text encoder. Using AR-generated joint plans instead yields 61.0%. Each configuration uses one training and evaluation seed. | §3.2; Tables 6–7; Appendix D.1 |
| Gains on LIBERO perturbation benchmarks | Same action-head architecture: VFlash improves average success over the frozen Qwen3-VL baseline by 0.8 points on LIBERO, 1.9 on LIBERO-Plus, and 6.8 on LIBERO-PRO. | §3.2; Figure 3; Appendix D.2 |
| Competitive driving scores | SFT yields 87.1 PDMS on NAVSIM and 7.79 RFS on Waymo; action-head-only RL raises these to 90.7 and 7.86. Plans remain unchanged between SFT and RL. | §3.2; Table 8; Appendices D.3, F.5 |
| Lower pipeline latency | Versus AR at batch size one, MolmoSpaces per-step latency improves 5.1–5.2× on Thor/Spark and 3.6× on H100. NAVSIM improves 3.4–3.6× on Thor/Spark and 2.6× on H100; Waymo improves 4.5× on H100. | §3.3; Figures 4–5; Appendix E |

The strongest evidence supports faster structured decoding and lower robot-trajectory errors relative to the same target. It does not establish uniformly better spatial prediction: dense-motion metrics regress in some settings, and boxes require confidence-based fallback. Downstream baseline comparisons also change conditioning or encoders; the MolmoSpaces AR-plan comparison more directly tests the decoder choice.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported qualification:** Dense-motion timing is preliminary and based on single rollouts. Dense-motion quality has explicit regressions, while direct box acceptance substantially harms Dense200 F1 (§3.1; Appendix F.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain variability:** MolmoSpaces and LIBERO use single training/evaluation seed pairs; the supplied text gives no confidence intervals or repeated-seed estimates (Appendices D.1–D.2).
- **Analyst inference—latency scope:** Timings use a modified TensorRT runtime, batch size one, supplied tool-call JSON, and selected records. They exclude several loading and preprocessing costs. Driving latency uses ten DDIM steps, whereas quality evaluation uses five (Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference—deployment validity:** MolmoSpaces pauses simulation during inference, so success rates do not establish performance under real-time environmental change (Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference—selection boundaries:** Waymo RL reward and checkpoint selection reuse the same validation scenes. NAVSIM action-head validation clips were seen during upstream VLM fine-tuning. Both reported test splits remain excluded from fitting and selection (Appendices D.3, F.5).
- **Analyst inference—structural validity is limited:** Schema acceptance checks formatting and coordinate rules; it does not itself establish geometric correctness or safe control (Appendices A, C).

## Open research questions
1. **Which design change causes the accuracy gains?** The supplied results do not isolate primitive alignment, annotation supervision, and direct acceptance. A factorial ablation with a fixed target and training budget could separate their effects.
2. **Can direct acceptance preserve dense-motion quality?** WorldTrack FDE and PWT regress. Compare frame-level confidence guards and selective fallback while measuring accuracy, point coverage, and latency.
3. **Do latency reductions improve control in changing environments?** MolmoSpaces pauses during inference. Evaluate asynchronous execution with moving objects or disturbances under matched wall-clock budgets.
4. **Do multiple complementary primitives improve control?** The authors explicitly leave this open (§5). Compare trajectory-only plans with trajectory-plus-box or point plans under the same latency budget.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose and appendices support the method and reported trends, but numerical table bodies and plotted values are absent, and some prose cross-references conflict with table captions. Material gaps include complete per-dataset scores, uncertainty estimates, controlled component ablations, a definition of PWT, and confirmation of code/checkpoint release.
