# Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08376) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08376)


## One-sentence takeaway

Muir reallocates linear-attention gate parameters toward a wider FFN and adds learned query readout gains, improving reported language-modeling results while exposing a tradeoff between dynamic readout and extreme-length passkey retrieval.

## Problem and motivation

Linear attention stores context in a fixed-size state, reducing sequence-length costs but introducing interference between stored associations. The authors identify three weaknesses in GDN-2: expensive dense erase/write gates, readout dominated by high-energy slow-decaying state rows, and unstable or inefficient chunkwise training at the large chunk sizes needed by wide matrix units (§§1–2).

Their central argument is that erase and write gates deserve different parameter budgets: erasure acts through normalized, concentrated keys, whereas writes directly modulate unnormalized value channels (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Sensitivity-allocated gating:** Fuse five gate projections, allocating lower rank to decay and erasure than to writes, output gating, and query readout. Saved parameters widen the FFN under the same nominal layer budget (§4.1).
- **Matched-filter readout:** Multiply normalized queries by learned static channel gains and token-dependent modulation. The theoretical motivation balances surviving target signal against row-specific interference; the implemented gains are learned rather than computed directly from the analytical formula (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Memory-dynamics analysis:** Derive gate sensitivities, an approximate stationary row-energy law, and conditions under which readout reweighting reduces the need for destructive erasure. Synthetic experiments and trained-model probes test these predictions (§3; Appendix I).
- **Boundary-pivoted training kernel:** Use bounded decay factors, recursive block inversion, and a custom backward pass to move chunkwise computation toward TPU matrix units without cumulative-prefix division (§4.2). Appendix J supplies reference JAX code.

The strongest available evidence supports improved loss under the specified training recipe, asymmetric gate sensitivity, and a substantial static-versus-dynamic passkey tradeoff. The controls indicate that FFN expansion contributes materially; they do not establish that gate compression alone improves language modeling. Stable stream perplexity also does not establish accurate retrieval throughout those streams.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Coverage limitation:** The source omits a large middle portion, including much of the main results, ablations, and analysis. This digest cannot provide complete coverage.
- **Theoretical scope:** The row-energy and readout results rely on stationarity, independence, uncorrelated channels/rows, and moment approximations; the row-energy formula drops cross-row coupling (§3.1). Empirical agreement supports its usefulness, not universal validity.
- **Author-reported analytical mismatch:** Directly substituting the theoretical single-horizon readout into trained models performs worse than fractional power profiles. The authors attribute this to overweighting weakly active channels and over-damping slow rows (Appendix I.3).
- **Rank-budget tradeoff:** Dynamic Muir halves write rank relative to static Muir. Authors attribute the extreme-length passkey gap to this allocation, supported by inference-time gate-freezing controls; those controls alone do not fully isolate the training-time cause (Appendix H.3; analyst assessment).
- **Evaluation scope:** Training uses one text corpus, models through 1.34B, and one accelerator family (§5.1). Cross-corpus, larger-scale, and cross-hardware generality remain unestablished.
- **Comparison constraints:** Transformer OOM results depend on the specified evaluation harness, which excludes rematerialization and chunked KV prefill (Appendix H.3).
- **Uncertainty:** Synthetic experiments use multiple seeds, but large-model training-seed replication is not established in the supplied text; authors acknowledge checkpoint sampling noise (§§3.2.2, 5.2.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can dynamic readout retain static Muir’s extreme-length retrieval?** Write rank changes alongside readout type. Train static and dynamic variants with matched write rank and controlled total budgets, then repeat passkey and extractive-recall evaluation.
2. **How robust is the memory theory under correlated, changing inputs?** Its assumptions simplify real token dynamics. Measure row-energy prediction errors and retrieval quality under controlled distribution shifts and correlated key streams.
3. **Does the kernel advantage transfer across hardware?** Reported timings target TPU7x. Benchmark matched implementations and numerical error on other accelerator families.
4. **Does drift-free perplexity imply useful long-range dependence?** The longest-stream result measures aggregate prediction quality. Insert controlled dependencies across increasing lags and measure retrieval accuracy alongside perplexity.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Method details and several important comparisons are explicit, but truncation removes substantial supporting evidence.

Unavailable information includes complete main-result tables, detailed long-stream and numerical-stability results, much of the ablation section, large-model seed variability, and confirmation of released checkpoints or a maintained code repository.
