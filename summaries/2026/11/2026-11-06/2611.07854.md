# Towards Foundation Decision Models: Drex

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07854) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07854)


## One-sentence takeaway
Drex provides four open-weight models that directly score user-supplied options without decoding, with controlled Max ablations supporting training gains but benchmark results showing persistent calibration, option-order, and knowledge limitations.

## Problem and motivation
Many classification, scoring, and routing tasks require probabilities over options that change with each request. The authors argue that generated answers add parsing, latency, and probability-estimation complications, while fixed classification heads cannot accommodate arbitrary option sets (§1).

Their goal is a general decision interface that combines accuracy, useful confidence estimates, option-order robustness, and efficient processing of multiple questions over shared context.

## Approach and contributions
- **Common interface and model family:** Four models return distributions for choice, yes/no, and ordinal-score questions. Pointer models cache a shared state; Max re-encodes it for each question and supports images (§§3–5; Appendix E).
- **Max training recipe:** Supervised fine-tuning adds option-permutation and state-shuffle consistency losses; RLAF combines verified-answer rewards, judge probabilities, and KL regularization toward the supervised checkpoint; a final stage tunes the image encoder and projection (§6).
- **Controlled training evidence:** Max ablations test RLAF, the combined consistency terms, and vision tuning, alongside broader calibration and order-sensitivity studies (§§8.4–8.8).
- **Released implementation:** The authors report released weights and inference code under model-specific licenses, plus serving optimizations (§1, release status; §9).

Architectural novelty is qualified: the interface and pointer machinery build on Jev/Kev, and Max adopts the attention/readout design of pplx-decider; its trained weights, training recipe, and evaluation are presented as contributions (§2).

These results support strong performance on supplied-option decisions and specific benefits from Max’s training components. They do not establish frontier-level knowledge or isolate architectural causes of Max’s overall gains. Nonsignificant differences do not prove equivalence. The detailed Artificial Analysis results also qualify the abstract’s rounded “second-highest” characterization.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated confounding:** Backbone, attention, head, training data, and serving setup change together across models; their individual contributions remain unresolved (§10; Appendix I).
- **Author-stated calibration and robustness limits:** Aggregate ECE hides domain errors, and many-option decisions are less stable. The order study excludes unsupported requests and includes few many-option cases (§§8.4–8.5).
- **Author-stated contamination risk:** Filtering cannot eliminate paraphrased benchmark overlap or benchmark knowledge inherited through teachers (§6.4).
- **Author-stated RLAF uncertainty:** Judge rewards can override verified answers; frequency, bias transfer, and possible overconfidence effects were not measured (§6.5).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Private-test outputs and launch-arena game records are unavailable; multiple-testing corrections were not applied (§7; Appendices H.3, I).
- **Analyst inference:** Native-image versus text-rendering gains demonstrate usefulness of image access but do not alone isolate the causal contribution of vision tuning (§§8.2, 8.8).
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated, and many tables retain captions without numeric bodies; this analysis cannot imply complete coverage.

## Open research questions
1. **Which architectural change drives Max’s gains?** Cross-model comparisons are confounded. Train matched pointer/readout variants with and without bidirectional full attention on the same backbone and data (§10).
2. **How does judge feedback affect correctness and calibration?** Verified-answer overrides and bias transfer are unmeasured. Sweep judge weight, verified-answer weight, and probability smoothing while measuring override frequency and domain calibration (§6.5).
3. **Does confidence subsume order-instability signals beyond this sample?** The current study is restricted and small for many-option decisions. Repeat matched-coverage abstention comparisons on independent workloads with more options and alternative option codes (§8.5; §10).
4. **Can reasoning before readout close the knowledge gap efficiently?** The paper proposes but does not test this. Compare single-pass scoring against reasoning- or tool-assisted scoring on the same knowledge questions, recording accuracy and latency (§10).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main findings, but coverage is incomplete.

Material missing information includes the omitted middle text, most numeric table bodies and intervals, the full Max training configuration/data extension, private-test predictions, and launch-arena records. Release availability and reported external evaluations were not independently verified.
