# Terminal-style Mathematical Modeling Bench: The First Agentic and Verifiable Benchmark for Mathematical Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08237) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08237)


## One-sentence takeaway
TB-MathModel evaluates terminal agents on contest-style mathematical modeling using human-reference numeric scores and feasibility checks, but reference ambiguity and inconsistent reporting limit confidence in its headline rankings.

## Problem and motivation
Mathematical modeling requires choosing assumptions, constructing models, executing computations, and defending recommendations. The authors argue that existing evaluations either check narrow solver artifacts or use LLM judges to grade reports without verifying their numbers. Their target is an executable evaluation of the numeric outcome of a complete modeling workflow (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and execution environment:** 18 CUMCM/MCM problems from 2023–2025, with official statements, attachments, and figures in isolated terminal containers. Agents submit JSON; only the numeric core of the final question is scored (§3; Appendix A).
- **Reference-based scoring:** Direction-aware rewards avoid penalizing improvements on directional metrics. Multi-reference evaluation averages agreement with available Outstanding-award papers, rather than treating one solution as definitive (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Feasibility auditing:** A programmatic gate checks domain bounds and internal constraints; Lean 4 obligations audit specified invariants. Failed gates zero task scores, while external physical validity requires independent replay (§3.3).
- **Empirical and benchmark audit:** Seven multimodal model/harness entries are evaluated, alongside harness sensitivity, cost comparisons, and cases where scoring rules or human anchors required correction (§4).

The authors claim the combination is a first; the supplied text does not independently establish priority.

These are author-reported results, not independently reproduced findings. They support the usefulness of executable checks and demonstrate reference sensitivity. They do not establish complete modeling quality, real-world physical correctness, or statistically resolved differences between adjacent leaderboard entries.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limitation:** Only selected final-question numbers are scored; assumptions, sensitivity analysis, and defended recommendations are not comprehensively evaluated (§3; Appendix A.1).
- **Authors’ verification limitation:** Range and consistency proofs do not establish physical truth. Some checks require replay, and missing evidence produces skipped obligations (§3.3; Appendix H.1).
- **Reference dependence:** Panels vary substantially in size, and valid modeling choices can disagree. Unit and scope mismatches affected scores; some anchors were corrected or replaced (§§3.2, 4.3; Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference—headline denominator is unclear:** §3.2 and Limitations exclude literature-split tasks from headline rankings, but §4.2 and Table 6’s caption describe headline means over all 18 tasks.
- **Analyst inference—proof claims need inspection:** Appendix H.2’s printed scaled identity appears arithmetically inconsistent and uses a band despite claims of exact checking. Appendix F also gives a different dragon-dance ratio from Appendix G.5.
- **Reproducibility qualifications:** Prompts changed after some trials; third-party artifacts were sometimes reconstructed from records; misfiled artifacts were recovered by replay (§C.3; §E.4).
- **Analyst inference—generalization is untested:** Harness sensitivity holds one model fixed, and the benchmark has no held-out partition. The text provides no training experiment validating its proposed RL use (§4.4; Appendix A.5).

## Open research questions
1. **How stable are rankings under reference selection?** Human disagreement and variable panel sizes remain consequential. Next experiment: recompute rankings with leave-one-paper-out panels and explicit inclusion/exclusion of literature-split tasks.
2. **Can richer artifacts establish stronger physical validity?** Current JSON can omit evidence needed for replay (§3.3). Next experiment: require layouts or trajectories and measure additional violations detected by independent simulators.
3. **Does verifier-based training improve unseen modeling performance?** §4.4 proposes training signals without testing them. Next experiment: train with feasibility-gated rewards and evaluate on newly held-out contest problems.
4. **Does harness invariance extend beyond Flash?** The reported comparison covers one model. Next experiment: cross several models with identical harness settings and delivered prompts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The design and reported trends are well described, but numerical interpretation has unresolved inconsistencies.

Material missing information includes full table bodies, complete per-task scores and uncertainty, metric weights and panel values, exact leaderboard task inclusion, trial-specific prompts and configurations, and executable artifacts needed to inspect the proof examples and reconstructed submissions.
