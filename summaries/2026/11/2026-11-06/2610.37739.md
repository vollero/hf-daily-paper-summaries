# OpenFrontiers: A Collection of Open Research Problems and a Benchmark for Scientific Rediscovery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37739) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37739)


## One-sentence takeaway
OpenFrontiers pairs a large research-problem corpus with formally graded mathematics tasks, finding limited closed-book rediscovery and substantially higher live-web success largely associated with fetching published solutions.

## Problem and motivation
Evaluating research ability requires both identifying genuinely open questions and grading solutions reliably. The authors argue that existing collections have inconsistent formats and drifting status labels, while conventional benchmarks usually test questions whose answers were already known to their creators (§1).

Recently resolved problems offer a retrospective test, but solution dates alone cannot establish that a model has never encountered a solution—especially when its own model lineage helped produce it (§§1, 4.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Problem corpus:** 12,151 source records with statements, provenance, dated status claims, licensing information, subject labels, and optional Lean attachments. Uncertain status remains `unassessed`; records are not deduplicated conjectures (§3; Table 1).
- **Rediscovery benchmark:** 200 mathematics problems comprising Rediscovery-110 and Open-90, with pinned Lean 4 statements, automated probes, and two independent AI-agent reviews. HQ-50 selects 40 resolved and 10 open items using reliability and diversity criteria (§4; Appendices C–D).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Closed-book, historical retrieval, and live-web conditions; sandboxed submissions accepted only by two Lean checkers; model-specific exclusions for solution lineage and cutoff dates (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Agent study:** Seven agents evaluated under native stopping and continuation protocols, separating formal rediscovery performance from solution retrieval (§§5.3–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus breadth is dominated by mathematics | 12,151 source records from 62 contributing collections; 11,349 records (93.4%) are mathematics; 46.0% have unassessed status. | Table 1; §3.2 |
| Benchmark reference reliability varies | Of 110 resolved items, 85 are Tier A, 21 Tier B, and 4 Tier C; HQ-50 excludes conditional Tier A and lower tiers. | §§4.1–4.3; Appendix C |
| Closed-book formal rediscovery is uncommon | On 40 resolved HQ-50 items, seven agents solve 0–3 under native stopping and 0–7 under continuation; pass@1, three hours per attempt. | Table 3 |
| Best aggregate closed-book count has a smaller clean denominator | GPT-6.1-sol under continuation solves 7/40 overall and 4/15 after lineage and cutoff exclusions. Claude Sonnet 5.5 obtains 4/40 overall and 3/30 clean. | Table 3 |
| Success concentrates on a few tasks | Across agents and protocols, 44 of 53 closed-book/frozen-retrieval solves occur on three human-solved items with short constructions. | §5.4 |
| Frozen retrieval offers no systematic improvement | Relative to closed-book, frozen retrieval improves two runs and worsens three; its content cutoff is fixed across tasks. | §5.4 |
| Live-web success is strongly associated with solution access | Across agents and protocols, 167 of 181 live-web solves fetched a located solution source; individual run totals range from 1–27/40. | §5.4; Table 3 |
| Open-item acceptance requires interpretation | Table 7 records one pre-review open-item acceptance in each of three continuation/live-web runs; §2 reports no open problem solved without a published solution. Final item-level review outcomes are not supplied. | §2; Table 7 |

These results support limited formal rediscovery under this particular task selection, budget, and agent setup. They do not establish general scientific discovery ability. Fetch logs support the authors’ retrieval interpretation, but fetching a solution source does not by itself quantify how much each proof depended on it.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ qualifications:** Corpus status records upstream claims rather than independently verified openness. Counts include overlapping source records, and corpus Lean attachments are display-only (§3; Appendix D).
- **Authors’ qualifications:** Solution-date audits are not exhaustive priority searches; importance scores are curator judgments. Statement reviews were performed by AI agents (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Evaluation covers a selected mathematics subset and requires Lean proofs, coupling mathematical reasoning with formalization skill. Other corpus fields are not evaluated (§§4–5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Clean denominators differ substantially between models, and there is one attempt per item and condition. CLI defaults also differ; no repeated-run uncertainty estimates are reported (Table 3; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—continuation effects:** Continuation changes both prompting and realized compute usage. Its gains do not isolate persistence from additional computation, and some model-condition totals decline (Tables 3, 7).
- **Internal timing ambiguity:** Frozen retrieval uses 2026-01-06, described as preceding every solution, while Appendix C says located solution dates start on 2026-01-05. The supplied text does not reconcile this (§5.1; Appendices C, E).
- **Reuse constraint:** The corpus carries heterogeneous source terms and has no uniform permissive license (Appendix F).

## Open research questions
1. **Can persistence improve rediscovery at matched compute?** Continuation sometimes helps but also increases time used. Next experiment: compare continuation prompts and uninterrupted sessions with matched token and tool budgets.
2. **How robust are formal statements to independent expert review?** Current reviews use AI agents, and accepted open items trigger further review. Next experiment: have blinded mathematics experts audit sampled statements and every open-item acceptance.
3. **Can retrieval help without exposing solutions?** Historical retrieval gives no systematic gain here. Next experiment: use task-specific, strictly pre-solution snapshots with audited retrieved passages and document-utility annotations.
4. **Do findings extend beyond the selected formal mathematics tasks?** Other disciplines in the corpus are unevaluated. Next experiment: construct a separately validated domain subset with independently checkable outputs and documented grading criteria.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial protocol and result detail, but this analysis cannot independently validate the assets or mathematical solution claims.

Material missing information includes final reviews of accepted open items, per-attempt proof and retrieval traces, repeated-run variability, reconciliation of the retrieval cutoff dates, and expert validation independent of the AI curation process.
