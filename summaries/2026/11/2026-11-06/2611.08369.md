# NPCBench: A Clinical Apprenticeship Benchmark for Multimodal Care-Pathway Reasoning in Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08369) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08369)


## One-sentence takeaway
NPCBench exposes a gap between individual clinical answers and joint diagnostic–care correctness: among 21 multimodal models, the best strict success is 23.0% on its selected 100-patient CarePath cohort (§4.5).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that medical examinations, isolated image tasks, and static vignettes inadequately measure whether models combine specialist knowledge, visual evidence, and changing patient states. NPCBench addresses this gap in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), evaluating component skills alongside jointly correct diagnostic and care answers for the same patient (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Apprenticeship evaluation framework:** Six levels cover guideline recall, guideline application, image grounding, case planning, longitudinal revision, and linked CarePath episodes; a separate consultation suite evaluates free-text answers (§3.3).
- **Specialist-reviewed benchmark assets:** The authors release 4,777 component/consultation items plus 100 CarePath episodes containing 417 diagnostic and care tasks, with multimodal inputs, annotations, rubrics, and evaluation code (§1; Appendix A).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Patient-level intersections, matched input controls, and physician-calibrated judges distinguish visual extraction errors, decision revisions, and consultation-scoring weaknesses (§§4.2–4.5).

CarePath’s primary protocol answers care questions independently using supplied clinical context and predicted diagnostic labels. It measures joint correctness rather than autonomous execution of an evolving care pathway; previous care answers appear only in separate serial controls (§3.3; Appendix B).

The evidence supports the authors’ central observation that component accuracy and joint patient correctness can rank models differently. It does not establish clinical benefit or autonomous care competence. The image-replacement results also do not establish that visual evidence is clinically unimportant: supplied N/M/overall-stage information remains fixed, and the control covers a small selected subset.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Primarily single-center records, assigned pathology in 75 CarePath episodes, sensitivity to class balance and episode length, and a six-case Expanded Audit limit generalization (§5).
- **Selection and modality linkage:** Four models helped select difficult patients and remove unanimously correct checkpoints. Only 25 episodes have patient-matched pathology; all assigned specimens are NPC-positive (§C.3.5).
- **Analyst inference—context redundancy:** Supplied N/M/overall-stage information in some care questions may reduce dependence on predicted image findings, limiting causal interpretation of diagnostic–care disagreement (§C.3.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison constraints:** Different image-processing interfaces and output budgets complicate attribution of model differences solely to reasoning ability (Appendix C).
- **Statistical caution:** Most history and image-replacement intervals include zero; multi-model intervals are descriptive and unadjusted (§§C.2.3, C.4).
- **Text inconsistency:** The introduction says complete invasion correctness reaches at most 5.0%, whereas §4.3 says it falls below 5.0%; Table A15’s numerical body is absent from the supplied text.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the composition gap persist in fully matched multicenter cohorts?** Assigned pathology and predominantly single-center records leave this unresolved. Next experiment: evaluate the same models on prospectively assembled, fully matched pathology–MRI–clinical episodes.
2. **When do better image findings improve care decisions?** Reference replacement gives mixed results under fixed clinical context. Next experiment: vary reference findings and supplied stage summaries separately, restricting analysis to decisions clinicians verify depend on those findings.
3. **Can longitudinal feedback improve complete episodes reliably?** Primary care questions are independent, and serial-control intervals remain broad. Next experiment: compare persistent and stateless interaction on a larger matched cohort, measuring both corrections and newly introduced errors.
4. **How stable are consultation rankings under physician scoring?** Judge rankings change across audits, and expanded calibration covers six cases. Next experiment: conduct a larger blinded physician audit across clinical topics and recompute model rankings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark design and explicitly reported results; lower in generalization and causal interpretations.

Material gaps in the supplied text include numerical bodies of many referenced tables, full task templates and illustrated inputs, a unique benchmark-wide patient count, and prospective evidence connecting benchmark gains to clinical outcomes. Public asset availability is author-reported and was not independently verified.
