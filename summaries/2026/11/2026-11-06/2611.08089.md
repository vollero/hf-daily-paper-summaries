# SAE Interprets MoE: Sparse AutoEncoders Reveal That MoE Experts Exhibit Human-Interpretable Fine-Grained Specialization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08089) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08089)


## One-sentence takeaway
SAE-based attribution in three layers of Qwen3-30B-A3B finds concentrated expert contributions and fine-grained specialization, although semantic labels rely on automated interpretation and causal effects remain untested.

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask what individual MoE experts contribute. Prior analyses count expert selections on domain-labeled corpora, conflating routing with contribution and assigning coarse document labels to individual tokens. The proposed gap is a feature-level account that recovers specialization from model activations rather than a predefined domain taxonomy (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Expert-to-feature attribution:** Train an SAE on an MoE layer’s output. Its affine encoder gives an exact decomposition of each latent’s pre-activation into expert terms plus encoder bias. Subsequent statistics retain only positive expert terms and normalize them into corpus-level contribution fractions (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Dominance graphs:** Connect experts to latents whose contribution fraction exceeds a threshold, then measure concentration, coverage, and overlap of expert-specific latent sets (§3.5; §4).
- **Semantic characterization:** Use GPT-5.5 to explain sampled latents, label experts, judge selected themes, and group expert labels into coarse domains. Structural analysis covers three layers; semantic analysis focuses on layer 30 (§5; Appendices E–J).

The authors state that code and trained SAEs are available in the repository named in the abstract; availability was not independently checked.

## Evidence and main results

All structural statistics use a disjoint 1.5B-token validation set; each analyzed layer has 128 experts and routes each token to eight (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Contributions concentrate more than gating | At layer 30, the largest ranked contribution averages 0.512 versus 0.231 for the largest gating weight; the smallest averages 0.005 versus 0.074. Rankings are computed separately. Similar curve shapes occur at layers 4 and 46. | §4.1; Figure 2 |
| Most latents have few dominators | With threshold τ = 0.2, exactly one expert dominates 57.4%, 62.1%, and 56.7% of latents at layers 4/30/46; one or two dominate 89.9%, 85.1%, and 92.7%. | §4.2; Figure 6 |
| Overlap is sparse and uneven | Across 8,128 expert pairs per layer, 37.8%/40.5%/37.5% share no latent. The top 10% of pairs by Jaccard carry 83.8%/78.5%/81.0% of total intersection at layers 4/30/46. | Table 1 |
| Selected experts have enriched subfield themes | Layer-30 GPT-5.5 theme shares are Java 86.7%, front-end 78.6%, geometry 78.1%, and arithmetic/polynomial 59.1%; corresponding random samples from other experts score 1.9%, 3.5%, 2.0%, and 6.7%. Expert samples contain up to 2,000 latents; each random baseline contains 2,000. | §5.2; Table 2 |
| Automated domains have unequal expert counts | Computing contains 28 experts and mathematics/reasoning 20, together 37.5% of layer 30. Text form, language, and entities/identifiers together contain 36 experts, or 28.1%. | §5.3; Figure 5 |
| Strongly overlapping pairs usually share a domain | Of the 20 layer-30 pairs with highest Jaccard similarity, 19 share an automated domain, versus 12.8% of all pairs. | §5.3 |
| SAE reconstruction is imperfect | Fraction of variance unexplained is 12.6%, 25.1%, and 7.6% at layers 4/30/46. Each SAE has 1,048,576 latents and trains on about 3.2B tokens. | Appendix I; Table 4 |

These results support concentrated **positive contribution within the learned SAE representation**, with selected expert sets showing coherent semantic enrichment. The exact algebraic decomposition does not make the normalized positive-only score a complete accounting of signed effects. Nor do observational attribution and automated labels establish what expert removal would causally change.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** SAE reconstruction errors, feature splitting, and absorption can distort the recovered specialization (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Results cover one model and a researcher-selected corpus. Qwen3’s undisclosed training mixture prevents testing the proposed link between training-domain proportions and expert allocation (Appendix A).
- **Scope:** Semantic results are reported only for layer 30; negative contributions are excluded because activation-conditioned observations would bias their measurement (§§3.3, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Semantic validation depends heavily on GPT-5.5, which both explains features and judges theme membership. No independent human evaluation or alternate-judge comparison is reported (§5; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** No sensitivity analysis is reported for dominance threshold, SAE size, sparsity, or training seed. Coarse grouping also constrains the number of domains, so its taxonomy is not entirely unconstrained (Appendices G, I).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported number of negative terms truncated per example does not by itself quantify how much truncation changes contribution concentration (§4.1). Expert intervention experiments are proposed rather than demonstrated (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Does dominance predict causal effects?** The paper proposes this use without testing it (§6). Ablate or edit individual experts and measure changes in dominated versus other latents and downstream behavior.
- **How stable are dominant sets?** SAE imperfections and a single threshold leave robustness unresolved (Appendix A; §4.2). Repeat across SAE seeds, sizes, sparsities, and dominance thresholds.
- **Do semantic labels survive independent validation?** Automated interpretation and judging lack reported independent checks (§5). Compare blinded human annotations and another judge on sampled explanations and expert themes.
- **Does training composition determine expert allocation?** The relevant Qwen3 mixture is unavailable (Appendix A). Train comparable MoEs with controlled domain mixtures and compare their recovered expert allocations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the structural statistics and reported automated theme enrichment; broader semantic and causal conclusions are less secure.

Material missing information includes independent interpretation accuracy, confidence/abstention distributions, sensitivity analyses, causal interventions, cross-model replication, uncertainty estimates, exact corpus subset identifiers, and Qwen3’s pretraining mixture.
