# Tools Need Not Speak in Text: Latent Context Compression for Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06500) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06500)


## One-sentence takeaway
Coca-Cola replaces textual tool observations with action-trained latent tokens, reducing total agent tokens by 70.01%–89.24% and runtime by 24.45%–55.97% while improving answer EM and F1 over uncompressed agents in every tested setting (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Tool-using agents accumulate lengthy, redundant observations that increase computation and may distract subsequent reasoning. The authors argue that pruning and summarization remain constrained by discrete text, while existing latent compression generally prioritizes reconstructing text rather than preserving information needed for agent decisions (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Context-conditioned latent interface:** An encoder processes the question, current reasoning state, and tool observation. An adapter produces continuous vectors in the agent’s input embedding space, replacing each observation with a fixed latent sequence (§3.3).
- **Action-Preserving Training:** The compressor learns through KL alignment between the frozen agent’s full-observation and latent-observation next-action distributions, plus cross-entropy supervision on recorded actions. Only encoder LoRA parameters and the adapter are updated; observation reconstruction is unnecessary (§3.4).
- **Capacity motivation and empirical evaluation:** A finite-precision codebook argument motivates greater capacity per input position, subject to explicit assumptions. Experiments compare decision-oriented compression with textual methods and a reconstruction-trained version of the same architecture (§§3.2, 4.3–5).

The authors claim priority for latent compression of tool outputs; the supplied text alone cannot independently establish that priority.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Concrete Qwen efficiency result | NQ: average input tokens fall from 14,362.12 to 1,766.53; runtime falls from 96.19 to 64.64 seconds, versus Vanilla. | Table 1 |

| Answer quality improves over uncompressed agents | Higher EM and F1 in all eight model–dataset settings; best scores among evaluated methods on 13/16 metric comparisons and both metrics in six/eight settings. | Table 3; §5.2 |
| Strong gains, with exceptions among compression baselines | GLM TriviaQA F1: 59.41 versus Vanilla 44.57 and strongest baseline LLMSummary 49.29. Qwen TriviaQA: ICAE exceeds Coca-Cola on EM and F1; Qwen 2Wiki: LongLLMLingua exceeds its F1. | Table 3 |
| Latent budget affects quality | On GLM NQ, separately trained budgets from 8 to 128 tokens increase F1 from 26.34% to 31.45%; total-token reduction is 78.83%–82.56% for budgets up to 64, versus 73.73% at 128. | §5.3; Figure 3 |

These results support an efficiency–accuracy benefit within the tested web-search setup. The adapted ICAE comparison supports decision-oriented training over reconstruction training within the shared architecture. It does not isolate the separate effects of KL alignment and action supervision. Reduced distraction is the authors’ proposed explanation for accuracy gains, rather than a directly measured mechanism (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated qualification:** The capacity argument assumes distinguishable finite-precision coordinates and realizable coordinate combinations; usable information still depends on encoder and agent capabilities (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evaluation covers web-search QA with capped search attempts. Applicability to other tools is asserted from the architecture, but not experimentally demonstrated (§4.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited ablations:** Budget and conditioning studies use only GLM NQ; no separate KL-only or cross-entropy-only results are supplied (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Baselines operate at different achieved compression rates. The results therefore do not establish superiority at matched token budgets (Tables 1–3).
- **Analyst inference—resource accounting:** The agent uses one GPU at inference, with an additional GPU hosting the compressor where used. Runtime gains do not establish lower total GPU-seconds, energy, or deployment cost (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and statistical uncertainty:** Evaluation sample sizes, repeated-run variability, detailed search configuration, adapter internals, and training-data provenance are unavailable. Validation is split by observation–action pairs; task-level separation is unspecified (Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does decision-oriented compression transfer to other tools?** Only web search is evaluated. A next experiment could train and test the interface on coding or structured-tool observations, measuring task success and runtime.
2. **Which training component drives the benefit?** Reconstruction is compared with the combined objective, but its components are not isolated. Compare KL-only, action-supervision-only, and combined training under matched budgets.
3. **Does next-action preservation retain evidence needed much later?** Training targets subsequent actions, and inference permits limited searches. Test longer trajectories containing evidence that becomes relevant only after several intervening steps.
4. **How robust is the quality–efficiency trade-off?** Budget ablation covers one model–dataset setting. Repeat budget sweeps across all benchmarks and compare methods at matched token and hardware budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported benchmark outcomes, but statistical reliability and broader applicability remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes training-task sources and benchmark overlap, evaluation splits and sample counts, model-pair role assignments, adapter construction, training duration, baseline tuning, search backend and timing protocol, uncertainty estimates, and availability of trained checkpoints and training data.
