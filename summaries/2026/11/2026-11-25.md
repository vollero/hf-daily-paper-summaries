# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-25

## Executive summary

- **Development scores can help select an artifact and still overstate its performance.** On OWE-Bench (`2611.30017`), selected submissions averaged 18 normalized points higher on provided cases than on hidden cases where both could be scored. Coding4Games (`2611.31974`) also separates development from independent testing of frozen artifacts, with mixed results in same-model comparisons.
- **Recognizing a rule and executing it are distinct capabilities.** In ICLWorld’s simulated robot video-rule ablation (`2611.32649`), GPT-6 Astra answered 98.0% of pre-action rule questions correctly and completed 19.6% of the corresponding tasks. EEGJev (`2611.32100`) likewise separates identifying the requested attribute from decoding its value, using a different task and metric.
- **Weight sharing changes storage, execution cost, and quality in different ways.** LoopedWAM (`2611.25595`) supports several action budgets from one checkpoint per benchmark. Dooop (`2611.31977`) reports lower per-image FLOPs at adjustable depth. LoopDVR (`2611.32067`) reduces parameters at fixed execution depth, with latency rising in several configurations.
- **Controlled visual tests reveal failures that aggregate scores can hide.** GRAPE-Bench (`2611.33020`) reports ranking reversals between synthetic and naturally degraded driving video. ZoomTrace (`2611.29150`) loses calibrated coverage on external slides, and RoboDojo-Mirror (`2611.33010`) reports lower aggregate manipulation success under every tested mirror configuration.
- **The choice of output metric changes what counts as progress.** Escriba-OCR (`2611.29874`) lowers character error while still normalizing many flagged words. The caption-metric audit (`2611.30488`) finds that duplicate exposure affected Pearl more than DENEB on Polaris, and that detecting the lowest-scored captions accounts for much of the headline correlation.
- **Several training methods make the reference behavior explicit.** RECAP (`2611.31258`) scores compacted-context continuations against the same policy with more history. InstructReplay (`2611.32918`) and CoT-Proxy (`2611.33148`) anchor specialized training to outputs from the initial student.
- **Physical motion needs its own evaluation.** Point Dynamics (`2611.25946`) reports lower tracked object-motion error with simulated scene-point conditioning. On PhyCoVE (`2611.29184`), the best of seven general-purpose editors reproduced about one seventh of the measured counterfactual change.

## Main results worth noting

- **`2611.25403` — Fixed-budget fraud intervention.** In the randomized production study, Antom reported a 37.88% relative reduction in pooled payment loss rate versus XGB-Online. Both arms operated at a 0.5% intervention budget. The supplied excerpt gives neither the absolute arm rates nor their uncertainty.
- **`2611.30017` — Hidden engineering scores.** For submissions with comparable development and hidden cases, the same scoring rule yielded an average gap of 18 normalized points in favor of provided cases. Post-run forecasts exceeded observed hidden scores by 21 points on average.
- **`2611.32649` — Rule knowledge versus execution.** In ICLWorld’s simulated robot video-rule ablation, GPT-6 Astra achieved 98.0% rule-quiz accuracy and 19.6% corresponding task completion. Those figures measure different stages of the same protocol.
- **`2611.29874` — Transcription fidelity.** On 199 BRESSAY pages, Escriba reduced CER to 2.06% from the strongest zero-shot baseline’s 5.60%. Separately, 60% of dictionary-flagged reference words were replaced by dictionary words, a measure whose interpretation depends on reference quality.
- **`2611.27404` — Controlled editing load.** Comparing higher with lower replacement density in synthetic passages, all seven models showed poorer synchronization and higher misfire odds. Correct rule execution on aligned rule words responded differently across models.
- **`2611.32639` — Targeted robot practice.** ReFocus improved mean success versus StructRL from 96.2% to 97.8% on LIBERO-Long and from 45.8% to 51.3% on RoboCasa365 Composite-Seen.
- **`2611.32918` — Retention during factual fine-tuning.** InstructReplay reduced average forgetting from 28.07 to 2.44 percentage points versus standard fine-tuning across five benchmarks and six model–domain settings, with similar mean factual acquisition.
- **`2611.31977` and `2611.32067` — Two weight-sharing trade-offs.** Dooop’s two-loop SD3-medium student slightly exceeded its teacher on the reported four-metric average at about 58% of per-image FLOPs. On COCO val2017, LoopDVR used 23–29% fewer parameters than matched EdgeCrafter models at fixed execution depth, with small detection and pose AP losses and unchanged or improved mask AP. These comparisons measure different aspects of cost and quality.

## Open research directions

The following are proposed experiments motivated by the supplied results.

1. **Measure when engineering development scores generalize.** For `2611.30017` and `2611.31974`, freeze the selection rule before testing and give each agent the same total development budget. Compare score-selected artifacts with a prespecified submission baseline across fresh repeats and withheld scenario variants. Track rank agreement and the development-to-test gap.
2. **Locate the bottleneck between understanding and execution.** For `2611.32649` and `2611.26444`, independently supply the correct rule interpretation and a validated primitive controller in a factorial experiment on matched simulated tasks. Score the next correct decision, intermediate progress, and final completion to estimate how much each intervention recovers.
3. **Test whether spatial foresight helps with reflections.** For `2611.30963` and `2611.33010`, train matched policies with and without aligned transition supervision, then test on paired mirror-free, reflective, and matte-control layouts where available. Measure task completion and empty grasps to test whether any gain is specific to reflections.
4. **Measure deployed cost across compute budgets.** For `2611.25595`, `2611.31977`, `2611.32067`, and `2611.31184`, sweep the method’s execution budgets on fixed hardware and include preprocessing, feature caching, and output decoding. Report end-to-end latency, peak memory, and task-specific quality at each budget.
5. **Test whether tracking fidelity improves action choice.** For `2611.25946` and `2611.25980`, compare point-motion conditioning and action-effect supervision on matched held-out robot perturbations. Evaluate both object-track error and whether model predictions correctly rank candidate actions by executed outcomes.
6. **Measure retention outside the replay distribution.** For `2611.31258`, `2611.32918`, and `2611.33148`, compare policies with and without retention losses at matched training budgets across independent seeds. Evaluate specialized tasks and separately held-out behaviors, including trajectories requiring multiple context compactions where applicable.
7. **Calibrate visual reliability on independent sources.** For `2611.29150`, `2611.32816`, and `2611.33020`, freeze thresholds and model selection on development data before testing on external slides, uncontrolled captures, or naturally degraded clips. Report coverage and accepted-case errors where abstention is supported, alongside per-condition accuracy or geometric error.
8. **Validate transcription fidelity against adjudicated originals.** For `2611.29874` and `2611.32617`, have independent reviewers annotate learner spellings, edits, formulas, and table structure on matched clean and degraded images. Score ordinary recognition errors and meaning-changing edits separately to see which improvements survive adjudication.
