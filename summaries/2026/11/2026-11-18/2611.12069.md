# MPEG-Diffusion: Mixed-Resolution Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12069) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12069)


## One-sentence takeaway

By selecting where to spend high-resolution compute during denoising, MPEG-Diffusion processes 30% of full-resolution tokens and reports 4.8× faster denoising on Wan2.1 14B at 720p on one NVIDIA B200, with a DOVER score 0.025 below full resolution (§5.1, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Video diffusion transformers spend compute uniformly across a frame, and attention cost grows quadratically with token count. Generating only important regions at high resolution (HR) can cut that cost, but the authors identify three obstacles in prior methods: shared attention changes how regions influence one another, separate decoding leaves visible seams, and the HR region must be specified before a recognizable subject has emerged (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

MPEG-Diffusion starts with the whole video at low resolution (LR), selects and upscales an HR region partway through denoising, then finishes with both resolutions and decodes a single HR video (§4, Fig. 2).

- **Dual-resolution attention.** Each query attends to a full set of keys and values at its own resolution, with learned linear maps filling missing positions from the other resolution. Proposition 1 shows that, under its exact equality assumptions, a single replacement token can preserve shared softmax attention only if the original keys coincide, the replacement value averages the original values, and the logit bias accounts for token count (§§3.1, 4.1, Appendix C).
- **Mixed-resolution decoder.** A learned upscaler and a mask-guided 3D convolutional mixer build a unified latent before VAE decoding. The mixer preserves the HR latent and predicts a correction on the LR side of the boundary (§4.2, Figs. 5–6).
- **Self-selected HR region.** A linear head reads intermediate diffusion-transformer features to choose the HR region from a text prompt during generation. The reported setup uses a head trained on subject masks derived from Qwen2.5 and Grounded SAM. The authors also describe a self-supervised alternative based on perceptual reconstruction error (§4.3, Appendix G).

The results support a better quality–compute trade-off at the tested settings. The attention ablation isolates the K/V design, and the real-clip decoder test isolates boundary effects from denoising errors. Equivalence to full-resolution quality remains unestablished, and the headline speedups measure denoising time. The mask results measure agreement with automatic subject labels, leaving agreement with human attention open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Linear K/V mappings constrain what can be reconstructed across resolutions. Sharp HR detail can still stand out against blurry LR regions. The reported selector depends on subject labels produced by an LLM and Grounded SAM (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** The main video comparison changes both region selection and scheduling. Prior mixed-resolution baselines use predefined moving circles at every step, while MPEG-Diffusion selects a subject region after an LR-only phase. The full-method comparison therefore leaves the selector’s independent contribution to perceived quality unresolved (§5 Baselines, §§5.1, 5.3).
- **Analyst inference.** One fixed-seed sample per prompt leaves seed sensitivity unmeasured. Single-B200 timing limits hardware generalization, and the prompt-blind GPT-5.6 image comparison addresses visual quality rather than prompt adherence (§5 Evaluation, Appendix I).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does automatic region selection improve perceived quality at a fixed cost?** The paper measures mask overlap, while the main baseline comparison also changes scheduling. Hold the model, decoder, and schedule fixed, then compare predicted masks, predefined circles, and independently annotated subject masks at matched token budgets (§§5.1, 5.3).
2. **Can nonlinear K/V maps recover more detail within the same runtime budget?** The reported maps are linear, and the authors identify this as a structural constraint. Compare linear and compact nonlinear maps at matched denoising latency using seam errors and blind preference judgments (§4.1, §6).
3. **Can a selector trained without subject labels handle scenes with several equally important regions?** The reported selector learns a main-subject target, and the paper gives no separate multi-subject evaluation. Compare object-supervised, perceptual-label, and end-to-end selectors on multi-subject prompts using independent human region judgments and matched compute (§4.3, §6, Appendix G).
