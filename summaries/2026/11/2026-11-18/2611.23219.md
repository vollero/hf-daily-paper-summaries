# Do LLM Agents Still Need to Be Told How to Self-Improve?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23219)


## One-sentence takeaway

Three of six frozen LLMs improved at held-out puzzle gameplay by designing their own practice and editing player context without a prescribed learning procedure, and Claude Opus-5’s learned context also improved performance across other game engines, horizons, and representations (§§4.1, 5.1).

## Problem and motivation

Self-improvement systems often delegate task generation or reflection to an LLM while still prescribing how it should select practice tasks, evaluate attempts, and update the learner. The authors ask whether an agent can organize those steps itself without access to human-authored practice games or external validation scores. Gameplay with hidden rules gives them a controlled way to test this capability under exploration, planning, and resource constraints (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled test of self-organized learning.** A frozen teacher and player are separate instances of the same LLM. The teacher can generate games, inspect player scores and transcripts, and revise the player’s prompt and auxiliary files. The researchers specify the goal, tools, and budgets, while the teacher chooses how to use them. Fresh player sessions test the resulting context on held-out games (§3, Algorithm 1).
- **Characterization of emergent strategies.** The authors combine behavioral measurements with an adapted self-regulated learning rubric. Successful teachers more often run A/B tests, generate fewer trivial games, and revisit challenging games (§4.2, Figs. 5–8, App. C.6).
- **Tests of transfer and specialization.** The authors evaluate transfer across game settings and between two models, and vary the information available to the teacher to test when it helps or narrows transfer (§5, Figs. 9–11, Table 1).

The held-out comparisons support gains from changing player context under the tested conditions. GEPA provides a comparison at a matched rollout budget, but its access to human-authored games and its prompt-only optimization leave the effect of the learning procedure itself unresolved. The strategy analysis establishes associations, with causal effects and transfer beyond gameplay still open. The text reports win-rate gains with `%` and leaves ambiguous whether those figures denote relative gains or percentage-point changes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Self-regulation scores do not establish which behaviors caused improvement. Models may draw on prior knowledge of games and curriculum design, and PuzzleScript may provide cues about the evaluation distribution. The Python ablation reduces concern about dependence on that engine. The authors also report diminishing returns at larger teacher budgets (§§5.1, 7, App. D.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Benchmark selection limits interpretation of the gains. Both PuzzleScript splits and the ARC-AGI-3 subset exclude levels solved on every screening rollout by default-context Opus-5. The reported gains therefore apply to filtered sets with room for that model to improve. A separate saturated PuzzleScript sample remained near ceiling (Apps. B, D.4).
- **Analyst inference.** Cross-environment transfer is centered on Opus-5, and cross-model transfer covers one model pair on one benchmark. GPT 6-Astra also judges the self-regulation traces, including those of its own model, so sensitivity to judge choice remains unclear (§§5.1–5.3, App. C.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Which teacher behaviors cause gains?** A/B testing and difficulty calibration co-occur with successful learning (§4.2). Randomize access to default-context comparison rollouts across teacher runs and measure held-out gains under the same gameplay budget.
2. **Do the gains extend beyond games where models may have strong priors?** All held-out evaluations are game-based (§7). Test the same protocol on unfamiliar interactive planning tasks with newly designed rules, and evaluate on independently authored environments.
3. **What causes the plateau with more practice?** Performance levels off as budgets grow, leaving the roles of context size and teacher decisions unresolved (§7, App. D.3). Vary update budget and context size independently, and compare teacher-selected consolidation with unrestricted revision.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The text supports the qualitative findings and describes the main protocol. It omits numerical cells from Tables 1–4 and most plotted values, leaving exact baselines, cross-model gain sizes, per-seed scores, and benchmark rollout counts unavailable. The appendices show the rubric and selected artifact excerpts, but availability of complete run logs, final artifact bundles, and executable evaluation code is unclear.
