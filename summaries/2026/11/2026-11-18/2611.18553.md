# WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18553) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18553)


## One-sentence takeaway

At WeightBench’s default difficulty and fixed seed, the strongest LLM agents diagnosed small networks and synthesized working control policies without running them, while language-model repair, data-free compression, and infeasibility judgments remained difficult (§5, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Prior demonstrations showed that LLMs can read, repair, and construct neural networks by operating directly on their weights. The authors identify a gap in measuring how reliably those abilities transfer across tasks, architectures, and model sizes. Unrestricted execution can turn a weight-analysis task into trial and error, while a successful target edit can conceal damage to other capabilities (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark.** WeightBench registers 42 tasks across five tracks, spanning 2K-parameter control policies to 7B-parameter language models. It includes non-agentic baselines and static reference solutions (§4).
- **Controlled evaluation.** A CPU sandbox meters task-permitted probes and forward passes, records audit events in a hash-chained ledger, and assigns quarantined runs zero pending review. Procedural regeneration and weight transformations aim to reduce memorization of parameter coordinates (§§3.1, 3.3, 5.1, Appendix E).
- **Scoring and comparison.** The composite score combines task gain with penalties for collateral damage, edit size, and compute use. The authors compare five agents across the tracks and report a shared-task macro score for the agent with partial coverage (§3.2, Table 1).

## Evidence and main results

All agent scores below are composite \(S_{\mathrm{WB}}\) at default difficulty and seed 0. Macro scores weight the five track means equally.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Opus led the full benchmark, while GPT led the shared subset | Across 42 tasks, macro scores were Opus 0.535, Sonnet 0.466, Haiku 0.402, and DeepSeek 0.215. Across 38 shared tasks excluding four reasoning-loop repairs, GPT scored 0.568 and Opus 0.546. | Table 1, §5.3 |
| GPT and Opus scored highest on diagnosis and synthesis | On 10 READ tasks, GPT scored 0.928 and Opus 0.744. On six WRITE tasks, GPT scored 0.863 and Opus 0.829. The authors report working game-playing weights synthesized without execution. | Table 1, §§5, 5.3 |
| Surgical repair remained difficult | On the four reasoning-loop repair tasks, Opus scored above zero on three and Sonnet on one. GPT skipped these tasks. The authors report that agents fell well short of a gradient-fitted language-model repair. | Table 1, §§5, 5.3 |
| Infeasibility detection and compression scored poorly | On five META tasks, Claude track means were 0.491–0.500, which the authors characterize as near random guessing, and GPT scored 0.260. On eight SYSTEMS tasks, agent means ranged from 0.177 to 0.238. | Table 1, §5.3 |
| Most tasks met the reference validity gate | At default difficulty, 39 of 42 tasks met the gate of reference \(S_{\mathrm{WB}}\geq0.5\) and a margin of at least 0.2 over the naive baseline. References for 21 of 42 tasks used only agent-accessible inputs. | §5.2 |
| The audit caught most scripted violations | The audit quarantined 28 of 29 scripted cheat scenarios in all three trials each. The OS sandbox blocked the remaining scenario, and the audit flagged none of the 90 honest control trials. | Appendix D |

The results demonstrate successful weight construction under the benchmark’s access restrictions and show large score differences across tracks. They leave open how rankings vary across seeds and difficulty levels and how much of the score gap reflects agent capability, scaffolding, or quarantine. Privileged references demonstrate that an edit exists within the weights while leaving its discoverability under agent restrictions open (§5.2, Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Three tasks fall below the reference validity gate. Half the references use privileged access. Unreviewed quarantines count as zero, so the authors describe the reported scores as lower bounds under their review procedure (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference.** One seed and one difficulty level leave robustness to procedural regeneration and difficulty scaling unmeasured. Unequal sampling, a scaffold switch in 10 of GPT’s 38 runs, and the 80-turn cap reported for GPT and DeepSeek limit attribution of differences to the underlying models (§§3.3, 5.1, Table 1).
- **Analyst inference.** Scripted audit controls establish behavior on the tested scenarios. The false-positive rate on actual agent runs remains unresolved while quarantines await review (§5.1, Appendix D).

## Open research questions

- **Do track rankings persist across regenerated tasks?** The reported runs use one seed and difficulty. Repeat the comparison across multiple seeds and difficulty levels with matched scaffolds and sample counts.
- **How much of the repair and compression gap comes from reference access?** Some references use privileged inputs, and the supplied results give no task-level breakdown by access. Compare agents and references under identical inputs and budgets, then measure the incremental effect of calibration access.
- **Can agents distinguish infeasible edits from difficult solvable edits?** META means do not reveal which cases drive the errors. Evaluate on matched pairs with verified solvable decoys and report error rates for each class.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported benchmark design and default-setting scores. The supplied text lacks task-level agent and reference scores, breakdowns of composite score components, uncertainty estimates, results across seeds or difficulty levels, and outcomes of manual quarantine review. The quantitative comparison against the gradient-fitted repair is also unavailable.
