# AuXSpec: Learning to Autoformalize Program Specifications via Executable Postconditions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21421) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21421)


## One-sentence takeaway

AuXSpec uses executable counterparts to declarative Lean postconditions to train specification generators with test feedback, raising the 27B model’s SpecGen pass@4 from 14.3% to 84.3% on the held-out AuXSpec-Test benchmark (§5.2, Table 4).

## Problem and motivation

Formal verification can show that an implementation satisfies a specification, so its value depends on whether that specification captures what the user intended. Writing specifications in Lean requires expertise, and the authors identify a training bottleneck in checking generated specifications at scale. Prior Lean approaches can require a separate proof for each test case, with some cases unresolved when proof search fails (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Executable specification format.** AuXSpec pairs a declarative Lean 4 postcondition with a Boolean counterpart. It uses Lean’s `decide` when a computable decidability instance is available. Otherwise, it generates a separate executable definition and proves equivalence on valid inputs after synthesis. The authors apply this established formal-methods pattern to scalable model training (§§2, 3.1, 6).
- **Synthesis pipeline and benchmark.** A staged pipeline generates and repairs specifications and implementations against frozen positive and negative tests and LLM review. The authors report releasing 3,037 LeetCode formalizations with equivalence proofs, including 300 expert-reviewed AuXSpec-Test problems with implementation correctness proofs (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Training recipe and model weights.** The authors combine supervised fine-tuning and expert iteration with RL that gives partial credit across validation stages and trains repairs on failed model outputs. They report releasing the 9B and 27B RL weights for specification and implementation generation and repair (§§1, 4.1–4.3).

The results establish higher acceptance rates under the reported evaluators and show transfer to Verina when its evaluation tests are absent from the original prompts. The Verina comparison with VeriSpecGen uses previously reported results, and its pass@10 score uses a different sampling budget from AuXSpec’s pass@4. Equivalence proofs establish agreement between two formal predicates on inputs admitted by the generated precondition. Faithfulness to the stated problem still depends on tests and review, and generated implementations generally lack correctness proofs.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Expert review covered a small sample of accepted model outputs and used a single reviewer for benchmark references. The training corpus lacks a comparable expert audit. Validity predicates receive limited test coverage, and review found missing constraints even among accepted outputs (Appendices I–J).
- **Author-stated.** The main benchmark evaluates single-round repair. The iterative ICPC-Eval comparison covers a filtered subset that excludes problems with multiple correct outputs or test cases beyond the AuXSpec-Test length range (§5.2, Appendices D.4 and J).
- **Analyst inference.** The SFT-to-RL comparison measures the effect of the combined RL recipe, including staged rewards and repair training. The paper cites recipe ablations and a shared-backbone comparison, but their results are absent from the excerpt, leaving the contribution of individual components uncertain (§4.3, Appendix F references).

## Open research questions

1. **Can explicit constraint checking reduce precondition errors?** Every error in the accepted-output audit involved a missing constraint. Compare the current evaluator with a constraint-focused judge and boundary tests, then have blinded experts review the accepted outputs (Appendices I.2 and J).
2. **How much of the RL gain comes from executing postconditions?** The reported headline comparison changes the full training recipe. Train matched models with and without execution feedback while holding data, judge feedback, repair sampling, and compute fixed. Score them with the same evaluator and expert audit (§§4.3, 5.4).
3. **Does the approach work when several outputs are valid?** The training filter and ICPC-Eval subset exclude such problems. Build a held-out set with independently reviewed output validators and test whether the reward learns to accept distinct valid outputs (Appendices C.1 and D.4).
