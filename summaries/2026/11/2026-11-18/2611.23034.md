# M3OPD-Reasoner: An Open Recipe for Post-Training Multimodal Reasoning with Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23034) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23034)


## One-sentence takeaway

Distilling three domain teachers into a single Qwen3.5-9B student and then applying mixed-domain reinforcement learning on the same prompts raised mean avg@8 accuracy from 75.2% to 75.9% across 21 image and video benchmarks (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks how one vision-language model can acquire image STEM, image perception, and video reasoning skills without sacrificing performance in individual domains. The authors argue that mixed-domain reinforcement learning (MixRL) must both consolidate skills and improve them, while prior multimodal distillation recipes generally stop at distillation. They test whether a separate reward-driven stage can still improve a distilled student using the prompts it has already seen (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Three-domain post-training recipe.** Train a Qwen3.5-9B teacher per domain with Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), with or without supervised fine-tuning (SFT). A fresh student generates its own responses, and the frozen teacher selected by each prompt’s domain scores those responses token by token. Distillation minimizes a top-32 reverse-KL-style loss without a task reward (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Comparison on shared training data.** Compare mixed SFT and MixRL with multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD), then apply MixRL to both distilled students using the same prompt union and GSPO settings as the mixed-training runs. The authors identify this separate post-distillation RL stage as the novel step for vision-language models (§§2, 3.5, 4.1).
- **Training assets.** The authors report releasing M3OPD-SFT-1.35M, M3OPD-RL-193K, training code, and checkpoints for every stage (§§1, 3.1, 6).

The shared internal evaluation supports an incremental benefit from reusing the same prompts for MixRL after distillation in both tested branches. Gains were uneven across individual benchmarks, and the authors report no sign of entropy collapse in the observed training curves. The comparisons leave statistical reliability and compute-matched superiority unresolved (§§4.1–4.2, §5, Figure 6, Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported qualifications.** The final model trails Video-MOPD on average over their shared video benchmarks. The prompt-filter study covers only the branch with an SFT warm-up for teachers (§4.2, §5).
- **Analyst inference — generalization and compute.** All internal runs use Qwen3.5-9B and train to convergence. Shared prompts and GSPO settings support recipe comparisons, but the supplied text gives no equal-total-compute comparison or across-seed uncertainty estimates for the small gains (§4.1, Table 3, Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference — reasoning measures.** The behavior analysis counts surface features such as timestamp citations on the first sampled response per question. Citation density alone cannot establish whether the cited moments support the answer (§5, Table 5).

## Open research questions

- **Does the sequence still lead at matched compute?** The reported runs train to convergence. Compare MOPD followed by MixRL with MixRL alone under the same total training budget, including teacher training and scoring, and repeat across seeds.
- **How much does reusing the prompts matter?** The filtering study varies subsets of the existing pool. From the same MOPD checkpoint, compare reused prompts with fresh, domain- and difficulty-matched prompts under the same rollout budget.
- **Does shorter video reasoning preserve evidence quality?** The paper measures timestamp density and answer accuracy. Have blinded annotators assess whether cited moments support the answers before and after final MixRL.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the recipe and internal benchmark scores shown in Table 3. The supplied text omits the table entries for several supporting analyses, including the exact published-model comparisons, prompt-filter scores and subset sizes, and full training configurations. It also provides no across-seed uncertainty or per-stage compute totals. The release is reported by the authors.
