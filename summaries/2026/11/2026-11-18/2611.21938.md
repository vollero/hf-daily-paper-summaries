# HubEgo-100K: In-the-Wild Egocentric Video of Skilled Work for Robot Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21938) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21938)


## One-sentence takeaway

HubEgo-100K v1.0 releases 10,124 hours of unscripted first-person work video with synchronized phone sensors and machine-generated annotations, and measures dataset diversity ahead of downstream robot-policy evaluation (Table 2, §§5, 8).

## Problem and motivation

Imitation-trained robot policies struggle with unfamiliar environments and objects, and collecting robot demonstrations across many settings is costly. The authors identify a gap in egocentric data for unscripted skilled work across trades, crafts, and services. Much prior footage centers on daily life, kitchens, scripted tasks, or factory and manual labor collections (§§1–2, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Capture and curation.** Contributors used head-mounted phones to record named, unscripted tasks with their own tools and materials. The pipeline keeps on-task segments, gates on hand visibility, applies privacy screening, checks capture integrity, and rebalances hours across task types (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Dataset and documentation.** The CC BY 4.0 release packages monocular video, frame-aligned accelerometer and gyroscope readings, native MCAP streams, and episode-level action, difficulty, and language annotations. It includes a LeRobot v3 layout, catalog, and datasheet (§§3.3–3.6, Appendix A).
- **Dataset analysis.** The authors quantify diversity and evenness, action-family coverage across trades, and vocabulary accumulation within the release. They use prior research to motivate which properties to measure (Table 3, §5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Release scale and coverage | v1.0 contains 10,124 hours of 1080p video across 72,754 episodes, 12,227 contributors, and 15,548 contributor-by-setting-type environments. | Workplace share | Of all v1.0 hours, 82.6% are in workplace settings, including 26.9% of total hours in trades done in homes. The household setting accounts for 17.4%. | §5.3, Fig. 2a |
| Contributor balance | No contributor accounts for more than 0.22% of v1.0 hours. The effective contributor count is 5,666, calculated as the exponential of Shannon entropy over contributor hours. | §5.1, Table 4 |
| Diversity continues to accumulate within the release | The final 10% of hours adds an average of 356 contributors and 570 environment pairs over five random episode orders, and about 39 verb and 84 noun lemmas from generated descriptions over 20 random orders. | §§5.1, 5.5, Figs. 10 and 15 |
| Hand visibility and task focus | Across the curated release, models estimate that at least one hand appears in 94.7% of sampled frames and both hands in 76.3%. Estimated on-task share is 96.9%, weighted by hours. | §5.8, Fig. 2b–c |
| Inertial coverage | All 72,754 episodes include accelerometer and gyroscope streams, with a median native sampling rate of 99.98 Hz measured as samples per episode duration. Readings are interpolated to each frame’s capture time. | §§3.5, 5.9, Fig. 18 |

These measurements characterize the curated release. The shuffled accumulation curves show how coverage builds within a fixed corpus. The paper leaves robot-policy gains, the effect of curation, and the added value of IMU unmeasured. The policy results reviewed in §4 come from prior studies (§8, Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Machine-generated annotations lack a human audit. Hand visibility and on-task share are model estimates, and instruction steps lack individual timestamps (§§3.3, 5.8, Appendix A).
- **Author-reported.** Camera calibration is unavailable, the residual camera–IMU offset is unmeasured, and native sensor rates vary by operating system. The curation and annotation pipeline is unreleased, and the analysis script is available on request (Appendix A).
- **Author-reported.** Task rebalancing changes the prevalence of tasks relative to real-world work. The environment definition counts shared sites separately by contributor and merges sites of the same type for a single contributor (§§3, 7).
- **Analyst inference.** A contributor-disjoint split can still place footage from a shared physical site on both sides. Noun lemmas from generated descriptions are also an indirect proxy for object diversity because vocabulary depends on what the annotation model names (§§3, 5.5, 6).

## Open research questions

- **Does HubEgo-100K improve robot-policy generalization?** The paper reports no training results (§8). Compare policies trained with and without HubEgo pretraining under the same robot-data budget, then measure success on unseen scenes and objects.
- **How much does contributor diversity matter at fixed video hours?** The paper measures contributor coverage without isolating its effect (§§4.5, 5.1). Compare equal-hour, task-matched subsets with different contributor counts using a common held-out evaluation.
- **Does synchronized IMU add value beyond video?** The paper reports sensor coverage and describes potential uses (§§4.6, 5.9, 6). Train video-only and video-plus-IMU models on the same episodes and compare action recognition on the contributor-disjoint split.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the release description and reported descriptive statistics. Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes downstream benchmark results, controlled ablations, human-audited annotation accuracy, physical-site identifiers, contributor demographics, camera calibration, and measured residual camera–IMU offset. The paper describes the preprocessing pipeline but provides neither its implementation nor the exact hand-visibility gating thresholds.
