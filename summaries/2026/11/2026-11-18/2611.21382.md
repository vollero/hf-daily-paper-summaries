# VisualArtifactSpec 1.0: Evaluating Visual Artifacts under Task Specifications

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21382) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21382)


## One-sentence takeaway

VisualArtifactSpec 1.0 evaluates nine model–harness configurations on 211 source-grounded visual authoring tasks and finds that, under its scoring protocol, Artifact Performance accounts for 73.4% of the total-score gap between the highest- and lowest-scoring configurations (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation

Visual authoring agents must turn source materials and specifications into artifacts usable by an intended audience. An artifact can include every required fact or feature while making evidence hard to interpret, text hard to read, or motion misleading. The authors identify a gap in evaluating audience fit and adaptation to the medium systematically across artifact families using a common framework (§1, Table 1, Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark.** The authors construct 211 tasks from 322 candidates across presentations, static graphics, animations and videos, and web interfaces. Tasks cover creation, adaptation, revision, repair, and conversion, with specified sources, audiences, purposes, constraints, and deliverables (§§3.1–3.2, Figure 5).
- **Evaluation protocol.** Task Fulfillment awards binary credit to acceptance groups whose checks all pass. Artifact Performance applies task-specific rating anchors to shared Content, Organization, Visuals, and Delivery criteria. A multimodal judge grounds judgments in source materials and native-file, rendered, temporal, or interaction evidence. The total adds the signed performance adjustment to the fulfillment base (§§3.3–3.4, Figure 6, Table 5).
- **Comparative study.** The authors evaluate nine model–harness configurations on the same tasks, examine family and criterion profiles, test sensitivity to scoring choices, and use matched-fulfillment case studies to identify concrete authoring differences (§§4–5).

The results show that the two scores distinguish requirement satisfaction from contextual authoring quality under the reported rubric. The case studies illustrate specific rating differences, and the scoring sensitivity analysis tests the effect of aggregation with judgments fixed. Agreement with human raters and the independent effects of model, harness, audience, and medium remain untested in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated limitations.** The authors call for human assessment and independent judges to validate judge reliability and contextual ratings (§6). They describe purpose scores as descriptive because task difficulty and artifact-family composition vary across purpose groups (§4.3, Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference.** GPT-6 Astra is both an authoring configuration and the common judge in a separate session. A single-judge design leaves possible preferential scoring unresolved. The supplied results do not demonstrate that it occurred (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Functionality groups apply only to web tasks, so family-level totals reflect differences in both task mix and fulfillment criteria. The temporal mismatch in P121 illustrates a behavior evaluated through Artifact Performance under the current rubric (§3.3, §5.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The observed ordering is robust to the reported aggregation changes for the compared configurations. Its sensitivity to contextual anchors or judge identity remains open because the analysis holds item judgments fixed (§4.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Do independent judges and intended users agree with contextual ratings?** The study uses one common judge. Have blinded domain raters and independent multimodal judges score a stratified sample, then compare criterion-level agreement and configuration rankings.
2. **Do contextual anchors improve assessment of audience and medium fit?** The sensitivity analysis changes aggregation while holding judgments fixed. Score the same artifacts with generic versus task-specific anchors and compare both sets of ratings with audience comprehension and usability measures.
3. **How much of a configuration’s score comes from the model versus the harness?** The reported comparison evaluates their combinations. Cross compatible models and harnesses on matched tasks under a common resource budget, with repeated runs to estimate variability.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the benchmark description and the main descriptive results explicitly reported in the prose. The supplied text omits the body entries of several referenced tables, including the complete configuration scores, criterion weights, and sensitivity results. It points to the repository for exact model identifiers, harness versions, execution details, task-level scores, and reproduction scripts. Human and independent-judge agreement estimates and repeated-run variability are unavailable in the supplied text.
