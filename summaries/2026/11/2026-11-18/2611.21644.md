# MInfoVisQA: A Multilingual Benchmark for Information-Rich Visual Question Answering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21644) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21644)


## One-sentence takeaway

On MInfoVisQA’s matched chart and table questions, all 25 evaluated models scored lower on average with non-English visuals than with English visuals when the question and requested answer language stayed English (§4.3).

## Problem and motivation

Answering questions about charts and tables requires reading labels, linking them to visual structure, and reasoning over values. The authors argue that multilingual evaluations using different examples in each language can confound language effects with differences in content or difficulty. They use matched examples to test whether models recover the same facts when embedded visual text is translated and re-rendered (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Construction pipeline.** An agent-based workflow reconstructs charts as executable Python and parses structured tables or recovers cells and spans from images. Stable label keys synchronize translated visual text with QA wording. Structural and numerical checks, model review, and human inspection assess fidelity and readability (§§3.2–3.3, Appendix F).
- **Parallel benchmark.** MInfoVisQA contains 8,960 QA configurations from 128 seeds, comprising 87 charts and 41 tables across 24 languages. Language-Aligned QA (LQA) matches the visual, question, and answer languages. Cross-Language QA (XQA) fixes QA to English or Chinese and varies the visual language. The authors identify table-specific reconstruction and broader coverage in a shared chart-and-table benchmark as their main extensions to prior multilingual chart QA (§3, Table 1, Appendix F).
- **Model evaluation.** The authors evaluate 25 models and compare semantic answer accuracy, changes from same-language baselines, and how often the same seed stays correct across visual languages (§§4.1–4.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Overall accuracy | GPT-6-Astra leads at 81.9%, followed by Gemini-3.8-Flash at 79.9% and Kimi-K3 at 79.2%. The aggregate equally weights the 70 visual-language and QA-language pairings across LQA and XQA. | §§4.2–4.3, Table 2 summary |
| Similar aligned means hide different language gaps | Gemini-3.8-Flash and Kimi-K3 each reach about 80.4% mean LQA accuracy, but their best-to-worst spreads across the 24 aligned languages are 7.8 and 20.3 percentage points, respectively. | §4.3 |
| Every model declines under fixed-English XQA | All 25 models score lower on average across the 23 non-English visuals than on matched English visuals with English QA. Drops include 1.5 points for Gemini-3.8-Flash, 4.2 for GPT-6-Astra, and 17.8 for Chart-R1. | §4.3 |
| Drops persist for answers that require no translation | On translation-invariant seeds, fixed-English XQA reduces accuracy relative to English LQA by 2.95 points for GPT-6-Astra and 11.15 for MiniMax-M3. | §4.4, Appendix E, Fig. 7 |
| Seed-level coverage changes model ordering | Among ten models, ordering changes as the required coverage rises from correctness in at least one visual language to correctness in all 24. | §4.4, Fig. 4 |
| Judge choice shifts scores | On saved predictions from four answer models, GPT-5.5 increased overall accuracy by 0.95–1.48 points and Qwen3.8-27B changed it by −2.41 to −0.03 points relative to GPT-6-Astra as judge. Both alternatives preserved the four-model ranking. | Appendix D |

The matched comparisons support a benchmark-specific sensitivity to visual localization, even when English QA is fixed and the reference wording needs no translation. LQA changes visual, question, and answer languages together. XQA holds QA language fixed, but translation and rendering still vary together, leaving the contributions of text recognition, label grounding, and reasoning unresolved (§§4.3–4.4, 5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** XQA covers only English and Chinese as fixed QA languages. The two reviewers were native Chinese speakers proficient in English, and review of other languages relied on translation tools. Every configuration received initial inspection, with second review targeted to flags or identified issues. Residual translation or rendering errors may affect comparisons (Limitations, Appendices C and G.2).
- **Author-stated.** Final acceptance reflects the review outcome. Per-round correction counts were not retained, so the repair rate and improvement in correctness cannot be quantified (§3.3, Appendix G.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The 8,960 configurations represent repeated variants of 128 seed questions. Transfer to naturally occurring localized documents remains uncertain, and the subgroup comparisons use different seeds whose reasoning demands may differ (Appendices E–F, §5).
- **Analyst inference.** Chart-R1’s mixed chart-and-table score cannot isolate the effect of chart specialization. The four-model judge check also leaves ranking sensitivity across the full model panel unresolved (§4.3, Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Where does the cross-language accuracy loss occur?** End-to-end scores do not separate text recognition, grounding, and reasoning. On the same seeds, supply verified label transcriptions and then verified label-to-mark or label-to-cell mappings, measuring how much accuracy each intervention recovers (§§4.3, 5).
2. **Do benchmark results transfer to naturally localized documents?** The paper evaluates reconstructed variants. Rerun the paired protocol on naturally published multilingual charts and tables with equivalent content verified by native speakers (§5, Limitations).
3. **How much does fixed QA language affect the measured drop?** English and Chinese XQA use different same-language baselines and exclude different visual languages. Compare them on an identical set of third-language visuals, then extend the same design to additional QA languages (§4.3).
