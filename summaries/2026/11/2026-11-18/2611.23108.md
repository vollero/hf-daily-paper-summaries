# FMT: Lossless Image Compression in a Fixed Number of Parallel Steps

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23108) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23108)


## One-sentence takeaway

FMT pairs a coarse-to-fine pixel schedule with persistent parallel entropy-decoding lanes to bound sequential steps independently of image size, and its 512×4 neural mode decoded Kodak photos 8.8–61× faster than three learned codecs run on the same A100 at comparable or smaller file sizes (§3.4, Table 4, §S6.6).

## Problem and motivation

Photo archives, editing pipelines, and training datasets often need exact pixels and read images repeatedly. The paper identifies two sources of delay in learned lossless decoding: predictors wait for previously decoded pixels, and entropy decoders read values serially. Earlier designs parallelized parts of this process, leaving a serial bottleneck or sacrificing compression gains (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Co-design the pixel schedule and entropy coder.** Start from a sparse grid and fill in centers and edges coarse to fine. Pixels in each step depend only on earlier steps, so the GPU can predict them together. Interleaved rANS lanes retain state across steps, and the number of lanes grows with the image to bound the work assigned to each lane. The contribution is the joint design of these two mechanisms (§§2, 3.1–3.5).
2. **Implement three bit-exact coding modes.** Fast mode uses a reversible integer wavelet and fixed-width fields. Dense mode uses neighbor averages and shared residual histograms. Neural modes use per-channel MLPs to estimate residual probabilities and can store a per-photo model update. Portable kernels and integer cumulative distributions support exact decoding across the GPU setups tested (§§3.3–3.4, S5).
3. **Measure where decoding time goes.** Baseline profiles, lane-length experiments, and comparisons with raster and wavefront schedules isolate the costs of serial entropy decoding and predictor synchronization (§4.4, §§S3.2–S3.4, §S6.6).

The strongest comparisons pair complete-file size with verified pixel reconstruction on the same hardware. They establish a size–speed trade-off for the tested implementations and workloads. Fixed schedule depth bounds dependencies, while wall-clock time still depends on image size, hardware, and setup costs. The JPEG XL comparison spans CPU and GPU, and published learned-codec timings may cover different stages of decoding (§§3.4, 4.1, S6.1, S6.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported limitations.** Neural tuning takes seconds per photo, size-specific tables and buffers can consume substantial memory, and the fixed neural schedule is costly for thumbnails. The larger networks lack a CPU decoder, while portable neural kernels can add latency. Evaluation centers on 8-bit photographs. Higher bit depths require new training and wider frequency tables, and bitstream errors can propagate to finer levels (§5, §§S5, S7).
- **Analyst inference.** The wavefront timing gap depends on kernels designed for FMT’s large steps. The authors acknowledge that a decoder optimized for many small steps could narrow it. The main latency figures also exclude first-use index construction, so workloads with frequently changing dimensions can see different trade-offs (§4.1, §§S3.4, S7.2–S7.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The large-photo latency results have limited coverage because timing uses every fourth validation image, and the authors report that the CLIC.p subset compresses more easily than the full set. Different timing repetitions and protocols also limit the precision of some speedup comparisons (§4.1, §§S6.1, S6.5–S6.6).

## Open research questions

1. **How much latency advantage remains against an optimized wavefront decoder?** The existing comparison uses FMT-oriented kernels. Implement a persistent-kernel wavefront decoder and compare complete-file size, single-image latency, and batched throughput on the same GPU (§S3.4).
2. **Can one-pass adaptation preserve the compression gains of iterative tuning?** The paper proposes a hypernetwork without evaluating one. Compare predicted updates against untuned and gradient-tuned encoding, counting update bytes and total encoding time (§§S4.2–S4.4, S10).
3. **How well does FMT extend to higher-bit-depth images?** The reported photo benchmarks use 8-bit inputs. Train higher-bit-depth models with wider frequency tables and measure exactness, compression, latency, and memory on held-out high-bit-depth images (§5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method and the reported benchmark results within their stated settings. The supplied text omits several tables’ numerical rows, including the full per-codec latency and throughput breakdowns. It announces release of the code, models, streams, and logs, but does not establish their public availability. Matched end-to-end timings for HPAC and CALLIC are also unavailable in the supplied text (§4.1, §§S6.5, S8–S9).
