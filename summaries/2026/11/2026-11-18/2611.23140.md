# GameRealm: Grounding Visual Learning in Interactive Games

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23140) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23140)


## One-sentence takeaway

GameRealm uses synchronized game-engine supervision to train action-responsive video models and improve stereo, reconstruction, and point-tracking results on several external benchmarks (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

Interactive world models need video aligned with controls and audio, while geometry models need calibrated views, depth, and temporal correspondences. The authors argue that existing synthetic and gameplay datasets rarely provide these signals together, limiting their reuse across generation and reconstruction (§1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **GameRealm dataset.** Synchronized RGB, metric depth, and camera parameters span single-view, stereo, multi-view, and panoramic recordings, with audio, controls, and point trajectories where available. Generated captions are stored separately from engine-native annotations (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Acquisition pipeline.** Engine plugins capture timestamped supervision during human-operated gameplay and automated free-roaming exploration. Integrity, motion, and visual quality checks operate on complete view groups (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Model adaptations.** The authors adapt Helios for single-view action control and joint two-view generation, and LTX-2.3 for action-conditioned panoramic video and audio. They also test GameRealm fine-tuning for stereo, reconstruction, and tracking (§§4–5).
- **TrajRealm-Bench.** The proposed benchmark evaluates 2D and 3D tracking across interaction and locomotion, with separate protocols for ground-truth and estimated geometry (§5.4, Tables 9–10).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

Original Helios scored 2.40% with directional text, a reference with different conditioning. | Stereo transfers to real-world benchmarks | Adding GameRealm to public-data fine-tuning improved 12 of 13 metrics across zero-shot Middlebury-H/Q, ETH3D, and KITTI 2015. ETH3D BP-3 fell from 0.49% to 0.42%. | §5.2, Table 7 |

The fixed-model action perturbation establishes that the adapted generator responds directionally to its controls. The stereo and multi-view fine-tuning comparisons support the value of adding GameRealm under the tested recipes. The experiments leave open how much of the transfer comes from dataset scale, game diversity, or particular supervision signals. Panoramic examples leave audio synchronization and long-horizon consistency unquantified.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated boundaries.** Original Helios is a text-conditioned reference for action control (§4.1). Tracking groups use different geometry and scoring protocols, and Track4World averages exclude sequences with non-finite predictions (§5.4, Table 10). The authors identify broader skeleton, segmentation, and instance annotations as future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** Group filtering removes low-motion clips and can discard an entire recording window when one view fails. This may underrepresent tasks where stillness or occlusion matters (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Public tracking comparisons use published baselines, while the stereo and reconstruction comparisons rerun public-data-only fine-tuning. The tracking results therefore provide less direct evidence about the effect of the training data alone (§§5.2–5.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Which properties of the data drive geometry transfer?** The reported fine-tuning comparisons add GameRealm as a bundle. Compare equal-size, equal-update training mixtures that vary game diversity and supervision signals (§§5.2–5.3).
2. **How well does panoramic generation preserve audio timing and spatial consistency over long rollouts?** The reported evidence is qualitative. Use held-out scripted action sequences to measure audiovisual event timing and consistency when revisiting a viewpoint (§4.3).
3. **How much of the tracking gain survives when camera poses and depth are noisy?** TrajRealm-Bench uses different scoring protocols for its geometry groups. Evaluate the same tracker and clips with progressively degraded geometry under a common scoring protocol (§5.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported comparisons and their stated settings. The supplied text leaves public access and licensing for the dataset, benchmark, plugins, and adapted checkpoints unconfirmed. It also omits source-specific filter thresholds, repeat-run uncertainty for the headline gains, and quantitative panoramic audio results. The introduction lists “110M+” frames in its contribution summary, while §3.4 and Table 1 report “120M+.” The scale reported above follows the detailed statistics.
