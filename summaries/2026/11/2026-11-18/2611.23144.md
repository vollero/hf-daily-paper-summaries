# π^4: A Foundation Model for 4D Scene Geometry and Motion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23144) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23144)


## One-sentence takeaway

π⁴ reconstructs dense geometry, camera poses, and metric scale from uncalibrated RGB images and, for multi-image inputs, produces moving-object masks and lifts geometry-conditioned 2D tracks onto reconstructed 3D surfaces in the same forward pass (§3, Fig. 3).

## Problem and motivation

Joint geometry and motion estimation is difficult when images vary in viewpoint, temporal continuity, and scene content. The authors identify a gap in maintaining reconstruction quality while recovering motion across single images, unordered image collections, and videos. They also identify gaps in training coverage for unusual camera configurations and foreground-dominated videos, where moving objects can disrupt background reconstruction (§1, §§4.2–4.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified reconstruction and tracking.** A shared DINOv3 encoder feeds separate looped Transformer decoders. The geometry branch retains π³’s reference-free formulation and adds sparse 3D refinement. During motion training, the geometry branch stays frozen and conditions the motion decoder through spatial adaptive layer normalization (§§3.2–3.5, Fig. 3).
- **Surface-consistent 3D tracking.** The motion head predicts dense, query-view 2D correspondences. Sampling the target view’s reconstructed metric pointmap gives each visible 3D endpoint, enforcing agreement with the predicted surface. A separate head predicts query-independent moving-object masks (§3.4, Fig. 10).
- **Training data and supervision.** The authors combine scene-level rendering with coverage-aware view sampling and sequence-consistent lens augmentation. They also construct geometry pseudo-labels for originally visible background pixels in foreground-dominated videos and use the jointly developed GameRealm dataset for engine-native geometry and persistent point tracks (§§4.2–4.6).

The geometry rank measures performance under the reported benchmark protocols, including aligned depth evaluations. The separately reported metric-scale result and the use of alignment in 3D tracking limit what those rankings establish about absolute metric accuracy. Surface agreement follows from the lifting rule, so a track can agree with the reconstruction even when the correspondence or surface is wrong. Oxford Spires provides quantitative evidence for long-route reconstruction, while the larger joint reconstruction and tracking examples are qualitative (Fig. 1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** A dominant moving foreground or weakly textured background can cause the model to mistake object motion for camera motion. Camera-position errors grow farther from the reconstruction origin. The authors also report an accuracy gap to traditional geometric optimization, particularly on sequences longer than those seen in training (§7).
- **Analyst inference.** The **0.01-percentage-point** single-view AJ lead has no reported uncertainty, so its stability is unclear. Track4World’s single-view average has different coverage because it excludes eight GameRealm clips with nonfinite outputs and is omitted from the ranking (Table 4).
- **Analyst inference.** The reported studies leave the individual contributions of long-view training, camera augmentation, and foreground-suppressed pseudo-labeling to final benchmark performance unquantified (§§4.2–4.4, §§6.1–6.9).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does foreground suppression reduce camera–object motion confusion?** The authors describe remaining failures but provide no controlled breakdown by background texture or foreground dominance. Train matched models with and without pseudo-labels, then compare pose and background reconstruction error across those conditions (§4.4, §7).
2. **How much 3D tracking error comes from correspondence, geometry, or camera pose?** The lifting rule ties these errors together, and aggregate scores do not isolate them. Replace each predicted component with ground truth in turn and measure both aligned and unaligned tracking error (§3.4, Table 4).
3. **What drives pose drift as input sets grow?** The paper reports larger position errors away from the origin and evaluates long reconstruction on one route suite. Compare alternative pose parameterizations and training context mixtures on matched routes across view counts, using an optimization-based baseline (§6.2, §7, Table 3).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and reported benchmark results. The supplied text does not give exact training probabilities for the view-count buckets, uncertainty across repeated runs, or a systematic inference latency and peak-memory comparison across input sizes. It reports code and model availability, while the release contents and access terms for the curated training data are unspecified.
