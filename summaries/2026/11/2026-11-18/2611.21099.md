# ForceHarness: Turning Force into Action and Physics for Frozen Frontier Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21099) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21099)


## One-sentence takeaway

ForceHarness puts fast contact control inside self-terminating robot tools and returns physical measurements to a frozen model, raising GPT-6 Astra’s zero-shot success from 2/60 to 54/60 across six tasks on a Franka FR3 without a wrist force sensor (§5.1, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

Contact-rich manipulation requires information that images may hide, such as payload mass, surface stiffness, and whether a part has seated. Contact also changes faster than a remote model can respond. The authors identify a gap between learned force-aware policies, which require demonstrations and weight updates, and frozen-model interfaces that leave the model to interpret raw readings or write low-level force control (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **ForceHarness** integrates three layers for frozen, API-only models. It calibrates force from a wrist sensor or estimated external joint torques, runs bounded contact primitives that regulate force locally and stop on events or completion, and converts traces into quantities such as mass, friction, and insertion depth. Fixed estimators attach uncertainty and status flags where applicable, and the model uses the results to choose its next tool call (§3, Figure 2, Table 1).
- **HiddenPhys** adds seven MuJoCo tasks in which hidden physical variables affect success. It provides expert force controllers, position-only controllers, and learned policy baselines evaluated on shared test seeds, with final-state success checks and handle-damage counts (§4, Table 5, Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Real-arm success rose with ForceHarness | GPT-6 Astra went from 2/60 (3.3%) without force to 54/60 (90.0%) with it across six FR3 tasks, with 10 episodes per task per condition. GPT-6.1 Sol reached 47/60 with ForceHarness under the same prompts. | §5.1, Table 3 |
| ForceBench gains were concentrated on Drawer Pull | On eight Isaac Lab tasks with 10 seeds each, Astra rose from 56/80 (70.0%) to 70/80 (87.5%) and Sol from 52/80 (65.0%) to 70/80 (87.5%). Drawer Pull rose from 0/10 to 10/10 for both. The best published trained policy scored 35.31% under its own evaluation protocol. | §5.2, Table 4, Tables H2–H3 |
| HiddenPhys totals were close to the hand-written expert | Across seven tasks with 20 shared test seeds each, Astra rose from 87/140 to 135/140 and Claude Fable 5.2 from 96/140 to 135/140. Each ForceHarness row recorded zero handle-damage episodes. The expert scored 136/140 with one damage episode, and learned policies scored 65–83/140. | §5.3, Table 5 |
| HiddenPhys performance held up under the tested shifts | On four tasks with 20 seeds each, Astra with ForceHarness went from 75/80 in distribution to 69/80 under shift, Fable from 75/80 to 71/80, and the expert from 77/80 to 68/80. Only frozen models received updated instructions. | §5.3, Table 5, Appendix D.7 |
| Primitives improved contact execution and computed quantities improved decisions | In HiddenPhys, primitives with event feedback raised Sol’s success rate from 0.63 to 0.77 over 70 episodes per condition and smaller GPT-5.x models’ rate from 0.18 to 0.54 over 125 episodes per condition. With primitives held fixed, the smaller models’ decision accuracy on Weight, CoM, and Lock rose from 0.69 with tared raw samples to 0.85 with L2 quantities (paired McNemar p = 0.023). | §5.4, Table 6, Figure 4, Appendix D.6 |
| Turn-by-turn decisions improved over one-shot programs | On HiddenPhys with five seeds per task, Astra’s one-shot program success rose from 23% without force to 77% with the force interface. Turn-by-turn use reached 91%. Only the authors’ harness was tuned. | §5.4, Figure 4c, Appendix G.3 |

The within-harness comparisons support the combined value of force-aware tools and feedback in these settings. The HiddenPhys ablations further support two distinct roles for force: local control improves contact execution, and computed quantities improve decisions for smaller models. Derived signals and the two quantity formats performed similarly, so the incremental value of explicit uncertainty and flags remains unclear. The expert and learned-policy comparisons report observed performance under different interfaces and protocols. The close HiddenPhys totals alone do not establish statistical equivalence or general superiority.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitations.** API calls add seconds between primitives and preclude fast reactive replanning. Primitives, estimators, and task instructions are hand-written. Evaluation focuses on single-arm tabletop manipulation with a parallel gripper, and component ablations are simulation-only. On the real arm, torque-derived force drifts with pose and supports relative weight comparisons more reliably than absolute measurements (§§5.1, 6, Appendices I and J.1).
- **Analyst inference on ForceBench.** Tuning overlapped eight of the ten evaluation seeds, and two tasks were revised after initial runs. The evaluator omits some task requirements, and published trained baselines used more evaluation episodes and different instructions. These differences limit the head-to-head comparison (§5.2, Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference on HiddenPhys.** Shared seeds align episodes, while observation modalities, force caps, and Lock verdict requirements vary by method. Learned baselines use one training seed each. These differences limit attribution of the performance gap to the force interface alone (§5.3, Appendices C.2, C.5, and G).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the component gains transfer to hardware?** The ablations were simulation-only. On the same physical tasks, compare pose-only tools with force primitives under event-only, raw-sample, and computed-quantity feedback, using blinded final-state scoring.
2. **When do uncertainty and status flags improve decisions?** The tested derived-signal, L1, and L2 formats performed similarly. Hold primitives and point estimates fixed, vary sensor bias and class separation, and measure decision accuracy and whether models re-probe ambiguous cases.
3. **How much of the ForceBench gain persists on untouched seeds?** Development overlapped the reported seed set. Freeze the port and prompts, then run paired force and no-force evaluations on fresh seeds with both the public evaluator and checks for the omitted task requirements.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method and reported aggregate results.
