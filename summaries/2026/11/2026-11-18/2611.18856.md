# Self-Improvement Memory Enables Continual Recursive Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18856) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18856)


## One-sentence takeaway
When an agent’s benchmark or policy model changed, carrying forward and updating a structured record of harness edits yielded higher final held-out scores than harness-only continuation in all ten main comparisons, with a mean advantage of 2.36 pass@1 points across ten independently initialized coding pairs (§§4.2–4.4, Tables 3, 5–6).

## Problem and motivation
An agent harness manages the model’s context, tools, and execution flow. The authors identify a gap in harness optimization across benchmark or model changes. An evolved harness carries the edits forward, but its code may leave their purpose, evaluation context, and measured outcomes unclear. The optimizer needs that evidence to judge which inherited fixes still apply, especially when the transferred harness starts below the original seed (§1, Figs. 3–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Continual Recursive Self-Improvement (CRSI).** The authors formalize continuation from an evolved harness under two controlled shifts. Cross-dataset continuation holds the policy fixed, while cross-model continuation holds the benchmark and split fixed. Both require a compatible executable harness interface and reuse the existing proposal, evaluation, and selection loop (§3.1).
- **Self-Improvement Memory (SIM).** SIM groups selected edits by hypothesized root cause and stores their rationale, predicted and observed task outcomes, and provenance. The harness proposer and selector read the full record each round. After selection, SIM appends the chosen attempt and its outcome, including missed predictions. Held-out feedback stays outside the loop (§§3.2–3.3, Fig. 2, Appendix D).
- **Matched continuation comparisons.** The authors compare full memory, root causes only, and harness-only continuation from the same evolved harness. A separate cold-start arm begins from the seed, and a paired coding study repeats source optimization to measure variation across evolved harnesses (§4.1, Table 3, Fig. 6).

The matched comparisons support a benefit for the **complete SIM procedure** under the tested shifts. The paired coding study provides the strongest evidence that the advantage recurs across source runs. The edit histories illustrate how inherited rationale can guide revisions, while some selected edits failed to fix their predicted tasks (Appendix F.2–F.4). The study leaves the effects of individual memory components, reproducibility outside coding, and statistical equivalence in the shared-harness comparison unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated.** Independent optimization repetitions focus on coding. The main comparisons in other domains use one run per condition, and repeated task rollouts estimate execution performance rather than optimization variability (§4.1, Appendix C.4, Limitations).
- **Author-stated.** The experiments require compatible harness interfaces and keep the optimizer’s planning and search procedures fixed. Full SIM combines inherited history with target-stage updates. Rounds and candidate counts match, while token use and runtime can differ (§3.3, Appendix C.1, Limitations).
- **Author-stated.** Workspace results may reflect the fixed LLM judge’s biases, and a harness can potentially satisfy a verifier without solving the intended task (Ethics Statement, Appendix B.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Reading the full record every round may become expensive over longer transition chains. The generation study gives a bounded example, and source retention is tracked in one coding transition, leaving the longer-run cost and retention trade-off uncertain (§3.2, Table 1, Appendix E.3).

## Open research questions
1. **How much of the gain comes from inherited history versus target-stage updates?** Full SIM combines both. From identical evolved harnesses, run a factorial comparison that independently enables source-memory inheritance and target-memory updates (§3.3, Appendix E.4.1).
2. **Do the gains recur outside coding?** The non-coding main comparisons use single optimization runs. Repeat paired continuation from independently evolved source harnesses in classification, workspace, and engineering, and report across-run intervals (§4.1, Appendix C.4).
3. **Does selective memory access preserve adaptation and source retention over longer chains?** SIM reads the full record each round, and the source-retention trace covers one coding transition. Compare full-record access with retrieval or summarization over a longer chain, measuring token use and held-out performance on both current and earlier settings (§3.2, Appendix E.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**High** for the reported comparisons and method description. Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes a factorial breakdown of inheritance versus target updates, across-run uncertainty for the non-coding comparisons, condition-level token and runtime totals, and a confirmed release location for the SIM implementation or full run artifacts.
