# Agentic On-Policy Distillation Implicitly Reshapes LLMs&#x27; ReAct Patterns

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19244) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19244)


## One-sentence takeaway

In Qwen3-4B agents, domain-specific on-policy distillation changed how students solve problems and gather feedback, and masking the lowest-ranked 5% of token positions by the teacher’s post-RL preference change partly recovered conversational task accuracy at a cost to household-task accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its own trajectories using a teacher’s token scores. The authors ask how specializing an agent in one domain changes its reasoning, tool use, and acquisition of feedback in another. Task accuracy alone leaves these changes hidden, including whether an agent waits for information or authorization before acting. (§§1–4)

## Approach and contributions

- **Cross-domain behavioral study.** The authors compare single- and multi-teacher policy-gradient OPD across tool-free math (GR), Python-enabled math (CI), ALFWorld, and τ²-bench. They pair task scores with trajectory measures that distinguish solution routes, responses to feedback, and calls that depend on missing user input. (§§3–4, Appendix C)
- **Source-only supervision selection.** For each student rollout, the proposed method ranks assistant token positions by the signed change in teacher log probability from before to after domain-specific RL. It applies the ordinary OPD loss at retained positions. The authors test masking and ranking controls on ALFWorld-to-τ² transfer and extend the method to GR-to-CI transfer. (§5, Appendices B.4 and F)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| GR supervision shifted how the student solved tool-enabled math | Base → GR-only OPD: accuracy 16.7% → 38.0%, tool attempts 70.4% → 14.4%, and correct outputs without a tool attempt 2.4% → 30.1% of all trajectories | Table 1, §4.1 |
| CI supervision improved household and customer-service performance | Base → CI-only OPD: ALFWorld accuracy 12.9% → 40.2% and adjacent action repetition 4.5% → 0.7%. τ² accuracy rose from 41.5% to 51.0%, and complete reference-query coverage rose from 54.5% to 67.5% on 200 eligible retail rollouts | Table 2, §4.2 |
| ALF supervision disrupted conversational timing | Base → ALF-only OPD: τ² accuracy 41.5% → 6.6%, and trajectories with a detected dependency violation 0.50% → 55.8%. Adding τ² training prompts and their specialist teacher raised τ² accuracy to 68.9%, with zero violations detected in 800 reviewed trajectories | Tables 3, 4, and 11 |
| Signed-Δ selection partly mitigated ALF-to-τ² degradation | After 20 updates using ALF-only training, full OPD → Top95: τ² accuracy 11.5% → 29.9%, ALFWorld accuracy 87.6% → 76.6%, and generated ask–call co-occurrence among detected request turns 97.1% → 24.3%. Pretraining τ² accuracy was 41.5% in this sweep | §5.1, Figure 4 |
| GR selection preserved more tool use with a small accuracy cost | After 20 updates with a separate RL-trained GR teacher, full OPD → Top95: tool-enabled math attempts 10.41% → 29.49%, CI accuracy 37.65% → 36.53%, and no-tool GR accuracy 46.02% → 46.33% on the paired math evaluation | §5.3, Table 6, Appendix F.5 |

The comparisons show that task accuracy and interaction behavior can move in different directions. The semantic review provides direct evidence of premature calls after ALF-only OPD. The selection sweep measures generated ask–call co-occurrence, so it leaves open how often Top95 still calls tools before obtaining required information. The authors attribute negative transfer to overfitting to source-specific interaction patterns. The experiments leave open how much of the mitigation comes from the selected positions versus the change in feedback strength. (Appendices A, C.3, and F.4)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** The experiments use Qwen3-4B students and a limited set of math and simulated interactive tasks. Selection effects may depend on the training recipe and interaction protocol. (Limitations, p. 10)
- **Analyst inference.** The τ² execution loop withholds prose accompanying a tool call from the simulated user. A system that delivers that prose or blocks premature calls could yield different outcomes. (Appendix C.1)
- **Analyst inference.** The closest feedback-strength control matches coefficient mass only approximately on fixed contexts. The runs leave the effects of token choice and online update strength partly confounded. (Appendix F.4)

## Open research questions

1. **Does Top95 reduce actual dependency violations?** The selection sweep reports ask–call co-occurrence, while the semantic review covers other checkpoints. Apply the same blinded semantic review to Top95, Random95, and full OPD. (§5.1, Appendix C.3)
2. **How much of the benefit comes from token choice versus feedback strength?** The reported controls leave these factors partly confounded. Compare signed-Δ and random masks under matched online update norms and track source accuracy, target accuracy, and dependency violations. (Appendix F.4)
3. **Does the mitigation generalize across model families and turn protocols?** The study uses one student family and a specific τ² execution loop. Repeat the ALF-only comparison with a different student family and vary how the runtime handles mixed message–tool turns. (Appendix C.1, Limitations)

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark comparisons and metric definitions. The supplied text does not provide run-to-run uncertainty across independent training seeds, semantic dependency-violation rates for the Top95 sweep, or confirmation that the study’s code, trained checkpoints, and full task instances were released.
