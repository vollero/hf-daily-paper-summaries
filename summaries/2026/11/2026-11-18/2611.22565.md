# MillBill: A Machining Agent with Geometric Feedback

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22565) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22565)


## One-sentence takeaway

MillBill combines language-model process planning with geometric toolpath generation and verification, achieving 97.4% of target surface area within 0.1 mm in simulation across 100 parts, compared with 97.0% for its no-LLM reference plan and 94.3% for the best code-writing baseline across 99 scored parts (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Turning a CAD part into a CNC program requires both machining judgment and precise geometric execution. Errors in coordinates or offsets can gouge a part or leave excess stock. The authors identify a gap between language-model planners that stop before toolpath verification and code-writing agents that must implement the geometry themselves (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **B-rep-to-G-code pipeline.** A locally hosted Qwen3.8-27B revises a standard-practice plan’s operation sequence, tools, and cutting parameters. The geometric harness generates the toolpaths, simulates material removal, attributes deviations to features and operations, and uses automatic repairs and model feedback to refine the plan. Feature recognition uses pretrained AAGNet (§§3.1, 3.3–3.6).
- **Reach-aware setup selection.** MillBill screens cutter reach and approximate holder clearance using height-map dilation, then greedily chooses axis-aligned setups by newly reachable removal volume. Later setups are planned on the stock left by earlier simulated cuts (§3.2).
- **Benchmark and scoring protocol.** The authors pair 4,294 GrabCAD parts with milling stocks and evaluate a stratified 100-part subset using a simulator that measures area-weighted surface deviations. The paper links implementation code and notes that the source models have varying usage terms (§1, §§3.5, 4.1, S2, abstract).

The results support the authors’ claim that the integrated pipeline improves simulated geometric fidelity at the default tolerance. The paired ablation shows a small average contribution from the language model within that pipeline. The fixed `+z` comparison tests the value of multi-direction setup planning as a package. It leaves the specific benefit of holder-aware reachability over visibility-based multi-setup planning unresolved. Physical machining performance remains unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The system covers six indexed directions and omits workholding, fixture and full holder collision checks, and machine travel limits. The simulator omits deflection, vibration, wear, and thread cutting. Cutting data are representative, and the feature recognizer was trained on synthetic parts (§5).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits.** The test subset comes from one public library, is filtered for near-complete reachability under the study’s setup assumptions, and uses one run per method and part (§§4.1, 5).
- **Analyst inference.** The baseline results compare complete systems under different budgets and feedback conditions, with different scored subsets. CNC-Net also starts from its native bounding-box stock. The reported ranking therefore depends on the study protocol. The ablations leave the separate effects of knowledge retrieval, automatic repairs, and model-proposed fixes unresolved (§§4.1, 4.3–4.4, S3, Table S3).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does holder-aware screening improve setup selection?** The single-direction ablation changes both the number of available directions and the setup policy. Compare visibility-based and reach-aware multi-setup planners under the same tool library, setup budget, and independent collision checks (§3.2, Table 2).
2. **Which feedback mechanism accounts for the refinement gains?** The build-budget replay measures their combined effect. Run matched variants with automatic repairs and model-proposed fixes independently enabled or disabled (§§3.6, 4.4).
3. **How well do simulation scores predict physical machining outcomes?** The paper reports simulated geometry and omits several physical constraints. Machine a representative subset with documented fixtures and travel checks, then compare measured surface deviations, thread quality, and surface finish with the simulated results (§5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark results and method summary. The supplied text provides per-part scores and simulator settings. Physical machining and metrology results, repeated-run variability, baseline results under matched feedback and budgets, and component-level refinement ablations are unavailable. The paper links code and describes the benchmark, while uniform redistribution rights for the source CAD files are unestablished (abstract, §S2).
