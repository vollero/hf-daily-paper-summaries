# Unreasonable Confidence from Simulator Misspecification: Directional Robustness on the SPEED+ Sim-to-Real Benchmark

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20321) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20321)


## One-sentence takeaway

On SPEED+, fine-tuning the final ResNet stage transferred more of the synthetic rotation-error improvement than scaling data and features with a frozen backbone, yet its nominal 90% synthetic-calibrated rotation cones covered only 9.0–15.4% of sunlamp cases across the two main fine-tuned arms (Tables 4, 5, and 10).

## Problem and motivation

Spacecraft pose estimators train largely on rendered images because labeled in-orbit imagery is scarce. The paper identifies a radiometric mismatch between SPEED+ renders and images of a physical Tango mockup, especially under directional lighting that produces specular highlights, hard shadows, and sensor saturation. The author asks which synthetic accuracy gains carry over to real images and whether uncertainty calibrated on synthetic data remains reliable there (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled transfer study.** A monocular ResNet-50 directly regresses translation and quaternion rotation. The three-seed factorial study crosses a frozen versus trainable final backbone stage with photometric augmentation off versus on. Checkpoints are selected on synthetic validation data. Separate frozen-head experiments isolate training-set size and feature choice (§§3–6, Tables 4–6).
- **Directional robustness diagnosis.** Paired-seed comparisons show that the domain benefiting most from augmentation changes with backbone adaptation. Ablations and pooled image statistics probe the lighting mismatch, while BatchNorm controls test whether normalization can explain the gains (§§6–8).
- **Uncertainty stress test and audit assets.** The study measures synthetic-calibrated translation and rotation coverage across domains and range bins, including rotation coverage after unlabeled target-domain BatchNorm adaptation. The author reports releasing code, checkpoints, per-image predictions, and the post-review preregistration record (§9, Apps. F–H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Scaling frozen-head inputs helped synthetic validation most | Increasing training data from 4,000 to 28,000 images and switching from logits to pooled features changed three-seed mean rotation error from 1.651 to 1.030 rad on synthetic validation, 1.962 to 1.761 rad on lightbox, and 2.040 to 1.986 rad on sunlamp. The sunlamp gain was below the study’s two-seed-SD threshold. | §5, Table 5 |
| Final-stage fine-tuning transferred more of the gain | Compared with the scaled, unaugmented cached-feature heads, unaugmented layer-4 fine-tuning changed mean rotation error from 1.030 to 0.317 rad on synthetic validation, 1.761 to 1.166 rad on lightbox, and 1.986 to 1.534 rad on sunlamp. The author reports 83% and 63% of the synthetic gain carrying over to lightbox and sunlamp, respectively. | §5, Tables 4–5 |
| Augmentation’s effect depended on backbone adaptation | With layer 4 trainable, augmentation lowered lightbox mean rotation error from 1.166 to 0.987 rad and helped on every seed. Sunlamp mean error moved from 1.534 to 1.468 rad, with mixed signs across paired seeds. With the backbone frozen, augmentation improved sunlamp more than lightbox on every paired seed. | §6, Tables 4, 6–7 |
| Rotation coverage fell sharply under shift | Across the two main layer-4 arms and their seeds, nominal 90% cones covered 88.8–91.4% on the independent synthetic test split, 21.5–38.0% on lightbox, and 9.0–15.4% on sunlamp. The main frozen arms used much wider cones spanning 42–56% of orientation space. | §9, Table 10, App. B |
| AdaBN improved point accuracy while coverage stayed low | With layer-4 BatchNorm statistics estimated on one unlabeled half of each target domain, mean rotation error on the other half fell by 0.09 rad on lightbox and 0.16 rad on sunlamp. The unchanged synthetic-calibrated cones covered 23.9–40.4% and 11.2–19.6%, respectively, across the checkpoints and seeds. | §8, Tables 9–10, App. D |
| Marginal coverage hid range-dependent failures | For unaugmented layer 4, three-seed mean coverage of the nominal 90% translation sphere scored in metres fell from 92.9% in the nearest range bin to 80.9% in the farthest on the synthetic test split, and from 58.4% to 0.1% on sunlamp. Bin boundaries came from synthetic calibration range quintiles. | §9, Fig. 5, Table 17 |

The experiments show that the source of a synthetic gain mattered for this estimator and that better target-domain point estimates could coexist with severe undercoverage. The author attributes the augmentation pattern to a closer match with diffuse lighting. The pooled image statistics support that interpretation, while the ablations leave the effects of individual components on sunlamp unresolved (§6, Tables 7–8). The coverage results document empirical failure under shift, where the exchangeability assumption underlying the conformal guarantee is broken (§9, Fig. 4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The evaluation covers one estimator, one benchmark, and downscaled grayscale images. Three seeds and the smaller sunlamp set leave modest effects unresolved. The frozen baseline combines training procedures, and hardware and software differences complicate the augmentation ablations. Whole-frame statistics are dominated by dark background, and the conformal analysis provides marginal coverage (§10, Apps. D, G).
- **Analyst inference.** The synthetic BatchNorm control changes statistics across the frozen backbone, whereas fine-tuning changes both weights and statistics in layer 4. The comparison leaves their separate contributions and interaction unresolved (§8, App. D). The hardware-in-the-loop results provide no direct measure of in-orbit or closed-loop navigation performance (§§3, 10).
- **Evidence status.** Preregistration used commits in the author’s repository. The transfer plot, the AdaBN translation result, and the sunlamp translation coverage collapse with range are identified as outside the preregistration (App. H).

## Open research questions

- **Will illumination-specific augmentation improve sunlamp transfer?** The tested stack and its removal ablations leave the effect unresolved. Compare the existing stack with controlled augmentations for specular highlights, hard shadows, and clipping, using matched hardware and paired seeds (§§6, 10).
- **How much of the layer-4 gain comes from learned weights versus BatchNorm statistics?** The main comparison changes both. Cross trainable versus frozen layer-4 weights with fixed versus re-estimated layer-4 statistics under the same training protocol (§8, App. D).
- **Can target coverage improve without making pose regions too wide?** AdaBN leaves synthetic-calibrated coverage low, and fixed translation scores show range-dependent failures. Compare fixed scores with predicted-range-dependent scores and a density-ratio-weighted calibration variant, assessing source–target overlap and reporting held-out coverage alongside region size (§§8–10).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported within-benchmark results and **limited** for extrapolation beyond this setup. The supplied text contains no in-orbit or closed-loop evaluation, no matched factorial isolation of layer-4 weights and statistics, and no results for illumination-specific augmentation or full-backbone fine-tuning. Exact Colab software versions were unrecorded (App. G). The reported release contents are described in the paper.
