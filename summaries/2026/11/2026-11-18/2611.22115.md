# TeleChat to Xing4: Advancing Frontier Agentic Models on Domestic Chips and Frameworks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22115) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22115)


## One-sentence takeaway

Xing 4 documents pretraining a 285B mixture-of-experts model on 8,192 Ascend 910C cards, reports setting-specific efficiency gains for training and inference, and introduces TeleGym for agent post-training without reporting agent performance outcomes (§§2–6).

## Problem and motivation

The authors address how to train and serve long-context language models at scale on Ascend with MindSpore. Long contexts increase attention indexing, memory traffic, and activation storage, while distributed execution adds communication and host scheduling delays. Agent post-training adds another source of imbalance because tool calls and evaluations finish at different times. The authors identify a need to coordinate model architecture, hardware-aware scheduling, and rollout management across these workloads (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hardware-aware model design.** The report evaluates combinations of existing attention and decoding methods for Ascend. The representative 285B model combines multi-head latent attention (MLA), DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), and cross-layer IndexShare with a wider, shallower MoE backbone and DSpark speculative decoding. The authors also report releasing Xing4.0-29B-A4B, trained entirely on Ascend with MindSpore (§§2.2–2.5, Abstract).
- **Distributed pretraining engineering.** MindSpore HyperParallel shards model state within SuperPoDs and replicates it across them, enabling training without pipeline stages in the reported configuration. The stack assigns each eligible Muon matrix operation to one owner rank, selectively recomputes activations, schedules indexer backward earlier, balances packed sequences, compacts document-local KV inputs, and manages host CPU contention (§§3, 5).
- **TeleGym and agent reinforcement learning.** TeleGym provides a shared episode interface for heterogeneous agent harnesses. The training pipeline constructs token-level trajectories, groups rewards by independent execution, and combines separate rollout and optimization pools with bounded oversampling and partial rollout. Unfinished episodes can resume across policy updates while retaining sampling-time log probabilities (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse attention and index reuse improved simulated serving efficiency | MLA + DSA + IndexShare reached **4.68× prefill throughput** and **1.27× decode throughput** versus MLA alone, with **0.21× time to first token** and **0.79× time per output token**. Zhanlu simulation modeled the 285B model at 256K context with disaggregated prefill and decode on 32 Ascend 910C cards. | §2.2, Tables 1–2 |
| Wider, shallower MoE improved throughput | The 45-layer configuration reached **1.20× prefill throughput** and **1.11× decode throughput** versus the 60-layer baseline in the 285B long-context study. The authors also report approximately **10% higher training throughput** for the wider topology in large-scale training, with limited setup detail for that figure. | §2.3, Tables 3–4 |
| DSpark increased output tokens per second | On six math and code workloads with the 285B model, DSpark delivered **2.67×–3.77×** base decoding throughput and **1.48×–2.10×** MTP3 throughput. HumanEval showed the largest gain against both baselines. | §2.4, Table 5 |
| Hybrid sharding enabled training without pipeline parallelism | On the 285B model at 262,144 tokens and global batch size 16 on 1,024 NPUs, HSDP took **31.8 s/step** versus **36.4 s** for PP-1F1B and **34.2 s** for interleaved PP. The schemes also differed in sharding and micro-batch size. | §3.1, Table 7 |
| Optimizer and activation changes reduced profiling step time | On 1,024 NPUs at sequence length 262,144, owner-based Muon reduced step time from **31.8 to 28.9 s** with HSDP fixed. Selective recomputation and early indexer backward were compared separately against **28.9 s** with HSDP + Muon, reaching **26.9 s** and **24.5 s**, respectively. | §§3.2–3.4.1, Tables 8, 10–11 |
| Host pinning and core isolation improved training throughput together | For 285B pretraining, the combined changes raised throughput by **1–2% on 512 accelerators** and **5–10% on 8,192 accelerators**, relative to each cluster’s pre-optimization throughput. | §5.2, Table 5-1 |

The production run demonstrates integration at scale. The strongest attention result comes from simulation, the decoding benchmarks measure speed, and the pretraining ablations come from the profiling cluster. The report provides no task-accuracy evidence for the claimed quality preservation of the wider topology, no capability evaluation at the 285B model’s stated one-million-token context limit, and no measured learning or efficiency outcomes for TeleGym (§§2.2–2.5, 3–4, 6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The attention simulation omits some production-serving overhead. Owner-based Muon gains depend on shard compatibility, work balance, and realized communication overlap. Reordered or batched computation can change floating-point results, and the authors leave convergence effects to measurement (§§2.2, 3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 7 compares complete configurations, so it cannot isolate the effect of removing pipeline stages. Its 1.14× speedup also has no clearly documented link to the abstract’s separately stated 16.1% throughput gain. The detailed optimizer and recomputation tables use different baselines despite §3’s opening summary describing a common baseline.
- **Analyst inference:** The combined long-sequence regression covers a small setup and a short run. It gives limited evidence about numerical behavior in production pretraining. The host table reports the combined intervention, leaving the separate effects of pinning granularity and isolation unresolved (§§3.4.3, 5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** TeleGym’s design supports policy-version tracking and trajectory continuation, but §4 supplies no synchronous control, stale-data ablation, or breakdown of which task durations enter training batches. Its effect on learning and the mix of consumed tasks remains unknown.

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the simulated attention gain carries over to production serving?** The reported attention comparison uses one context length in simulation (§2.2). Replay the same requests across all three attention variants on real Ascend hardware, varying context length and concurrency while measuring throughput and tail latency.
2. **Does the wider, shallower topology preserve model quality at a matched training budget?** The report provides efficiency results without comparative quality scores (§2.3). Train both topologies on the same data and token budget, then compare held-out loss and long-context task accuracy.
3. **How does partial rollout affect learning on slow tasks?** TeleGym describes version tracking and filtering without reporting learning outcomes by task duration (§4.4). Compare synchronous and partial-rollout runs at a fixed device budget, measuring success by task duration, policy lag, and time to a target success rate.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported configurations and table-level comparisons. Confidence in broader performance and capability claims is limited by missing production-serving measurements, repetition and variability statistics, a full pretraining data and schedule specification, model-quality and convergence comparisons, per-condition CPU results, and quantitative TeleGym evaluations. Release status for the 285B weights and TeleGym implementation is unspecified in the supplied text.
