# ReWeave: Emergent Physical Task Topologies for Embodied Harness Optimization and Cross-Task Meta-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31053) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31053)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReWeave improves held-out robot manipulation success over a matched seed harness by revising physical prerequisites from execution traces, re-scoring past trials, and adapting the harness around frozen policies, with uneven zero-shot gains on unseen tasks (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon manipulation depends on conditions such as stable grasps and sufficient clearance that may only become apparent during execution. Binary success feedback hides which conditions a failed trial achieved or later lost, and a successful task-specific routine gives little guidance about which part will transfer. The authors identify a gap in how existing harness methods reinterpret archived trials when their understanding of physical prerequisites changes (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Revisable physical task topology.** The outer agent builds a graph of required, optional, and goal states. Transitions link physical conditions to observations and harness code. After a graph revision, ReWeave re-scores archived trials to identify retained milestones, regressions, and unmet milestones whose prerequisites still hold (§3.2.1).
- **Topology-guided harness search.** The optimizer ranks archived harnesses by development success first and revised progress second, then uses evidence from stalled transitions to edit context, workflows, and feedback loops. A Bayesian allocation rule trades candidate evaluations against sequential rounds. Execution policies and perception stay frozen (§§3.1–3.2.2, Supp. A.1–A.3).
- **Condition-based meta-distillation.** ReWeave matches observed effects of specialist mechanisms to missing conditions in the target task, adapts the retrieved code, and validates it in rollouts. Shared-harness distillation scores success, retained progress, and code growth. Continual updates reuse retrieval while selecting checkpoints by development failure rate and code growth (§§3.2.3–3.3, Supp. A.4).

The matched seed-harness comparisons support gains from harness adaptation under the tested execution interfaces. The MiniSet ablation supports a contribution from re-scoring archived trials. Zero-shot performance varied across task categories, and the task-cluster interval leaves the average gain across new tasks uncertain. The continual result measures adaptation to target tasks with development feedback, using separate held-out initializations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** Ambiguous contacts can delay topology inference. Optimization requires robot or simulator trials and model calls, and agent reasoning adds execution latency. Initial zero-shot transfer was especially weak on the hardest Goal and Long tasks (§§4.5, 5, Table 3A).
- **Analyst inference.** The simulator scoring audit supports milestone accuracy on the resolved subset of one LIBERO-PRO source-development panel. The reference left some physical propositions and all epistemic propositions unresolved, so scoring validity outside that subset remains open (Table 6, Supp. A.5).
- **Analyst inference.** External-system results use different backbones, adaptation procedures, or budgets, which limits comparative ranking. G2 revisions used recordings from earlier batches on the same matched layouts, so the hardware results establish improvement on that panel and leave generalization to fresh layouts open (§4.1, Tables 18–19, Supp. D).

## Open research questions

1. **Does mechanism retrieval transfer reliably to new long-horizon tasks?** The zero-shot gain was concentrated in Spatial tasks, and the task-cluster interval included zero. Compare retrieval and direct distillation on a larger preregistered target panel with more Goal and Long tasks and matched development budgets (Table 22, Supp. C.3).
2. **How robust is retrospective scoring when contacts are hard to observe?** The reference audit covered one LIBERO-PRO panel and left some conditions unresolved. Repeat the audit across RoboDojo and G2 with independently annotated contact events, and measure how disagreements affect parent selection (Supp. A.5, §5).
3. **When is continual adaptation more cost-effective than starting from scratch?** Its success advantage came with more launches and higher combined token use under the reported stopping rules. Compare both approaches at matched launch and token budgets while tracking success on new tasks and retention on old tasks (Tables 23–24).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the summary of the reported methods and results. The supplied text leaves several material questions unresolved, including real-robot performance on fresh layouts, transfer stability across independent optimization runs and larger task panels, and continual-learning performance at matched total budgets. The paper reports public source code, while separate release of the evaluated harness checkpoints, mechanism bank, and rollout logs is unspecified.
