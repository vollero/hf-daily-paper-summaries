# GlanceNav: Efficient Vision-and-Language Navigation with Streaming Memory and Counterfactual Rewind

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30900) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30900)


## One-sentence takeaway

GlanceNav encodes each RGB frame once, uses fixed-size memory to revisit drifting trajectories during training, and achieves 71.9% and 73.6% success on R2R-CE and English RxR-CE val-unseen, respectively (Tables 1 and 9).

## Problem and motivation

Vision-and-language navigation in continuous environments asks an agent to follow a natural-language route from egocentric observations and stop near the goal. The authors identify two bottlenecks. Step-wise policies repeatedly encode sampled history, while bounded attention caches discard older observations from exact recall. Outcome-based reinforcement learning gives an entire trajectory one advantage, so it offers little guidance about where a deviation began and yields no contrast when every rollout in a group fails (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Streaming memory.** A hybrid Qwen3.5-4B policy carries a fixed-size Gated DeltaNet state through the episode. Attention retains the instruction prefix, recent observation–action groups, and older visual keyframes ranked separately at each layer by a parameter-free measure of relative recurrent-state change (§§3.1–3.2, Fig. 2).
- **Episode-level training.** A causal attention mask reproduces the deployment memory rules in a single teacher-forced pass over an episode. The subsequent DAgger stage preserves the actions actually executed in the input stream and uses expert actions as training targets (§3.3, Fig. 3, §4.3).
- **Counterfactual Rewind (CoRe).** During training, a geodesic drift trigger selects the closest checkpoint before the drift. CoRe restores the pose and policy memory, lets a shortest-path expert intervene briefly, and lets the policy finish. The branch’s return relative to the original rollout gives signed credit to the policy continuation and rewards imitation of the expert only when the branch improves the outcome (§3.4, Fig. 2).

The experiments support lower cost in the measured configurations and higher simulated navigation success for the reported CoRe runs. The episode outcomes suggest that CoRe helped agents reach the goal area, while stopping reliability after arrival showed little change (Figs. 5–6). The separate training codebases prevent isolating the episode-level training effect, and single RL runs per setting leave the stability of the margins uncertain (§§4.2, 4.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ qualifications.** CoRe assigns credit to the branch as a whole, so a useful expert correction can receive zero credit if the sampled continuation fails. The authors also suggest that shortest-path corrections can reward shortcuts on RxR-CE routes that intentionally detour (§§3.4, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The authors report roughly two points of variation across runs of one RL configuration. Several CoRe ablation differences are of similar magnitude, which limits confidence in their ordering. Table 1 also uses published baseline results across different backbones and training recipes, so it cannot isolate the contribution of GlanceNav’s architecture (§§4.1, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The robot demonstrations show feasibility on a Unitree G1 with workstation inference over Wi-Fi. The text gives successful examples without a trial denominator or aggregate metrics, leaving real-world reliability and on-device performance uncertain (§4.5, Appendix A).

## Open research questions

- **Can route-aware rewind preserve intended detours?** The authors identify shortcutting as a possible failure mode on RxR-CE. Compare goal-distance and route-progress triggers with matched branch budgets, and measure SR and nDTW on routes that deliberately move away from the goal (§4.4).
- **Which visual history needs exact recall?** Stage-1 keyframe preferences differ across the two benchmarks, and the strongest scoring rules are close on R2R-CE. Compare them at fixed cache budgets after full training, with results stratified by how long ago the instruction-relevant landmark was observed (Tables 5 and 7).
- **How stable is CoRe’s gain at matched training cost?** Each reported RL setting has one run. Repeat CoRe and training-time-matched GRPO across seeds on both benchmarks and report uncertainty for SR and SPL (§4.4, Tables 9–10).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text specifies the architecture, evaluation splits, and main comparisons. It does not supply multi-seed estimates for the main RL comparison, aggregate robot trial results, or the full hyperparameters and data-collection settings promised with the code. The paper lists a repository link, but the supplied text does not establish which artifacts are available there (p. 1, §§4.1, 4.4–4.5).
