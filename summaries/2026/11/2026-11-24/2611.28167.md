# VideoJSON: A Minimal Harness Beats Purpose-Built Memory for Ultra-Long Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28167) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28167)


## One-sentence takeaway

VideoJSON lets a coding agent search timestamped video captions and structured records, and with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash it beat the authors’ same-model MAGIC-Video rerun on all three shared evaluation settings without accessing raw video at query time.

## Problem and motivation

Questions about days or weeks of video can require finding an event, inspecting what happened afterward, and comparing the pattern across days. The authors argue that predefined graphs, tables, and retrieval tools make some of these searches harder to express. They test whether a coding agent can assemble the retrieval procedure at query time from a simple, prebuilt memory (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured clip memory.** Before seeing any questions, a vision-language model converts each minute into a timestamped JSON record using sampled frames and speech transcripts. Records contain a dense caption and fields for events, entities, actions, places, and on-screen text (§3.1, Fig. 1).
- **Agent-written retrieval.** The policy model iteratively writes Python or Bash to filter, link, and aggregate records in a sandbox with read-only access to the index. The minimal harness uses a 30-model-call budget per question and requires no task-specific training (§3.2).
- **Comparative evaluation and trajectory distillation.** The authors test how the harness responds to stronger policy models, vary its retrieval and memory components, and use search trajectories from a separate livestream to fine-tune a smaller policy model (Fig. 2, Table 3, §6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Same-policy gains over MAGIC-Video | With DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, VideoJSON vs. the authors’ MAGIC-Video rerun scored **77.4% vs. 70.4%** option accuracy on EgoLifeQA A1 JAKE, **78.0% vs. 58.0%** on Ego-R1-Bench A1 JAKE, and **59.75 vs. 35.00** on the GPT-5-judged MM-Lifelong Week test score. With Qwen-3.5-Flash, VideoJSON trailed the EgoLifeQA rerun, **65.8% vs. 68.6%**. | Table 1, §4.1 |
| EgoMemReason gains varied by task | On the full test set, caption-only VideoJSON with DeepSeek scored **52.2%** overall accuracy vs. **39.6%** for the best published overall result. Ordering was **86.0% vs. 44.0%** and counting was **32.0% vs. 50.0%**, each against its own best prior category score. | Table 2, §5.3 |
| Greater gains across tested policy models | On the average over the EgoLifeQA subset, Ego-R1-Bench, and MM-Lifelong Week, VideoJSON gained **11.58 points** from the weakest policy to its best result, versus **5.50 points** for MAGIC-Video from the same starting policy to its own best result. | Fig. 2, §5.2 |
| Ablations favored structured records and iterative code search | With DeepSeek at xhigh effort and 200 questions per setting, Week scored **59.75** with the minimal harness, **52.00** with dense captions only, **50.25** with the rebuilt MAGIC index, and **52.50** with BM25-only search. Adding raw-video QA raised Day from **64.75 to 67.25** and estimated query cost from **1.52 to 3.57 US¢**. | Table 3, §5.4 |
| Faster indexing in the measured setup | Indexing three hours of MM-Lifelong Day took **44.2 minutes vs. 122.9 minutes** for VideoJSON and the optimized MAGIC-Video pipeline, respectively, with concurrency 16 on one B200 GPU. | Fig. 3, §5.5 |
| Small trajectory set improved some transfer scores | Fine-tuning Qwen-3.5-Flash on **64** livestream trajectories changed GPT-5-judged MM-Lifelong scores by **+8.25** on Week test, **+3.25** on Day test, and **+5.38** on Month validation. The paired 95% bootstrap interval excluded zero for Week and Month and included zero for Day. Both policies could request targeted video QA. | Table 7, §6.3 |

The experiments support the combination of structured records and agent-written retrieval in the tested settings. The main reruns compare complete systems with different indexing pipelines, and the BM25 ablation tests a single retrieval configuration. The results therefore leave open how much of the advantage would carry over to other memory systems, retrieval methods, or video collections.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported protocol constraints.** Three benchmarks share EgoLife recordings, and EgoLifeQA and Ego-R1-Bench use only A1 JAKE. The published MAGIC-Video Month evaluator restricts retrieval to annotated clue videos, while VideoJSON searches the full collection. On EgoLifeQA, the main comparison uses Chinese-only questions for VideoJSON and bilingual questions for MAGIC-Video. Appendix C reports VideoJSON’s bilingual sensitivity check (§4.1, Table 10).
- **Analyst inference on indexing.** The dense-caption ablation removes structured fields together, so it cannot identify which fields matter or how performance changes with clip duration (§3.1, Table 3).
- **Analyst inference on fine-tuning.** The tuned policy requested video QA more often. The reported design leaves unresolved whether the transfer gains came from better caption search, more frequent frame inspection, or both (§6.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Which record fields and clip durations preserve the evidence needed for counting and ordering?** The paper tests dense captions against the full schema, and the two tasks differ markedly in performance. Vary clip duration and remove individual fields with the policy, footage, and query budget fixed (§3.1, Tables 2–3).
2. **How much of the advantage comes from the retrieval policy versus the index?** The rebuilt MAGIC index and BM25 comparisons cover only some pairings. Compare code search and fixed retrieval across both index formats using matched caption inputs, query languages, and retrieval budgets (§4.1, Tables 1, 3, and 6).
3. **Does trajectory fine-tuning improve caption search when frame access is held fixed?** The tuned model uses video QA more often. Compare base and tuned policies with video QA disabled, then repeat with matched video-access budgets (§6.3, Table 7).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark results and their stated protocols. The supplied text leaves public access to the code, MediaStorm question set, and trajectories unconfirmed. It also leaves field-level and clip-duration effects unresolved, and provides no same-protocol comparison against the released MAGIC-Video index on the full Month collection.
