# Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31201) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31201)


## One-sentence takeaway

Embo-Harness achieved 83.3% episode success on RoboLab-120 versus 57.1% for the strongest reported baseline, and its controlled probes suggest that GPT-6-Astra is more effective at predicting spatial targets than at generating joint commands or frame-by-frame trajectories directly (Tables 2, 4–6).

## Problem and motivation

Frontier multimodal models can turn instructions and camera observations into robot actions inside a closed feedback loop. The authors argue that prior work mainly demonstrated or improved individual systems, leaving two practical questions open: which harness choices improve manipulation, and which model capabilities account for that performance? The answers affect how to divide work among the frontier model, conventional controllers, and learned robot policies (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Progressive harness study.** The authors varied one factor at a time across prompting, planning, action execution, and tool use, carrying the selected configuration forward. The resulting recipe uses GPT-6-Astra at `xhigh` reasoning effort, high-resolution wrist and third-person views, short fixed-length chunks of absolute end-effector actions, a tool for issuing short action sequences, and frontier-model refinement of actions proposed by a vision-language-action (VLA) policy (§§3.1–3.5, Table 1, Fig. 4).
- **Evaluation in simulation and on hardware.** The paper compares the integrated harness with agentic, VLA, and world-action-model baselines on RoboLab-120 and with its starting harness on zero-shot physical tabletop tasks (§3.6, Tables 2–3).
- **Capability probes.** The authors compare direct joint and frame-level generation with kinematics-assisted and target-pose control. They also measure 3D target localization against ground truth and compare its error with gripper error in failed grasps (§§4.1–4.3, Tables 4–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The integrated harness led the reported benchmark comparison. | On full RoboLab-120 without task-specific fine-tuning, Embo-Harness achieved 83.3% episode success and a task completion score of 89.2, versus 57.1% and 69.9 for SenseNova-RoboRSI, the strongest reported baseline. The gains were 26.2 percentage points in success and 19.3 score points. | §3.6.1, Table 2 |
| The harness improved physical task success. | In zero-shot trials, Embo-Harness succeeded in 15/20 attempts versus 7/20 for its starting harness. By task, the counts were 5/5 vs 4/5 for stacking, 5/5 vs 3/5 for packing, 2/5 vs 0/5 for the toy-sink faucet task, and 3/5 vs 0/5 for flower arranging. | §3.6.2, Table 3, §6 |
| Camera views and resolution mattered in the ablations. | On 20 RoboLab tasks with three trials per task, dual views achieved 65.0% success versus 30.0% with third-person view alone. In the resolution comparison, success rose from 65.0% at 224 × 126 to 83.3% at 640 × 360. | §3.3, Table 1 |
| Fixed chunks and selective tool integration improved subset performance. | On the same 20-task, three-trial RoboLab subset, fixed chunks of eight achieved 58.3% success versus 15.0% for adaptive chunking in the chunking study. In later stages, the short-sequence tool raised success from 83.3% to 90.0% and reduced reported harness time from 14.6 to 10.5 minutes. VLA refinement reached 96.7% success versus 90.0% with the frontier model alone and 58.3% with model-directed VLA delegation. | §§3.1, 3.4, Tables 1 and 7 |
| Target-based control outperformed direct low-level generation in the probes. | On 20 RoboLab tasks with three trials per task, direct absolute joint control achieved 20.0% success versus 43.3% with kinematics assistance. Direct eight-frame generation achieved 40.0% versus 58.3% for target-pose prediction with interpolation. In a separate control comparison, direct end-effector destination prediction reached 50.0% versus 43.3% for absolute and 45.0% for delta short-range control. | §§4.1–4.2, Tables 4–5 |
| Better visual inputs and higher reasoning effort reduced localization error. | On 20 RoboLab tasks with one localization trial per task per setting, mean 3D error fell from 14.10 cm at medium effort to 9.07 cm at `xhigh`, and from 14.14 cm at 224 × 126 to 9.07 cm at 640 × 360. The separate failed-grasp reference had a mean gripper-to-target error of 11.04 cm. | §4.2, Table 6, §6 |

The experiments establish a benchmark advantage over the reported comparisons and show that the action interface affects performance in the tested setup. The authors interpret the control probes and localization trends as evidence that spatial target estimation is a major contributor. The experiments leave its causal share of the overall gain unresolved, and the similar error magnitudes alone cannot identify the cause of individual grasp failures.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The conclusions depend on current frontier-model capabilities. Dexterous manipulation, locomotion, and tactile tasks may require additional low-level or dynamic capabilities. The authors also identify small-target localization and poor visibility at tilted wrist-camera angles as practical failure modes (§§3.6.2, 5).
- **Author-stated.** Action interfaces and numerical generation may partly explain the direct-control deficits. The authors also exclude a direct comparison with AMP1 because its evaluation uses an external high-accuracy depth estimator (§§3.6.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Carrying forward choices in a fixed order leaves interactions among components unresolved. The alternatives in each ablation were tested within a particular configuration, so their rankings could change under other configurations (§3, Table 1).
- **Analyst inference.** The physical trial counts and single-run localization settings limit precision. The supplied text also leaves unclear whether the full benchmark or physical evaluation used the same VLA refinement configuration as the final ablation (Tables 1–3 and 6).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does localization accuracy causally improve manipulation?** Reasoning effort and image resolution can affect several capabilities at once (§4.2). Compare model-estimated, ground-truth, and deliberately perturbed target coordinates while holding the policy and controller fixed.
2. **Which harness choices transfer across models and robots?** The full recipe and capability probes center on Astra and Franka tabletop setups (§§3–5). Repeat the key view, chunking, and action-interface comparisons across model families and robot configurations.
3. **Can calibrated VLA delegation retain its speed advantage while recovering success?** The tested delegation strategy frequently accepted unreliable proposals, and the authors leave confidence-based delegation open (§3.4). Compare confidence-gated delegation, unrestricted delegation, and refinement on the same episodes using success rate and harness time.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported comparisons and their stated settings. The mechanism remains provisional. The supplied text does not establish the VLA checkpoint used for refinement, whether refinement ran in each headline evaluation, episode-level uncertainty for the main comparisons, or the availability of runnable harness code and full evaluation logs.
