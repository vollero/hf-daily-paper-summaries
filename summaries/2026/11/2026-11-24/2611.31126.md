# WeaveVLA: 3D Relational Grounding for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31126) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31126)


## One-sentence takeaway

WeaveVLA conditions robot actions on a sparse 3D relation graph with pair-specific memory, improving both tested vision-language-action backbones in simulation and the StarVLA-GR00T variant on three real-robot tasks.

## Problem and motivation

The relations that matter for manipulation change as a task unfolds. Grasping depends on the gripper’s position relative to an object, while placement depends on the object’s position relative to its destination. Occlusion, viewpoint changes, and geometric estimation errors can interrupt or distort these observations. The authors identify a gap in how vision-language-action (VLA) policies explicitly represent task-relevant 3D relations and update them during manipulation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task-conditioned 3D graph.** WeaveVLA combines tracked object masks, visual features, and estimated metric depth, anchoring the gripper at its calibrated tool center point. Instruction-matched objects, the gripper, and nearby references form a sparse graph whose directed edges encode relative 3D displacement and distance (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Observation-gated memory for each pair.** A shared GRU maintains a separate state for each ordered pair. It updates only when both endpoints have valid current geometric support. Eligible cached geometry can still inform spatial encoding during occlusion (§3.3, Appendix B).
- **Relational conditioning across two backbones.** Edge-aware graph attention and vision-language cross-attention produce features that a learned-query readout compresses into tokens for the StarVLA-GR00T and π₀.₅ action experts. An auxiliary loss reconstructs relative geometry from the final edge features (§§3.2–3.4, Fig. 2).

The reported comparisons support gains for the complete system on both simulated backbones and for one augmented backbone on the physical platform. The matched ablations support a contribution from each tested design choice in the StarVLA LIBERO settings. The geometry analysis measures estimation error on selected trajectories. Whether lower relative error explains the policy gains, or whether those gains persist across training runs and deployments, remains unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-described scope.** CALVIN training and evaluation both use environment D, with graph state reset at each new instruction. The physical evaluation covers one platform, three tasks, and three perturbation types, using only the StarVLA-based WeaveVLA variant (§4.1, Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference — component attribution.** The matched policy ablations share the same perception pipeline and cover only StarVLA on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus. Appendix E reports much better masks after detector adaptation, but the supplied control results do not isolate how much policy performance depends on that adaptation (§4.4.1, Appendices E–F).
- **Analyst inference — uncertainty and generalization.** The supplied policy results give point estimates without seed-level variability or confidence intervals. CALVIN detector AP is measured on the validation frames used for checkpoint selection, limiting its value as an independent detector check. The paper separately reports a mask comparison on evaluation-sequence frames (Appendix E, Tables 7–8).

## Open research questions

1. **How much do policy gains depend on benchmark-specific detector adaptation?** Appendix E compares mask quality, while the policy ablations hold perception fixed. Train and evaluate otherwise matched WeaveVLA variants with original and adapted detectors, then compare mask quality and control success on held-out scenes.
2. **When does pair-specific memory help under occlusion?** The no-memory ablation reports aggregate success without varying occlusion duration or object motion while hidden. Compare full and no-memory policies under controlled occlusions of varying duration, with separate static-object and moving-object conditions.
3. **How much of the policy gain comes from relative coordinates?** Figure 5 compares estimation errors without a matched absolute-coordinate policy. Train matched policies with relative and absolute geometric encodings, then measure task success under controlled depth bias and clean observations.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the architecture and headline results as reported in the supplied text. Material gaps include full numerical table bodies, including detailed baseline entries, absolute latency values, and training configurations listed in Table 6. The text also leaves seed counts and uncertainty estimates, selection criteria for the geometry-error trajectories, and code, checkpoint, and demonstration release status unavailable or unestablished.
