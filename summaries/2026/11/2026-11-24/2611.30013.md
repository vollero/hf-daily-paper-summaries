# 3AGameFactory-Motion: Agentic Rigging and Reliable Complex Motion Generation for Diverse 3D Assets

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30013) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30013)


## One-sentence takeaway

On DivDynBench, the authors report that having vision-language agents write compact rigging and motion programs for a deterministic executor improves raw-mesh rigging and numeric motion control on reference rigs compared with the same agents without the framework (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation

Animating a raw 3D asset from instructions requires inferring how its parts articulate, then coordinating their motion across ordered phases with precise timing, angles, repetitions, and trajectories. The authors argue that existing generators and benchmarks emphasize conventional skeletons and simpler instructions, so visually plausible motion can score well without meeting the requirements that matter for authoring animation on diverse assets (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **DivDynBench.** Introduces separate raw-mesh rigging and instruction-driven motion tasks across humanoid, multiped, axial, winged, mixed-limb, and mechanical assets. Manually authored reference rigs, visual rubrics, and programmatic checks support evaluation of structure, semantic compliance, and numeric control (§3, Figs. 2–3).
- **Comparison of three generation pipelines.** Tests LLM-guided specialized generators, video generation followed by motion recovery, and direct LLM generation under shared benchmark instructions. Humanoid-specific methods are evaluated on a separate subset (§5.1, Tables 4–5).
- **Agentic Motion Framework.** A vision-language agent annotates joints in calibrated views and writes motion programs with phases, root trajectories, part targets, and contact windows. A deterministic executor reconstructs skeletons, computes skinning, solves kinematics, and uses mesh feedback to refine the motion. Its chain operator distributes a single bend parameter across intermediate joints while preserving bone lengths (§§4, 8, Fig. 4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Direct generation varies substantially across agents and morphologies (§6). The chain solver searches a one-parameter bend family, and its continuity penalty trades some target-distance optimality for frame-to-frame stability. Automatic mesh refinement addresses small continuous errors, with larger failures potentially requiring another agent turn (§§8.2–8.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Source Fidelity requires preserving source geometry, so it measures suitability for a mesh-preserving workflow. Comparisons also depend on failure handling, since J2J and deformation metrics average only measurable valid submissions, while mIoU and joint validity count failures as zero (§7.2).
- **Analyst inference.** End-to-end comparisons cover three morphology categories and one agent. The ArtiMo comparison and the chain-solving and phase-separation ablations are qualitative, leaving the size and consistency of their effects across the benchmark open (§§9.1–9.3, Fig. 8).

## Open research questions

1. **Which morphologies are most sensitive to rigging errors?** The end-to-end evaluation covers three categories, and mixed-limb Overall and Accuracy move in opposite directions. Run paired predicted-rig and reference-rig trials across all six categories and relate motion errors to rigging metrics (§9.1).
2. **When does the one-parameter chain solver become too restrictive?** The supplied ablation illustrates failures on selected cases, while the solver’s search is explicitly limited to its bend family. Compare it with multi-degree-of-freedom IK on matched long-chain tasks using target error, phase completion, and smoothness (§8.2, §9.3).
3. **How closely does the visual judge agree with humans on individual cases?** The study reports matching method rankings. Collect blinded ratings across morphology categories and measure per-case agreement for each subjective rubric dimension (§§5.4, 7.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text describes the method and evaluation protocol and reports several exact results in prose. It omits the data rows of Tables 2–9, including the full baseline scores, exact per-case costs, baseline settings, and user-study setup. Per-case variability, quantitative ablation effects, and the exact contents available through the listed repository are also unavailable.
