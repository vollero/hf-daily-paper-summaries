# CCFinance: Reconstructing Financial News Samples from Web Archives

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30737) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30737)


## One-sentence takeaway

CCFinance reconstructs firm-level financial news samples from archived article versions and shows how issuer-focus rules, publisher selection, and verified publication times change which observations enter financial studies.

## Problem and motivation

Reproducing financial news studies often requires proprietary article archives and vendor firm tags. Even with the same URL, researchers may see different revisions or disagree on whether a named firm is a subject of the story. The paper identifies a gap between cleaned web news corpora and the versioned, dated, issuer-linked records needed for financial panels and trading cutoffs (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Archive reconstruction.** The pipeline filters a frozen English Fine News release by publisher, retains capture provenance, selects canonical copies by exact text and normalized URL, and checks publication dates against capture dates (§3, Appendix A).
- **Dated issuer linking and configurable coverage.** GLiNER2.5 Small extracts company spans. A linker uses SEC and Wikidata evidence, dated aliases, and ticker or Reuters-code anchors to resolve mentions, with abstention for ambiguity. The resulting ledger records link evidence and mention counts so researchers can vary the subject rule and measure its effect on issuer coverage (§§3–4, Appendix C).
- **Audits and financial reimplementations.** The paper compares company-span extraction with Stanza, audits linking and subject focus against model judgments, and tests sample sensitivity in volatility tracking, bank co-mentions, and overnight return portfolios. It also audits auxiliary metadata and explores issuer-conditioned embeddings (§§4–5, Appendices B–E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus scale | From 14,807,287 staged English Fine News records, 11,260,839 were canonical and consistently dated, and 5.22 million distinct consistently dated articles had issuer links. | Abstract, §3, Table 1 |
| Exact-span advantage depends on scoring | On 250 model-labeled leads, GLiNER led Stanza by 10.34 exact-match micro-F1 points (paired 95% interval 5.84 to 14.56). With overlapping spans, the lead was 0.41 points (−3.36 to +4.40). | Appendix B, Table A2 |
| Accepted links have high estimated precision | On 886 held-out extracted mentions, estimated precision across 475 accepted links was 96.8% (95% interval 94.0–99.6%). Recall among reference-linkable mentions was 70.8%. Weighted F1 was 81.81% for the full linker, 78.57% using prior-year alias evidence, and 59.46% for unique dated-name matching. | §4, Tables A5–A6 |
| A stricter focus rule trades recall and coverage for precision | On 183 held-out articles with a detected company, requiring three mentions raised subject precision from 31.9% to 66.1% and reduced recall from 92.3% to 56.9% versus all accepted links. At a 50-story annual minimum, qualifying issuer-years fell from 13,918 to 8,641 in 2017–2025. | §4, Table 1, Table A4 |
| Volatility fit varies with the sample | For contemporaneous monthly VIX from September 2019 through September 2023, the CCFinance tracker had R² of 0.515 versus 0.647 for the archived tracker. The paired 95% interval for the −0.132 gap was [−0.215, +0.110]. | §5.1, Table A10 |
| Bank-network direction varies by publisher sample | Between the January and April 2020 earnings weeks, connected pairs rose from 177 to 242 across CCFinance publishers and fell from 50 to 35 within Reuters. On 14-week common publisher support, the mean January–April increase was 19.1 pairs, with a central 95% matched-story draw range of [−25, +59]. | §5.2, Table A11 |
| Timestamp recovery increases portfolio support | For the two-publisher overnight exercise, recovering publication times increased evaluated sessions from 277 to 340 and sessions with both legs from 42 to 110. After refitting, gross open-to-close drift averaged −7.73 bp per evaluated session on the expanded panel versus −10.15 bp on the fixed panel. On their 277 common dates, the paired gross change was +2.45 bp (95% interval −6.80 to +13.74). | §5.3, Table A12 |

The audits quantify agreement with model references within the sampled frames. In particular, link recall is conditional on extraction, and the subject audit excludes articles with zero detected companies. The financial exercises establish sensitivity to sample construction in these settings. The volatility and paired return intervals admit either sign, and the bank draw range measures variation across stories within selected weeks. These comparisons leave the individual effects of publisher mix, story supply, and other design changes unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** The production linker uses full-archive alias frequencies, which can introduce future evidence into historical links. The overnight encoder was released after the study period, and its pretraining overlap is unmeasured (§§4 and 5.3, Appendix C).
- **Author-reported.** Subject judges see a body prefix, so they can miss late mentions used by the full-body rule. Auxiliary form, event, and sentiment labels show substantial audit errors or disagreement. The issuer-conditioned retrieval pilot fell below its planned sample minima (§4, Appendices B–D).
- **Author-reported.** Exact reconstruction requires restricted inputs, and a fresh upstream selection has yet to be independently checked row by row. The source pilot estimates physical-row yield, leaving unique-story overlap unresolved (§6, Appendices A and F).
- **Analyst inference.** The focus requirement can change which firms drive a pooled estimate because it removes proportionally more qualifying issuer-years in the lowest public-float quintile than in the highest. The reported coverage counts do not quantify the resulting change in any pooled coefficient (§4, Table A8).

## Open research questions

- **How well does subject identification work across the full corpus?** The audit conditions on company detection, and the two-mention rule had higher held-out F1 than the development-selected three-mention rule. Compare both thresholds and coreference-assisted counts on a human-labeled full-body sample that includes articles with zero detected companies (Appendix C).
- **How much of the bank-network change comes from publisher composition?** The Reuters restriction changes both the publisher mix and the available story count. Compare publisher mixes at equal story budgets with a fixed bank roster and syndication rule across common weeks (§5.2).
- **Does timestamp recovery improve returns after costs under historically available inputs?** The paired net change is uncertain, and the production linker and encoder raise historical information questions. Rerun the rolling comparison on common dates with frozen prior-year link evidence and an encoder available before the test period (§5.3, Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the summary of the reported methods, numerical results, and stated scope. The supplied text gives table captions and selected values, with some full table cells unavailable. It also lacks the restricted snapshot, code, and frozen outputs needed for independent reconstruction. Human-validated performance for the reported audits, encoder pretraining overlap, and independent row-level equivalence of a fresh source selection remain unestablished.
