# UniVEP: Unifying Velocity and Endpoint Prediction for Robot Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26103) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26103)


## One-sentence takeaway

On RoboTwin Clean, UniVEP’s jointly trained endpoint predictor beats the best 16-call single-target baseline with one StarVLA call or two LiLa-WAM calls, while fixed-checkpoint ablations show that switching to velocity can improve refinement at larger budgets (§5.3, Tables 4 and 6).

## Problem and motivation

Flow-matching robot policies generate continuous action chunks through repeated network evaluations, so sampling quality competes with control latency. Velocity and clean-endpoint predictions are algebraically interchangeable along the same linear path, but the authors ask whether finite networks learn them equally well. Recovering a plausible action from heavy noise and correcting a nearly clean action place different demands on the predictor (§§1, 3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled comparison.** The authors compare direct velocity (V), direct endpoint (X), and endpoint prediction with bounded velocity-weighted supervision (X–V), matching architecture, data, and training schedule within each host policy (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Noise-stage diagnostics.** They measure how strongly clean-action variation concentrates and probe prediction stability from pure noise and recovery error near recorded actions (§§3.3, 4.3).
- **Robot-policy adaptation.** Building on SC-Flow, UniVEP trains a shared action transformer in two modes, with separate target losses and a consistency loss between their implied endpoints. Its Euler sampler uses endpoint predictions at high noise and velocity predictions below a fixed threshold, with one network call per step. The evaluated one- and two-call grids use endpoint prediction throughout (§§2.1, 5, Appendix B.3).

The few-call results support an advantage from joint training because those samplers use only the endpoint mode. The fixed-weight ablations support a separate benefit from switching at larger budgets. The pure-noise probe measures dispersion across noise draws, so it cannot by itself establish accuracy against recorded actions. The latency measurements cover action-chunk generation through CPU delivery, leaving physical control-loop latency unmeasured (§5.3, Table 6, Appendices C.2–C.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Velocity-pretrained FastWAM favors V on RoboTwin Clean at its native budget, and standard LIBERO is near saturation, with StarVLA X–V ahead of UniVEP. Joint training also raises measured per-update cost in the reported StarVLA setup (§§6.1, 6.5, Table 12).
- **Analyst inference.** The threshold was selected in a StarVLA RoboTwin Clean sweep. Its optimality across policies, tasks, and sampling grids remains uncertain, and the paper reports task-level reversals in preferred order (§6.4, Table 6, Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference.** LiLa-WAM Full trains on Randomized demonstrations, while StarVLA and FastWAM train on Clean demonstrations. Randomized scores therefore support different transfer claims across hosts (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Physical tests cover one platform and a fixed four-call budget. Real-robot few-call performance and the reported qualitative motion smoothness remain unquantified (§6.6).

## Open research questions

- **Can policy-specific switching improve success at the same call budget?** The threshold sweep covers one policy and setting. Fit a rule using held-out rollouts, then compare it with the fixed threshold on unseen tasks at matched budgets (§6.4, §7).
- **Which part of joint training drives the few-call gains?** The few-call comparisons add velocity supervision and consistency together, while the consistency-only ablation uses native budgets. Compare endpoint-only training, dual supervision, and dual supervision with consistency at one and two calls (§6.3, Table 5).
- **Does the few-call advantage carry over to physical control?** Real-robot evaluation fixes the budget at four calls. Sweep the budget on the same tasks and measure both success and end-to-end control latency (§6.6).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported comparisons within their stated settings.
