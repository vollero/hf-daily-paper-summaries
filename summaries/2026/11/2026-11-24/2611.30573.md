# Write, Evolve, Read: Streaming Cameras into a Persistent World State for 4D Occupancy Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30573) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30573)


## One-sentence takeaway

PersistWorld writes each surround-camera capture into a scene-anchored 3D state once and uses that state to forecast semantic occupancy along a supplied ego trajectory, improving mean occupied-voxel IoU by 19.13 points over SparseWorld-TC at 1–3 s on Occ3D-nuScenes with ground-truth future poses (§§1, 4.1–4.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Autonomous vehicles need to anticipate which parts of the surrounding 3D scene will be occupied and by what. The authors find that 80.85% of occupied future voxels at 1 s on Occ3D-nuScenes were already observed and remain unchanged. Yet SparseWorld-TC gains only 2.39 IoU points on this subset over warping its own current prediction forward, averaged over 1–3 s. The authors identify retention of observed geometry as a major gap and argue that repeatedly rebuilding the scene from image history adds avoidable computation (§§1, 4.3, Figs. 2 and 5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Visibility-based diagnosis.** The authors partition occupied future space into observed and unchanged, observed and changed, and never observed. Ground-truth and predicted-occupancy warp baselines probe how much accuracy comes from estimating the current scene versus evolving it (§4.3, Fig. 5).
- **Persistent state learned for forecasting.** A visibility-aware Writer uses ray-depth predictions to place features from each camera capture into a fixed 3D lattice. The Evolver advances the state using elapsed time, cell ages, and ego poses. The Reader decodes semantic occupancy in the queried ego frame. The components train jointly with voxel and ray supervision (§§3.1–3.6, Fig. 3).
- **Streaming inference across horizons.** Each capture group is written once, the state resets periodically, and one trained model answers short- and long-horizon queries. The authors compare streaming with rebuilding the same model’s state from recent captures (§§3.5–3.6, 4.2, 4.4).

## Evidence and main results

The results below use Occ3D-nuScenes and ground-truth future ego poses unless specified. Camera-based scores use the target-frame camera mask (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Short-horizon accuracy | At 1–3 s, PersistWorld averages 67.82 IoU and 28.72 mIoU, gains of 19.13 and 3.32 points over SparseWorld-TC. | §§1, 4.2, Table 1, Appendix B |
| Long-horizon accuracy | With the same model, mean gains over SparseWorld-TC at 1–8 s are 21.58 IoU and 4.21 mIoU points. Per-horizon IoU gains range from 18.19 to 24.41 points. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Retention and completion | At 1–3 s, PersistWorld vs. SparseWorld-TC scores 70.86 vs. 51.07 IoU on unchanged space and 62.71 vs. 40.92 on unseen space. Warping their respective current estimates gives 68.60 vs. 48.68 IoU on unchanged space. | §§1, 4.3, Fig. 5a |
| State ablations | At 1–3 s, switching to an ego-centric lattice lowers overall IoU by 3.86 points and unchanged-space IoU by 4.38. Removing the Evolver lowers unseen-space IoU by 12.22 points relative to the full model. | §4.4, Table 3 |
| Streaming speed | Streaming reaches 23.15 FPS and 67.82 mean IoU versus 12.02 FPS and 67.97 mean IoU when rebuilding from eight captures with cached image features. Timing uses one A800 at batch size one and includes current and 1–3 s outputs. | §§4.1–4.2, 4.4, Fig. 4 |
| Planned-trajectory comparison | With BEV-Planner poses at 1–3 s, PersistWorld leads SparseWorld-TC by 22.63 IoU and 4.00 mIoU points. PersistWorld itself loses 6.40 IoU and 6.35 mIoU points versus ground-truth pose conditioning. Both are scored in the realized target frame. | Appendix E, Table 12 |

The warped-current comparison supports the authors’ interpretation that most of the unchanged-space advantage is already present in the current estimate. The Evolver ablation supports a separate contribution to unseen-space completion. The evidence establishes gains under the reported validation and timing protocols. Cross-dataset performance and closed-loop driving outcomes remain untested in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported failures.** PersistWorld can retain a stationary vehicle after it starts moving, stretch pedestrians into streaks, and produce thin or fragmented structures in newly revealed space. The authors identify motion with no visible precursor and completion of never-observed space as difficult cases (Appendix F, Figs. 10–11).
- **Analyst inference — evaluation scope.** The camera results come from one benchmark and score within the target camera mask. The planned-pose test combines pose error with occupancy error because forecasts are scored against the realized target frame. The undriven alternative path has a qualitative example only (§4.1, Appendix E, Fig. 8).
- **Analyst inference — attribution and memory span.** The ego-centric ablation changes both the coordinate frame and repeated resampling, so it cannot isolate their individual effects. The reset sweep varies memory at inference for a model trained with an eight-capture reset, leaving the effect of training for longer memory open (§4.4, Table 3, Fig. 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the model better anticipate motion that has yet to start?** Appendix F documents a stopped car carried forward after departure. Train a variant with explicit agent-motion hypotheses and compare both models on stopped-to-moving events and changed-space IoU.
2. **How much of the planned-trajectory score loss comes from pose mismatch?** Appendix E scores in the realized target frame, where pose and occupancy errors both count. Apply controlled pose perturbations and compare scores in the perturbed and realized frames using the same scene labels.
3. **When does longer memory help forecasting?** The reset sweep tests inference-time changes on a model trained with one reset span (§4.4). Train models with different reset spans and compare them on scenes where observed structures leave view for extended periods.
