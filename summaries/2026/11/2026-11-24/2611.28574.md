# EvoViM: Recursively Self-Evolving Visual Agents for Exploration with Deferred Queries

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28574) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28574)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across three frozen multimodal models, EvoViM used interaction traces to evolve visual memory and action tools, scoring 31.3–47.6 percentage points above AutoMem on the equally weighted average of six held-out tasks (§§5.1–5.2, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

When questions arrive after exploration, agents must retain details before knowing which ones will matter. Text summaries can make visually similar items indistinguishable, while even a complete frame archive with a matcher can leave the agent unable to link a retrieved image to a location or decide that the queried item was never seen. The authors identify a gap in learning both what visual evidence to retain and how to use it from interactive experience (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **One program for memory and skills.** A frozen multimodal model uses Python tools and prompts to store images, compute on pixels, retrieve evidence, and suggest actions. Episode memory resets between runs, while the evolved program persists (§4.1).
- **Recursive revision from interaction.** The same model diagnoses failures in development trajectories and revises the program, along with the playbook, notes, and analysis scripts that guide future revisions. Candidates undergo replay checks and a score-based gate on two development seeds (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Deferred-query environments and failure analysis.** The authors build MC Gallery, Poster Maze, and DMV-Hard on existing benchmarks and trace how observed failures lead to changes such as indexing images by location or product (§§5.1, 5.3–5.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| The clearest DMV-Hard gains were on generated patterns | Across ten held-out episodes, EvoViM answered 106, 82, and 49 of 106 pattern queries correctly for GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Haiku 5.5, and Qwen3.8-27B, versus 7, 1, and 4 for AutoMem. In DMV-Hard, a fixed every-step matcher answered most questions because the matched page identified the product. In MC Gallery and Poster Maze, EvoViM beat fixed visual memory with a generic matcher for Claude and Qwen by linking matches to locations. | §§3, 5.2–5.3, Appendix F.2 |
| Revision support accelerated development gains in the ablation | With Claude on MC Gallery, all three full runs reached at least 75% development score by generation two, versus zero of three without revision support. Removing the visual pathway reduced scores to AutoMem levels on both tasks. | §5.5, Tables 9–10 |

The results support task-specific evolution of visual tools on the tested held-out episodes. The authors’ failure attribution places most of the lead over AutoMem in storage and retrieval (§5.4, Figures 12–13). The visual ablation removes pixel-capable memory and images during revision together, leaving their individual effects unresolved. The one-shot control also gets a single attempt without a retry, so its weaker results do not isolate the effect of interaction records from the larger revision budget (§5.5, Appendix F.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated limitation.** Audio was unevaluated. Robustness to sensory noise and integration of audio with vision in embodied settings remain open (§6).
- **Analyst inference — selection and uncertainty.** Each candidate was gated on two reused development seeds, with one episode per seed and parent remeasurement disabled. Some accepted changes fell within the authors’ post-hoc noise band. The main bootstrap intervals capture held-out seed variation, while repeated evolution was tested only for Claude on MC Gallery (§§4.3, 5.5, Appendices A, E).
- **Analyst inference — metric scope.** Poster Maze’s fixed denominator makes its score reflect exploration coverage as well as recall. Tetris rewards drops as well as line clears, and Pac-Man uses one starting layout with wall-clock-dependent dynamics. These settings limit what the scores reveal about recall and game-playing ability in isolation (§5.1, Appendix B.3, B.5).
- **Analyst inference — component attribution and cost.** The revision-support ablation bundles enhanced feedback with reusable revision experience, so it cannot isolate the value of rewriting the playbook or scripts. Interrupted Claude and Qwen sessions leave revision-token totals incomplete (§5.5, Appendix A.1, Appendix E).

## Open research questions

1. **Does rewriting revision guidance improve held-out performance independently of enhanced feedback?** The paper removes both together. Run a factorial ablation that varies structured feedback and editable revision guidance independently under matched budgets (§5.5, Appendix A.1).
2. **How much of the Poster Maze gain comes from seeing more posters versus answering better from the same evidence?** Routes change which questions can be asked. Compare programs on identical replayed trajectories and query sets, then measure coverage separately during free exploration (§5.4, Appendix B.3).
3. **Would a larger or rotating development set improve candidate selection?** The gate reuses two seeds, and several accepted changes fall within the post-hoc noise band. Compare seed-selection protocols at matched episode budgets across repeated evolution runs and a common held-out set (§4.3, Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and the direction of the reported aggregate results. The supplied text omits several table bodies, including the full per-task and per-model main scores, exact confidence-interval endpoints, and paired differences against the fixed visual controls. Total revision-token use for interrupted sessions and the contents of the reported code and dataset release are unavailable from the supplied text.
