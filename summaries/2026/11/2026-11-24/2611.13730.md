# Deterministic, Reproducible Inference in Exact Integer Arithmetic, and Verifiable Distributed Inference as Its Consequence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13730) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13730)


## One-sentence takeaway

Tritify reproduced single-host integer output bit-for-bit when sharding a 46-layer GLM-4.5-Air run across three machines, while agreement with the floating-point model was measured separately on a 16-layer slice (§§6, 8.2).

## Problem and motivation

Optimized floating-point inference can produce different output bits when hardware, reduction order, or math libraries change. This makes exact replay across heterogeneous machines difficult. The author identifies a gap in serving systems that can reproduce an offloaded result byte-for-byte by default using an independent reference (§§1–2, 10).

## Approach and contributions

- **Specified integer execution.** Tritify converts weights offline to a balanced-ternary grid and quantizes activations dynamically per tensor without calibration. Fixed widths, overflow bounds, rounding rules, integer normalization, and lookup tables define the runtime arithmetic. The paper identifies the contribution as the integrated execution path and treats the quantization primitives as established (§§1, 3).
- **Verification and routing introspection.** A generic engine uses model-family plugins and pairs integer operators with floating-point references. The verification gate measures operator error, checks repeat-run identity, and hashes integer outputs. A read-only hook records reproducible MoE routing traces (§§3.3, 4, 7).
- **Distributed replay.** The coordinator routes tokens and recombines vectors returned by expert workers. Two fleet experiments reproduce single-host integer hashes. The author proposes independent recomputation as a way to check offloaded results and leaves adversarial verification as future work (§8).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-backend integer identity | Decoder logits had the same digest on ARM and x86-64. CPU/GPU identity was shown for ternary GEMM on Apple M3 Metal. | §§1, 5, 9 |
| Decision fidelity | Qwen2.5-0.5B matched the float-reference argmax at 509/512 positions on a synthetic probe, with three reported near-tie flips. On the SAM 2.1 image, the selected mask matched the internal float reference at IoU 1.000, with a maximum raw logit difference of 0.049. | §2, §5 |
| Fidelity through depth | On a fixed 32-token probe against CPU FP32, median per-position residual cosine stayed above 0.999 at every measured layer across all 16 OLMoE layers and the first 16 of 46 GLM layers. OLMoE matched 508/512 routing decisions. GLM differed on 32/480 top-k sets, and one position dipped to 0.9735 before recovering to 0.9988 at layer 16. | §2, §6, Table 4 and Figure 1 captions |
| Whole-model OLMoE replay | All 16 layers across six ARM and x86-64 machines matched the single-host integer digest on the 8-token probe. The fleet and reference used the same pre-alignment normalization configuration. | §8.1, Appendix A |
| GLM capacity and exact replay | Three machines, each with less RAM than the model’s 427 GB of integer codes, reproduced the disk-streamed single-host integer reference on one prompt. The seven generated tokens, generated-logits hash, and hidden-state hashes at all 46 layers matched. Total fleet wall time was 633.6 s, including setup, prefill, and decode. | §8.2, Table 5 and Figure 6 captions |

The matching hashes support exact replay for the tested integer runs under fixed configurations and schedules. The float comparisons support close agreement on the measured probes, with observed routing and argmax differences. Full-depth GLM fidelity to floating point, comparative performance against single-host streaming, and security against dishonest workers remain unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Dynamic per-tensor scaling makes output bits sensitive to batching and execution schedule. Incremental KV-cache decoding and whole-model CPU/GPU identity remain future work. The paper reports speedups against tritify’s own scalar baseline, and the distributed setup adds communication latency (§§5.1, 8, 9).
- **Author-stated.** Recomputing the full result carries the cost of re-execution. Sampled audits still need a sampling rule, binding of intermediate states, and an attacker model. Workers see the activations they process (§8.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The fixed depth probe, single SAM image, and single reported GLM generation leave workload-level decision fidelity uncertain. The earlier OLMoE normalization mismatch also shows why routing comparisons require matched checkpoint settings (§§5–6, 8.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The cache results answer different questions. The loopback traffic reduction used experts selected on the same probe, while the separate out-of-sample LRU study measured the share of routing served locally. The supplied evidence leaves out-of-sample network savings open (Figure 3, §8.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How often do float and integer GLM decisions diverge at full depth?** The FP32 comparison stops at layer 16. Run a full 46-layer FP32 reference on a broader prompt set and measure routing agreement, greedy-token agreement, and the first divergence (§6).
2. **Can schedule-invariant scaling preserve decision fidelity?** The reported prefill/decode comparison changes integer outputs under per-tensor scaling. Compare per-tensor, per-token, and static scaling across batch compositions and prefill/decode splits, measuring hashes and float-reference agreement (§9).
3. **When does sharding outperform single-host expert streaming?** The flagship run gives aggregate fleet time without a matched performance breakdown. Benchmark both placements on the same prompts and report setup, prefill, decode, peak RAM, disk I/O, and network traffic (§8.2).
