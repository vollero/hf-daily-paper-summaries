# AutoPILOT: A Visual Agent Harness for Real-Time Gameplay

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28202) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28202)


## One-sentence takeaway

By letting a model delegate multiple actions to macros or screen-reading controllers, AutoPILOT raised real-time AFK-Bench episode success from VISTA’s 38.3% to 68.6% with GPT-6.1 Sol under the same nominal budgets (Table 2, §4).

## Problem and motivation

Single-step visual agents can send at most one action per model call. When the game keeps running during inference, this limits action frequency and makes the observed frame stale by the time the action lands. The authors identify a gap in harness design: prior work has largely improved what models see and remember, while giving less attention to how their outputs become actions over time (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Runtime action delegation.** AutoPILOT extends VISTA so each model call can send a primitive action, launch an open-loop macro, or write a sandboxed Python controller that reads screenshots and acts without further model calls. Wake events return control to the model with a fresh frame. The distinctive contribution is writing screen-driven controllers during play and integrating them into a shared harness that needs no game-specific training (§§2–3, Table 1).
- **AFK-Bench.** The authors introduce 120 browser games, each with one task and a verifier. The games are evenly split by what happens during inaction and support both live and paused clocks (§4, Appendix E).
- **Controlled evaluation.** The paper compares clock protocols, removes delegation tools, and varies budgets, reasoning effort, and simulated generation speed. It also tests on a nonoverlapping set of GameWorld games (§5, Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results

Success rate is the share of episodes that meet the game’s success check within budget, including wins after automatic restarts (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher live success on AFK-Bench | With GPT-6.1 Sol at low effort over 360 episodes, AutoPILOT reached 68.6% versus VISTA’s 38.3% and plain Codex’s 38.9%. On hostile real-time games, AutoPILOT reached 62.5% versus VISTA’s 16.7%. | §5.2, Tables 2, 12 |
| Gains across models and with the clock paused | AutoPILOT led VISTA in overall AFK-Bench success with all six head-to-head models under both clocks. With GPT-6.1 Sol at low effort, paused success was 73.1% versus 56.1%. | Table 2, §F.2 |
| Live-clock robustness carried over to GameWorld | Across 170 tasks with GPT-6.1 Sol, AutoPILOT went from 82.9% paused to 80.0% live, while VISTA went from 84.1% to 42.9%. | §5.5, Tables 6, 12 |
| Delegation drove much of the gain | On matched live AFK-Bench episodes with GPT-6.1 Sol, the full harness won 83 of 120 tasks. Removing both delegation tools reduced this to 55 of 120. Disabling controller screen reads and its screen watch yielded 78 of 120 wins versus 83 for the full harness (paired \(p=0.30\)). | §5.3, Table 12, §F.3 |
| The hostile-game gap grew with retries | With GPT-6.1 Sol in live hostile AFK-Bench episodes, first-attempt success was 11.7% for AutoPILOT and 8.3% for VISTA. Within 20 restarts, cumulative success reached 54.2% and 15.8%, respectively. | §5.3, Figure 4a, §F.4 |
| Lower list-price API cost in the main comparison | On live AFK-Bench with GPT-6.1 Sol, AutoPILOT averaged $0.89 per episode versus VISTA’s $1.50. In the per-episode spending-cap analysis, AutoPILOT matched VISTA’s final success at roughly one-fifth of its total list-price API cost with GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5. | §5.4, Figure 4d, Table 18 |

The matched ablation and paused-clock results support the authors’ explanation that executing several actions per model call improves use of the call budget. The open-loop comparison leaves the added benefit of runtime visual feedback uncertain because it also removed the screen watch. The strongest results concern success within a retry-enabled episode. Cost savings depend on the model and provider: Appendix F.2 reports higher per-episode cost for AutoPILOT with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Writing perception and control code is easier in 2D browser games than in cluttered 3D scenes. Most hostile-game wins followed a restart, and a variant that kept controllers running longer during model handoffs produced no additional wins in the tested subset (Appendix H, §F.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Both benchmarks use browser games, and AFK-Bench selected tasks that scripted policies could win in real time. Transfer to non-game interfaces, cluttered 3D environments, or tasks without a feasible scripted policy remains untested here (Appendix E, §5.5).
- **Analyst inference.** AutoPILOT differs from VISTA in prompts, budget reporting, and restart and decision-screen signals. The matched ablations support a contribution from delegation, while the full head-to-head comparison measures the combined harness changes. Excluding the decision-screen tasks changed the reported margins only modestly (§§3, 5.3, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference.** Most configurations use a single episode per task, and provider request rejections and timeouts count as failures. This limits estimates of seed-to-seed variability and makes some scores partly dependent on the serving setup (§5.1, §§F.1–F.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can AutoPILOT win unfamiliar hostile games on the first attempt?** The reported gap grew substantially after restarts. Test both harnesses on held-out hostile games with retries disabled and report first-attempt success and time to first loss (§5.3, Appendix H).
2. **When does live visual feedback improve a delegated program?** The existing open-loop ablation also removes the screen watch. Vary controller access to frames and the harness screen watch independently, using multiple seeds and matched timing budgets (§F.3).
3. **Does runtime controller synthesis transfer to cluttered 3D scenes?** Both evaluations use browser-game suites, and the authors flag cluttered 3D perception as harder. Compare macros and closed-loop controllers on held-out 3D tasks with matched observations, action interfaces, and budgets (§5.5, Appendix H).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark comparisons. The text provides protocols, ablations, seed-level results, and paired tests for the main findings. It does not establish public release or access terms for the harness and benchmark bundle, redistribution permissions for games without stated licenses, or multi-seed variability for configurations run once per task (Appendix E, §§F.1–F.2).
