# The norm axiom and factor hyperfields

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29473) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29473)


## One-sentence takeaway

The norm axiom forces a field quotient presenting a Krasner valued hyperfield to be the prescribed relative-error quotient, and the paper proves such a presentation always exists for value group \(\mathbb Z\), while constructing counterexamples under the weaker valuation axioms.

## Problem and motivation

Linzi asks when a single Krasner valued hyperfield can be realized as \(\mathcal H_\rho(K)\), the structure obtained by reading a valued field \(K\) up to relative error \(\rho\). The norm axiom (V3) requires each hypersum to fill an ultrametric ball of prescribed radius. A published proof had lifted the valuation to a presenting field and shown that the quotient subgroup consists of 1-units, leaving the equality with the subgroup at the prescribed norm unproved. It also remained open whether every Krasner valued hyperfield has a field quotient presentation at all. (§§1.1–1.2)

## Approach and contributions

- **Theorem A.** Pulls the valuation back to a presenting field \(K\) and uses two consequences of (V3) to prove \(T=1+\mathfrak m_\rho\). This completes the published proof and makes the realization question equivalent to the existence of a factor presentation. (§3)
- **Theorem B.** For value group \(\mathbb Z\), constructs a truncated discrete valuation ring from the hyperfield, lifts it to a discrete valuation ring using Hungerford’s theorem, and recovers the hyperfield from the fraction field. The contribution is a direct proof in the stated axioms of a result also obtainable via Tolliver’s comparison and Theorem A. (§§1.2–1.3, 4)
- **Theorem C.** Proves that a layering is a factor hyperfield exactly when its base is. The proof recovers the base from the residue field in one direction and extends a leading-coefficient construction to arbitrary quotient subgroups in the other. Layered Massouros hyperfields then supply counterexamples under (V0)–(V2). (§§5–7)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| A factor presentation of a Krasner valued hyperfield is the prescribed relative-error quotient. | Pullback gives a field valuation \(w\). The identity \(1\boxminus1=\mathfrak m_\rho\) and absorption of \(\rho\)-small elements give both inclusions in \(T=1+\mathfrak m_\rho(w)\). | Theorem A, §3 |
| Every Krasner valued hyperfield with value group \(\mathbb Z\) has a valued-field realization. | For finite norm \(\rho=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), the proof reconstructs the hyperfield from a discrete valuation ring with the corresponding length-\(n\) quotient. Convex-subgroup cases cover \(\rho=\{0\}\) and the full nonnegative segment. | Theorem B, §§3–4 |
| Layering preserves and reflects factor representability. | Residue reduction proves one direction, and a fraction field of a group algebra proves the other. For Massouros bases with \(G\) of exponent \(2\) and \(|G|\ge4\), the layerings have a nontrivial valuation satisfying (V0)–(V2) and admit neither a factor presentation nor any Krasner valuation. | Theorem C, §§5–7 |
| Computational checks corroborate selected special cases. | The layering script checks axioms and sum formulas on specified bases over \(\mathbb Z\), using triples of values in \(\{0,1,2\}\). The discrete-ring script checks congruence modulo \(p^n\) and full sum classes in canonical \(\mathcal H_\rho(\mathbb Q_p)\) examples for small \(p,n\). | §8 |

The proofs establish the results under their stated algebraic hypotheses. The scripts check selected identities and examples. General Krasner-valued representability remains open, and the Massouros examples cannot settle it because they admit no valuation satisfying (V3). (§§1.4, 7.3, 8)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Theorem B gives existence only. The paper leaves open the cases outside its \(\mathbb Z\) result and the previously covered norms given by nonnegative parts of convex subgroups. (§§1.2, 1.4)
- **Analyst inference.** The discrete-ring checks start from canonical examples already known to come from fields, so they give limited independent coverage of reconstruction from an abstract hyperfield. Several parameters are described only as “small,” which limits reproducibility from the text alone. (§8)

## Open research questions

1. **Does realization hold for lexicographic \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\) with \(\rho=\{(0,0),(0,1)\}\)?** Neither covered regime applies (§1.4, Question \(1'\)). Test a rank-two analogue of the discrete proof by truncating along \(\{0\}\times\mathbb Z\) and checking whether the resulting data recover sums across outer degrees.
2. **How much does a discrete valued hyperfield determine about its realizing field?** Theorem B proves existence, while the stronger equivalences discussed in §1.2 use additional hypotheses. Construct different discrete valuation ring lifts with isomorphic length-\(n\) quotients, then compare their associated hyperfields and the invariants of their realizing fields.

## Confidence and missing information
