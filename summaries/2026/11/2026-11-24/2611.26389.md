# ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26389) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26389)


## One-sentence takeaway

ViDecide combines causal video generation, learned stopping, high-noise training, and targeted correction of failed decisions to reach 83.8% average strict success across four puzzle and simulated robot test sets, up from 23.3% with standard fine-tuning (Fig. 1, §5).

## Problem and motivation

A generated video can appear to reach its goal while violating the rules along the way, for example by making a Sokoban box disappear. The authors argue that fixed-length joint denoising poorly matches planning, where each decision depends on earlier states and the required number of steps varies by problem. They also identify two training gaps. Low-noise reconstruction can reveal the next state before the model has to infer it, and a single reward for the whole video gives weak feedback about which decision caused failure (§§1, 3.2–3.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Causal generation with learned stopping.** Generate each video segment conditioned on the preceding visual history. A learned end-of-sequence (EOS) frame marks completion. Segment size is configurable, with shorter segments working best for puzzles and longer segments for simulated manipulation (§§3.2, 4.2).
- **Decision-focused training.** Shift flow-matching timestep sampling toward higher noise so that the model more often has to infer the next state from context (§3.3).
- **Corrective Fine-tuning (CFT).** Branch from a failed rollout, keep verifier-approved continuations, and pair the noise that produced the failed decision with the first divergent segment of a successful continuation. Train on that correction, the successful suffix, and expert trajectories using regression (§3.4, §C.4).
- **RoboTwin-Plan and process-aware evaluation.** Build a dual-arm block-planning benchmark in simulation and apply a unified verifier-based success metric across the evaluated benchmarks (§4.1, §§A–B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Higher-noise training improves simulated planning | With the same data and training budget, chunk-wise Wan2.2-5B on RoboTwin-Plan rose from **33.3% SSR** at the default training shift \(s=5\) to **73.9%** at \(s=10\). | §4.3, Table 4 |
| CFT makes its largest post-training gains on hard Sokoban | On Wan2.2-5B Sokoban-Hard, CFT reached **78.2% SSR**, versus **50.0%** for its supervised starting model, **61.6%** for sample-level rejection fine-tuning (RFT), and **73.8%** for decision-level RFT. | Table 5 |
| Reusing failure noise changes the sampled decision | At **149** selected Sokoban and Sokoban-Hard divergence points with a successful resample, the original failure noise generated the successful decision at **57.0%** of points after CFT, versus **48.3%** after decision-level RFT. | Fig. 6, §D.5, Table S10 |
| Transfer varies by setting | On five-box Sokoban, Wan2.2-5B CFT raised frame-wise SSR from **37.0% to 54.0%**. Across **150** simulated robot episodes with new layouts or longer plans, CFT lowered chunk-wise SSR from **26.7% to 21.3%** and increased first-error visual defects from **20.7% to 32.0%**. | Table 7, Tables S14–S15 |

SSR requires a goal-reaching trajectory with valid transitions and, where requested in VBVR-MultiStep, a shortest solution (§4.1, §B). The controlled comparisons support gains in verifier-scored visual planning on the tested tasks. The larger puzzles provide evidence of transfer beyond training sizes, while the robot results show that visual defects can offset planning gains. The authors interpret the transfer as evidence of learned rules. The experiments measure behavior on selected shifts, leaving broader rule generalization and physical execution open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Training is benchmark-specific, with game specialists in VideoRLVR. CFT depends on verifiers that can locate failures and confirm successful continuations. RoboTwin-Plan evaluates generated videos in simulation, and converting those videos into robot actions is left to future work (§§4.1, F).
- **Analyst inference.** The main generation comparison changes causal structure and adaptive stopping together, so it cannot isolate their contributions. The CFT mechanism probe is also conditional on finding a successful resample, so its correction rate applies to that selected subset (Table 1, §D.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Zero-shot comparisons use subsets and relaxed manual scoring. Text-planner comparisons use a shared verifier but require different outputs, so they compare success under distinct generation requirements (Table 1, §B, §D.8).
- **Analyst inference.** The sampling analysis uses fixed seeds, and the supplied results give no independent-training-run uncertainty. Narrow margins, such as CFT’s lead over sample-level RFT on the original RoboTwin-Plan test set, are therefore hard to interpret (Table 5, §D.4, Table S5).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does learned stopping contribute on its own?** The generation comparison bundles it with causal factorization. Train and evaluate causal models with learned EOS or a fixed horizon across matched task-length groups.
2. **Can a learned verifier support CFT when task rules are difficult to encode?** The robot verifier uses a learned state reader, but explicit rules still identify failures. Compare corrections collected by a learned judge and an explicit verifier on the same rollouts, then have humans audit the resulting trajectories.
3. **Can CFT improve robot planning while preserving visual fidelity?** On the shifted robot sets, CFT reduced unfinished plans but increased visual defects (Table S14). Vary the share of expert trajectories during CFT and measure rule violations, completion, and visual defects on the same held-out layouts.
4. **Can one model cover all three benchmarks and transfer to held-out task families?** Training is benchmark-specific. Compare a joint model with the reported specialists and generalists under matched per-task data budgets, then test it on a held-out task family.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and benchmark comparisons, which are documented in the main text and supplement. The supplied text does not establish public availability of code, checkpoints, or the newly built evaluation assets. It also gives no independent-training-run uncertainty estimates or measurements of real-world robot execution.
