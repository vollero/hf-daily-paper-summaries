# UUBench: Understanding the Unspoken toward Full-Duplex Multimodal Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30157) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30157)


## One-sentence takeaway

On UUBench’s 216 shared clips, all nine digital human systems earned positive overall audiovisual quality scores but negative mean intent-fulfillment scores for each of the four visual cue dimensions, according to the benchmark’s automated judge (§4.2, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

Users convey needs such as acknowledgment or reassurance through wording, vocal delivery, facial behavior, and gestures. The authors argue that existing benchmarks offer limited evidence about whether a generated reply actually addresses those needs. A reply can sound natural and stay on topic while overlooking what the speaker wants from the exchange (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Annotated benchmark.** Human reviewers selected 216 evaluation clips from 28,548 candidates screened from TalkVerse, TalkCuts, TalkVid, and SpeakerVid-5M. Annotators recorded each speaker’s inferred intent, applicable auditory and visual cues, and the evidence supporting their interpretation. Systems received the original video and audio with annotations withheld (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Two scoring perspectives.** Process-oriented scoring rates how well a reply addresses the need conveyed by each applicable cue dimension. Fixed, clip-specific weights based on embedding similarity between the annotated evidence and cue labels yield the aggregate intent score \(S\). Outcome-oriented scoring averages naturalness, appropriateness, and human-likeness ratings across audio-only, visual-only, and joint audiovisual views of the reply to produce \(Q\) (§§3.5–3.6).
- **Evidence-grounded judge and comparison.** The JEV module scores one dimension or criterion at a time using timestamped observations. Technical measurements supplement selected outcome ratings. The authors applied the protocol to five closed-source and four open-source systems using shared clips and a common prompt (§§3.4, 4.1, Fig. 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Positive overall quality coexisted with negative visual cue scores | Across nine systems on 216 shared clips, overall \(Q\) ranged from 1.01 to 2.03. Every system’s mean for each visual primitive was negative, spanning −0.35 to −0.02 across systems and dimensions. Both scores use a −3 to +3 scale. | §4.2, Table 3 |
| Quality and intent fulfillment had different leaders | On the shared set, Emma led all systems on \(Q\) (2.03), while Tavus led on \(S\) (0.47). CyberVerse with SoulX-LiveAct led the four open-source systems on both \(Q\) (1.64) and \(S\) (0.18). | §4.2, Fig. 1 |
| Speech function was handled more successfully | Eight of nine systems had positive mean speech-function scores. Tavus led that dimension at 1.24 across 214 applicable clips. | §4.4, Table 3 |
| Visible naturalness and human-likeness diverged | On the shared set, all nine systems scored positively for visual naturalness, while five scored negatively for visual human-likeness under the visual-only reply condition. | §§3.5, 4.3 |

The authors interpret the visual scores as a shared difficulty in addressing needs conveyed by visible behavior. The results establish that pattern under UUBench’s judge on the selected clips. Human agreement remains unmeasured, co-occurring cues prevent causal attribution to vision, and the single-reply setup leaves live full-duplex performance untested (§3.6, Limitations, §6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The curated set has limited coverage of intents, contexts, and cues. Some dimensions have few applicable clips. Independent human validation of JEV and the weighting scheme, systematic observation and weighting ablations, and checks across additional sources and systems remain outstanding (Limitations, §6).
- **Analyst inference.** Because JEV scores generated observation records and visual descriptions use sampled frames, brief cues could be lost before scoring. The evaluation leaves open how much the reported visual shortfall reflects observation errors (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference.** Closed-source timing stops at speech onset, while open-source timing includes generation and rendering or recording. A pooled latency ranking would compare different endpoints (§4.5, Fig. 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Do JEV’s scores and relevance weights agree with independent human judgments?** The paper reports no such validation. Have independent raters score individual dimensions and overall fulfillment, then compare judge agreement and test evidence weighting against equal weighting with primitive scores held fixed (Limitations, §6).
2. **Do the systems use visible cues when generating replies?** The reported primitive means combine co-occurring cues. Generate paired replies with and without user video, keeping audio and system configuration fixed, then have blinded raters assess both replies against the original audiovisual clip (§3.6).
3. **Does the visual cue shortfall persist in live conversation?** The benchmark evaluates one reply per prerecorded clip. Test live multi-turn exchanges where intent develops or changes, and measure acknowledgment, repair, and response timing (§6).
