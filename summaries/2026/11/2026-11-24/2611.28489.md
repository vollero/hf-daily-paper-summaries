# Open Vision Banana: An Open Framework for Unified Visual Perception through Generation in RGB Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28489) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28489)


## One-sentence takeaway

Open Vision Banana uses one shared adapter to generate RGB outputs for four perception tasks, and its evaluation shows that monocular depth structure is learned early while absolute scale remains unstable and depth gains can coincide with segmentation losses.

## Problem and motivation

The authors ask whether a public image generator can reproduce the perception capabilities reported for the closed Vision Banana system. They identify two gaps: the closed model and its training data are unavailable for controlled comparison, and an RGB prediction can become unusable if its encoder, instruction, and decoder disagree even as training loss falls (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Validated task contracts.** One specification defines the target encoder, instruction, and deterministic decoder for each task. Pretraining checks cover RGB target validity, instruction compatibility, adapter coverage, and train–evaluation image overlap (§3.1, Table 1, Fig. 2).
- **Shared adaptation on a public backbone.** A single rank-64 adapter is trained by conditional flow matching on a frozen Qwen-Image-2.1 checkpoint that already incorporates two earlier adapters. At inference, an image and task instruction condition generation, and the task decoder converts the RGB output to metric depths, class IDs, or masks (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Diagnostic study and artifacts.** The authors use training trajectories, controlled short runs, seed ensembles, and exact weight-space merges to examine absolute scale, task trade-offs, and checkpoint selection. They report releasing code, configurations, checkpoints, training targets, and evaluation outputs (§5, Appendix H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Depth structure is learned early, with a large absolute-scale error | On NYUv2, single-sample median-aligned δ₁ spans 0.886–0.933 across evaluated training checkpoints, using ground truth to rescale each image. Unaligned δ₁ spans 0.111–0.497. | §5.1, Fig. 4, Table 12 |
| Sampling changes metric depth accuracy | On full NYUv2 validation with the earlier C1 run and no retraining, unaligned δ₁ rises from 0.348 with one sample to 0.436 with five seeds and 0.516 with ten. On uncapped DIODE-Indoor, AbsRel falls from 686.77 to 0.377 with ten seeds. | §5.2, Table 4, Appendix A |
| Depth and segmentation trade off in run B | From the 17.5k EMA adapter to the final 100k adapter, single-sample unaligned NYUv2 δ₁ rises from 0.407 to 0.497, while Cityscapes mIoU falls from 0.355 to 0.298, RefCOCOg cIoU from 0.518 to 0.481, and ReasonSeg gIoU from 0.431 to 0.333. | Table 2, §4 |
| Merging yields consistent gains over interpolation on Cityscapes and consistent losses on ReasonSeg | Across six run-B weight merges, Cityscapes mIoU exceeds the coefficient-weighted parent scores by 0.66–1.72 points, while ReasonSeg gIoU falls 0.14–1.20 points below them. Every merge trails the balanced checkpoint on at least one benchmark. | §5.4, Tables 8–9 |
| Lower flow loss tracks worse Cityscapes accuracy | Across EMA checkpoints selected for successive validation-loss minima, Spearman correlations between loss reduction and Cityscapes mIoU are −0.88 in run A and −0.89 in run B. The correlations for unaligned NYUv2 δ₁ and ReasonSeg gIoU change sign between runs. | §5.5, Table 10 |

Ground-truth alignment and ground-truth-free ensembling support the authors’ interpretation that scale error and sampling variability both matter. The ensemble tests one earlier checkpoint, so its gains leave the size of any persistent per-image bias unresolved. The checkpoint trajectories show trade-offs in this setup, while the closed Vision Banana scores provide context under partly undisclosed settings and a different ReasonSeg query pipeline (§3.4, Appendix A). Table 9 also qualifies §5.4’s claim that only Cityscapes beats interpolation: some depth and RefCOCOg results do so as well, while Cityscapes does so across all six merges.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** All experiments use Qwen-Image-2.1. Checkpoints and merge recipes were inspected on the evaluation benchmarks, and P5 and P6 weights were chosen after viewing results. The closed reference’s settings are partly undisclosed, and it rewrites ReasonSeg queries before segmentation (§3.4, §6, Appendix A).
- **Author-stated.** Seed ensembling multiplies inference cost by the number of samples. The optional scale loss remains unevaluated, and editing and generation quality are illustrated qualitatively (§5.2, §6, Fig. 7).
- **Analyst inference.** Instance segmentation has a specified decoder and selected examples, but the supplied text gives no aggregate instance score. The short full-fine-tuning comparison uses one recipe and evaluation seed, so it cannot isolate adapter capacity as the cause of referring degradation (Table 3, §5.3, Fig. 6).
- **Analyst inference.** Uncapped DIODE-Indoor AbsRel is sensitive to rare extreme decoded depths. Its large ensemble improvement should be read alongside δ₁, which also improves but measures a different aspect of error (Table 4, Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **How much residual depth error is stable per-image scale bias?** C1 improves through ten seeds, leaving the saturation point unknown. Extend seed sweeps across multiple checkpoints and measure within-image scale variation and residual bias against ground truth (§5.2).
2. **Can explicit scale supervision improve metric depth while preserving segmentation?** The authors supply an optional log-depth residual loss without evaluating it. Compare otherwise identical runs with and without that loss, then score unaligned depth and segmentation on separately held-out data (§6).
3. **Does shared adapter capacity drive the observed task trade-off?** The reported mixture, learning-rate, and merge experiments leave that mechanism unresolved. Compare shared and task-specific adapters with matched total trainable parameters, training data, and update budgets across repeated runs (§§5.3–5.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported within-study results. Cross-backbone transfer and the causes of task competition remain uncertain. The supplied text lacks aggregate instance-segmentation and editing scores, matched closed-reference settings, repeated-training uncertainty estimates, and a complete reconciliation of the headline 42-checkpoint count with the displayed checkpoint rows and correlation subsets (§3.4, Table 10, Fig. 7, Appendix J).
