# SAVIOR: Sample-efficient Adaptation of Vision-Language Models for OCR Representation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27319) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27319)


## One-sentence takeaway

Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL on 2,234 OCR examples selected for recurring failure modes improved word recall, layout similarity, and downstream document QA F1 for both tested model sizes on SAVIOR-Bench and its QA subset (§3.2, Tables 4 and 7).

## Problem and motivation

OCR systems can transcribe most of a financial document while missing a vendor name embedded in a logo, a rotated field label, or a fine-print clause. They can also recover the words but associate them with the wrong field in a dense layout. The authors argue that standard benchmarks underrepresent several of these failure modes and that word-level metrics alone miss spatial errors that matter for extraction and question answering (§§1, 3.1–3.2, 4.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Failure-driven curation and adaptation.** Annotators audited OCR errors and used the recurring patterns to select 2,234 document–OCR pairs for SAVIOR-Train. Most documents were real, with controlled orientation variants added for rare extreme vertical text. The authors fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct 3B and 7B using a structured OCR prompt and LoRA on the final vision-encoder layer and language decoder (§§3.1–3.4, 5).
- **SAVIOR-Bench.** The authors assembled a 509-document test set with expert OCR annotations and evaluated downstream QA on a subset. The benchmark combines material from public sources with in-house synthetic documents (§§3.3, 6.3).
- **PaIRS.** The proposed layout metric maps reference and predicted words to two-dimensional text grids and scores how well pairwise horizontal, vertical, and Euclidean distances are preserved (§4.3, Fig. 4).

The results support the effectiveness of the combined adaptation recipe on the targeted benchmark. Holding the QA reader fixed makes the choice of OCR output the comparison, but the experiment cannot isolate the effect of improved transcription from that of improved layout. The authors claim that targeted curation can outperform scale-driven training (§7). The reported comparisons show gains over untuned models and native baselines, without a matched training-data comparison that would establish that claim.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ caveats.** Output formats can change PaIRS scores through grid conversion. §5.2 limits full PaIRS comparisons to models that can produce compatible sequential text, yet Tables 4–6 report scores for several fixed-format systems. The supplied text leaves their exact scoring procedure unclear. The authors also report that the cost of double annotation restricted the agreement study to a subset (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The reported experiments do not separate the effects of targeted selection, synthetic orientation variants, structured prompting, and LoRA placement. Both adapted models come from the same family, and QA uses one reader and a failure-targeted subset. Transfer to other model families, document populations, or QA setups remains uncertain (§§3.2–3.4, 5, 6.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The deployment results establish throughput and VRAM use for the reported A100 setup. Without an unquantized serving comparison or measurements on smaller GPUs, the memory and latency benefits of quantization remain unquantified (§6.5, Table 9).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does failure-driven selection improve sample efficiency?** The paper reports no matched random-sampling control or learning curve. Fine-tune the same backbone on curated and randomly sampled subsets at several matched sizes, using the same prompt and LoRA configuration (§§3.2, 5–6).
2. **How much of the QA gain comes from layout?** The OCR outputs vary in both word recovery and structure. Give the same QA reader versions of each transcript with identical words but preserved, flattened, or perturbed layout (§6.3).
3. **Does the recipe transfer to new document populations and model families?** The reported adaptation uses Qwen2.5-VL and a benchmark designed around the training failure modes. Repeat the comparison with another pretrained VLM and independently sourced test documents, reporting scores by document type and failure mode (§§3.3, 5, 7).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied tables support the benchmark results directly. Material gaps include the exact PaIRS equations and word-matching rules, conversion details for fixed-format OCR outputs, QA F1 scoring and aggregation, uncertainty estimates for model comparisons, and the availability of training data, benchmark annotations, code, and checkpoints. The paper specifies request-based access for SAVIOR-Bench test images only (§1 footnote 2, §§4.3, 5.2, 6).
