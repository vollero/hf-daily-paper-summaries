# ViDiHand2: Unifying Video Diffusion Priors and Hand Geometry for 3D Hand Motion Reconstruction in the Wild

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30597) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30597)


## One-sentence takeaway

ViDiHand2 fits predicted hand geometry to camera rays across egocentric video clips, reducing absolute 3D joint error with a missed-detection penalty from 56.55 mm for the strongest retrained baseline to 32.59 mm on held-out Gen-HumanEgo (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

The paper aims to recover both hands’ metric 3D trajectories in camera coordinates from monocular egocentric video, with the goal of extracting supervision for robot manipulation. The authors identify two gaps in existing methods. Crop-based pipelines can lose hands under occlusion, and full-frame video diffusion methods can recover occluded hands but struggle to place them at the correct depth when camera intrinsics differ from training. Occlusion, image truncation, and coarse feature grids also weaken 2D localization (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Fine-grained geometric lifting and clip-level ray fitting.** ViDiHand2 estimates the wrist trajectory by fitting predicted 2D joint rays to metric joint-pair distances from MANO. The clip-wide objective combines uncertainty-weighted distance and reprojection terms with a wrist-acceleration penalty (§3.2, Eq. 5).
- **Joint refinement and confidence-gated fusion.** Fine heatmaps refine in-frame locations, and an unbounded regression branch handles joints beyond the image border. A learned gate combines back-projected joints with fitted MANO joints according to localization confidence (§3.3, Fig. 4).
- **Video feature and temporal modeling.** A LoRA-adapted Wan2.2 encoder exposes task tokens to multiple transformer blocks. A learned decoder produces video-rate predictions, and cached attention carries pose context across chunks (§3.4).

The penalized metric combines localization error and missed detections. The smaller gap against WiLoR on the shared-detection subset suggests that detection coverage accounts for much of the full-set advantage against that baseline, although the subset also changes which cases are evaluated. The depth ablations support a placement benefit from geometric fitting, while the focal sweep tests sensitivity when the supplied intrinsics are updated consistently with image rescaling. The evidence leaves performance under calibration error and downstream robot-learning gains open. Jitter measures predicted smoothness independently of ground-truth motion (§A.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitations.** ViDiHand2 needs camera intrinsics and runs offline, making it unsuitable for closed-loop control (§5).
- **Analyst inference.** The quantitative in-the-wild test uses one camera stream from Gen-HumanEgo. The additional datasets in Fig. 6 provide qualitative image alignment without reliable metric ground truth, so transfer across a broader range of cameras remains uncertain (§A.1, Table S1, Fig. 6).
- **Analyst inference.** Evaluation uses filtered frames and scores visible ground-truth hands. Some in-domain splits also share participants or scenes, which limits what the results establish about fully out-of-view motion, unusable footage, and generalization to new subjects (§§A.2–A.5).

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive is reconstruction to inaccurate intrinsics?** The focal sweep changes video and intrinsics together. Perturb focal length and principal point while holding video fixed, then measure depth bias and MPJPE-abs.
2. **Can causal fitting preserve accuracy at low latency?** The reported throughput uses offline whole-clip fitting. Compare a causal sliding-window fit with the offline model on the same clips, reporting latency, absolute joint error, and error during fast motion.
3. **Do the recovered trajectories improve robot imitation?** The supplied experiments evaluate reconstruction quality. Train the same imitation policy on trajectories from ViDiHand2 and from a strong baseline, then compare held-out task success.
