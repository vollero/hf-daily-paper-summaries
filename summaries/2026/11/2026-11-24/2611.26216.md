# Frontier models need only one text to unmask its human or AI author

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26216) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26216)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that frontier language models can match specialized AI-writing detectors on selected comparisons and identify source labs or human writers from a single text, with attribution accuracy varying sharply by lab, author familiarity, and dataset.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks what a single document reveals about who wrote its prose, who supplied its ideas, and which model or person produced it. These distinctions matter for AI-use accusations, double-blind peer review, and online pseudonymity. Prior work had already separated prose from ideas, and a recent evaluation had found that some prompted models were competitive at prose detection. The gap the authors address is how far one zero-shot setup can extend across both kinds of detection and into attribution without a supplied candidate pool, reference writing, or external tools. (§§1–4, 6)

## Approach and contributions

- **Prose and idea detection.** A single prompt asks for separate AI-share estimates and supporting evidence for prose and ideas. The study pools 24 public benchmarks and uses a common sample to compare 72 models over time. (§2.1, Figures 2–3)
- **Model attribution.** The authors assemble a corpus of fiction, news, and op-eds from 34 models across 11 labs, reusing some existing generations. They score free-form predictions at the lab, family, and exact-model levels and aggregate explanations into lab writing profiles. (§3, Tables 2–3)
- **Human attribution and privacy.** The authors evaluate open-world name or username predictions on private and recent texts across six domains. They test whether name-only familiarity predicts attribution and examine how reasoning effort and paraphrasing affect accuracy. (§§4–5)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Frontier models were competitive with trained detectors | On 36,427 commonly answered documents from 24 benchmarks, GPT-6.1 Sol, Opus 5.5, and Gemini 4 Carbon scored 89–90% case-macro idea accuracy versus IdeaLens at 87%. On the three prose cases scored for Pangram 4.1, they reached 98–99% versus 98%. | §§2.1–2.2, Appendix B.2 |
| Observed prose false positives were rare | On 9,687 trusted fully human English documents, Gemini 4 Carbon flagged one as AI (0.01%) and Pangram 4.1 flagged zero. | §2.2, Table 10 |
| Lab attribution depended strongly on the source lab | On the 34-model, 11-lab corpus, Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5, and Gemini 4 Argon each achieved at least 98% lab accuracy on Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google texts. Overall lab accuracy was 60%, 50%, and 45%, respectively. Fable scored 35% on the other eight labs and named the exact model for one of 1,792 texts from 2026 releases. | §3.1, Table 2 |
| Private reviews were attributable | On the 15,136-review ICML 2020 corpus, Fable 5.2 identified reviewers with 55.4% accuracy under lenient name matching and 50.1% under exact matching. Opus 5.5 scored 48.8% under the lenient rule. | §§4.1–4.2, Figure 4 |
| Reddit accuracy varied with account activity | In the sample of 1,258 comments of at least 200 words, Fable 5.2 identified 31% of comments overall among answered texts and 69% in the subgroup with more than 500 posts or comments in the same subreddit over the activity window. Its author-macro accuracy was 24%. | §§4.1–4.2, 5.1, Figure 6, Tables 28–29 |
| Familiarity predicted attribution | For Opus 5.5 on Reddit, name-only familiarity distinguished accounts it identified from those it missed with an AUC of 0.89, versus 0.78 for posting activity. | §5.1 |
| Paraphrasing reduced attribution accuracy | On 1,236 Reddit comments with valid Opus 5.5 judgments for the original and both Argon rewrites, username accuracy fell from 16.8% to 10.4% averaged across the two rewrites. | §5.2, Table 40 |

The private-review and post-release results support the authors’ claim that prior access to the exact test text is unnecessary for some successful attributions. The evidence leaves the relative roles of style, topic, and contextual clues unresolved. Lab accuracy on the three best-recognized providers measures success on texts from those providers. Precision when a detector *names* one of those providers can be considerably lower (Appendix C.2.3, Table 23).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated constraints.** The full detection comparison excludes refusals and ambiguous labels. Fable 5.2 ran only on the smaller detection sample because of cost. Pangram’s prose comparison omits the lightly edited case because the benchmark and detector use different definitions. The authors caution that rare errors preclude definitive false-positive rankings and that post-release news authorship lacks independent verification. (§§2–2.3)
- **Authors’ stated constraints.** Frontier training corpora are inaccessible, so familiarity measures indirect evidence of exposure. The ICML data and predictions are withheld for privacy, and the IRB protocol limited follow-up tests on that corpus, including topic-only comparisons, self-citation checks, and paraphrasing. (§5.1, Appendix D.4, Ethical considerations)
- **Analyst inference.** The headline Reddit rates have limited applicability to short posts or less technical communities given the sampling criteria and cross-community variation. Likewise, full-text gains and agreement between explanations leave room for retained content cues and differences in the prompts used for topic-only comparisons. (§§4.1–4.3, Appendices D.1–D.4.3)

## Open research questions

1. **How much attribution survives when topic and identity clues are controlled?** The paper retains some indirect clues, and its topic-only paper comparison changes both the input and the question. A follow-up could use consented, topic-matched texts from multiple writers and compare intact and clue-masked versions under identical prompts. (§4.3, Appendices D.1, D.4.3)
2. **How well do low false-positive rates transfer to independently verified recent writing?** The post-release news check uses Pangram labels as a reference. A follow-up could collect provenance-verified human and AI texts after model release and compare sensitivity at a fixed false-positive rate across genres. (§2.3)
3. **Can privacy-preserving edits reduce attribution while retaining meaning?** The reported defense evaluation uses one rewriting model and leaves residual accuracy. A follow-up could compare style-only and clue-removing edits on consented texts, with human ratings of meaning preservation. (§5.2, Table 40)

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text was truncated in the middle. It supports the main reported comparisons, while several referenced tables appear only as captions or prose summaries. Material gaps include full per-model and per-dataset results, complete subgroup counts, the full benchmark composition, and the exact prompt text. The private ICML texts and reviewer-level predictions are also unavailable by design. The introduction and §5.1 give different low-to-maximum reasoning-effort endpoints for slightly familiar Reddit accounts, so the precise gain cannot be reconciled from the excerpt.
