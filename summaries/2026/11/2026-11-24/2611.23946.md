# How Complete Must an Environment Be to Train a Capable GUI Agent?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23946) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23946)


## One-sentence takeaway

In MobileGym, restricting reward to one solution path or filtering unrelated records reduced full-environment success, while pruning dispensable features improved success on unchanged tasks under response averaging, a benefit that reversed on a held-out retention set when episodes received equal weight (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

Executable GUI environments are costly to build, so builders often simplify verification, populate fewer records, or implement fewer functions. The authors identify a gap in prior comparisons: they do not isolate how each of these choices affects training when the agent must later operate with the original records and controls (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Coverage taxonomy and controlled study.** The authors distinguish rewarded solution paths, application records beyond task needs, and application functions beyond task needs. They vary one dimension at a time while training Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, preserve task instructions and native goal checks, and evaluate all main policies in the original MobileGym environment (§3).
2. **Episode-weighting experiment.** Starting from a shared checkpoint, the authors cross feature pruning with response averaging or equal episode weighting. They measure retention on previously practiced templates omitted from further training and use partial balancing to localize the effect (§5).
3. **MobileGym-Lite.** The authors build a training variant that retains all records and native goal rewards while selectively pruning dispensable functions and preserving nearby navigation (§6).

## Evidence and main results

Success requires native goal satisfaction and valid termination. Unless otherwise indicated, scores below use the 465 full-environment evaluation episodes across three seeds (§3.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Single-path reward reduced success | At step 16, the restricted verifier yielded 191/465 successes versus 240/465 with Full, a 10.53 percentage-point drop. In an alarm-deletion rollout group, valid edit-sheet solutions received negative advantage under path restriction and positive advantage under native goal reward. | §4.1, Table 1 |
| Record filtering hurt at the endpoint | At step 16, R0, R1, and R2, which retained up to zero, one, or two unrelated records per reviewed list, scored 47.96%, 47.31%, and 49.68% versus Full’s 51.61%. All three led Full at step 8. The endpoint deficit was concentrated on seen templates. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Feature pruning helped on unchanged tasks | At step 16, F0 reached 261/465 (56.13%) versus Full’s 240/465 (51.61%); F1 reached 242/465. F0 gained 27 successes on 213 unmodified instances, lost one on 183 affected instances, and lost five on 69 unseen-template instances. | §4.3, Table 3, Figure 2 |
| Episode weighting reversed the pruning effect on retention | In continuations from the same F0 step-12 checkpoint, F0 scored 80/153 versus Full’s 62/153 under response averaging, and 70/153 versus Full’s 80/153 under equal episode weighting, an 18.30 percentage-point interaction. Balancing only episodes on affected templates produced 80/153 versus 79/153. | §§5.2–5.3, Tables 4–5, Figure 3 |
| Lite improved success and reduced source requirements | At step 16, Lite reached 258/465 (55.48%) with narrow clipping after step 8, versus Full’s 240/465 (51.61%) with wide clipping throughout. Under wide clipping throughout, Lite reached 250/465. Lite omitted 87 template–module pairs across 72 training templates and reduced application source lines required per pruned template by 14.05% on average. | §§6.2–6.4, Tables 6–7 |

The controlled variants establish how each tested reduction performed in this MobileGym setup. The continuation experiment supports the authors’ explanation that response averaging amplified long successful detours on affected templates and contributed to losses on other tasks. Paired outcomes also show that some of F0’s lead came from newly solved instances. The weighting reversal is established for the tested late-training continuation and retention set. Both selective pruning and the clipping schedule contribute to the headline Lite comparison (§§5.2–5.3, 6.3–6.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The study uses one base model and one simulator. The reward experiment keeps alternative routes executable and leaves open what happens when those routes are removed while tasks remain solvable (§7).
- **Analyst inference.** Evaluating filtered policies on full lists captures the cost of restoring those records at evaluation. The supplied record-filtering results leave open how much of the deficit comes from the change in list length between training and evaluation (§§3.3, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The retention experiment starts from an F0 checkpoint and changes weighting only during the training tail, so the interaction may depend on prior training and the remaining curriculum (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Source lines and coding-agent tokens measure specific aspects of construction cost. The token comparison uses a rebuild described as close to F0, which limits what it establishes about the cost of constructing Lite itself (§6.2, Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the pruning-by-weighting interaction replicate across models and simulators?** The paper tests one of each. Repeat the environment-by-weighting comparison with multiple starting policies and a second executable GUI environment (§§5.2, 7).
2. **How much of the record-filtering penalty comes from evaluating on longer lists?** The reported comparison evaluates every filtered policy in Full. Cross full and filtered training with full and filtered evaluation while preserving task-relevant records and goal checks (§§3.2–3.3, 4.2).
3. **What happens when alternative routes are removed from the environment?** The reward experiment changes which routes earn credit while leaving them executable. Compare native-goal training with and without verified alternative routes, keeping a valid route available for each task (§§3.2, 7).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported comparisons within MobileGym.
