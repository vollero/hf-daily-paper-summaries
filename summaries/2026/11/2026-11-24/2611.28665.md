# Seeing Right, Writing Wrong: Where Short Chain-of-Thought Breaks Spatial VQA

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28665) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28665)


## One-sentence takeaway

In the tested vision-language models, short chain-of-thought often reduced sensitivity to the image after a spatial relation was written, and about four in five losses relative to direct answering on three occlusion sets involved either a false written relation or a wrong answer derived from a true one (§§5–6, Table 4).

## Problem and motivation

Short chain-of-thought (CoT) can hurt spatial visual question answering even when the image remains in context. Zhang asks whether this comes from extra tokens, turn formatting, reduced access to the image, or the content of the rationale itself. Aggregate accuracy can obscure the failure because CoT corrects some direct-answer errors while introducing others (§§1, 4, 7, 10).

## Approach and contributions

- **Locate where the answer changes.** Compare direct option-letter readouts with readouts after each rationale sentence. Length-matched filler, turn-format controls, and image re-presentation test alternative explanations. The sentence-level audit identifies where the correct option first loses its lead and never regains it (§§3–5).
- **Test whether the image or the rationale drives the answer.** Hold each model’s own conclusion-removed rationale fixed while swapping the image for a counterpart with opposite ground truth. Complement the swaps with claim edits and attention knockouts, then decompose the accuracy change into written-relation accuracy and errors in mapping the relation to an answer (§§5–7).
- **Test whether models can consult the image again at the answer step.** Compare closed API models and run a single-model LoRA pilot that trains on fixed rationales with labels determined by the displayed image (§§8–9).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Format controls explain little of the main-pool loss. | On the 2,850-item pool, turn-closed length-matched filler changed accuracy by −2.3 to +0.7 points versus Direct across eight open models. Re-showing the image and restating the question together recovered at most 1.5 points, while CoT significantly lowered accuracy by 1.4–9.2 points in six models. | §4, Table 2 |
| Writing changes cue use and can introduce or misapply a relation. | CoT weakened the lower-is-closer cue in all 13 open models tested on the cue-factorial set. Among 3,014 Direct-correct, CoT-wrong cases pooled across three occlusion sets and seven core models, 56.6% had a false final body relation, 23.8% had a true relation paired with a wrong answer, and 19.6% had no explicit relation. After a true relation, conclusion errors were 29.5% on converse questions versus 2.4% on restatements. | §§5–6, Fig. 3, Table 4 |
| Fixed rationales substantially reduce image sensitivity. | Across seven core models, the discrete-answer image effect on rendered twins ranged from 3.0–76.5 points under Direct and 2.0–8.9 with the rationale fixed. The seven-model mean fell from 69.6 to 3.4 points on mirrored CV-Bench photos on the discrete-answer scale and from 20.5 to 3.0 on COCO composites on the forced-readout probability scale. | §6, Tables 1, 5–6 |
| Correcting the first claim can improve the answer. | On parsed claims from Synthetic Depth and rendered twins with “in front” phrasing, an oracle correction followed by greedy continuation beat Direct accuracy by 14.3–34.9 points in five of six core models. Molmo-7B-D lost 12.3 points under that edit phrasing. | §6, Table 7 |
| The net accuracy effect varies by setting. | On the frozen 767-item BLINK and 3DSRBench pool, per-model CoT-minus-Direct accuracy ranged from −3.1 to +1.3 points, with every confidence interval including zero. Across 13 instruct-model natural-depth cells on CV-Bench and BLINK, written-relation accuracy minus Direct accuracy predicted the CoT-minus-Direct change with 1.31 points mean absolute error. | §7, Tables 9–10 |
| Answer-step image checking is possible. | On hard occlusion swaps with fixed conclusion-removed text, three of six closed models retained image effects of 74–92 points, close to their regenerated-rationale effects. In the Qwen3-VL-8B pilot with base rationales fixed, the image effect rose from 1.3 points to 90.7 with matched training and 93.4 with counterfactual training on mirrored CV-Bench photos. | §§8–9.2, Tables 13, 15 |

The swaps and claim edits provide intervention evidence that both the displayed image and the written claim can influence the answer, with the claim often dominating under fixed-rationale conditions. The author interprets this as a failure to consult the image again at the answer step. The results support that interpretation for the tested protocols. The loss rates and aggregate accuracy changes vary by task and model, and the closed-model comparison leaves the causal role of answer-turn reasoning unresolved (§§6–8).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Synthetic Depth confounds drawn size with occlusion, so high Direct accuracy there cannot identify which cue the model used. The commit-sentence drop follows from its definition, and the before/after swap analysis on loss cases is exploratory and selection-constrained (§§3, 5–6).
- **Author-reported.** Closed-model Direct calls can include hidden reasoning, and answer-turn reasoning settings and reporting differ across APIs. The pilot uses one model, and mirrored CV-Bench tests transfer to photographs using a question template already seen in rendered training (§§8–9.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Residual conclusion text can affect the estimated strength of lock-in because stricter removal materially increased image sensitivity for some models. Relation extraction also limits the accuracy decomposition where few rationales contain an identifiable claim. The small human check supports the audited sentence labels, while broader annotation validity remains uncertain (§§5–7).

## Open research questions

1. **Does answer-turn reasoning cause re-grounding on hard occlusion items?** The API comparison confounds model identity with reasoning settings, and zero-reasoning subsets are self-selected. Randomize supported reasoning settings within the same models and reuse the same fixed rationales and image pairs (§8).
2. **Does the training result transfer across model families and question templates?** The pilot tunes one model and reuses the mirrored left/right template. Replicate matched and counterfactual training across model families, then test unseen templates and report image sensitivity, accuracy, and retention together (§9.2).
3. **How much can explicit relation-to-question mapping help on natural converse queries?** Converse errors are concentrated in the occlusion analysis, while the tested body-relation intervention did not beat Direct on pooled natural depth. Build paired converse and restatement questions for the same natural images and compare deterministic mapping with ordinary CoT under fixed, verified relations (§§6, 9.1).
