# NoHairCache: Calibrate-Once KV Cache Eviction with One Power-Law Exponent

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29006) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29006)


## One-sentence takeaway

On Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct at a 512-token cache budget, NoHairCache used a single WikiText-103 calibration to achieve significantly lower perplexity than H2O and StreamingLLM on WikiText-103, PG19, and arXiv (§4.4, Table 4).

## Problem and motivation

The key–value (KV) cache grows with sequence length during autoregressive decoding, so long-context inference needs a way to choose which cached tokens to discard under a fixed budget. The paper identifies a gap between two common heuristics: H2O favors tokens with high cumulative attention, while StreamingLLM favors recent tokens and protects designated attention sinks. A common scoring rule lets the authors ask whether one offline calibration can balance attention and recency across domains (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **A unified eviction score.** NoHairCache ranks tokens within each head by cumulative attention multiplied by a power-law age penalty, \(A_i(t)(t-i)^{-\alpha}\). Setting \(\alpha=0\) recovers H2O’s scoring term. At a fixed step with positive, finite attention ratios, a sufficiently large exponent recovers pure recency ordering. StreamingLLM adds sink retention. The prior no-hair bound constrains the exponent within the chosen power-law family (§§3.1–3.2, Appendix C).
- **A training-free calibration and scorer.** The method fits decay exponents from hidden-state differences between paired prompts with shared continuations, then freezes the exponents and precomputes age weights. The default uses one exponent per layer, with a calibrated global scalar and a fixed scalar also available (§3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **An empirical test of calibration transfer.** The paper compares hidden-state estimates with independent perplexity sweeps and reuses the WikiText-103 calibration across corpora, budgets, and QA tasks. A sensitivity comparison finds little benefit from preserving the calibrated layer ordering on the tested slice (§§4.3–4.7).

The results support reusing a WikiText-103 calibration on the tested PG19 and arXiv samples. The small differences among calibrated variants suggest that a global scalar may suffice in the tested setting. The evidence leaves open whether the exponent is intrinsically corpus-independent, whether the scorer improves retrieval of middle-context information, and whether the simulated gains translate into deployment speed or memory savings.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The evaluation covers models up to 1.5B parameters and sequences up to 2,048 tokens. StreamingLLM can win at the tightest budgets, with a corpus-dependent crossover. The paper includes no direct middle-context retrieval benchmark and does not quantify the discrepancy between attention-mass proxies and hidden-state estimates (§§4.5–4.7, 5–6).
- **Analyst inference.** Freezing a WikiText-103 fit demonstrates transfer of an eviction setting. Establishing corpus invariance would require fitting exponents independently on other corpora. The power-law sweep also leaves the proposed advantage over exponential decay untested (§§3.2, 4.1–4.4).
- **Analyst inference.** Mask-based evaluation supports quality comparisons under simulated eviction. The stated lookup and multiplication costs do not quantify end-to-end latency, peak memory, or age-table overhead under physical eviction (§§3.3, 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How stable is the fitted exponent across calibration corpora?** The paper fits WikiText-103 and tests transfer. Fit the same checkpoints independently on PG19 and arXiv, then cross-evaluate the frozen fits at matched budgets.
2. **Does a power-law penalty outperform exponential decay under matched conditions?** The paper motivates the power law analytically. Compare both families using the same calibration data, scoring pipeline, and held-out budgets.
3. **Do the gains persist in longer contexts and translate to runtime benefits?** The reported perplexity evaluation uses mask simulation on 2,048-token sequences. Run physical KV eviction with larger checkpoints and longer inputs, measuring perplexity, decode latency, and peak memory at matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
