# Typhoon OCR 2: From Thai Document Transcription to Multilingual, Multi-Task OCR

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27494) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27494)


## One-sentence takeaway

Typhoon OCR 2 unifies Thai targeted extraction with Thai, English, and Chinese full-page parsing, and its 2B model leads the evaluated open models on two Thai benchmarks while scoring close to its base parser on the reported English and English–Chinese benchmarks (Abstract, §3.2).

## Problem and motivation

A plain transcript leaves document workflows to recover page structure or locate requested fields in a separate step. The authors extend Typhoon OCR 1.5 to answer targeted prompts directly from page images and preserve layout and reading order for downstream processing. They identify a training-data gap in public Thai corpora, which rarely provide fine-grained layout and reading-order annotations (§§1, 2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Prompt-selectable OCR.** The 2B model adapts Infinity-Parser2-Flash and the 0.8B model adapts OvisOCR2. Each supports targeted requests and full-page parsing. The latter returns reading-order JSON with normalized boxes, semantic categories, and content represented as Markdown, HTML, or LaTeX as appropriate (§2.1, Fig. 1).
- **Thai supervision.** The authors filter legacy labels and construct new Thai annotations using larger vision-language models, deterministic checks, an LLM judge, and targeted human box correction. They combine these annotations with public multilingual and instruction data for supervised fine-tuning (§§2.2–2.4, Fig. 2).
- **Sequence-level post-training.** GRPO uses task-specific accuracy and structural penalties. A response that hits the token cap without an end-of-sequence token receives zero reward. The authors report releasing both model sizes, a Python package, and evaluation code (§2.5, Eq. 1, Project Resources).

The reported scores support stronger aggregate Thai performance at 2B within the evaluated open-model set. The multilingual scores give point comparisons for the 2B model on the named benchmarks. Statistical equivalence to the base, Chinese performance in isolation, and multilingual targeted extraction remain unestablished. The loop result measures outputs flagged by the paper’s text heuristics.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitations.** A valid long answer can hit the token cap, and a repeating answer can stop on its own. The authors therefore use separate text heuristics to detect loops at evaluation time. They also report incorrect outputs under vLLM 0.27.0 and use 0.33.0 for the reported setup. The 0.8B GRPO score gain is statistically uncertain (§§2.5, 3.1, Abstract).
- **Analyst inference.** ThaiOCRBench’s single-run aggregate leaves run-to-run variability and task-level gains unresolved. The supplied text gives no component ablation that isolates the EOS rule from the other GRPO reward terms (§3.2, Table 3 caption, §2.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The reported contamination check uses exact image hashes on selected benchmarks. Near-duplicate overlap and the full coverage of the check remain unestablished (§2.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Which reward component reduces loops, and how does it affect dense pages?** The reported comparison bundles task scoring, structural penalties, and the EOS rule. Train matched variants that toggle the EOS rule and repetition penalty, then measure loop rate and page completeness across reference output lengths.
2. **Does targeted extraction transfer to English and Chinese?** The multilingual benchmarks cover full-page parsing, while the targeted-task benchmark is Thai. Evaluate matched chart, form, and question-answering tasks across all three languages with equivalent prompts and task metrics.
3. **How reliable is the GRPO gain at 0.8B?** The abstract calls the gain statistically uncertain and gives no interval. Repeat supervised and GRPO training across seeds and estimate uncertainty in paired MDPBench score and loop-rate differences.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text gives the core method and headline scores explicitly. It ends at the Table 3 column header and omits the table body, detailed benchmark and training-mixture breakdowns, full training and rollout settings, uncertainty estimates, reward-component ablations, and the list of benchmarks checked for exact overlap.
