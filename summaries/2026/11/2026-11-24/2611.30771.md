# GameDojo: Benchmarking Real-Time Interactive Agents in Video Games

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30771) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30771)


## One-sentence takeaway

On GameDojo’s 120 reconstructed encounters, the frontier models tested at medium difficulty recorded zero real-time wins, while GameDojo-4B, fine-tuned on scripted demonstrations, led learned models in real-time progress on the CUA interface at every tested difficulty (§§3.2, 4.2–4.3, Tables 3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Interactive agents must interpret changing observations, anticipate consequences, and act before opportunities expire. The authors argue that whole-game evaluations mix combat with navigation, puzzles, and memory, making control bottlenecks hard to isolate. Modern commercial games also typically offer limited access to source code and internal state. GameDojo uses standalone encounters to make these demands easier to evaluate under controlled conditions (§§1, 3.1–3.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Configurable benchmark.** GameDojo provides 120 independently reconstructed encounters across 20 environments and nine interaction patterns. Its Gymnasium interface supports keyboard-and-mouse CUA controls or game-specific Semantic actions, with time-paused and real-time modes and configurable difficulty (§§3.2–3.3, Table 2, Appendix A.2).
- **Automated demonstrations.** Coding agents use the reconstructed environments’ source code to write state-aware behavior trees. The scripts calibrate difficulty and generate parallel rollouts. Replay and engagement checks yield GameDojo-Data, with 44,368 winning episodes totaling 604.6 hours (§3.4, Appendix A.4).
- **Trained baseline and transfer studies.** GameDojo-4B fine-tunes Qwen3.5-4B on 244.9 hours of demonstrations. The paper also trains visual PPO specialists and studies joint training and transfer across bosses and games (§§4.3–5.3, Table S3).

## Evidence and main results

WR is win rate. PG is the mean fraction of boss health removed, expressed as a percentage.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Frontier models made progress when paused and recorded no real-time wins. | Across 120 medium-difficulty bosses, GPT-6.1-Sol averaged 4.6% WR and 44.8% PG across paused interfaces, versus 0% WR and 2.7% PG in real time. All Table 3 learned models had 0% real-time WR and lower PG than Random, which averaged 7.7% PG. | Table 3, §4.2 |
| Real-time runs recorded far fewer decisions. | In a medium-difficulty CUA diagnostic with one episode per boss, mean recorded decisions per episode fell from 222.3 to 6.7 for GPT-6.1-Sol and from 229.1 to 5.4 for Claude-Opus-5.5 when switching from paused to real-time play. | §4.2 |
| Fine-tuning improved CUA performance. | Across all 120 bosses and three difficulty levels, Table 4 reports average paused WR / PG of 21.4% / 42.9% for GameDojo-4B versus 0.1% / 3.0% for base Qwen3.5-4B. GameDojo-4B led learned models in real-time PG at Easy, Medium, and Hard with 26.3%, 8.2%, and 2.5%, respectively. Its real-time WR was 6.4% on Easy and 0% on Medium and Hard. | Table 4, §§4.3, 5.1 |
| Joint training improved average progress over separate training. | Across 12 Terraria and Doom bosses at matched per-boss training budgets, mean PG rose from 47.7% to 64.7% for paused Easy Semantic PPO and from 69.5% to 87.5% for CUA supervised fine-tuning. | §§4.4, 5.2, Fig. 4, Appendix B |
| Transfer reached held-out bosses and games. | Fixed-source PPO and supervised fine-tuning reached 48.2% and 68.7% mean PG, respectively, from initialization values of 28.3% and 9.5% on 10 held-out Terraria and Doom bosses. In paused Easy CUA, joint fine-tuning on both games gained 14.94 percentage points of mean PG over base Qwen3.5-4B across 108 bosses from unseen reconstructed games. | §§5.2–5.3, Figs. 5–6 |

The results show that the reported real-time protocol is difficult for the tested agents and that scripted supervision improves performance on the benchmark. The decision-count diagnostic is consistent with latency limiting opportunities to act, though it leaves latency’s independent effect unresolved. GameDojo-4B’s main evaluation covers bosses seen during training. Evidence for unseen-game transfer comes from the separate paused Easy CUA experiment.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitation.** Reconstruction may omit implicit mechanics and visual cues. Transfer to the original games and physical environments requires evaluation in those settings (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** The real-time protocol inserts neutral actions during inference delays, and the scripted reference observes privileged state. Model rankings and script gaps therefore reflect serving setup and state access as well as control ability (Appendices C.1–C.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The joint runs match training exposure per boss, so each joint model receives more total experience than each single-boss model. The reported comparison leaves the effect of task diversity at a fixed total budget unresolved (§5.2, Tables S3–S4).
- **Reporting ambiguity.** Table 4 describes three model episodes per boss and a 300-step paused cap. Appendix C.1 describes five seeds and a 180-second, 900-step budget for the self-hosted Qwen3.5-4B and GameDojo-4B checkpoints. The supplied text leaves the exact protocol for those table entries ambiguous.

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the real-time performance drop comes from inference latency?** Timing changes observation freshness and action opportunities together. Evaluate the same checkpoint under injected delays and a common episode horizon, then compare WR and PG (§4.2, Appendix C.1).
2. **How much of the fine-tuning gain comes from valid action formatting?** Format validity and gameplay scores improve together. Compare base and fine-tuned checkpoints under the same constrained CUA decoder (§4.3).
3. **Does broader training improve transfer at a fixed total data budget?** The cross-game study matches data per boss. Compare single-game and mixed-game fine-tuning with equal total demonstration hours and evaluate both on the same unseen environments (§5.3, Fig. 6).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text supports the main findings, but the exact episode count and paused cap for the self-hosted Table 4 entries remain ambiguous. Closed-source per-step latency averages are absent from Table S5. The supplied text also gives no uncertainty intervals for the main or transfer comparisons and no validation against the original games.
