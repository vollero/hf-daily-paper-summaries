# LLMs Memorize Their Own Hallucinations During Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26863) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26863)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across six open models trained on QuALITY and TriviaQA with final-answer rewards, selection of self-generated false claims on multiple-choice probes rose by 4.83 percentage points on average for claims reinforced during training, versus 0.17 points for claims from held-out questions (§3, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

A reasoning trace can contain false claims even when its final answer is correct. In outcome-based reinforcement learning (RL), a correct answer can give the entire rollout a positive training signal, including false claims the model later retracts. The authors identify a gap in prior hallucination research and ask whether models become more likely to reproduce specific false claims they generated during post-training (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Checkpoint-level measurement.** The authors extract atomic false claims from model-generated traces and convert them into multiple-choice probes. The memorization score subtracts the false-claim selection rate at the last saved checkpoint before the claim was generated from the average rate across subsequent checkpoints (§2, Eq. 1).
- **Training-exposure controls.** HalluSet-train draws from correct-answer rollouts used in training. HalluSet-heldout draws from questions that receive no training signal, and HalluSet-neg provides an additional comparison using incorrect-answer rollouts (§2.1, §§A.3–A.4).
- **Empirical characterization.** The study tracks when false-claim recall increases, whether it persists, and how it varies with retraction, question difficulty, abstention options, KL regularization, and RL algorithm (§§3–4, §A.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reinforced false claims were selected more often after training exposure | Mean change in multiple-choice selection across six models and two datasets was **+4.83 percentage points** on HalluSet-train versus **+0.17** on HalluSet-heldout. The corresponding changes were +5.66 versus +0.47 on QuALITY and +4.01 versus −0.13 on TriviaQA. | §3, Tables 1 and 8 |
| Target likelihood rose around reinforcement | Question-conditioned log-probability of the target false claim rose sharply at the first post-exposure checkpoint for HalluSet-train and showed no comparable jump for HalluSet-heldout. | §3, Figure 2 |
| False claims were frequent in sampled correct-answer traces | Across model–dataset pairs, 26.94%–54.88% of reasoning sentences were labeled as containing a false claim in samples of 200 correct-answer rollouts per model and dataset drawn from rollouts used to construct HalluSet-train. | Figure 3, §A.4, Table 7 |
| Retracted claims showed similar recall gains | In the TriviaQA training probes, backtracked and non-backtracked claims differed by at most 0.18 memorization points per model. Paired permutation tests detected no significant difference for any model. | §3, Figure 4, §A.4 |
| Harder questions generally showed larger gains | HalluSet-train memorization scores tended to be higher when fewer rollouts answered the originating training question correctly. | §3, Figure 5 |
| Multiple-choice abstention fell | Replacing a distractor with “None of the above” yielded a mean 7.23-percentage-point drop in selecting that option on HalluSet-train across models and datasets, comparing checkpoint averages before and after exposure. Held-out probes showed no consistent change. | §4, Table 2, §A.4, Tables 9–10 |
| The effect persisted across additional training and recipe changes | Recall generally held through the end of the second epoch relative to the first post-exposure checkpoint. On QuALITY, a KL coefficient of 0.001 reduced HalluSet-train scores by 0.52 points on average relative to no KL penalty. PPO produced similar training-set scores to GRPO on both datasets, and incorrect-rollout scores were generally near or below zero. | §4, Figure 6 and Table 3, §A.4, Tables 11, 14, 17 |

The authors interpret the checkpoint timing, held-out comparison, and incorrect-rollout control as evidence that outcome-based rewards reinforce false claims embedded in successful traces. The probes establish increased selection and conditional likelihood for specific claims in the tested settings. The frequency of spontaneous free-form repetition and the effect on overall answer factuality remain unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** Multiple-choice selection is a controlled recall probe (§2.2). QuALITY had too little backtracking for a meaningful retraction comparison, and the KL ablation covered only QuALITY (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference — causal attribution.** Training and held-out probes come from different questions and have different pre-exposure selection rates, especially on QuALITY. The controls support the reinforcement explanation, while a matched intervention on the same false claim would better isolate the reward effect. The difficulty comparison also leaves the proposed GRPO advantage mechanism untested (§3, Figure 5, §A.4, Table 8).
- **Analyst inference — annotation and sampling.** The reported manual audit covers TriviaQA. It checks precision among claims already labeled false and finds imperfect backtracking labels. The sentence-level prevalence estimates apply to sampled correct rollouts used to build HalluSet-train, so extrapolation to all generated traces is uncertain (§A.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Do reinforced claims recur in free-form answers?** The likelihood analysis scores a specified target string. Sample open-ended answers before and after exposure using original and paraphrased prompts, then independently label semantic matches to the claim (§2.2, Figure 2).
2. **Does GRPO advantage explain the difficulty trend?** Figure 5 groups questions by correct-rollout count, which leaves advantage and difficulty entangled. Run matched training branches that vary the advantage assigned to equivalent correct rollouts and compare subsequent recall (§3, Figure 5).
3. **Can trace-level supervision reduce memorization while preserving useful backtracking?** The paper discusses the trade-off without testing a process reward. Compare final-answer rewards with a trace-level objective that distinguishes retracted hypotheses from endorsed claims, measuring both false-claim recall and reasoning accuracy (§5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported direction of the probe effect within the tested settings. Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes direct free-form recurrence rates, uncertainty intervals or repeated-seed variability for the headline contrast, and confirmed release locations for the HalluSet data, training code, or study checkpoints.
