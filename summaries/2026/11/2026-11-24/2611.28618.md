# Distilling Visual Agents from Limited Interaction Experience

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28618) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28618)


## One-sentence takeaway

At a fixed training-response budget, on-policy distillation from four selected trajectories recovered over 90% of the measured 4B-to-27B teacher gap on the original standard Navigation and Sokoban panels, while broader training yielded better two-room mapping scores (Table 1, Appendix G).

## Problem and motivation

The authors ask how much distinct interaction experience a smaller visual agent needs to learn from a stronger teacher and transfer to unseen tasks. Each action changes what the agent observes next, so a small set of recorded histories may omit states the adapted agent would encounter. The study tests how far fixed-history replay can go and when fresh interaction helps (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Whole-trajectory selection.** A separate annotation model identifies recurring skill–context opportunities and describes decisions using visual evidence available before each response. The greedy selector prioritizes new opportunity coverage, then breaks ties using facility-location gains based on order-preserving TF–IDF similarity. It keeps at most one trajectory per task and preserves the original model inputs (§§3.1–3.3, Appendix I).
- **Comparison of replay and fresh interaction.** Fixed-history on-policy distillation (OPD) samples fresh student responses at recorded prefixes and trains on teacher token scores. Online OPD executes student responses to obtain new observations. Matched supervised fine-tuning (SFT) uses teacher-generated answers at the same replay prefixes. Selection annotations stay out of the student and teacher prompts (§3.4, Fig. 2).
- **Transfer evaluation.** The authors compare selected subsets, random and component controls, full-pool training, and online training across three environments. They also report Navigation training repetitions, an independent Navigation panel, and reuse of the same selected histories for a 9B student (§4, Appendices C and E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Four histories close most of the original teacher gap | 4B K4 replay raised standard Navigation success from 49.17% to 82.08% and Sokoban success from 60.16% to 85.55%. The 27B teacher scored 85.00% and 88.28%, giving 91.86% and 90.28% measured gap recovery. | §4.2, Table 1 |
| Replay retains most of the same-task online gain on independent Navigation | Standard Navigation success was 45.83% initially, 76.67% with K4 replay, and 79.58% with task-matched online OPD. Replay retained 91.36% of the online gain in the collaborator-reported panel of 240 episodes per model. | §4.2, Appendix C, Table S4 |
| One online training task supports transfer | 4B online K1 reached 80.42% standard Navigation and 80.47% Sokoban success, versus 49.17% and 60.16% initially on the original panels. Each run used 100 updates and 6,400 responses. | §4.3, Fig. 4, Table 1 |
| Broader training helps two-room mapping | On the 4B two-room panel, `cogmap_score` rose from 0.1157 initially to 0.2700 with K4 replay. Task-matched online reached 0.2998, full-task online 0.3051, and full-pool replay 0.3253. | §4.2, Appendices D and G, Table S10 |
| Selection helps, with a less consistent advantage over representativeness alone | Across four additional 4B runs per selector, standard Navigation success averaged 81.98% for the complete selector, 80.73% for representativeness-only, and 76.88% for a fixed random K4 subset. The ordering of the first two varied across runs. | §4.4, Appendix C, Tables S2–S3 |
| Reusing the histories for 9B yields mixed transfer | On Sokoban, K4 replay raised success from 64.06% to 87.50%, matching full-task online OPD. On long-horizon Navigation, success fell from 25.83% to 24.58% with K4 replay. | Table 1, Appendix E |

The experiments establish transfer to unseen tasks within the tested environments and a substantial replay gain on an independent standard Navigation panel. The close replay and online scores on the original Navigation and Sokoban panels are single-training-run estimates and do not establish statistical equivalence. Matching updates and response counts leaves token and compute costs unequal. The study also leaves open how much of the gain comes from selection, repeated supervision, or their interaction (Appendices D, H, K, and L).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Small training subsets still require candidate collection and annotation. Core comparisons use single training runs and development-inspected panels, including the four held-out SpatialGym single rooms. Task-cluster intervals condition on fixed trained weights and omit training and selection variability (§5, Appendices G, H, and K).
- **Author-stated.** Raw records and precise subset mapping for some collaborator-reported results had not been audited locally. Offline and online task-ID matching was verified at K4, with K1 and K16 replay pairing unverified (Appendices B and D).
- **Analyst inference.** Both students and the teacher belong to one model family, and the environments use constrained task variants. The results leave cross-family transfer and performance on more complex variants uncertain (§4.1, Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference.** At a fixed response budget, increasing the history pool also changes how often each prefix is revisited. These comparisons cannot identify the best history count when each pool receives enough training to saturate (§4.5, Appendix K).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gain comes from repeated supervision versus history diversity?** The fixed response budget changes both together. Cross history count with draws per prefix and evaluate each condition across training seeds.
2. **When does refreshing observations improve transfer?** Two-room mapping favors online K4 over replay K4, but their update protocols and history distributions differ. Vary the fraction of freshly executed responses while matching task IDs, update rules, and response budgets.
3. **Does decision order improve trajectory selection consistently?** The reported unordered control has limited run-level evidence, and Sokoban reused the same selection. Compare ordered and unordered selectors across independently collected pools and multiple training seeds.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported within-setting results. Their generality and causal explanation remain uncertain. Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes end-to-end GPU-hours, label-generation wall time and API costs, consolidated subset manifests and raw records for some collaborator results, independent visual-state or teacher-gradient diagnostics, and confirmation of public access to the reproduction artifacts (Appendices B, H, I, and L).
