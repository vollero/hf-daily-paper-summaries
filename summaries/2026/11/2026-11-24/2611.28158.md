# Measuring and Teaching Language Model Agents to Act on Delayed Consequences

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28158) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28158)


## One-sentence takeaway

In SMB Bench’s simulated wholesale operations, frontier agents often failed to act on problems they had diagnosed, while training Qwen3.8-27B from replayed states improved follow-through on customer protection on one held-out business, with an uncertain profit gain (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

An agent’s purchasing decisions change the evidence and resources it sees later. Stockouts hide walk-in demand, delivery shortfalls reduce customer orders weeks later, and emergency purchases consume cash needed for future inventory. The authors ask what experience and learning signal help agents recognize and act on those delayed effects in unseen businesses. Their evaluation measures written diagnosis, subsequent action, and business profit separately (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Replayable benchmark.** SMB Bench runs 90-day quarters across six simulated produce wholesalers and 18 tasks. Normal, customer-loss, and supplier-shock conditions share buyer-visible Day-0 observations. Deterministic replay lets researchers test alternative decisions from the same morning (§2, Fig. 1).
- **Public traces and behavioral audits.** The authors release 859 scored frontier runs and use forecast audits, matched cover trajectories, and restock replays to measure whether agents follow through on their own diagnoses (§§1, 3–4).
- **Training from archived states.** The researchers derive operational instructions from training-business records and use late-quarter archived states to generate fresh Qwen3.8-27B continuations. The MaxRL/CISPO update rewards valid continuations that beat the archived run’s quarterly profit. Trained and untrained policies then receive identical instructions on the sealed test business (§5, Figs. 4–5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Few frontier models beat the fixed rule | Across 859 scored runs on 18 tasks, 3 of 16 models exceeded the Fixed Buying Rule’s mean quarterly profit of +$6,412. All trailed the Scripted Expert Buyer’s +$10,795. | §4, Fig. 2a, Table 5 |
| Written diagnoses rarely translated into timely action | In the nine-model cohort, agents stocked below 60% of their own demand forecast in 107 of 136 audited cases. Across 323 reactive-account quarters, 69 (21.4%) recorded the shortfall–attrition link and 12 (3.7%) also extended cover to all six accounts within three mornings. | §§3–4, Fig. 2c |
| Selected missed restocks would have helped in replay | For audited GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol episodes with written restock plans following substantial stockouts, replaying the noted orders increased quarterly net value by 1.3%–2.7% of sales. | §§3–4 |
| Later cover adaptation was uncertain | For the nine-model behavioral cohort, no model’s 95% bootstrap interval was strictly above zero for the difference in Day-14-to-Day-88 cover growth between customer-loss and matched normal quarters. | §4, Fig. 3 |
| Training improved follow-through under fixed instructions | Across 12 sealed Business 6 quarters per policy, untrained-to-trained cover-off reversals fell from 5.8 to 1.0 per quarter and losses over $10,000 fell from 3/12 to 1/12. Measured stockout recognition moved from 84.7% to 85.6%. | §5.2, Fig. 5 |
| The incremental profit gain was uncertain | On the same sealed test under identical instructions, mean quarterly profit was −$5,984 untrained and −$4,495 trained, a difference of +$1,489 ± $1,133 SE. Both policies completed every quarter. | §5.2, Fig. 5a |
| Emergency supply remained costly | On Business 6, trained Qwen spent $11,868 per quarter on emergency purchases versus $4,190 for GPT-6 Astra under its benchmark prompt. The recorded-offer audit found that 89.6% of Qwen’s emergency spend coincided with open, funded wholesale credit offers within physical shelf life. | §6, Fig. 6a |

The matched restock replays support a causal claim for the selected simulator episodes. The sealed test shows more consistent customer protection with little change in measured stockout recognition, while the profit estimate remains imprecise. The supplier-offer audit identifies purchasing opportunities. Their net value after changes to inventory, spoilage, and cash would require dynamic replay.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** All businesses share one simulated workflow, and task screening favors delayed mechanisms with measurable profit effects. Notes and provider reasoning summaries give an incomplete view of why agents acted (§8.1).
- **Authors’ limitations.** Validation and sealed testing each use one business. Frontier standings compare systems under different prompts, and Business 6 lacks the starting-prompt Qwen baseline. The early supervised and reinforcement learning comparisons also vary in backbone and instructions (§8.2).
- **Authors’ limitations.** Researchers extracted the operational rules. The supplier-offer audit examines individual purchases without dynamically replaying a different purchasing policy (§8.2).
- **Analyst inference.** High cover by Day 14 leaves little room for some models to improve on the trajectory metric, so a weak later change can coexist with successful early adaptation. The binary-MaxRL versus dollar-RLOO comparison also changes both reward and advantage construction, leaving the cause of the completion difference unresolved (§4, Fig. 3, Appendix A.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the reduction in severe losses transfer across businesses?** The sealed test covers one business. Repeat the fixed-instruction comparison across additional sealed businesses with different supplier lead times and markdown schedules.
2. **Can earlier training improve inventory and cash planning?** The main RL run branches from Days 60–85. Compare earlier and late-quarter starts at matched rollout budgets, then measure emergency spending, unfunded cover, and quarterly profit (§§5.2, 8.2).
3. **Can agents discover and revise their own operating rules?** Researchers supplied the rules, and the audit leaves the economic effect of changing the lot cap unresolved. Let agents propose rules from training records, test them through dynamic replay, and evaluate the selected rules on sealed businesses (§§6, 8.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported design and aggregate results. The supplied text omits the individual quarter outcomes referenced in Table 8 and the full per-model cover estimates referenced in Table 6. It reports no sealed-test starting-prompt baseline or replication across independently trained checkpoints, leaving the full development gain and sensitivity to training randomness unresolved.
