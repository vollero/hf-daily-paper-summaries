# WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30989) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30989)


## One-sentence takeaway

WorldLeague scores robot trajectories across 60 simulated tasks, and its highest-scoring system, Claude Fable 5.2, averaged 28.58/100 for physical progress and fulfilled all goal requirements and constraints on 13 tasks at seed 0 (§§3.4, 4.1–4.2).

## Problem and motivation

The authors ask which physical goals AI systems can achieve autonomously. They argue that grouping benchmarks by household activity can hide differences in what an agent must decide. Delivering a visible cup and retrieving the cup that moved since an earlier visit both count as delivery, but the second requires using past observations to choose the target. The authors seek a common evaluation that varies these demands and verifies outcomes across systems with different control interfaces (§§1, 2.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Validated task suite.** WorldSeek tests navigation and goal grounding, WorldShape tests geometric reconfiguration and physical reasoning, and WorldShift tests temporal memory and constraint management. The authors inspected all 60 fixed task–scene pairs and verified that reference runs satisfied their goals and constraints. A scene generator varies configurations while preserving the scoring predicates (§§3.1–3.2, App. F).
- **Common scoring across control interfaces.** WorldHarness adapts Show-Harness so vision-language models choose targets, motions, and responses to feedback while the controller executes commands. Vision-language-action and world-action policies use native controllers. The Physical Agency Profile reports mean Interactive Score for progress and physical success rate for satisfying all goals and constraints in each dimension (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Episode-level failure analysis.** The authors compare 13 systems and use trajectory checks to identify where progress stops, such as reaching a destination without achieving stable placement. Terminal question answering (QA) gives a separate measure of what vision-language models can report about the same episode (§§4.1–4.5).

The results show that terminal QA and command execution can coexist with unmet physical goals. Terminal QA can credit an accurate report of failure, so the pooled QA result cannot establish what a model knew before acting or why it failed. The rankings describe the tested combinations of models, controllers, and robots under the reported simulation protocol (§§4.1, 4.4–4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported scope.** The main comparison uses one episode per system and task at seed 0, and only Sol repeats the suite at seed 1. Continuous-control policies omit T52, whose containment condition already holds at initialization. Terminal QA applies only to VLMs (§§3.4, 4.1, 4.6).
- **Analyst inference on attribution.** Robot embodiments, action interfaces, observations, and budget units differ across systems, so rankings cannot isolate the effect of model architecture. The interface ablations use Sol alone, with one episode per selected task and condition, which limits claims about the effects of feedback, notes, or named skills (§4.1, Apps. C.2, D.6).
- **Analyst inference on generalization.** Reference runs establish feasibility for the fixed simulated instances. The reported experiments leave transfer to newly generated layouts and real robots open. Tasks also draw on several abilities, so a dimension score cannot isolate the capability used to label it (§§3.1–3.2, 5, App. F).

## Open research questions

1. **How stable are system rankings across scene configurations?** Only Sol repeats the fixed suite at another seed. Evaluate every system on multiple screened instances per template and report both task-level variability and rank stability (§4.6, App. F).
2. **How much do feedback and action abstraction affect success?** The reported interventions cover selected tasks with Sol and one episode per condition. Repeat matched comparisons across several VLMs on the same robot, varying one interface feature at a time (§4.4, App. D.6).
3. **Does action-conditioned prediction improve insertion and disturbance recovery?** The authors propose this experiment in future work. Compare a planner with and without latent predictions under matched interaction budgets, using stable insertion and successful recovery as endpoints (§5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported protocol and headline results. The supplied text omits the full cells of several referenced tables, including the complete per-dimension profiles in Table 3. It does not establish public access to the implementation or raw trajectories, uncertainty estimates across systems, performance on generated instances, or transfer to real robots.
