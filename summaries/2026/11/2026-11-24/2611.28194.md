# HEP-Ex-Bench: Evaluating Agentic Performance in High Energy Physics Experiment Data Analyses with Sealed Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28194) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194)


## One-sentence takeaway

HEP-Ex-Bench evaluates frozen agent-built physics pipelines against hidden truths in simulated CMS datasets, where the leading agent, Gemini 4 Argon, averaged 60% of the expert-reference score across four tasks (§6.2, Fig. 5).

## Problem and motivation

A physics pipeline can run successfully and return a plausible measurement while relying on a remembered value, overfitting its development data, or understating uncertainty. The authors identify a gap between benchmarks that check agreement with published or fixed reference values and the expert review needed to assess an entire analysis. They propose a repeatable test with known, varying truths that scores both the measurement and aspects of the method (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Sealed-world evaluation.** An agent develops one pipeline with public pseudodata, nominal simulation, and a fixed output contract. The evaluator freezes the pipeline and reruns it offline on private simulated datasets with varying injected signals and detector responses. Some Higgs width worlds also vary a theory prediction (§3, Fig. 1, §5.1).
- **Scoring against truth and expert workflows.** Logarithmic scoring rewards precision when the uncertainty is consistent with the residuals. A cross-world term checks for shared bias, and automated quality checks assess properties such as goodness of fit, systematic coverage, and runtime. Expert pipelines run on the same worlds under the same contract (§3.2, Apps. B and E).
- **Benchmark and agent study.** The authors instantiate the protocol for Higgs mass and signal strength, Higgs width and on-shell signal strength, lepton calibration, and jet calibration. They evaluate multiple agent configurations across three prompt tiers and release the public worlds, nominal simulation, and scoring harness (§§4–6, App. E, Code and data availability).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Gemini 4 Argon led overall | Mean session scores were averaged within each task, then across the four tasks. Gemini 4 Argon reached 60% of the same-world expert reference, with a reported ±8 percentage-point range from 68% session resampling. GPT 6 Astra reached 38%, Claude Opus 5.5 reached 31%, and no open-weight model exceeded 10% overall. | §6.2, Fig. 5 |
| Above-reference sessions occurred only in jet calibration | Six JetCalib sessions, all Gemini 4 Argon, exceeded the expert reference. No session reached it on HiggsLepMass, HiggsWidth, or LepCalib. The authors attribute the jet gains chiefly to energy-scale uncertainties about half the reference size, with similar agreement with the injected corrections. | §6.2, Fig. 4, §7 |
| Session variability was larger than differences between prompt tiers | Claude Opus 5.5 reached 89% of the expert-reference score in its best HiggsLepMass session versus a 49% mean on that task. Averaged over the five leading models, adding an expert plan in P2 produced no gain over P1 on any task. | §6.2, Fig. 4 |
| Large pulls were common among higher-scoring sessions | In the plotted subset of sessions scoring at least 10% of the expert reference, pull penalties exceeded the reference more often than they fell below it. Both bias and understated uncertainty can raise the pull penalty. | §7, Fig. 7, App. B.6 |
| Multiple worlds helped distinguish tested failure modes | In a one-parameter Higgs-mass toy study using only the measurement score, each tested failure archetype scored below the same-world expert reference in at least 96% of the sets of ten worlds. | App. B.8, Fig. B.2 |

The results show that these simulated tests can distinguish selected failure modes and identify where agent pipelines lose score. The model ordering applies to the tested model–harness combinations, and the jet advantage applies to the modeled variations. Performance on recorded collision data, robustness to broader generator shifts, and the independent effect of the underlying model remain unestablished (§§6–7, App. B.8).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** All worlds come from one experiment’s simulation, and most variation comes from injected signals and detector response. The authors note that the JetCalib reference includes modeling uncertainties larger than the variations represented in the worlds. Scores also depend on the chosen expert reference and quality weights (§7).
- **Authors’ limitations.** More involved analysis frameworks were left for future evaluation. Building each task still requires experts to choose the variations, scoring criteria, and deliverables (§6.2, §8).
- **Analyst inference.** Each model family uses one harness, so cross-family comparisons cannot isolate model effects from harness effects. With three repeats per model, tier, and task, the reported run-to-run variability leaves modest guidance effects unresolved (§§6.1–6.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Exact independent reruns of the private evaluation are constrained because the sealed datasets and their construction code are withheld. The JetCalib evaluation count is also ambiguous. Section 5 says the reported results use ten worlds, while Apps. B.5 and E.4 describe 32 worlds, with B.5 saying ten were timed and all 32 used for the other terms.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the JetCalib gains survive generator shifts?** The authors say the reference uncertainties cover modeling differences underrepresented in the current worlds. Rerun the frozen submissions and reference on alternative-generator worlds and compare energy-scale pulls and scores (§7).
2. **How much of the agent ranking depends on the harness?** The campaign uses one harness per model family. Run compatible models across multiple harnesses with matched tools and budgets and compare repeated-session score distributions (§6.1).
3. **Does structured analysis support improve reliability?** The prompt tiers supply basic guidance, while an integrated framework was left untested. Compare a framework-assisted condition with the existing expert-plan prompt at matched budgets, using enough repeats to measure pipeline completion and score variability (§6.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark design and headline results, which are stated directly in the supplied prose. The retrieval does not expose the full table cells or underlying figure values, including the complete model–harness mapping, exact quality-term weights, and complete per-session scores. The exact detector-profile functions, sealed datasets, and world-building code are withheld, and the supplied text does not reconcile the JetCalib world counts.
