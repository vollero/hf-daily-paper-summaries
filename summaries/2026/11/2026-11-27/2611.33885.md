# LayoutRecall: Preserving Appearance in Multi-Round 3D Layout-Controlled Image Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33885) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33885)


## One-sentence takeaway

LayoutRecall stores earlier appearances in object-local 3D box coordinates, and the authors report better preservation and consistency than with shorter or no memory in multi-round editing tests.

## Problem and motivation

A 3D layout tells an editor where to place an object and how to view it, but leaves its appearance underdetermined. If an object is occluded or leaves the frame, an editor that sees only the latest image can bring it back as a different instance. Background revealed after an occluder moves poses a similar problem. The authors identify a gap in recovering specific earlier observations and aligning them after object or camera motion, alongside the broader problem of recursive image degradation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Box-Surface Latent Memory (BSLM).** Store visible image latents by object identity and box-face cell, then retrieve and reproject them by casting rays from the target camera. A resolution-based replacement rule favors higher-resolution observations (§3.2, Fig. 3).
- **Geometry-Aligned Memory Conditioning (GAMC).** Feed retrieved tokens to a LoRA-adapted FLUX.2 editor using target-view positions, shared box-local Fourier embeddings, and object-wise attention masks (§3.3).
- **Correspondence Attention Loss (CAL).** Train on two-edit revisits with a loss that penalizes low attention to matching surface observations. At inference, the editor writes each generated frame back into memory for later rounds (§§3.1, 3.4, Appendix A).
- **LayoutLoop.** Introduce a calibrated multiview dataset and benchmark with single edits, exact returns, and revisits at changed object poses or nearby views (§4.1, Appendix E).

The reported rankings and ablations support the usefulness of persistent memory in the tested revisit settings. The supplied text includes the result-table captions and narrative rankings, so effect sizes, variability, and statistical reliability cannot be assessed. Self measures consistency against each method’s own first observation, and real-scene Back compares against its own earlier output. These metrics establish agreement with earlier outputs, while fidelity to the true edited scene remains unverified (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** A loose box can cause neighboring-cell retrieval to place appearance in empty space. Background recovery depends on earlier camera observations, with approximate warping available for unseen views (§5, Appendix A).
- **Author-stated.** LayoutLoop’s secondary lighting effects depend on the scene, some visible-mask annotations are empty, and some arrangements retain flagged intersections (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference.** The real-scene evaluation uses 20 hand-selected photographs with tight boxes, a visible ground plane, and coverage of major objects. Results may transfer less reliably to scenes with incomplete layouts or poorer box fits (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The reported ablations isolate memory duration and BLC-Fourier, leaving the individual effects of CAL and object-wise attention unresolved. The general multi-turn baselines receive text instructions while the 3D baselines receive geometry, so their comparison also reflects differences in available conditioning (§§4.2, 4.5).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does CAL improve appearance recovery?** The reported ablations do not isolate it. Retrain matched variants with and without CAL and compare attention to matched tokens and object similarity on exact and offset revisits (§§3.4, 4.5).
2. **How sensitive is recovery to box and camera errors?** The paper identifies loose-box artifacts, while the real-scene protocol selects tight boxes. Perturb box extent, orientation, and camera pose independently on LayoutLoop and measure recovery against reference object crops (§5, Appendix B.2).
3. **How well does background recovery work at unseen viewpoints?** The warp is described, but the reported results do not isolate its effect. Hold out target camera views and compare the full method with warping disabled against reference renders in newly exposed background regions (Appendix A, §4.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The method, evaluation settings, and directional findings are described explicitly. The supplied text lacks numerical entries for Tables 1–6, uncertainty estimates for the reported comparisons, a CAL-only ablation, and confirmation of code or checkpoint availability. Appendix E specifies that the dataset release contains renders and annotations and excludes scene and asset files.
