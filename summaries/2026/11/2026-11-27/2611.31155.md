# So Many Ways to Multiply 3times3 Matrices: A Large Dataset of Rank-23 Schemes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31155) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31155)


## One-sentence takeaway

Perminov presents 62,029 exact schemes for multiplying \(3\times3\) matrices with 23 bilinear products, with a distinct rank pattern certifying each scheme’s inequivalence to every other scheme in the dataset (§§3.1, 5.1, 5.7).

## Problem and motivation

Earlier collections contained thousands of rank-23 schemes but exhibited relatively few distinct rank patterns, leaving the structural diversity of these decompositions poorly understood. Schemes with the same multiplication count can differ in how many additions they require, so a more diverse collection gives researchers more candidates for addition minimization and more examples for studying the matrix multiplication tensor (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Search.** GradALS combines gradient descent, alternating least squares, and regularization toward small coefficients, then converts numerical candidates to exact schemes and verifies them. Flip-graph random walks explore ternary schemes and schemes over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), with finite-field candidates lifted and reconstructed before exact verification (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Dataset and diversity bound.** The search retained one scheme per observed rank pattern, prioritizing coefficient class and then sparsity. The release includes the schemes in JSON, plain text, and Maple formats with an exact verifier. Comparison with the Heule collection raises the documented lower bound on inequivalent schemes (§§4.2, 5.1, 5.5, 5.7).
- **Sparse construction.** GradALS found a rational scheme with 138 nonzero coefficients. Appendix A gives it separately because the dataset’s selection rule favored a ternary representative with the same pattern (§5.6, Appendix A).

Exact verification supports algebraic correctness, and distinct rank patterns certify inequivalence. Matching patterns leave equivalence unresolved, so the combined count is a lower bound. The sparsity result measures coefficient support. Optimized addition counts and runtime remain unmeasured in this study (§§3.1, 5.5, 6.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The dataset is incomplete at the orbit level, and coverage of all possible rank patterns is unknown. The study did not test equivalence directly for schemes sharing a pattern (§§3.1, 5.1, 6.3).
- **Author-stated.** Preferring ternary coefficients can exclude sparser representatives. Nonzero counts also omit savings from reusing intermediate sums. Rank 23 is optimal for integer coefficients under the cited modulo-two result, while the tensor rank over characteristic-zero fields remains unresolved (§§4.2, 6.1–6.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Search restrictions and the retention rule shape the observed distributions, limiting their use as estimates for all rank-23 schemes. The reported GradALS share also cannot establish relative search efficiency without matched compute budgets and method-by-method discovery counts (§§4.2–4.4, 5.3).

## Open research questions

- **How many additional inequivalent schemes share an observed rank pattern?** The paper leaves same-pattern matches unresolved. Apply the cited equivalence algorithm within shared-pattern groups across the collections (§§3.1, 5.5, 6.4).
- **How well does coefficient sparsity predict optimized addition count?** The paper measures nonzeros, while intermediate sums can be reused. Apply a common addition optimizer across sparsity bands and coefficient classes, then compare the resulting addition counts (§§5.3, 6.1).
- **Which search method discovers more new patterns per unit of compute?** The paper reports GradALS’s overall share without a matched-budget comparison. Run repeated, budget-matched searches, record the starting pool, and track new exactly verified patterns per compute-hour (§4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported dataset counts, the logic of the inequivalence bound, and the scope of the sparsity result. The supplied text omits full GradALS settings, exact per-method discovery counts and compute budgets, direct equivalence results for shared patterns, and dataset-wide optimized addition counts or runtime measurements (§§4, 5.5, 6.1, 6.4).
