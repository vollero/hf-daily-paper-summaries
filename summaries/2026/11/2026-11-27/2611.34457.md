# ProxyGenBench: Benchmarking Proxy-Guided Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34457) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34457)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across five video generators, ProxyGenBench finds that structural proxies improve measured spatiotemporal control, but finer proxies can hinder completion and strong structural adherence can coexist with poor binding of text, image, and audio conditions (§§4.2, 4.4, 4.6).

## Problem and motivation

Text prompts and reference images can specify what a video should depict while leaving trajectories, camera movement, and event timing implicit. Structural proxies such as animated 3D blockouts make those constraints explicit, but can also contain placeholders, rigid motion, and awkward contacts that the generator should correct. The authors identify a gap in existing benchmarks: limited evidence on whether generators can preserve intended structure, repair proxy artifacts, and assign supplementary conditions to the right entities and events within the same scene (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark.** ProxyGenBench contains 312 cases from 60 scenes spanning dynamic action, multi-shot storytelling, and multi-view consistency. It varies proxy fidelity and text detail along separate slices of the input grid, with matched reference removals and targeted changes to identity, voice, timing, and camera instructions (§3.2, Fig. 2, App. A.1–A.3).
- **Evaluation.** Gemini instantiates and scores case-specific checklist questions, and Qwen verifies the questions before scoring. Each checklist is grounded in the inputs supplied to the generator and shared across models for that case. The framework organizes 32 criteria into Preserve, Complete, and Bind, and reports both Strict, which discounts the average score by the squared critical Preserve pass fraction, and Joint Structural Success, which requires every critical Preserve check to pass (§§3.3–3.4, Table 1, App. B.1–B.3).
- **Comparative analysis.** The authors use matched runs to measure the benefits of proxy guidance, diagnose fidelity and binding failures, and audit the automated evaluator against human judgments (§§4.1–4.7, Apps. C–E).

The matched proxy/no-proxy comparisons support a benefit for the measured structural controls. In the fidelity comparison, the completion decline has clearer statistical support than the Preserve improvement. The authors attribute much of the decline to difficulty correcting proxy abstractions, but the design leaves geometry and motion inseparable. Lower Strict scores after adding image references are configuration-level observations because the applicable requirements can change with the inputs (§§4.1, 4.6, Apps. C.3, D.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Proxy levels jointly change geometric and motion detail, so their individual effects are unresolved. The authors also flag subtle physical interactions and precise audiovisual synchronization as difficult for the VLM evaluator (App. F).
- **Analyst inference.** One retained output per successful model–case pair leaves run-to-run generation variability unmeasured. Scene-cluster intervals address dependence among observations from the same scene, while repeat-generation stability remains open (Apps. A.6, E.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Rankings have limited portability across input settings. The independent-audio common-support set covers only nine scenes, excludes Kling-4.0 because that input is unsupported, and shows ranking sensitivity to the strength of the Strict penalty (§4.1, Apps. E.1–E.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does geometric detail or motion detail drive the completion loss?** Both vary together in the benchmark. Cross coarse and detailed geometry with coarse and detailed motion for the same scenes, holding prompts and references fixed, then compare Preserve and Proxy Abstraction Handling (Apps. C.3, F).
2. **Can explicit entity and timing cues improve binding while preserving structure?** The Studio 3D and voice-swap results show that Preserve and Bind can move independently. Compare standard conditioning with explicit entity-to-reference and event-time cues on matched cases, using shared structural checks and human-reviewed binding checks (§4.6, App. D.2).
3. **How stable are Joint Success and model rankings across repeated generations?** The protocol retains one successful output per model–case pair. Generate multiple outputs per case under fixed settings and estimate how much Strict and Joint Success vary within scenes and across runs (Apps. A.6, E.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and matched findings, with greater uncertainty around small model differences and timing-sensitive automated judgments.

The supplied text lacks numerical rows for several captioned tables, so complete per-model breakdowns and some configuration or criterion denominators are unavailable. It also leaves benchmark download and licensing status, repeat-generation variability, and the separate effects of geometry and motion detail unestablished.
