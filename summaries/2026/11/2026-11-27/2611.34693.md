# ShowNow: Fine-Grained Speculative Streaming for Responsive World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34693) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34693)


## One-sentence takeaway

ShowNow combines single-latent updates with advance preparation of action-conditioned video, sustaining 480p at 15.99 fps with eight futures on one H100 and improving restart continuity and deadline attainment in matched ViZDoom tests (Abstract, Table 3, Fig. 3).

## Problem and motivation

An interactive world model can sustain its target frame rate while displaying the effects of earlier commands. Coarse blocks limit when a new action can take effect, and reactive generation adds work after input arrives. The authors identify unused GPU time during playback as an opportunity to generate alternative continuations before the next command (§1, §3.1, Table 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Prepare, select, and commit.** Generate one to eight action-conditioned candidates from the same committed history. Select one at a single-latent boundary using the latest input, decode it, and carry it forward as history. Regenerate a candidate if the action is uncovered (§3.1).
- **Matched training futures.** Restore identical ViZDoom states to record eight alternative continuations per fork, including held and switching actions. The authors report releasing this dataset. Teacher adaptation uses shared noise and a branch-difference loss, followed by AnyFlow distillation and Self-Forcing refinement to produce a three-step causal student (§1, §§3.2–3.4, App. B).
- **Shared-history inference.** Branch-tree attention and a shared key/value cache reuse committed history while isolating alternative futures. Fused commitment moves the selected branch’s clean-history update into the next generation pass, shortening the post-decision path (§§3.3–3.5, App. C).

The matched policy experiments establish improved restart continuity and deadline attainment for the tested schedules. At the default queue threshold, similar display quantiles indicate that earlier readiness often leads to the same display slot. The larger deployment latency gain combines block size, training, runtime, and scheduling changes, so its magnitude cannot be assigned to speculation alone. The presentation endpoint ends at software scheduling and leaves physical display latency and perceived responsiveness unmeasured (Apps. A, B, D, and H).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Multi-future evidence covers mutually exclusive ViZDoom controls on synthetic traces. Matched supervision requires restorable simulator states. Brief commands can be superseded before display, and bounded history can allow appearance drift or inconsistent revisits (Apps. E and I).
- **Analyst inference.** The matched-runtime block-size comparison still uses separately trained students with different action learning rates and auxiliary-loss weights. Its latency difference supports the single-latent deployment, with architecture and training effects still entangled (App. B).
- **Analyst inference.** The offline action-effect proxy uses future latent context and can timestamp recognition one latent early. The 33B study also models decoding and uses different checkpoints for timing and quality, limiting claims about perceived response and joint quality–latency performance (Apps. G.1 and H).

## Open research questions

1. **How much preparation helps on real player inputs?** The traces are synthetic, and burst tests emphasize the final held command. Replay timestamped player traces across policies and measure deadline hits, command retention, and perceived responsiveness (Apps. E and I).
2. **How should the system choose candidates in larger action spaces?** The main multi-future comparisons use full coverage of small discrete interfaces. At a fixed GPU budget, compare held-action, predicted, and sampled candidates on a larger or continuous control set (§3.1, Apps. A and I).
3. **Which execution and training choices drive long-horizon drift?** The fusions-off comparison disables several options together, and the aligned-update diagnostic compares different durations and action histories. Toggle fusions individually on paired scenes and seeds, then measure fidelity at fixed wall-clock horizons and update counts (Apps. C and G).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported ViZDoom comparisons and their stated settings. The supplied text leaves open physical end-to-end latency, user-perceived responsiveness, broader multi-action generalization, the individual fidelity effects of runtime fusions, and the dataset’s release packaging and license.
