# What a Forecasting Leaderboard Can Establish: Resolution Reporting for Zero-Shot Time-Series Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31619) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31619)


## One-sentence takeaway

Vernier finds that GIFT-Eval’s median detectable difference is 6.8 mean-rank places versus a 0.91-place gap between top-ten neighbors, while submission-pool changes and inference settings can materially affect leaderboard comparisons (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

Zero-shot forecasting leaderboards display strict rankings that readers use to judge which models transfer best to unseen time series. Multiple configurations can come from the same dataset, so a large configuration count can overstate how much independent evidence the board has. The authors identify three reporting gaps: the smallest difference a board can reliably detect, how much its order depends on the submission pool, and the inference settings needed to reproduce a score (§§1–2, 5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Resolution and leading tiers.** Vernier uses published per-entry, per-unit scores and clusters evaluation units by data source. It estimates each pair’s minimum detectable effect at a 5% test level and 80% power using CR2 with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. The board’s resolution is the median across adjacent top-ten pairs. Vernier also reports a leading tier based on pairwise tests against the leader and checks separation with Holm correction and cluster sign-flip tests (§3, Appendix A).
- **Submission-history audit.** The bystander test replays updates with a fixed evaluation set and counts reversals among previously top-ten entries whose result and metadata files stayed unchanged. The authors apply Vernier to six boards and report releasing the tool and pinned snapshots (§§3–4, Appendix C).
- **Inference-setting audit.** The authors compare a 512-step history with each model’s documented notebook setting and propose a disclosure checklist covering evaluation-run settings (§5, Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| GIFT-Eval’s displayed order is finer than its resolution | Across 28 datasets, the median detectable difference under mean rank on MASE was 6.80 rank places versus a 0.91-place median gap. One of nine adjacent top-ten pairs separated under uncorrected CR2 and none separated after Holm correction. | §4, Figure 1a, Appendix B.1 |
| Source grouping changes what a board can resolve | Across boards with 15–39 sources, estimated resolution was 3.6–19 times the median adjacent gap under the rules used in Table 1. BOOM’s ratio was 0.63 with 2,554 series treated as separate sources, and all nine adjacent pairs separated under uncorrected CR2. Grouping those series into 23 domains raised the ratio to 2.04, with five of nine separating. | Tables 1 and 8 |
| Pool-dependent rules reversed unchanged entries | Across four board histories, pool-dependent rules reversed 43 untouched top-ten pairs in 31 of 226 usable updates. Pool-independent rules produced zero such reversals. TIME’s contribution came from its MASE rank column. | §4, Tables 1 and 8 |
| Test calibration depended on the aggregation rule | On null resamples of GIFT-Eval’s 28 datasets, CR2’s false-positive rate at a nominal 5% was 5.2% for mean rank and 15.7% for the geometric mean. Exact Wilcoxon signed-rank gave 5.6% for dataset-level log-error differences, weighting each dataset equally. | Appendix A.2, Table 3 |
| Shorter history raised error for three models | On 70 eligible GIFT-Eval configurations from 14 sources, shortening the documented history to 512 steps increased geometric mean scaled MASE by 6.2% for Moirai-2.0 and 15.3% each for TimesFM-2.5 and Toto. All three increases survived Holm correction. Chronos-Bolt’s full-set estimate was +2.7% (95% interval −0.9% to +6.2%). The test against the displayed top-ten spread rejected for zero models. | §5, Appendix D, Table 9 |

The authors interpret resolution as evidence about whether an ordering would survive another plausible draw from the board’s source population. Fixed-set scores remain exact, and pairs in the same tier may still differ in accuracy. The replay establishes that submission-pool changes can reverse unchanged entries. The inference experiment establishes sensitivity for the tested models and configurations, while leaving open whether the effects exceed the displayed top-ten spread (§4, Appendices A.4 and D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** BOOM’s strongest result depends on treating individual series as independent sources. GIFT-Eval’s estimate that closing the median gap would require about 48 times as many datasets assumes new datasets vary like the current ones. The comparison with nonoverlapping fev-bench families suggests higher noise (§4, Appendices A.4 and C).
- **Author-stated.** Geometric-mean comparisons can be dominated by a single source. The calibrated signed-rank procedure targets the typical dataset, leaving inference for the configuration-weighted mean unresolved in the reported calibration checks (Appendix A.2).
- **Author-stated.** The history experiment’s confirmation split shares base datasets with discovery. The sign-flip test had an inflated two-sided error rate in the authors’ skew checks, though false claims of an increase were conservative for the three models with reported increases. Toto’s sampling comparison also changed a cache setting and two numerical switches, leaving the sample-count effect unisolated (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference.** The observed reversal frequency applies to the four replayable histories. It cannot establish how often reversals occur on boards without usable version histories or reproducible scoring rules (§4, Appendix C).

## Open research questions

- **How much independent data would make adjacent ranks reproducible?** The sample-size projection depends on how new sources vary. Add prospectively selected, nonoverlapping source batches and measure how resolution and rank stability change (Appendices B.1 and C).
- **Can a calibrated test retain the configuration-weighted geometric-mean target?** The signed-rank procedure uses equal dataset weights. Evaluate candidate tests for the weighted mean under simulated and held-out source layouts with concentrated variance (Appendix A.2).
- **How do history length and sampling separately affect scores on new sources?** The main experiment compares two history settings, and Toto’s sampling comparison changes several settings together. Cross multiple history lengths with sample counts on source-disjoint datasets while holding the other inference switches fixed (Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported comparisons and their stated scope. The supplied text includes the caption for the five-field disclosure checklist but omits its rows, so the full schema is unavailable. It also leaves the actual history and sampling settings behind most published submissions, and the degree of independence among BOOM’s individual series, unestablished.
