# Reading, Not Adapting: Unlocking Frozen Visual Knowledge for AI-Generated Image Detection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34719) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34719)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReMoT reads spatially aligned RGB and wavelet features across a frozen DINOv3 backbone and achieves a reported 91.2% average across eight benchmark groups, the highest among evaluated detectors, with 3,074 trainable parameters and training on a single generator (§§4.1–4.2, Table 1, Appendix C).

## Problem and motivation

AI-generated image detectors must generalize to unfamiliar generators, image sources, and post-processing. The authors argue that task-specific backbone adaptation can favor generator artifacts or dataset biases, while conventional frozen linear probes access only the final RGB representation. Existing multi-cue approaches can surface additional evidence, but separate encoders can complicate feature alignment and late fusion prevents exchange during feature extraction (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Staged cross-view processing.** ReMoT embeds aligned RGB and discrete wavelet views of the same image with frozen stems and Transformer weights. The views start independently, then exchange information through neighboring patches and globally connected register tokens (§§3.1–3.2, Figures 3–4).
- **Progressive one-way readout.** Trainable fusion queries collect evidence from both views at every block through frozen attention projections. Visual tokens cannot attend to these queries. Training updates only the query embeddings and a shared linear classifier (§3.3).
- **Exact reparameterization.** Identical frozen slots can share weights, and identically initialized, symmetrically processed queries collapse to one query with a fixed attention bias. The paper derives the conditions under which these changes preserve predictions (Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported overall transfer | ReMoT averaged **91.2%** across eight equally weighted groups, ranking first on five and second on three. Excluding Mirage-Test, it averaged about **92.2%** versus **90.1%** for DDA. Both aggregates use balanced accuracy for two-class subsets and fake-image accuracy for fake-only subsets. ReMoT used one SD v1.4-trained model and a fixed threshold across evaluations. | §4.1–4.2, Table 1, Appendix A |
| Largest reported benchmark separation | On Mirage-Test, ReMoT scored **84.1%** overall versus **55.7%** for the strongest evaluated baseline, using balanced accuracy for two-class subsets and fake-image accuracy for Anime. | §4.2, Tables 1 and 4, Appendix A |
| Readout and interaction ablations | On the eight-group metric, full ReMoT scored **91.2%**, final-layer RGB linear probing **84.2%**, the tested attention-LoRA variant **70.5%**, and dual-view query readout with direct exchange disabled **87.0%**. Keeping only local patch exchange or only global register exchange yielded **89.6%** and **89.1%**, respectively. | §4.3, Table 6, Appendix A |
| Robustness varied by benchmark | At JPEG quality 60 and Gaussian blur radius 2.0, respectively, ReMoT scored **86.3%** and **86.4%** on Chameleon, retaining **95.7%** and **95.8%** of its unmodified score. On original GenImage validation images at the same severities, ReMoT scored **83.0%** and **81.2%**, versus DDA’s **89.0%** and **89.2%**. Perturbations were applied separately to both classes. | §4.4, Figure 5, Appendix E |

The authors interpret these results as evidence that structured readout can improve transfer from a frozen backbone. The direct-exchange ablation supports that interpretation within ReMoT, while the linear-probe comparison changes both the input views and the readout. The LoRA result applies to one tested adaptation configuration, and the robustness ranking depends on the benchmark.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-described scope.** The robustness study applies JPEG and blur separately on three benchmarks. Blur is specified in source-image pixels, so its relative severity varies with image resolution (§4.4, Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference.** The experiments use one training generator and one backbone. The supplied text leaves open whether the gains carry over to other backbones or training-generator choices (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Baseline scores combine published results with local evaluations of released checkpoints. Full table entries and run-level uncertainty are unavailable in the supplied text, limiting assessment of close rankings and protocol comparability (Table 1 caption, Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference.** The trainable parameter count measures the size of the optimized components. Both visual token streams are still processed, and the supplied text gives no measured latency, throughput, or peak memory (Appendix C).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the gains persist across backbones and training generators?** The reported setup fixes both. Retrain the same readout and matched RGB probes across multiple frozen backbones and single-generator splits, then evaluate them on the same held-out groups (§4.1).
2. **How much does progressive access contribute on its own?** The reported final-layer probe also changes the input views and readout. Compare progressive and final-block-only queries with the same two streams, mask, and trainable parameter budget (§4.3, Table 6).
3. **How does ReMoT handle compound post-processing?** JPEG and blur are evaluated independently. Apply them in combination at multiple source resolutions and compare performance at the same fixed threshold (Appendix E).
