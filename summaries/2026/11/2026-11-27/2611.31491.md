# Pretrained Tabular Features and the Measurement of Financial Proximity

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31491) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31491)


## One-sentence takeaway

In a retrospective panel of large US equities, all four frozen tabular encoders improved estimated in-sample spatial return fit over a direct distance on the same disclosures when broad SEC filing fields were included, and the largest gain, from TabPFN, persisted under Fama–French five-factor plus momentum controls (§5, Tables 2 and 7).

## Problem and motivation

Spatial asset pricing depends on which firms count as peers because their returns enter each issuer’s spatial lag and their weights shape the model-implied covariance. The author asks whether frozen tabular foundation model representations provide a useful measure of financial proximity in a small, dependent panel. Comparing embeddings only with a characteristic-history distance leaves a gap: improvements could come from how the disclosures are preprocessed and compared, so the paper also evaluates a direct distance on the same inputs (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Peer construction and matched evaluation.** The study extracts issuer embeddings from frozen TabPFN 3.5, TabFM 1.1, Kumo-Tabular, and LimiX-2, conditioned on historical examples labeled with observed next-quarter market equity over assets. Chord distances define symmetrized ten-nearest-neighbor graphs. A quarterly spatial-lag model with quarter effects compares these graphs with direct chord distances on the same preprocessed fields and, for ratio histories, with Fernández’s rank-based characteristic-history distance (§§3–4, Appendix B).
2. **Evidence on when encoding adds fit.** The comparison spans ratio histories, their underlying components, and ratios augmented with broad SEC filing fields. The broad inputs yield the only unanimous positive encoder gains over the matched direct graph (§5, Table 2).
3. **Theory and empirical probes of peer geometry.** Proposition 1 shows that prediction-preserving recodings can change peers. Proposition 2 bounds the effect of changing weights on implied covariance for a common roster, spatial coefficient, and innovation covariance. Projection and input-omission experiments test how much the selected TabPFN graph depends on its dominant valuation coordinate and engineered ratios (§§3, 5, Appendices A and D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The original-style characteristic kernel spreads weight nearly uniformly. | In the December 2015 US cohort, the joint exponential kernel averaged about 837 effective neighbors across 878 fitted issuers. The estimated spatial coefficient was 0.86 without an intercept, and the intercept fit was invalid or on the boundary. | §5, Table 6, Appendix C |
| Ratio-based TabPFN improves on Fernández under full factor controls. | Both graphs improve on the factor-only model across the reported controls. With FF5 plus momentum, TabPFN gains 0.0013 nats per issuer-quarter over Fernández on 51,782 matched issuer-quarters, with a paired conditional 95% interval of [0.0003, 0.0024]. Under market controls the ranking also holds with exponential and Gaussian weights. | §5, Table 1, Figure 1 |
| Direct ratio distance accounts for a comparable gain over Fernández. | On the market-controlled panel of 54,906 issuer-quarters, direct ratios gain 5.91 log-likelihood units per quarter over Fernández and ratio-based TabPFN gains 4.98. TabPFN minus direct is −0.94 units per quarter, with a paired conditional 95% interval of [−2.67, 0.75]. | §5, Table 5, Appendix C |
| Broad filing inputs yield the clearest encoder gains over direct distance. | With market controls and matched broad inputs, all four point estimates are positive and three conditional 95% intervals exclude zero. TabPFN leads at 0.0085 nats per issuer-quarter, and its interval remains above zero under FF5 and FF5 plus momentum. | §5, Tables 2 and 7 |
| A dominant valuation coordinate captures little of the full graph’s peer membership. | In January 2016, the selected broad TabPFN embedding’s leading coordinate carries 46.15% of positive inertia and has an absolute rank correlation of 0.93 with market equity over assets. Across formations, the one-coordinate graph has mean peer Jaccard overlap of 0.053 and loses 0.0116 nats per issuer-quarter relative to the full graph under market controls. | §5, Table 3, Appendix D |
| Changing the inputs can substantially alter peers while leaving fit similar. | Omitting the engineered market-equity-to-assets history while retaining broad fields and valuation labels yields a Jaccard overlap of 0.25 with the full graph. The market-controlled fit change has a conditional 95% interval of [−0.0008, 0.0022] nats per issuer-quarter. | §5, Table 3, Appendix D |

The evidence supports a comparative claim about in-sample fit to contemporaneous returns on the observed panel. Different peer sets can achieve similar fit, so the likelihood cannot identify a unique economic network. The study provides no held-out forecast test or causal spillover estimate, and the spatial-lag fits leave the S-APT pricing restrictions untested (§§4–6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The analysis is retrospective. Encoders were released after the sample, filing values incorporate later revisions, and factor exposures use each issuer’s full observed quarterly panel (§4, §6).
- **Author-stated.** Panel intervals condition on fitted exposures, return-model parameters, and graphs. The selected broad-input TabPFN analysis uses the best market-controlled gain among twelve configurations, and its intervals do not adjust for that selection (§3, §5, Appendix E).
- **Author-stated.** Sparse overlapping histories make some Fernández distances uninformative, especially for dividend yield early in the panel. The direct comparison changes the distance, missing-data treatment, and scaling together, leaving their individual effects unresolved (§5, Table 4).
- **Analyst inference.** The matched direct benchmark isolates the gain of the complete encoder-based peer-selection method. Because the encoder also receives valuation-labeled context, the evidence cannot attribute the broad-input gain specifically to pretrained geometry, conditioning labels, or their interaction (§4, Table 2).
- **Analyst inference.** The geometry probes focus on TabPFN, and the return evaluation uses large US issuers. Transfer to other encoders, smaller firms, or other markets remains untested in the supplied text (§§4–5, Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the gains persist in real time?** The study uses retrospective model releases, revised filings, and full-panel exposures. A rolling held-out evaluation could use only filings, model versions, and exposure estimates available at each formation.
2. **How much of the broad-input gain comes from the valuation labels?** The matched comparison includes both encoding and label conditioning. Rebuild graphs with observed, shuffled, and alternative historical labels while holding the disclosed fields, context rows, graph rule, and return sample fixed.
3. **Which changes explain the direct ratio graph’s gain over Fernández?** The reported comparison changes several steps at once. A factorial ablation could vary the history distance, missing-data handling, and feature scaling on the same issuer-quarters.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported direction and scope of the main findings. The supplied text includes table captions and selected results in prose, but omits the full numerical tables and Figure 1 values.
