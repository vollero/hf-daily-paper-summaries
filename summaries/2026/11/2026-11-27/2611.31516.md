# InitFree-SfM: When Initialization-Free Bundle Adjustment meets Learned Structure-from-Motion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31516) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31516)


## One-sentence takeaway
On the tested benchmarks, learned metric depth and verified pairwise geometry enable competitive camera-pose recovery from random global initialization, and adding a depth-augmented object-space optimization stage improves accuracy further.

## Problem and motivation
Bundle adjustment jointly estimates camera poses and 3D landmarks, typically starting from a good global reconstruction. Earlier initialization-free methods start randomly and minimize a surrogate object-space error (OSE), but the authors show that decreasing this loss can still yield inaccurate geometry after reprojection refinement and metric upgrade. OSE constrains landmarks to viewing rays, leaving their positions along those rays unconstrained by the individual observations (§§1, 3.1, Appendix A, Fig. 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Depth-augmented OSE (dOSE).** A robust relative-depth residual constrains each landmark along its viewing ray. With robust weights held fixed during each variable-projection step, the solver eliminates landmarks and updates camera poses directly on SE(3), bypassing the projective-to-metric upgrade (§3.2, Appendices F–G).
- **End-to-end initialization-free SfM.** The pipeline cross-checks depth-based alignment against epipolar geometry before building multiview tracks. Joint refinement then combines image observations, depth, and relative pose and scale constraints, with a separate depth scale for each image (§4, Fig. 2).
- **Controlled study of the optimization stages.** With the same correspondences on ETH3D, the authors show that joint metric refinement can recover accurate poses directly from a random start. Adding dOSE before refinement improves the result (§§5.1, 5.4, Fig. 3, Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Joint refinement recovers accurate poses from random starts, with dOSE improving them further | On 25 ETH3D scenes with up to 20 images each, the controlled comparison uses identical correspondences across configurations. Refinement alone improves pose AUC@20° over the traditional OSE pipelines, and the full dOSE pipeline ranks highest. | §5.1, Fig. 3 |
| Competitive against established SfM pipelines | InitFree-SfM leads the reported pose AUC@20° comparisons on ETH3D and LaMAR. Traditional SfM methods retain an advantage at AUC@1°. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Stronger low-overlap results on ETH3D than on SMERF | Second-best pose AUC in 23 of 24 ETH3D comparisons and 10 of 24 SMERF comparisons across overlap levels, thresholds, and matchers. Mean runtime per subset is reported as substantially lower than MP-SfM in the LoMa comparison. | §5.3, Table 2, Fig. 4 |
| dOSE adds value under low overlap | With LoMa, adding dOSE before joint refinement raises pose AUC@20° by 4.8–7.9 percentage points on ETH3D and 12.9–22.0 points on SMERF compared with refinement directly from random initialization. | Appendix C, Fig. 7 |
| Depth filtering improves track selection | With LoMa and MoGe-2, changing only track filtering from image-based verification alone to verification with depth filtering raises pose AUC@20° by 10.30 points on ETH3D and 6.42 points on LaMAR. | Appendix D, Table 6 |
| Estimated intrinsics are viable but reduce accuracy | With MoGe-2 intrinsics, InitFree-SfM reaches 81.51% pose AUC@20° on ETH3D versus 77.18% for calibrated GLOMAP. On LaMAR it reaches 63.44%, versus 64.15% for uncalibrated GLOMAP and 69.95% for VGGT. The authors report a larger drop from known intrinsics on LaMAR. | §5.4, Fig. 5 |

The controlled comparisons support the authors’ claim that geometrically constrained refinement can work from a random global start and that dOSE provides an additional benefit in the tested settings. Pose AUC measures relative rotation and sign-invariant translation direction, so these rankings leave absolute metric scale and quantitative landmark accuracy unresolved (Appendix F).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The method benefits from known camera calibration, with a more pronounced accuracy drop on LaMAR when using estimated intrinsics. Scaling to larger image collections remains future work (§§5.4, 6).
- **Analyst inference.** The main ETH3D and LaMAR experiments use small image sets, and LaMAR windows are selected using reference poses to exclude near-static or abrupt motion. This limits what the results establish about larger or less curated collections (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference.** SMERF’s larger gap to MP-SfM coincides with a more fragmented verified pose graph under LoMa. The reported comparison leaves open how much of the gap fragmentation explains (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Section 4.2 describes checking depth-derived rotations against pairwise epipolar rotations, while Appendix F describes checking against rotations implied by a strongest-edge spanning tree. The supplied text leaves their relationship unclear for replication.

## Open research questions

1. **How much does pose accuracy depend on intrinsics error?** The known-versus-estimated comparison leaves sensitivity to specific calibration errors open. Perturb focal lengths and principal points independently on the same scenes, then measure pose AUC and track retention (§5.4).
2. **When does dOSE help most as the verified pose graph fragments?** The low-overlap results show dataset-dependent gains without isolating graph connectivity. Remove verified edges under controlled connectivity levels and compare full dOSE with refinement-only across random seeds (§5.3, Appendix C).
3. **Does pose recovery translate into accurate metric scale and landmarks as collections grow?** The reported metric measures pose angles and the main benchmark uses small image sets. Evaluate nested collections of increasing size against reference geometry, recording scale error, landmark error, pose AUC, runtime, and peak memory (§6, Appendix F).
