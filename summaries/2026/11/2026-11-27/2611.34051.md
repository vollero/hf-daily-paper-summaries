# Stable Gated DeltaNet-2: Understanding and Mitigating Recurrent State Instability

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34051) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34051)


## One-sentence takeaway

Stable GDN-2 prevents existing recurrent state from growing when writes stop, bounds state growth by \(O(\sqrt{T})\) under bounded values and zero initialization, and raises the recurrent model’s 128K average RULER score from 4.82% to 11.62% versus GDN-2 (§4.2, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

GDN-2 uses separate channel-wise erase and write gates to give fixed-size recurrent memory more flexible updates. Its erase operation reads along a gated key and modifies along the original key. The resulting non-normal transition can amplify some state directions, and gains can compound across tokens even after writes cease. The authors identify a gap in loss-based monitoring: recurrent state magnitudes can become extreme during runs that finish with finite loss, and a failure replay shows the state’s effective rank collapsing before overflow (§§1–3, Fig. 2, Table 4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Diagnose the amplification mechanism.** The authors derive the exact maximum gain of a single erase step, construct a sequence with exponential state growth after one finite write, and isolate the same propagation mechanism in a failed-run replay (§3, Appendix A).
- **Make erasure symmetric.** Apply the square root of the channel-wise erase gate to both key factors. This aligns the read and modification directions, preserves the original diagonal attenuation, and makes propagation non-expansive under the stated key and gate constraints (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Regulate residual writing by decay.** Add a write along the difference between the original and gated keys, scaled by the square root of the fraction of state energy removed by decay in each channel. The authors prove a cumulative write-energy bound and derive a chunkwise forward computation that retains the original triangular system size (§§4.1–4.3, Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| GDN-2 can amplify state without further writes | The erase operator has spectral norm above one when its read and modification directions are noncollinear. An admissible alternating sequence yields exponential norm growth after a single finite write. | Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2, Appendix A |
| GDN-2 failures and extreme states appear in training | Across designated recurrent and hybrid runs, loss became NaN in 3/8 pretraining runs and 4/8 standalone post-training runs started from random initialization. All 5 post-training stages launched after successful pretraining completed. One completed recurrent pretraining run recorded an absolute state entry of \(3.157\times10^{36}\). | Tables 1 and 4, Appendix B.1 |
| Replay isolates amplification and rank collapse | In the selected head of a failed recurrent pretraining run, the first sampled nonfinite state appeared at token 52,736 and stable rank approached one before overflow. With writes disabled after token 64 and transitions held fixed, GDN-2’s state continued to grow while GDN and KDA states decayed. | Fig. 2, Appendix B.2 |
| Stable GDN-2 controls state growth | With writes disabled, state Frobenius norm cannot increase. From zero state with all value norms bounded by \(V\), the norm after \(T\) tokens is at most \(\sqrt{3T/2}\,V\). | Propositions 4.1–4.2, Appendix C.2 |
| Recurrent retrieval improves most at 128K | With 64K pretraining, recurrent Stable GDN-2 scores 11.62% on 128K average RULER versus 4.82% for GDN-2, a gain of 6.80 points and the highest average among tested baselines. At 128K in the hybrid setting, it scores 8.78% versus 8.57% for GDN-2 and 10.52% for β-Guard. | Table 3, §§5.1–5.2 |
| Downstream accuracy and perplexity trade off | Across nine zero-shot tasks, hybrid Stable GDN-2 averages 50.53% versus 49.91% for GDN-2, while the recurrent models tie at 50.39%. At a 64K WikiText window, Stable GDN-2 versus GDN-2 perplexity is 7.759 versus 7.804 recurrently and 7.558 versus 7.493 in the hybrid setting. | Table 2 |
| Both write components help in the reported ablation | On the mean of four RULER needle tasks across 32K, 64K, and 128K, the score falls from 25.43 with the full method to 22.22 without residual writing and 13.53 without decay regulation. | Fig. 4, §5.2 |

The proof establishes a forward-state guarantee under the specified recurrence assumptions. The replay establishes that amplification without further writes occurred in one observed failure. The authors report stable training with the proposed method under the main comparison protocol. Those completed runs and benchmark scores leave the cross-seed failure-rate reduction and the consistency of the performance gains unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The GDN-2 construction proves that exponential growth is possible. Its occurrence and severity depend on the learned transitions and token sequence. Stable GDN-2’s square-root bound assumes bounded values and zero initialization (§§3.1, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Table 1 contains GDN-2 failure counts only. The main Stable GDN-2 comparison uses one initialization and data-order pair, leaving both the size of its stability advantage and benchmark variance across seeds unclear. The designated Hybrid Seed 2 standalone post-training run failed, while another trajectory with the same seeds completed (§5.1.3, Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The detailed replay covers one triggering sequence and one layer/head. The component ablations report task scores and perplexity, leaving the state magnitudes and failure rates of those variants unquantified. Chunkwise compatibility is derived, while measured throughput and memory overhead are unavailable (§4.3, Fig. 4, Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does Stable GDN-2 reduce failures across seeds and training stages?** The failure sweep covers GDN-2 only. Repeat the matched pretraining and post-training protocols with Stable GDN-2, recording loss failures and state maxima across repeated trajectories.
2. **Do transition alignment and stable rank predict failures early enough to be useful?** The detailed replay covers a single triggering sequence and head. Log transition gains, state norms, and stable rank across layers and heads in both completed and failed runs, then measure how early these signals distinguish the outcomes.
3. **Does decay regulation improve retrieval by controlling state growth during continued writes?** The ablation shows a retrieval penalty when regulation is removed, but supplies no corresponding state diagnostics. Compare the full and unregulated variants under matched training while measuring write energy, state norms, stable rank, and retrieval across context lengths.
