# StateWeaver: Multi-Agent Coordination of Story-State Transitions for Long-Horizon Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34290) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34290)


## One-sentence takeaway

StateWeaver uses multi-agent review to repair visual state changes and pass verified results to later shots, with the clearest reported gains in cross-shot continuity and state transitions (§§3.2–3.3, 4.4).

## Problem and motivation

Long-form video production needs to preserve character, prop, and scene identity while showing how events change them. The authors identify a gap in existing agentic workflows: recording an intended state change does little to ensure that the renderer depicts it or that later shots inherit the corrected appearance. Repeated planning and repair also accumulate context that agents must sift through to find relevant evidence (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Active States.** Asset state trees track mutable attributes by story interval. Review identifies what to fix, what to preserve, and which visual references are missing. The system creates and validates targeted references, regenerates the shot, and records confirmed attributes against the asset state, story interval, and source version (§3.2).
- **Hierarchical Collaboration.** Agents work in separate contexts by task, clip, and repair round, retrieving relevant records from set-level and global memory. A dependency graph unlocks shots once the required observations are verified, allowing independent work to proceed in parallel (§3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **Comparative evaluation.** The authors assemble a 75-task suite from existing benchmarks and compare workflows using the same underlying models, with component ablations and blinded human judgments (§§4.1–4.4, Appendix D.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Base-model errors survive repair and compromise selection. Examples include incorrect cup count, incorrect prop orientation, and framing that hides the moment of landing (Appendix E.5).
- **Author-reported.** Visible asset changes are easier to track than gradual emotional or relationship changes. A reviewer who misses an action error can pass faulty evidence to later shots (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference.** The ablations use DirectorBench alone, and the frozen-reference variant also makes defect-conditioned synthesis inapplicable. The results leave open how these components behave under fixed storyboards and how much reference expansion contributes independently (§4.3, Table 2).
- **Analyst inference.** GPT-6.1-sol serves as both the agent model and an evaluator for some automated criteria, so those scores may be sensitive to judge choice. Blinded human judgments provide a separate check on continuity, state transitions, and visual quality (§4.1, §4.4, Appendix D.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the gains transfer across video renderers?** The comparison uses one shared stack. Repeat matched workflow comparisons with another reference-conditioned renderer and measure continuity and state-transition fidelity (§4.1).
2. **How often does a mistaken handoff cause downstream errors?** The authors identify this failure mode without quantifying it. Annotate correct and incorrect upstream observations, then trace downstream shots to measure error propagation with and without handoff verification (§3.3, Appendix F).
3. **What quality does each additional repair buy?** The workflow can authorize extra repairs or select a compromise, but the supplied results give no quality–cost curve. Vary the repair budget on the same stories and record state fidelity, generation calls, and elapsed time (§3.2, Appendix E.5).
