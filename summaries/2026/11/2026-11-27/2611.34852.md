# DRBench Pro: A Knowledge-Centric Benchmark for Professional Deep Research Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34852) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34852)


## One-sentence takeaway

DRBench Pro uses Wikipedia-derived knowledge trees to distinguish how widely deep research reports cover a topic from how far they follow related knowledge chains, with the strongest of 16 evaluated configurations reaching 57.04% mean overall coverage (§§3–4, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Open-ended research questions admit several valid reports, so factuality checks alone leave omissions unmeasured. The authors argue that existing benchmarks often combine coverage with other aspects of report quality or aggregate rubric judgments as independent items, obscuring whether a report covers many research directions or develops a few in depth (§§1–2, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark construction.** The pipeline expands Wikipedia links into rooted entity–relation trees, samples complete branches, and uses each sampled subgraph to generate a research query and one evidence-grounded rubric per relation. Agents see only the query. The benchmark contains 144 cases across 24 domains and 44,102 rubrics (Abstract, §§3.1–3.3, Figure 2).
- **Coverage metrics.** Breadth weights research directions equally and gives credit to covered descendants. Depth emphasizes the best-developed subtrees and discounts descendant credit when an intermediate relation is missing. Overall coverage is the geometric mean of breadth and depth within each case, averaged across cases (§3.4, Appendix B).
- **Evaluation and local framework.** The study compares end-to-end research systems, native-search LLMs, and backbone LLMs in a shared local framework. The local setting fixes the English Wikipedia snapshot, Search and Open tools, workflow, and interaction budget across backbones (§4.1, Appendix F).

The results support using separate breadth and depth scores to diagnose coverage of the benchmark’s reference trees. Expert agreement supports that interpretation on the rated sample. The scores do not establish the factual accuracy of every report claim, the quality of its citations, or coverage of valuable material outside the reference tree. The Qwen comparison measures a difference between complete configurations and leaves the contribution of each component unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Cross-setting comparisons involve different retrieval resources, interfaces, workflows, and budgets. The supplementary harness comparison used unmatched budgets, and adherence varied even in the local experiment with a common report-length target (Appendices C.3, D.3 and D.5).
- **Author-reported.** The construction-model check covered a limited sample and Claude’s lead narrowed. The leakage audit checked literal title matches on sampled cases, which leaves semantic disclosure unmeasured (§§4.4, 4.7).
- **Analyst inference.** Selecting relations from bounded Wikipedia link trees may favor well-linked topics and omit legitimate lines of inquiry. Binary scoring also assigns the same coverage credit to substantive partial answers and absent answers (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The expert studies support the scoring method on sampled reports. Agreement across every domain, configuration, or rubric type remains uncertain (§4.5, Appendix I).

## Open research questions

1. **Do coverage rankings hold with reference material beyond Wikipedia?** All benchmark rubrics derive from Wikipedia relations. Build parallel rubric trees from expert-selected primary sources for the same queries and compare agent rankings and expert agreement.
2. **Which components explain the local versus native-search difference?** The Qwen comparison changes the full research configuration. Hold the backbone and report budget fixed, then vary the corpus, search interface, and orchestration separately.
3. **What reduces knowledge loss during synthesis?** The retention study measures omissions after retrieval but does not identify which writing-stage intervention would help. Give each synthesis method identical retrieved evidence and compare standard writing with explicit evidence tracking at a matched output length.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark design and headline findings explicitly reported in the supplied text. Several referenced tables appear only as captions, leaving the complete per-agent results, judge scores by model and label scheme, construction-model gaps, exact local tool budgets, and report collection dates unavailable. Appendix J supplies the prompts. Public availability of the full case and rubric data, framework code, and evaluation traces is unconfirmed in the supplied text.
