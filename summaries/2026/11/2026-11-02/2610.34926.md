# Supervising the Visible State: Separating Representation from View Selection in Streaming Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34926) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34926)


## One-sentence takeaway
On one video-language backbone, explicit temporal-state supervision improves OVO-Bench beyond matched generic training, while adaptive access to historical frames adds complementary gains but sometimes replaces correct answers.

## Problem and motivation
Streaming video assistants must answer from an observed prefix under limited context. Ordinary answer labels may leave event counts, order, relation changes, and evidence sufficiency unconstrained: recognizing an action does not require counting its repetitions. The authors call this **state under-specification** and distinguish it from missing evidence caused by a narrow frame view (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Visible-State Supervision (ViSS):** one-epoch supervised training on 7,677 source-grounded examples probing event identity, order, accumulation, relation change, and answer sufficiency. Targets contain direct answers; no architectural memory is added (§4.1).
- **Test-Time Causal View Search (TVS):** freezes the trained checkpoint and conditionally expands from four recent frames to event and compact-history views. Confidence triggers acquisition; disagreement and a calibrated margin rule govern revisions (§4.2).
- **Controlled separation and diagnosis:** matched training comparisons test supervision formulation, fixed-checkpoint comparisons test evidence access, and exhaustive replay identifies where available correct proposals are lost (§§3.2, 5.3–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Combined system improves both benchmarks | Qwen3-VL-8B recent-4 → ViSS+TVS: StreamingBench accuracy **80.52 → 86.33%**; OVO official macro accuracy **58.97 → 69.77%**, on full splits. | §§5.1–5.2 |
| ViSS adds beyond generic temporal training | Strict-matched recent-4 comparison: **+1.73 percentage points** OVO; three paired seeds give mean **+1.72**, source-video-bootstrap 95% CI **[+0.80, +2.60]**. StreamingBench changes **−0.20 points** in the main paired comparison. | §5.3; Appendix C.1 |
| Additional supervision benefit concentrates in hallucination detection | OVO HLD: **+8.06 points**, or **15 additional correct answers out of 186**, versus strict-matched generic SFT; differences calculated before rounding. | Appendix C.1 |
| Sufficiency removal degrades target performance | Removing **116 examples** lowers recent-4 OVO macro accuracy by **1.69 points** and HLD by **4.30 points**; removal also changes training mass. | §5.3; Appendix C.2 |
| TVS adds accuracy with the checkpoint frozen | Relative to ViSS recent-4, TVS adds **1.56 points** on StreamingBench and **4.56 points** on OVO. | §5.2 |
| Adaptive access offers a frame-budget trade-off | StreamingBench: TVS **86.33% at 17.08 processed frames/query**, versus history-only **86.45% at 29.97** and static sampling **85.69% at 17.00**. | §5.3; Appendix C.3 |
| Commitment heuristic lacks consistent aggregate benefit | Accepting every acquired disagreeing proposal changes StreamingBench **86.33 → 86.53%** and OVO **69.77 → 69.75%**, with acquisition unchanged. | Appendix C.3 |
| Replacement dominates the covered selection gap | Correct proposals were retained then replaced in **85/127 (66.9%)** StreamingBench and **194/270 (71.9%)** OVO oracle-gap queries. | §5.4; Table 15 |
| Selection cannot resolve most recent-view errors | No tested view produces the reference answer for **56.3%** of StreamingBench and **62.9%** of OVO recent-view errors. | Appendix D.1 |

These results support complementary, task-dependent benefits from supervision and evidence access. They do not demonstrate a unique internal state representation, independent additive effects across arbitrary checkpoints, or universal latency savings. The authors’ best-among-listed-systems claim uses external rows with differing protocols; the matched internal comparisons provide stronger evidence (§3.2; Appendix C.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope:** controlled experiments use one backbone and two benchmarks; transfer across scales and model families remains untested (§6).
- **Supervision attribution:** the strict control changes questions and answers together, so wording, label quality, and historical dependence are not separately isolated. Family removals also change visual exposure and weighted training mass (Appendices C.1–C.2).
- **Grounding limits:** verification checks source binding and answer stability, but does not certify human-unambiguous labels or that every probe requires multiple frames (Appendix B.2).
- **Access and selection limits:** views are query-independent and not strictly nested. TVS can reduce hallucination-detection and real-time accuracy, and its commitment margin has no consistent aggregate advantage (Appendices A.1, C.3–C.4).
- **Unresolved-error ambiguity:** missing evidence, failed interpretation, and visual ambiguity are not independently separated (Appendix D.3).
- **Reproducibility and deployment:** training annotations, raw media, and internal experiment records are excluded from the stated release. Frame counts do not establish wall-clock efficiency; social and demographic biases were not audited (Reproducibility and Ethics statements; Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does ViSS transfer across model families and scales?** Only one backbone is controlled here. Repeat the matched generic-versus-ViSS comparison on additional backbones with the same data and views.
2. **Which supervision property drives the extra gain?** Wording, target quality, temporal dependence, and training mass remain entangled. Run matched replacements that preserve exposure and weighting while varying one property.
3. **Can answer selection preserve correct proposals more reliably?** Replacement dominates covered selection errors, yet the current margin rule shows no consistent benefit. Compare alternative selectors on frozen proposals using disjoint calibration and evaluation.
4. **Why do all tested views fail on unresolved queries?** Evidence coverage and interpretation are not independently audited. Evaluate dense causal prefixes with human evidence annotations to distinguish omitted observations from readout failures.
