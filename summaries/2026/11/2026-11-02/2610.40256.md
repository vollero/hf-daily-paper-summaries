# When Requirements Interact: Scaling Long-Horizon Coding via Interdependent Specifications

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40256) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40256)


## One-sentence takeaway

CodeSpecWeave synthesizes repository coding tasks with coupled requirements and trajectory-reviewed tests, with reported reinforcement-learning gains on long-horizon benchmarks, although the supplied evidence does not isolate requirement interdependence as the cause.

## Problem and motivation

Repository changes must satisfy interacting obligations involving compatibility, state, execution order, and error handling. The authors argue that existing training tasks underrepresent these obligations, while simply adding independent requirements produces checklists rather than joint engineering reasoning (§1–2.2).

Verification is another bottleneck: generated tests can overlook interaction failures or reject valid implementations by imposing unstated constraints (§2.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task synthesis:** Starting from developer requirements mined from repository histories, issues, and pull requests, an agent jointly develops requests, specifications, and executable tests. Requirements share components or state transitions so that implementation decisions constrain one another (§2.2–2.3).
- **Validation and repair:** Multiple solver trials produce patches and execution trajectories. Review and repair agents use these to detect missing checks and unintended restrictions, followed by fresh validation (§2.4).
- **Training data and evaluation asset:** The authors construct 4,670 training tasks across 2,865 repositories and a manually reviewed benchmark containing 100 tasks and 1,044 specifications. They evaluate GRPO training on two MiMo backbones (§2.4, §3.2; Appendix B.1).

These results support the authors’ claim that the complete training pipeline improves the tested models, including on an external long-horizon benchmark. They do not establish that interdependence alone causes the gains. Likewise, output-token correlations do not establish that longer reasoning causes poorer performance.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** RL requires substantial compute; task execution requires concurrent container infrastructure; strong generation models and extensive filtering make construction expensive (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No reported controlled ablation separates interdependence from requirement count, longer prompts, verifier quality, or difficulty filtering. CodeSpecWeave queries are also longer than comparison datasets (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Training covers two MiMo backbones, and frontier evaluation uses different agent harnesses across model families, limiting attribution of cross-family differences to models alone (§3.1–3.2).
- **Analyst inference—behavioral interpretation:** Full trajectories contain more edit–test loops, but within the first 40 turns Flash-SFT’s proportion with at least two loops falls from 47.7% to 45.7%. This weakens the interpretation that training uniformly increases verification intensity independently of trajectory length (Appendix E).
- **Evidence availability:** Several tables appear only as captions. Full baseline results, training hyperparameters, and detailed review outcomes cannot be inspected from this text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does coupling itself improve transfer?** The supplied experiments do not isolate it. Compare coupled and additive tasks matched for repositories, requirement count, prompt length, verifier quality, and training compute.
2. **How reliable are accepted verifiers?** Review detects many initial flaws, but residual error is not quantified. Independently audit accepted tasks using valid alternative implementations and deliberately faulty patches.
3. **Do gains extend beyond the tested backbones?** RL evidence covers two MiMo models. Repeat compute-matched training on other model families and sizes.
4. **Are longer trajectories responsible for increased verification?** Appendix E gives a weaker fixed-turn result for Flash-SFT. Compare verification and task success under matched turn and token budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative clearly describes the method and selected results, but omits table bodies and figure data.

Material missing information includes exact baseline scores and gains, complete RL hyperparameters and compute budgets, uncertainty estimates for reported performance differences, train–evaluation overlap checks, residual verifier error rates, detailed repository/language coverage, and public asset release locations.
