# TernScope: Model-Hardware Co-Design for Efficient Ternary Endoscopic Segmentation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29617) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29617)


## One-sentence takeaway
TernScope combines a compact segmentation network, progressive ternary training, and a tailored accelerator, reporting improved cross-dataset segmentation and lower synthesized hardware costs, although the supplied text omits the detailed result tables.

## Problem and motivation
Edge endoscopic segmentation must balance lesion delineation, model storage, and inference speed. The authors argue that compact architectures alone do not guarantee efficient execution: memory traffic, operator structure, and arithmetic precision also matter. Their target gap is retaining segmentation quality while converting every convolution—including the input and prediction layers—to ternary weights on the fused deployment graph (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Compact architecture:** SplitLK applies large spatial kernels to a channel subset, then mixes all channels. The Nearest Unifier combines projected encoder features through narrow additive fusion (§II-A–B).
- **Fusion before progressive quantization:** Training branches are merged before Ternary Progressive Quantization-aware Training (TPQ). Conversion proceeds from deeper encoder layers through the decoder to the input and head; all 25 convolutions ultimately use ternary weights and 8-bit activations. Bias, scaling, and accumulation retain wider precision (§II-C).
- **Matching hardware:** A Gemmini-based ternary array replaces general multiplication in the processing-element product path with sign selection and accumulation. A shared engine handles scaling, bias, feature addition, and requantization (§III).

The contribution is the integrated architecture–training–hardware design; the paper explicitly builds on prior structural re-parameterization and quantization methods (§I).

All results below are author-reported.

The evidence supports a promising tradeoff for the evaluated segmentation workload and synthesis setting. It does not establish clinical effectiveness or measured whole-device energy savings. Tables I–III appear only as captions in the supplied retrieval, so absolute segmentation scores and detailed hardware entries cannot be checked. The throughput improvement reflects both ternary arithmetic and changed post-processing placement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified future work:** Sparsity-aware execution and compressed storage for zero-valued ternary weights remain proposed extensions (§V).
- **Analyst inference—limited scope:** Training uses one dataset, with transfer tested on two external datasets at a fixed input resolution. Broader endoscopic or clinical generalization remains unestablished (§IV-A).
- **Analyst inference—training confounds:** Baselines and TPQ use different optimizers, batch sizes, and initialization procedures. The supplied text does not isolate how much improvement comes from precision, progressive conversion, or the training recipe (§IV-A–B).
- **Analyst inference—hardware attribution:** Arithmetic, accumulator width, control logic, and post-processing placement all change. Their individual contributions are not quantified in the supplied text (§IV-C).
- **Missing evidence:** No repeated-run variability, confidence intervals, physical-device measurements, or separate SplitLK/decoder ablations are available in the supplied text. A repository is linked, but its contents are not supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Which TPQ choices drive the transfer gains?** Conversion order and scheduling are not separately isolated. Compare direct QAT and alternative conversion orders under matched training budgets and multiple seeds.
- **How robust is the model beyond these datasets?** Evidence covers one training source and two external image datasets. Test unchanged checkpoints on additional acquisition settings and endoscopic video.
- **How much hardware benefit comes from each design change?** The comparison combines arithmetic and execution-placement changes. Ablate ternary processing, accumulator width, and shared post-processing while measuring latency and energy.
- **Can ternary zeros yield additional savings?** Sparse execution is proposed but not evaluated. Compare dense and sparsity-aware implementations, including indexing and data-movement overhead (§V).
