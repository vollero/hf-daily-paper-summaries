# Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37481) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481)


## One-sentence takeaway
SimpleLoRIF makes gradient-based filtering practical for large instruction datasets, and removing the highest-scoring fifth of Tulu 3 reduces measured jailbreak success across several model families while generally retaining capability (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Benign-looking instruction data can weaken model safety during fine-tuning, making content-based filtering insufficient. The authors seek examples whose *training updates* align with unsafe behavior, addressing the computational cost that limits gradient attribution on large datasets (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Scalable attribution:** SimpleLoRIF retains LoRIF’s random projections and rank-one gradient factors, drops curvature correction, and restricts backpropagation to the final four transformer blocks. It ranks examples by gradient similarity rather than estimating calibrated removal effects (§3).
- **Emergent-misalignment evidence:** The authors test whether insecure-code examples align with unrelated harmful generations in gradient space, using matched secure-code controls and two model families (§4).
- **Dataset filtering and release:** SafeTulu removes the 20% of Tulu 3 examples most aligned with unsafe SafeRLHF responses, using Qwen 3 0.6B embeddings and clustered targets. The authors report releasing the dataset and a configurable Hugging Face Space (§§5.1, 7.2).
- **Exploratory analysis:** Removed examples reveal candidate patterns—including tool-execution failures and narrative arithmetic—but the authors explicitly describe these associations as noncausal (§§6, 13).

The filtering interventions support a dataset-level safety benefit under the tested attacks and models. They do not establish that every removed example individually causes degradation. Emergent-misalignment AUC measures ordering of insecure versus secure examples, **not harmful-response frequency**. Capability preservation and superiority over every baseline are author-reported, but their full numerical support cannot be inspected in this retrieval.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Only general safety degradation is tested; specific behaviors such as sycophancy and alternative safety target sets remain untested (§9).
- **Author-stated transfer constraint:** Emergent-misalignment attribution transfers asymmetrically across models, limiting assumptions about universal gradient signals (§15.2).
- **Author-stated evaluation caveat:** ActorBreaker uses a smaller actor-network generator than its original setup and obtains lower ASRs; the authors suggest generator choice as a possible explanation (§5.1).
- **Author-stated causal boundary:** Qualitative patterns are correlational and do not establish that tracebacks, arithmetic styles, or direct answers cause safety loss (§13.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Choosing the computational configuration on an unnamed math task leaves its optimality for safety attribution uncertain; the supplied text does not report a safety-specific layer-selection ablation (§3.3).
- **Evidence availability:** Missing table cells prevent assessment of uncertainty, capability trade-offs, and detailed baseline comparisons. Cluster count and the final global ranking procedure are also not fully specified in the supplied scoring description (§14.4).

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive is filtering to the unsafe target set?** Only SafeRLHF is tested (§9). Rebuild filters using alternative targets at matched removal budgets, then evaluate both shared and target-specific harms.
2. **Which removed features causally affect safety?** The qualitative analysis establishes associations (§13). Remove only traceback-containing or narrative-arithmetic examples and compare against size- and subset-matched random removals.
3. **When does attribution transfer between models?** SafeTulu transfers across families, whereas emergent-misalignment attribution can fail (§15.2). Cross embedding checkpoints, target generators, and downstream models while holding data and scoring fixed.
4. **Does the four-block choice remain best for safety filtering?** Its selection uses math detection (§3.3). Compare layer budgets on downstream ASR, capability, and embedding cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and principal numerical findings, but omits numerical table bodies, most standard errors, detailed capability and ActorBreaker scores, emergent-misalignment target-set sizes, the math-ablation dataset identity, and complete clustering/ranking settings. Releases are author-reported and were not independently verified.
