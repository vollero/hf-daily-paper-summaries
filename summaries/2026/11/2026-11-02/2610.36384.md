# SOMA: A Benchmark for Fine-Grained Egocentric Behavior and Body-Motion Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36384) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36384)


## One-sentence takeaway

SOMA exposes substantial weaknesses in egocentric body-motion understanding: the strongest zero-shot model scores 50.0% versus humans’ 68.5%, while aligned motion input yields small, task-dependent fine-tuning gains (Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation

Egocentric cameras often show only fragments of the wearer’s body, requiring models to infer posture, limb use, and movement from indirect cues distributed across time. The authors argue that existing evaluations emphasize objects, scenes, and coarse activities, leaving unclear whether models understand the moving body itself (§1; Table 1).

This gap matters for wearable assistants, augmented reality, and systems that assist or learn from humans. These are motivating applications; the paper evaluates benchmark performance rather than deployed assistance (§1; Ethics statement).

## Approach and contributions

- **A body-centric benchmark:** SOMA contains 1,632 human-verified, four-option questions over 1,611 clips from Nymeria and Ego-Exo4D. Its twelve tasks cover body configuration, motion dynamics, and temporal reasoning (§§3.1–3.3).
- **A curated evaluation pipeline:** Task-specific annotation selection and question generation are followed by language-only filtering and human review using video plus synchronized body pose. Repetition-counting questions are manually constructed (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Comparative evaluation and diagnosis:** The authors evaluate 37 MLLMs and five independent human evaluators, then study sampling density, reasoning strategies, handedness bias, counting errors, and reasoning-trace failures (§§4–5.3).
- **Training assets and multimodal baselines:** SOMA-Train supplies 28,596 QA pairs over 110.2 hours of aligned video and motion, disjoint from evaluation participants and scenes. Two existing backbones receive frozen motion-encoder features through a learned projector and undergo LoRA fine-tuning. This is an adaptation of an established architecture, rather than a new fusion mechanism (§§3.4, 5.4; Appendix E.3).
- **Denser motion reconstruction:** An adapted multi-view fitting pipeline improves Ego-Exo4D motion coverage and reconstruction accuracy relative to EE4D-Motion (Appendix E.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large zero-shot human–model gap | On SOMA, Qwen3.6-27B reaches 50.0% overall accuracy; five humans average 68.5% ± 5.5% SD. Models normally receive 16 uniformly sampled frames at temperature 0; humans receive videos with unlimited answering time. | §4; Tables 2, 4; Appendix C.1 |
| Counting is especially difficult for models | Repetition-counting accuracy is 88.9% for humans versus 43.6% for the best model on that task, GPT-5.6 Luna, under the standard evaluation. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| More uniformly sampled frames have limited aggregate benefit | Across nine models, mean accuracy increases by 5.4 percentage points from 1 to 16 frames; sampling beyond 16, up to 64, adds at most 0.5 points. | §5.2; Figure 4A |
| Explicit reasoning helps selectively | Among five models tested with CoT prompting, Qwen3.8-27B improves from 47.0% to 50.9%; the other four show negligible change or decline. | Appendix D.1; Table 5 |
| Handedness accuracy is asymmetric | Across 37 models, right-hand questions average 78.8% accuracy versus 44.3% for left-hand questions; human means are 89.8% and 92.7%, respectively. | §5.3; Appendix D.4.2 |
| Motion adds modest gains over separately trained video baselines | With matched video preprocessing, Qwen3-VL-8B improves from 41.49% video-only to 42.44% video+motion; InternVL3.5-8B improves from 41.42% to 41.93%. | §5.4; Table 3 |
| Motion is not consistently beneficial at inference | For the same jointly trained checkpoint, removing motion changes overall accuracy from 42.4% to 41.0% for Qwen, but from 41.9% to 42.7% for InternVL. | Appendix E.4; Table 12 |
| Blind transfer is weaker than perceptual transfer | Qwen3-VL-8B trained on text alone scores 28.1%; the video+motion model scores 42.4% with perceptual inputs and 25.8% without them; chance is 25.0%. | Appendix E.1.2; Table 8 |
| Refitting improves pose reconstruction | On 194 scored validation takes, corrected take-averaged PA-MPJPE is 36.7 mm versus EE4D-Motion’s 40.3 mm; joint offsets are fitted on training takes. | Appendix E.2; Table 9 |

The evidence establishes a substantial gap under the tested protocols and supports limited complementarity between video and motion. It does not establish that temporal integration alone causes the gap, that handedness bias originates in pretraining, or that motion reliably improves all tasks. Those explanations remain author interpretations. Human and model input access also differs, so their comparison is a performance reference rather than a controlled comparison of perceptual efficiency.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated constraints:** Motion-only baselines face out-of-distribution inputs and inconsistent suitability for multiple-choice QA, limiting interpretation of their near-chance results (Appendix D.5). Motion reconstruction excludes rock climbing and cannot compare global trajectories directly against EE4D-Motion (Appendix E.2).
- **Training-label quality:** SOMA-Train lacks human verification, although automated factuality checks are applied. The AI-use statement’s broad claim that all generated questions were human-verified should therefore be read cautiously alongside §3.4 and Appendix E.1.
- **Analyst inference—selection effects:** Filtering with four language-only solvers defines a deliberately challenging distribution; it does not prove that every retained question requires vision or is free of textual shortcuts (§3.2; Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—error-analysis scope:** Reasoning-trace analysis uses questions both selected models answered incorrectly, with LLM-assisted classification and manual verification of a sample. Its error distribution need not represent all models or all mistakes (§5.3; Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference—small-gain uncertainty:** The supplied results provide no repeated-run variability or significance tests for the modest motion gains. Their stability is therefore unclear (Tables 3, 11–12).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Release is deferred until acceptance, and several referenced prompt blocks and figure values are absent from the retrieved text (Reproducibility statement; Appendices B–D).

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptive sampling improve counting and event tracking?** Uniform sampling largely saturates, but the paper does not test event-aware frame selection. Compare uniform and motion-triggered sampling at equal frame budgets, emphasizing counting and repeated-event tasks (§5.2).

2. **Can fusion use motion selectively?** Motion helps posture but can hurt visible hand-use judgments. Test a learned modality gate against the provided token-concatenation baseline, with repeated runs and task-level reporting (Appendix E.4).

3. **What causes the handedness asymmetry?** Pretraining bias is proposed but not isolated. Evaluate carefully relabeled mirrored clips and balanced left/right fine-tuning to separate visual laterality errors from answer priors (§5.3).

4. **How well do gains transfer beyond the source corpora?** Participant and scene separation is established, but external-corpus transfer is not. Evaluate the trained baselines on independently annotated first-person recordings with the same task taxonomy (§3.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and tabulated results; **medium** in causal explanations and generalization claims.

Material missing information includes complete rendered prompt templates, numerical values behind several figures, repeated-run uncertainty for fine-tuning gains, external-dataset validation, and confirmation that promised artifacts are publicly available.
