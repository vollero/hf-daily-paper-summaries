# Learning Quality-Controllable Robot Policies with Iterative Hindsight Relabeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37393) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37393)


## One-sentence takeaway
QCP combines quality-conditioned behavior cloning with repeated collection, relabeling, and retraining to expand robot execution quality beyond initial demonstrations while improving task success in the evaluated settings (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Successful demonstrations can differ substantially in speed, smoothness, and stability, but ordinary behavior cloning does not let users select these attributes at deployment. The authors identify a further gap in existing quality-controllable policies: static demonstration datasets bound the quality range available for learning (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Quality-conditioned policy:** Measure each successful trajectory with rule-based quality scores, divide scores into equal-frequency bins, and condition a flow-matching policy on the resulting labels. Multiple attributes receive separate labels; real-world VLA policies receive labels through their language prompts (§3.1).
- **Iterative range expansion:** Collect rollouts under the preferred quality label, retain successful trajectories, recompute labels across the accumulated dataset, and retrain. Each iteration starts from random weights or the original pretrained VLA checkpoint, rather than the previous policy (§3.2, Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical validation:** Test individual and joint quality control in simulation and practical throughput and stability gains on a real robot (§4). The authors explicitly acknowledge a similar sampling-and-relabeling loop in Quark; their contribution applies this structure to robot quality using automatically measured successful trajectories (§2).

The results support controllability and improvement in the tested tasks. Main results are averaged across three seeds, although some auxiliary experiments use fewer. Quality is evaluated only on successful rollouts, so quality gains must be interpreted alongside success rates (§4.1; Appendix B).

The authors report superiority over RL baselines, but Table 1’s numerical cells are absent from the supplied text. The finite frozen-policy experiment establishes that sampled frozen rollouts did not reach the final policy’s quality range; it cannot establish that such behavior has zero probability.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ qualification:** Continuous conditioning uses different target selection from adaptive bins, so the comparison does not rule out continuous conditioning generally (Appendix F).
- **Authors’ observed boundary:** Six-attribute experiments drop conflicting or unresponsive attributes; slower two-attribute commands incur lower success. This limits claims of unrestricted joint control (Appendix E).
- **Authors’ observed boundary:** Short-segment quality labels underperform whole-trajectory labels and can reward local motion without task progress. That comparison uses one seed (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Automatic scoring and success detection are material dependencies. The experiments do not establish effectiveness for subjective qualities without reliable measurable proxies (§3).
- **Analyst inference:** Reinitialization is part of the method, but its separate benefit is not established by a reported ablation. The proposed segment-recombination explanation for exceeding demonstrations is an author hypothesis (§3.2).
- **Evidence gap:** Numerical table bodies and most plot values are missing, limiting independent assessment of baseline margins and uncertainty.

## Open research questions
1. **Can continuous conditioning match adaptive bins under matched target selection?** The current comparison changes both representation and requested targets. Test continuous conditioning with targets updated from the same per-iteration quantiles (Appendix F).
2. **How should QCP handle conflicting quality objectives?** Preliminary experiments exclude conflicting attributes. Retain those attributes and evaluate success and quality across a grid of joint requests (Appendix E).
3. **Is full reinitialization necessary?** Its benefit is motivated but not isolated. Compare restarting, continued fine-tuning, and resetting only quality embeddings under matched training budgets (§3.2).
4. **Can failed rollouts contribute useful information?** The authors report that adding failures as low-quality data degraded performance, without detailed results. Compare success-aware failure conditioning against successful-only training (§3.2, footnote 3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several key results are explicit, but quantitative coverage is incomplete.

Missing information includes numerical bodies of Tables 1–5, most plotted measurements and error bars, exact task-specific attribute exclusions, compute costs, and confirmed availability of code, trained models, or collected datasets.
