# RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42141) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42141)


## One-sentence takeaway
RoboArchitect iteratively rewrites control code around frozen robot policies, reporting substantially better held-out simulation performance without weight updates, although persistent memory’s independent contribution remains unmeasured.

## Problem and motivation
Robot policies often require new demonstrations and finetuning for unfamiliar tasks or hardware. Handwritten agent harnesses can improve frozen policies, but require repeated engineering to diagnose failures and revise control logic. The authors address automating this entire development loop while testing whether its improvements transfer beyond optimization episodes (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Automated harness optimization:** A coding model inspects rollout images, robot readings, and tool outputs, then revises executable control code. Candidates are accepted only when they improve performance on optimization episodes; evolution uses at most five iterations (§§3, 4.1).
- **Adaptive control allocation:** The harness can query the frozen policy, use auxiliary perception and language tools, or command motion directly. Evolved designs range from policy assistance to complete policy replacement (§§3, 4.2).
- **Automatically designed persistent memory:** The harness determines its own storage and retrieval structures for grasp experience, task clues, and robot calibration. Memory persists across execution episodes; each evaluation starts empty (§4.4; Appendix A.2).
- **Generalization evaluation:** Separate protocols test unseen arrangements, unseen tasks sharing manipulation skills, and one unified harness across multiple embodiments (§4.1).

These are the authors’ proposed contributions; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority over all related methods.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large average gains on unseen arrangements | Authors report average scores rising from **9.7% to 43.3%**, comparing bare policies with evolved harnesses on held-out scenes across evaluated setups. | Abstract; §1; Fig. 1 |
| Gains transfer to different tasks | Average scores rise from **12.7% to 40.2%** on held-out tasks sharing underlying manipulation skills. | Abstract; §1; Fig. 1 |
| An unusable policy can be bypassed | General X-VLA on SimplerEnv WidowX improves from **0% to 100% on optimization episodes**; evolved harness achieves **87.5% on unseen scenes** and **66.7% on unseen tasks**. | §4.2 |
| Unified control works across evaluated embodiments | With general X-VLA, the unified harness reaches **41.6% training-pool score** and **28.8% held-out score**, from a policy described as near zero. Pool sizes are **2,201 optimization episodes** and **2,160 standard evaluation episodes**. | §4.2; Appendix B |
| Finetuning comparisons favor harnesses in some settings | General-policy harnesses outperform corresponding finetuned checkpoints on VLABench under both protocols and WidowX under unseen scenes, but fall short on WidowX unseen tasks. Exact comparison scores are unavailable. | §4.2; Table 1 caption |
| Iteration generally improves held-out performance | Authors report iterative designs outperforming one-shot designs on held-out sets except RLDX-1/RoboCasa-365 under unseen scenes. | §4.2; Figs. 10–11 captions |

| Optimization has a reported modest coding budget | A complete five-iteration evolution takes **under six hours of coding-model time** and costs **under $150 on average**. Total rollout and deployment costs are not specified. | Appendix B |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that automated harness engineering improves performance in the evaluated simulations. However, the paper combines binary success rates with VLABench partial-credit progress scores under the same “success” label (§4.1), so headline averages are not uniformly task-completion rates. The supplied text contains table captions but omits table bodies, preventing verification of detailed comparisons and aggregate calculations. It also does not isolate gains from memory, auxiliary tools, or control-code changes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is entirely simulated; real-robot transfer remains future work. Optimization stops after five iterations, leaving saturation unknown. Dexterous manipulation and richer contact dynamics remain unexplored (§6).
- **Observed exception:** RLDX-1 on RoboCasa-365 improves on optimization episodes without corresponding unseen-scene improvement, demonstrating that optimization gains need not generalize (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Memory usage and illustrative examples do not establish its independent benefit; no memory-removal ablation is supplied (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Matching episode step limits helps comparison, but does not establish equal tool access, inference compute, or adaptation budgets against finetuned references (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Because memory accumulates across evaluation episodes, episode ordering may affect results; ordering sensitivity is not reported (§4.4).

## Open research questions
1. **How much does persistent memory improve success?** Retrieval hit rates do not answer this. Compare identical evolved harnesses with persistent, episode-reset, and disabled memory.
2. **Does simulation-evolved control transfer to hardware?** No physical evaluations are reported (§6). Deploy a frozen harness on corresponding robots, then measure changes after a fixed real-world refinement budget.
3. **When does additional evolution stop helping?** The iteration cap leaves saturation unresolved (§6). Run longer optimization with a fixed held-out set and track performance, cost, and overfitting.
4. **How sensitive is performance to evaluation history?** Cross-episode accumulation creates possible order dependence (§4.4). Repeat evaluation over shuffled episode orders and report variability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline findings are explicit, but detailed quantitative assessment is constrained by missing table bodies and plotted values.

Material unavailable information includes per-setup results, individual-harness task/trial counts, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variability, exact prompts and generated code, memory ablations and ordering controls, complete computational costs, and confirmed artifact-release details.
