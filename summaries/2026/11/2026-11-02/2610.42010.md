# TurnTurk-GPT: Text-Based Turn-Taking Prediction for Turkish

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42010) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42010)


## One-sentence takeaway
TurnTurk-GPT improves Turkish turn-end ranking over LiveKit on both evaluation splits, but its advantage over a zero-shot Turkish language model disappears for unseen speakers and at deployment-oriented recall levels (§VI-A–B).

## Problem and motivation
Spoken dialogue systems must distinguish completed turns from temporary pauses to avoid interruptions and delayed responses. The authors identify a lack of public evaluation of multilingual detectors on real Turkish conversation, where turn-final morphology may provide different cues from English (§I). They study how much task-specific training adds beyond an untuned Turkish language model.

## Approach and contributions
- **Turkish model adaptation:** Fine-tune a 124M-parameter Turkish GPT-2 using the existing TurnGPT objective: insert turn-shift and speaker tokens, train with causal language modelling, and score each word using the turn-shift probability at its final subword (§IV-A). Compare synthetic-only, real-only, mixed, and synthetic-then-real training (§IV-B).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Evaluate normalized text at word boundaries with recording-disjoint and speaker-disjoint folds, nested threshold selection, recording-level bootstrap comparisons, and pause-specific negatives (§V).
- **Empirical analyses:** Examine zero-shot boundary scores, synthetic-data training, final-word morphology, backbone choice, and matched-recall operating points (§VI). The datasets come from prior work; the authors report releasing the model and evaluation protocol (§I, §VIII).

The evidence supports improved ranking over the evaluated multilingual detector and a narrower advantage over zero-shot scoring. It does not establish reliable standalone endpointing, superiority on unseen speakers over zero-shot scoring, or improved live interaction. Evaluation uses final transcripts, not streaming recognition (§IV-B, §VII-D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only six conversations and six speakers; speaker-disjoint training contains just two or three recordings (§V-A, §VII-D).
- **Author-stated deployment gap:** Offline final transcripts exclude streaming hypothesis revisions and recognition latency. LiveKit is evaluated outside its complete production pipeline (§VII-D).
- **Author-stated label mismatch:** Floor-transfer boundaries do not necessarily indicate when a system response is appropriate; internal pauses may invite backchannels (§VII-D).
- **Analyst inference:** Final-word truncation supports reliance on word endings, but does not isolate particular grammatical suffixes from other effects of truncation. The English-backbone comparison also does not isolate tokenizer effects from backbone differences (§VI-D).
- **Missing evidence:** Most table bodies are absent. The claim that synthetic-only training has the “highest” pre-pause false-alarm rate is difficult to reconcile directly with larger rates reported elsewhere; thresholds differ across reported settings (§VI-A–B, §VI-E).

## Open research questions
- **Does more speaker diversity improve generalization?** The zero-shot advantage disappears on unseen speakers. Train on progressively more speakers while controlling training volume and retain speaker-disjoint testing (§VI-A, §VII-A).
- **Can realistic synthetic dialogue improve ranking?** Synthetic-only training is weak and S2R does not significantly beat real-only training. Compare synthetic corpora with pauses, repairs, and resumptions against the existing corpus under equal budgets (§VI-E, §VII-C).
- **Can additional modalities resolve pauses?** Text baselines remain near chance at pause moments. Compare text, text-plus-audio, and text-plus-audio/video on identical pause decisions (§VI-C, §VIII).
- **Do offline gains survive streaming recognition?** Partial hypotheses and latency are unevaluated. Replay streaming recognizer outputs and measure interruption errors and response delay (§VII-D).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The narrative and paired-comparison table support the central findings, but Tables I, II, IV, V, and VI lack their full contents, and figure values are unavailable. Material gaps include exact pause-only model scores, complete operating-point results, corpus-size statistics, learning rates and training-step budgets, detailed backbone controls, and streaming or interactive evaluation.
