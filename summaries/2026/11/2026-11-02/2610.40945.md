# On the Origin and Evolution of Training-Inference Mismatch in LLM Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40945) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40945)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors separate engine numerical error from update-driven policy lag and propose discrepancy-lag correction (DLC), which combines score centering, lag-ratio clipping, and adaptive sampler synchronization to stabilize the tested LLM reinforcement-learning runs.

## Problem and motivation
LLM reinforcement learning often samples responses with an inference engine and computes gradients with a separate trainer. Different numerics and stale sampler weights make these responses off-policy, even when training nominally assumes current-policy samples.

Existing corrections behave differently under quantization and staleness. The authors ask why corrections that remove the same prefix-level drift can diverge during training, identifying the evolution of the remaining bias as the missing explanation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Component-wise diagnosis:** Evaluate the trainer at the sampler’s weights to split each token’s log-probability mismatch exactly into numerical discrepancy and policy lag. The factorization builds on prior work; the contribution is tracing its components and their residual biases through training (§4, Eq. 2).
- **Feedback-loop analysis:** Decompose the bias remaining after centering into prefix, continuation, token, and tail terms. The authors show how lag-dependent residual bias feeds into subsequent updates, and measure the resulting excess movement under score centering (§5, Eqs. 3–5).
- **DLC:** Center the numerical discrepancy, clip the policy ratio against the sampler’s weights, and synchronize when the measured lag-ratio effective sample size (ESS) falls below half the batch. DLC requires an additional trainer forward pass at the sampler’s weights and sampler top-token probabilities (§7.1, Algorithm 1).
- **Diagnostic finding:** Evaluate trainer-generated responses to expose collapses that KL measured on stale-sampler prefixes misses (§7.3).

## Evidence and main results

Deviation below means full-vocabulary KL to a paired on-policy model on fixed initial-policy prefixes, divided by the mean KL between independent on-policy trainings. Main experiments use three runs per configuration, each lasting 40 steps (§3.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical discrepancy and lag evolve differently | Quantized-sampler discrepancy stays near **0.02 nats/token**, while stale-sampler lag grows by **two orders of magnitude**, on both principal model sizes. | §4.2, Fig. 2d |
| Centering alone struggles with staleness | Qwen3-0.6B, stale sampler, step 40: score centering reaches **32×** deviation and **12.2%** accuracy; importance sampling with centering reaches **2.1×** and **32.0%**. | §4.1, Table 2 discussion |
| Measurements support a lag-dependent feedback term | Qwen3-0.6B stale runs at two learning rates: quadratic excess-update fit has **R² = 0.987**, versus **0.90** for a linear fit. | §5.3, Fig. 3f |
| Importance weighting loses reliability as ESS falls | Qwen3-1.7B under staleness: measured ESS crosses below **0.5 at step 11**, versus Gaussian prediction at step 14; deviation leaves the floor at step 11 and reaches **152× at step 30**. | §6.1, Fig. 4 |
| DLC limits deviation but retains an accuracy gap | Stale sampler, step 40: Qwen3-0.6B DLC reaches **1.9×** deviation and **31.7%** accuracy versus **46.5%** on-policy; Qwen3-1.7B reaches **0.6×** and **51.9%** versus **57.6%** on-policy. | §7.2 |
| Synchronization prevents observed DLC collapses | On Qwen3-1.7B, **two of three** DLC runs without synchronization fall below initial-model accuracy; all three full-DLC runs remain above it. Prefix-based deviation misses the collapses. | §7.3, Fig. 5 |
| Extensions provide limited broader evidence | Single-run extensions: stale Qwen3-8B DLC reaches **3.1×** deviation and **49.6%** accuracy; continuously asynchronous Qwen3-1.7B reaches **1.2×** and **55.9%**. | Appendix C |

These results support the proposed mechanism and correction within the tested settings. They do not establish the half-batch ESS threshold as a universal boundary, or low prefix-based KL as sufficient evidence of preserved task performance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described approximation limits:** The first-order loop analysis ceases to apply once lag becomes large. Its bias prediction omits the continuation term and substitutes sampled advantages for unavailable prefix-conditioned means (Appendices F–H).
- **Imperfect quantitative explanation:** Exact-weight bias predictions still overestimate measured norms by roughly 1.9–7.9 times, with modest directional agreement (Appendix G, Table 10).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Main evidence covers short Countdown runs with small Qwen3 models and SGD. Larger-model and continuous-asynchrony extensions have one run per correction (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—unresolved tuning:** Clip range, head size, and synchronization threshold are fixed; the supplied text reports no sensitivity sweep (§7.1).
- **Measurement risk demonstrated by the authors:** KL on initial-policy prefixes can miss collapse on trainer-generated trajectories (§7.3).
- **Cost:** Reporting top-128 probabilities doubles generation time in this implementation; DLC also adds a reference forward pass (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **How transferable is the ESS threshold?** Its strongest crossing evidence comes from the tested stale Qwen3-1.7B setting. Sweep thresholds across tasks, optimizers, and training lengths, measuring collapse frequency, accuracy, and synchronization cost.
- **How much does the estimated tail affect stability?** DLC’s centering depends on a proportional-tail approximation. Compare different head sizes with full-vocabulary centering on tractable models.
- **Can trajectory diagnostics improve synchronization?** Prefix KL misses observed collapses. Compare ESS-only control with control incorporating trainer-response off-support statistics.
- **Does the feedback account remain predictive over longer training?** The local approximation breaks down at larger lag. Test predictions across repeated synchronization periods while estimating continuation contributions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the mechanism and reported outcomes, but generality remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes complete numeric contents of several referenced tables, uncertainty intervals for main comparisons, hyperparameter sensitivity results, longer multi-task evaluations, and an explicit code or checkpoint release location.
