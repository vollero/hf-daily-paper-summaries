# WeFair: Fidelity-Aware Alignment from Human Feedback for Personalized Query Rewriting in Short-Video Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16927) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16927)


## One-sentence takeaway
WeFair combines curriculum SFT, consumption-based DPO, and exact-target GRPO to train a compact personalized query rewriter, reporting improved rewriting accuracy and online search outcomes while reducing intent drift.

## Problem and motivation
Ambiguous short-video queries require personalization, but rewrites can substitute historically engaging interests for the user’s immediate need. The system must also decide when to preserve the original query and meet production latency constraints. The authors identify a gap in how training stages use feedback with different degrees of direct evidence about intent: subsequent consumption is informative, but less explicit than actual reformulations (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Screened behavioral supervision:** Mine context-to-query records, adjacent user reformulations, and preservation examples. Apply lexical rules, independent LLM majority votes, and contextual-support checks to reduce unsupported personalization (§3.1).
- **Feedback matched to training stages:** Curriculum SFT progresses through context-to-query prediction, teacher-generated reasoning, and direct target prediction. DPO learns preferences using reformulations and subsequent consumption. Final GRPO rewards exact agreement with screened reformulation or preservation targets (§3.2).
- **Empirical supervision-fidelity analysis:** The authors report that broad supervision benefits the complete SFT curriculum, whereas strict verification outperforms tested auxiliary GRPO rewards. The contribution is this feedback allocation and its evaluation; SFT, DPO, and GRPO themselves are established components (§§4.3–4.4).
- **Production integration:** Direct decoding produces a rewrite or preservation marker; rewrites support cached and vector retrieval, with the original query retained in ranking (§3.3).

These results support the authors’ account of a useful rewriting–preservation trade-off in their system. They do not directly prove that engagement gains measure immediate-intent satisfaction. The online result also combines the rewriter with retrieval integration, so its headline gains do not isolate training alone. Missing table bodies prevent checking most baseline margins and ablation magnitudes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged target rigidity:** Exact matching discourages some acceptable alternative rewrites; strict verification is therefore imperfect semantic supervision (§4.4).
- **Author-acknowledged preservation bias:** Preservation is easier to reward than generating the exact rewrite, creating a reward-hacking risk. The authors use data ratios and reward weights to mitigate it (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—selection and labeling:** Screening favors context-supported, lexically related reformulations. Performance on valid rewrites excluded by those filters remains unclear; the Chinese function-word filter also limits demonstrated portability (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Evidence comes from one platform and a short temporal test window. Judge agreement was checked on one baseline’s outputs, leaving cross-model agreement uncertain (§4.1).
- **Comparison qualification:** AWHCP is a platform adaptation with changed retrieval feedback and weak-label generation, rather than an unchanged implementation (Appendix A.1).
- **Reproducibility:** Raw user records and privacy-sensitive feedback processing are withheld. The supplied text contains table captions without their data, including training statistics and GRPO settings (Appendix A.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Can verification accept equivalent rewrites without losing reward reliability?** Exact matching rejects acceptable alternatives (§4.4). Compare single-target verification with human-validated sets of equivalent targets, measuring drift, preservation, and advantage purity.
2. **How much online benefit comes from training versus retrieval expansion?** The headline experiment combines WeFair with cache and vector retrieval (§4.6). Run a factorial test crossing rewriter choice with retrieval configuration.
3. **Does the approach generalize beyond the screened WeChat distribution?** Evaluation uses internal logs and language-specific filtering (§§3.1, 4.1). Test another search domain or language with independent human intent judgments.
4. **How sensitive is preservation to reward and data balance?** The authors identify excessive preservation as a risk (§3.2). Sweep rewrite/preservation sampling ratios and reward weights while tracking both decision recalls and overall accuracy.
