# HALO: Human-Grounded Granular Evaluation of Proactive Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39086) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39086)


## One-sentence takeaway
HALO combines executable personal-assistant scenarios with granular human judgments, finding that premature or excessive intervention accounts for 61.20% of rejected responses in its constructed benchmark (§4.2.3).

## Problem and motivation
Proactive agents must decide whether, when, and how far to help without an explicit request. Recognizing a need is insufficient: assistance can still arrive too early, lack useful detail, or exceed authorization. The authors identify limited per-response human diagnosis in existing evaluations, particularly for connecting retrieved evidence to intervention timing and scope (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **HALO-Arena:** Executable simulated apps plus SQLite databases and text files, covering Dependency, Blockage, Risk, and Opportunity needs across 400 scenarios in 50 personal environments and 10 domains. Model-generated scenarios undergo automated checks and human audit (§3.1).
- **HALO-8K:** 8,000 responses from 10 models, evaluated by 20 annotators for acceptability and action level: observation, attention, preparation, or execution. Rejected interventions receive separate timing and intensity diagnoses, with optional additional issue labels (§3.2).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Analyses separate accepted intervention from warranted restraint and examine evidence acquisition, resource consumption, and scope failures despite appropriate timing (§§4–5). The contribution is an evaluation framework and dataset rather than a new proactive-agent policy.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best-model acceptability remains incomplete | Among 800 annotated responses per model, Gemini3.8-Flash reaches 72.88%; open-source leader DeepSeekV4-Pro reaches 56.63%. Transport and parsing failures are excluded. | Table 1; §4.2.1 |
| Over-proactivity dominates pooled rejections | Premature timing or excessive intensity occurs in 2,188 of 3,575 rejected responses (61.20%), counting overlapping diagnoses once. | §4.2.3; Figure 3 |
| More intervention can accompany worse outcomes | At 623 shared checkpoints, Gemini3.1-Pro intervenes 28.89 percentage points more often than Gemini3.8-Flash but has 14.77 points lower acceptability; accepted-restraint contribution declines by 20.71 points. | §5.1; Figure 4 |
| Retrieved content accompanies better reminders | Among 5,536 L1 responses, acceptability is 59.0% with database/file content versus 40.8% without; insufficient-help rates are 5.2% versus 15.5%. | §5.3; Figure 6 |
| Retrieval association persists after adjustment | With checkpoint fixed effects and model indicators, content access corresponds to +9.25 percentage points acceptability and −5.86 points insufficient-help diagnosis across 5,232 L1 responses at 1,730 checkpoints. | §5.3 |
| Appropriate timing does not ensure appropriate scope | Among appropriately timed rejected interventions, 329/623 L1 responses (52.81%) are insufficient, while 238/312 L3 responses (76.28%) are excessive. | §5.4; Figure 7 |
| Exploratory judge adaptation improves verdicts but harms diagnosis | On 400 validation items sharing scenarios with training, Qwen3.5-9B LoRA raises verdict accuracy from 48.50% to 56.00%, while joint-label accuracy falls from 33.75% to 22.75%. | Appendix H; Table 11 |

These results establish diagnostic patterns within the sampled constructed environments. Retrieval comparisons and model-pair comparisons are observational; they do not establish that retrieval or intervention frequency causes the measured changes. The judge-adaptation results are validation comparisons, not independent test evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Simulated scenarios and designed need frequencies do not estimate everyday prevalence. Independent checkpoints omit user reactions and cumulative intervention effects (Limitations, p. 9).
- **Author-stated:** Annotators have broadly similar backgrounds; aggregate acceptability does not represent universal preferences. Action levels also omit differences in reversibility and burden, while binary acceptance does not rank useful alternatives (p. 9).
- **Analyst inference:** Each response receives one verdict, and no response-level inter-annotator agreement is reported. Scenario-audit agreement does not establish response-label reliability (§3.2.2; Appendices C, E).
- **Author-qualified comparisons:** Models are not evaluated on identical checkpoint samples throughout; adjustment preserves rankings but does not resolve small score gaps. Resource comparisons depend on recorded inference settings and provider accounting conventions (§4.2.1; Appendix D).
- **Reproducibility constraint:** The original construction-candidate archive is no longer retained; pipeline pass rates rely on historical project records (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does retrieving evidence causally improve calibration?** The reported association may reflect model or response-selection differences. Randomize retrieval access or budgets for the same model at identical checkpoints.
- **Do the findings transfer to sustained assistance?** Checkpoint resets exclude feedback and accumulated burden. Evaluate persistent episodes with user responses, measuring accepted help, interruption burden, and changing preferences.
- **How stable are acceptability judgments across users?** Similar annotator backgrounds and single verdicts leave preference variation unresolved. Collect repeated ratings from diverse participants and compare shared versus personalized rubrics.
- **Can automated judges preserve granular diagnosis?** Exploratory LoRA improves verdict accuracy while reducing joint accuracy. Test diagnostic training objectives on held-out scenarios and personal environments.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and descriptive results; external validity and causal interpretations remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes response-level annotation agreement, detailed annotator demographics, complete model inference configurations, uncertainty estimates for main comparisons, independent judge-test results, and verified asset availability or licensing.
