# Think Twice: The Metis-1.6 Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37903) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37903)


## One-sentence takeaway
Metis-1.6 combines competitive small-model mathematics results with useful training-failure diagnoses, but incomplete contest decontamination and confounded efficiency comparisons limit its strongest claims (§§9–11).

## Problem and motivation
The author investigates whether improved data, training methods and architecture can deliver useful language models with less training data and fewer active parameters than larger-scale recipes. The report also addresses a practical reporting gap: documenting implementation mistakes and unsuccessful post-training experiments alongside released-model performance (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Two released language models:** Praxis and Logos repeatedly apply a shared hybrid Mamba-2/attention stack with latent mixture-of-experts blocks, pass-conditioned residual mixing and cross-pass depth memory. Both use fixed double passes; the released models do not adapt recursion depth (§3).
- **Training and implementation audit:** The report documents unintended optimizer routing, nearly unused hashed n-gram memory, supervised fine-tuning failure modes and unsuccessful reinforcement-learning/distillation stages. Released checkpoints stop after supervised fine-tuning (§§4–8).
- **Length-reward analysis:** A proposition identifies when group-mean normalization gives long correct answers negative advantages. A correctness-conditioned advantage clamp removes this negative signal, although the reasoning specialist was not rerun with it (§7.2).
- **Proxy architecture and optimizer experiments:** Separate controlled campaigns select a revised loop design and optimizer for MoRE. These are preliminary proxy results rather than validation of the forthcoming full model (§10).

The architectural ingredients largely build on cited methods; the report’s distinctive contributions are their integration, released models, failure analysis and controlled proxy comparisons.

These results establish reported performance under the author’s harness and an exact reward-sign failure under stated assumptions. They do not isolate the cause of the released models’ efficiency, demonstrate lower measured serving cost, or establish that the clamp restores reasoning performance. Active parameters are a compute proxy; generated reasoning length also varies substantially (§§6.1, 9.2, 11).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated contamination risk:** Several recent contests—including those supporting the strongest mathematics claims—were absent from the decontamination manifest. Dedicated overlap checks remain outstanding (§11).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** The custom harness may disadvantage comparators relative to their recommended settings. Training data, model size and post-training differ across models, and some training-token counts are undisclosed (§§9.1–9.2, 11).
- **Author-stated architectural confounding:** Attention actually trained with AdamW while other routed matrices used plain Muon. Released-model results therefore do not isolate architectural effects (§§4.2, 11).
- **Author-stated proxy uncertainty:** Each ablation arm has one run on a smaller proxy; reported errors represent evaluation noise rather than training-seed variance (§11).
- **Author-stated clamp-validation gap:** The reasoning specialist showing degradation was not rerun with the clamp; its use elsewhere involved short specialist runs (§11).
- **Analyst inference:** Memory redundancy and hash collisions are plausible explanations offered by the author, but the supplied experiments do not separately test these mechanisms (§5).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Some gate and release scores differ, and §4.3 reports different Praxis RULER values from Appendix A. These settings should not be merged without clarification.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the mathematics advantage survive complete overlap checking?** Recent contests were omitted from decontamination (§11). Next experiment: audit all training stages for contest overlap and reevaluate on clean subsets.
2. **Does the advantage clamp preserve reasoning while shortening answers?** The mathematical fix is established, but the affected specialist lacks a matched rerun (§7.2). Next experiment: compare clamped and unclamped runs with identical prompts, caps and seeds.
3. **Do MoRE’s proxy gains persist at release scale?** Current evidence uses smaller, single-run arms (§§10–11). Next experiment: repeat matched architecture and optimizer comparisons across seeds and larger token budgets.
4. **Why did hashed memory become unused?** Redundancy and collision noise remain unseparated hypotheses (§5). Next experiment: independently vary injection position, table capacity and local convolution access, measuring gate usage and held-out factual recall.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main descriptive findings and reward-sign derivation, but causal and efficiency interpretations remain limited.

Material unavailable information includes complete contents of several caption-only training/ablation tables, contest overlap-audit results, training-seed variance, matched reasoning-specialist clamp experiments, detailed serving-cost measurements and a full MoRE specification.
