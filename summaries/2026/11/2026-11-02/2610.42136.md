# ReLift: Dense 3D Tracking as Lifted Trajectory Repair

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42136) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42136)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReLift repairs 3D trajectories lifted from pretrained 2D tracks and geometry using a corrector trained on simulated errors, improving all evaluated provider combinations without retraining and achieving the authors’ reported best mean WorldTrack accuracy (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Dense monocular 3D tracking must establish correspondences, estimate geometry, and infer motion during occlusion. The authors argue that learning all three jointly stretches scarce 4D supervision when pretrained trackers and geometry models already provide strong inputs (§1).

Simply lifting 2D tracks into 3D is competitive, but inherits tracking drift, depth fluctuations, and observations on incorrect surfaces. Occluded tracks can sample the occluder’s depth. ReLift concentrates supervision on repairing these errors and completing missing motion (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Lifting baseline:** Combine frozen 2D tracks with estimated depth, intrinsics, and camera poses; mask unreliable observations, interpolate internal gaps, and hold the last observed position afterward. The authors show this initialization can rival dedicated trackers under matched geometry (§§3.1–3.2, 4.2).
- **Geometric trajectory corrector:** Encode positions, rays, camera centers, and availability; alternate temporal and across-track attention; predict unrestricted 3D residuals. Temporal chunks and independently processed track groups control inference cost. The attention factorization follows TracksTo4D; its use for lifted-trajectory repair is the contribution here (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Training without provider inference:** Corrupt clean PointOdyssey trajectories with randomized ray, range, wrong-surface, and availability errors. Training requires neither images nor rendered/provider-generated inputs; one corrector transfers across the evaluated tracker–geometry combinations (§3.4).

## Evidence and main results

WorldTrack APD3D averages accuracy at distance thresholds of 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, and 1.0 metres. Its mean weights the four datasets equally; All and Dyn receive separately fitted per-clip alignments (§4.1, Appendix B.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Initialized lifting is a strong baseline | With CoWTracker and VGGT-Ω, mean All APD3D is **84.9%**, versus **83.9%** for TrackCraft3R and **81.1%** for DELTAv2 using the same geometry; WorldTrack, RANSAC Sim(3). | §4.2, references to Tables 1–2 |
| Correction improves the lift | Same CoWTracker–VGGT-Ω setting rises from **84.9% to 87.5%** mean All APD3D. All evaluated tracker–geometry combinations improve, with largest gains on ground-truth-occluded points. | §4.2, Fig. 3 |
| Gains persist with geometry held fixed | CoWTracker–ReLift using 4RC geometry exceeds 4RC by **2.1 All / 5.4 Dyn percentage points** in mean WorldTrack APD3D after RANSAC Sim(3). | §4.2, Table 1 discussion |
| Results extend beyond Sim(3) | Under scale-only alignment, correction adds **1.4–2.6 All / 2.6–3.8 Dyn points** across all evaluated combinations; 4RC-geometry variants retain best reported means. | Appendix C.3 |
| Simulated training transfers better in tested comparisons | Same architecture and training budget; simulated lifts outperform provider-generated training lifts. A control using matched training windows preserves the advantage. | §4.4, Figs. 5–6 |

These results support the decomposition and correction strategy under the evaluated providers and protocols. They do not establish transfer to arbitrary future providers or downstream benefits. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Performance remains sensitive to input geometry; DA3 and VGGT-Ω configurations trail 4RC on Kubric (§5, Appendix C.1).
- **Explicit modeling boundary:** Training does not simulate camera-pose errors or persistent switches to another object’s trajectory (§3.4).
- **Ablation caveat:** Removing synthetic occluders also removes false-visible observations, so the gap ablation does not isolate missing-observation effects (Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Whole-sequence attention and interpolation using later observations leave causal streaming performance unestablished (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Evaluation scope:** WorldTrack scores dense predictions at sparse reference-visible queries; dense ground truth is evaluated separately on short synthetic Kubric clips. Runtime evidence comes from one clip and one GPU model (§4.1, Appendix D.4).
- Visibility scores are inherited unchanged, so visibility accuracy should not be attributed to the corrector (Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Can repair withstand pose errors and identity switches?** These are omitted from simulation. Add controlled pose drift and persistent track swaps, then measure correction gains under matched inputs (§3.4).
2. **Can ReLift operate causally?** Current processing uses full-sequence evidence. Compare causal attention and past-only initialization against the existing method, especially during long occlusions (§§3.2–3.3).
3. **How well does transfer extend beyond the tested providers?** Evaluated transfer is encouraging but geometry-sensitive. Hold out additional providers and test frozen corrector weights across geometry-quality levels (§4.2, Appendix C.1).
