# SciGame: Let Agents Play the Scientific Discovery Games in the Real and Counterfactual World

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37919) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37919)


## One-sentence takeaway
SciGame benchmarks scientific law inference using paired original and counterfactual simulators, finding that numerical fit and convincing explanations can coexist with poor exact-equation recovery, while adaptive experimentation helps selectively and depends on budget (§§4.2–4.3; Appendix B).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing benchmarks conflate law inference with programming, inadequately test predictions on unseen inputs, and struggle to distinguish evidence-driven inference from pretrained knowledge. SciGame seeks controlled comparisons of candidate-guided versus open-ended inference, familiar versus altered laws, and different observation protocols (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** Scientific cards specify equations, variables, units, domains, and applicability conditions. Each produces Original and dimensionally consistent Counterfactual environments sharing inputs and experimental constraints (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Unified investigation settings:** Identification supplies candidate relations through a shared parameterization; Discovery supplies none. Both support Prior-only, Fixed, and Adaptive observation protocols. Predictors use arithmetic expression trees to reduce programming demands (§3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Updatable collection:** Historical relations provide canonical references; Temporal relations undergo date filtering, LLM extraction, and separate LLM review. The current benchmark contains 20 Historical and 24 Temporal families (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Separate evaluation dimensions:** Hidden-input prediction score, weak and strict episode success rates, and explanation scoring distinguish numerical accuracy, exact-expression recovery, and scientific interpretation (§3.4).

These are benchmark and evaluation contributions; the paper does not introduce a trained discovery model.

## Evidence and main results

All figures below are author-reported. Strict ESR requires numerical tolerance, protocol compliance, and exact-expression matching; weak ESR omits exact-expression matching (§3.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Counterfactual difficulty differs across collections | Fixed Discovery, four observations: mean strict ESR across nine models is 58.6% Historical and 21.3% Temporal for Original laws, versus 0% and 8.9% for Counterfactual laws. | §4.2, referring to Table 2 |
| Adaptive gains depend on model | Temporal Counterfactual Discovery: strict ESR changes from Fixed to Adaptive from 9.7% to 16.7% for GPT-6 Astra, but from 8.3% to 1.4% for GPT-5.6 Terra. | §4.2 |
| Fixed observations outperform Adaptive at a matched small budget | Temporal Counterfactual Discovery, budget eight: GPT-6 Astra achieves 65.3% weak ESR with Fixed versus 30.6% cumulative weak ESR with Adaptive. | §4.3; Figure 5 |
| Candidate-guided identification is substantially easier | Adaptive Temporal Identification: GPT-6 Astra solves over 95% of both Original and Counterfactual episodes within one query under the weak criterion. | §4.3; Figure 5 |
| Explanation quality can conceal prediction failure | GPT-5.6 Terra, Adaptive Temporal Counterfactual Discovery: explanation score 0.815, strict ESR 1.4%, prediction score 0.060. | §4.2 |
| Numerical success can greatly exceed symbolic recovery | Historical Counterfactual Discovery: GPT-6 Astra reaches 95.0% weak ESR but 1.7% strict ESR; the accompanying paragraph does not explicitly identify the protocol. | Appendix B, “Weak and strict ESR results” |

The evidence supports separating predictive accuracy, symbolic recovery, and explanation quality. It also shows that Adaptive performance should be interpreted with its interaction budget: the main Adaptive protocol has no preset budget, whereas Fixed uses four observations. The Historical–Temporal reversal is consistent with the authors’ prior-interference hypothesis, but does not isolate it causally because the collections also differ in relations and mutation schemes.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** The benchmark has limited task and model coverage, mostly physics families, three repetitions per condition, and one agent harness. Simulators lack measurement noise; Temporal equations simplify source findings. Mutations are synthetic and differ between collections. Provider-reported cutoffs cannot eliminate contamination. Card construction and explanation assessment depend on LLMs, including an evaluator that is also a tested Player (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences from the supplied text:**

- Unrestricted Adaptive versus Fixed comparisons confound experiment selection with observation count; the matched-budget analysis only partly resolves this (§3.3; §4.3).
- Exact-expression matching may materially affect rankings, but the supplied text does not explain symbolic-equivalence handling (§3.4; Appendix A.2).
- Reproducibility is difficult to assess: several tables contain captions without their entries, including the main results, model cutoffs, operators, and collection details.
- Reporting alternates between five and nine models; the cost table covers five. The LLM disclosure also describes narrower AI use than the curation and judging methodology documents (§4; Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **Does adaptive selection help at equal resource cost?** Main comparisons allow unequal observation counts. Next experiment: match query and inference-token budgets across Fixed and Adaptive, reporting both ESR standards.
- **How much difficulty comes from familiar priors?** Collections use different mutation schemes. Next experiment: apply matched mutations and complexity controls across Historical and Temporal families.
- **Can numerical fits be converted into exact laws?** Weak–strict ESR gaps remain large. Next experiment: compare existing agents with an explicit symbolic refinement stage and document mathematical-equivalence checks.
- **Do findings survive realistic observations?** Current environments are deterministic. Next experiment: introduce controlled measurement noise and resolution changes while preserving the underlying relations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark design and quoted results are explicit, but complete result tables and implementation details are unavailable.

Material gaps include task equations and source lists, model cutoff dates, hidden-input sampling and tolerances, confidence thresholds, full prompts and operator lists, symbolic-equivalence procedures, per-condition validity counts, uncertainty estimates, and code/data release status.
