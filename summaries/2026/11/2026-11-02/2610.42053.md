# REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42053) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42053)


## One-sentence takeaway
REA routes among fast robot policies without blocking running controllers on model deliberation, improving LIBERO-Pro success and control efficiency, while visual recovery judging adds success at substantial latency cost (§§4.2–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify two obstacles to combining deliberative foundation models with fast embodied policies: incompatible evaluation stacks and difficult asynchronous coordination. Calling the model inside the execution loop creates waiting; triggering recovery only on exceptions misses silent failures, while unconditional retries can undo completed work (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Common runtime:** System 2 generates policies and supplies advice or completion judgments; System 1 routes execution; System 0 runs programs, VLAs, or whole-body controllers through a shared execution contract (§3.1).
- **Routing and recovery:** Development-set route memory ranks policies by measured success and cost. A boundary judge can stop completed tasks or escalate from the current state, with executions and model calls sharing step and time budgets (§3.2).
- **Aligned evaluation and mixed policies:** Matched comparisons isolate routing effects; a separate learned selector dispatches among VLA and code policies from initial observations (§§3.3, 4.6).

The claimed novelty is the runtime and placement of routing and judging—not a new success detector (§2). The strongest LIBERO-Pro configuration, REA-M, uses exception gating and no online model calls; visual judging is evaluated separately.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Exact policy alignment supports a routing effect | On 37 tasks with matching baseline policy files, REA-M achieves 95.3% versus SDK’s 93.7%; p=0.001 | §4.2, Table 1 |

| Real-robot results show higher observed success | G1: REA 8/10 versus SDK 5/10, Fisher p=0.35. Aloha four-method study: REA 10/10 versus ASPIRE 8/10, with longer mean elapsed time, 1,618.0 versus 1,314.9 seconds | Tables 18, 20 |

The clearest evidence supports development-based routing within the tested LIBERO-Pro policy pools. It also supports a success–latency trade-off for visual judging. The RoboTwin VLA study demonstrates initial policy selection and interface compatibility, but does not test mid-episode switching or judge-based recovery (§C.1). Small hardware studies and campaign differences limit broader superiority claims.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-disclosed comparison limits:** Only part of the full LIBERO-Pro pool exactly matches individual baseline programs; the aligned-task analysis addresses this explicitly (§A.2).
- **Restricted benchmark coverage:** LIBERO-Pro excludes the Long suite and object, language, and environment perturbations (§4.1).
- **Recovery attribution remains approximate:** First-run outcomes use separate REA-M executions as proxies, and judge confusion matrices cover final verdicts only (§B.6).
- **Campaign sensitivity:** RoboTwin transfer success changes substantially between nights; historical baselines and a separate-host MEDex run weaken comparisons outside matched campaigns (§§4.7, C.1).
- **Development reuse and baseline defect:** The fixed VLA evaluation scenes appeared in earlier REA development. The historical Show-Harness row also contains an acknowledged orientation-adapter error (§C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Hardware generalization remains uncertain because tasks and trial counts are narrow, scoring is manual, and the Aloha controller comparison changes session settings (§D).
- **Reproducibility gap:** The supplied text does not establish public availability of runtime code, policy libraries, selector checkpoints, or execution logs.

## Open research questions
- **Can route memory generalize beyond previously profiled tasks?** Current lookup uses task-specific development outcomes (§3.2). Test held-out tasks against observation-conditioned selection under equal development budgets.
- **When is visual judging worth its latency?** Gains depend on policy quality and silent failures (§5). Compare exception-only, always-judge, and selectively triggered judging across libraries with matched budgets.
- **Does mixed VLA–code recovery improve over initial selection?** RoboTwin fixes the selected controller for the episode (§C.1). Enable boundary switching and ablate judging, switching, and initial selection separately.
- **Do hardware gains persist under matched collection?** Existing studies are small and partly differ in session settings (§D). Run interleaved trials with fixed prompts, budgets, layouts, and blinded outcome review.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed matched LIBERO-Pro results support the central routing claim; heterogeneous transfer protocols constrain generalization.

Material missing information includes public artifact availability, complete original VLA-selector training details, uncertainty tests for the small mixed-policy gain, and a matched hardware ablation isolating asynchronous routing.
