# Probing Spatial State Representations in Visual Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40636) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40636)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across ten frozen visual representations, spatial readout, history use, predictive updating, and goal-directed utility dissociate, so success on one spatial diagnostic does not establish a persistent, actionable spatial state (Sections 4.1–4.5).

## Problem and motivation
Spatial perception requires tracking previously seen objects, updating their locations under observer motion, and using that information to act. The authors argue that visible-scene geometry or future-feature prediction alone cannot establish these capabilities. They therefore evaluate several functional demands together, without requiring an explicit map or a particular representation architecture (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Functional framework:** Four levels—spatial structure, object persistence, predictive spatial update, and actionability—operationalize different demands on spatial representations (Section 2.1).
- **Comparative probing:** Frozen features from ten models support trained readouts for geometry, instance identity, correspondence, out-of-view localization, future-feature retrieval, and goal-conditioned actions. Layer and prefix sweeps expose extraction-setting dependence (Sections 2–3; Table 1).
- **Controlled diagnostics:** Fixed-probe interventions alter history, motion, or goals; additional experiments test motion composition and spatial readout through native prediction. These distinguish decodability, input use, and functional utility (Sections 4.3–4.5).

Each causal prefix is encoded independently; this evaluates information accessible from a history representation rather than an incrementally maintained online state (Section 2.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Visible-scene readout does not determine persistence | At designated layers, averaged over prefixes, VideoMAE’s out-of-view bearing error is **36.45°**, versus DINOv2’s **65.38°**, despite weaker depth, instance, and correspondence readouts. | Tables 2, 8; Section 4.1 |
| Prediction tasks favor different models | Under the common mixed-horizon protocol, DINOv2 leads direct future-feature retrieval with global R@1 **0.2373**; V-JEPA2 leads path integration with **0.1588**. | Longer prefixes worsen persistence in the endpoint comparison | All eight evaluated models have worse persistence at prefix 24 than prefix 8, with paired scene-bootstrap intervals excluding zero; VideoMAE rises from **29.55° to 54.80°**. Endpoints also change. | Section 4.2; Table 10 |
| Accurate localization need not demonstrate history use | At prefix 12, full history reduces bearing error versus current-frame-only by **10.26° [3.51, 16.80]** for V-JEPA2, but **2.08° [−6.10, 9.97]** for VideoMAE; 165 samples from 65 scenes. | Tables 3, 5; Appendix A.7 |
| Learned prediction can lose to copying | In the separate one-step global retrieval protocol, V-JEPA2 achieves **13.04%** with learned prediction versus **19.93%** by copying the last feature. | Section 4.3 |
| Native prediction changes the spatial readout interface | For V-JEPA2 at horizon two, normalized-depth MAE is **0.1824** on real features, **0.2459** on native predictions with the frozen reader, and **0.2233** after reader retraining; layout prior: **0.2406**. Means over three seeds on 69 validation scenes. | Table 15; Section 4.4 |
| Candidate selection shows goal use | Across four representations, direct two-action selection achieves **75.9–79.9%** accuracy versus **67.8%** for action-only input; shuffling goals reduces accuracy by **12.2–20.7 percentage points**. | Table 4; Section 4.5 |
| Continuous action sensitivity does not establish goal utility | Goal substitution changes predicted directions by **29.8–45.6°**, but correct-minus-shuffled-goal positive-progress effects range from **−2.3 to +1.2 percentage points**, all with 95% intervals containing zero. | Section 4.5; Appendix A.9 |

These results establish task- and interface-dependent accessibility under the tested probes. Retrieval compares predictions within each model’s own feature space, not on a shared physical-error scale. The authors’ proposed pose–persistence–action and visible-scene–prediction “ingredient chains” summarize associations; **analyst inference:** they do not establish causal dependencies or necessary conditions (Sections 3.2, 4.6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fast3R trained on some validation scenes, potentially benefiting its leading pose, persistence, and action results (Section 3.1).
- **Author-stated:** Static indoor observer motion excludes object dynamics, contact physics, and broader physical world modeling; action evaluation is offline, without closed-loop execution (Sections 2.2, 4.5).
- **Author-stated:** Longer prefixes change both history and endpoint. Direct and recurrent prediction also use different readout architectures, preventing isolation of recurrence (Sections 2.1–2.2).
- **Author-stated:** Main-table bootstrap intervals describe evaluation sampling for one training seed. MiniMax-H3 uses a different prediction subset and training horizon; matched evaluations retain some protocol differences (Section 3.2; Appendix A.2–A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Correlations across ten representations—and action comparisons across only four—provide limited support for general architectural or causal conclusions. Architecture and training data are confounded (Sections 4.1, 4.6).
- **Reproducibility gap:** The supplied text gives splits and extraction settings, but does not fully specify every main probe’s architecture and optimization configuration.

## Open research questions
1. **Does additional history help when the endpoint is fixed?** Prefix comparisons confound history with viewpoint. Compare histories of different lengths ending at the same frame, using matched targets and galleries.
2. **Can stronger action readouts recover goal-specific utility?** The continuous shallow probes respond to goals without reliable progress gains. Compare probe capacities and training objectives under fixed features, then test closed-loop execution.
3. **Are the proposed capability chains causal?** Cross-model correlations cannot establish this. Intervene on pose-related feature information and measure persistence and action changes with matched controls.
4. **Does native prediction preserve other spatial properties?** Native readout transfer is demonstrated for V-JEPA2 depth. Extend the frozen-versus-retrained-reader experiment to pose, object identity, persistence, and additional native predictors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported diagnostic results; lower for causal or general capability interpretations.

Material gaps include fully specified main-probe training configurations, verifiable code-release contents, training-seed variability for the main comparison, contamination-free Fast3R results, and evidence from dynamic scenes or closed-loop action.
