# Wizard: Manipulate Any World in Real Time via Agentic Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40339) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40339)


## One-sentence takeaway
Wizard combines explicit game-state control, articulated skeletons, and scene geometry with video generation to demonstrate customizable gameplay on unseen references, but its generalization evidence is qualitative and its reported real-time speed lacks hardware context.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target interactive game world models that support precise character animation, configurable environments, and consistent gameplay rules. They argue that existing systems restrict actions, depend on familiar characters or environments, or emphasize basic movement rather than articulated animation; engine-based alternatives introduce scene and latency constraints (§1).

The proposed solution separates controllable world state from visual rendering so that gameplay logic and motion can remain explicit while a video model supplies appearance.

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated control framework:** Reconstruct scene geometry and rigged characters from reference images, retrieve and retarget compatible library motions, and maintain explicit combat states. Language-directed programs compose predefined geometry operators to modify the scene (§4.1).
- **Unified training representation and dataset:** Construct aligned video–proxy data combining scene geometry and articulated skeletons. Proxy channels encode height, depth, character identity slots, and facing direction (§§3–3.2).
- **Character-aware adaptive masking:** Restrict character-reference attention to skeleton-derived regions that move with each character. During transformations, suppress cached tokens associated with the previous identity (§4.2; Fig. 4).
- **Real-time rendering integration:** Condition Wan2.2-TI2V-5B on proxies and references, then apply the existing Causal Forcing++ adaptation and distillation pipeline. The contribution is its integration with Wizard’s controls, rather than a newly introduced distillation method (§4.2).

These results support demonstrated capabilities within the presented scenarios. They do not establish comparative superiority, generalization success rates, the isolated benefit of masking or data diversity, or end-to-end input responsiveness. Explicit controller rules establish how combat outcomes are computed; they do not quantify whether generated visuals always reflect those outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Compatible motions are difficult to retrieve for uncommon body structures, and alignment between scene proxies and generated video can deteriorate during longer rollouts (§6).

**Analyst assessment:**

- Evaluation consists of qualitative examples, with no reported quantitative control, appearance, gameplay, or generalization metrics, baseline comparisons, or ablations (§5).
- “Zero-shot” means no additional video-model fine-tuning for the shown references; it still requires reconstruction, rigging, and target-specific motion retrieval (§§4–5.1).
- Scene exploration beyond the input view uses terrain variations inferred from observed roughness. Geometric reconstruction accuracy and behavior in unseen regions are not evaluated (§4.1).
- Hardware, end-to-end latency, evaluation sample counts, and code/model availability are unspecified. WildWorld-derived data is explicitly not redistributed (§6 footnote).

## Open research questions
- **How much does adaptive masking improve identity preservation?** Its benefit is asserted without an ablation (§4.2). Compare identical models with and without masking on transformations, occlusions, and multiple characters.
- **How broadly does motion retargeting generalize across morphologies?** Uncommon structures remain a stated weakness (§6). Evaluate held-out body structures with compatibility, ground-contact, and penetration measurements.
- **When does visual consistency degrade?** Long-rollout drift is acknowledged without a measured horizon (§6). Run extended camera-return and scene-edit sequences, measuring proxy adherence and appearance consistency over time.
- **Does reported throughput yield responsive gameplay?** FPS is reported without input-latency measurements (§4.2). Measure input-to-visible-response latency and sustained throughput on specified hardware.
