# eDMD: Endpoint Distribution Matching for Few-Step Diffusion Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17194) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194)


## One-sentence takeaway
eDMD trains its auxiliary fake model exclusively on final student outputs, removing an intermediate-reference bias under exact-denoiser assumptions and improving reported four-step image-generation results through endpoint matching and stronger teacher supervision (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-step distribution matching distillation uses the difference between teacher and auxiliary fake-model predictions to train a fast student. Existing fake models learn mixtures of intermediate predictions or stage-specific states, although only the final image is delivered.

The authors show that this mismatch introduces a reference residual: intermediate distributions can keep driving updates even when final outputs match the teacher, or mask incorrect final outputs. Their goal is to align the correction with delivered samples while retaining useful intermediate-step training (§3, Propositions 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reference analysis:** Decomposes the correction into teacher enhancement, endpoint matching, and reference residual. Under exact conditional denoisers, the residual vanishes exactly when the fake reference equals the endpoint distribution (§3; Appendix A).
- **Endpoint reference:** Trains one shared fake model only on final outputs, then uses it to correct every student step. Rollouts are detached; gradients pass through the selected step rather than the whole sampling chain (§4.1).
- **Improved teacher targets:** A precise teacher (PT) integrates a short teacher trajectory for low-noise final-step queries. A precomputed query density favors intermediate noise levels with more consistent teacher predictions (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Empirical validation:** Evaluates four-step students across four image-generation backbones, including component ablations and endpoint-reference substitutions in TDM (§5; Appendices G–H).

Endpoint-only fake training itself has precedent in ADM, acknowledged by the authors; the contribution combines its theoretical characterization, direct correction of every step, and teacher refinements (§2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified theory:** Exact-denoiser assumptions are essential. Guided and trajectory-integrated targets generally do not represent the denoiser of one clean distribution across noise levels, so the endpoint-equivalence corollary does not automatically apply to the complete practical method (Appendix A.5).
- **Author-qualified local updates:** Intermediate local updates omit the remaining rollout’s Jacobian and need not equal endpoint-divergence gradients. Directional agreement has restricted Gaussian conditions, with countercases acknowledged (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Results concern modified or adapted baselines: DMD2 excludes its GAN loss, TDM uses a local variant on FLUX, and Qwen uses adapted recipes. Conclusions apply to these documented configurations (§5.1; Appendices E–F).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Large-model experiments cover four-step image generation. Checkpoint selection uses evaluation prompts; held-out prompt splits mitigate this concern but reuse the same prompt collection. Some training-prompt overlap is documented (Appendices E, G).
- **Analyst inference—robustness and cost:** Seed evidence is uneven across methods and backbones, PT increases training cost, and query construction requires backbone-specific choices (Appendices C, F–G).

## Open research questions
1. **How much fake-model approximation error can endpoint training tolerate?** The theorem assumes exact denoisers; one LoRA-fake experiment offers limited practical coverage. Next experiment: vary fake capacity and update frequency while measuring score error and generation quality.
2. **Can PT improve quality without shifting distributional equilibrium?** The Gaussian analysis shows integrated targets can change the equilibrium variance. Next experiment: compare PT schedules using both preference scores and explicit teacher–student distribution measurements (Appendix A.5).
3. **Does the benefit persist beyond four-step image generation?** Other step counts and modalities are not experimentally established. Next experiment: hold teacher and compute budgets fixed while varying student steps, then test another modality.
4. **Can query construction be chosen automatically?** Landing-state queries help Qwen but reduce scores on SD3 and FLUX-4B. Next experiment: select query type using a separate validation set and test transfer across teachers (Appendix F).
