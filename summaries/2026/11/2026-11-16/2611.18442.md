# CANTraj: Recovering Vehicle Trajectories from CAN Signals in Naturalistic Driving

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18442) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18442)


## One-sentence takeaway
CANTraj shows that, given a starting location, vehicle speed and steering combined with road maps can recover most five-minute routes in its benchmark, despite removing subsequent GPS observations (Table II).

## Problem and motivation
Removing GPS coordinates from vehicle logs may leave substantial location information in CAN signals. The authors identify a comparison gap: existing trajectory attacks use different signals, location priors, datasets and success metrics, making published results difficult to compare. They address this with paired CAN–GNSS data and common protocols that measure positional accuracy, route recovery, failures and computational cost (§§I–III).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset and benchmark:** CANTraj contains 3,940 drives from 454 drivers; CANTraj-Bench uses 3,675 drives, covering 1,380 hours and 85,919 km. It specifies signal provenance, information access, driver-disjoint splits and failure-aware scoring (§§I, IV).
- **Common evaluation framework:** Six published attacks are reimplemented alongside dead reckoning, online map particle filtering, offline beam search and learned motion baselines. Protocols vary map access, initial heading and online versus offline observations (§§III, V).
- **Controlled analysis:** Fixed-model signal comparisons isolate steering and pedal contributions; additional experiments examine duration, candidate ranking, failures, quantisation and route selection by an LLM harness (§VI).

The principal contribution is the shared dataset and evaluation framework, supported by empirical analysis; trajectory inference from CAN signals itself predates this work (§§I–II).

## Evidence and main results

“Route recovered” means path recall within 50 m is at least 0.8; it does not require accurate positioning at every timestamp. Expected missing outputs count as infinite ADE and zero recovery (§III).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Maps sharply improve steering-based reconstruction | Five-minute test windows, known start/heading: nominal map-free steering dead reckoning has median ADE **921 m**; online steering map filter **43 m**, 95% driver-bootstrap CI **[29, 59]**, with **87%** route recovery. | Table II; §VI-A |
| Steering improves the same online map baseline | Five-minute test windows, known start/heading: speed-only filter achieves **286 m** median ADE and **56%** recovery, versus **43 m** and **87%** with steering. | Table II |
| A controlled signal study supports steering’s contribution | On **407** five-minute windows from **31** evaluation drivers with continuous accelerator data, adding steering lowers median ADE from **339 to 49 m**; every seed’s paired CI excludes zero. Pedal additions give small, inconsistent changes. | §VI-A1 |
| Offline decoding reduces heading dependence | Five-minute test windows: steering beam achieves **37 m / 88%** recovery with heading and **41 m / 81%** without; filter achieves **43 m / 87%** and **138 m / 79%**, respectively. | Table II; §VI-B |
| Longer-window failures remain substantial | Same-start cohort of **205** test drives: at fifteen minutes, steering beam median ADE is **303 m**, versus filter **672 m**. Paired mean difference is **−552 m [−1,421, −98.6]**. | §VI-B; Fig. 5 |
| Mild coarsening preserves substantial leakage | Across five-minute evaluation windows, steering quantisation to **10°** changes recovery by at most **1.3 percentage points** for the tested steering filter/beam; speed quantisation to **1 m/s** changes route rates within **0.7 points**, with all CIs including zero. Attacks remain fixed. | §VI-C1 |
| LLM selection has no clear base advantage over supervised selection | One window per drive: base agent versus selector recovery difference is **0.1 points [−1.6, 3.4]** on test; median reconstructed serial cost is **36.4 s versus 1.1 s**. | §VI-C2; Table IV |

These results establish leakage under the benchmark’s stated priors and population. They do not establish unrestricted unknown-start tracking, universal method rankings or privacy protection from attack failure. Route recovery and time-aligned position accuracy remain distinct.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limitations:** North American driving and vehicles supported by the logging system dominate. Drivers are held out, but platforms and regions are not; rankings change on the small controlled supplement (§VII-b).
- **Prior and comparison differences:** Most methods know the start. Area adaptations receive a square centred on it, frequently too small to contain the route. OPD-II’s Meili map access differs from other methods’ bounded discs (§§III, V, VII-b).
- **Implementation evidence:** Only MacroTracking and OPD-II receive checks against original-author samples. Other attacks lack equivalent validation, and adaptations prevent treating their results as direct reproductions of original evaluations (§§V, VII-b).
- **Limited robustness claim:** Degradation experiments use fixed attacks, without adaptation or downstream utility measurement; they establish no protection threshold (§VI-C1).
- **Analyst inference—reporting ambiguity:** The abstract reports best LLM-harness recovery of 79% test and 83% few-shot, while Table IV’s extended agent reports 78% and 81%. The supplied text does not reconcile these summaries.
- **Analyst inference—reference validity:** Evaluation uses logged GNSS with receiver-specific lag correction rather than a separately validated high-precision reference; residual reference error is not quantified (§IV-b).

## Open research questions
1. **Does recovery generalise to unseen platforms and regions?** Current splits hold out drivers only. Next experiment: evaluate platform-disjoint and region-disjoint splits with unchanged methods (§VII-b).
2. **How effective is genuinely unknown-start recovery?** Existing area variants inherit start-centred placement and often exclude the true route. Next experiment: search independently specified containing regions and report accuracy, failures and cost by region size (§VII-b).
3. **Can candidate selection reliably approach the oracle?** The base LLM shows no clear selector advantage, and steering-beam confidence poorly separates correct routes. Next experiment: train a calibrated selector on validation diagnostics and test on held-out drivers (§VI-C).
4. **Can signal protection reduce leakage while preserving utility?** Fixed-attack coarsening does little, and utility is unmeasured. Next experiment: jointly measure downstream task performance and recovery by attackers retrained on protected signals (§VII-a).
