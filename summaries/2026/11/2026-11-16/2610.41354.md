# OneSteer: Unlocking Steerable General Humanoid Motion Tracking via One-Step Diffusion Whole-Body Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41354) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41354)


## One-sentence takeaway
OneSteer combines general humanoid reference tracking with frozen-weight, cost-guided steering, showing strong low-bar avoidance gains while leaving meaningful gaps between predicted plans and executed motion.

## Problem and motivation
Humanoid trackers follow detailed reference motions, but deployment adds objectives—such as avoiding obstacles or accommodating weak motors—without supplying revised trajectories. The authors identify two gaps: reference editing can produce motions a separate tracker cannot execute, and existing guided generative controllers generally lack full-body reference conditioning that preserves selected tracking targets (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Predictive tracking:** A reference-conditioned one-step generator predicts a short state–action plan. A separate feedback-conditioned action head uses near-term predicted states, proprioception, and visible reference components to issue commands; predicted actions are not executed directly. Training uses either MimicLite teacher distillation or model-based RL from scratch (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Selective test-time steering:** Differentiable task costs optimize generator inputs with weights frozen. Body-group masks relax conflicting reference targets, particularly at the action head. Nominal tracking needs one generator call; default steering uses a second, re-noised draft call with gradient updates, so “one-step” does not mean a single evaluation for steered control (§3.2; Appendix S1).
- **Failure discovery:** Variation across predictions from different noise inputs ranks difficult reference clips without physics rollouts during scoring, then guides targeted fine-tuning. The authors explicitly treat this as an empirical proxy, not a calibrated failure probability (§3.4; Appendix S5).

The claimed novelty is this combination for general humanoid tracking; the authors acknowledge prior input-noise optimization and guided generative control (§2).

## Evidence and main results

|---|---|---|
| Strong local tracking accuracy | Scratch-trained model reports lowest root-relative error among evaluated baselines: **26.5 mm AMASS / 22.6 mm BONES**, measured on each method’s successful clips from **150 / 512** held-out windows. Shared-successful-clip analysis retains the ranking. | §4.1; Table 2 discussion; Appendix S3, “Tracking on shared clips” |

| Hurdle gains mostly allow contact | In the same simulation benchmark with **10 cm hurdles**, feasible passage increases **0.40→0.68**, but clean passage is only **0.00→0.11**. Feasible passage permits foot contact. | Table 3; Appendix S2 |

| Physical deployment succeeds in small trials | Unitree G1 completes **27/27** nominal trials without falling across nine motions. Steering passes a **1.1 m bar in 4/5 attempts** and traverses a **12 cm hurdle without falling in 3/3**, with only **1/3** hurdle crossings clean; unsteered attempts fail **3/3** for each obstacle. | §4.1; Appendix S3, “Steering on the robot” |
| Latency depends on execution setup | RTX 4090 nominal inference is **3.8 ms per generator call**; second-call steering takes **10.6–13.7 ms per control step**. Compiled Orin NX steering is timed offline at **14.4 ms**, but paced-loop steering was not timed and hardware steering used a tethered RTX 4090. | §4.1; Table 3; Appendix S3, “Latency” |
| Prediction spread identifies concentrated failures | Scratch model, **7,884 clips**: whole-clip scoring achieves **0.90 top-2% precision**, **0.84 AUC**. Whole-clip scoring is **11–28× cheaper** than five simulated rollouts per clip in the authors’ stack, depending on startup accounting. | §4.4; Appendix S5 |
| Targeted fine-tuning improves repair | Selecting **329 clips** by spread fixes **48.3±4.2%** of base failures on a **7,788-clip** rerun, versus **35.1±5.9%** for random selection from the same candidate pool; three training seeds. Regressions remain **1.8±0.2%**. | Appendix S5, “Fine-tuning”; Table S27 discussion |

These results support effective steering in the tested settings, especially under lower bars. They do not establish universal task adaptation, guaranteed collision avoidance, or onboard real-time steering in the deployed paced loop. Tracking, steering, and failure discovery also emphasize different training variants; the best results should not be read as measurements of one identical checkpoint.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Plan–execution mismatch, acknowledged:** Guided plans can be dynamically inconsistent, and the action head realizes only part of their edits. Clearance predictions become optimistic farther ahead; torque reductions are also partly lost during execution (Appendices S3–S4).
- **Limited hardware scope, analyst inference:** Hardware steering uses a walking reference, few attempts, a fixed pre-run LiDAR map, and tethered computation. Unobserved map voxels incur no cost. This leaves dynamic environments and broader deployment reliability unestablished (Appendix S3).
- **Qualified comparisons:** Steering settings were selected on the main walking windows, although separate walking and dance tests provide transfer evidence. Hurdle improvement over the closed-loop editor is not statistically resolved by the window-level interval; lower-bar gains are clearer (Appendices S3–S4).
- **Conditional tracking metrics:** Errors exclude failed clips; shared-clip analysis partly addresses comparability. Some baselines’ training data include the named evaluation datasets, and missing scene geometry causes common BONES failures (Table 2 caption; Appendices S2–S3).
- **Failure score is incomplete:** Low spread can hide biased predictions. Its theoretical motivation uses a Gaussian approximation, and its relationship to control failure is measured rather than derived (Appendix S5).
- **Scope beyond kinematics:** Sustained force interaction is not represented in the current plan; terrain-conditioned evaluation remains simulation-only (§5; Appendix S3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can steering become more dynamically faithful within the control budget?** The head only partially executes guided plans, while world-model projection helps but exceeds the real-time budget. Test task-agnostic training on steered plans against projection at matched latency (Appendix S4).
2. **Does fully onboard steering remain reliable with changing obstacles?** Hardware uses a fixed map and tethered computation. Run paced onboard trials with live map updates, moving obstacles, and measured end-to-end latency (Appendix S3).
3. **Can failure discovery detect confident but wrong plans?** Spread misses bias, while rarity supplies complementary information. Evaluate a combined score on held-out skill families at a fixed selection and fine-tuning budget (Appendix S5).
4. **Can the controller handle sustained contact objectives?** Current plans lack contact-force representations. Add force or wrench predictions and test pushing or carrying against pose-only steering (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and scoped findings; **medium** for broader deployment conclusions.
