# JVT: Just Video Transformer makes pixel-space video diffusion simple

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18549) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18549)


## One-sentence takeaway
JVT demonstrates competitive short-video generation with a simple pixel-space Transformer and improves camera following through joint RGB-disparity generation, although guidance benefits depend on the dataset and broader superiority is not established.

## Problem and motivation
Latent video diffusion relies on a separately trained autoencoder, which the authors identify as a source of reconstruction error, added costs, and difficulty jointly modeling aligned modalities. Existing pixel-space video methods often introduce specialized architectures or cascades. The paper asks whether a simple DiT-style Transformer can generate raw video effectively while providing a direct interface for RGB and geometry (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Simple pixel-space video model:** JVT predicts clean video from noisy pixels using rectified-flow velocity supervision, without a pretrained autoencoder, detailers, cascades, or U-Net-style skip connections. The authors claim novelty for this architectural combination; the clean-data prediction formulation follows JiT (§§1, 3.1).
- **Empirical design recipe:** Ablations favor spatial-first, temporally factorized patch embedding with a channel bottleneck and standard adaptive layer normalization. Register guidance improves some settings despite keeping registers active throughout training; register-based conditioning itself provides no clear benefit (§§3.2–3.3, 4.2).
- **Joint RGB and geometry generation:** Channel concatenation lets JVT jointly denoise aligned RGB and disparity. Given an initial frame and camera trajectory, generated depth can be unprojected into colored point clouds without an additional reconstruction model at inference (§§3.4, 4.3). Other aligned modalities are proposed, not experimentally demonstrated.

The evidence supports the feasibility of this simple pixel-space architecture and a measured camera-following benefit from joint geometry prediction. It does not establish universal superiority or better end-to-end efficiency than latent diffusion: comparison results use different training strategies, and efficiency measurements primarily compare JVT variants. Point-cloud construction is demonstrated, but geometric accuracy is not quantitatively established.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments cover short clips at a single spatial resolution; larger models, longer videos, higher resolutions, and more datasets remain future work (§5).
- **Author-stated guidance limitation:** Register guidance is dataset-dependent. The proposed explanation involving semantic diversity is a hypothesis, not a controlled causal result (§4.2).
- **Author-stated camera limitation:** Translation following remains weaker than rotation following (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—geometry evaluation dependence:** Pi3X supplies training depth targets and estimates poses for evaluation. An independent geometry evaluator would strengthen validity; the paper does not establish such a check (§4.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Historical baseline scores, differing training recipes, some differing resolutions, and FVD sample-count sensitivity limit conclusions from rankings. UCF-101 training uses both training and test splits, so these results do not establish held-out generalization (§4.1; Tables 1, 13–15).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 1 includes guided results for datasets where Appendix A.1 says registers are used only on UCF-101. Reported quality results lack uncertainty estimates, and code/checkpoint availability is not established.

## Open research questions
1. **Does JVT remain competitive as duration and resolution increase?** Current experiments leave scaling untested. Train matched pixel-space and latent baselines across clip lengths and resolutions, measuring quality, training cost, and full sampling latency.
2. **Does joint disparity improve independently measured geometry?** Current camera evaluation depends on Pi3X and point-cloud examples are qualitative. Evaluate with an independent pose estimator and scenes with reference depth.
3. **What determines when register guidance helps?** Semantic diversity is only a proposed explanation. Compare controlled dataset subsets with varying diversity under matched training and sampling budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported benchmark results, but material gaps include quality-result uncertainty, independent geometric validation, matched end-to-end latent-versus-pixel efficiency, scaling experiments, confirmed code/checkpoint releases, and clarification of register usage across datasets.
