# V-JEPA-LM: Video-Language Models That Read Dynamics Beyond Static, Text-Aligned Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17831) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17831)


## One-sentence takeaway
Answer-balanced video transformations teach a language model to use dynamics already present in frozen V-JEPA features, improving temporal understanding and benchmark averages under the paper’s controlled training and evaluation settings.

## Problem and motivation
Video encoders can retain motion information that their attached language models fail to use. The authors argue that ordinary captions and question–answer supervision often reward recognizing scene attributes without tracking change.

Their central diagnostic separates missing information from unused information: on identical speed questions, dynamics remains readable from the frozen encoder and bridge while the caption/QA-trained model answers near chance (Sec. 3.2, Table 3). The proposed gap is therefore supervision that requires the answer to depend on video dynamics.

## Approach and contributions
- **Encoder analysis:** Decompose original/transformed features into common and change components. Measure whether change directions generalize across clips, finding stronger shared structure in V-JEPA than in the tested text-aligned encoders. The theoretical account is conditional on symmetry, identifiability and readout assumptions (Sec. 3; Appendix A).
- **Counterfactual supervision:** Generate fourteen constructions across six families, including reversal, direction changes, event order, speed, counting and completion. Transformations determine answers, while balancing reduces scene–answer shortcuts. Some constructions reuse existing action labels or timestamps; they require no new manual annotation (Sec. 4, Tables 1 and 10).
- **Compact video-language models:** Connect frozen V-JEPA 2.1 to Qwen3 or Llama through a spectral bridge producing 1,024 tokens per 32-frame clip. Train the bridge and language model in one continuous stage, including a brief bridge-only warm-up (Sec. 4; Appendix C).
- **Controlled mechanism study:** Cross encoder choice with supervision, compare matched-weight temporal controls, trace speed information through model layers, and remove measured feature subspaces at inference (Secs. 5–6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Speed information can be present but unused | On 540 held-out, three-class speed-ratio items, the 2B caption/QA V-JEPA model has encoder-probe accuracy **86.7%**, bridge-probe **83.5%**, but answer accuracy **33.7%** versus **33.3%** chance. With counterfactual supervision, answers reach **92.8%** at the same 8k-step checkpoint. | Sec. 3.2, Table 3 |
| Counterfactual supervision improves the controlled model | With matched mixture and schedule, final 2B V-JEPA twelve-score average increases from **61.53 to 66.02**; SigLIP increases from **58.52 to 62.13**. Arrow-of-time-only V-JEPA reaches **61.17**. | Table 6 |
| Natural speed questions benefit substantially | Final 2B V-JEPA gains **38.1 percentage points** across **527** speed items from natural benchmarks; individual gains are **62.9** on TOMATO and **21.8** on TempCompass. | Appendix E, Table 26 |
| The 4B model leads the tested aggregate comparison | Under common 32-frame prompts and option-letter scoring, Ours-4B-Q averages **72.14** across twelve scores from eleven benchmarks, versus **61.08** for Molmo2-4B and **64.79** for PLM-8B. Reported bootstrap intervals for model averages are approximately **±0.7 points**. | Table 4; Appendix C |
| Held-out transformation performance transfers within the tested scope | Ours-4B-Q averages **96.3%** across six held-out sets, versus **50.0%** for Qwen3-VL-4B. On successive playback, it scores **96.7%** single-item accuracy versus **36.9%**. Held-out questions retain generator wordings and option pools. | Sec. 5.2, Tables 17–20 |
| Motion is necessary for most held-out-set performance | Repeating the middle frame reduces six-set averages from **95.0% to 37.9%** for 2B and **96.3% to 37.6%** for 4B; stated average chance is **33.3%**. | Appendix C, single-frame control |
| Measured speed directions matter causally to predictions | Removing eight speed directions from final Ours-2B-Q reduces unseen speed-combination accuracy from **98.9% to 65.7%**; an approximately energy-matched random removal leaves **97.2%**. TOMATO changes from **50.9% to 46.4%**, versus **49.9%** under random removal. | Table 29 |
| Training is compact under the authors’ accounting | The 4B model uses **4.53M** sampled rows, **1,024** visual tokens per clip and **499 H100-hours**. Backbone pretraining is excluded; ViT-L models actually use **4.60M** sampled rows. | Tables 11–12; Appendix C |

The controlled comparisons support the claim that supervision changes whether the model uses available dynamics. Probes, motion removal and subspace interventions provide complementary evidence.

The aggregate ranking establishes performance under this particular benchmark suite and protocol. It does not establish universal superiority, equal total pretraining cost, or that every improvement comes from counterfactual supervision: the bridge, mixture and encoder also contribute. Comparisons with Meta’s unreleased VLMs use quoted results and differing evaluation splits, making them less controlled (Table 5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation concerns short clips sampled into 32 frames. Hour-long video, OCR, documents, charts and general image QA are outside the target; knowledge-heavy video benchmarks remain weaker than some larger-data competitors (Appendix I).
- **Conditional theory:** Shared-subspace results assume identifiability and approximate transformation symmetry. Content cancellation requires separability; speed consistency is measured rather than derived. The theory does not fully cover nonlinear, position-dependent readouts or multiclass answers (Appendix A).
- **Label assumptions:** Speed-change labels assume roughly steady source motion; audited accelerating/decelerating rows agree with the motion proxy in 95–98% of cases. Absolute playback-rate labels assume naturally recorded source speed (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—limited distribution shift:** Held-out sets vary selected factors but mostly retain generator language and options. Successive playback broadens construction transfer, but unrestricted temporal reasoning remains untested (Sec. 5.2; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and comparison scope:** Each released model is trained once. Item-bootstrap intervals quantify evaluation uncertainty, not training-seed variability. The plain average also combines different metrics and weights each score equally (Appendices C and I).
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** The text reports weights and inference code released, says training code/generator/manifests “will follow,” and elsewhere refers to released manifests. Availability cannot be resolved from the supplied text alone.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the gain survive independent question formats?** Held-out tests largely reuse generator language. Next experiment: independently authored natural-video questions with new wording, options and continuous speed judgments.
2. **Which construction families drive transfer?** Reported ability gains mostly compare the whole counterfactual mixture against controls. Next experiment: matched-budget leave-one-family-out and single-family training studies.
3. **How general is the encoder–supervision interaction?** Full language-model training compares V-JEPA and SigLIP; ViCLIP and VideoMAE receive diagnostic measurements only. Next experiment: extend the controlled training grid to those encoders.
4. **Can the method scale to long video?** Long-context memory and retrieval are explicitly deferred. Next experiment: combine clip representations with memory and evaluate questions requiring evidence across distant clips.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported controlled findings; **medium** in broader generalization and efficiency claims.

Material information unavailable or unresolved includes training-seed variability, isolated bridge and construction-family contributions, independently authored held-out question formats, long-video performance, full backbone pretraining costs, and definitive release status of training code, generator and manifests.
