# Re-Search: Dissecting LLM Ideation Through a Two-Player Questioning Game

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14487) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14487)


## One-sentence takeaway
Re-Search finds that supplying candidate questions or ideas improves LLM recovery of hidden research methods, supporting a candidate-generation bottleneck within its controlled questioning game (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Existing evaluations either score standalone research proposals or combine ideation with coding and experimentation, obscuring how ideas develop and why search stalls. The authors seek an observable, iterative evaluation that separates finding an idea from executing it (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled ideation benchmark:** Re-Search uses problem–solution pairs extracted from 200 ICML 2026 method papers. An inquisitor sees only the problem and asks up to 100 questions; a stateless answerer consults the paper and returns **Yes, No, or Unknown**. Three LLM judges score idea cards before questioning and every ten questions on a 1–10 recovery scale (§3).
- **Interventions separating generation from selection:** Models select questions from a shared proposal pool, or receive surveys with the target idea, a decoy, or other target components removed. These experiments test whether candidate availability explains performance differences (§5).
- **Analysis of search dynamics:** Prefix continuations assess early trajectory persistence, while LLM-annotated question trees expose contrasting exploration patterns (§§4.2, 6).

## Evidence and main results

Recovery scores below are means of three LLM ratings on a 1–10 scale (§3.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Progress is concentrated early | Across six models, 200 papers, and three games per paper, questions 1–10 add **1.3–3.2 score points**; questions 11–30 add **0.2–0.8**. Three models subsequently stall, two improve slowly, and GPT-5.4 continues improving. | §4.1; Fig. 3 |
| Early scores predict final recovery | Reported correlation between scores at question 10 and question 100 is **r=0.76–0.87**, rising to **r=0.87–0.92** at question 30, alongside the prefix-fork experiment. | §4.2; Table 1 caption and surrounding text |
| Prior candidate recall does not eliminate the model gap | GPT-5.4 exceeds GPT-OSS-120B by **2.54 points** on a 100-paper subset and by **2.28 points** on the 24 papers where both list the core idea in a closed-book probe. | §5.1 |
| Shared candidates substantially improve weaker selectors | After 30 questions on 200 papers, four selectors using the same four-model question pool score **5.19–5.34**, with no significant difference reported; weaker models gain over one point versus solo play. | §5.2; Fig. 5; Table 2 caption |
| Candidate diversity and selection both matter | For GPT-OSS-120B at 30 questions, solo recovery is **4.16**, self-generated best-of-four **4.57**, four-model proposals with selection **5.20**, and random selection from that pool **4.43**. Random selection is reported as not significantly better than solo play. | §5.2 |
| Naming the target idea helps weaker models most | On 200 papers with three games per paper, target-containing surveys improve question-100 recovery by **up to 1.25 points** versus matched decoy surveys. Gain correlates with decoy-condition performance at **Pearson r=−0.74** across six inquisitors. | §5.3; Figs. 6–7 |
| Moderate breadth is associated with better recovery | Across **1,200 games**, a quadratic model with model and paper fixed effects estimates peak recovery at **8.3 top-level branches**. | §6.2; Fig. 9 |
| Evaluation has substantial, imperfect human agreement | Judges agree with human majority preferences or ties on **91.2% of 498 card pairs**. The selected answerer agrees with human labels on **94.0% of 795 annotated answers**. | §3.3; §4 model selection |

The interventions support the authors’ diagnosis that access to useful candidates explains an important part of the performance gap. They also show that selection remains necessary: random choice loses much of the shared-pool benefit. They do **not** establish that generation is the universal bottleneck in scientific discovery, that prior knowledge is irrelevant, or that enforcing a particular search breadth causes improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only machine learning method papers from one conference are tested. Other disciplines and the newest models remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated measurement risks:** LLM judges and LLM tree annotations can err, especially for ambiguous solutions. Same-family judge exclusion barely changes the survey gain, but does not fully rule out bias (Limitations; Appendix B).
- **Extraction quality:** Human checking covers only half the paper set; some extracted targets omit distinguishing method components (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—target recovery versus discovery:** Matching one published solution does not measure whether a model can invent a different valid solution or execute successful research (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—privileged candidate information:** Target-containing surveys are constructed with solution access. Appendix E explicitly recognizes that they supply additional information; their benefit does not establish that practical retrieval can obtain equally useful candidates.
- **Analyst inference—observational tree results:** Search breadth is associated with recovery, rather than experimentally manipulated. The supplied text reports no human validation of tree parent assignments (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Can practical candidate sourcing reproduce the gains?** Surveys use hidden-solution information. A next experiment could build surveys through retrieval using only the visible problem and compare them with the controlled survey conditions (§5.3; Appendix C).
- **Does controlling exploration improve recovery?** The breadth result is correlational. Randomly assign branch-expansion or restart policies and compare recovery under matched question budgets (§6.2).
- **Do findings transfer to open-ended research?** The task rewards rediscovery without execution. Test whether candidate augmentation improves independently assessed novel proposals and subsequent experimental outcomes (§3.1; Limitations).
- **How sensitive are trajectories to answerer errors?** The selected answerer is imperfect, but no end-to-end answerer-sensitivity experiment is reported. Replay matched games with human-verified feedback and alternative answerers (Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the principal findings and several controlled comparisons, but limits independent assessment.

Material missing information includes numerical bodies of the referenced tables, many confidence intervals and significance-test details, actual prompt templates beyond Appendix H’s field descriptions, inference settings and costs, tree-annotation validation, selection of the single game per model–paper pair used for tree analysis, and code/data release availability.
