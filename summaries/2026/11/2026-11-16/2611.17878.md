# DySENSE: Dynamic Spatiotemporal ENergy Synthesis and Emulation with Satellite Imagery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17878) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17878)


## One-sentence takeaway
DySENSE jointly generates satellite imagery and aligned monthly energy maps, improving three downstream energy predictors on MUSE2’s real test set under limited-data training, while leaving unseen-city transfer and counterfactual validity unestablished (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify three gaps in urban building energy modelling: methods mostly predict consumption for existing cities, aligned monthly energy labels are scarce, and image generators generally lack corresponding energy dynamics. They propose generating urban imagery alongside monthly energy maps to support data augmentation and future planning scenarios (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint imagery–energy generation:** Separate RGB and Energy VAEs encode the modalities; a U-ViT diffusion network jointly denoises their latents, conditioned on land-use and monthly climate text (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Fixed-form monthly generation:** Holding the imagery latent fixed while changing month and climate prompts generates energy maps for the same urban morphology without retraining (§3.4).
- **MUSE2 dataset:** The authors assemble and report releasing 23,880 monthly pairs from NYC, Washington, D.C., Norfolk, and Busan, aligning imagery, energy records, land use, and climate (§2.3).
- **Augmentation evaluation:** Static and dynamic synthetic pairs augment three existing predictors; duplication and Gaussian-noise augmentation provide additional controls (Table 1).

The claimed methodological advance is the combination of joint spatial generation and monthly conditioning, building on existing diffusion architectures (§§1, 3).

These results support synthetic-data usefulness under limited-data conditions and similarity of aggregate seasonal patterns. They do not establish physically correct responses to hypothetical climate or urban-design changes. The reported mIoU gain of 0.0868 is **8.68 percentage points**; the CVRMSE change from 33.58% to 15.31% is **18.27 percentage points**, clarifying the paper’s percentage wording.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Geographic coverage is limited; broader climate-zone coverage and external utility-audit or smart-meter validation remain future work (§5.2). Busan winter peaks are overestimated, while NYC and Washington summer peaks are slightly underestimated (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Tile holdout tests spatial generalization within the dataset, but does not establish unseen-city or future-year transfer (§4.3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Energy is aggregated into 100 m cells and discretized into classes, then recovered using city-specific representative values. Continuous-energy metrics therefore reflect quantized reconstruction rather than unrestricted continuous estimation (§2.2.2; Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Distribution similarity and monthly-average correlations can conceal local errors; downstream augmentation is indirect evidence of imagery–energy alignment (§§4.3–4.4).
- No repeated-run uncertainty or ablations isolating the Energy VAE, joint denoising, or individual climate variables are reported. RemoteCLIP is not uniformly better on downstream metrics despite the authors’ broad wording (Table 5).
- Synthetic sample counts, mixing ratios, and several evaluation details are unavailable, limiting reproducibility.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the method transfer to unseen cities and years?** Current evaluation uses tile holdout. A next experiment would hold out an entire city and a later year, reporting city-level energy errors.
2. **Are climate-conditioned changes physically reliable?** Seasonal agreement does not validate counterfactual responses. Compare controlled climate-prompt perturbations against independently validated simulations or meter observations.
3. **Which components drive augmentation gains?** Existing ablations focus on text encoders. Compare joint versus separate generation, generic versus Energy VAE, and month-only versus climate-conditioned prompts under matched compute.
4. **When does synthetic augmentation become harmful?** Full-data results show degradation. Sweep synthetic-to-real ratios at each real-data budget using validation-selected mixing.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods and results, but lacks synthetic corpus sizes, mixing schedules, repeated-run uncertainty, exact distribution-metric aggregation, complete predictor adaptations, and the procedure for selecting class representatives. Unseen-city, future-year, and external physical validation are not reported. The abstract’s “less than 25%” headline also differs from the detailed 25% setting for the main Yap result.
