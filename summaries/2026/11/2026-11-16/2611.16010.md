# Iris-Z: Scaling One Generative Model Past Specialist Geometry Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16010) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16010)


## One-sentence takeaway
Iris-Z jointly generates images and relative depth with one transformer, achieving lower mean aligned depth error than MoGe-3 on two evaluated benchmark suites, at substantially higher inference cost and with some degradation in image-generation scores.

## Problem and motivation
Depth estimators typically specialize in geometry, while generators adapted for depth can lose image-generation capabilities. The authors investigate whether a single generative backbone can retain image synthesis while matching specialist depth accuracy. They identify specialist-level benchmark performance as the missing evidence for joint appearance–geometry generators (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared image–depth generation:** RGB and depth use the same frozen autoencoder and enter one diffusion-transformer token sequence. Shared spatial positions associate corresponding patches; a learned modality embedding distinguishes supplied and generated content. There is no separate depth head (§3).
- **One model, four inference tasks:** Training mixes joint RGBD generation, image-conditioned depth, depth-conditioned image generation, independently noised modalities, and image-only generation. Flow matching is supplemented by a decoded-depth loss; sparse sources supervise depth only at measured pixels (§§3–4.2).
- **Two model sizes and benchmark evidence:** The authors report releasing full transformer fine-tunes of FLUX.2 backbones as Iris-Z-small and Iris-Z-big. Their experiments compare depth accuracy, inference cost, generation retention, and a three-run scaling progression (§§1, 4, 6).

Depth is represented as per-image normalized log ray distance, so outputs lack absolute metric scale and camera parameters (§§3, 7).

## Evidence and main results

All depth errors below are **AbsRel × 100**, with lower values better; δ1 is the percentage of valid pixels within a factor of 1.25 after alignment, with higher values better.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best mean error on Marigold’s suite | Across NYUv2, ETH3D, ScanNet, KITTI, and DIODE, using RANSAC alignment in log depth: big **2.92**, small **3.11**, MoGe-3 **3.12**, Marigold V2 **4.10**. Iris-Z uses 40 transformer passes. | §§4.4–5; Table 4 |
| Best mean error on the evaluated MoGe-3 subset | Across seven of nine sets with relative-error L1 alignment: big **3.56**, small **3.84**, MoGe-3 **3.70**, Marigold V2 **5.40**. Big wins six sets; on DIODE it scores **3.18** versus MoGe-3’s **2.65**. | §6; Table 3 |
| Superiority depends on the metric | Mean δ1 on Marigold’s suite: big **98.78%**, MoGe-3 **98.46%**. On the seven-set MoGe-3 suite: big **97.03%**, MoGe-3 **97.15%**. | Appendix B, Tables 5–6 |
| Scaling progression lowers error | On Marigold’s suite at 40 passes: LoRA trained on **0.2M** frames scores **3.83**; full fine-tuned small on **3.8M** scores **3.11**; big on **3.6M** scores **2.92**. | Figure 2b; §6 |
| Accuracy carries substantial latency | At approximately **512²** pixels on one H200: big takes approximately **15 s**, small **4.8 s**, and MoGe-3 **0.41 s** per prediction. | §6; Table 2 |
| Image generation persists with measurable drift | Same held-out prompts and seeds, 50 guided steps: big versus its base has CLIP **27.42 vs 27.85**, HPSv2.1 **24.30 vs 26.71**, and photograph-classification probability **0.48 vs 0.38**. | §4.5; Table 1 |

These results support improved **mean aligned relative-depth error** on the evaluated suites while retaining image-generation functionality. They do not establish dominance across metrics, metric-depth recovery, or all MoGe-3 benchmarks. The scaling progression is consistent with benefits from scale, but does not isolate their causes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Outputs lack metric distances and camera estimates; inference is expensive; training resolution is limited; generation drifts toward the training distribution; consistency across frames or views is unmeasured (§7).
- **Evaluation scope:** Spring and Synth4K are omitted from MoGe-3’s suite because of resolution. ETH3D remains despite its high resolution, so the boundary is an evaluation choice rather than a uniform exclusion rule (§5).
- **Analyst inference—scaling attribution:** The progression changes training-set size, adaptation strategy, and backbone. Small and big also differ in training settings and depth-range handling (§§4.1–4.3). It therefore cannot cleanly establish that scale alone causes the gains.
- **Analyst inference—generalist validation:** Quantitative evaluation concentrates on depth estimation and prompt-to-image scores. Joint RGBD consistency and depth-conditioned image fidelity receive examples but no dedicated quantitative assessment (Table 1; Appendix A).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied report omits named training datasets, their mixture proportions, numerical learning rates, batch sizes, total training steps, and task-sampling probabilities (§4). It also provides no uncertainty estimates for benchmark gains.

## Open research questions
1. **Which scaling factor drives the improvement?** The three runs confound data volume, adaptation strategy, and backbone. A matched experiment varying each factor separately could test the authors’ attribution (Figure 2b).
2. **Can the accuracy survive lower inference cost?** Reported results rely on multi-seed sampling, and distillation is proposed rather than demonstrated. Evaluate distilled models under matched latency budgets against MoGe-3 (§§4.4, 6–7).
3. **How geometrically faithful are the other generation tasks?** Examples demonstrate execution but not measured consistency. Evaluate generated RGBD pairs and depth-conditioned images with explicit geometry-consistency tests (Appendix A).
4. **Does the method extend to metric or temporally consistent geometry?** Neither camera recovery nor cross-frame consistency is established. Test camera-token training and held-out multiview or video sequences (§7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark comparisons and stated method; **medium** in the causal scaling and broad generalist conclusions.

Material missing information includes detailed training recipes and source composition, evaluation uncertainty, the size and composition of the generation prompt set, quantitative validation of joint and depth-conditioned generation, and independent confirmation of the announced code/model release.
