# Mobile AI Factory: Building Specialized Language Models for Your Mobile Device

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13509) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13509)


## One-sentence takeaway
Mobile AI Factory combines agent-guided fine-tuning with measured phone constraints to build specialized small language models, reporting five task wins over two five-shot frontier baselines on unseen tests while leaving sustained deployment performance unverified (§5; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
Running language models locally can support private, offline mobile applications, but developers must jointly choose training data, hyperparameters, model size, quantization, inference engine, and backend. The authors identify a gap between automated fine-tuning that prioritizes accuracy and mobile deployment that must also satisfy device-specific resource budgets: hardware specifications alone do not reliably predict feasible configurations (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Agent-guided training with deterministic safeguards.** An orchestrator chooses hyperparameter changes, real-data mining, or targeted synthetic examples. Fixed rules roll back regressions, enforce budgets, and escalate stalled searches; accuracy goals start from teacher performance and can increase (§4).
- **Joint model and precision search.** Candidates are grouped by file size and searched from small to large. Quantized candidates are scored in their deployment engine, rather than assuming full-precision accuracy survives export (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Measured device feasibility.** Reusable phone profiles filter candidates by throughput, memory, and energy, and select their runtime/backend. The authors evaluate this integrated task-to-phone workflow across ten tasks and four Android phones (§§3, 5).

The claimed novelty is the integration of task specialization, size-aware search, and measured phone constraints; LoRA, quantization, and synthetic data are supporting techniques rather than individually new methods (§§2–3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Specialized models retain several frontier-baseline wins on unseen data | Authors report five wins over both five-shot Claude Sonnet 5 and DeepSeek v4-Flash, with task-score margins of 0.024–0.342; three ties use a 0.015 threshold. Spam lacks an unseen test. | Appendix A, Table 5 |
| Biomedical extraction improves substantially | On unseen BC5CDR, the factory scores **0.828 span F1**, versus **0.652** for five-shot Claude and **0.560** for five-shot DeepSeek. The selected model is a **517 MB** Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4 artifact. | Tables 3, 5 |
| Iterative search adds gains beyond the first fine-tune | Across **42 task-tier pairs**, **34** improve by more than **0.015**; median gain is **0.051** in each task’s score. Median gains are **0.082** in the smallest tier and **0.023** in the largest. | §5.2.1; Fig. 5 |
| Tiered search trades some accuracy for substantially less training | On BC5CDR, tiered search reaches **0.830 span F1 in 8.6 training GPU-hours**; exhaustive search reaches **0.859** and consumes **165 hours** across all **27 variants**, approximately **19×** more time. | §5.2.3; Fig. 7a |
| Phone constraints can preserve most of the unconstrained score | For Galaxy S23 call summarization, the constrained factory returns Qwen3-0.6B Q8 at **0.469 ROUGE-L**, versus **0.482** for the unconstrained winner: a **0.013** difference. | §5.3; Fig. 9b |
| Specification-based selection often misses measured budgets | Across **20 requirements per difficulty group**, Claude Opus 5.5 with specifications fails **25%** of easy, **25%** of moderate, and **60%** of hard cases. Measured selection succeeds by construction. | §5.3; Fig. 9d |
| Building the models remains computationally substantial | The ten main runs reserve **663 total GPU-hours**, counting training and teacher GPUs; orchestrator API expenditure totals **$57.37**. | §5.2.5 |

The evidence supports effective specialization on the evaluated tasks and a strong role for measured device feasibility. It does not establish universal superiority over frontier models: function calling remains a loss, default-goal intent classification stops below the baselines, and several unseen comparisons are ties. The search-efficiency result comes from one task. Resource compliance is checked against the same profiles used for selection, rather than independently demonstrating reliability under everyday phone workloads (§5; Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Developers still supply data sources, metrics, and verifiers; constructing this harness remains the most manual component. The candidate pool contains only decoder models, and training uses LoRA only (§6).
- **Authors’ stated deployment limits:** Profiles cover CPUs and GPUs under cool, idle conditions at one prompt length and thread setting. Thermal changes, concurrent applications, sustained use, and longer prompts could change feasibility (§6).
- **Evaluation differs from the default synthesis policy:** Main experiments enable synthesis on seven tasks that fail the system’s teacher-quality gate. Reported gains therefore do not directly characterize an unchanged default configuration (§§4.3.2, 5.1).
- **Selection and statistical uncertainty:** Each configuration has a single run. Repeated held-out evaluation guides search, and the authors acknowledge optimistic scores; unseen testing reduces this concern but is unavailable for spam (§§4.2.3, 5.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution is incomplete:** Intervention gains credit accepted steps rather than controlled removals. Their ordering and carried-over data confound attribution; the paper explicitly cautions against causal interpretation (§5.2.2). Separate controlled rollback and adaptive-goal ablations are not reported.
- **Analyst inference—profiling/export mismatch needs validation:** Four-bit cost profiles use llama.cpp Q4_0 and default MNN exports, whereas accuracy scoring uses Q4_K_M and HQQ exports. This weakens the alignment between measured resource cost and scored artifact; accuracy transfer is checked on only one phone (§§3, 5.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Do selected configurations remain feasible during sustained use?** Cool, idle profiles leave this unresolved (§6). Next experiment: replay realistic request streams under thermal load, background applications, and varying prompt lengths, measuring budget violations.
2. **Can synthetic-example quality replace teacher task accuracy as the synthesis gate?** Helpful synthesis occurred below the default gate, while low row-survival rates accompanied poor scarce-data results (§5.2.2; Appendix B). Next experiment: compare task-score and generated-row-quality gates under matched compute budgets.
3. **How much do orchestration, rollback, and adaptive goals independently contribute?** Current intervention accounting and goal extensions do not isolate these effects (§5.2). Next experiment: run repeated, budget-matched component ablations across multiple tasks using untouched final tests.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported design and descriptive results; **medium** in generalization beyond the tested settings.

Material gaps include multi-seed uncertainty estimates, exhaustive-search comparisons beyond BC5CDR, unseen spam evaluation, sustained-load deployment measurements, and resource measurements for the exact four-bit artifacts used in accuracy scoring. The text claims code availability but does not establish separate releases of trained checkpoints or phone profiles.
