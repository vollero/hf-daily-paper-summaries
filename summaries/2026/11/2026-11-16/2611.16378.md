# Evidence-Governed Adoption of Self-Generated Updates in Autonomous AI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16378) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16378)


## One-sentence takeaway
Evidence-Governed Evolution (EGE) provides a conditional statistical gate for persistent agent updates, with synthetic evidence that candidate-specific evaluator calibration can prevent harmful acceptance under evaluator optimism, but no real-agent validation.

## Problem and motivation
Agents can modify prompts, memories, tools, and workflows, but generating a plausible update does not justify retaining it. Adaptive selection can overfit evaluation evidence, evaluator bias can create apparent gains, and successive updates can conceal capability degradation. The author identifies persistent adoption as a separate decision requiring independent evidence, stable capability references, and deployment controls (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adoption protocol:** Freeze each candidate, collect fresh paired evidence, require meaningful improvement against the incumbent on at least one primary objective, and require non-inferiority against a versioned reference on every protected capability. Mandatory governance checks and bounded canary deployment precede commitment (§§3–4, 7).
- **Evaluator-calibrated inference:** Subtract a candidate-specific calibration upper bound from the operational evaluator’s lower bound. Summable error allocations provide simultaneous coverage across a countable adaptive candidate sequence (§5, Theorem 1).
- **Reference lineage:** Audit reference replacements through bridge tests, making cumulative permitted degradation relative to the original reference explicit (§6, Theorem 2).
- **Planning and stress testing:** Give conservative sample-size conditions and synthetic tests of evaluator optimism and calibration mismatch (§§5.4, 8).

The claimed novelty is this formulation and composition; the author explicitly disclaims a new concentration inequality or complete safety architecture (§1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional family-wise error control | Under Assumptions 1–3, simultaneous lower-bound coverage is at least \(1-\alpha\), and the probability of any population-invalid statistical acceptance is at most \(\alpha\) across the adaptive campaign. | §5.3, Theorem 1; §5.6, Corollary 1 |
| Explicit cumulative degradation allowance | On simultaneous coverage, a committed configuration’s protected score relative to the original reference is bounded below by \(-(\epsilon_{K,r}+\sum_{k<K}\rho_{k,r})\), using common metrics and distributions. | §6, Theorem 2 |
| Calibration blocked harmful acceptance in the specified simulation | Across nine conditions, each with 3,000 campaigns and 10 candidates, calibrated EGE observed no harmful campaign acceptance; each condition’s one-sided exact 95% upper limit was approximately \(9.98\times10^{-4}\). Settings varied optimism over 0, 0.10, 0.25 and \(n=m\) over 5,000, 20,000, 80,000 per candidate and metric. | §8.1, Table 2 discussion |
| Naive acceptance failed under high optimism | At optimism 0.25, the evaluator-naive gate accepted a harmful candidate in 2,997/3,000 campaigns (99.9%). Harmful primary effect was −0.10; primary threshold 0.05; protected effect 0 and tolerance 0.20; global \(\alpha=0.05\). | §8.1 |
| Calibration imposed an acceptance-power cost | With beneficial primary effect +0.25, calibrated beneficial-candidate acceptance was 52.6–76.2% at \(n=m=5{,}000\), and 100% at 20,000 and 80,000, across the specified optimism settings. | §8.1 |
| Nonrepresentative calibration defeated the gate | With audit optimism +0.25 but calibration optimism +0.05, harmful effect −0.10, and \(n=m=100{,}000\), harmful acceptance occurred in 3,000/3,000 campaigns (100%); exact two-sided 95% interval approximately [0.9988, 1.0000]. Each campaign contained 10 harmful candidates. | §8.2, Table 3 discussion |

These results support conditional validity and illustrate a specific evaluator-bias failure mode. They do not establish real-agent performance gains, operational efficiency, canary safety, or robustness to distribution shift. The simulation confidence limits are distinct from the theorem’s error guarantee (§§8, 11).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Guarantees depend on fresh, conditionally independent bounded observations and representative calibration. Correlated traces, nonstationarity, or invalid reference evaluation can undermine applicability (§§5.1, 9–10).
- **Author-stated:** Candidate-specific calibration is expensive; later audit indices receive smaller error allocations, increasing sample requirements. Real-agent costs and outcomes remain unmeasured (§§5.4, 9.4).
- **Author-stated:** Aggregate metrics can hide subgroup regressions or rare severe failures. Pairing may be infeasible when updates change task eligibility or execution pathways (§10).
- **Author-stated:** Rollback cannot undo irreversible actions, and EGE does not supply comprehensive security controls (§§7, 10).
- **Analyst observation:** §5.4 first states an intermediate bound subtracting only one evaluation radius, then correctly explains that two are needed. The subsequent sufficient-power condition uses two; the earlier displayed inequality is internally inconsistent.
- **Analyst observation:** Real-agent baselines, ablations, and canary studies in §8.3 are proposed experiments, not executed evidence.

## Open research questions
1. **Does EGE improve deployed update decisions at acceptable cost?** No real-agent study is reported. Run a shared candidate stream through full EGE and the proposed adoption baselines, measuring harmful acceptance, missed improvements, audit cost, and canary failures (§8.3).
2. **Can calibration remain valid with fewer reference evaluations?** Candidate-specific calibration is sample-intensive. Compare it with a prespecified candidate-class calibration method under adaptive selection, measuring coverage and acceptance power (§§4.1, 10).
3. **How should inference handle dependent or shifting tasks?** The present guarantee does not cover arbitrary correlated traces or distribution shift. Test a justified cluster-aware or nonstationary extension on controlled update episodes (§§4.2, 10).
