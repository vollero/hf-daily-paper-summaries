# MemInStream: Memorizing or Merely Recognizing? Rethinking Streaming Video Memory Evaluation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15640) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15640)


## One-sentence takeaway
MemInStream evaluates streaming video memory using questions filtered for evidence solvability and necessity, with a controlled memory ablation supporting its sensitivity to retained history while leaving model-independent memory validity unproven.

## Problem and motivation
Answer accuracy can confuse memory with other capabilities: an incorrect answer may reflect poor visual understanding, while a correct answer may come from recent frames or textual cues. The authors argue that existing evaluations insufficiently verify whether historical evidence is both sufficient and necessary to answer a question (Secs. 1–2, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and taxonomy:** 3,799 questions covering four memory categories and 17 tasks: episodic retrieval, state tracking and updating, multi-evidence integration, and contextual memory (Secs. 3, 4.4).
- **Evidence-controlled construction:** Annotate query timestamps and supporting historical intervals. Retain questions that both validation models answer correctly with evidence but incorrectly with text alone and post-evidence video; human reviewers then check answers and annotations (Secs. 4.2–4.3).
- **Dialogue-dependent evaluation:** Contextual questions additionally test whether relevant historical dialogue and visual evidence are jointly required. Dialogue enters memory sequentially rather than as a complete transcript at query time (Secs. 3.1, 4.3).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Compare recent-frame and streaming models, examine evidence coverage and temporal distance, and ablate memory in JoyAI under a fixed recent-frame budget (Sec. 5).

The contribution is an evaluation asset and validation protocol, rather than a new memory architecture.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Questions often require multiple historical segments | 3,799 questions with 8,217 evidence intervals; 56.7% have multiple intervals. Median query distances are 36 seconds from latest evidence end and 96 seconds from earliest evidence start. | Sec. 4.4, Fig. 3 |
| Memory improves a controlled baseline | JoyAI with versus without memory, keeping recent input fixed at up to 60 frames at 2 fps: category macro exact-match accuracy rises from 31.8%→41.0% for episodic retrieval, 28.6%→43.9% for state tracking, 22.3%→32.6% for integration, and 26.9%→29.7% for contextual memory. | Sec. 5.4, Fig. 5 |
| Historical integration and dialogue remain difficult | StreamForest at 1 fps scores 23.9% exact match on multi-hop binding and 18.3% on conversational context, versus 52.5% on entity–identity binding. | Table 2 |
| Recent-frame success strongly depends on evidence coverage | Gemini-3.8-Flash with 30 recent frames at 1 fps achieves 86.2% accuracy with full evidence overlap versus 19.8% with none; these are different question subsets. | Sec. 5.4, Fig. 4(b) |
| The narrative overstates StreamForest’s ranking | StreamForest’s category macro scores are 42.0%, 37.1%, 37.3%, and 35.4%; JoyAI exceeds its state-tracking score with 43.9%. Thus, the statement that StreamForest leads every category is inconsistent with Table 2. | Sec. 5.3, Table 2 |

The JoyAI ablation directly supports sensitivity to memory under the stated input budget. Coverage comparisons support the importance of evidence availability, but do not isolate causality because question subsets differ. Filtering establishes solvability and resistance to tested shortcuts for the selected validators; it does not prove historical memory is necessary for every model.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The benchmark does not fully capture prolonged video streams; longer videos and greater evidence–query separation remain future work (Sec. 6).
- **Author-stated:** Different question difficulty and composition across temporal bins prevent interpreting accuracy trends as isolated memory decay (Sec. 5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Filtering with Gemini and Doubao, then evaluating those same models, makes benchmark selection dependent on their capabilities. Transfer of the solvability and necessity criteria to other models is not demonstrated (Secs. 4.3, 5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** General memory effects remain partly confounded by different backbones, frame rates, and context budgets. The controlled memory ablation covers JoyAI alone (Secs. 5.2–5.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Earlier descriptions mention recent-only validation, but the detailed filtering procedure specifies text-only and post-evidence checks without a separate recent-only check (Secs. 1, 4.3).
- **Reporting gap:** Figure 4’s overlap-group counts do not account for all benchmark questions, and the supplied text does not explain the exclusions.

## Open research questions
- **Does validation generalize beyond its construction models?** Necessity and solvability are tested with two validators. Revalidate retained questions with held-out model families and report agreement and newly discovered shortcuts.
- **How does retention change with delay alone?** Existing temporal bins differ in question composition. Replay matched evidence and questions with controlled delays and distractors.
- **Which memory designs improve performance under equal resources?** Most comparisons vary model and input settings. Compare memory mechanisms using one backbone and matched frame, storage, and computation budgets.
- **What fails in conversational memory?** Low conversational-context scores do not identify whether dialogue retention, reference resolution, or visual binding is responsible. Ablate access to relevant dialogue and visual evidence separately.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The protocol and central results are clearly described, but ranking inconsistencies and incomplete construction details limit stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes per-task and source counts, video-duration and domain distributions, filtering rejection rates, detailed human-review procedures, option-count distributions, uncertainty estimates, complete prompts and memory configurations, explanations for Figure 4 exclusions, and usable release URLs or licensing details.
