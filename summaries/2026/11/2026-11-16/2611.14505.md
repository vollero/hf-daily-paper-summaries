# Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14505) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505)


## One-sentence takeaway

Continual-ARC separates retention of recurring ARC skills from transfer to unseen tasks, finding measurable transfer from teacher-trained weight updates but none from the evaluated persistent harness methods (§4).

## Problem and motivation

The authors ask whether agents retain skills across separate jobs and become better at unfamiliar jobs. They identify three gaps in existing benchmarks: dependence on applied domains with substantial pretrained knowledge, instances that appear only once or recur unchanged, and protocols that favor either memory or weight updates (§1).

Their proposed benchmark makes conceptual tasks recur with fresh inputs and charges for feedback, aiming to distinguish reusable skill from cached answers while comparing different learning mechanisms (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Recurring-task benchmark:** Three ARC-AGI tracks introduce tasks gradually and revisit them after variable gaps. The main ARC-AGI-1 setting serves 200 instances across 25 tasks per user (§2.2).
- **Feedback-based measurement:** Demonstrations and incorrect submissions each cost one; failures consume the full budget of 16. Budget use and solve rate are reported together. Comparisons with an instance-by-instance reset estimate learning beyond initial capability (§§2.1, 2.4).
- **Separate retention and transfer tests:** Frozen learners face fresh instances of encountered tasks and tasks absent from the stream. Individual and collective regimes allow shared learning across users (§§2.3–2.5).
- **Comparative empirical study:** The authors compare demonstration caching, program libraries, persistent harnesses, teacher-written notes, and DAgger updates to a Qwen LoRA adapter. They also describe private-rule verification and report releasing code and run records (§§2.6, 3; reproducibility statement).

The claimed novelty is the benchmark’s combination of recurrence, fresh instances, growing task sets, charged feedback, and support for memory and weight updates—not invention of the evaluated learning algorithms (§5).

These results support a difference between **the tested implementations**: task-specific programs aid retention, while teacher-trained weights also improve unseen-task performance. They do not establish that memory cannot generalize. Appendix A explicitly notes that none of the evaluated learners maintains reusable primitive abstractions.

DAgger’s gain also mixes several effects: improved harness operation, learning from expert guidance, and possible ARC skill acquisition. The authors interpret its performance beyond unguided and guided untrained controls as evidence of ARC learning (§4); the supplied evidence does not fully isolate those components.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Truncated evidence:** A substantial middle portion is missing. Several tables appear only as captions, and figure images are not numerically available here; coverage is incomplete.
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Most experiments use relatively small models on ARC-AGI-1. Harder-track evaluations are lightweight, and generalization to applied domains remains open (§6; Appendix K).
- **Implementation qualifications:** prime-agent’s bundled Python skills failed to import in first-track runs; openclaw could not reach its expected embedding service. The authors report limited skill-installation reruns without significant budget differences, but these qualifications constrain claims about harnesses “as shipped” (Appendix D).
- **Capability and baseline confounding:** Qwen frequently fails to produce readable harness actions. Reset-relative savings therefore include learning to operate the harness, and weak resets can inflate relative improvements (Appendices A, D).
- **Limited uncertainty coverage:** Reported intervals condition on learned state and do not capture variation across independent learning runs. Canonical results use one stream seed (§2.2; Appendix A).
- **Public-task exposure:** Held-out tasks are unseen during the stream, not necessarily unseen during pretraining. Reset comparisons address incremental improvement but cannot establish contamination-free task novelty (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—resource comparability:** Equal feedback scoring does not imply equal total learning cost: compute is logged but unscored, and DAgger requires a stronger teacher (Appendix A; §6).
- **Private-set validity:** Rules are model-generated and lack human testing; first-sight calibration does not establish equivalent learnability on recurrence (Appendix J).

## Open research questions

1. **Can compositional memory transfer across tasks?** None of the evaluated methods stores a reusable primitive library (Appendix A). Next experiment: compare task-program storage with explicit primitive extraction and retrieval under matched feedback and compute reporting.

2. **How much of DAgger’s transfer comes from harness fluency?** Formatting failures and guidance distillation contribute to its gain (§4; Appendix D). Next experiment: train separate controls on protocol compliance, expert guidance, and task-solving traces, then evaluate all in both the harness and stateless loop.

3. **Can teacher-free weight updates learn effectively from sparse feedback?** The successful method requires teacher replies, while full details of unsuccessful alternatives are unavailable in the truncated source (§6). Next experiment: compare teacher-free updates with DAgger across several stream seeds and matched resource budgets.

4. **Does collective learning help through diversity or simply more experience?** More users increase both distinct tasks and total instances (§2.5). Next experiment: hold total instances fixed while varying task count and user count independently.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly defines the protocol and reports central comparisons, but truncation prevents a complete audit.

Material missing information includes much of Appendices E–H, full central result tables, detailed training schedules and compute, complete paired ablations, and numerical contents of several figures and cost tables. Code availability is an author report; no artifacts were independently inspected.
