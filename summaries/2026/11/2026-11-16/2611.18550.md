# Restoring Diversity in Distilled Image and Video Generators by Probing Activations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18550) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18550)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReDiverse restores diversity in evaluated distilled image and video generators by repairing teacher–student activation differences through localized LoRA training or teacher-direction steering, although some quality trade-offs remain (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Few-step diffusion students can generate similar compositions across different seeds despite their teachers producing varied outputs. The authors identify a diagnostic gap: existing remedies modify training or sampling without locating where internal variation disappears (§1).

Their finding is that students retain substantial activation variance but concentrate it into fewer directions, motivating targeted repair rather than complete re-distillation (§3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Representation diagnosis:** Compare teacher and student activation total variance, participation ratio, and concentration across blocks under matched prompts and noises. Identify an intermediate region where effective dimensionality decreases; select a repair block where concentration rises clearly (§3; App. A).
- **ReDiverse-FT:** Train LoRA adapters through the selected block using activation MSE between the student’s first step and a later teacher step, with matched noise and prompt. Enable adapters only at the first sampling step (§4.1).
- **ReDiverse-TF:** Estimate prompt-specific teacher principal directions and perturb first-step student activations along them with randomly sampled, teacher-scaled coefficients. It requires no parameter updates and can complement FT (§4.2).
- **Cross-modal evaluation:** Test on SDXL, SD3, Wan 2.1, and MiniMax-H3, using automatic metrics, human comparisons, and a VLM judge (§5).

The distinctive contribution is the joint teacher–student diagnosis and its use to target repairs; feature regression, LoRA, and PCA are existing components (§§2, 4).

Teacher-direction interventions provide stronger mechanistic evidence than correlation alone, but do not establish a universal explanation of distillation collapse. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Quality sometimes decreases, especially image reward scores and video realism relative to teachers (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Both variants assume matching teacher–student architectures; structurally compressed students would require a learned projection (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** The “single collapsed block” framing is approximate: Appendix A says the PR decline develops over several blocks, with the repair block selected by a clear concentration increase.
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization of design choices remains uncertain because all detailed ablations use SD3; the within-image diversity example is limited to a two-vase prompt (App. E; Fig. 9).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparative results depend on author reproductions, including custom architecture adaptations and bounded training/optimization budgets (App. G).
- **Analyst inference:** TF incurs prompt-specific teacher computation. Wall-clock cost, memory use, and amortization across generated samples are not established in the supplied text (App. D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Human evidence covers prompts deliberately chosen to allow variation, limiting conclusions about more constrained prompts (App. J).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the diagnostic generalize beyond these models and step budgets?** Main evaluations use four-step students. Test additional architectures and sampling schedules with identical activation and output-diversity measurements (§5.1).
2. **Can diversity be preserved during distillation?** Post-distillation repair still loses quality. Compare otherwise matched distillation runs with and without selected-block feature supervision (§6).
3. **Can TF preprocessing be amortized across prompts?** Directions are estimated separately for each prompt. Test shared or predicted subspaces against prompt-specific PCA, measuring diversity, alignment, and end-to-end cost (§4.2).
4. **Can structurally compressed students use the same repair?** Matching architectures are assumed. Train a block projection and compare projected feature regression with unmodified compressed students (§6).
