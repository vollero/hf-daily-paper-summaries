# AI Reviews Are Biased Towards the Appendix

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17811) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17811)


## One-sentence takeaway
On papers selected for long appendices, the evaluated LLMs draw substantially more review weaknesses from appendix material than historical human reviewers, but the study does not establish whether those criticisms are less useful or whether LLMs caused the recent human-review trend.

## Problem and motivation
LLM-generated weaknesses may influence submitted peer reviews, yet prior research has focused more on agreement with humans than on which parts of papers models criticize. The authors investigate whether appendix details disproportionately shape AI reviews and divert attention from core contributions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Section-attribution pipeline:** Generate conference-style reviews of anonymized papers, then ask the same model to attribute each weakness to up to two sections. Any appendix involvement counts as appendix-related (§§3–4).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Analyze 800 recent papers across ACL, EMNLP, COLM, and ICLR using four GPT/Claude models; compare GPT generations and access methods (§§3–4).
- **Human comparison and taxonomy:** Examine historical ICLR reviews, track appendix-related criticism over time, and classify its subject matter using GPT-6.1-Sol (§5).

The contribution is an empirical analysis and attribution workflow, rather than a new reviewing model. Code is reported publicly available on page 1.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Appendix involvement is common, but not universally a majority | Across four models and four venues, appendix-related weakness shares range from **42.7%** to **72.4%**; recent papers have appendices of at least 15 pages. | Figure 3; §3.1 |
| Newer GPT models show higher appendix shares | Across the four venues, GPT-5: **17.9–29.6%**; GPT-6.1-Sol: **58.3–66.5%**; GPT-6-Astra: **59.3–72.4%**, using the same pipeline. | Figure 4a; §4.2 |
| The pattern persists across access methods | On 100 ICLR 2027 submissions with GPT-6-Astra: Codex **72.4%**, direct-PDF API **62.2%**, extracted-text API **73.7%**, web **62.4%** appendix-related weaknesses. | Figure 4b; §4.3 |
| Historical human reviews differ substantially | Human ICLR 2021–24 reviews: **8.40 ± 1.44%** appendix-related and **4.36 ± 1.03%** appendix-only; ICLR 2027 LLM reviews: **62.00 ± 7.70%** and **14.19 ± 3.32%**, respectively. The meaning of ± is unspecified. | Table 1; §5.1 |
| Posted human-review appendix shares recently increased | With 200 sampled papers/reviews per ICLR edition, appendix-related weaknesses rose from **6.06%** in review year 2024 to **11.27%** in 2025 and **27.60%** in 2026. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Criticism categories differ | Among appendix-related weaknesses, methodology accounts for **54.0%** in LLM reviews versus **35.7%** in human reviews; presentation accounts for **2.1%** versus **32.2%**. Human and LLM cohorts are ICLR 2021–24 and ICLR 2027, respectively. | Table 3; §5.2 |

These results establish differences in attributed criticism location and subject matter within the sampled settings. They do not establish that appendix criticisms are incorrect, disproportionately frequent relative to appendix length, or harmful to decisions. The authors’ interpretation that models insufficiently prioritize core contributions goes beyond directly measuring section shares. The human-review trend is explicitly correlational.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** LLM attribution and classification can fail. Manual spot-checking is reported, but its sample size and agreement statistics are unavailable (Limitations, p. 11).
- **Author-stated:** Human and LLM reviews concern different papers and years; changing writing styles and appendix norms may confound comparisons (p. 11).
- **Author-stated:** Neither criticism correctness/usefulness nor effects on numerical review scores are measured (p. 11).
- **Analyst inference:** Long-appendix selection limits generalization. Recent papers require at least 15 appendix pages, while historical human samples use a 10-page threshold (§§3.1, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** No appendix-length-normalized analysis or controlled content-relocation experiment is reported. Location shares alone cannot establish disproportionate attention.
- **Analyst inference:** Figure 5 conditions human reviews on having appendix criticism but includes all LLM reviews, limiting direct comparison of page-level distributions (§5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Are appendix criticisms useful?** Correctness and contribution relevance remain unmeasured. Next experiment: blinded expert ratings of matched main-body and appendix criticisms.
- **Does location itself change criticism?** Appendix length and content are not experimentally separated. Next experiment: move identical material between main text and appendix while holding total content fixed.
- **Do LLMs cause the human-review shift?** Release timing provides correlation only. Next experiment: randomize reviewer access to LLM assistance on the same paper pool.
- **Does appendix emphasis affect scores?** Scores are outside this study’s scope. Next experiment: compare scores and expert-rated review quality under standard versus contribution-prioritizing prompts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The descriptive pattern is supported across several settings, but causal and normative conclusions remain unresolved.

Material missing information includes attribution-validation statistics, uncertainty definitions, exact aggregation rules, sampling seeds, model configurations, repeated-run variability, and dataset-release status.
