# JFinBench: A Continuously Refreshable Japanese Financial Benchmark from the Latest Certification Exams

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18130) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18130)


## One-sentence takeaway
JFinBench combines a human-verified construction pipeline with Japanese financial exam evaluation, finding that tools improve every tested model but leave a 4.4-percentage-point gap between the strongest proprietary and open-weight systems (§§3.2, 5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Financial expertise depends on local regulations, tax rules, and accounting standards, while existing financial benchmarks largely emphasize English and U.S. settings. The authors identify additional gaps in Japanese benchmarks: limited question formats, aging exam editions, and costly updates. Regularly incorporating new exams could address changing rules and reduce contamination risk, although freshness alone cannot guarantee uncontaminated evaluation (§§1–2; Limitations).

## Approach and contributions
- **Broader Japanese financial evaluation:** 321 separately graded examples from 267 problems across six 2026 certification exams, including multiple choice, short answers, structured forms, essays, and image-dependent questions (§3; Table 2).
- **Refreshable construction pipeline:** a VLM transcribes PDFs; a coding agent writes exam-specific conversion and review code; a native Japanese author verifies every example and requests corrections. Reported verification took approximately 12 hours overall (§3.2; Figure 1).
- **Comparative evaluation and released assets:** evaluation of 12 models with and without tools, plus grading checks, failure analysis, and harness comparisons. The authors report releasing the dataset, evaluation code, and construction pipeline; some source materials require local reconstruction rather than direct download (Abstract; §§4–5; Appendix A.1).

## Evidence and main results

Overall scores average example scores within each exam, then weight the six exams equally; free-response grading can award partial credit (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong proprietary lead without tools | At maximum effort, three-run mean ± SD: Claude Fable 5 **95.8 ± 0.4%**, GPT-6 Astra **93.6 ± 0.6%**, best open-weight Kimi K3.1 Pro **83.4 ± 0.9%**; best-to-best gap **12.4 points**. | Table 3; §5.5 |
| Tax remains challenging | Claude Fable 5 scores **79.4%** on the tax exam versus **97.1–100.0%** on the other exams, without tools at maximum effort, averaged over three runs. | Table 3 |
| Tools improve every tested model | Selecting each model’s best harness, GPT-6 Astra reaches **97.3%**, versus Kimi K3.1 Pro’s **92.9%**; the gap is **4.4 points**. Open-weight gains range from **9.5 to 29.9 points**, using three-run means. | Table 4; §5.5 |
| Rankings remain similar | Spearman **ρ = 0.87** between without-tools and best-harness with-tools rankings across **12 models**. | §5.5 |
| Knowledge errors predominate | Classification covers first-run, maximum-effort failures scoring **<0.5**. Human validation agrees on **26/30** sampled labels, **κ = 0.84**; disagreements concern knowledge versus reasoning. | §5.4; Figure 4 |
| Judge substitution preserves ranking | Regrading first-run, maximum-effort non-MCQ responses yields pooled Pearson **r = 0.94**; overall scores change by at most **1.7 points**, with ranking unchanged. Failed second-judge calls are excluded. | Appendix B.5; Table 8 |

These results establish performance differences on this exam collection and substantial gains under the tested tool settings. The authors interpret ranking stability as evidence that stored knowledge remains useful with tools. **Analyst inference:** this correlation does not isolate knowledge causally from reasoning, retrieval skill, or harness effects. Exam performance also does not establish professional competence (Ethical Considerations).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** coverage excludes exams without officially published PDFs; contamination remains possible because cutoffs are missing or self-declared; leading models approach saturation outside tax (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—refreshability:** only one exam vintage is evaluated. Verification time is reported, but no manual-construction baseline or subsequent refresh demonstrates the claimed effort reduction (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—tool comparisons:** best-harness scores compare model–harness combinations. Table 11 shows material harness sensitivity, limiting attribution to models alone.
- **Analyst inference—automated assessment:** second-judge agreement supports consistency rather than correctness against expert grading. Answer-exposure auditing uses condensed trajectories, leaving its detection reliability unestablished (Appendices B.5, D.1).
- **Reproducibility constraint:** some hosted answers are null and require source acquisition and reconstruction; tax and Nissho answer keys come from TAC rather than examination bodies (Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does refreshing remain efficient and accurate?** No later-edition refresh is demonstrated. Apply the pipeline to the next editions and compare human time, total cost, and expert-audited errors against manual conversion.
2. **Which tool capability drives improvement?** Terminal and web access are introduced together. Compare retrieval-only, calculation-only, and combined access under a common harness and budget (§5.5).
3. **Does stronger stored knowledge causally improve tool use?** Ranking correlation leaves this unresolved. Compare matched models before and after targeted financial training using identical retrieval resources and harnesses.
4. **How accurate is free-response grading?** Cross-judge agreement lacks an expert grading reference. Have financial experts independently score a stratified sample of essays, forms, and short answers (Appendix B.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and tabulated results; **medium** in causal interpretations and demonstrated long-term refreshability.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes the actual prompt boxes referenced in Appendices B.3–B.4, direct release repository identifiers, measured contamination, expert validation of answer grading, audit detection accuracy, and a controlled construction-cost comparison. Figure images are also absent, so their detailed plotted distributions cannot be inspected.
