# Rethinking in Spikes: Mitigating Hallucinations in MDLMs with Step-Aware Decoding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12901) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12901)


## One-sentence takeaway
StepRefine selectively applies continuous token refinement at entropy spikes, improving hallucination and reasoning benchmarks across four multimodal diffusion backbones while reallocating a fixed forward-pass budget (Sections 3–4, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Multimodal diffusion language models predict masked tokens in parallel and progressively commit them to discrete outputs. The authors find that uncertainty generally decreases during decoding but occasionally spikes; these steps correlate with hallucinated tokens, and confidence-based commitment appears less useful there (Section 3.1, Figure 2).

The identified gap is temporal selectivity: existing approaches modify commitment policies or introduce continuous representations without explicitly targeting irregular uncertainty across denoising steps. The authors hypothesize that discrete commitment during spikes prematurely discards useful candidate semantics (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Uncertainty analysis:** The authors associate hallucinated tokens with locally anomalous increases in mean masked-token entropy and examine when these spikes occur (Section 3.1).
- **Selective latent refinement:** StepRefine triggers when the current entropy change exceeds the mean plus one standard deviation of preceding changes in a local window. It repeatedly feeds probability-weighted token embeddings into the model before discrete commitment (Sections 3.1–3.2).
- **Adaptive stopping and budget allocation:** Refinement stops when consecutive predictive distributions have sufficiently small average KL divergence. Extra refinement passes replace later denoising steps, preserving the total forward-pass budget (Section 3.2, Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical validation:** Evaluations cover four backbones, multiple reasoning and hallucination benchmarks, decoding settings, and acceleration methods (Section 4, Appendix B).

Continuous token representations already appear in the cited prior work; the distinctive contribution is their entropy-spike-conditioned use with adaptive termination (Section 5).

The reported results support consistent improvements over the corresponding base decoders across the evaluated benchmarks. They establish an association between spikes and hallucination, but do not prove that spikes cause hallucinations or that distributional stability guarantees correctness.

Raw scores above avoid the paper’s ambiguous percentage annotations for MMHalu and Bingo, which use different score ranges.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged failure:** Appendix C.3 and Figure 9 show a challenging case where refinement fails to recover the correct answer.
- **Analyst inference—mechanism remains partly unresolved:** The token-level spike analysis does not specify its sample size, hallucination-labeling procedure, or spike precision. The reported concentration therefore does not establish detector reliability.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty is unquantified:** The supplied results lack defined error bars, repeated-run statistics, or significance tests, despite the checklist asserting statistical reporting.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Tests concern image-conditioned text generation on the listed backbones. Other modalities, model scales, and substantially longer outputs are not established.
- **Analyst inference—implementation gaps:** Initialization of the entropy window, refinement limits near budget exhaustion, and KL-threshold sensitivity are not specified. Equal forward-pass counts also do not alone establish equal computational cost.
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Dream-VL’s nominal default MMHalu setting differs between Tables 1 and 5; Figure 5(a) reports adaptive VStar accuracy of 59.1%, versus 58.6% in Table 1. The supplied text does not explain these differences.

## Open research questions
- **How accurately do spikes identify hallucination-prone commitments?** Detection precision and annotation details are missing. Next experiment: label committed tokens on a documented sample and report detector precision and recall across thresholds.
- **Does stability indicate correctness or merely convergence?** KL-based stopping measures distributional change, and a failure case remains. Next experiment: compare final correctness against KL trajectories for successful and failed refinements.
- **How much improvement comes from refinement versus reallocating steps?** The ablations do not fully isolate both effects. Next experiment: compare entropy-triggered refinement against matched-budget step skipping and alternative triggers.
- **Does the method transfer beyond the tested scope?** Longer outputs and other modalities are untested. Next experiment: vary generation length and modality while reporting accuracy, actual latency, and refinement frequency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The algorithm and benchmark trends are clear, but statistical and implementation gaps limit stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes spike-analysis sample sizes and labels, exact evaluation prompts and scoring procedures, run variability, entropy-window initialization, refinement-budget safeguards, and explanations for inconsistent results. Code availability is promised rather than demonstrated in the supplied text.
