# BrickFlow: Connectivity-Guided Brick Reconstruction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17321) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17321)


## One-sentence takeaway
BrickFlow combines a procedural assembly prior with analytic connectivity guidance to improve single-image LEGO reconstruction given an exact part inventory, but fully valid outputs remain rare for large assemblies (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The task is to recover every part’s 6D pose from a single image and a known inventory, including placements hidden by occlusion. Small pose errors can break connector alignment, so visual similarity alone is insufficient. The authors identify a gap in jointly reconstructing assemblies from fixed inventories across the full part library, rather than selecting parts or placing them sequentially (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Procedural data and assembly prior:** ProcBrick generates 100 million connected assemblies, each containing 100 parts, spanning 6,339 geometry classes. A set-conditioned SE(3) flow model learns to denoise unordered part poses while accounting for duplicate parts, intrinsic symmetries, and global alignment (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Image-conditioned adaptation:** The pretrained DiT receives frozen DINOv3 features through cross-attention and is finetuned on one million rendered assemblies—1% of the pretraining assembly count. Sampling hands the final steps back to the assembly prior (§§3.5, 4.1).
- **Connectivity-field guidance:** An analytic energy represents parts as an electrical network, with conductances derived from connector alignment. Effective resistance drives late sampling corrections toward connected configurations without retraining; this is the central methodological addition beyond the standard DiT and flow formulation (§§3.6–3.7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Larger models improve validity | On 512 BrickNet validation models, unguided image-conditioned DiT-S/B/L achieve 19.4%/26.1%/30.5% validity; guidance adds 4.6–5.6 percentage points across sizes. Evaluation uses 100 samples per structure. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 referenced |
| Image conditioning improves pose accuracy | BrickNet mass movement falls from 58.9 LDU for inventory-only PT DiT-L to 31.0 LDU for IC DiT-L; lower is better. | §4.2 |
| The pretrained pipeline improves connectivity | Unguided BrickNet IC DiT-S validity falls from 19.4% to 2.3% when trained from random initialization without the pretrained-model hand-off, despite lower pose error. Ablation variants use 10 samples per structure; reference rows use 100. | §4.2; Appendix G |
| Guidance improves large-assembly connectivity | For ProcBrick IC DiT-L, guidance roughly halves component counts beyond 80 parts; the largest component grows from 51 to 66 parts for assemblies containing 96–100 parts. | Fig. 5; §4.3 |
| Full validity remains difficult | ProcBrick IC DiT-L validity falls below 1% beyond about 50 parts for single samples and beyond 85 parts for best-of-100 sampling. | Fig. 5; §4.3 |
| Guidance has limited pose impact but adds computation | Across reported models, guidance changes mass movement by at most 0.3 LDU. Single-sample inference becomes 1.5–3.4× slower; batched ProcBrick IC inference slows by at most 1.15×. | §4.3; Appendices E–F |
| Reported performance includes snapping | Every reported result follows connector-based snapping; the authors report that snapping improves validity for every model while changing mass movement by at most 0.07 LDU. | Appendix B; Table 3 caption |

The evidence supports improvements within the authors’ model family, particularly in connectivity rather than pose accuracy alone. It does not establish superiority over external reconstruction methods or reliable reconstruction from arbitrary photographs. “Valid” means passing connector-connectivity and collision checks, rather than demonstrating physical stability or an executable assembly sequence (§4.1). Numerical table bodies and their confidence intervals are absent from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Guidance guarantees neither connectivity nor physical validity and has no explicit collision term (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Dense builds with similar-looking parts remain difficult; the authors suspect a synthetic-to-target distribution gap (§5).
- **Author-stated:** The inventory must be exact, and training stops at 100 parts; larger assemblies introduce computational and occlusion challenges (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** The initialization ablation also removes the pretrained-model hand-off, so it does not isolate pretraining from that sampling choice (§4.2; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Guidance settings are selected on a subset of the ProcBrick evaluation set, weakening its independence as an evaluation of those settings; BrickNet provides an additional check (§3.7; Appendix D).
- **Evidence boundary:** Physical photographs receive qualitative examples only; quantitative real-photo generalization is not established (§4.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Can explicit collision handling improve full validity?** Connectivity guidance leaves collisions and disconnected parts unresolved (§5). Compare the current field against a collision-aware objective under matched inference budgets.
2. **How much of the structural gain comes from pretraining versus the final-step hand-off?** The reported ablation changes both. Independently vary initialization and hand-off while holding data and sampling fixed.
3. **Can reconstruction tolerate uncertain inventories?** Missing, extra, and incorrect parts are outside training scope (§5). Evaluate controlled inventory corruption and joint part-type/pose prediction.
4. **Does the method generalize quantitatively to photographs and dense builds?** Real-photo evidence is qualitative, and dense builds remain difficult (§§4.4–5). Test a held-out photographic set stratified by density and visual part similarity.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and main trends, but omits table bodies containing full results, confidence intervals, training settings, and absolute inference times. Quantitative real-photo performance, isolated hand-off effects, and external baseline comparisons are unavailable. Asset availability is an author statement, not independently verified.
