# eDIAR: Training-Free Open-Speaker Streaming Diarization for Long Meetings and Large Archives via e-means

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18529) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18529)


## One-sentence takeaway
eDIAR extends fixed-slot diarizers without retraining through capacity-aware slot splitting and bounded revision, improving speaker-rich diarization and enabling efficient archive linking, while still struggling with prolonged rapid speaker alternation.

## Problem and motivation
Fixed-slot streaming diarizers cannot assign distinct outputs once speakers exceed their available slots. Online embedding clustering can accommodate new speakers but may preserve early mistakes and drift over long recordings. The authors seek to combine the activity and overlap decisions of a neural slot head with flexible speaker identities, while keeping update costs bounded (§1).

A related problem is linking the same speakers across recordings: clustering individual frames may improve identity decisions, but large archives make conventional clustering expensive (§6.4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Capacity-aware slot splitting:** Preserve the head’s activity and overlap outputs; cluster embeddings within individual slots only after all slots have been used. Candidate splits use the existing spherical e-means algorithm, with centroid separation and minimum-duration checks selecting the cluster count (§3).
- **Streaming identity management:** Recluster a rolling 90-second window, revise labels for up to 10 seconds, and match subclusters to permanent identities registered within each slot. Delayed centred embeddings replace causal embeddings during revision (§4).
- **Archive linking:** Cluster frame embeddings across sessions, then assign each local speaker its majority archive identity. The contribution is applying and evaluating e-means for this scale of linking; e-means itself is prior work (§§2, 6.4).
- **Portability without retraining:** Demonstrate the wrapper with both Nemotron-3 and Streaming Sortformer. This supports compatibility with two heads, rather than establishing universal compatibility (Table 4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

Paired 95% intervals for differences are **[−1.5, −0.1]** and **[−5.8, −1.3]** percentage points. | Tables 2–3 |
| Held-out results preserve that pattern | Revised streaming DER is **5.2% vs 5.6%** for the head on held-out VoxConverse and **17.6% vs 21.9%** on held-out LibriSpeech-OpenSpk. | Table 12 |
| Long real meetings show modest head-relative gains | Full-length, 1.04-second streaming DER: eDIAR/head **7.9/8.5%** on VoxConverse, **9.6/9.7%** on AMI, and **28.6/28.5%** on ICSI. | Table 5 |
| Rapid alternation exposes a failure case | In constructed hour-long meetings with 12–20 speakers and short turns, eDIAR/head DER changes from **45/51%** after 15 minutes to **60/52%** after an hour. | Appendix B.2, Figure 4b |
| Frame-level archive linking improves quality and runtime | On **1.16 million frames**, with **1,251 clusters** and 16 CPU threads, five-epoch e-means achieves **19.3 ± 0.5%** archive DER in **13.88 ± 0.40 seconds**, versus Lloyd’s 25 iterations at **21.0 ± 0.5%** in **43.86 ± 2.62 seconds**. Reported deviations are across three seeds. | Table 6 |
| e-means is not the fastest within-session engine | Revised streaming DER is nearly identical across engines; mean whole-pipeline update time is **49 ms** with e-means versus **36 ms** with five-iteration Lloyd. | Table 24 |

Standard diarization results use a 0.25-second collar and score overlap (§5); archive DER instead uses one global identity mapping (§6.4). The evidence supports improved capacity handling and efficient archive clustering, but not consistent superiority across corpora, clustering baselines, or difficult long-meeting settings. “After revision” results also differ materially from labels initially shown to users (Table 2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Real datasets are small; constructed read-speech and interview benchmarks do not replace large natural meeting collections. Evaluation is English-only and excludes far-field rooms (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Thresholds were tuned on alternating sessions within the evaluated corpora. Held-out halves provide a check, but remain within those corpora (§8, Table 12).
- **Author-stated:** Long meetings with many rapidly alternating speakers can become worse than the head; archive linking assumes the speaker count is supplied (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** “Unlimited speakers” should be read cautiously. Each slot considers only a bounded number of subclusters within a bounded window, while permanent identities can accumulate across updates. The paper does not establish arbitrary active-speaker capacity (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** The distinctive within-session benefit comes primarily from the hybrid design, rather than a demonstrated e-means accuracy advantage: alternative engines perform similarly and can run faster (Tables 22, 24).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline coverage limits broad superiority claims. Leader–follower beats revised eDIAR on unseen synthetic conversations, and offline pyannote performs better in some larger-speaker settings (Table 16; Appendix B.1).

## Open research questions
- **How can identities remain stable during prolonged rapid alternation?** The bounded-window system deteriorates in this setting. Test longer or adaptive windows and revisable identity memories against the short-turn benchmark, measuring DER and update cost (Appendix B.2).
- **Can archive speaker counts be estimated reliably?** Only supplied counts and nearby perturbations are evaluated. Compare automatic count estimation with the existing linking protocol, including archives with newly arriving speakers (Table 7).
- **Do the gains transfer to natural, multilingual, far-field meetings?** Those conditions are absent or explicitly excluded. Evaluate frozen thresholds on independently collected recordings covering these conditions (§8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed protocols, held-out checks, ablations, and counterexamples. Material gaps include independently verified code/model availability, broad natural speaker-rich meeting evaluation, multilingual and far-field results, automatic archive speaker-count selection, and evidence establishing the claimed unlimited capacity beyond the tested settings.
