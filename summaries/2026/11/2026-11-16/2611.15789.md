# VibePrevisBench: Evaluating Agentic Coding for 4D Scene Generation in Blender

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15789) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15789)


## One-sentence takeaway
VibePrevisBench evaluates coding agents on executable Blender scenes and their rendered videos, finding persistent failures in spatial reconstruction, challenging camera requests, and action legibility across 11 agents (§§3–4, Appendix C.3).

## Problem and motivation
Coding agents can turn briefs into animated Blender scenes that supply layout, motion, and camera control for subsequent video generation. Evaluating only rendered frames can miss incorrect scene structure; checking only scene state can miss actions that viewers cannot recognize.

The authors identify a gap in benchmarks combining diverse inputs, extended scenes with multiple people and shots, and checks of both underlying scene correctness and visible readability (§1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 242 tasks comprising scripts, photos, continuations from an opening frame, and reference videos. Outputs are executable Blender programs and graybox proxy videos; median output duration is 40 seconds (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Complementary evaluation:** deterministic **Scene Check** compares replayed scene states with hidden facts across nine dimensions. **Screen Check** uses a VLM to assess recognition, action readability, visibility, and story, evaluating only items accepted by the rules. Overall score averages the two checks (§3.2).
- **Diagnostic study:** evaluation of 11 coding agents, including trajectory analysis, limited perception ablations, judge comparisons, and injected-defect tests (§4, Appendices B.4–C.3).

This is primarily a benchmark and evaluation contribution; it does not introduce a new scene-generation model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Leading scores remain below complete success | Across 242 equally weighted tasks, overall scores: GPT-6 Astra 77.7, GPT-6.1 Sol 76.1, Gemini 4 Argon 75.0, Opus 5.5 74.1; failed deliveries score zero. | §4.2 |
| Agents share substantial unsolved difficulty | Only 35/242 tasks have any agent reach Scene ≥90; selecting the best overall result per task across all 11 agents yields 80.6. | §4.2 |
| Visual inputs widen Scene-score separation | Best–worst gap: 12.0 points on scripts versus 19.9 on photos. An oracle text description improves DeepSeek V4.1 Flash’s overall score by 14.0 points on five video tasks. | §4.3, Appendix C.2; Table 5 caption |
| Challenging camera requests often become common alternatives | Averaged over agents, 66% of low-angle shots become eye-level under pitch-based classification; only 4% of requested orbits remain orbits. Including camera height reduces the low-angle substitution estimate to 35%. | §4.3, Appendix C.2 |
| Scene correctness does not guarantee readable events | Among annotated events with usable judge verdicts, 36% both pass rules and receive screen confirmation; another 31% pass rules but receive partial confirmation (17%) or rejection (14%). | Appendix C.3, Fig. 16 |
| Self-reported render correctness weakly tracks errors | With agent and task fixed effects, camera-angle errors differ by −0.8 percentage points between runs claiming a match and other runs; 95% CI: −4.0 to +2.1. | Appendix C.2 |
| Rankings are more stable than individual judgments | Across 18 scoring variants, ranking agreement is Kendall τ ≥0.855 and Astra remains first; five adjacent agent pairs are not distinguishable by paired bootstrap. Alternative judges correlate at 0.94–0.96 across agent means, but 0.73–0.76 across runs. | Appendix B.4 |

These results support complementary scene and screen evaluation and identify recurring failures under the tested protocol. They do not establish that visual perception is the sole bottleneck, that additional inspection cannot help, or that proxy scores predict downstream video quality broadly. The oracle experiment is small, inspection analyses are largely correlational, and downstream transfer is illustrated on only two tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors acknowledge measurement gaps:** physical-validity scoring poorly detects floating people; rules miss some posture events; reference cut annotations miss some cuts (Appendices B.4, C.2–C.3).
- **Authors acknowledge diagnostic limits:** the oracle-caption and no-image ablations cover one agent and small task subsets. Challenging/common request pairs were selected after observing errors; training-data scarcity is an interpretation, not a tested cause (Appendix C.2).
- **Authors acknowledge potential bias:** evaluated model families helped construct tasks and estimate facts. Human review provides safeguards, but a small construction preference cannot be excluded; Gemini’s screen judge shows a measured own-family preference (Appendices A.1, B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** pruning tasks using these same 11 agents’ discrimination scores may favor distinctions specific to this cohort (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** different agent harnesses and one run per task limit attribution to model capability and estimation of run variability (§4.1).
- **Scope:** appearance is excluded, Screen Check is conditional on rule acceptance, and CPU-only, asset-free execution limits generalization to production workflows (§§3.1–3.2, 4.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does structured verification improve correction?** Render inspection has weak associations with success, but itemized verification is proposed rather than tested. Compare ordinary inspection against brief-derived checklists under matched budgets.
2. **How much does better perception help across agents?** The oracle-caption result covers one agent and five tasks. Repeat across models and input kinds, separating camera, event, and layout information.
3. **Do proxy scores predict downstream video quality?** Two examples show error propagation without estimating prevalence. Render a stratified proxy sample through downstream generators and compare scene/screen scores with human video judgments.
4. **Will rankings generalize beyond the construction cohort?** Tasks were pruned using the evaluated agents. Test new agents on an independently curated, unpruned task set.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark design and headline findings, but many tables contain captions without their numerical bodies.

Material missing information includes complete per-agent scores and reasoning settings, full ablation results and rerun variability, detailed dimension definitions, exact scan-dataset identities, and artifact contents needed to verify reproducibility.
