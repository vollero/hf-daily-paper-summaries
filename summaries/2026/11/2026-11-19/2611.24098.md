# RoughCut-Bench: A Benchmark for Automatic Video Rough-Cut with Large Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24098) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24098)


## One-sentence takeaway

RoughCut-Bench evaluates executable video rough-cuts from source footage or ASR transcripts and finds that the eight tested vision-language models excel at different editing subtasks, with no model leading across all metrics.

## Problem and motivation

A rough-cut selects and orders material for an initial edit, saving creators from manually reviewing all raw footage before fine editing. The authors identify two gaps: existing benchmarks test components such as temporal grounding and highlight detection without jointly assessing the decisions needed for a rough-cut, and existing workflows rarely turn those decisions into an executable cut plan (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task and benchmark.** The visual track tests timestamped highlight selection and source-video ordering, with user constraints in some cases. It contains 101 cases, 831 raw videos, and 1,476 professionally annotated highlights. The speech track contains 84 annotated talking-head ASR transcripts and tests filler removal, expression deduplication, and low-value content trimming (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Executable workflows.** The visual pipeline processes each source video independently, then passes candidate timestamps, descriptions, and key frames to a planner that selects and orders segments. The speech pipeline processes a complete transcript and returns structured cleanup decisions plus the cleaned text. ASR timestamps can map those decisions back to the video timeline (§§3.1, 3.4, Fig. 2).
- **Complementary evaluation.** Annotation-based metrics score both tracks. A Gemini 3.0 Pro evaluator, whose prompt was refined using professional scores and rationales, also assesses rendered visual cuts in the context of the source footage and any user requirements (§§3.3, 4.1, 4.3).

The results show that model rankings depend on the editing decision and metric under the tested workflows. The visual sets contain different cases, so their score differences cannot isolate the effect of adding user instructions. The evaluator agreement measures model-level averages during refinement, leaving per-cut accuracy and agreement on unseen cases or model families unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated boundaries.** A single professional reference can miss other acceptable visual edits. The speech track uses transcripts and excludes cues such as prosody, emotion, and speaker interaction. The shared visual workflow prioritizes reliable cross-model execution, and API image limits constrain joint processing (§§3.3, 5, App. A.1–A.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The Gemini 3.0 Pro evaluator scores cuts from its own model family, including Gemini 3.0 Pro itself. The paper gives no cross-judge analysis that isolates a same-family effect. All reported workflow ablations also use Gemini 3.0 Pro, so whether those trade-offs transfer to other models remains open (§§4.1, 4.5, App. A.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does the evaluator generalize?** The reported agreement comes from refinement against fixed professional feedback and is measured at model level. Freeze the prompt, then compare evaluator and professional ratings at both cut and model level on held-out cases and model families (§4.3).
2. **How do user constraints change highlight selection and ordering?** The general and constrained sets contain different cases. Run the same footage with and without controlled duration, selection, and ordering instructions, then compare both annotation agreement and rendered-cut ratings (§§4.1–4.3).
3. **Do audio and visual cues improve talking-head edits?** The speech track evaluates transcript annotations alone. Compare transcript-only, transcript-plus-audio, and joint audiovisual systems on the same inputs, with professionals rating the rendered edits for meaning preservation and pacing (§§3.1, 5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark design and reported table values. The supplied text does not provide a held-out evaluator validation, agreement statistics among professional raters, uncertainty intervals for model comparisons, or full endpoint and repair settings. The abstract describes the assets as released, while §5 describes the release prospectively, so availability is unclear from the supplied text.
