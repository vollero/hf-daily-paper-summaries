# Tideway: Phase-Aware Tiered Memory Management for MoE Inference on Consumer Devices

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14848) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14848)


## One-sentence takeaway

Tideway pools GPU and host memory, adapts expert placement between prefill and decode, and preserves CUDA Graph replay around paging, delivering a reported 1.97× average end-to-end speedup over the fastest baseline on fixed-shape workloads on an RTX 5090 (§6.1, Fig. 7a).

## Problem and motivation

Large mixture-of-experts (MoE) models can exceed the combined VRAM and host RAM of a consumer PC, putting SSD reads on the inference critical path. Each long-prompt prefill chunk can scan nearly the entire expert pool, so independent caches repeatedly evict weights needed by the next chunk. Decode touches fewer experts per step, but misses still stall execution, and host-managed paging can disrupt CUDA Graph replay. The authors identify a gap in coordinating expert residency and GPU execution across these two phases (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Phase-aware memory management.** Tideway tracks expert residency across VRAM and RAM with SSD backing. Prefill protects reusable RAM experts and streams SSD weights through a small window. Decode reclaims temporary VRAM workspace for expert slots, preferentially reclaims redundant RAM copies, and writes back VRAM-only victims when RAM has room without evicting another unique expert (§4).
- **Paging-aware execution.** Double buffering overlaps next-layer weight delivery with current-layer prefill computation. During SSD-backed decode, reusable CUDA Graph segments operate on fixed-address buffers and expert slots, with host paging between segments (§5).
- **Bottleneck characterization.** The authors profile SSD traffic and decode overhead under RAM pressure, then use residency traces, chunk-size sensitivity, and a cumulative ablation to examine the proposed mechanisms (§2.2, §§6.2–6.4).

The measurements support latency and throughput gains on the tested desktop, and the traffic profile shows reduced SSD dependence in the profiled prefill case. The cumulative ablation measures gains in one component order. The evidence leaves performance on other hardware, concurrent serving, and model-output quality unresolved. Figure 7b labels its decode metric as throughput, while the abstract uses TPOT for the same reported average factor.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Growing KV caches shrink the expert pool because KV capacity is reserved first. The authors discuss joint KV and expert management as future work and cap prefill chunks at 8K tokens in their experiments to limit activation memory (§§6.3, 7).
- **Analyst inference.** Three models and multiple RAM caps test memory pressure on one physical desktop. The relative gains may depend on the bandwidth gap between PCIe and SSD, and coding-agent replay leaves live concurrent serving untested (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** The FreeToken baseline was extended with SSD paging, while SSD-LLaMA and FlashMoE were reproduced on a FreeToken backend. Comparison fidelity therefore depends on those adaptations. The single cumulative ablation also leaves the independent effects of anchoring, replica reclamation, and victim writeback unresolved (§6, §6.4).

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive are the gains to PCIe and SSD bandwidth?** The paper evaluates one hardware configuration. Repeat the model–RAM sweep on desktops with different PCIe and NVMe bandwidth and measure request latency and SSD bytes read (§6).
2. **Can joint KV and expert placement improve concurrent long-context serving?** Tideway reserves KV capacity up front and evaluates trace replay. Compare fixed KV reservation with a joint allocator as concurrent sessions and context lengths increase, measuring TTFT, tail latency, and expert cache misses (§§6–7).
3. **Which residency policies matter most under different levels of memory pressure?** The ablation enables the memory manager as a bundle in one workload. Toggle anchoring, replica reclamation, and victim writeback independently and in combination across RAM budgets (§6.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the description of Tideway and the reported aggregate results. The supplied text does not provide run-to-run variability, a complete per-configuration timing table with fastest-baseline identities, the RAM budget for the ablation, or end-to-end output-equivalence results beyond the described warmup reference check (§5.2).
