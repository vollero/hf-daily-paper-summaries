# Learning Anisotropic Prediction Targets for Flow Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17767) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17767)


## One-sentence takeaway

AnisoFlow learns how much noise to include in each direction of a flow model’s prediction target, lowering 600-epoch FID from 1.90 to 1.55 on class-conditional ImageNet 256 × 256 with the same JiT-H/16 backbone and yielding comparable FID to the default targets in two latent-space models (Tables 1 and 4).

## Problem and motivation

Noise, clean-data, and velocity targets can recover the same velocity given the noisy input and timestep, yet they pose different regression problems. Pixel patches contain directions with widely different data variances. A uniform noise coefficient can make low-variance directions hard to predict, while clean-data prediction amplifies errors when recovering velocity at low noise. The authors identify a gap between scalar interpolations or binary masks and a target that can adapt continuously in each direction (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Learnable target.** AnisoFlow predicts \(W\epsilon-x\), where a positive semidefinite \(W\) assigns a nonnegative noise weight to each direction of an orthonormal basis. A closed-form map converts the prediction to velocity, and training minimizes squared error on that recovered velocity. The target family includes clean-data, velocity, scalar interpolation, binary masking, and the pure-noise limit (§4, Fig. 2, Appendix A.1).
- **Basis learning.** The authors compare a frozen PCA basis, Stiefel Adam, and a spectral update that eigendecomposes a running prediction-error moment. The spectral update gives the best final ImageNet FID in the reported ablation (§4, Table 1, Appendix A.4).
- **Error analysis.** A local model relates preferred weights to the network’s data- and noise-estimation errors. Under its assumptions, weights scale approximately with directional data variance and can be shared across timesteps. ImageNet weight profiles and auxiliary readouts are consistent with these predictions (§5, Figs. 3, 5, and 7).

The fixed-backbone ImageNet ablation supports a substantial contribution from learning continuous directional weights. The reported FIDs include per-model guidance selection, and the paper’s best-in-class claim applies to the listed plain DiT-like pixel models. Table 2 mixes evaluation protocols, which limits cross-family rankings. The quantized examples demonstrate visible artifacts under the tested settings, with their prevalence across prompts and seeds left unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors.** The local error model leaves full training dynamics and the proportionality constant in the spectral scaling unexplained. The two tested latent models learn flatter profiles close to their default targets and show smaller gains (§§6.3, 7, Appendix A.2).
- **Authors.** The error analysis uses auxiliary readouts because the model’s single output head does not uniquely identify separate data and noise estimates. The main fit uses directions selected for reconstruction fidelity. The one-megapixel samples are qualitative because training a matching baseline was costly (Appendices A.3 and B.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The ImageNet ablation reports point estimates and uses separately selected guidance settings. Run-to-run variability and the size of the gain under a single shared guidance setting are unresolved (Tables 1 and 6).
- **Analyst inference.** The spectral derivation relies on joint diagonalizability, and the local stability analysis uses an idealized linear predictor. These results leave convergence during joint nonlinear training unresolved (Appendices A.4–A.5).

## Open research questions

- **Does patch embedding width determine where near-linear scaling breaks down?** Appendix C observes a transition near the JiT bottleneck without varying its width. Train matched JiT variants across embedding widths and test whether the transition moves with that width.
- **When do timestep-dependent targets help?** The reported bucket ablation uses one smaller pixel-space model, and the theory gives sufficient conditions for shared weights. Compare shared and bucketed profiles across architectures and timestep distributions, measuring both recovered-velocity error and FID (§5, §6.1.2).
- **How often does better-conditioned recovery prevent quantization artifacts?** The evidence is qualitative. Run a matched prompt-and-seed sweep across the reported W8A8 formats and measure patch-boundary artifacts and quality changes relative to full precision (§6.2, Fig. 6, Appendix B.3).
