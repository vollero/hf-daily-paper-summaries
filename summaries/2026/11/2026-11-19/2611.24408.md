# StrideWorld: Benchmarking Scene Rigidity and Action Consistency in Interactive Video World Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24408) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24408)


## One-sentence takeaway

StrideWorld uses start-view geometry and separately calibrated action strides to show that most tested interactive video world models keep a steady pace under a held command but deform scenes or miss the endpoints predicted for multi-action routes.

## Problem and motivation

An explorable generated scene needs to preserve its 3D structure as the camera moves and respond predictably when commands change. The authors argue that reconstructing geometry from the video being scored can absorb gradual deformation, while fitting trajectory scale can hide changes in pace or distance. Loop-closure tests also commonly assume that opposite commands cancel, an assumption the authors find unreliable for many tested models (§§1–2, App. H.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Scene rigidity.** Lift image features into 3D using ground-truth start-view depth, recover the camera from one set of tracked anchors, and measure angular reprojection error on a disjoint set. Rigid renderings along recovered camera paths provide a tracking-noise baseline (§4.1, App. G.3).
- **Action consistency.** Estimate a planar stride for each action from the first four held steps for each model, view, and seed. Use it to predict later held motion and compose open routes. Score individual and paired endpoints, and diagnose stalls and action-switch failures (§§4.2–4.3, App. F.2).
- **Benchmark and intervention.** Evaluate 15 open models on 54,600 videos, validate the metrics with controlled renderings and blinded raters, and test LoRA fine-tuning on exact-path procedural clips for two models (§§3, 5.4, 6).

| Exact-path fine-tuning reduces stalls | With 112 procedural training clips per model, LoRA fine-tuning lowers stalled-route share from 72.0% to 11.2% for HY-World 1.5 and from 57.9% to 23.1% for SANA-WM, each against its non-distilled base on the full benchmark. Composition scores rise from 10.1 to 48.7 and from 16.1 to 47.0, respectively, with no drop in MUSIQ against those bases. | §6, Table J.1 |

The controlled renderings support sensitivity to the tested failures, and the human study supports agreement for the judgments sampled. For completed routes, raters’ judgments of motion along the path agree better with several whole-route metrics than with the endpoint score (Table I.1). The two Near leaders both use depth-scaled translation, but the cross-model comparison leaves its causal contribution unresolved (§5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** Start-view anchors limit coverage to surfaces that remain visible. Long-horizon exploration and revisits are outside scope. Scenes are static, the main action score is planar, and pitch is tested separately. AlayaWorld is unranked on stride-based metrics because its early motion is too small to calibrate reliably (§§3, 5.2, App. M).
- **Author-stated measurement risk.** Wrong but fixed depth can inflate the Rigidity offset, and deformation can bias the recovered camera. At comparable injected deformation levels, pose recovery changes the Near classification for 8–20% of counterpart routes while preserving the model ranking (§5.4, Apps. G.3–G.4, M).
- **Analyst inference.** Model-specific stride units and native command durations make the scores measures of consistency under each interface. They leave absolute motion accuracy and matched-duration performance unresolved (§4, Apps. H.6–H.7).
- **Analyst inference.** The fine-tuning evidence covers two non-distilled bases. The HY-World 1.5 perturbation changes both step magnitudes and switch timing, so their separate effects remain unknown. After fine-tuning, the median completed route for each model still misses by more than one stride (Table J.1, App. J.4, App. M).

## Open research questions

1. **Does depth-scaled translation itself improve composition?** The strongest models share that feature, but the study compares different architectures. Toggle depth scaling within a single model and compare Near and endpoint errors on the same views and routes.
2. **Can rigidity and control be measured after the initial surfaces leave view?** The current anchor set limits coverage, and the main test uses planar actions in static scenes. Use rendered full 6-DoF routes with ground-truth camera poses and object motion to test whether new reference anchors preserve scoring accuracy through occlusion and revisits.
3. **Which training signal reduces switch stalls and which improves endpoint accuracy?** The perturbation control varies both step magnitude and switch timing. Vary them independently on otherwise identical training clips, then measure switch stalls and completed-route endpoint error separately.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported benchmark results within the tested scope. The supplied text does not establish whether every listed artifact was already downloadable. The abstract describes public availability, while App. K describes some artifacts as forthcoming. The supplied text also gives no full benchmark compute or runtime total, no within-model depth-scaling ablation, and no estimate of rater agreement for subtle rigidity differences below the study’s selection threshold.
