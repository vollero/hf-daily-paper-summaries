# General progress substitutes for specific training data in language models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24844) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24844)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across the tested language models, withholding direct supervision delayed skill onset, while further training on the remaining corpus reduced the data needed to reach a fixed skill threshold once supervision returned.

## Problem and motivation

Training-data filters can suppress a capability, but their practical effect depends on how long they delay it and how much data it takes to acquire later. The authors identify a gap in prior work on scaling, transfer, and unlearning. It did not quantify both outcomes as a function of progress on the rest of the corpus or test whether the acquisition savings were specific to the withheld skill. (§1)

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled measurement of delay.** Target thinning removes a selected fraction of a skill’s next-token targets from the loss while preserving every input token. The authors measure onset at a fixed competence threshold and compare models using held-out cross-entropy outside the thinned targets. Separate document-filtering experiments also remove inputs. (§§2.1, 4.3, 4.6)
- **Prediction across model sizes.** A weighted-likelihood relation uses fully supervised and never-supervised reference models to predict thinning offsets for chess king safety and capital recall without fitting to the partially thinned runs. The authors also test whether responses calibrated on smaller models predict onset shifts in larger models. (§§2.1, 4.4–4.5, App. A)
- **Measurement of acquisition cost and skill specificity.** The authors restore targets at different checkpoints, compare the tokens needed to reach a fixed threshold with training from initialization, and model that cost using the supervised reference’s distance from the threshold. Permuted-label controls test skill specificity, and removal-route comparisons test the effect of prior supervision. (§§2.3–2.5, 4.9–4.10)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Target thinning delayed chess king safety. | Keeping 10% of in-check targets in 9.7M-parameter models shifted onset at competence \(q=0.70\) by 0.40 nats toward lower background loss relative to full supervision. Masking an equal density of random targets left onset unchanged. | §2.1, Figs. 1e, B2i |
| Thinning responses transferred across sizes. | For six web-text skills at 7B parameters, calibration on 60M–1B models yielded a mean absolute onset-shift error of 0.058 nats across three seeds and competence levels from 0.3 to 0.7. In a separate 7B capitals test at 30% retention, the endpoint-derived log-odds offset had 0.14-logit RMSE versus 0.61 for retained fraction alone. | §§4.4–4.5, App. B.5, Figs. 1c, 1h |
| Chess models learned partial skill without seeing checks in training. | At held-out first checks with multiple legal replies, models trained only on games without check ranked the played reply first among legal moves at 0.65–0.68 of positions versus 0.68–0.71 for fully supervised models. Across the same 9.7M–163M sizes, their total legal-move probability mass at first checks was 0.45–0.52 versus 0.85–0.91. | §4.7, Table C1 |
| Late chess checkpoints acquired the true rule cheaply. | From each never-supervised source run’s lowest-loss checkpoint, reaching 0.60 in-check legal-move mass took at most 0.10–0.78% of same-size from-initialization tokens, or 3.1–7.5% including optimizer preparation. A matched permutation control needed 2.4–16 times the true-rule tokens from the same checkpoints, with from-initialization costs within a factor of 1.06. | §2.3, §4.9, Figs. 3b, D1e–h |
| Acquisition cost fell with progress beyond the reference threshold. | Per additional logit by which the supervised reference exceeded the acquisition level at matched background loss, relative token cost fell by 1.12 ± 0.04 decades in chess, 0.91 ± 0.04 in web-text agreement, and 0.59 ± 0.07 in SMILES ring closure. These are fitted rates with standard errors from 312 returns across 27 source runs at 9.7M–431M parameters. | §§2.3, 4.10, Fig. 4, Table D1 |
| Recovery extended to selected 7B settings. | For capitals, the latest checkpoint in each seed reached smoothed competence 0.20 using 1.8% and 1.1% of the respective same-seed reference’s tokens from initialization. The three earliest checkpoints in each seed missed the threshold within 592M tokens. For Rust, with 3% of reference-labeled content retained during pretraining, the model closed half its term-accuracy gap using 2.1% of the unfiltered reference’s from-initialization tokens to the same accuracy. Sampled function completions compiled at 17.6% versus 44.7% for the unfiltered model at return, and 37.4% versus 41.1% after both continued for 500M tokens. | §§4.13–4.14, Fig. 3f, Tables E1–E2 |
| Prior supervision affected recovery. | With all in-check targets restored, never-supervised chess models needed 5–20 times as many tokens as models whose previously learned skill was suppressed by withdrawal or unlearning, comparing from the same starting legal-move mass to the 0.60 threshold. | §§2.5, 4.15, Fig. 5a–b |

The evidence supports the authors’ distinction between onset delay and acquisition cost. A filtered model can improve on the background corpus while the measured competence gap widens and the cost of reaching a fixed threshold after target return falls. The chess results distinguish ranking the played reply among legal options from assigning probability to legal moves overall. The experiments leave the durability of filtering for harmful capabilities and the generality of the fitted rates across architectures unresolved. (§§2.1–2.5, 3)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported model-fit limits.** The endpoint-derived relation needed fitted sensitivities for other tested skills, and the 7B capitals derivation covered one partial-retention setting. Othello was excluded from the main acquisition-cost fit because many returns were too fast for the measurement resolution. (§§4.4, 4.10)
- **Author-reported evaluation and replication limits.** The main 7B onset contrasts changed several target families together. The 7B Rust return used one seed and retained residual Rust data. The authors also found training-stream overlap with the Rust term evaluation and reported similar gap closure on the unread subset. (§§4.14, 4.19, App. B.5)
- **Author-reported reproducibility limits.** The released code omits the Rust function tasks and transplanted-weight experiments. Its rebuilt SMILES stream retains molecules excluded in the paper runs, changing subsequent stream positions. (§§4.21–4.22)
- **Analyst inference.** Logit distance is a stronger predictor than the source model’s starting score in the reported near-floor comparison, but it requires a supervised reference trajectory. Its usefulness where that reference is unavailable remains untested. (§4.10, Fig. 4d, Fig. D2i)

## Open research questions

1. **Which related examples account for cheaper acquisition?** The chess masking experiment implicates related king-safety positions, but inputs stayed intact. In a controlled chess corpus, independently remove those positions from the input stream and from the loss, then compare onset and return cost at matched background loss. (§§2.5, 4.17)
2. **How well do forecasts transfer across architectures?** Cross-size predictions were tested within dense decoder-only models and used a large-model reference trajectory. Calibrate on one model family, then test onset and acquisition forecasts on a second architecture using the same corpus, masks, and thresholds. (§§4.2, 4.5, 4.10)
3. **How sensitive is functional recovery to residual domain exposure?** The large Rust experiment used one filtered run with residual Rust content, and term accuracy and function correctness were measured separately. Repeat it across seeds and residual shares using a verified held-out term set and hidden-assertion function tasks. (§4.14, App. E.2)

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported within-study comparisons, with narrower confidence in generalization beyond the tested settings. The supplied text gives no checkpoint or model-weight release information and does not include the Rust function-task prompts, hidden assertions, or the release README’s full list of rerun differences. (§§4.14, 4.21–4.22)
