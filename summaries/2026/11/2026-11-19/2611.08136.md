# How Far Can Untrained Neural Networks Go in Anomaly Detection? From Tables to Pixels

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08136) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08136)


## One-sentence takeaway

Frozen random neural features paired with Mahalanobis scoring matched leading tabular detectors within the reported statistical tests and localized industrial defects at pixel level, with a performance gap to the strongest pretrained visual baselines (§V-B–C, Tables III–IV).

## Problem and motivation

When only normal examples are available for model construction, anomaly detection depends on how well deviations from that reference can be scored. The authors ask how much of that separation is available before neural feature learning. Earlier work had paired random neural features with Mahalanobis scoring, but its tabular evaluation covered few datasets and its visual evaluation used flattened images with image-level scores, leaving dense localization untested (§I, §II-C).

## Approach and contributions

- **Shared framework.** PRISM freezes a randomly initialized linear operator in each block, followed by normalization and LeakyReLU. It estimates means and covariances from normal references and applies ridge-regularized Mahalanobis scoring (§III, Fig. 1).
- **Two detector designs.** PRISM-T uses one fully connected block with mixed instance and batch normalization and scores each row globally. PRISM-V combines multiscale random convolutional features with location-specific scoring, smooths the resulting pixel map, and aggregates its upper tail for image-level detection (§III-B, §V-A).
- **Benchmark and mechanism analysis.** The paper expands the earlier random-feature approach to broad tabular evaluation and dense visual localization. It decomposes the expected squared Mahalanobis score gap into mean and covariance terms and tests how activation, feature maps, and scoring rules affect performance (§IV, §V–VI).

The experiments establish that normal-reference detectors with frozen random features can be competitive on these benchmarks. The tabular tests support competitiveness, while statistical equivalence remains untested. The visual comparisons show that strong pixel ranking can coexist with weaker coverage of individual defect regions. The association between \(G\) and detection metrics is empirical. The theory explains changes in the expected score gap, while the effect on score dispersion and AUROC depends on the distributions (§IV-D, §VI-B–C).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The local anomaly model describes added variation with zero conditional mean and is explicitly stylized. The authors give no general guarantee that nonlinear features improve \(G\). Visual postprocessing also prevents an exact application of the quadratic mean–covariance decomposition to the final scores (§IV-B–D, §VI-C).
- **Author-observed.** PRISM-V sometimes responds to normal boundaries and surrounding structures, with diffuse localization in some multi-instance scenes. Increasing feature width also makes covariance estimation and inversion more expensive (§VI-C–D, Fig. 13).
- **Analyst inference.** Development datasets and categories come from the reported benchmark suite, so the aggregate results include cases used to select the configuration. Visual baselines retain their own preprocessing and resolutions, and three tabular baselines use capped fit sets on large datasets. These choices limit attribution of differences to representation learning alone (§V-A).
- **Analyst inference.** The normal-only construction protocol and two visual benchmarks leave robustness to reference contamination and transfer to other visual settings unestablished (§III-A, §V-A).

## Open research questions

- **When does a random nonlinearity improve anomaly ranking?** The theory decomposes the expected score gap, while score dispersion and rankings remain distribution-dependent. A next experiment could vary mean shifts and conditional variance shifts in controlled data and measure the gap, dispersion, \(G\), and AUROC together (§IV, §VI-B).
- **How much of the visual gap comes from learned weights?** The randomized-backbone comparison retains different scoring pipelines across methods. A next experiment could compare random and learned weights in the same architecture with identical preprocessing, features tapped, scoring, and postprocessing (§V-A, §VI-C).
- **How sensitive is PRISM to the size and quality of the normal reference set?** The reported protocols use prescribed normal-only splits. A next experiment could vary reference size and introduce controlled contamination, tracking detection performance and sensitivity to covariance regularization (§III-A–B, §V-A).
