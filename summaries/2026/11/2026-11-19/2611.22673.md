# FVScore: Specification-Driven Neuro-Symbolic Video Evaluation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22673) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22673)


## One-sentence takeaway

FVScore checks prompt requirements and video-implied constraints against timestamped evidence, yielding higher correlations with human ratings than the reported FVBench baselines for text–video alignment, physical commonsense, and audiovisual synchronization (§4.2, §C.2).

## Problem and motivation

Generated videos can look realistic while violating an action sequence, changing an object’s identity, or playing a sound before its visible cause. The authors argue that fixed metric suites and holistic multimodal judges often miss these failures or summarize them too broadly to diagnose. They identify a gap in deriving unstated constraints from each video and pinpointing when those constraints fail (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Video-conditioned specifications.** FVScore compiles explicit checks from the prompt and derives implicit checks from observed state transitions, entity continuity, and foreground sounds. Each proposition specifies a predicate, a temporal operator, and a verification route (§§3.1–3.2.1, Fig. 2).
- **Hybrid temporal verification and calibration.** Neural tools and scene-graph queries produce time-indexed evidence. Soft versions of *Always*, *Eventually*, and *Until* score each proposition independently. Perception-Aware Calibration (PAC) uses held-out examples to decide which violations matter to viewers. FVScore averages the retained proposition scores by dimension and generates a report grounded in timestamps and verification rationales (§3.2.2).
- **FVBench.** The authors introduce a benchmark of 200 compositional prompts and 400 videos from Veo 3.1 and Seedance 2.0, with consensus human ratings across four faithfulness dimensions. The prompts stress deadlines, interdependent events, and audio or cross-modal requirements (§4.1, §D.3).

The correlations support better agreement with human ratings in the listed FVBench comparisons. The report margins measure automated preferences with partial human validation. Timestamped examples illustrate localization (App. A, Fig. 3), but the supplied text gives no aggregate timestamp-accuracy measure or evidence that the reports improve subsequent generations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Soft temporal scores can downweight brief, severe violations. Verification inherits errors from the backbone and perception tools. Denser sampling sometimes introduced blur-related false violations, and text-level PAC can miss how visual context changes the importance of a defect (App. B, §§5.1, 5.3).
- **Analyst inference.** FVBench’s two generators and deliberately logic-heavy prompts limit what the results establish about broader prompt and generator distributions. The length-matched report audit covers FVBench alignment only, leaving the contribution of report length unresolved for other dimensions (§§C.3, D.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The PAC audit samples only propositions PAC excluded. Its reported metrics leave performance across the full set of accepted and rejected propositions unresolved (§5.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can verification preserve brief, severe failures while tolerating noisy frames?** Appendix B identifies this trade-off. Compare the current operators with severity-aware operators on clips with human-marked violation intervals, varying both defect duration and perceptual noise.
2. **Does visual context improve PAC?** The current calibration is text-level, and the audit covers excluded propositions (§5.3, App. B). Compare text-only and clip-conditioned calibration on a held-out sample of both retained and excluded propositions, with independent human salience labels.
3. **Do the FVBench advantages transfer to other generators and ordinary user prompts?** FVBench uses two generators and deliberately dense constraints (§D.3). Repeat the human-correlation and report comparisons across additional generators, stratifying natural prompts by temporal complexity.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The method and selected comparisons are described directly. The supplied Markdown omits full entries for several cited tables, including the FVBench correlation and core ablation grids, and does not establish uncertainty intervals or significance tests for the headline comparisons. Section 5.1 also mixes VideoFeedback and FVBench in its description of the densest-sampling result, so that numeric comparison is omitted here. Public availability of code and FVBench files, and end-to-end runtime, are unspecified.
