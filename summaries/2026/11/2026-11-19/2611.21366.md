# ALIVE: Making Streaming Video Models Interactive

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21366) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21366)


## One-sentence takeaway

ALIVE post-trains a vision-language model to monitor events, update active requests, and seek missing information as video unfolds, leading the compared models on four public benchmarks and both FSI-Bench protocols while trailing the reported human reference (Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation

The authors identify a gap between continuously observing video and sustaining an interaction. A streaming model can answer questions or monitor a fixed request, but an interactive assistant also needs to handle concurrent requests, honor cancellations and modifications, and ask for clarification or a user action when evidence is missing. Training these behaviors requires aligning user turns and model outputs with what the model could observe at each decision point (§§1, 3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task formulation and training data.** The authors define three task families and construct ALIVE-SFT-288K and ALIVE-RL-3K from public video clips paired with synthesized, timestamped interactions. They filter examples for appropriate strategy and causal answerability (§3, Table 1, Fig. 2).
- **Contrastive supervised fine-tuning.** Paired examples vary the interaction state to teach when to respond or stay silent, and vary available evidence or ambiguity to teach when to answer, clarify, request an action, or propose continued monitoring (§4.1, Fig. 3).
- **Behavior-Aligned Credit Assignment (BACA).** Reinforcement learning combines trajectory-level advantages with local advantages for strategy selection, task responses, and critical silence. The model starts from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, with the vision encoder and connector frozen (§§4.2, 5.1).
- **FSI-Bench.** The new benchmark scores the initial decision, response timing, and eventual answer across three feedback-seeking tasks and a direct-answer control, under explicit and free-form decision protocols (§5.2).

The reported results support the authors’ claim that the approach improves interactive behavior under the tested protocols. FSI-Bench Accuracy requires a suitable initial decision and a timely response before answer correctness earns credit. The evidence leaves reliability with live users and transfer to other base models unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** ALIVE needs external speech recognition and text-to-speech for spoken interaction. The authors also note the substantial gap to the human reference on free-form FSI-Bench (§6, Table 3).
- **Analyst inference.** FSI-Bench uses supplied feedback streams and LLM judging for free-form decisions and answer correctness. Scores may depend on the judge and provide limited evidence about whether live users can interpret and act on the model’s requests (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Table 4 tests the two contrast families together and the local credit units together. Their individual effects are unresolved. The experiments use one base model and report point estimates without run-to-run variability (§5.1, Tables 2–4).

## Open research questions

1. **Which training signals drive the gains?** Table 4 groups the contrast families and local credit units. Remove each component individually under matched training budgets and score strategy selection, task completion, and unwarranted responses separately.
2. **Do requests for clarification or action work with live users?** FSI-Bench supplies feedback through benchmark streams (§5.2). Run a live-user study that allows delayed, incomplete, or declined feedback and measures task completion and the number of follow-up turns.
3. **Does BACA help across base models?** The paper trains from one base model (§5.1). Repeat the matched trajectory-only versus BACA comparison on another vision-language backbone and report variability across training runs.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and reported benchmark rankings. The supplied text gives limited details about the human evaluation sample and procedure, judge agreement with human raters, run-to-run variability, and deployment latency or compute. It lists a code URL but does not establish public availability of the model weights, training datasets, or benchmark files.
