# BLIP-Live: Balancing Timeliness &amp; Firing Precision in Proactive Streaming Video Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21553) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21553)


## One-sentence takeaway

BLIP-Live aligns human-verified response timing, an evaluation metric that penalizes excess replies, and causal streaming inference and training, with BLIP-Live-Qwen scoring 49.08 PAUC-F versus MMDuet2’s 34.01 on ProactiveVideoQA (§6.1, Table 6).

## Problem and motivation

A streaming video assistant must decide when the frames seen so far justify answering a standing query and when to stay silent. Even a correct answer can lose value if it arrives late or keeps repeating. The authors identify gaps in timing supervision, metrics that overlook some redundant or out-of-window replies, and offline evaluations that can let models see frames beyond the timestamp they are answering for (§§1–2, §5.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **BLIP-Live-780K.** The authors generated candidate queries and response timelines from LLaVA-Video-178K source videos, then used human correction and expert batch audits to verify answer content, timing, and silence. The final training dataset contains 784,944 proactive responses across 177,789 videos (§3, Appendix C).
2. **PAUC-F.** The metric takes the harmonic mean of quality-weighted timeliness (PAUC) and firing precision. Each gold turn credits at most its earliest qualifying in-window reply, while every emitted reply counts toward the precision denominator (§4.2, Appendix D).
3. **Inference harness.** An agent-assisted search tunes a frozen vision-language model’s streaming interface on validation PAUC-F. The selected design combines a rolling window of past frames, interaction memory, a task prompt, and structured response-or-silence outputs (§5.1, Figure 3).
4. **Streaming fine-tuning.** BLIP-Live-Gemma and BLIP-Live-Qwen learn to predict Silence, Response, or Hold in causal episodes. Hold represents an ongoing event already reported, and loss masking reduces the influence of repeated non-response targets (§5.2, Figure 4).

The Hold ablation shows that firing selectivity can change sharply while PAUC stays nearly constant. The human study supports the preference claim for the two tested variants. Agreement between PAUC-F and human rankings across model families, statistical uncertainty around benchmark gains, and wall-clock performance in live use remain unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The preference study covers a single harness pair. Harness search converged on a similar design for two backbones, while stability across validation subsets and decision rates above 1 Hz remain untested (Appendices A and D).
- **Author-stated.** PO-I injects a new query only after the preceding gold response, so it tests switching after completed requests. On ESTP-Bench, several baselines outperform both BLIP-Live models on the Implicit and Contextual splits under ESTP-F1. The authors attribute this in part to longer gold windows that make repeat firing less costly (Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference.** Dataset generation exposed the model to full videos and relied on prompting plus human repair for causal timing. Residual hindsight is possible, and silent, visually unambiguous source clips limit what the results say about audio-dependent or ambiguous requests (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference.** Comparisons mix calibrated offline harnesses, native streaming settings, and some previously published scores. The fixed-backbone experiments reduce architecture confounding, but the supplied evidence does not quantify the effect of every protocol difference (§6, Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **Does PAUC-F predict human preferences across model families?** The study compares two harness variants. A blinded test across more model pairs could compare human rankings with PAUC-F, PAUC, and ESTP-F1.
2. **Do faster decisions catch brief events without increasing unnecessary replies?** The paper evaluates a 1 Hz cadence. Replay densely annotated clips at multiple decision rates and measure missed events, firing precision, and wall-clock latency.
3. **Can the assistant handle a new query while an earlier request is still pending?** PO-I only tests completed-request switches. Inject overlapping queries and score whether the model preserves each pending request and assigns replies to the right one.
