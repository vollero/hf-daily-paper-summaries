# Choosing Quantized Code LLMs by File Size and Energy per Solved Task

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17605) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17605)


## One-sentence takeaway

On a fanless MacBook Air M2, the 3.00 GB Qwen2.5-Coder 7B-q2_K file showed no significant solve-count gain over the 1.90 GB 3B-q4_K_M file on 542 EvalPlus tasks and used 2.03× as much CPU, GPU, and memory rail energy per solve (Tables I and III-C).

## Problem and motivation

Choosing a local code model under a download budget means choosing both a parameter count and a quantization recipe. The author identifies a gap in file-by-file guidance: a larger model at lower precision may score better, but its extra solves may come at a substantial energy cost, and a quantization tag can obscure the file’s effective precision (Secs. I–II).

## Approach and contributions

The study runs 24 shipped Qwen2.5-Coder-Instruct GGUF files across four model sizes on the same 542 Python EvalPlus tasks, using one zero-shot prompt and greedy decoding. It compares correctness with paired tests, divides pooled inference energy by the number of solved tasks, and identifies the files on the pass-rate–energy frontier (Sec. III).

- **Budget-indexed selection guide.** Ranks measured files by solves within each Ollama-listed size budget and labels the evidence behind the recommendations (Table II, Sec. V).
- **Repeated test of the 2-to-3 GB upgrade.** Compares the 2 GB pick with the smallest measured 7B file, then repeats their energy comparison in alternating HumanEval+ sessions (Sec. IV-C, Fig. 2).
- **Reproducible file-level measurements.** Reports effective bits per weight, pass rate, joules per solve, and energy per output token, with a harness and analysis script (Table I, Data Availability).

The strongest repeated evidence concerns the 3B-versus-7B energy gap on HumanEval+ on the study machine. The pooled correctness interval supports 3B non-inferiority at the author’s post hoc margin, while formal equivalence remains unestablished. The other frontier rankings rely on single sessions per benchmark, so their stability across sessions remains uncertain.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported measurement and session limits.** Power came from on-device estimates without an external meter. The main grid used a fixed order that confounds model size and recipe with time and accumulated load. The alternating repeat covered one pair on HumanEval+ in one night, and every pair was flagged for thermal pressure (Sec. VI).
- **Author-reported statistical and scope limits.** The non-inferiority margin and energy screening threshold were chosen after seeing the grid. Simultaneous non-inferiority evidence at 1.5B and 7B depended on the test. The study covered one family, runtime, machine, prompt, decoding setup, and programming language, and omitted registry files that could change the picks (Secs. III-E and VI).
- **Analyst inference.** Because the cross-size comparison changes both parameter count and quantization recipe, it establishes a trade-off between the shipped files. The independent contribution of effective precision to that trade-off remains unresolved (Secs. III-A and IV-C).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the 3B-to-7B energy ratio persist across hardware and workloads?** The repeat used one machine and HumanEval+ only. Repeat randomized blocks on both EvalPlus subsets across machines with different cooling designs, using an external meter alongside rail telemetry.
2. **Where does a larger model start to outperform a smaller one at similar file sizes?** The shipped-file grid couples parameter count, effective precision, and recipe. Build matched-size GGUF pairs with controlled quantization and calibration, then compare paired solve rates and repeated J/solve across several budgets.
3. **Do the picks change when a coding workflow samples multiple candidates?** Each task received one greedy sample. Compare the same files under a fixed sampling and test budget, counting the energy of every attempted candidate per task ultimately solved.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported file-level comparisons in the tested setup. The supplied text lacks between-session energy estimates for most comparisons, external-meter validation, and results for omitted registry variants, other machines, and alternative prompting or decoding setups. The paper reports a successful artifact rerun on the study machine. The supplied text does not include the artifact contents for independent verification.
