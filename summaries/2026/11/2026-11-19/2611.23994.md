# WaveView: The Novel View Is in the Noise

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23994) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23994)


## One-sentence takeaway

WaveView uses depth-warped wavelet phase to initialize diffusion, relaxing guidance along epipolar lines where depth is uncertain, and improves reported static novel-view synthesis on DL3DV and zero-shot dynamic synthesis on AnyViewBench (§§4.2–4.4, 5.2).

## Problem and motivation

Novel-view synthesis from one image or a monocular video must place observed content correctly in a target camera view and plausibly fill newly visible regions. The authors argue that explicit warp methods can carry reprojection errors into the output, while implicit camera conditioning can let the layout drift as viewpoint overlap decreases. They identify an opportunity to guide layout through the diffusion model’s initial noise and vary that guidance with the direction of reprojection uncertainty (§§1, 2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Geometry in the initial noise.** WaveView depth-warps the source into the target view and decomposes the warped latent and a Gaussian sample with a dual-tree complex wavelet packet transform. Complex-band magnitudes come from the noise, while selected phases come from the warp. The lowest real-valued packet carries standardized coarse warp content at visible locations, and disoccluded locations take noise coefficients (§§3, 4.3, Fig. 2).
- **Direction-dependent phase selection.** The method propagates local depth error through the target projection to estimate positional uncertainty along the epipolar line. It preferentially drops warp phase for frequencies along that line while retaining more across it. An isotropic floor also reduces high-frequency guidance in all directions (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Static and dynamic adaptation.** The static system adapts Qwen-Image-Edit with LoRA. The dynamic system applies the same noise construction per latent frame and fine-tunes AnyView while retaining its Plücker camera conditioning (§§4.4, 5.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Static benchmark gains | The authors report the best results in all three DL3DV overlap bins. On Hard, WaveView beats MetaView by 0.84 dB PSNR, 0.052 SSIM, and 0.030 lower LPIPS on covisible pixels. Whole-image gains are 0.59 dB PSNR, 0.033 SSIM, and 0.017 lower LPIPS. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Static runtime | On one B200, WaveView takes 17.9 s per view versus MetaView’s 38.0 s. Both use 40 sampling steps, guidance scale 4, and 960 × 528 resolution. Depth estimation and noise construction account for 0.46 s. | §5.2, Runtime |
| Zero-shot dynamic gains | On AnyViewBench zero-shot, WaveView has the best reported averages. Compared with AnyView, PSNR rises from 11.58 to 12.35 dB, SSIM from 0.331 to 0.372, and LPIPS falls from 0.621 to 0.587. | §5.2, Table 2 |
| In-distribution dynamic trade-off | On AnyViewBench in-distribution, WaveView averages 17.56 dB PSNR versus AnyView’s 17.61 dB, with slightly better SSIM and LPIPS. | §5.2, Table 2 |
| Dynamic training control | With matched fine-tuning settings, WaveView versus the Gaussian-noise control achieves 12.35 vs. 11.35 dB PSNR, 0.372 vs. 0.327 SSIM, and 0.587 vs. 0.641 LPIPS on AnyViewBench zero-shot. | §5.2 |
| Direction and band phase matter | On DL3DV Hard covisible pixels at the default settings, WaveView exceeds the trained isotropic variant by 0.24 dB PSNR and 0.014 SSIM, and the inference-time rotated variant by 0.50 dB and 0.025. The trained low-pass-only variant scores 12.85 dB PSNR, 1.97 dB below WaveView. | §5.4, Figs. 5–6 |

The covisible scores measure fidelity where the source provides correspondence. The directional ablation supports epipolar phase selection in the static Hard setting, and the matched dynamic control supports a contribution from structured noise beyond additional fine-tuning. Plausibility in disoccluded regions, temporal consistency, and directional phase selection’s specific contribution to video remain unmeasured by these comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** The covariance models local depth error along a ray. Global depth-scale errors, inaccurate cameras, and surfaces incorrectly hidden by the warp fall outside that model. Where the source provides no pixels, content comes entirely from the generator’s learned distribution (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** The dynamic Gaussian-noise control tests the full structured initialization. The reported directionality and low-pass ablations are confined to DL3DV Hard, so their individual effects on video remain uncertain (§§5.2, 5.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The published AnyView comparison involves different training coverage, and the fisheye result uses known dataset calibration. The matched fine-tuning control addresses training as an explanation for the aggregate noise comparison, while broader camera-model robustness remains open (§§4.4, 5.1–5.2).

## Open research questions

- **How does WaveView respond to depth-scale and camera-calibration errors?** The covariance omits both error sources. Independently perturb depth scale, camera poses, and intrinsics, then measure covisible fidelity across overlap bins (§§4.2, 6).
- **Does epipolar directionality improve temporal consistency?** The dynamic control compares structured and Gaussian noise, while directionality is ablated on static scenes. Train matched anisotropic and isotropic AnyView variants and measure both frame fidelity and consistency along tracked points (§§5.2, 5.4).
- **How plausible and consistent are disocclusion completions?** Whole-image scores combine observed and newly visible content. Evaluate disocclusion masks separately using blinded plausibility judgments and consistency across nearby target views (§§5.1–5.2, 6).
