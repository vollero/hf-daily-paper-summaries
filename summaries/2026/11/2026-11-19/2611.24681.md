# DrawScore: Evaluating Generated CAD Models against Engineering Drawings

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24681) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24681)


## One-sentence takeaway

DrawScore compares visible and hidden curves in three orthographic views and detects some small CAD feature errors that volumetric IoU scores as nearly perfect (§§3, 5.2, Fig. 5).

## Problem and motivation

A missing hole or a slot on the wrong face can matter for manufacturing while changing only a small fraction of a part’s volume or surface area. The authors argue that standard geometric metrics give these errors too little weight and that engineering drawings make some of them easier to detect because the features leave distinct projected curves (§1, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Metric analysis.** The authors express geometric discrepancies as normalized integrals and separate each local error’s weight into its share of the integration domain and its average penalty. Equation 4 predicts a decay order of \(k+q\) when the affected measure and penalty scale with those exponents. A saturated penalty contributes no extra decay exponent (§2).
- **DrawScore.** The metric projects edge curves and smooth contours into front, top, and right views. It matches curves of the same visibility using a Gaussian saturating distance penalty, computes arc-length-weighted precision and recall, and takes the lowest per-view harmonic mean (§3).
- **Empirical uses.** The authors test controlled defects and existing agent outputs, then evaluate DrawScore as a reinforcement-learning reward and as a drawing-based test-time verifier (§§5.1–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled decay matched the predicted orders | On a removed blind-hole radius sweep, the fitted log–log slopes for all five metrics were within 0.2 of their predicted \(k+q\). | §5.1, Fig. 3 |
| Projected curves captured defects missed by specific baselines | A smooth waist left reported Edge Chamfer Distance at 0.006 while DrawScore fell to 79.55%. A 0.9 mm bolt-hole shift left distance-only surface F-score at 100% while DrawScore fell to 95.94%. | §§4.2, 5.1, Fig. 4 |
| High IoU often coexisted with lower DrawScore | Across 1,188 valid outputs from six agent configurations, 190 of 1,007 outputs with IoU ≥ 0.95 had DrawScore < 0.95. In the reverse comparison, 10 of 827 outputs with DrawScore ≥ 0.95 had IoU < 0.95. | §§4.1, 5.2, Fig. 5 |
| DrawScore reward improved validity | On 153 held-out Fusion360 models, Qwen3.5-4B validity rose from 75.2% after supervised fine-tuning to 89.5% after DrawScore reinforcement learning. The authors also report improvements in all geometric metrics listed for that run and improvements over the intermediate validity-only checkpoint when training continued with DrawScore. | §1, §5.3, Table 2 |
| Drawing-based reranking recovered part of the oracle gain | On 20 valid BenchCAD Public T1 cases with eight generated candidates each and simulated vector drawings, DrawScore selection recovered 4.7%, 17.2%, and 13.0% of the per-metric oracle gains over the mean valid-candidate baseline for IoU, Chamfer Distance, and surface F-score, respectively. | §5.4, Table 3 |
| Rankings were fairly stable across the tested tolerance settings | When tolerance and its linked angular averaging cone varied together from \(0.001L\) to \(0.02L\), fitted blind-hole decay orders stayed at 0.96–0.97. On the 1,188 valid agent outputs, Spearman correlation with default DrawScore was at least 0.84. | §7.1, Fig. 6 |

The controlled examples and inspected agent outputs support using DrawScore alongside volumetric metrics to catch small feature defects. The training and reranking gains apply to the tested setups. The evidence leaves manufacturing acceptability, performance on real drawings, and sensitivity across other defect families open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Three views can conceal internal features when hidden lines overlap. The proposed section-view extension remains unevaluated. Raster drawings require vectorization, and repeated hidden-line removal makes DrawScore slower than the comparison metrics (§§7.2–7.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The fitted decay evidence comes from a removed blind hole. For nearby matching curves with displacements far below the tolerance, Eq. 5 gives an approximately quadratic pointwise penalty, so linear error-weight decay should not be assumed down to arbitrarily small displacements (§§2.4, 3.2, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The reward experiment uses scale-normalized scoring for one model, which leaves absolute dimensional accuracy and transfer to other generators uncertain. The reranking experiment uses one generator configuration and clean vector projections, which leaves performance on real drawings uncertain (§§4.3, 5.3–5.4).

## Open research questions

1. **When does linear decay hold for small feature errors?** The fitted sweep uses a removed blind hole. Sweep the size of missing features and the displacement of existing features across the positional tolerance, then fit decay slopes in each regime (§§2.4, 3.2, 5.1).
2. **How well does reranking transfer to real engineering drawings?** The test used vector projections of reference solids. Compare rankings on those projections, engineer-authored vector drawings, and vectorized scans for the same parts (§§5.4, 7.3).
3. **How much do section views improve detection of internal defects?** The extension is proposed without an evaluation. Construct matched parts with different internal geometry and measure detection gains and runtime as section views are added (§7.2).
