# Code World Model 2.0: Towards Infinite Controllable Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22925) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22925)


## One-sentence takeaway

Invoker-Render uses the same coarse-to-fine proxy interface for offline video creation and four-step causal streaming, with the streaming model leading three baselines on a short-rollout preference benchmark and showing qualitative stability in extended racing sessions.

## Problem and motivation

A video renderer driven by an upstream simulator must handle varying levels of structural detail. Coarse proxies are cheaper to construct and transmit but leave motion such as limb articulation unspecified, while fine proxies can prescribe that motion directly. Interactive streaming adds two constraints: future inputs are unavailable, and errors can accumulate as the renderer conditions on its own output. The authors identify a gap in supporting both proxy granularities with one checkpoint per rendering mode while keeping causal generation fast and stable (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Shared proxy conditioning.** Invoker-Render adapts MiniMax-H3 with Guidance-Free Training. Both checkpoints accept structural proxies, an appearance reference, and text through the same interface. The offline model sees the whole clip’s conditions, while the streaming model sees only conditions available through the current block (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Progressive Forcing.** The curriculum adapts the model to causal attention using clean and then resampled video histories. It distills a four-step student using distribution matching and a teacher-feature adversarial loss, then carries the student, trainable score model, discriminator, and optimizer state into training on continuous self-generated histories (§§4.3–4.4, Fig. 4).
- **Aligned training corpus.** The authors assemble approximately 200 hours of unique source footage, about 150 hours self-collected and 50 hours from public subsets. Where both proxy variants are available, they describe the same event and share the same RGB target. Text annotations cover whole clips and timestamped local actions (§§3.1–3.3, Figs. 2–3).
- **Streaming serving stack.** A lightweight proxy encoder, reused attention-cache workspaces, fused prefill and denoising, and overlapped encoding and decoding reduce the steady-state chunk period (§5, Figs. 5–6).

The preference results support a ranking under the reported model-judge protocol. The serving measurements establish steady-state performance for the tested configuration. The racing examples support feasibility over extended sessions, while reliability across environments, indefinite consistency, and end-to-end response time in a closed interaction loop remain unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated limitations.** The sliding attention cache eventually evicts visual details outside its active window. Training holds proxy granularity fixed within each rollout window, and closed-loop integration with interactive state engines remains future work (§7).
- **Analyst inference.** The short-rollout ranking depends on one automated judge. Human agreement and uncertainty intervals are unreported, and the long-horizon evidence is qualitative and confined to racing (§§6.1–6.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The baseline comparison measures the complete renderer. Without controlled training ablations, it cannot isolate the effects of resampled histories, adversarial supervision, or transferring the critics and optimizer state into self-forcing (§§4.3–4.4, 6.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Which Progressive Forcing stages most reduce drift?** The paper compares complete renderers. Train compute-matched variants that omit resampled-history distillation or reset the critics at the self-forcing transition, then measure structural and reference fidelity over time.
2. **Can proxy detail change mid-stream without a visible discontinuity?** Training fixes granularity within each rollout window (§7). Switch between matched coarse and fine proxies mid-action and measure pose, identity, and temporal continuity around the switch.
3. **Can the renderer recall a scene after it leaves the attention window?** The racing examples do not test delayed revisits (§§6.3, 7). Compare the sliding cache with a persistent memory mechanism on routes that revisit locations and entities after controlled delays.
