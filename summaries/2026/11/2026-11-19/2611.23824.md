# Evidence-Based Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23824) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23824)


## One-sentence takeaway

Adding training-only credit based on how retrieved documents change reference-answer likelihood, ECA-Horizon improves strict macro exact match over matched outcome-only GRPO by 1.03 percentage points at 3B and 4.80 at 7B across seven Search QA benchmarks (§5.1, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Outcome-only reinforcement learning assigns the same advantage to every generated token in a rollout, so a useful search receives the same credit as a redundant one. When every rollout for a question fails, the group-centered outcome advantage is zero even if some rollouts found useful evidence. The authors identify a gap in existing likelihood-based approaches: scoring the full dialogue lets generated reasoning and query phrasing change the score even when retrieved evidence stays fixed (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evidence-only scoring.** A frozen snapshot of the current policy scores a canonical reference answer under teacher forcing, conditioned on the question and deduplicated retrieved documents. The score excludes generated reasoning and queries. It uses answer labels without requiring supporting-passage annotations or a separate reward model (§3.2, Figure 2).
- **Two ways to credit searches.** ECA-Step clips each likelihood change before summing the remaining gains. ECA-Horizon uses the net change from immediately before a search to the end of the horizon, preserving cumulative gains before normalization (§3.3, Figure 3).
- **Search-local policy updates.** ECA normalizes each return against the opposite rollout fold and withholds positive auxiliary credit when a search adds no new document IDs. It applies the bounded correction to the query and its preceding reasoning, while final-answer tokens receive only outcome credit (§§3.4–3.5).

The matched GRPO comparison isolates the addition of the auxiliary credit mechanism as a whole under the reported setup (§3.5). The query-only result shows that frequent recovery of a nonzero signal can coexist with lower accuracy. The ablations leave the independent effect of novelty gating unresolved, and the reported point estimates leave run-to-run stability uncertain.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** Evaluation is confined to search-augmented QA. The scorer adds training-time forward passes. A single answer alias can undervalue bridge passages, and document-ID deduplication can miss near-duplicate text (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The larger gain at 7B could reflect the scorer, the acting policy, or their interaction. Both change together, so the authors’ explanation that the larger model scores evidence better remains untested (§§3.2, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The broadcast ablation changes both token placement and local gating, so it cannot isolate either effect. Published TIPS and Search-R1 scores provide reference points with less experimental control than the reproduced baselines (§4, §§5.1–5.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does evidence-only scoring improve learning over full-dialogue scoring?** The likelihood-shaping comparisons do not hold the rest of the pipeline fixed. Compare the two prompts with the same return, gating, and training budget (§2, §§4–5.1).
2. **How much of the 7B gain comes from the scorer?** The actor and scorer scale together. Cross 3B and 7B actors with 3B and 7B scorers under a matched training protocol (§§3.2, 5.1).
3. **Does novelty gating improve accuracy independently of credit placement?** The broadcast ablation changes both. Toggle the gate with the search-local token mask fixed and measure strict EM, duplicate queries, and repeated passages (§§5.2–5.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the described method and the point estimates stated in the supplied prose. The text omits full numerical rows for several tables, including the 3B RLOO macro EM, and does not report seed-to-seed variability, measured training overhead, scoring-overflow frequency or downstream handling, or whether code and checkpoints were released.
