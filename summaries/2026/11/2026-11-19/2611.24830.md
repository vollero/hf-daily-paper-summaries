# A Foundation Model to Measure and Predict Human Behaviour

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24830) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24830)


## One-sentence takeaway

Chiron jointly trains one language model to score psychological constructs, predict structured individual responses, and simulate experimental cohorts, improving on the tested baselines on self-report agreement, mean prediction accuracy, and effect-size error while scoring lower on significance F1.

## Problem and motivation

Behavioural science needs interpretable measures of individual differences and reliable predictions of how people respond across situations. The authors argue that general-purpose language models compress psychological scores and simulated response variance, while existing approaches often treat construct measurement and individual prediction as separate tasks. Scarce psychometric labels and limited access to participant-level experimental records make calibration harder. (§§1–2)

## Approach and contributions

1. **Direct psychometric readout.** A centred cosine head scores 109 predefined constructs from the hidden state at the end of a person’s records. Human self-reports, scores from withheld questionnaire items, and synthetic examples supervise the head and shared representation. (§§3.2–3.3.1)
2. **Joint training with masked observation prediction.** Chiron adapts Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with LoRA and shares its backbone across all three objectives. For unlabelled writing, it extracts quote-supported observations and turns withheld observations into multiple-choice prediction targets. (§§3.3.2, 3.3.4, B.2)
3. **Constrained population synthesis.** The method reweights and minimally edits archived model responses to fit published human statistics. Multiple synthesized answer vectors for each condition then supervise the model’s response distribution. (§3.3.3, Appendix B.3)

The results support better average benchmark performance for the jointly trained model. Self-report agreement and recovery of literature correlations provide different kinds of validity evidence, and direct agreement with self-reports was evaluated for nine constructs. The authors attribute the lower significance F1 partly to a benchmark dominated by significant findings and report that Chiron correctly identified more human null results. The population evaluation compares simulated cohorts with reported human summaries, leaving respondent-level fidelity and prospective validity open. (§§4.1–4.3, 5)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated boundaries.** Chiron scores a fixed inventory, predicts structured choices, ratings, and categories, and treats records as static snapshots. The authors frame simulation as a tool for hypothesis generation and pretesting that requires confirmation with human subjects. (§5)
- **Analyst inference — component attribution.** The supplied experiments compare the full model with baselines. They report no matched ablations isolating the measurement objective, centred readout, masked observation task, or cohort synthesis. In Fig. 5, a shared input manipulation can move scores and predictions together without showing that the readout causally mediates prediction. (§§3.3–4.3)
- **Analyst inference — scope of transfer.** HEXACO, MACH-IV, and RIASEC were excluded from prediction training but appear in measurement training. The held-out result therefore tests transfer across objectives for those sources. (§4.2, Table 2)
- **Analyst inference — comparison and calibration.** Baseline refusals were excluded or left unimputed in several evaluations, and prediction malformed outputs were scored as zero. Rankings under a common failure-scoring policy are unreported. Population NMAE summarizes mean and SD errors, leaving item-level dependence untested by that metric. (§§4.1.1, 4.2–4.3, C.3.2)

## Open research questions

1. **How much does psychometric supervision improve prediction?** The reported comparisons leave its contribution unresolved. Train matched models with and without the measurement loss and compare them on instruments excluded from every training objective. (§§3.3.4, 4.2, Table 2)
2. **Does cohort synthesis preserve individual response patterns?** The population benchmark tests aggregate agreement. Use consented participant-level data from new studies to evaluate item correlations and response-tail frequencies while training on aggregate statistics alone. (§§B.3, C.3.2)
3. **Can the model distinguish stable traits from transient states?** The current input is a static snapshot. Compare timestamp-aware and static versions on repeated assessments and later responses from the same participants. (§5)

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and reported benchmark summaries. The supplied text does not establish public availability of Chiron weights, code, or generated datasets. It also omits several verbatim prompts referenced in Appendices B–C and reports no matched component ablations or results under a common policy for scoring baseline failures.
