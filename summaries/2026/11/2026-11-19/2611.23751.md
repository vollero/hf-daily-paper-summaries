# Thinking Fast, Anywhere: One System-1 for the Decision Points of Any Agent Workflow

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23751) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23751)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across three held-out agent benchmarks and two System-2 backbones, AnyS1 combined workflow compilation with confidence-gated decisions from a single 4B model, cutting episode cost by 75–91% and mean critical-path model-call time by 63–92% versus uncompiled agents under warm calibration, with small average effectiveness differences from compiled All-S2 (§4.2, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

LLM agents incur cost and latency when they call a large model at every step, including routine choices among known options. The authors identify a transfer gap in prior dual-system agents: engineers define decision hooks and collect supervision for each workflow. AnyS1 uses branches and checks already written into procedural skills as a source of decisions that one smaller model can learn across workflows (§§1–2, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Skill-grounded decision mining and Any-DP.** The pipeline extracts finite-choice decision templates grounded in skill text, runs verified synthetic tasks in isolated containers, and labels the resulting observations using agreement between two teacher passes with reversed option order (§3.2, Table 1, Appendix A).
2. **One calibrated model across workflows.** A label-smoothed pointer model scores candidate options in one forward pass. Compiled workflows execute deterministic steps as code, send open-ended generation to System-2, and use System-2 as a fallback for low-confidence choices. Cold mode transfers a fixed threshold, while warm mode fits the threshold to System-2 decisions on target development episodes (§§3.1–3.3).
3. **Separate comparisons for compilation and delegation.** The evaluation compares raw-skill agents with compiled All-S2 and then measures the additional effect of selective delegation. Ablations test the gate, the source of training observations, and the calibration loss (§§4.2–4.5).

The reported results support incremental savings from delegation after compilation, with small average effectiveness changes across the tested workflows. The averages mask individual declines, including ALFWorld success under both AnyS1 modes. Statistical non-inferiority remains unestablished, model-call time covers only the model portion of episode latency, and System-2 agreement measures imitation rather than independently verified branch correctness (§§4.1–4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Warm calibration uses about 50 development episodes per workflow with System-2 answering every enumerable decision. The large episode-level savings depend on explicit workflow compilation. Automatic compilation of open-ended repository skills and online threshold adaptation remain future work (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The training-state ablation changes corpus size as well as observation source, so the accuracy gain applies to the tested pipelines and cannot be attributed to state source alone (§4.3, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference.** Consensus filtering favors cases where the two teacher passes agree, and benchmark calibration uses System-2 as the reference. These measurements leave correctness on disputed or confidently wrong decisions uncertain (§§3.2–3.3, 4.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The tested settings support transfer across the three compiled benchmarks. Generalization to broader workflows and realized latency under different serving conditions remain uncertain. The reported cost estimates use API list rates and L4 throughput assumptions, while local decision latency was measured on an H100 (§4.1, Appendix B).

## Open research questions

1. **Can a compiler recover usable decision points from open-ended skills?** The evaluation uses structured SOPs and benchmark-specific skill programs. Compile held-out repository skills automatically and compare workflow fidelity, task success, and episode cost with manually checked programs (Appendix B, Limitations).
2. **Can thresholds adapt online without dedicated development runs?** The fixed cold threshold yields different coverage and agreement across workflows. Compare cold and warm thresholds with an online update rule that learns from deferred decisions and periodically audits accepted decisions (§4.5, Limitations).
3. **How much of the accuracy gain comes from executed observations themselves?** The published ablation changes both source and corpus size. Train on matched sets of executed observations and LLM-written summaries with the same templates, labels, and example counts, then evaluate on held-out raw observations (Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported method and point estimates. The supplied text lacks uncertainty intervals or a formal non-inferiority analysis for the online results, independently verified branch-level labels for the agreement analyses, and a direct Any-DP download link or confirmation that trained weights and compiled workflows are available. Several ablation tables are represented by captions and selected results in prose, leaving some per-setting values unavailable.
