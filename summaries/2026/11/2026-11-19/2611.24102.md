# IDEA: Intent-Dominance Evidence Audit for Inference-Time Backdoor Detection in GUI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24102) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24102)


## One-sentence takeaway

IDEA audits GUI-agent actions before execution using instruction redirects and screen-region ablations, detecting 89.8–100% of successful backdoor activations across five development families at a 1% target clean false-positive rate (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

A downloaded GUI-agent checkpoint can pass clean acceptance tests while an implanted visual trigger redirects actions at runtime. The authors identify a gap in inference-time detection for individual GUI actions, since prior detectors primarily target image classifiers or encoders. They ask whether a defender with white-box access and clean examples can detect activation before execution and return a suspect region without knowing the trigger or attacker’s target (Secs. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Step-level detection and attribution.** The authors propose *intent–evidence dominance inversion* as a signature: the instruction has less influence than expected, or a screen region has more influence than expected (Secs. 1, 3).
2. **Intervention-based audit with clean-only calibration.** A Mahalanobis gate screens the decision state. For steps that pass, Intent Counterfactual Probing measures hidden-state shifts under valid instruction redirects, and Region Causal Audit measures the largest shift under candidate-region ablation. IDEA compares both effects with clean steps at a similar distance from the clean mean, scores deviations in either direction of interest, and applies a split-conformal threshold. Each alarm returns the region whose ablation moved the state most (Sec. 4, Algorithm 1).
3. **Comparative evaluation.** The authors build a five-family AndroidControl evaluation with 13 adapted baselines under the main protocol, then freeze the score before testing two held-out victims (Secs. 5–5.2, Table 1).

The evidence supports pre-execution detection of successful activations in the tested setting, with a large gain over a distance-only screen on development Grounding. Each blind victim had a different leading comparator. The conformal guarantee is marginal over exchangeable clean steps and calibration draws. An alarm indicates an anomalous decision response, and attributing it to an implanted backdoor requires corroboration (Sec. 4.3, Appendices C and G).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported:** The score was finalized after inspecting the five development families, making the two frozen-score victims the blind evidence. The full suite uses one 3B backbone, and supplementary Patch-only runs on larger backbones used an overlapping split and exceeded the clean FPR target (Sec. 5.2, Appendices J–K, Table 19).
- **Author-reported:** IDEA requires white-box access. Diffuse triggers provide little meaningful spatial attribution, and steps with too few valid redirects use a clean median fallback. The robustness of the audit against an attacker targeting the detection statistic remains untested (Secs. 4.1, 5.4, Appendices I–J).
- **Author-reported:** Clean and triggered examples differ in their relationship to victim-training episodes. Deployment shifts can also break exchangeability, and the tested benign shifts barely changed gate passage (Sec. 5, Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Matching clean and triggered examples by episode provenance would better isolate the contribution of activation from possible provenance effects. Proposition 1 bounds action-likelihood contrasts under its assumptions, so the corresponding hidden-state behavior remains an empirical hypothesis (Sec. 4.3, Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does episode provenance affect detection?** The main protocol draws positives and negatives from different episode pools. Score paired clean and triggered views of the same unseen steps with a frozen detector and separate clean calibration.
2. **How stable is clean FPR across deployment shifts?** The reported benign shifts barely change gate passage. Evaluate the frozen thresholds on clean cohorts collected across app updates, themes, and layouts, and report app-cluster intervals and gate passage for each cohort.
3. **Can the score transfer across unrelated GUI backbones with the same calibration budget?** The full suite uses one backbone, with limited blind backbone evidence. Freeze the protocol and compare IDEA, the screen, and the log-ratio across independently developed vision-language backbones on matched tasks and clean-data budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported main-protocol metrics and calibration scope. The supplied text does not establish public availability of code, checkpoints, or exact split manifests, and it does not give measured clean FPR for every held-out comparator. Broader blind evidence across unrelated backbones and materially shifted deployment distributions is unavailable.
