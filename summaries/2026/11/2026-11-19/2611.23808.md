# PReMind: When Personal Memory Meets Visual Hazards

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23808) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23808)


## One-sentence takeaway

On 140 benchmark cases where the target memory was retrievable, PReMind’s two-phase reinforcement learning increased 4B on-demand joint success from 12.9% to 28.6% under GPT-6-Luna scoring, while the effect on critical harm remained uncertain (Table S.6, §S.6).

## Problem and motivation

A visual assistant may recognize a physical hazard yet give inappropriate advice if it overlooks the person’s allergy, mobility constraint, or other relevant fact. It must decide whether to retrieve memory, identify whose facts apply, and address the visible hazard even when personal evidence is missing. The authors identify a gap in the evaluations surveyed in Table 1, which do not jointly score these decisions and the resulting advice (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark.** PReMind-Bench varies hazard presence, memory need, and target availability through paired cases. It scores retrieval decisions, grounding, abstention, and hazard response separately, then measures joint success on cases requiring both hazard guidance and personal adaptation (§4, Fig. 2).
- **Training method.** PReMind mixes on-demand and forced-call episodes in both phases of DAPO-style reinforcement learning. Phase I trains memory access, grounding, abstention, and hazard response. Phase II adds difficulty ordering, replay of fact-use failures, a bonus for stating and citing the decisive fact, and credit for leading with a scene-specific action. Memory-Informed Effect guides replay selection (§5, §S.3).
- **Diagnostic analysis.** Safety-only and personalization-only training leave complementary failures. Forced-call evaluation and visual ablations probe whether models use the evidence they receive after retrieval (Fig. 4, Tables S.13 and S.20).

Joint success measures whether written advice meets the benchmark’s hazard and personal-context criteria. The forced-call result supports better use of retrieved evidence beyond the decision to call memory. The results leave real-world harm reduction, clinical correctness, and overall human preference unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The test covers four edited capture scenes, uses fictitious memories and English prompts, and lacks independent clinical validation. Primary automated judges see annotated scene descriptions. Luna also supplies training rewards, and Sonnet rescoring uses a different input format (§7, §§S.2.3 and S.6).
- **Author-stated.** Retrieval uses small stores, and every scored interdependency case has the target in the top four. The Phase II seeds share a Phase I checkpoint. Outdated, conflicting, and adversarial memories are outside the threat model (§§S.1.3, S.3.4 and S.6).
- **Analyst inference.** The 4B headline gain may overstate performance in settings with more retrieval misses. The study conditions that estimate on target retrieval and evaluates absent-target behavior separately (§S.1.3, Table S.13).
- **Analyst inference.** The study gives stronger evidence for the combined recipe than for the individual JSR contribution of curriculum ordering or replay. Their matched intervals include zero, and the regime ablations use single seeds with imperfectly matched replay selection (§S.3.4).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does the policy work when retrieval often misses?** The headline set contains only target-retrieved cases from small stores. Vary store size and look-alike density, retain retrieval misses, and report end-to-end joint success alongside abstention and hazard adequacy (§S.1.3, §S.6).
2. **Does the assistant reliably change its advice when the decisive fact changes?** The exploratory paired-fact diagnostic had few clearly applicable pairs and found no measurable advantage. Build same-image, same-request pairs with independently reviewed condition-specific references and score both answers (§S.6).
3. **Can training preserve adequacy while reducing false alarms and critical harm?** PReMind over-flags safe requests, and harm comparisons vary by judge. Compare calibration strategies across independent full training runs using blinded, image-aware expert review of safe and hazardous cases (§7, §S.6).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported 4B benchmark gain over the base. Confidence in real-world safety and transfer remains limited by the evaluation scope. The supplied text omits numerical cells from several referenced tables, including the full main-test and forced-call breakdowns, and provides summaries of the training and evaluation judge prompts. It also lacks independent clinical validation, full-pipeline seed variability, and supplied-memory 9B results beyond seed 0. The paper describes planned benchmark access, while current artifact availability is unverified (§§S.3.4, S.4, S.6, S.7 and S.9.3).
