# AVTR: Adaptive Multimodal Reasoning with Visual Imagination

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24161) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24161)


## One-sentence takeaway

AVTR learns to choose among direct answering, latent visual reasoning, textual reasoning, and their combination, raising the reported mean score across 16 benchmarks from 65.54 to 70.08 while reducing average latency from 13.2 to 8.9 seconds per query versus Qwen3.5-9B with thinking enabled (Figure 6).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that textual reasoning can only do so much when a vision–language model has missed the visual evidence needed to answer a question. On MMSI-Bench, enabling thinking raises Qwen3.5-9B accuracy from 34.7% to 35.0% (§1, Table 1). Uniformly generating a reasoning trace also adds latency when direct answering or a brief visual check would suffice. The gap is a way to recover question-relevant visual evidence and decide per input which kind of reasoning to use (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Question-conditioned visual states.** Learnable queries within the VLM first learn to reconstruct the input through a training-time generator, then learn to represent a question-relevant image crop or video frame. Joint visual and answer supervision teaches the VLM to use the resulting state. At inference, the VLM processes the queries in parallel and uses their continuous states directly, without decoding pixels (§§3.1–3.2, Figure 2).
- **Learned routing across modalities.** Two token gates select direct, visual, textual, or visual-then-textual reasoning. Training samples all four routes and supervises the gates with soft targets based on answer correctness minus route cost. Route-conditioned GRPO updates reasoning and answer tokens using rewards centered within each route (§3.3).

The within-model comparisons support two conclusions under the reported protocol. The learned visual state helps answer questions, and per-question routing improves average accuracy over the tested fixed routes. On the representative subtasks shown, the visual route favors geometry and viewpoint judgments, while the textual route favors tasks such as counting and sequencing (Figure 3, Appendix F.2). Because Figure 3 selects subtasks with large performance gaps, it gives limited evidence about how common these advantages are across all questions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported evaluation constraints.** Thinking models are capped at 8,192 new tokens, below the backbone’s official 224K long-video setting. A capped run without a valid answer falls back to the backbone’s direct prediction. The reported comparisons apply to this capped protocol (§4.2, Appendix B).
- **Author-observed failure mode.** In a BLINK counting example, visual imagination changes count-relevant details and gives the wrong answer. The textual route answers correctly (Figure 11).
- **Analyst inference on training coverage.** Reflection targets are image crops or single video frames and are retained when appending them improves the base VLM’s answer or temporal IoU. This selection may favor evidence that can be isolated in one region or frame. Fixed-route and visual-state interventions omit temporal grounding, so the source of the grounding gains is less directly tested (Appendix C, Tables 2, 4 and 10).
- **Analyst inference on generalization.** Both tested backbones belong to Qwen3.5, and the smaller model’s reported evaluation omits temporal grounding. Transfer to other model families and smaller-model grounding performance remain open (§4.1, Table 3).

## Open research questions

- **What drives the temporal-grounding gains?** The main comparison reports higher mIoU, while the route and visual-state ablations omit grounding. Run all fixed routes, adaptive routing, and visual-state replacements on the four grounding benchmarks under the same protocol.
- **Can visual states preserve evidence distributed across time?** Reflection supervises a single salient video frame. Compare single-frame and multi-frame targets under matched training budgets on questions that require evidence from multiple separated moments.
- **Do the accuracy–latency gains hold at other generation budgets?** The main result uses one token cap with a direct-answer fallback. Sweep the cap for AVTR and backbone thinking, and report accuracy, latency, and fallback frequency by benchmark.
