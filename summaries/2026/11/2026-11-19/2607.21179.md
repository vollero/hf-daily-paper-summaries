# Out of Sight, Still in Mind: Token Compression for Omni-LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21179) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21179)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReMo uses aligned audio embeddings and spatially grounded object labels to cut Qwen2.5-Omni’s full input token count by 54% before decoding while scoring 101.2% and 101.3% of the uncompressed models’ normalized five-benchmark averages at 3B and 7B, respectively (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Visual patch tokens dominate audio–video–text inputs, driving decoder attention costs in latency and memory. The authors identify a gap in training-free, input-level compression. Decoder-side pruning still processes the full sequence in early layers, while input-level methods such as OmniZip estimate redundancy using raw audio and video embeddings whose spaces are poorly aligned (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Cross-modal redundancy scoring.** ReMo computes a regularized canonical projection offline from paired VGGSound embeddings. Before decoding, it ranks visual tokens by dissimilarity to both the projected chunk audio mean and the chunk visual mean, then keeps the highest-scoring tokens (§4.1, §5.1).
- **Spatially grounded text proxies.** ReMo embeds YOLOv8n-seg object-class labels as text tokens and places them at the corresponding mask centroids and chunk times using TMRoPE. A matched-budget comparison tests these proxies against restoring visual tokens from the same detected regions (§4.2, Table 7).
- **Visual detail recovery and temporal merging.** ReMo restores discarded tokens that differ most from their four spatial neighbors and merges similar, co-located tokens across adjacent chunks using saliency-weighted fusion (§4.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-Omni improves on the normalized aggregate | At 54% full-input compression, ReMo scores 101.2% (3B) and 101.3% (7B), versus 100% for Full Token and 98.7% and 97.6% for OmniZip at the same compression. The average covers four understanding benchmarks and one captioning benchmark, using an inverse ratio for the lower-is-better captioning score. | §5.1, Table 1 |
| Some comparisons favor other methods | On 7B Video-MME, ReMo scores 77.9 versus 78.8 for Full Token. On 3B video-SALMONN2, ReMo improves on Full Token (52.9 versus 53.5), while OmniZip scores 52.4 on the same lower-is-better metric. | Table 1 |

| Projection improves paired retrieval | On VGGSound test audio-to-video retrieval, averaged over 50 sampling seeds, raw-to-projected R@1 rises from 2.7 to 31.3 at 3B and from 5.0 to 35.6 at 7B. Median rank falls from 62 to 4 and from 52 to 3, respectively. | §5.3, Table 5 |
| Text proxies help under a matched token budget | At 54% compression on 3B, proxies score 47.8 on WorldSense and 58.3 on DailyOmni, versus 47.2 and 57.8 for random visual-token restoration and 47.3 and 57.3 for saliency-based restoration from the same regions. | Table 7 |
| ReMo cuts latency and memory against Full Token | On Video-MME, ReMo takes 4.78 s and 15.5 GiB at 3B, and 5.92 s and 24.4 GiB at 7B, versus 7.35 s and 20.3 GiB, and 10.30 s and 29.7 GiB for Full Token. OmniZip has similar latency (4.77 s and 5.94 s) and lower memory (14.2 GiB and 23.1 GiB). | Table 3 |

The reported results support an aggregate quality gain at substantial compression in the tested Qwen setups, with runtime savings against Full Token measured on Video-MME. The retrieval diagnostic supports improved alignment of paired clips. The downstream projection ablation shows smaller gains, so the evidence for identifying redundancy at individual patches is indirect (Tables 2 and 5, Appendix B). Table 1 is used for the OmniZip comparison because the 7B aggregate in §5.2 differs from the table.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Evaluation focuses on short clips. On long-video benchmarks, memory constraints restricted evaluation to QA pairs with videos under one minute. Streaming and arbitrary-length processing remain open. The detector’s vocabulary limits which entities the proxies can name, and the best compression ratio may vary with task and input (§5.1, Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The backbone tests cover two Qwen Omni generations. The 30B study changes the selection budget and compares against Full Token and Random, leaving performance against stronger compressors at that scale unresolved (Tables 1 and 4, Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference.** The main component ablation covers two benchmarks on the 3B model. Removing proxies or local detail also changes compression, which limits fixed-budget attribution for those ablations. The matched-budget proxy comparison partially addresses this for text proxies (Tables 2 and 7).
- **Analyst inference.** The downstream tables provide point estimates without uncertainty intervals. Small aggregate gains therefore have unquantified statistical uncertainty (Tables 1 and 4).

## Open research questions

| Question | Why it remains open | Plausible next experiment |
|---|---|---|
| How does ReMo perform when audio is weak or unrelated to the visual answer? | Selection uses a chunk-average audio anchor, and results are reported by benchmark without stratifying audio relevance (§4.1, Tables 1 and 4). | Compare fused and vision-only selection at matched token budgets on clean, muted, and temporally mismatched audio tracks. |
| Can ReMo preserve evidence across long or streaming inputs? | Long-video evaluations exclude clips of one minute or more, and the authors flag streaming as open (§5.1, Limitations). | Evaluate progressively longer clips and a streaming variant, measuring QA accuracy, peak memory, and latency by duration. |
| Can broader proxy vocabularies recover entities the detector cannot name? | Proxies are detector class labels, so out-of-vocabulary entities cannot be explicitly represented (§4.2, Limitations). | Compare closed-vocabulary and open-vocabulary proxies on questions about rare entities at matched token budgets, recording accuracy and detector overhead. |

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and tabled results in the tested settings. The supplied text gives no downstream uncertainty estimates, exact sample counts for the filtered long-video evaluations, or confirmation of a public code or projection release. The 7B OmniZip discrepancy between Table 1 and §5.2 remains unresolved.
