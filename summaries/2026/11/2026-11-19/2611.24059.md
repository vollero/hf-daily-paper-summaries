# Replay, Don&#x27;t Resample: Off-Policy Consolidation of Long-Horizon RL Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24059) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24059)


## One-sentence takeaway

On 16 held-out math, search, and SQL benchmarks, chronological replay of expert RL logs produced the highest reported overall student score, 58.6, with 9 hours of consolidation versus 29 hours for MOPD + GRPO on eight H100s (Table 1, Figure 4).

## Problem and motivation

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates domain experts by generating fresh student trajectories, running tools in each environment, and having experts score the resulting tokens. Long rollouts make this expensive. The authors identify an unused source of training data: the expert runs already generated trajectories with rewards and sampling probabilities, and recorded the order in which the experts learned from them (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Chronological replay.** Multi-expert replay policy distillation (MRPD) starts the student from the experts’ shared checkpoint and combines their corresponding step batches for each update. Consolidation uses the existing logs without generating new trajectories or calling environments (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Two learning signals.** MRPD recomputes a clipped, GRPO-style policy gradient using logged rewards and sampling probabilities. It also distills each final expert’s next-token distribution at the logged prefixes using reverse KL over the expert’s top 256 tokens and a pooled tail (§3.3, Appendix A).
- **Replay-order ablations.** With the same trajectories, shuffling the training steps lowers final scores across the tested losses. The largest drops occur when the losses omit teacher distillation (Tables 3–4).

The timing results show a substantial consolidation advantage in the measured setup, with expert training already paid for. The benchmark results establish the observed ranking, but the small lead over on-policy distillation remains uncertain across training runs. The shuffle ablation shows that chronology helps on these logs. The clipping diagnostic measures agreement on logged tokens, leaving behavior on student-generated trajectories and the precise mechanism behind the order effect open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** Every model starts from the same supervised checkpoint. The experiments cover same-size dense 4B models, three domains, and 300 steps. The authors report one run per configuration plus a second MRPD run on the same replay batches, which scored 58.5 overall versus 58.6 for the first (Appendices B and G).
- **Analyst inference on comparison strength.** Full replay uses more samples per update than the online baselines. Matching sample counts reduces that difference, but the reported online distillation baseline uses sampled-token KL while default MRPD uses dense KL. The matched-count comparison with the sampled-token objective scored 57.7 versus 58.2 for MOPD + GRPO, so the headline comparison does not isolate the effect of replaying instead of resampling (§4.2, §4.4, Table 4).
- **Analyst inference on timing.** Plain MOPD and the headline online GRPO run used A100s. The eight-H100 timing uses MOPD + GRPO and a variant of online GRPO, and MRPD timing comes from an optimized rerun. The ratios therefore apply to the timed implementations (Appendix E).

## Open research questions

1. **How much initialization mismatch can MRPD tolerate?** All runs share a starting checkpoint. Replay the same logs from checkpoints at increasing distances from that start and measure clipping and held-out scores, with and without dense KL (Appendix G).
2. **Why does chronological replay improve the final score?** Full shuffling changes both local policy continuity and which batches the student sees last. Compare chronological replay with block shuffling and shuffling that preserves the final block, using identical samples (Tables 3–4).
3. **Does replay retain its advantage when the distillation objective is matched?** The data-matched comparison still changes the KL formulation. Compare replay with fresh student rollouts using dense KL in both conditions, matched sample counts, and repeated training runs (§4.2, §4.4, Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and measurements. Material gaps include run-to-run uncertainty for the baselines and ablations, replication with independently trained expert logs, and measurements at larger scales or with different initializations. The authors report a code release, but the supplied text does not establish whether the replay logs or trained checkpoints are available.
