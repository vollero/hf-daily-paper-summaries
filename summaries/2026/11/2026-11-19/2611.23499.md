# BiCAT: Bimanual Coordination Action Tokenization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23499) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23499)


## One-sentence takeaway

BiCAT uses partner-aware action tokens and left-first block prediction to reach 69.3% mean success on ten RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, 17.6 percentage points above NAC, the strongest of eight compared baselines (§4.2, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Bimanual robots reuse similar motions across arms, but each arm often has to adapt to what the other is doing. Concatenated action tokenizers can encode these dependencies implicitly. Independent per-arm tokenizers expose arm identity and can share a motion representation, but leave coordination to the policy. The authors ask whether separate token streams can capture partner context within the tokenizer itself (§§1, 2.2–2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Partner-aware tokenizer.** BiCAT maps mirrored joint trajectories into a shared convention and encodes both arms with the same causal temporal encoder. Symmetric causal cross-attention adds partner context before shared finite scalar quantization. A joint decoder reconstructs both trajectories from time-aligned token pairs. Reconstruction is the tokenizer’s sole training objective (§3.2, Fig. 2).
- **Arm-wise policy prediction.** With the tokenizer frozen, the vision-language-action policy predicts all left-arm tokens in parallel, then predicts all right-arm tokens in parallel conditioned on the completed left block (§3.3).
- **Controlled component analysis.** Simulation ablations test whether the gains depend on mirror alignment, parameter sharing, coupling before and after quantization, and the arm prediction schedule (§4.2, Table 3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher simulated success, especially on tightly coupled tasks | BiCAT scored 69.3% versus NAC’s 51.7% unweighted mean across ten RoboTwin 2.0 tasks with 100 rollouts per task. Gains over the strongest baseline in each group were 1.5 points for single-arm, 17.5 for loosely coupled, and 21.3 for tightly coupled tasks. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Coupling and prediction order both mattered | On the same ten tasks, mean success fell from 69.3% to 51.7% with decoder-only coupling, 56.0% with encoder-only coupling, 50.0% with independent arms, 51.1% with one prediction block, and 54.2% with right-first prediction. | §4.2, Tables 3 and 9 |
| Reconstruction error had little association with rollout success | Held-out reconstruction MSE had Spearman ρ = −0.13 with mean RoboTwin success across nine tokenizer configurations and seven BiCAT ablations. Replanning disagreement had ρ = −0.80 in the same comparison. | §4.2, Fig. 3 |
| Two-block prediction reduced token-generation time | On one H200 at batch size one, BiCAT took 40.5 ms per chunk versus 40.8 ms for two-block NAC, 229.1 ms for token-wise OAT, and 26.8 ms for single-block ActionCodec. | §§4.1–4.2, Table 1 |
| BiCAT led both physical baselines | Across six tasks on three separately trained embodiments, BiCAT succeeded in 113/150 trials, token-wise OAT in 74/150, and FAST in 25/150. On YAM with an RTX 5090, mean observation-to-action-chunk latency was 91.7 ms, 251.6 ms, and 434.1 ms, respectively. | §4.3, Table 5, §11.2 |

The rollout results support the combined tokenizer and prediction schedule under the tested protocols. The simulation ablations support the contribution of each coupling mechanism in the evaluated configuration. The authors treat Figure 3’s correlations as descriptive. Performance on unseen tasks and transfer to a held-out embodiment remain untested.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The fixed mirror mapping operates in joint space and does not directly extend to end-effector positions and orientations. Each tokenizer is trained for one embodiment and assigns both arms the same fixed token budget (§5).
- **Author-reported.** BiCAT trails the best baseline on Handover Mic and Dump Bin Bigbin. The quantization sweep changes latent dimensionality along with vocabulary size, so it cannot isolate the effect of vocabulary size (§4.2, Tables 2 and 4).
- **Analyst inference.** All ten simulated evaluation tasks occur in the training task collection. Physical comparisons cover FAST and token-wise OAT, while the controlled component ablations are simulation-only. The robot waits during inference, so the latency measurements leave continuous control under inference delays untested (§§4.2–4.3, 6.1, 11.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does left-first prediction help because of task roles or because of the fixed arm order?** Reversing the order reduced mean simulation success, but the ablation did not swap task roles. Mirror the task layouts and compare fixed orders with a policy that chooses the first arm from the observation (§4.2, Tables 3 and 9).
2. **Can BiCAT transfer to a held-out embodiment?** Each tokenizer was trained separately. Train a shared tokenizer on multiple embodiments and compare its success and target-data requirements with embodiment-specific training on a held-out robot (§5, §11.1).
3. **Can adaptive token allocation improve control at the same total budget?** The experiments use equal fixed budgets. Compare fixed and adaptive allocation at a matched total token count, with results grouped by how unevenly the two arms contribute (§§3.3, 5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported architecture, protocols, and aggregate comparisons. The supplied text does not report variation across independent training runs, provide confidence intervals for success-rate differences, or establish public availability of BiCAT code, checkpoints, or the real-world demonstrations.
