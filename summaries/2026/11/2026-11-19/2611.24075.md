# Recurrent Domain Incremental Adaptation of Time-Series Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24075) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24075)


## One-sentence takeaway

PACT adapts frozen time-series classifiers to recurring conditions by softly mixing local embedding corrections with an identity path, achieving the highest final average macro-F1 on four of five benchmarks in the main Sundial comparison, with mixed results against replay (Table 1, Fig. 5).

## Problem and motivation

A deployed time-series classifier may encounter new machine operating conditions, users, or subjects while still receiving data from earlier conditions. The task and labels stay fixed, each new condition supplies few calibration labels, and test inputs arrive without condition identifiers. The authors identify an underexplored question: can a frozen classifier adapt where its embedding space has shifted while preserving predictions where it already works? Their bearing-fault diagnostic shows that performance degrades unevenly across embedding regions, motivating local correction (§§1, 3.1, Fig. 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Local correction.** PACT partitions source embeddings into fixed nearest-centroid regions using k-means. For each arriving condition, labeled calibration examples fit low-rank residual maps through the frozen classifier. The new maps are then frozen and appended to the bank (§§3.2.1–3.2.2).
- **Identity-preserving routing.** A gate in each region is refit on retained embeddings and their condition identifiers. At inference, it softly mixes the identity path with supported correction branches. The encoder, classifier head, and earlier maps stay frozen. PACT discards raw series and past class labels (§§3.2.3–3.2.4).
- **Analysis and validation.** The paper characterizes uneven local degradation, derives margin-based sufficient conditions for preserving a frozen prediction and correctly classifying a target example, and tests the design across five benchmarks and eight frozen backbones (Fig. 3, §§4.1–4.5, Appendix A).

The evidence supports few-label adaptation and retention under the tested fixed-label protocols. Recurrence is measured mainly by retesting held-out examples from earlier conditions after each update, with two additional simulated FD revisits (Appendix C.5). The margin result applies per input after fitting. Zero forgetting across updates and performance on naturally recurring deployment streams remain unestablished (§§4.5, 6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** PACT depends on class separability in the frozen representation and adequate coverage by source-defined regions. Overlapping conditions make routing harder, gate refits can change earlier predictions, and skewed or missing calibration classes can hurt adaptation. Stored embeddings and the transport bank grow as conditions arrive. Concept shift is outside the study’s scope (§3.1, §6, Table 13).
- **Analyst inference.** The replay experiments answer specific memory-budget questions. DER++ is sized against PACT’s retained embedding array, while PACT also stores centroids, transports, gates, and metadata. The embedding cap likewise matches embedding counts, so total persistent-byte parity remains unverified (Appendix B.3, Table 5).
- **Analyst inference.** The WISDM replay comparison contains an internal inconsistency. Appendix C.1 says replay leads in its paired-seed comparison, but Table 4 lists a higher mean for PACT. The source does not resolve which is correct.

## Open research questions

- **Can routing preserve already-correct predictions when conditions overlap?** The oracle-routing gaps on HHAR and UCI-HAR leave room for improvement. Compare domain-only gate training with margin-aware routing at the same calibration budget, tracking final average macro-F1 and damage to initially correct predictions (Fig. 8, Appendix C.6).
- **Can adaptive regions help when new conditions fall far from source support?** The placement experiments compare fixed partitions. Test soft assignment or chart refinement at matched parameter and label budgets, with results stratified by distance to the source centroids (Table 6, §6).
- **How does PACT perform over longer, naturally recurring streams under a fixed total storage budget?** The paper tests fixed benchmark streams, simulated revisits, and a cap on historical embeddings. Compare PACT with replay on timestamped streams while matching total persistent bytes and tracking per-condition performance after each revisit (Appendix C.2, Appendix C.5, §6).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and the main Table 1 results. The supplied text gives no measured per-update latency or end-to-end persistent storage totals, no naturally recurring long-horizon deployment results, and no clarification of the WISDM prose–table discrepancy in Appendix C.1.
