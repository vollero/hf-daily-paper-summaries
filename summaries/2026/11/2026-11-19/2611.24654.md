# CurvDelta: Low-Curvature Delta Modeling For Surgical Learning and Unlearning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24654) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24654)


## One-sentence takeaway

CurvDelta and its low-rank variant constrain language-model updates to directions estimated to cause little drift on preservation data, improving several reported learning and unlearning trade-offs while bounding only the local quadratic approximation to that drift.

## Problem and motivation

Fine-tuning a language model to acquire a capability or forget specific knowledge can degrade unrelated behavior. The authors frame both tasks as minimizing a task loss subject to a tolerance on output drift over a designated preservation set. They argue that a preservation penalty does not map directly to a drift tolerance and can introduce conflicting gradients, while constraints based on parameter or representation orthogonality are only indirectly tied to the behavior being preserved. (§§1–2)

## Approach and contributions

- **Constrain the applied update.** CurvDelta estimates the local curvature of token-level KL divergence from the original model on a preservation set. For each adapted linear weight, it uses block-diagonal K-FAC and keeps directions formed from trailing eigenvectors of both Kronecker factors. Fixed two-sided projections keep the applied update in this space throughout optimization, including under Adam. (§§3.1–3.3, Appendix C.1)
- **Reuse the same geometry for dense and low-rank adaptation.** CurvDelta trains a dense delta. CurvLoRA projects the two LoRA factors directly, avoiding a dense delta during training. The cached eigenbases are reusable across task objectives and parameterizations when the base model, preservation set, target weights, and spectral threshold are fixed. (§3.3, Appendix C.2)
- **Derive a conditional preservation bound.** Under the local quadratic and block-diagonal K-FAC models, Theorem 1 bounds preservation loss by a sum of layerwise terms, each proportional to the maximum retained curvature and the squared Frobenius norm of the applied update. A sufficiently small weighted sum satisfies the modeled tolerance. (§3.4, Appendix B.7)

The results support better acquisition–retention or forgetting–retention trade-offs in several tested settings, with rankings that vary by backbone, objective, and metric. For example, GradDiff often drove forget-answer probability to zero while yielding very low Forget Quality (Tables 8–10). TOFU and MUSE measure behavior on their benchmark data, so these results establish neither comprehensive removal of the target knowledge nor preservation outside the evaluated data. The spectral bound applies to the quadratic model, and Table 13 shows that it can substantially underestimate empirical KL if used as a bound on the observed loss.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Factorwise truncation can discard directions whose high curvature in one factor is offset by low curvature in the other. Increasing the energy threshold also reduces the available update space. (§3.2, Appendix B.5)
- **Author-stated:** The guarantee depends on the local quadratic and block-diagonal K-FAC assumptions, and feasibility still depends on the size of the realized update. The authors observe a substantial scale gap between the local model and empirical retain-set KL. (§§3.4, 5.1)
- **Analyst inference:** The projection and preservation-data ablations use one TOFU split, backbone, and objective. They leave open whether the same effects hold for capability learning, other objectives, or preservation data drawn from a different distribution. (§4.3)
- **Analyst inference:** The main tables give point estimates without run-to-run uncertainty. Appendix E.1 also describes different sources of hyperparameter configurations across methods, leaving the sensitivity of close rankings to tuning and random seeds unclear. (Tables 1–3, Appendix E.1)

## Open research questions

1. **When does the local model predict empirical drift closely enough to select a tolerance?** Table 13 shows similar trends but a large scale gap. Sweep realized update norms and compare empirical KL, the K-FAC quadratic, and a fuller curvature calculation on a tractable model.
2. **How much adaptation capacity is lost by discarding mixed-factor directions?** Appendix B.5 identifies the excluded directions, while the reported ablation compares low-curvature projections with random and high-curvature projections. Compare factorwise and product-spectrum projectors under matched curvature ceilings and, in a separate comparison, matched subspace dimensions.
3. **Which preservation examples protect behavior outside the preservation set?** Figure 2c varies the amount of TOFU retain data within one setting. Hold the sample count fixed, vary the source and domain coverage, and measure both in-domain retention and performance on held-out domains.
