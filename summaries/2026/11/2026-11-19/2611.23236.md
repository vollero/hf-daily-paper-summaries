# PGPO: Prototype-Guided Progressive Obfuscation for Privacy-Preserving LLM-Enhanced Recommendation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23236) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23236)


## One-sentence takeaway

On MovieLens-1M and Amazon-Book, the authors report that PGPO retains recommendation performance close to the raw-description reference while providing comparable resistance to embedding inversion as other obfuscation methods at matched average cosine similarity (§5.2, Tables 3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Textual item attributes help recommenders connect items with sparse interaction histories, but embeddings of those attributes can expose information through inversion. The authors argue that protections applied after collection leave the input exposed and that independent word substitutions can disrupt the semantic neighborhoods that support recommendation. PGPO targets **relational fidelity** by preserving relative neighborhood structure while moving obfuscated terms away from their originals (§1, §3.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Relational-fidelity objective.** The authors formulate input obfuscation as a joint objective that lowers original–variant cosine similarity and preserves the relative similarity of neighboring terms (§3.1).
2. **Prototype-guided generation.** PGPO ranks terms by their influence in a static cosine-neighbor graph, learns obfuscated variants for high-influence prototypes, and uses finalized variants as cached examples when generating replacements for the remaining vocabulary (§§3.2–3.3, Fig. 2).
3. **Reward design and leakage analysis.** GRPO combines cosine dissimilarity, neighborhood consistency based on Spearman correlation and Wasserstein distance, and a category constraint based on Mahalanobis distance. The authors also derive a conditional re-identification bound that accounts for uncertainty about the hidden generation trajectory (§§3.4, 4, Appendix A).

Table 3 supports the claim that PGPO stays close to raw-description utility across the tested recommenders, with the direction of the difference varying by model and metric. The reported Spearman values establish a relative advantage on the paper’s neighborhood metric. The privacy evidence is specific to the tested recovery protocols. Matching average cosine similarity controls average semantic displacement, and the supplied results leave comparative leakage under broader attacker capabilities unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** PGPO applies a uniform strategy across terms, including rare or sensitive ones, and adds LLM generation cost. The study focuses on textual side information. The authors also flag potential misuse to evade content moderation (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference — generalization.** Both benchmarks use attributes generated from titles, and all ablations and sensitivity experiments use MovieLens-1M. Robustness to naturally collected sensitive metadata and transfer of the component-level findings to other datasets remain uncertain (§§B.2, B.6).
- **Analyst inference — privacy scope.** Replay-MLE scores the true source against 50 sampled negatives with history omitted. Its posterior entropy and mutual-information estimates are attack-specific proxies. The theoretical bound assumes an approximately uniform conditional prior, and the tighter representation-level argument assumes limited residual leakage from collaborative signals (§§B.4, B.7.4, A.4, A.6).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does high-influence prototype selection contribute?** The supplied studies vary prototype count and remove context, leaving the selection rule unisolated. Compare influence-ranked and randomly selected prototypes at the same count, generation budget, and mean cosine similarity (§§5.3–5.4).
2. **Does the trade-off hold for naturally collected sensitive attributes?** The benchmarks use title-generated attributes and uniform protection. Compare uniform and sensitivity-weighted PGPO on a consented metadata benchmark, reporting recovery and utility by attribute rarity and sensitivity (§B.2, Limitations).
3. **How sensitive is unlinkability to what an evaluator can observe?** The reported white-box test omits history and uses sampled candidates. In a controlled audit, vary how much generation history is visible and measure re-identification as the candidate pool grows (§§B.4, B.7.4, A.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text gives the method, threat model, metric definitions, and recommendation results. It omits the numerical entries for Tables 1–2 and 4–9, including the achieved cosine-similarity targets, per-method inversion scores, and ablation magnitudes. The reported ANOVA lacks test statistics and comparison details. Measured generation cost and the empirical tightness of the theoretical bound are also unavailable.
