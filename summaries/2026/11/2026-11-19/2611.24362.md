# PIPETTE: Prompt Injection Purple-Teaming for Robust LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24362) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24362)


## One-sentence takeaway

Pipette pairs strategy-diverse adversarial training data with token-level defensive supervision, reducing indirect prompt injection success on held-out benchmarks while keeping clean-task completion close to that of the base models.

## Problem and motivation

Tool-using LLM agents can mistake instructions embedded in external data for user authorization, leading to unauthorized actions or data disclosure. A useful defense must still extract the surrounding data and complete the legitimate task. The authors identify two gaps: high red-team attack success can come from repeatedly exploiting a narrow set of strategies, and trajectory-level defense rewards provide limited feedback about which decisions caused a failure (§§I–III).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evidence for an objective mismatch.** The authors show that the red-teamer with higher training-time attack success can produce a less robust defender, and use a strategy-coverage score to investigate whether repeated strategies explain the gap (§§III-B, IV-A).
- **Pipette-Red.** The paper introduces a strategy-coverage objective based on a rolling sparse dictionary to encourage a broader adversarial training set (§IV-B, Fig. 3).
- **Pipette-Blue.** The defense combines on-policy reinforcement learning, explicit checks that planned tool calls serve the user’s authorized task, and contrastive token-level self-distillation using privileged training-time annotations (§IV-C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher red-team success can yield a weaker defender | On Qwen3-4B, PISmith versus RL-Hammer reached 68.75% versus 33.39% cumulative training ASR on AgentDyn. After RL adversarial training, worst-case ASR across three held-out attacks on the selected AgentDojo pairs was 47.5% versus 27.5%. | §III-B, Table II, Fig. 2 |
| Pipette-Red data improved the tested downstream robustness comparisons | Replacing RL-Hammer or PISmith data with Pipette-Red data lowered worst-case AgentDojo ASR under both SFT and RL on Qwen3-4B and Llama-8B. The collections also differed in size and sampling. | §V-B, Fig. 5, Appendix D |
| Lower ASR under iterative-adaptive evaluation | On 40 selected AgentDojo pairs, TAP/Genetic ASR was 0%/2.5% versus 2.5%/12.5% for Llama-8B with Meta-SecAlign, 2.5%/5% versus 15%/30% for Qwen3.6-27B with SecOPD, and 5%/10% versus 50%/92.5% for GPT-OSS-20B with native Instruction Hierarchy. | §V-C, Table V, Appendix C |
| Low average static ASR against inference-time baselines | Across five attacks per benchmark, Pipette had the lowest average ASR among the inference-time defenses compared. Qwen3-4B and Llama-8B respectively reached 0.48% and 0.43% on AgentDojo, and 0.70% and 0.50% on ASB. | §V-D, Table VI, Fig. 6 |
| Clean utility stayed close to base performance, with some losses under adaptive attack | On 97 clean AgentDojo tasks, Pipette/base utility was 64.95%/61.86% on Qwen3-4B, 41.24%/40.21% on Llama-8B, 92.78%/93.81% on Qwen3.6-27B, and 49.48%/50.52% on GPT-OSS-20B. On the selected adaptive pairs, Qwen3.6-27B utility fell from 97.5% under either search to 87.5% under TAP and 90% under Genetic. | Tables IV, V, IX, Appendix C |
| The defensive training components improved the tested ablation | With reasoning supervision already in place, adding token-level supervision on Qwen3-4B reduced ChatInject ASR from 2.38% to 1.43%, raised utility under ChatInject from 56.90% to 58.10%, and raised clean AgentDojo utility from 60.82% to 64.95%. | §V-F, Table VII |

The results support transfer from AgentDyn to held-out benchmark tasks under the tested conditions. Adaptive ASR describes a stress test on pairs selected for prior vulnerability. Utility under attack counts legitimate task completion even when the injection task also succeeds, so the reported utility rates leave the joint rate of safe task completion unresolved. The coverage results are consistent with the authors’ explanation for improved robustness, while differences in collection size and sampling leave the causal contribution uncertain (§V-B, §VI, Appendix C).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Adaptive evaluation used two gradient-free searches on 40 selected AgentDojo pairs with a 4B attacker model and budgets of up to 250 attempts per pair. The grader behind the strategy-coverage score lacks validation against human labels. The single training pass leaves vulnerabilities specific to the hardened policy unexplored (§VI, Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference.** The shared tools and similar injection goals across AgentDyn and AgentDojo limit what disjoint task IDs establish about transfer to different tool ecosystems (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference.** The defensive component ablation uses one model and one static attack, and adds the reasoning warm-up and reasoning reward together. Their separate effects and generality remain uncertain (§V-F, Table VII).

## Open research questions

- **How much of the gain comes from strategy coverage?** Collection size and sampling differ across red-teamers. Compare defenses trained on equally sized collections with matched task sampling and independently annotated strategy coverage (§V-B, Appendix D).
- **Does the coverage score agree with human judgments?** The authors report no human validation. Have blinded annotators rate whether a dictionary covers held-out examples from multiple attack families, then measure agreement with the LLM grader (§IV-A, §VI).
- **How well does the defense generalize beyond the selected adaptive subset?** Selection favored previously compromised pairs and pairs where the user task could still succeed. Repeat the defensive evaluation on a stratified sample of AgentDojo and on ASB, and report the joint rate of user-task completion without injection success (Table V, Appendix C).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark comparisons within their stated settings. The supplied text does not establish public release of Pipette code, checkpoints, or training collections. It also lacks full prompt templates, end-to-end training cost, and uncertainty estimates for the main ASR and utility comparisons.
