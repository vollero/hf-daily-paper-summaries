# VAE Rhythm: Temporal Decoding Traces for AI-Generated Video Detection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21114) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21114)


## One-sentence takeaway

A training-free scan detects periodic VAE reconstruction errors in many generated videos and improves all 19 tested detectors on DF26 when added to their scores.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether AI-generated video can be detected from pixels alone as generators improve at removing visible artifacts. The authors identify a gap in reconstruction-based detection: existing methods use the magnitude of reconstruction error, while temporal grouping in causal video VAEs can leave a periodic signature across frame offsets. (§§1–3.1)

## Approach and contributions

1. **Temporal trace and phase scan.** The authors identify *VAE Rhythm* and measure it by reconstructing overlapping windows with a frozen Wan2.1 VAE. They subtract each frame’s mean error across windows, exclude the first frame of each window, and score periodicity across start offsets. They characterize the signal across generator families and test its origin with VAE round trips, temporal shifts, and codec controls. (§§3.1–3.3, S1.2)
2. **Score fusion without retraining.** A plug-in standardizes the detector and rhythm scores using held-out real clips, then adds the positive part of the standardized rhythm score. It uses a single spectral peak statistic across generators. (§4.3)
3. **Matched training pairs and CaDet.** The pair engine applies identical post-processing to a real clip and its VAE reconstruction. CaDet uses those pairs to train an artifact path and trains a separate semantic path on benchmark data, with both paths sharing a frozen DINOv3 backbone. CaDet-GF trains on real clips and their reconstructions alone. The paper cites prior reconstruction-pair training and contributes shared post-processing and this detector design. (§§2, 4.1–4.2)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Rhythm was common across generators | Among 36 window-matched variants, 30 had stronger rhythm than their reference real clips at Holm-corrected \(p<0.05\). Family-specific zero-shot scores exceeded chance for 34 of 36 variants at native resolution capped at 480p. | §§3.2–3.3, S1.1–S1.3; Tables S1–S2 |
| A VAE round trip induced the trace | The period-4 score separated Wan2.1 reconstructions from their real originals at 90% AUROC. The same scan separated DF26 reals re-encoded with Grok’s x264 settings from their originals at about 49% AUROC. | §S1.2 |
| One zero-shot statistic beat the retrained DF26 baselines | Across DF26’s ten generators, the common spectral peak score reached 76.2% AUROC versus 70.4% for Effort, the best of 15 retrained baselines. | §3.3, Table 2 |
| CaDet led the reported benchmark averages | On AIGVDBench, CaDet reached 93.9% open-source and 95.8% closed-source AUROC, versus 85.7% for the best reproduced ForgeLens setting and 92.5% for Effort, respectively. With no test-set retraining, CaDet reached 78.5% on DF26 and 88.3% across the three 2026 closed-source models against DF26 reals, versus the best reproduced baseline scores of 70.4% and 86.4%, respectively. | §§5.1–5.2, Table 2 |
| CaDet-GF transferred from reconstruction pairs | Without generator-produced training clips, CaDet-GF reached 90.9% open-source and 91.9% closed-source AUROC on AIGVDBench. Its DF26 closed-source AUROC was 51.3%. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| The plug-in improved all tested DF26 configurations | Across 19 detector configurations, the authors report AUROC gains of 6.7% to 23.7% over the corresponding base scores. CaDet rose from 78.5% to 85.2% AUROC on DF26. | §5.3, Table 2 |

The controls show that a VAE round trip can induce the measured rhythm, and the benchmark results show that it can complement learned detector scores. For closed-source generators with undisclosed VAEs, the measured period supports the authors’ interpretation while the underlying architecture remains unverified. The codec controls cover specific configurations, and the reported AUROCs measure benchmark ranking performance. (§§S1.2–S1.3, S6)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limits.** The trace weakens in nearly static clips, when scan resolution is well below generation resolution, and under strong re-encoding or frame-rate conversion. Future decoder changes could alter the signal. Five 16-frame variants were too short for the default census, and three short-window entries lacked a matched real reference. The default 720p scan took about 30 seconds per clip on an H200. (§§6, S1.3, S2)
- **Analyst inference on selection and comparison.** The authors chose the census’s family-specific exceptions after viewing the curves and selected the common spectral peak statistic after inspecting DF26. The reported performance therefore leaves uncertainty about a prospectively fixed score on a fresh benchmark. Baseline rankings also depend on the shared frame-cache protocol and reproduction changes, including shortened temporal inputs for ReStraV. (§§S1.1, S5, S6)
- **Analyst inference on real-world transfer.** Swapping the real-video source sharply degraded trained detectors, and performance on ViF-Bench was near chance. The 2026 single-shot collections reused DF26 real clips as the comparison cohort. Together, these settings leave performance against diverse, independently matched real footage uncertain. (§S6)

## Open research questions

1. **How well does a fixed rhythm score transfer to entirely unseen generators?** The paper selected the common statistic after inspecting DF26. Freeze the score, scan settings, and calibration before evaluating on a fresh generator cohort with matched real clips. (§S6)
2. **How much can more diverse real training footage improve transfer?** The Youku extension added a real source and broadened the processing chain together. Vary source diversity and post-processing independently, then test on held-out real-video sources. (§S6)
3. **Can shorter or cheaper scans retain accuracy on brief clips?** The scan-budget study used clips with at least 80 frames, while the default census excluded the 16-frame variants. Compare fixed-budget scan settings on length-matched real and generated clips, stratified by motion. (§S2)

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in the reported methods and aggregate comparisons, with lower confidence in transfer beyond the tested cohorts.
