# The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution&#x27;s Key Is Identifying Improvements

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23068) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23068)


## One-sentence takeaway

In the studied frozen-LLM agents, helpful skills existed, but low-budget selectors identified them at roughly chance rates, while variation across independent evolution runs made small differences between methods difficult to rank.

## Problem and motivation

Agent evolution updates persistent prompts, skills, memories, or harnesses while keeping model weights frozen. Benchmark scores guide both which artifacts survive each round and which methods appear strongest in published comparisons. The authors ask whether those scores can distinguish small, repeatable improvements from stochastic variation, and whether failures to improve originate in proposal or selection (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Variance decomposition and replication.** The authors separate variation from repeatedly evaluating a fixed artifact from variation in the expected quality of artifacts produced by independent runs. They apply this framework to seven methods across six models and four benchmarks, with model coverage varying by experiment (§3, Appendix B, Table 5).
- **Audit of large gains.** They compare evolved artifacts with an unevolved baseline given one rule extracted from the artifact, then re-score artifacts with repaired evaluations and rerun selected methods under those repairs (§4.2, Appendix E).
- **Controlled tests of proposal and selection.** Two fixed skill pools measure available improvements and how much selectors recover using held-out rollouts as a quality reference. Independent re-evaluation tracks within-run gains, and a follow-up tests repeated validation during evolution (§5, Appendices F–G).
- **Evaluation diagnostics.** The authors propose separate proposer and selector quality curves and test whether intermediate behavior metrics can detect changes obscured by terminal pass/fail scores (§6, Appendix H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Typical method gaps are small relative to single-run variability | In 217 of 341 comparable same-model, same-benchmark configuration pairs, the gap between all-run means fell below one standard deviation of a single-run comparison. The median lead over the strongest optimization competitor across 171 literature results was 3.4 percentage points (pp). | §4.1, Fig. 2a, Appendices D.1 and D.3 |
| Single-run rankings often miss the reference winner | Resampling one run and one evaluation per method recovered the all-run leader among four text-artifact methods 33% of the time on Terminal-Bench v2.1, versus 25% for random choice. At 80% power and α = 0.05, the estimated requirement for a 3.4 pp gap on that benchmark was 23 independent runs per method with one evaluation per artifact. | §4.1, Appendix D.2, Table 7 |
| Evaluation shortcuts explain many large audited gains | Across 27 audited model–benchmark pairs with gains above 3 pp, adding one extracted rule to the unevolved baseline recovered a median 90% of the gain. Among evaluations with repairs, median evolved-artifact gain over the corresponding baseline fell from 17.6 to 0.8 pp when the same artifacts were re-scored. | §4.2, Fig. 3, Tables 9–11 |
| Proposals contain measurable headroom | On ten disjoint Terminal-Bench v2.1 test tasks, per-task oracle Quality@K rose from 49.2% at K=1 to 61.8% at K=100 under a two-hour budget, versus 58.4% for the matched equal-quality null at K=100. Under original timeout cutoffs, it rose from 39.7% to 51.6%, versus 48.3% for the null at K=100. | §5.1, Figs. 4a and 9 |
| Low-budget selectors distinguish better skills at roughly chance rates | On the separate 11-task selection pool, one-rollout execution achieved 49.7% pairwise accuracy (95% credible interval half-width 1.9 pp), versus 50% for random choice. The two-rollout native SkillOpt-Lite and GEPA operators each achieved 48.6%. None of the tested trajectory judges showed reliable discrimination. | §5.2, Table 1, Appendix F.3 |
| In-loop gains largely disappear on re-evaluation | Across the Terminal-Bench method–model groups for SkillOpt-Lite and GEPA, in-loop gains over initialization reached 6.9–9.0 pp. Independent repeats on the same validation tasks put gains between −3.2 and +0.4 pp. Final noise-corrected variance across runs reached 4.2–24.5 pp². | §5.3, Figs. 5–6, Appendix G |
| More validation rollouts improved mean test performance in one experiment | In the SkillOpt-Lite follow-up, averaging 16 validation rollouts per candidate instead of one raised mean final test success across runs from 61.4% to 64.4%. Final artifacts were evaluated with 32 independent test rollouts per task. | Appendix F.3, p. 33 |
| A process metric had lower measurement variance | For DeepSeek-V4-Flash, the evaluation-noise variance of the fraction of checks with visible failure diagnostics was 13–57 times smaller than that of terminal pass/fail across the baseline and seven methods. | §6, Appendix H, Table 13 |

The repeated evaluations support the authors’ claim that selection is a bottleneck in the tested settings. The proposal oracle measures improvement available in a fixed pool and can choose a different skill per task. The ranking simulation measures agreement with an empirical all-run reference. The audit also found gains that survived repair, including improved HotpotQA retrieval. The process diagnostic establishes greater sensitivity to a particular behavioral change, while its usefulness as a selection signal for task success remains open (§§4–6, Appendix E.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The study covers frozen weights and selected text-artifact and harness methods. Candidate-level evaluation is confined to Terminal-Bench v2.1, with both fixed-pool experiments using DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Transfer to other domains, proposal strategies, larger pools, weight-updating methods, or program evolution remains open (Table 5, p. 10).
- **Author-stated.** The audit rules were chosen after artifact inspection, so their success shows sufficiency in the tested comparison. Some repairs are partial or change the evaluated items. For example, the repaired LiveMathematicianBench score uses only questions without the leaked option (Table 11, Appendix E.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The amount of proposer headroom depends on the quality estimator. Under the tightest tested empirical-Bayes concentration cap, the observed and matched-null bands overlap. The primary setting and looser caps show separation (Appendix F.2, Fig. 11).
- **Analyst inference.** The repeated-validation follow-up supports a benefit in one SkillOpt-Lite setting. Its description gives point estimates without uncertainty intervals, limiting assessment of the size and stability of the benefit (Appendix F.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptive validation retain the gains of repeated rollouts at lower cost?** Appendix F.3 compares one rollout with a fixed 16-rollout average. Compare fixed and adaptive allocation at matched total rollout budgets across independent evolution runs, using final held-out task success as the outcome.
2. **Do intermediate behavior checks improve selection?** Appendix H measures the sensitivity of diagnostics, while downstream selection benefit remains untested. Compare terminal-only and process-assisted selectors on the same candidate pool and measure both held-out pairwise accuracy and final task success.
3. **Does the proposal–selection gap transfer beyond Terminal-Bench?** The fixed-pool analysis evaluates one benchmark with one model. Repeat the protocol on SpreadsheetBench and OfficeQA with multiple models, disjoint generation and test tasks, and matched selector budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported descriptive findings for the studied configurations. Broader generalization is uncertain given the candidate-level scope. The supplied text omits raw per-run and per-rollout data, the full candidate pools, and uncertainty intervals for the repeated-validation comparison. The authors state that reproducibility materials and trajectory data will be released upon acceptance, so their availability is unverified from the supplied text.
