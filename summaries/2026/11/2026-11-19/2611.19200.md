# LogT: Logically Think with Images for Visual Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19200) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19200)


## One-sentence takeaway

LogT trains vision-language models on synthetic visual QA and step-by-step crop traces, helping Qwen2.5-VL-7B surpass the strongest fully evaluated open-source baseline with supervised fine-tuning alone and widen the lead with a simple reinforcement-learning recipe (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Vision-language models can miss small details in high-resolution images, and text-only reasoning cannot recover details lost during visual encoding. A Zoom-in tool lets models inspect image regions, but the authors argue that prior post-training teaches call formatting more reliably than it teaches when to zoom, where to look, and when to stop. LogT tests whether better supervised training data can address that gap (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Object-centered QA synthesis.** LogT uses full SA1B images, contextual crops, and tight crops around small annotated objects to generate questions about fine visual details. A separate vision-language model judges correctness and clarity in both candidate orders to reduce positional bias (§3.1, Fig. 3).
2. **Per-question search plans.** Questions the base model answers correctly on all eight tool-free samples get direct-answer traces. For other questions, LogT generates multi-hop and systematic-search plans, selects one, and uses it to guide a trace that interleaves reasoning with Zoom-in calls on the original image or prior crops (§3.2, Figs. 1 and 3).
3. **Training and evaluation.** The engine produces 7.1k QA–trace pairs. The authors fine-tune two base models and test whether the Qwen checkpoint benefits from modified GRPO with answer-correctness and format rewards (§§3.2, 4.2, B.4).

The results support the authors’ claim that better SFT data can improve visual search and provide a stronger starting point for the tested RL recipe. The logic-chain ablation isolates one part of the pipeline. The grounding metric credits a response for one qualifying crop, and the human study combines three criteria into one preference. As a result, neither measures the correctness of every reasoning step.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Training centers on Zoom-in, and automated judges can introduce noise. A manual audit of 100 samples per stage found error rates of 12% for QA generation and filtering, 2% for logic-chain generation, and 7% for trace generation (§5.3, Table 7).
- **Analyst inference.** The V*-Rotated comparison gives LogT-RL a rotation tool and reports a base-model row without a matched rotation-tool condition. The result leaves the effects of post-training and tool access unresolved (§B.6, Table 8).
- **Analyst inference.** Grounding and human preferences are measured only on V*. Pixel Reasoner and Chain-of-Focus are evaluated with their usual box padding or enlargement disabled, so the ranking applies to the shared raw-box protocol (§4.1 n. 1, §4.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The smaller-synthesizer ablation compares model quality using 4k samples on the visual-search benchmarks. The cheaper 7.1k synthesis estimate leaves full-scale downstream performance with those synthesizers unverified (§4.6, Table 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Which QA synthesis choices drive the gain?** The difficulty ablation compares easier and harder samples, so the individual effects of contextual crops, tight crops, and judge filtering remain open. Run a factorial ablation with fixed sample counts and the same SFT recipe (§§3.1, 4.6, Table 4).
2. **How much unseen-tool transfer comes from LogT training?** The rotation comparison lacks matched access. Evaluate the base model, LogT-SFT, LogT-RL, and a competing checkpoint with the same rotation interface, each with and without tool access (§B.6, Table 8).
3. **Are intermediate crops necessary and faithful?** The grounding score needs only one qualifying crop, and the preference study combines several qualities. Annotate every call for relevance and test how removing individual crops affects answer accuracy (§4.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark comparisons and the main pipeline description. Material gaps include run-to-run variability, the identity of the original 106B judge, complete generation prompts and examples, public release status for the code, dataset, and checkpoints, and a like-for-like end-to-end compute comparison that includes data synthesis.
