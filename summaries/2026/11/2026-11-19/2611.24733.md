# Mechanistic Critics for Sample-Efficient NPU Design-Space Exploration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24733) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24733)


## One-sentence takeaway

CriticNPU uses an online-calibrated Roofline model to screen and guide NPU designs, reaching best-or-tied-best simulated latency in 74% of prefill and 87.5% of decode settings with about half the simulator calls of Bayesian optimization and greedy search (§4.1, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

The paper searches for NPU configurations that minimize latency for a specified LLM and deployment while meeting power, memory, and bandwidth constraints. Hardware parameters interact, and prefill and decode can stress different resources. The authors identify a gap between scalar simulator scores, which rank designs already evaluated, and bottleneck analyses, which explain those designs but offer limited guidance on whether to simulate a new candidate or which parameter to change next (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Use a mechanistic critic at two points in the search.** After warm-up, the critic simulates candidates whose predicted latency is no worse than the incumbent’s. After each simulation, it summarizes bottlenecks by phase and predicts how proposed hardware edits would affect latency (§3.2, Eqs. 3–5).
- **Calibrate Roofline limits online by operator type.** The critic derives peak compute and memory bandwidth from the hardware configuration, then updates operator-type efficiency factors from simulator timing with an exponential moving average. These factors shift the compute–memory crossover used for screening and diagnosis (§3.2, Eq. 6).
- **Integrate the critic with workload extraction and architecture planning.** One LLM agent extracts operator FLOPs, byte traffic, and execution counts from the model and deployment specification. A second proposes hardware configurations using the workload, constraints, search history, and critic feedback (§§3.1–3.3, Fig. 1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Near-best latency with fewer simulator calls | Across six workloads and 16 deployments per workload, CriticNPU reached best-or-tied-best TTFT in 74% of prefill settings and TPOT in 87.5% of decode settings. It averaged 54 calls, versus 117 for Bayesian optimization and 129–130 for greedy search in Table 1. Its mean TTFT and TPOT were 1.012× and 1.008× the best valid latency observed per setting, respectively. | §4.1, Table 1 |
| Equal-budget gains were clearer in prefill | Across 96 settings per phase, median calls to get within 2% of the best valid latency observed in the 100-call trajectories were 14 for CriticNPU, 22 for Bayesian optimization, and 30 for greedy search in prefill. The corresponding decode medians were 8, 8, and 7. | Appendix E.6, Table 9 |
| Gating drove decode call savings in the ablation | Across three Llama workloads at batch size 48 and 300 W with disaggregated serving, the full method used 13–15 decode calls versus 35–44 with diagnosis alone and no gate, with identical reported TPOT for each workload. Gating alone matched the full method’s reported TTFT and TPOT in these settings. | Table 2 |
| Roofline predictions ranked proposals better than the LLM comparator | With identical inputs, the Roofline model achieved Kendall’s τ of 0.62 versus 0.52 for GPT-5.4-mini on 150 absolute-TTFT prediction pairs, and 0.31 versus 0.16 on 83 pairs for ranking TTFT changes. Absolute-TTFT mean absolute percentage error was 47% versus 96%. | §4.2B, Fig. 3 |
| Screening had small misses in the checked sample | In Llama3-8B decode across 16 deployments, the authors re-simulated 60 of 91 skipped candidates. Of those, 27 improved on the incumbent by less than 0.01 ms, and none improved TPOT by 1% or more. | Appendix E.7 |

The strongest evidence is for reaching competitive latency with fewer NeuSIM evaluations, particularly in prefill. The latency reference is the best valid design observed among compared methods. Global optimality, physical-hardware performance, and end-to-end search speed remain unverified.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** All experiments use NeuSIM, so calibration inherits its systematic errors. The authors identify MoE routing imbalance, cross-expert communication, and compiler scheduling as effects the critic may capture poorly. Workload extraction was checked against native NeuSIM profiles for only two model families (§5, Appendix E.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Table 2 isolates gating and diagnosis in a narrow setting. Figure 4 illustrates how calibration shifts operator ceilings, but the supplied results give no matched static-versus-calibrated Roofline search comparison, so the incremental search benefit of calibration remains unclear (§4.2C, Table 2).
- **Analyst inference.** The precise gate is ambiguous. Equation 4 specifies an incumbent cutoff, while §4.2A and Appendix E.2 describe a margin. The method also discusses inter-chip communication, but Eqs. 3 and 5 show only compute and memory terms.
- **Reporting discrepancies.** Appendix E.3 says all backbone swaps stay within 5% of default latency, while Table 11 reports a 23% TPOT reduction for Llama2-13B with either alternate backbone in its batch-size-48, 300 W setting. Section 1 briefly frames the task as choosing the target LLM, while the formal problem and experiments take the LLM as given (§§2, 4).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does online calibration improve search?** The paper illustrates changed bottleneck thresholds without isolating their effect on search outcomes. Compare static, globally calibrated, and operator-type-calibrated Roofline models under the same planner and simulator budget (§4.2C, Table 2).
2. **Would explicit routing and communication modeling improve MoE decode?** DSv3-671B trails Bayesian optimization in best-or-tied-best TPOT. Add expert-load and inter-chip traffic terms, then compare candidate rankings and final TPOT under equal call budgets (§4.1, Table 1).
3. **Do the selected designs and call savings transfer beyond NeuSIM?** The noise experiment perturbs the same simulator. Re-evaluate a common set of proposed designs with an independent simulator or hardware measurements and compare rankings, constraint satisfaction, and final latency (§5, Appendix E.8).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied tables support the overall in-simulator sample-efficiency result. Material gaps include the prompt bodies under Appendix D, the exact adaptive gate implementation, headline uncertainty estimates, repeat-run variability, wall-clock runtime, and compute requirements. Repository readiness is uncertain because the abstract gives a code URL while the checklist says the repository was still being finalized.
