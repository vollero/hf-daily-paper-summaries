# WorldRuler: Interactive World Modeling under Configurable Rules

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24869) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24869)


## One-sentence takeaway

WorldRuler uses an executable 3D rule graph and local video denoising edits to carry user-defined rules across save and load and improve their visible execution over the reported RuleVerse baselines (§§3.2–3.5, Tables 1 and 4).

## Problem and motivation

The authors aim to make interactive video worlds follow configurable gameplay rules, including rules that depart from ordinary behavior. They identify two gaps in prior work: restoring rule dependencies and execution progress after a save, and showing each rule’s effect on the correct object at the right time, even when the effect occurred off-screen (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Executable 3D rule graph.** WorldRuler checkpoints rules, object states, execution progress, and visual bindings together. Dynamic binding maps rule targets to scene instances, while perceptual feedback updates correspondences and supplies confirmed events for later generation windows (§§3.1–3.3, Fig. 2).
2. **Local attribute replacement.** A frozen reader estimates attributes from masked video latents during denoising. A direct correction sets the reader’s output to the prescribed target under the stated rank condition, and a learned adjustment changes appearance and motion while preserving that readout. The writer is trained on paired reconstruction, state differences, and bidirectional attribute swaps (§§3.4–3.5, Fig. 3).
3. **RuleVerse.** An automated Habitat and GTA-V pipeline records paired rule-enabled and rule-disabled interactions with object, state, event, and visibility annotations. The dataset supports writer training and evaluation of off-screen effects and multi-stage tasks (§4, Fig. 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher rule and task scores in Habitat | WorldRuler averaged 64.99% RSR and 48.04% FCR versus SWM’s 44.06% and 17.98% on RuleVerse Habitat. Timing accuracy was 37.36% versus 15.67%. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Higher rule and task scores in GTA-V | WorldRuler averaged 67.42% RSR and 66.66% FCR on RuleVerse GTA-V. The highest baseline RSR was 44.19% for SWM, and the highest baseline FCR was 13.33% for MiniMax-H3. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Modest gains on the external benchmark | On 50 official WorldState-Bench pairs using the original judging protocol, WorldRuler scored 94% PairFollow, 99% CntAbsent, and 92% PairStrict versus SWM’s 92%, 98%, and 90%. It tied SWM on the other three metrics. WorldRuler used one 124-frame window and allowed up to two feedback corrections before publication. | §5.2, Table 2 |
| Dynamic binding improved first-appearance scores | On RuleVerse Habitat, first-appearance correctness was 29.69% with the full method and 20.58% with fixed binding. Preservation was 28.71% with the full method and 20.63% without the swapping loss. | §5.3, Table 3 |
| Joint checkpointing improved continuation | With identical Habitat checkpoints and continuation actions, joint restoration scored 98.17% consistency, 54.36% post-load RSR, and 47.89% post-load FCR. Restoring rules and attributes without execution progress scored 95.13%, 51.26%, and 45.53%, respectively. | §5.3, Table 4 |

The authors interpret these results as evidence that explicit state tracking and local generation control improve rule realization. The reported comparisons support gains under the tested protocols. The low absolute first-appearance score and the gap between post-load consistency and task completion leave reliable off-screen control and reliable task completion unestablished.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-described constraints.** First-appearance control requires spatial grounding before generation. Effects on unlocalized entities remain pending, and perceptual corrections apply to later windows. The exact attribute equality in Eq. 7 applies to the latent reader under the stated rank condition. Decoded visual correctness is assessed separately (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference on measurement.** RuleVerse uses a frozen vision-language judge on frames sampled at 3 fps, so brief triggers or timing errors may be missed. The supplied text gives no human-agreement check or uncertainty estimates for the reported scores (§4.3, Tables 1–4).
- **Analyst inference on attribution and scope.** The component and save/load studies are confined to Habitat. The joint checkpoint comparison does not isolate execution progress from retained bindings, and the single-window WorldState-Bench result leaves multi-window behavior untested on that benchmark (§§5.2–5.3).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does first-appearance accuracy depend on prior localization?** Table 3 reports an aggregate score, while §3.3 distinguishes grounded targets from unresolved ones. Evaluate matched scenes with and without prior grounding and measure correctness at first visibility and after binding.
2. **Which checkpoint fields account for the continuation gains?** Table 4 compares bundles of retained information. Independently vary saved execution progress and bindings while holding rules, attributes, and visual boundary context fixed.
3. **How sensitive are timing scores to the judging protocol?** RuleVerse reports one frame-sampling protocol (§4.3). Compare its judgments with denser sampling and human frame-level annotations on the same short events.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method description and reported comparisons. The supplied text leaves code, weights, and dataset access unconfirmed and omits training compute and inference latency, checkpoint and continuation sample counts, score uncertainty, human agreement with the visual judge, and whether external baselines received equivalent pre-publication correction opportunities.
