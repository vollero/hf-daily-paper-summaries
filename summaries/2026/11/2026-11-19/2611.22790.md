# NexSight: Verifiable Tasks for Fine-Grained Agentic Long-Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22790) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22790)


## One-sentence takeaway

NexSight uses independently checked video evidence to benchmark and train long-video agents, showing that adaptive search improves temporal evidence recall at a fixed frame budget and that combined QA and trajectory post-training improves interactive accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Fine-grained questions about long videos can hinge on brief moments scattered across the timeline. A clip can show that an action occurred, while verifying an exact count requires checking for missed or duplicate occurrences across the relevant span. The authors identify two gaps: answer scores alone conflate failures to find evidence with failures to use it, and final-answer supervision gives agents no direct feedback on where to look next (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **SightForge and SightLine.** SightForge proposes tasks from a timeline index, reacquires dense evidence from raw video, and requires a verifier who has not seen the draft answer to reconstruct it. Independent review and counterexample searches gate acceptance. The resulting SightLine benchmark pairs 310 tasks from 108 Video-MME Long videos with 1,080 annotated evidence artifacts (§§3, 5; Fig. 3).
- **SightLoop.** A shared, budgeted interface lets agents select temporal windows, inspect local image regions, revisit observations, and integrate evidence. It logs the frames actually returned, decoded timestamps, and resource use alongside answers (§4).
- **Sight-35B.** The authors post-train Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base on 5,938 examples combining direct QA with step-level solver actions and answers. Solvers work without reference answers or evidence annotations, and training and SightLine are disjoint by original-source video ID (§§5.4, 6.4; App. B).

The matched-budget study supports a temporal localization gain for the tested model and budget. The accuracy interval against Fixed includes zero, and several cross-window tasks show comparable or lower accuracy despite higher recall (§6.5). The authors interpret the Oracle gap as evidence that acquisition matters. Its size cannot be attributed to acquisition alone because reasoning effort, input format, and execution also change. The training comparison supports the combined recipe, and the external Direct-64 results measure fixed-input transfer.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** SightLine covers visual evidence and on-screen text from a limited video pool. Frame-hit recall records temporal intersections and leaves spatial detail unresolved. The joint training recipe leaves the individual effects of QA supervision and trajectory imitation open (§7).
- **Analyst inference.** Full-harness rankings reflect model and deployment differences because inference budgets vary. The matched-budget result isolates temporal selection for one model with spatial tools disabled (§6.2; Table 2; App. D).
- **Analyst inference.** External accuracy can reflect response completion as well as video understanding. On Video-MME Long Direct-64, Sight-35B produces valid non-empty responses on 849/900 questions versus 729/900 for Base, so the accuracy gain alone cannot quantify improved reasoning (App. G).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does trajectory supervision contribute beyond direct QA?** The paper tests the combined recipe (§7). Compare QA-only, trajectory-only, and combined training from the same checkpoint with matched training-token budgets.
2. **Which failures persist after the relevant frames are found?** Frame-hit recall does not measure whether a model resolves spatial details or integrates observations across windows (§§4.3, 6.5). Hold timestamps fixed and vary access to annotated crops and structured cross-window evidence, then score grounding and answer accuracy.
3. **How much of the Oracle gain comes from supplying evidence?** The Oracle changes several conditions at once (§6.3; App. E). Replay agent-selected and annotated evidence in the same single-pass format with matched reasoning effort and visual input budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported benchmark design, headline scores, and matched-budget comparison. The supplied text omits many table bodies and figure data, including complete per-model resource and validity profiles, exact content-domain counts, and absolute transfer scores for both checkpoints. Artifact categories and checksums are described, but download locations and public availability of the Sight-35B checkpoint and training trajectories are unconfirmed in the supplied text.
