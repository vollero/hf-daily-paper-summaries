# Decoding Without a Decoder: Direct Pixel Prediction from Diffusion Transformers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22287) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22287)


## One-sentence takeaway

A separate linear head that predicts pixels at the final denoiser evaluation replaces the standalone decoder in the tested latent diffusion transformers and improves fine detail in tested pixel-space models.

## Problem and motivation

Latent diffusion transformers rely on a separate decoder to turn sampled latents into pixels, adding deployment complexity and inference cost. The authors ask whether the transformer’s existing features can render pixels directly and whether separating final rendering from intermediate denoising lets perceptual losses improve detail without disrupting sampling. (§§1–3)

## Approach and contributions

- **Direct pixel readout.** A linear probe finds that frozen last-layer tokens reconstruct pixels more accurately than denoised latents. DWD maps those tokens directly to pixel patches at the final denoiser evaluation, at a positive timestep, and skips classifier-free guidance for that evaluation. (§3.2, Figs. 2–3)
- **Separate training objectives.** DWD freezes the transformer prefix and finetunes the final blocks and both heads. The pixel head receives reconstruction, perceptual, and late-stage adversarial losses. The denoising head is trained to match the frozen pretrained model’s velocity predictions. (§3.3, Appendix A.2)
- **Transfer to pixel-space and few-step models.** Pixel-space tests isolate the benefit of a separate rendering head because there is no decoder to remove. Few-step variants adapt the training timestep ranges to the sampler. (§3.4, Table 1, Appendix B.2)

The matched-loss shared-head ablation supports the separate-head design on the two tested pixel-space ImageNet models. The authors attribute the shared head’s worse FID to reduced diversity, but the reported FID and Inception Score do not directly measure that mechanism. The Wan decoder control supports a benefit beyond simply reusing the same data and pixel losses, although it trains different components for different durations. Final-step FLOP savings combine decoder removal, where applicable, with skipping the classifier-free guidance pair. (Table 1, Appendix A.5, Appendix B.1)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** Quality gains shrink when the original decoder is strong, as on FLUX.2. Linear-head sufficiency beyond 16×16 spatial patches remains open, and the authors did not tune pixel losses per model. The larger-patch MiniMax-H3 results are preliminary. (§5, Appendix B.5)
- **Analyst inference.** The ImageNet comparison for SiT-XL/2-REPA includes a guidance change, so its small FID gain cannot be attributed solely to DWD. The tables provide point estimates without run-to-run uncertainty. (Table 1, Appendix A.3)
- **Analyst inference.** Lower warping error supports improved motion-compensated frame consistency in the tested Wan configurations. Lower dynamic degree and imaging quality leave a trade-off that the reported metrics and selected examples do not resolve. (Tables 3 and 5, Fig. 6, Appendix A.4)

## Open research questions

1. **When do larger patches require a nonlinear head?** MiniMax-H3 results are preliminary and do not establish the best refiner. Compare linear and convolutional heads across patch sizes with matched training budgets, then measure quality and latency. (§5, Appendix B.5)
2. **How much does trajectory anchoring preserve same-seed content?** The supplied comparisons show selected examples and aggregate quality metrics. Sweep the anchoring weight, including zero, and measure intermediate trajectory drift and final composition across matched prompts and seeds. (§3.3, Figs. 5–6)
3. **Do temporal consistency gains hold at matched motion levels?** Wan’s warping error and dynamic degree both decline. Stratify prompts by motion, compare models at matched motion levels, and collect blinded ratings of flicker and detail. (Tables 3 and 5)
