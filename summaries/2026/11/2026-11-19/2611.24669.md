# Movie2Game-Bench: Holistic Evaluation of Film-to-Game Adaptation Beyond Playability

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24669) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24669)


## One-sentence takeaway

Movie2Game-Bench separates game quality, source fidelity, and player experience, revealing that film sequences suited to gameplay were the least-preserved source category for every evaluated system (§5.2, Fig. 4).

## Problem and motivation

A film supplies events, relationships, and audiovisual material from which a developer must devise game rules and player actions. Multiple mechanics can validly adapt the same scene. The authors identify a gap in game-generation benchmarks focused on functionality, presentation, or player experience and propose evaluating which source elements survive adaptation and what players can do with them (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Full-film adaptation benchmark.** Coding agents turn complete audiovisual films into self-contained browser games under a common artifact contract, with freedom to choose the genre and mechanics. The benchmark covers 22 films and 658 fixed, timestamp-grounded reference items across five categories (§3.1, App. A.3).
- **Evidence-grounded scoring protocol.** A general game-quality rubric, item-level adaptation judgments, and three independent GUI model playtests produce separate scores. Adaptation judgments distinguish content presence from interactive realization. The highest interaction rating requires both an observed gameplay step and supporting code (§4, Fig. 3).
- **Comparative analysis.** The authors compare 14 model–harness systems and use item-level judgments to examine which source elements survive and how retained content becomes playable (§§5.1–5.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Game quality and adaptation have different leaders | Across 14 primary model–harness systems on the same 22 films, with failures scored zero, Astra led General Evaluation (82.0/100) and Playtesting (59.0/100), while Gemini led Movie-specific Evaluation (36.0/100). Astra and Sol scored 55.4 and 55.2 Overall, respectively. Their unrounded difference was 0.109 points, with a paired film-bootstrap 95% interval of [−3.055, 3.082]. | §5.1, Table 1, App. C.3 |
| Playable sequences had the lowest content presence in every system | Means on the 0–100 presence scale, averaged equally across 14 primary systems after averaging over the same 22 films with failures scored zero, were 37.9 for playable sequences, 46.0 for characters and relationships, 52.8 for emotional development, 54.4 for plot, and 54.8 for iconic elements. | §5.2, Fig. 4, App. C.2 |
| Retained emotional development was expressed mostly through narrative choices | Across both evaluators and primary-setting games, among judgments with positive presence and a numeric interaction label, narrative choices and scenario operation accounted for 60.0% and 7.2% of emotional-development judgments, 44.3% and 12.6% of plot judgments, and 40.1% and 20.1% of playable-sequence judgments. | §5.3, Fig. 5 |
| Model playtesting scores tracked human rankings imperfectly and ran higher | In 100 distinct games rated blind by ten participants, one human score per game was compared with the mean of three GUI model players. Game-level Spearman correlation was 0.60, and model scores averaged 11.1 points higher on the 0–100 scale. Separately, the two movie-specific evaluators had Pearson r = 0.91 on paired valid game scores. | §5.1, App. C.4–C.5 |
| Harness choice changed observed rankings | On eight shared films, with failures scored zero, Kimi exceeded Opus in mean Overall under DeepSeek Harness (51.3 vs. 44.7), and Opus exceeded Kimi under Codex (45.4 vs. 39.7). | App. C.1, Table S5 |
| Similar Overall means came at different recorded generation costs | Over the same 22 primary-setting tasks, Sol averaged 55.2 Overall at $1.129 per film and Astra averaged 55.4 at $3.925. Cost includes model and media generation for the run of record. Failed runs retain their incurred cost and receive zero Overall. | §5.5, Fig. 7, App. C.7 |

The evidence supports a recurring preservation gap under this benchmark’s rubric and a distinction between strong general game scores and strong adaptation scores. The operation-form percentages describe retained-content judgments, so they cannot estimate how often all source items became playable. The paired interval leaves the Astra–Sol ordering uncertain across films, and the bootstrap holds the generated artifacts fixed.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ scope.** The inventory emphasizes source coverage and interaction. The authors acknowledge that transformative adaptations can pursue other artistic goals and identify longer games, additional engines, and broader human playtesting as future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference — exploration coverage.** Because untriggered fields receive zero and top interaction ratings require observed play, hard-to-find content can lower scores even when implemented. Fixed GUI budgets make this especially relevant for games with hidden branches (§§4.2–4.3, App. B.3).
- **Analyst inference — attribution.** Primary rankings combine model and harness effects. The matched harness comparison covers eight films, and the separate engine study varies instructions and resources alongside engine access, limiting attribution to the engine alone (§3.2, App. C.1, C.6).
- **Analyst inference — human validity.** One human rating per sampled game leaves human inter-rater reliability unknown. Correlation between the two movie-specific evaluators establishes score consistency across games, while agreement with independent human adaptation judgments remains unmeasured in the supplied text (App. C.4–C.5).

## Open research questions

1. **Can explicit scene-to-mechanic planning improve sequence preservation?** The study documents the gap and proposes explicit mapping as future work. Compare the original task with a planning condition that maps film sequences to player actions, game states, and feedback, holding the model, harness, films, and budget fixed (§§5.2, 6).
2. **How much of the preservation gap depends on exploration coverage?** Untriggered fields receive zero under bounded play. Re-evaluate the same games with standard and extended play budgets plus targeted branch exploration, then measure changes in status labels and category means (§§4.2–4.3).
3. **How do human players rate different ways of conveying a film’s emotional development?** The form counts are descriptive, and the human study collected one questionnaire per game. Have multiple blinded players rate matched adaptations expressing the same emotional beat through narrative choice or scenario operation (§5.3, App. C.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported protocol and headline descriptive findings. The supplied text omits full table cells, the complete film and item inventory, the original prompt files and full rubric wording, and per-run artifacts and judgments. It also leaves public asset availability and variability across repeated generations unresolved.
