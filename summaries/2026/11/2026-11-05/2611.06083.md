# SleuthVLM: Do We Really Need Audio-in-Video for Omni Video Understanding?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06083) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06083)


## One-sentence takeaway
SleuthVLM reports strong video question-answering results using a VLM that searches muted video and timestamped transcripts, supporting agentic evidence gathering while leaving the broader claim that audio training harms reasoning unproven (Tables 1, 3, 5).

## Problem and motivation
Single-pass omni models receive sampled frames and audio but cannot revisit evidence they missed. The authors ask whether a VLM can instead use transcribed speech to locate relevant moments and inspect them selectively, avoiding direct audio input to the reasoning model (§1).

The identified gap is a simple, training-free harness that lets one VLM control visual inspection and transcript search, rather than relying on fixed sampling or perception delegated to specialized models (§§2–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Agent harness:** A VLM autonomously searches transcripts, renders contact sheets, extracts frames, crops images, and watches muted clips before answering. A common guide and resource budget constrain exploration without fixing its trajectory (§3.3).
- **Speech as a temporal index:** ASR and forced alignment produce word timestamps; explicit gaps preserve intervals without recognized speech. These help localize visual evidence and check temporal coincidence (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Empirical comparison and ablations:** The paper evaluates different reasoning models, removes transcripts and word timestamps, and optionally adds an external audio-captioning model for information absent from text (§4; §12). The contribution is the harness and its evaluation, rather than a newly trained backbone or benchmark.

These results support transcript-guided agents as competitive systems on the evaluated benchmarks. They do not isolate direct audio access as harmful: backbone capability, training, inference resources, and harness differ across many comparisons. The optional audio tool also demonstrates that acoustic information can add value.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Scope acknowledged in the text:** Transcripts cannot resolve every musical or acoustic question; the authors add audio captioning for such cases (§§4.3, 5.2). Alignment failures require fallback to chunk-level timestamps (§9).
- **Analyst inference—causal overreach:** Cross-model results and one detailed failure case do not establish the authors’ claim that omni training causes loss of planning or reasoning. No controlled before/after training comparison is supplied (§5.3; §10).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Baseline scores come from other reports, without matched inference-cost evaluation (§4.1). The coding-agent comparison additionally contrasts full-benchmark performance with baselines evaluated on only the first 100 LVOmniBench questions (Table 9).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** All evaluated questions are multiple choice, and LVOmniBench is predominantly speech-based (§4.1; Table 4). General audio understanding and open-ended answering remain unestablished.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and efficiency:** No repeated-run variance or confidence intervals are provided. Aggregate end-to-end latency, token cost, and cost-matched accuracy comparisons are absent; a single-case efficiency comparison cannot establish typical savings (Table 11).
- **Reporting caveat:** “Best on every metric” for Video-MME-v2 applies to reported model entries; several strong baselines lack non-accuracy metrics, and human experts score higher (Table 12).

## Open research questions
1. **Does audio training itself reduce agentic ability?** Backbone differences confound the current evidence. Compare matched checkpoints before and after audio training using identical tools, transcripts, and budgets.
2. **When should an agent request acoustic evidence?** Audio captioning helps selected music questions unevenly. Test learned or rule-based routing against always-on and transcript-only variants on acoustically necessary questions.
3. **How robust is transcript indexing to recognition errors?** Alignment fallback is described, but sensitivity is not measured. Evaluate controlled transcription errors, timestamp shifts, and unsupported-language cases.
4. **What is the accuracy–cost tradeoff?** Broad cost-matched comparisons are missing. Sweep turn, frame, and time budgets against same-backbone single-pass baselines, including transcription overhead.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains detailed methods, tables, and ablations, but its strongest causal interpretation exceeds the experiments.

Material missing information includes release availability, complete executable prompts and code, run-to-run uncertainty, aggregate inference costs, ASR/alignment quality and fallback frequency, and controlled evidence connecting audio training to reasoning degradation.
