# On the Generalization Limitation of On-Policy Distillation for Post-RL Consolidation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05609) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05609)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the tested Qwen3 post-training settings, matching teachers on supervised capabilities does not ensure retention or transfer beyond that supervision, while broader data, base-preserving replay, and parameter patching provide partial remedies.

## Problem and motivation
Post-RL consolidation aims to combine specialized teachers’ improvements while preserving the base model’s broader capabilities. The authors identify a gap in evaluating this process: strong in-distribution (IND) distillation can conceal out-of-distribution (OOD) forgetting or failure to combine separately learned capabilities (§§1–3).

They distinguish **extrapolation** beyond supervised difficulty, **retention** of base-model performance, and **composition** of separately supervised capabilities. These outcomes require separate evaluation; teacher matching on component tasks does not establish any of them.

## Approach and contributions
- **Generalization diagnosis:** Compare single-teacher OPD and multi-teacher OPD (MOPD) with specialist RL teachers, external generalist teachers, and mixed RL. Students mainly start from Qwen3-8B; evaluations cover mathematical extrapolation and three capability combinations (§§3–6).
- **Bottleneck investigation:** Test whether stronger teachers and broader distillation data jointly improve OOD outcomes (§7.1).
- **Geometric analysis:** Measure update magnitude, spectral concentration, and overlap with single-task update spans. A complementary theoretical analysis concerns local updates under explicit assumptions, rather than a general guarantee (§7.2; Appendix D).
- **Mitigation experiments:** Introduce Procrustes-attention patching after distillation and base-preserving replay during distillation (§8).

OPD and MOPD themselves are existing methods; this paper’s contributions are the diagnosis, analysis, and mitigation experiments.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit difficulty extrapolation reveals incomplete transfer | A Qwen2-100M synthetic student transfers approximately **93–96%** of the teacher’s improvement on post-training IND problems requiring 11–14 operations, versus **76–86%** on OOD problems requiring 15–17 operations. The ratio measures improvement over the shared base using acc@1. | §4, Figure 1 |
| Specialist OPD matches IND performance while forgetting OOD | Across GSM8K-, MATH-, and DAPO-trained specialists, OPD averages **97.10%** of teacher IND scores, but its mean mathematical OOD score is **8.56 percentage points** below the base. This is **25.09% less forgetting** than the specialists’ 11.43-point loss. | §5.1, Figure 2 |
| MOPD reduces forgetting relative to mixed RL without eliminating it | Across pairwise and three-teacher mathematical mixtures, MOPD’s mean OOD score is **11.18 points** below the base, versus **15.73 points** for mixed RL: a **28.92% reduction** in forgetting. AIME uses avg@8. | §5.1, Figure 3 |
| A stronger external teacher alone is insufficient | Qwen3-32B → Qwen3-8B OPD across three mathematical training datasets reaches **99.34%** of teacher IND performance but remains **2.96 points below the base** on mean OOD performance. | §5.2, Figure 4 |
| Component gains can coexist with no composed-task gain | Code-plus-long-context MOPD improves HumanEval, HumanEval+, and LongBench-v2 Hard, yet scores **44.00%** on LongBench-v2 Code, matching the base and trailing teachers at **50.00%/58.00%**. Single-component OPD students score **42.00%/40.00%**. | Figure 7; Appendix B.1, Table A3 |
| Stronger teaching plus broader data removes measured forgetting in one setting | With Qwen3-32B supervision and **90% GSM8K + 10% OpenR1**, the student exceeds the base on all five mathematical benchmarks, matches teacher AIME24/25 **pass@8 at 86.67%/83.33%**, and reaches **100.00% AMC23 accuracy** versus the teacher’s **95.00%**. | §7.1, Figure 9 |
| OPD updates are smaller and more concentrated | Mean update norm is **0.154 versus 0.278** for specialist RL; the top 64 singular directions capture **69.0% versus 39.9%** of update energy. Single-OPD spans explain **11.0–18.6%** of MOPD update squared norm, versus **less than 0.240%** for corresponding mixed-RL projections. | §7.2, Table 1 |
| Patching partially recovers OOD performance | At patch strength **α = 0.40**, Procrustes-attention patching raises AIME25 **avg@8 from 60.83% to 65.00%**, below the base’s **65.83%**; patched GSM8K accuracy is **93.48%**. | §8.1, Figure 10 |
| Replay improves selected OOD outcomes | Coding replay improves **five of eight OOD scores** over GSM8K-only specialist OPD; AIME25 **avg@8 rises 6.67 points to 67.50%**, while GSM8K accuracy decreases **0.18 points**. | §8.2, Figure 11 |
| MOPD’s advantage over mixed RL is conditional | In a separate synthetic arithmetic experiment, five-seed mean AB/BA accuracies are **93%/90%** for mixed warm RL versus **78%/74%** for reverse-KL MOPD from warm-RL teachers. Direct composition-supervised SFT reaches **100%/100%**. | §9.2; Appendix C |

The evidence supports a failure of **reliable** transfer and retention under the tested recipes, rather than an impossibility result for distillation. The broader-data experiment also changes supervision coverage, so its success does not establish generalization beyond that expanded coverage. Its AIME **pass@8** results should not be directly compared with the main experiments’ **avg@8** results.

The geometric measurements are associations. The authors explicitly leave their causal relationship with generalization unresolved (§7.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Main experiments concentrate on Qwen3-8B students and a limited teacher pool. A preliminary larger-model experiment does not establish systematic scaling behavior (§9.1).
- **Operational OOD definition:** Mathematical aggregates classify benchmarks using specialist performance relative to the base. The authors acknowledge that this measures compatibility with post-training behavior, not actual distribution membership or absence from pretraining (Appendix A.2.1).
- **Comparison limits:** MOPD and mixed RL use fixed mixtures without extensive balancing or curriculum tuning. Training configurations also differ in responses per prompt, so the supplied comparison does not establish equal-compute superiority (§9.1; Table A1).
- **Replication limits:** Sampling-seed checks on a mathematical subset support qualitative consistency but do not establish robustness across independent training runs (§9.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The expanded-data result does not fully separate teacher strength, data diversity, and training-budget effects; a controlled factorial comparison would be needed (§7.1).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Introductory compositional IND summaries differ from figure-level values—for example, Figure 7 reports 95.5% teacher matching, below the introduction’s stated range. Figure 10 also labels its 60.83 AIME25 reference as “RL teacher,” whereas §8.1 describes improvement from the OPD student. These inconsistencies warrant care when interpreting summaries and patching results.

## Open research questions
1. **Does update geometry causally improve retention?** Current measurements are correlational. Manipulate update directions with random-direction controls while matching norm, training budget, and IND performance (§9.3).
2. **Which expanded supervision produces transferable gains?** The OpenR1 experiment changes coverage and succeeds on evaluated benchmarks. Compare matched-budget data mixtures and test on held-out difficulty levels beyond the expanded supervision (§§7.1, 9.3).
3. **Can tuned consolidation close compositional gaps?** Fixed mixtures leave each objective’s best achievable performance unknown. Sweep mixture weights and curricula for MOPD and mixed RL under matched budgets (§9.1).
4. **How broadly do patching and replay work?** Reported intervention evidence centers on mathematical specialist OPD. Test both interventions across model families, independent training seeds, and composed tasks (§§8–9).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods and direct evidence for the central empirical finding, but broader conclusions face narrow model coverage and limited training-run replication.

Material missing information includes confirmed code/checkpoint releases, comprehensive training-seed uncertainty, complete compute accounting, patch-strength selection and broader patching sweeps, and exact replay proportions and training budgets.
