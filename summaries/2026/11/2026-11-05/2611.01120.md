# StreamDuo: A Live Commentator and a Research Agent for Unseen Games

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01120) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01120)


## One-sentence takeaway
StreamDuo combines learned requests for wiki assistance with privileged self-distillation to improve entity naming and reduce source-game name leakage when a commentator trained on one game is evaluated on ten others (Table 1; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Live game commentary requires both timely reactions and knowledge of characters, items, places, and players. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that train and test on the same games: under their cross-game protocol, speaking timing transfers better than entity knowledge, and commentators sometimes substitute names from their training game (§§1, 3.1).

A static game dossier provides little help identifying what is currently visible. Streaming retrieval additionally requires deciding when assistance is useful and handling answers that arrive after the scene has progressed (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Cross-game evaluation:** Train on Baldur’s Gate 3 alone, evaluate on ten other games, sanitize contextual game names, and measure source-game leakage alongside timing and knowledge metrics (§§4.1, 6).
- **Asynchronous agent interaction:** A trainable commentator sends current frames to a frozen vision-language research agent with wiki search. Up to three entity entries enter its history after a fixed two-second delay while commentary continues (§3.2).
- **Learning when to ask:** Information-gain labels identify moments where an ideal answer increases the likelihood of the human commentary. Random entity renaming discourages memorization, while training answers include distractors and omissions (§3.3).
- **Learning to use answers:** On-policy self-distillation trains the commentator using noisy answers against a frozen copy given ideal answers. Answer-amplified teacher predictions and reverse KL guide LoRA updates (§3.4).

The contribution is this task formulation and integrated training/interface design; retrieval and self-distillation themselves are acknowledged prior methods (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better entity naming and timing than matched Proact-VL | Both trained on 10,286 Baldur’s Gate 3 clips; tested on 2,470 clips from ten other games. Ent-R: **0.082 → 0.116**; Ent-F1: **0.118 → 0.141**; K-strict: **0.085 → 0.127**. Knowledge metrics use the first 126 words per clip. TimeDiff: **1.84 → 1.76 s**; timing F1: **0.536 → 0.552**. | §4.1; Table 1 |
| Higher judged liveliness and overall quality | On the same test set, Qwen3-30B scores LiveU **4.91 → 5.14** and FinalQ **3.32 → 3.53** versus matched Proact-VL. StreamDuo leads Table 1 except on CC. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Substantially less source-game leakage | Without a word cap, Proact-VL produces **200 leaked names among 2,011 total names (9.9%)**; StreamDuo produces **34 among 3,363 (1.0%)**, including **3,329 test-game mentions**. | Table 5; §6 |
| Stage 2 supplies the largest incremental knowledge gain | Adding self-distillation to Stage 1 raises Ent-R **0.092 → 0.116** and K-strict **0.094 → 0.127**, under identical inference settings. | Table 2 |
| Learned asking trades recall for precision and quality | Asking every three seconds yields Ent-R **0.199** but Ent-F1 **0.105** and FinalQ **3.36**; learned asking yields **0.116**, **0.141**, and **3.53**, respectively. Disabling asking yields FinalQ **3.54**, so learned asking does not improve that score. | Table 3 |
| Measured compute fits the imposed delay | On one H100, one request at a time: research-agent latency averages **0.405 s** over 200 moments; commentator compute averages **0.256 s per video second** over 20 clips. Proposed live deployment uses two GPUs. | Table 6; §6.1 |

These results support improved knowledge grounding under the specified cross-game protocol, with component ablations supporting the training design. They do not establish universal superiority: periodic retrieval achieves higher recall, learned asking does not beat no asking on every judge metric, and quality judgments come from an LLM rather than a reported human study.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed failures:** The commentator sometimes repeats irrelevant retrieved entities or recites encyclopedia-style descriptions instead of contextual commentary (§§4.8, 7).
- **Analyst inference—limited transfer scope:** Evaluation uses one source game and one benchmark. “Unseen” establishes exclusion from commentary training; the text does not establish exclusion from pretrained models’ data (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—dependence on supporting knowledge:** The method assumes an available game wiki and ideal answers during training. Performance with incomplete wikis or imperfect ideal-answer construction is not established (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation uncertainty:** No confidence intervals, repeated-seed results, or human preference evaluation are reported. Entity matching measures overlap with human-mentioned wiki entities rather than exhaustive factual correctness (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—ablation and reporting gaps:** Renaming and dossier provision are bundled in Table 2. The continuation threshold is described but its value is absent. Table 4 describes answering every second while reproducing the learned-policy main result; how cached answers and actual requests interact is unclear (§§3.2, 4.3, 4.7).

## Open research questions
- **Does the method transfer from other source games?** Only Baldur’s Gate 3 supplies training data. Repeat training with several source games and compare held-out entity recall, leakage, and timing.
- **How robust is asynchronous grounding to variable latency?** Evaluation fixes the answer delay. Sweep delays and introduce latency jitter, measuring whether answers still describe the current scene.
- **Can weaker supervision replace ideal answers?** Alternative teachers add less knowledge (§4.6). Vary training-answer correctness and annotation effort to measure the supervision–performance tradeoff.
- **Do people prefer the resulting commentary?** Reported quality uses LLM judging, while qualitative examples include misplaced wiki exposition. Run blinded human comparisons covering factuality, relevance, liveliness, and interruption timing.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main comparisons and method clearly, but leaves material uncertainty about statistical reliability and deployment generalization.

Missing information includes code/checkpoint release status, ideal-answer construction details, complete judge rubrics and metric definitions, per-game quantitative breakdowns beyond selected leakage results, continuation-threshold settings, and clarification of the every-second research-agent evaluation protocol.
