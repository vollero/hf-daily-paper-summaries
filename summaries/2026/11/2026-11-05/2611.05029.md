# DRAP: Dynamics-Risk Adaptive Plasticity for Continual Learning in Physical AI

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05029) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05029)


## One-sentence takeaway
DRAP combines demonstration-derived dynamics sensitivity with tensor-level learning-rate suppression to improve replay-free OpenVLA retention across sequential LIBERO suites, according to qualitative results whose numerical tables are absent from the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation
Sequential policy adaptation can erase earlier robot skills, while small action errors can change subsequent observations and compound into trajectory failure. The author identifies a gap in importance-based continual learning: model sensitivity alone does not represent the physical consequences of behavioral errors (§§I–IV).

The target setting permits only current-suite demonstrations during training and preserves one checkpoint across Spatial → Object → Goal (§III).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dynamics-risk estimator:** Fit ridge-regularized local linear dynamics to standardized expert state-action transitions. Use the largest singular value of the state-transition matrix as a relative indicator of one-step perturbation amplification, then median-normalize and clip it (§§VI-B–C).
- **Risk-weighted tensor importance:** After each suite, average sample-level squared action-loss gradients within each adaptable tensor, weighted by squared risk. Normalize and accumulate these scalar importance values across suites (§VI-D).
- **Direct plasticity control:** Convert cumulative importance into tensor-specific AdamW learning rates, protecting important tensors from the first update of the next stage without reference-parameter penalties (§VI-D).

KappaTune supplies the fixed adaptable subnetwork; it is an existing component combined with DRAP rather than a new contribution (§§II, VI-A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Risk measures local state sensitivity under a fixed logged action, omitting policy feedback. It is not a closed-loop stability certificate and does not capture nonlinearities, contact transitions, observation aliasing, or departures from the expert neighborhood (§VI-C).
- **Author-stated scope:** Longer sequences and richer control-path, multi-step, online, and contact-aware risk estimators remain future work (§VIII).
- **Analyst inference:** One suite order and one evaluated VLA leave generalization to other orders, architectures, and physical deployments unestablished (§§III, VII).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed-checkpoint rollout repeats do not quantify training variability (§VII).
- **Reproducibility gap:** The supplied text lacks numerical table contents and key experimental settings, including training budgets, selected tensor counts, dynamics-window and ridge settings, importance-sample counts, and suppression hyperparameters. Code availability is asserted, but its contents are not supplied.

## Open research questions
1. **Does richer risk estimation improve retention?** The current score omits control-path and multi-step sensitivity (§VI-C). Compare matched-budget variants incorporating these terms against current and uniform-risk DRAP.
2. **Does protection persist over longer or reordered streams?** Only Spatial → Object → Goal is evaluated (§III). Test multiple suite orders and longer task streams with independent training seeds.
3. **How much benefit comes from physical-risk alignment?** The uniform-risk ablation shows an incremental benefit but does not test whether correctly aligned risk is essential (§VII-D). Compare aligned risk with shuffled risk weights while holding the weight distribution and training setup fixed.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method is described clearly, but performance conclusions rely on qualitative author reports.

Material missing information includes Tables I–III contents, numerical effect sizes and uncertainty, independent training-seed results, detailed hyperparameters and compute overhead, and evidence beyond the evaluated OpenVLA suite sequence.
