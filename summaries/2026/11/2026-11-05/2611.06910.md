# FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06910) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910)


## One-sentence takeaway

FrontierPhysics evaluates complete physics research workflows on 56 expert-authored tasks, with the best tested configuration passing 27.4% of rollouts and a single-model expert audit identifying reasoning rigor and expert intuition as prominent weaknesses (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that fixed-answer questions, isolated research subtasks, and reproduction with supplied papers inadequately test open-ended scientific inquiry. Their proposed gap is an evaluation combining authentic research problems, independently chosen methods, numerical correctness, manuscript quality, and expert inspection of failures (§1).

This matters because producing correct numerical outputs alone does not establish that an agent selected defensible methods, validated its findings, or communicated scientifically supported conclusions.

## Approach and contributions

- **Expert-authored benchmark:** 56 tasks across nine physics domains, created by 42 researchers from their own projects. Tasks provide high-level research instructions and supporting inputs where needed, requiring structured artifacts and a scientific manuscript (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Combined evaluation:** Deterministic verifiers check outputs; a reviewer agent evaluates trajectories, code, artifacts, and manuscripts using global and expert-authored criteria. Passing requires every verifier test and blocking criterion; weighted non-blocking criteria measure quality (§2.3, Eq. 1).
- **Task quality review:** Provenance checks, domain review, trial runs, and failure inspection seek to preserve open-endedness while reducing grading errors (§2.2).
- **Empirical evaluation and diagnosis:** The authors evaluate 22 models in 26 configurations and manually audit GPT-6 Astra trajectories, producing a failure taxonomy and identifying consequential false failures (§3; Appendix E).

The evidence supports difficulty on this benchmark under the tested configurations. It does not establish actual journal publishability: that is the authors’ interpretation of passing their evaluation gates. The failure audit supports descriptive findings for GPT-6 Astra, without establishing the same failure distribution across all evaluated agents or causal explanations for their weaknesses.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope limit:** Detailed failure analysis and accurate false-negative correction cover GPT-6 Astra only; other model–harness pairs may exhibit different failures (§3.4).
- **Remaining grading uncertainty:** Expert review found consequential errors after task curation. The alternative reviewer was reportedly more lenient toward its own model family (§§2.3, 3.3).
- **Analyst inference—reference validation is incomplete:** High scores on selected manuscript criteria do not validate the entire evaluation pipeline. Some expert reference materials fail blockers, and the supplied text does not explain those cases (Table 11).
- **Analyst inference—comparisons mix model and harness effects:** Models use different harnesses, and Claude Code accounting includes subagent work. The supplied evidence does not isolate model capability from orchestration (§3.1; Appendix G.1).
- **Resource and outcome accounting:** Unavailable outcomes count as failures, whereas missing resource counters are omitted from resource means. Provider accounting differs, and latency can inflate execution time (Appendices F–G).
- **Bounded openness:** Tasks retain predefined goals and reference-based checks. The authors identify independent problem definition and evaluation of unexpected findings as future needs (§4).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the observed weaknesses generalize across agents?** The manual audit covers one model. Next experiment: apply blinded expert annotation to matched tasks from several model–harness pairs (§3.4).
2. **How stable are rankings under expert adjudication?** False failures and reviewer-family leniency remain concerns. Next experiment: independently adjudicate verifier-passing submissions and a sample of failures across configurations (§§2.3, 3.3).
3. **Can independent validation improve research rigor?** Circular self-verification is a recurring observation, but no intervention is tested. Next experiment: compare matched agents with and without a separately constructed validation procedure (§3.4).
4. **Can evaluation recognize valid unexpected discoveries?** Current tasks use prescribed goals and correctness gates. Next experiment: pilot tasks with expert review explicitly assessing partial progress and defensible findings outside reference pathways (§4).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the benchmark and headline findings, but omits most numerical table bodies and figure contents.

Material missing information includes complete model rankings and confidence intervals; per-configuration missing-outcome counts; detailed cost and resource measurements; numerical reviewer-agreement results; full task prompts, verifiers, and rubrics; and concrete benchmark download or release instructions. These gaps prevent independent assessment of ranking significance, reproducibility, and detailed efficiency comparisons.
