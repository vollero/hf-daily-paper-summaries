# What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06483) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the evaluated agents, multi-turn RL improves error recovery and long-horizon success more consistently than individual decisions, while BudgetMix training reduces the loss of gains under tight interaction budgets (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Trajectory success can conceal weak individual actions and dependence on opportunities to retry. The authors ask whether multi-turn RL improves decisions generally or primarily helps agents recover and sustain execution. They identify evaluation across interaction budgets and matched intermediate states as gaps in conventional success-based assessment (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Budget-sensitive diagnosis:** Apply the previously introduced Pass@(K, T) framework to distinguish sampling breadth from interaction depth, with structural solvability checks (§§2–3; Appendix B.3).
- **Decision and recovery analysis:** Compare policies at identical states, injected error states, and trajectory depths; supplement this with likelihood and training-dynamics analyses (§§3.3–4).
- **BudgetMix RL:** Uniformly sample training budgets from unique rounded values at 10% intervals of the full budget. Each rollout group shares a budget, prompts disclose available turns, and unsuccessful budget exhaustion receives zero terminal success reward. The method retains the corresponding RL algorithm and reward design (§5.2; Appendix A.4).
- **Mitigation comparison:** Examine when process signals help and evaluate BudgetMix with GRPO, GiGPO, and PPO (§5).

Pass@(K, T) itself is attributed to prior work, rather than introduced here (§6.2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| RL trajectory gains depend on interaction depth | At fixed sampling breadth **K=8**, GRPO advantages shrink or reverse as turn budgets tighten across the four main tasks; structural ceilings leave substantial headroom. | §3.2; Figures 3, 10 |
| Ordinary matched-state decisions do not uniformly improve | Qwen3-4B GRPO beats base action quality on **46.8%** of DeepCoder-MT states and **42.8%** of FrozenLake states; mean RL-minus-base quality is **−0.005** and **−0.018**, respectively. Scores average **16 actions per policy per state**, using unit-test pass rates or finite-horizon optimal Q-values. | §3.3; Figure 4 |
| Recovery is stronger on constructed error states | For the same GRPO analysis, RL wins action quality on **67.3%** of DeepCoder-MT and **65.7%** of FrozenLake error states. Continuation success is **38.5% vs. 22.9%** and **51.3% vs. 38.6%**, respectively, for RL versus base. | §3.3; Figure 5 |
| Tool-call recovery also improves | On **191** identical BFCL error prefixes, next-action correction is **41.4% RL vs. 27.2% base**. | Appendix E.4 |
| Trajectory policy separation survives approximate length matching | With scored token counts matched within **10%**, RL-generated minus base-generated cumulative log-likelihood-ratio differences are **185.9 nats** on Terminal and **805.8 nats** on BFCL; both reported **95% confidence intervals** exceed zero. | Appendix E.2; Figure 23 |
| BudgetMix improves constrained-budget success | Across four tasks, two Qwen3 sizes, and three RL algorithms, reported mean **Pass@8** improvements over full-budget RL are **7.5, 5.3, and 4.0 percentage points** at **25%, 50%, and 75%** of maximum turns. | Table 1; §5.4 |
| BudgetMix preserves local quality during training | On DeepCoder-MT, full-budget training changes trajectory/step rewards by **+0.331/−0.097**, versus **+0.323/+0.006** for BudgetMix. Step rewards use a fixed held-out state pool. | Figure 8; Appendix E.3 |

These results support the authors’ behavioral interpretation within the evaluated settings. Likelihood separation offers a possible explanation involving more reliable trajectory composition; it does **not** establish a causal mechanism or prove that successful trajectories are unreachable by the base model.

BudgetMix generally retains full-budget performance, but does not win every cell: Table 1 includes small full-budget regressions. Thus, “consistent improvement” is best read as an aggregate pattern.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Error states are proxies, constructed through distance-increasing FrozenLake actions or code mutations that reduce test performance (Appendix B.5).
- **Author-stated:** Reliable step-quality measurements and process rewards are difficult to obtain; direct quantitative action-quality analysis focuses on FrozenLake and DeepCoder-MT (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Recovery and benchmark success do not establish safety for irreversible actions. Such deployments motivate the paper but are not directly tested (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The likelihood argument remains observational. Action likelihood scores exclude reasoning tokens, while trajectory likelihood ratios include complete responses; length matching only partly addresses confounding (§4.1; Appendix E.2).
- **Analyst inference:** BudgetMix comparisons match algorithm settings, but equal total training tokens, environment interactions, and compute are not documented. Budget-distribution and budget-prompt ablations are also absent (Appendix A).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §4.1 describes per-token NLL, whereas its displayed definition sums action-token log probabilities, leaving normalization unclear.

## Open research questions
- **Does recovery explain the budget effect causally?** Current evidence is correlational. Compare training variants that selectively reward recovery versus initial-action accuracy, then measure both budget curves and matched-state quality.
- **Which budget distribution works best?** Only a fixed uniform distribution over candidate budgets is specified. Compare uniform, deployment-weighted, and adaptive distributions under equal training compute.
- **Does robustness transfer beyond disclosed turn limits?** Training and evaluation explicitly reveal budgets. Test hidden or unexpectedly reduced budgets, alongside token- and time-limited execution.
- **Do local gains extend to irreversible tasks?** Current quantitative scoring centers on coding and grid planning. Evaluate BudgetMix in a sandbox with irreversible tool actions and explicit action-level harm metrics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed protocols and converging benchmark evidence, but causal and deployment interpretations remain limited.

Material missing information includes dataset and evaluation-pool sizes for several analyses, training-run seed variability, uncertainty for Table 1, complete compute accounting, exact fractional-budget rounding in evaluation, and confirmed availability of code, checkpoints, and constructed datasets.
