# VeriSpoof: Beyond Real or Fake with One Audio LLM

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04634) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04634)


## One-sentence takeaway
VeriSpoof combines speech deepfake detection, source analysis and checkable rationales in one audio language model, reporting strong benchmark detection while exposing calibration, localization and source-comparison weaknesses (§§6.1–6.8).

## Problem and motivation
A forensic analyst needs more than a synthetic-speech score: they may need to identify the generator, compare recordings’ sources and inspect the evidence behind a decision. The authors identify three gaps in existing open systems: separate models for separate questions, explanations without independently checked statements, and limited controlled evidence about which model components or supervision improve performance (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified forensic question answering.** A Qwen3-ASR encoder supplies pooled and temporal audio tokens to MiniCPM5-2B. Candidate answer sentences are scored by likelihood; binary tasks use their log-likelihood difference. The same weights support detection, attribution, recording-condition analysis, localization and source verification (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Checkable rationale training.** Fixed rationale fields use targets derived from waveform measurements, augmentation logs, span labels and generator metadata. The detection decision follows the stated localization. Counterfactual training reverses supplied localizations and trains the corresponding decision (§§3.3–3.5).
- **Controlled studies and evaluation assets.** The paper studies representations, LLM choice, channel shortcuts and source supervision, and reports releasing Base/Plus models, inference code and VeriSpoof-Bench with item lists, labels, scoring scripts and model outputs, without audio (§§4–6; Appendix A).

Training proceeds through perception alignment, forensic question tuning and rationale tuning, with earlier question-answering tasks replayed during the final stage (§3.4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong reported open-weight detection | On all 14 Speech-DF-Arena protocols, pooled EER is **2.14% for Plus**, **4.43% for Base**, versus **9.52% for DF-Arena-1B**; Plus mean EER is **1.79%**. Authors place Plus third of 28 in the dated public snapshot. | §6.1; Table 1; Figure 1a |
| Source verification transfers to held-out generators | On MLAAD spoof–spoof pairs, Plus EER is **6.17%** for unseen models of known architectures and **15.00%** for unseen architectures; the frozen DF-Arena-1B probe scores **26.5%** and **24.8%**, respectively. | Table 4 |
| Source supervision has a selective benefit | On CodecFake+, multitask training yields **2.24% EER**, versus **12.62%** for authenticity-only training with matched steps and approximately five times as many authenticity questions; difference **10.38 points**, 95% bootstrap interval **[8.73, 11.59]**. | §6.5; Table 10 |
| Rationales improve some detection decisions | On the same **500 PartialEdit utterances**, Base’s decision accuracy rises from **78.4%** directly to **99.6%** after reasoning. Improvement is not universal: In-the-Wild falls from **99.4%** to **98.8%**. | Table 13 |
| Decisions follow tested rationale interventions | Paraphrased inverted localizations, held out as an edit form, receive **99.5%** decision-following with counterfactual training versus **73.8%** without. | §6.8; Figure 8 |
| Checkability does not ensure correctness | Across Base’s rationale sets, bandwidth-statement accuracy is **0.64–0.91**; stated-span IoU is **0.25** on PartialSpoof and **0.31** on LlamaPartialSpoof, versus synthesized-token F1 of **0.74** and **0.82**. | §6.6; Tables 11–12 |
| Pooled calibration can fail by domain | At Plus’s pooled SpoofRadar EER threshold, **92.9%** of genuine Russian XMAD-Bench audiobook recordings are classified as spoofed, despite within-language EERs of **≤1.95%** across the discussed languages. | §6.1, pp. 10–11 |
| Small-GPU training is partly measured | With checkpointing and micro-batch **32**, logged stage peaks are **16.8 / 11.1 / 14.4 GiB**. Training on a **12 GB** GPU and inference on an **8 GB** GPU are estimates, not demonstrated configurations. | §6.9; Table 15 |

These results support a unified model with strong detection and useful structured explanations on the supplied protocols. They do not establish universally reliable forensic evidence: checks can expose false statements, source comparison remains sensitive to degradation, and following edited text does not establish that the original statement was acoustically correct. Leaderboard comparisons were computed locally using public protocol files, without leaderboard submissions (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Calibration and data-mixture tradeoffs—author reported.** Plus improves overall detection but worsens several individual sets and substantially shifts scores on some unseen genuine speech. The authors recommend domain-specific threshold calibration (§6.1).
- **Source comparison remains difficult—author reported.** Degraded pair sets have substantial errors, and new architectures are harder than new models of known architectures. Verification rationales can also be misled by matching attributes (§§6.2, 6.7).
- **Rationales remain fallible—author reported.** Bandwidth and clipping statements can be wrong; writing many short synthetic spans is a bottleneck (§6.6).
- **Supervision is not uniformly beneficial—author reported.** Source supervision hurts ASVspoof 2021 LA, while many benefits become equivalent or inconclusive under matched training steps (§6.5).
- **Faithfulness has a restricted interpretation—analyst inference.** The intervention tests establish dependence on supplied localization text under tested edits, rather than complete causal faithfulness to waveform evidence (§§3.5, 6.8).
- **Some causal attribution is limited—analyst inference.** Representation-ladder step R4 changes both the one-class loss and ASVspoof 5 training coverage, so it cannot isolate their individual effects. The proposed attention-based explanation for comparison performance is also not directly isolated (Table 8).
- **Resource claims have boundaries—author reported.** Lower-memory training, quantization and alternatives to bf16 were not tested (§6.9).

## Open research questions
1. **Can calibration transfer to unseen domains?** Plus’s language-dependent false alarms leave this unresolved. Next experiment: calibrate using limited genuine speech from held-out domains and measure false-alarm and detection rates (§6.1).
2. **Can localization improve without sacrificing detection?** Token predictions outperform written spans on fragmented edits. Next experiment: compare text-generated spans with intervals decoded from the token head on identical recordings (§6.6).
3. **How robust is source verification to channel mismatch?** Degraded pairs remain difficult. Next experiment: vary each recording’s codec and noise independently while holding generator identity fixed (Table 4).
4. **Can rationale dependence remain reliable when evidence is wrong?** Edited-localization obedience is high, but generated statements can fail checks. Next experiment: evaluate whether the model flags conflicts between supplied rationale fields, waveform evidence and token predictions (§§6.6–6.8).
