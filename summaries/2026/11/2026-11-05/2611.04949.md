# HERIT: Enabling Global Access to Hanja-to-English Historical Translation by Mitigating Temporal Bias

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04949) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04949)


## One-sentence takeaway
HERIT combines retrieval-assisted synthetic translation data with two-stage fine-tuning to improve direct Hanja-to-English translation, with expert evaluations supporting gains beyond the reign dominating its human-translated training data.

## Problem and motivation
English translations of Korean historical archives are scarce and concentrated in King Sejong’s reign, creating a risk of temporal overfitting. General Chinese translation models also struggle with Korean readings of names, historical terminology, and Hanja-specific syntax and semantics. The authors seek scalable translation of the Annals of the Joseon Dynasty (AJD) and Journal of the Royal Secretariat (JRS) that generalizes across reigns (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Auxiliary-resource pseudo-labeling:** Gemini-2.5-Flash generates five drafts using existing Korean translations, a glossary, and retrieved expert-translated examples; a second generation stage fuses the drafts. The final configuration uses different retrieval queries and example counts for drafting and fusion (§4.2).
- **Two-stage domain adaptation:** Qwen3 models first learn from approximately 315K pseudo-labeled records, then from 15.7K human-translated records. The resulting model translates directly from Hanja without inference-time retrieval (§§3–4.3).
- **Evaluation and access assets:** The authors provide cross-reign automated and expert evaluations and report releasing the model and translations of previously untranslated AJD/JRS documents (§§4.3, 5.3).

The contribution is the historical-translation pipeline and its evaluation; retrieval augmentation and draft fusion are adopted techniques, rather than newly introduced algorithms (§§2, 4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Auxiliary resources substantially improve pseudo-labeling | On reference test set \(D_{\text{test}}\), Gemini-2.5-Flash rises from 15.720 BLEU with the base prompt to 25.457 with glossary and Korean text, then 36.430 with 12 Korean-query retrieved examples. | Table 3 |
| Fusion provides an additional, smaller gain | Pseudo-labeling scores: single-stage 64-example retrieval 37.905 BLEU; Best-of-N 38.337; FusioN 38.462. These use auxiliary resources, rather than Hanja-only input. | Tables 3, 8; §5.1 |

| Direct translation beats the evaluated pivot baseline | On \(D_{\text{test}}\), HERIT scores 38.432 BLEU versus 35.539 for the Hanja→Korean→English baseline. | Table 4; §5.2 |
| Cross-reign model judging favors HERIT | Sonnet-4.6 ratings on held-out \(D_{\text{test}}^{NT}\), on a 1–10 scale: Qwen3-32B HERIT 8.009 versus human-only 6.883 and human-only with glossary/RAG 7.003. Judge receives Korean references and glossary information. | Table 5; §5.3 |
| Experts favor HERIT | Three experts evaluated a nominal 200-item cross-reign sample under double-blind conditions: mean score 3.93 versus 3.22 on a 1–5 scale; reported preference shares are 66.84% versus 33.16%. | Figure 3; §5.3; Appendix D |
| Proper-noun accuracy improves under LLM judging | On \(D_{\text{test}}\), Sonnet-4.6 reports accuracy 0.878 for two-stage Qwen3-32B versus 0.818 for human-only training and 0.875 for mixed training. | Table 11; Appendix C.3 |
| Large-scale translation is operationally demonstrated | Authors report translating 2.09 million AJD/JRS documents in 94.6 hours using eight H200 GPUs. | §4.3 |

The evidence supports improved translation within the evaluated AJD settings and better performance across sampled reigns. It does not establish error-free translations, equivalent quality on JRS, or generalization to other historical languages. Cross-reign gains concern records from periods represented in synthetic training, rather than wholly unseen historical periods (§§3, 5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated constraints:** Pseudo-labeling targets AJD only. Training limits of 2,048 tokens and 800 Hanja characters exclude approximately 6.7% of training data; longer deployment documents are chunked (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—test-set selection risk:** Backbone selection and pseudo-labeling hyperparameter comparisons explicitly use \(D_{\text{test}}\), despite a separate validation split being defined. This weakens its independence as a final benchmark (§§3–5.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation uncertainty:** Confidence intervals, significance tests, inter-rater agreement, and detailed score aggregation are not supplied. The human subset is described as 200 items, although §3 reports removing one during decontamination.
- **Analyst inference—temporal coverage ambiguity:** The cross-reign set is described as excluding Sejong, yet §6.1 discusses Sejong within the human-score analysis. Exact evaluation membership is unclear.
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** H2KE’s BLEU is copied from its original paper rather than rerun; comparability is therefore uncertain (Table 4).
- **Internal tension:** The Limitations section anticipates benefits from diverse proposer models, but the tested diverse-model configuration performs worse than the single-model configuration (Table 9).

## Open research questions
- **Does HERIT generalize to entirely unseen reigns?** Synthetic training covers the evaluated historical periods. Next experiment: withhold whole reigns from both human and synthetic training, then obtain independent expert ratings.
- **How well does it translate JRS and long documents?** Deployment covers JRS, but pseudo-labeling and reported evaluations focus on AJD; chunking quality is unmeasured. Next experiment: expert-evaluate JRS and long-document subsets, comparing chunking with longer-context training.
- **Which synthetic-data components drive downstream gains?** Pipeline components are ablated mainly through pseudo-label quality, rather than matched downstream training runs. Next experiment: train models on equal-sized synthetic corpora generated with and without glossary, retrieval, Korean inputs, and fusion.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Multiple evaluation methods support the central improvement claim, but test-set tuning and reporting ambiguities limit certainty.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, expert agreement and aggregation details, exact post-filter evaluation sizes and reign membership, comprehensive decontamination checks against synthetic training data, JRS-specific quality measurements, and validation of long-document chunking. Model and corpus availability is author-reported and was not independently checked.
