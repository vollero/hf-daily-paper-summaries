# Multi-layer Indexer: Overlapping Selection and Attention in DeepSeek Sparse Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03768) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03768)


## One-sentence takeaway
Multi-layer Indexer overlaps DSA token selection with attention by consuming an earlier layer’s selection, improving reported single-request decode throughput over IndexCache while introducing small, sometimes measurable quality losses.

## Problem and motivation
DSA’s indexer scans the entire token cache before sparse attention can begin, creating a serial bottleneck that grows with context. IndexCache reduces how many layers run indexers but leaves retained scans serial. The authors exploit overlap between neighboring layers’ selections to reduce the time spent waiting for those remaining scans (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Overlapped scheduling:** Attention consumes a preceding retained layer’s selection while its own indexer runs on another CUDA stream. Selections are recomputed for every decoded token; the default method requires neither retraining nor new weights (§3.1).
- **Composition with IndexCache:** Indexer retention and selection lag are independent controls. The implementation runs inside SGLang’s decode CUDA graph using unchanged production kernels (§3.2).
- **Optional fused kernel:** A combined sparse-attention/indexer-scan launch improves DeepSeek-V3.2 performance but loses to separate streams on GLM-5 (§5.3).
- **Optional indexer distillation:** Frozen model indexers supervise retained indexers using combined KL and ranking losses. Identically budgeted IndexCache distillation provides the comparison (§3.3; Appendix K).

The proposed co-design of attention and selection is a future direction, not a trained architecture demonstrated here (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Faster than IndexCache at matched retention | At single concurrency and retention 1/2, reported maximum throughput gains are 14.5% for DeepSeek-V3.2 and 9.8% for GLM-5; at 1/4, 7.9% and 5.1%. DeepSeek uses fusion; GLM uses streams. | §5.1; Table 1 caption |
| More retained indexers can still yield higher throughput | DeepSeek-V3.2 at 128K: IndexCache+MLI at retention 1/4 reaches approximately 1.32× base throughput versus IndexCache at 1/8 reaching 1.28×. | §5.1 |
| Quality differences are usually small, but equivalence is unproven | At retentions 1/2 and 1/4, both methods reportedly remain within about three points of base across LongBench, RULER and LongBench v2. Equal-retention paired intervals span zero on the two LongBench suites but resolve some RULER differences. | §5.4; Appendix I |
| A halving-pair quality loss is statistically resolved | One DeepSeek-V3.2 RULER comparison reports −3.1 with a paired 95% interval of [−5.9, −0.6]; Appendix I defines these contrasts as percentage changes relative to base score. | §5.6; Appendix I |
| High concurrency can reverse the benefit | With a full KV cache and 64 concurrent requests, MLI alone is 2.2% slower than base, and the composition trails IndexCache by up to four percentage points of base throughput. | Appendix D |
| Fusion is model-dependent | DeepSeek-V3.2, 128K, retention 1/4: fused composition is +32.3% over base versus +27.5% for streams. On GLM-5, fusion loses two to four percentage points to streams. | §5.3 |

These are author-reported measurements under matched serving jobs, with drift and baseline-parity controls (§4). They support a benefit concentrated in low-concurrency decoding, rather than universal serving acceleration. The retrieved text contains table captions but omits numerical table bodies, preventing independent inspection of the complete sweep. Quality prose also uses “points” while captions define relative percentage changes, making some effect sizes ambiguous.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Benefits shrink with concurrency; fusion helps only one evaluated model. GLM-5.1 and GLM-5.2 are not directly evaluated (§7).
- **Comparison bias:** The modified build runs IndexCache slightly slower than stock. The authors report that small margins at aggressive retention fall within this discrepancy (Appendix C).
- **Quality uncertainty:** Frontier results use single runs, and deeper-lag quality is uncharacterized (§5.6; Appendix B). Intervals spanning zero do not establish equivalence.
- **Evaluation constraints:** LongBench v2 uses non-thinking decoding; its answer budget leaves some base-model responses unparsed. Some quality entries are missing after prefill out-of-memory failures (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** The bandwidth explanation remains provisional because bandwidth saturation was not directly profiled (§5.2).
- **Specification ambiguity:** Appendix K’s MLI consumer-layer range appears inconsistent with the lagged schedule and consumer description in §3, limiting faithful reproduction of distillation.

## Open research questions
1. **What determines the concurrency crossover?** The gain reverses at high concurrency without direct bandwidth profiling. Sweep batch sizes at fixed contexts while measuring memory bandwidth and kernel overlap.
2. **How robust are the quality tradeoffs?** Frontier results are single runs. Repeat matched-retention and halving-pair evaluations across seeds, reporting relative changes separately from absolute score differences.
3. **Can fusion generalize across DSA architectures?** It helps DeepSeek-V3.2 but hurts GLM-5. Compare tuned kernels across index-head counts and feed-forward workloads.
4. **Would shared attention/indexer primitives improve the frontier?** The paper proposes but does not train this architecture. Compare a jointly designed model against separate-indexer DSA under matched training and serving budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The scheduling idea and evaluation setting are clear, but numerical table bodies are unavailable. Material gaps include complete per-cell results and intervals, direct bandwidth profiles, repeated frontier quality runs, an unambiguous distillation consumer mapping, and independent measurements on the unevaluated GLM variants.
