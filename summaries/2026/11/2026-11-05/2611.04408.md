# Halley: A Predictive Control Plane for Optical AI Data Center Networks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04408) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04408)


## One-sentence takeaway
Halley combines correlation-aware laser provisioning with predictive optical-network control, reporting composed stress-case goodput gains driven mainly by additional spares, while deployment benefits remain unverified on real optics (§§IV–VII).

## Problem and motivation
Bulk-synchronous AI training waits for its slowest communicating link, making optical failures, microring drift, and switch reconfiguration potential cluster-wide stalls. The authors identify a gap between traffic-aware control planes and controllers that anticipate optical hardware failures or drift (§I).

Their central reliability argument is that shared cooling and power correlate laser failures: increasing pool size at a fixed spare ratio therefore leaves a coverage ceiling under the assumed failure model. Coverage here means the probability that spares absorb every failure during a restock window, rather than measured uptime (§III-A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Correlation-aware provisioning:** LightReserve computes spare counts from failure probability, correlation, and a coverage target, flagging targets outside a budget cap. The theoretical contribution applies classical exchangeable-failure limits to laser pools and extends the analysis to correlations within and across modules (§§III, IV-A, V-A).
- **Optical switch scheduling:** FoldFit adapts MULTIFIT packing to traffic demands. Its conditional dwell-time guarantee excludes switching delay; realizing the packing as port-disjoint circuits remains a material constraint (§IV-B).
- **Thermal prediction and selective protection:** ThermaLead forecasts ring drift from temperature history, with an error-based fallback gate. TriageGuard allocates hot spares to the highest estimated module risks; its optimality assumes those estimates (§§IV-C–D).
- **Composition and fallback implementation:** The paper composes component outputs into goodput and tail models, and reports formal verification, FPGA implementation results, and ASIC layout signoff for the watchdog and local thermal servo (§§V-D–F, VI). These extend established mathematical tools rather than introduce new general prediction or scheduling theory (§II-B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation limits spare-pool coverage | At assumed per-laser window failure probability λ=0.05 and correlation ρ=0.15, the large-pool ceiling with spares equal to active lasers is 99.670%; the finite 32-active-channel pool reaches 99.623% at that cap. | §III-B, Corollary 1; §V-D |
| Correlation substantially changes provisioning | For 32 active channels at the same stress setting, four spares provide 86.3% coverage; 99% requires 23 spares versus five under independence. Achieving 99.999% requires 156 spares, exceeding the 32-spare cap. | §IV-A |
| Separation is cheaper under assumed prices | At the stress setting, reaching 99.999% coverage costs $44,100 after reducing ρ to 0.03, versus $234,000 through spares alone. Prices and separation premiums are assumptions. | §V-B, Fig. 9 |
| FoldFit’s guarantee and simulation cover different settings | The stated dwell bound is ≤11/9 of optimal under specified conditions. Simulations omit port conflicts and use a variant outside that proof; a reconfiguration proxy falls 45% at 16 engines against the modified LPT baseline. | §IV-B; §V-F |
| Ungated thermal prediction underperforms on the stress trace | Across 30 seeds and 2,000 control steps per seed, average lock-loss counts are 472 for ThermaLead versus 462 for dither locking; gating reduces the count to 452, a 2.1% reduction against dither. | §IV-C |
| Risk ranking modestly improves recovery | With four hot spares among 18 modules, assumed fresh-telemetry noise yields a 10.4% MTTR reduction against uniform sparing; stale telemetry yields 5.1%, and oracle ranking 10.9%. | §IV-D, Fig. 7 |
| Headline goodput depends strongly on shortfall semantics | From a calibrated 65.2% baseline, arithmetic composition reaches 76.5% when an exhausted window is entirely lost, versus 67.4% when only dark-channel bandwidth is lost. Capping the spare pool gives 76.3% under the first reading. | §V-D, Figs. 11–12 |
| Gains shrink at a milder failure setting | At 50 FIT per laser and a 90-day restock interval, the composed relative goodput gain is about 1.3%, from the reconfiguration factor. | §§V-D, VII-A |
| Coupling exposes a lower goodput tail | Under the stress-case coverage reading, the coupled model reports mean goodput of 76.5%, fifth percentile 67.8%, and first percentile 59.7%. | §V-D, Fig. 13 |
| Fallback logic passes implementation checks | The combined core uses 133 LUTs and closes at 68.97–74.69 MHz across ten FPGA placement seeds against a 50 MHz target. Four RTL proofs succeed; the 130 nm layout passes signoff but is unfabricated. | §VI-B, Table IV |

The evidence supports conditional reliability analysis, synthetic component results, and implementability of the fallback logic. It does **not** establish measured end-to-end training gains: the baseline is a calibration anchor, goodput is composed arithmetically, and most headline improvement comes from provisioning additional lasers (§V-D). The thermal and scheduling caveats also prevent treating the framework as four independently validated predictive improvements.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Laser failure probability and correlation lack field calibration for the target modules; the headline setting is explicitly stressful (§§III-B, VII-D–E).
- **Author-stated:** FoldFit simulations omit port alignment, evaluate a variant outside the proof, and exclude switching delay from the guarantee (§§IV-B, V-F).
- **Author-stated:** ThermaLead can worsen lock losses under abrupt transients. Its gate provides empirical protection, without a formal harm bound; confident but incorrect predictions remain a general fallback gap (§VII-D).
- **Author-stated:** Goodput loss shares, ranking-noise levels, and separation costs are assumed. Shortfall timing and duration are unmodeled; the two shortfall readings are cases, not bounds (§§IV-D, V-B, V-D).
- **Author-stated:** No results are hardware measurements; necessary telemetry interfaces are only partly available (§§VI, VII-B–D).
- **Analyst inference:** The abstract’s suggestion that every component carries a formal guarantee is broader than the detailed account: Table III identifies ThermaLead’s formula as a benchmark that its implemented predictor does not attain.
- **Analyst inference:** The reported power and capacity savings inherit the composition assumptions and the unmeasured fraction of stalls attributable to the fabric (§VII-A).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the assumed failure distribution match deployed laser pools?** Correlation and tail shape remain unmeasured. Instrument many pools across restock windows, recording failure timestamps and shared thermal/power events, then test predicted coverage against observed exhaustion (§VII-E).
2. **Can FoldFit deliver its advantage with physical switch constraints?** Port alignment and switching delay remain unresolved in the evaluated setting. Run port-aware schedules on the same matrices and compare total completion time with BvN and delay-aware schedulers (§IV-B).
3. **Can thermal prediction reliably outperform strong reactive control?** Stress-trace performance and abrupt-transient deficits leave this open. Compare gated predictors, fast reactive locking, and kernel-triggered heater control using measured traces and control steps expressed in seconds (§VII-D).
4. **How much composed goodput survives in a real job?** Shortfall consequences and fabric-attributable stall are unmeasured. Inject controlled channel outages into an instrumented training workload and measure stall duration, throughput, and recovery (§§V-D, VII-E).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly distinguishes many assumptions and evidence types, supporting a confident account of its conditional findings but limited confidence in deployment gains.

Material missing information includes field laser co-failure distributions, measured thermal traces and control-step timing, port-aware scheduling results including switching delay, measured goodput loss shares, actual shortfall behavior, real separation costs, and end-to-end hardware measurements. The paper describes a reproducibility bundle; its accessibility and contents were not independently verified.
