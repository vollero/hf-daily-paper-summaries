# PyRUA-REAL: Robot Exploration and Adaptive Learning through RSI in the Real World

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06768) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06768)


## One-sentence takeaway
PyRUA-REAL turns robot interaction into reusable, agent-free control programs through coding-agent collaboration and persistent experience, but its strongest real-world success headline aggregates development attempts rather than independent tests of frozen programs (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask how coding agents can retain and improve robot capabilities across physical trials while foundation-model parameters remain fixed. Completing one task once does not establish reliable execution, transfer, or efficient deployment. Physical failures also provide ambiguous evidence: problems may arise from control, perception, or the success verifier itself (§1).

The identified gap is an empirical account of the full real-world process—from exploration and continued refinement to reusable skills and deployment without repeated language-model decisions (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Collaborative program improvement:** A researcher selects approaches, experimental objectives, and stopping criteria; an engineer writes code, conducts trials, and diagnoses failures. Structured experiment reports support reassessment when local revisions stall (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Persistent experience:** Environmental facts, failure analyses, task implementations, and primitive skills are retained for subsequent experiments. Improvement changes programs and stored experience rather than model parameters (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Independent deployment:** Frozen programs use live perception, feedback control, and recovery routines without runtime language-model calls. Locally deployed perception models may remain part of execution (§3.2; §4.8).
- **Empirical contribution:** Physical development histories and complementary simulation studies examine feedback, coordination, task specifications, transfer, and execution costs. The contribution is primarily this framework and empirical investigation; the supplied text does not establish priority for its individual ingredients (§1; §4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Physical capability acquisition | Reported mean success is **91.1% across eight physical tasks**, aggregating development attempts across revisions. Agent-free programs emerge for seven tasks; Ethernet insertion still requires assistance. | §1; §4.3.1; Fig. 5 |
| Continued stacking improvement | Across development revisions, median stack–unstack cycle time falls **285.3 → 156.0 s**; full-cycle success rises **88.7% → 98.7%** across two reported smooth-stage evaluations. Visually estimated lateral error falls **2.69 → 0.44 mm**, rather than independently measured physical error. | §4.3.2; Figs. 6–7 |
| Stronger reported simulation performance | On **42 RoboDojo tasks**, frozen programs achieve **84.42%** success averaged equally over five capability groups, versus reported **50.35%** for PyRUA-Lean and **22.48%** for GPT-6-Astra. Evaluations are not matched by seed. | §4.3.3; Fig. 8 |
| Extra review adds coordination delay | Median result-to-next-trial interval is **16.9 min with a reviewer** over **40 transitions**, versus **3.4 min without** over **9 transitions**. Development histories do not isolate a causal effect on task acquisition. | §4.4; Fig. 9 |
| Richer revision feedback helps in simulation | After five revisions on four tasks, mean held-out success is **15.3%** with basic outcomes, **45.8%** with numeric states, and **54.2%** with video/joint trajectories. Each task–condition has one development session and **18 test executions**. Numeric states include privileged object information. | §4.5; Fig. 11 |
| Specifications plus further exploration improve robustness | On **32 tasks**, category-mean held-out success gains range **11.4–36.6 percentage points**. Both versions face identical test scenarios; added specifications are confounded with additional exploration. | §4.6; Fig. 12; Appendix B |
| Primitive skills support transfer | Across a separate **eight-task** comparison, mean success is **40.0% with the library versus 21.3% without**; five tasks improve and three show no gain. | §4.7; Fig. 13 |
| Frozen programs reduce deployment time | On tic-tac-toe and stacking, online coding takes approximately **4.6–5.0×** the offline execution time; online action selection takes **9.9–11.5×**. Offline measurements are successful-run medians including reset; online values are recorded times. Development costs are excluded. | §4.8; Fig. 14 |
| Contact-rich execution remains a boundary | Ethernet insertion records **0/4 scripted successes versus 10/17 with online assistance** during development. The assisted workflow includes online decisions; some recorded decision gaps also include human review. | §4.8 |

These results support the feasibility of retaining useful physical experience in executable programs and show task-dependent benefits from refinement and reuse. They do not establish autonomous, uniformly reliable improvement or equal-budget superiority over online approaches.

The RoboDojo headline is internally inconsistent: §4.3.3 reports **84.42%**, the abstract rounds to **84.4%**, and §1 reports **84.82%**. The table above uses the detailed results section.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ qualifications:** Development success mixes program versions; improvement histories are not final repeated tests. Stacking precision relies on visual estimates (§§4.3.1–4.3.2).
- **Confounded comparisons:** Reviewer phases are sequential, reassessment accompanies continued exploration, and specification-assisted improvement adds exploration time. These studies cannot isolate the proposed mechanisms (§§4.4, 4.6).
- **Limited replication:** Feedback experiments use one development session per task–condition, leaving variation across independent sessions unmeasured (§4.5).
- **Autonomy boundaries:** Human suggestions affect method selection, and Ethernet insertion remains assisted. The authors explicitly discuss how intervention shapes acquired skills (§5).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** One physical platform and the described task configurations do not establish transfer across robot hardware. “Hanoi” evaluates ordered disk placement, not the conventional three-peg puzzle; Ethernet completion excludes electrical connectivity (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Several tables appear only as captions in the supplied retrieval, including the full benchmark comparison. Appendix D’s seed attribution is also ambiguous, limiting reconstruction of comparisons and uncertainty.

## Open research questions
- **Which experience component causes transfer?** The library comparison does not separate executable skills from stored facts and failure memories. Next experiment: ablate these components under matched exploration budgets and repeated independent sessions (§§3.4, 4.7).
- **Do explicit specifications help independently of extra trials?** The robustness comparison changes both information and exploration. Next experiment: give specification and control conditions identical revision and interaction budgets (§4.6).
- **When should frozen programs request assistance?** Ethernet insertion exposes situations beyond encoded recovery logic. Next experiment: evaluate an assistance trigger using success, intervention frequency, and total execution time (§§4.8–5).
- **When does development cost pay off?** Deployment timing excludes acquisition cost. Next experiment: measure cumulative development-plus-execution cost across repeated deployments and changing layouts (§§4.3.1, 4.8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the framework and several protocols, but material gaps include complete physical trial counts and frozen-program reliability, repeated-session uncertainty, transfer-study budgets, comprehensive human-intervention records, full table contents, release completeness, and consistent RoboDojo headline and seed attribution.
