# Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06728) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06728)


## One-sentence takeaway
TurnWise combines turn-specific remaining-return credit with equal weighting of response and waiting decisions, achieving the best reported aggregate scores across three streaming-video benchmarks with two Qwen backbones (§5.1).

## Problem and motivation
Streaming video assistants must repeatedly decide whether to answer or keep observing. The authors identify two mismatches in existing streaming GRPO formulations: a shared trajectory advantage can reward redundant later replies or penalize useful follow-ups, while token-mean loss aggregation gives longer responses more weight than short waiting actions (§§1, 3.1–3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Turn-level credit:** Score each sampled prefix with all subsequent actions replaced by silence. Subtract that prefix score from the full trajectory reward, then compare remaining returns across aligned rollouts to obtain turn-specific advantages (§3.2).
- **Turn-mean optimization:** Average token losses within each turn, then average across turns, giving each response-or-wait decision equal aggregation weight (§3.3).
- **Implementation and evidence:** Cached response assessments support prefix scoring without a learned critic, additional rollouts, or additional judge calls. Component experiments test credit assignment and aggregation separately (§§3.2, 5.1; Table 2).

The contribution is the credit-and-aggregation framework; the PAUC-based reward follows MMDuet2 (§3.1).

## Evidence and main results
ProactiveVQA uses PAUC with ω=0.5 and GPT-5.6-luna judging. OVO-Timing averages task-level Timing F1 with ±2-second matching. StreamingBench averages real-time multiple-choice accuracy and proactive joint answer-and-timing accuracy, also using ±2 seconds (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported aggregate scores with the 3B backbone | Qwen2.5-VL-3B: PAUC **43.56**, Timing F1 **21.59**, StreamingBench average accuracy **49.43**; gains of **3.96**, **3.87**, and **0.69 points** over the strongest non-TurnWise result for each metric. | §5.1, referring to Table 1 |
| Gains also occur with the 4B backbone | Qwen3-VL-4B: corresponding scores **42.44**, **11.15**, **58.96**; gains of **6.26**, **1.92**, **2.38 points** over the strongest non-TurnWise results. | §5.1, referring to Table 1 |
| Aggregation complements credit assignment | On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, TurnWise versus credit-only: repetition among non-silent replies **5.98 vs 15.53** (**61.5% reduction**); Timing F1 **21.59 vs 18.63**; StreamingBench average accuracy **49.43 vs 48.94**. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Aggregation alone has mixed effects | On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, MMDuet2 plus turn-mean aggregation versus MMDuet2: PAUC **41.87 vs 39.60**, but Timing F1 **8.17 vs 17.72** and repetition **9.78 vs 6.08**. | Table 2 |

The results support complementary benefits in the evaluated setup. The logged advantage comparison demonstrates credit redistribution; it does not independently establish the causal effect of each sign change on final performance. Full Table 1 cells are absent from the supplied text, so its aggregate comparisons rely partly on the authors’ narrative.

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no explicit limitations section.

- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation covers two Qwen backbones, while component ablations and trajectory diagnostics focus on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Transfer to other model families or substantially longer interactions remains unestablished (§§4.1, 5).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** Training uses gpt-oss-20b assessments, and ProactiveVQA evaluation uses GPT-5.6-luna. Human agreement and sensitivity to judge choice are not reported (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—credit interpretation:** Remaining returns compare sampled continuations from different dialogue histories at aligned times; they are not isolated causal estimates of a single action’s value (§7.3).
- **Reporting gaps:** No seed variability, confidence intervals, wall-clock overhead, or hardware requirements are reported. Table 3’s numerical cells are missing, limiting inspection of correction-strength sensitivity.

## Open research questions
- **Does the method scale to longer interactions?** RL uses short target segments (§4.1). A next experiment could vary interaction horizon while measuring timing, repetition, and quality.
- **How dependent are gains on the scorer?** Experiments retain a fixed PAUC-based reward (§3.1). Repeat training with alternative scorers and evaluate a human-rated subset.
- **Which aggregation weights generalize best?** Table 2 shows that turn-mean aggregation alone can hurt timing. Compare token-mean, turn-mean, and intermediate weighting under both credit rules.
- **How stable are the smaller benchmark gains?** Variability is unreported. Repeat matched runs across seeds and report paired uncertainty estimates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and component results are clearly described, but complete Tables 1 and 3, uncertainty estimates, detailed baseline configurations, human judge validation, and measured compute overhead are unavailable in the supplied text. Resource links are provided, but their contents were not inspected.
