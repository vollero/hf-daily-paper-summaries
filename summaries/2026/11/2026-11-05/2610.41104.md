# MUON-RT Reusing the Teacher&#x27;s Trajectory for Momentum-Free Muon for LLM Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41104) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41104)


## One-sentence takeaway

Muon-RT reuses an existing EMA teacher’s displacement from the student to replace hidden-matrix momentum, reporting better self-distillation accuracy and lower persistent memory than AdamW and momentum Muon in the studied 7B-model settings (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

EMA-based self-distillation already stores student and teacher weights, but conventional optimizers add separate histories: two moment buffers for AdamW or one momentum buffer for Muon. The authors ask whether the teacher’s existing trajectory can supply useful optimization history without another matrix-sized buffer (§1).

The gap is methodological: teacher weights average parameters, whereas momentum averages gradients. Their displacement therefore records **applied updates**, which cannot simply be substituted for raw-gradient momentum before Muon’s nonlinear orthogonalization (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Trajectory characterization:** The authors derive an exact discounted-update identity under constant learning rate, zero weight decay, and matching initialization. They also prove noise reduction under a local fixed-direction model with temporally uncorrelated noise (§3.4, Lemmas 1–2).
- **Optimizer:** Muon-RT orthogonalizes the current gradient without momentum, removes the weight-parallel component of the teacher–student displacement, spectrally normalizes the remainder, and mixes the branches afterward. Hidden matrices retain a small power-iteration vector; other parameters still use AdamW (§3.2, Algorithm 1).
- **Conditional theory and implementation:** The paper provides a contraction bound toward a residual objective gap under smoothness, PL, objective-drift, and alignment assumptions, plus an FSDP implementation and evaluations using SDFT and SDPO (§§3.3–4; Appendix A.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers 7B models from two families and four tasks. Larger models and other domains remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated assumptions:** PL, smoothness, and alignment constants are not estimated for LLMs. Synthetic single-matrix checks support the proposed mechanism but do not validate all assumptions during actual training (Limitations; Appendices A.7, A.10).
- **Author-stated dependency:** Adding a teacher solely to replace momentum would not save memory. Teacher precision matters, and student-only clipping can corrupt the trajectory signal (Limitations; Appendices A.3, A.8).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Three seeds are reported, but dispersion and significance statistics are unavailable in the supplied text. Some baselines are imported from prior work; the authors claim matched-precision reproduction within run-to-run variation without supplying those detailed results here (Appendices A.3, A.5).
- **Analyst inference—selection and systems limits:** Reporting best checkpoints and the better learning-rate run makes selection procedures material to comparison. Persistent-state savings exclude peak activation/generation memory; the very-large-model memory figures are projections (Appendices A.5, A.6, A.9).

## Open research questions

- **Does the tradeoff persist at larger scales?** Only 7B models are evaluated. Run matched optimizer comparisons on larger models, measuring accuracy, peak memory, communication, and wall-clock time.
- **When does teacher history become stale?** Noise reduction assumes a locally fixed direction and uncorrelated noise. Vary EMA decay and mixing weight under controlled target drift and correlated gradient noise.
- **Can cheaper teacher storage preserve useful trajectories?** The paper diagnoses bfloat16 EMA rounding but evaluates the method with a 32-bit teacher. Compare compensated or alternative precision schemes against full-precision EMA at matched accuracy and memory.
- **Can the theoretical advantage be verified during training?** Alignment evidence uses synthetic known-mean gradients. Estimate large-batch reference gradients during SDFT/SDPO and test whether measured alignment predicts subsequent loss reduction.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported settings are clear, but complete table values, seed-level variability, detailed hyperparameter tables, checkpoint-selection splits, peak-memory measurements, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability are missing from the supplied text.
