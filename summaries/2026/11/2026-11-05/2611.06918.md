# From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06918) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918)


## One-sentence takeaway
NEO-VLA improves manipulation by letting a continuous-action expert read evolving visual-language representations, with matched readout ablations supporting early-layer access while backbone differences prevent isolating encoder removal as the cause (§§5.1–5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Conventional vision-language-action policies receive features after a separate vision encoder has processed the image. The authors argue that this interface can obscure intermediate spatial and geometric information needed for precise manipulation. They investigate whether action prediction benefits from accessing visual computation across backbone depths rather than relying on a fixed visual endpoint (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Native policy architecture:** A lightweight convolutional patch embedder feeds pixels directly into a shared vision-language backbone. A separate flow-matching expert reads cached keys and values from selected backbone layers to generate continuous action chunks. “Native” means structurally encoder-free, not multimodal training from scratch (§§2–3).
- **Readout-depth study:** Capacity-matched expert configurations compare distributed and late-layer access; activation swaps and input masking probe where action-relevant information influences predictions (§§3.3, 5.2).
- **Transfer and robustness evaluation:** The authors compare backbone families under shared robot-training settings, examine understanding-data co-training, and evaluate simulated distribution shifts and limited-demonstration real-world manipulation (§§5.1–5.5).

The patch interface builds on prior NEO work, and continuous flow-based action modeling follows prior work; the contribution is their policy integration and empirical investigation (§§3.1, 3.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Native backbone transfers better when frozen | After shared Open X-Embodiment pretraining, LIBERO success is 90.9% for NEO-VLA, 79.5% for Qwen3-VL, and 84.5% for Gemma-3; final 30,000-step checkpoints, 2,000 evaluation episodes total. NEO-VLA reaches 95.3% with full fine-tuning. | §5.1, Table 2 |
| Early-layer access helps under matched depth and expert capacity | On LIBERO, skip-28 versus end-28 achieves 90.9% versus 86.7% frozen, and 95.3% versus 92.5% fully fine-tuned; both retain the same backbone depth and expert size, evaluated over 2,000 rollouts per configuration at step 30k. | §5.2, Table 4 |
| Intermediate encoder access alone does not recover the advantage | Routing expert layers to Qwen3-VL vision-tower features reduces frozen LIBERO success from 79.5% to 64.9%; fully fine-tuned interfaces score similarly, 90.9% and 90.8%. | §5.1, Table 3 |
| Early attachments are especially content-sensitive | Observation/task KV swaps at early layers cause the largest translation-error increases; matched residual-noise controls have little effect. Wrist-image, instruction, and camera-pose masking show different depth profiles. | §5.2, Figure 4 |
| Understanding co-training strongly outperforms action-only post-training | RoboTwin 2.0 C2R success is 69.4% with co-training versus 10.7% with action-only training; the latter cannot produce valid text. | §5.3, Table 6 |
| Robustness is strongest for perceptual and semantic shifts | LIBERO-plus reports an overall task-weighted score of 89.7, camera score 94.5, and language score 91.7. LIBERO-Pro reports semantic-variation score 98.0, but substantial degradation under position and task-goal changes. | §5.4, Tables 5 and 8 |
| Randomized-environment performance remains comparatively strong | RoboTwin 2.0 success is 75.78% clean and 69.40% Clean2Rand under the official 2.5K-episode setting; authors report the strongest Clean2Rand result among methods with public training code and weights. | §5.4, Table 9 |
| Real-world manipulation succeeds with limited demonstrations | Success counts are 12/20, 17/20, and 13/20 across three tasks after 50 demonstrations per task; each evaluation combines 10 in-domain and 10 out-of-domain trials. | §§5.5.1–5.5.2, Table 10 |
| Deployment has a measurable sampling cost | On one RTX 3090 in BF16, compiled forward latency is 282.5 ms for a raw 30-step chunk using 10 Euler steps. Two-step sampling reduces full query latency to 178.5 ms, but corresponding task success is not reported. | §5.5.3, Table 11 |

The strongest evidence concerns the value of early-layer access within NEO-VLA: the distributed-versus-late comparison holds backbone depth and expert capacity fixed. Cross-family results support practical transferability, but do not establish that removing the encoder alone causes the gains. Benchmark rankings remain author-reported context because prior methods use differing protocols (§§1, 5.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged confounding:** Backbone pretraining and capacity differ, preventing attribution solely to encoder removal (§1).
- **Author-acknowledged ablation confounding:** Early-only configurations also shorten the backbone; those comparisons change compute and readout location together (§5.2).
- **Author-reported generalization boundary:** Robustness to appearance and language does not extend equally to unfamiliar spatial configurations or task goals (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited physical evidence:** Three tasks and small trial counts support initial real-world transfer, but provide limited evidence for broad deployment. Separate in-domain/out-of-domain results and baseline counts are unavailable in the supplied text (§5.5).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and uncertainty:** Table captions are supplied without table bodies, and the referenced appendix is absent. Consequently, many comparisons, detailed configurations, and uncertainty estimates cannot be checked. The in-house action dataset is not characterized sufficiently (§§4.3, 5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does encoder removal itself improve control?** Backbone-family confounds leave this unresolved. Train encoder-based and native variants with matched pretraining data, capacity, and action interfaces.
- **Does early-layer access specifically cause robustness gains?** The paper connects probing results with robustness patterns but does not report a readout-map robustness ablation. Compare matched maps on each distribution-shift axis.
- **How can spatial and task-goal adaptation improve?** These remain reported weaknesses. Test targeted spatial and goal variation during training, measuring both these axes and retained perceptual robustness.
- **Can fewer flow steps preserve task success?** Faster sampling changes actions without reported success evaluation. Compare sampling-step settings on identical simulated and physical trials.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative supports the architecture and principal findings, but omits table bodies, appendix details, uncertainty estimates, complete baseline results, dataset sampling proportions, in-house data composition, explicit release links, and success rates for reduced-step inference.
