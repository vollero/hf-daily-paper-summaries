# Why Pay for Long Reasoning When Rewrite Do Trick? Causal Effects of Reasoning History Compaction on Future Generation and Agent Cost

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03852) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03852)


## One-sentence takeaway
Rewriting prior scratchpads into compact state records usually reduces subsequent reasoning tokens in accepted controlled histories, but software-agent cost savings depend on the model and compressor, and quality preservation remains unestablished.

## Problem and motivation
Long reasoning traces cost tokens both when generated and when replayed as agent history. The paper asks whether rewriting a model’s own earlier reasoning can shorten future reasoning without retraining or explicitly instructing the target to be brief. Its central gap is separating this downstream effect from the immediate savings of a shorter input history (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled history intervention:** An LLM rewrites only assistant reasoning, preserving other messages, tool calls, arguments, and observations. Rewrites occur before pending tool results are available; accepted clean and compact histories then receive identical initial continuation requests except for reasoning (§2; Appendix A).
- **Behavioral analysis:** Paired continuations, length-matched paraphrases, syntax controls, a style sweep, and a selected coding study examine shortening and verification behavior. These identify effects of the rewrite intervention without isolating its linguistic mechanism (§3.1; Appendices B–C).
- **Deployment accounting:** Continuous external or self-compaction is evaluated in software agents, counting rewrite spending and separately analyzing rewrite-inclusive request time. The authors report releasing code and frozen experimental records (§2; §3.2; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Compaction usually shortens future reasoning | Six routes retained 58/72 sources. In 172 retry-augmented equal-horizon pairs, 151 shortened; median change was −32.0% (source-clustered 95% CI [−38.8, −24.3]%). | §3.1; Appendix A.7 |
| Shortening survives first-attempt analysis | Original captures yielded 149/169 completed equal-horizon pairs with shortening and a −32.1% paired median change. | Appendix A.7 |
| Aggregate success changes little but is unstable | Retry-augmented exact success changed from 165/174 clean to 166/174 compact; omitting GLM 4.7 Flash reversed the aggregate quality change to a loss. | §3.1; Appendix A.7 |
| Generic paraphrasing does not reproduce systematic shortening | Length-matched paraphrases produced a +0.7% median reasoning change across 65 comparable pairs (95% CI [−8.2, +9.7]%); success fell from 68/69 to 59/69. | Appendix B.1 |
| Selected coding branches show less reverification | Among successful DeepSeek branches after shared candidate acquisition, submit-turn reasoning ≥350 tokens occurred in 11/18 clean branches and zero compact branches; the threshold was retrospective. | Appendix B.2 |
| Deployment savings depend on configuration | On the fixed 50-task sample, six of eight configurations reduced spending including rewrites; seven reduced target request time, but only two MiniMax configurations reduced rewrite-inclusive request time. Success differed from normal by at most three tasks. | §3.2; Appendix D.5 |
| Shorter continuations need not recover rewrite overhead | In a separate two-route, 69-pair hosted analysis, continuation-only median time fell 25.9%, but adding one serial rewrite increased time 27.0%; every one-use case cost more. | Appendix E.1 |

The controlled evidence supports a downstream effect of changing reasoning history within accepted sources, beyond merely shortening input. It does not establish that length alone causes the effect, that removed checking is unnecessary, or that capability is preserved. Deployment results describe captured retained runs; provider changes, excluded work, and incomplete latency accounting limit causal and operational conclusions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated selection limits:** Narrow task structure, asymmetric acceptance, and equal-horizon filtering constrain generalization. Retrying three capped clean branches changes the estimand, although first-attempt shortening persists (Limitations; Appendices A.1, A.7).
- **Author-stated fidelity limits:** The same model writes and judges controlled rewrites. Human review covers a small accepted subset using nonexpert family raters and a post-start extension (Appendices A.4–A.5).
- **Author-stated mechanism gaps:** No matched deletion, truncation, or extractive-retention baseline is provided. Style changes jointly vary length, wording, syntax, and compressor provenance (Limitations; Appendix C).
- **Author-stated deployment limits:** One nonrandom sample, no repeated seeds, mixed providers, and excluded abandoned or uncaptured requests prevent a complete causal cost comparison. Quality noninferiority is unestablished (Appendices D.1, D.4–D.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Reduced visible reasoning is an efficiency signal with uncertain capability implications, because the coding screen cannot distinguish redundant verification from lost uncertainty (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which rewrite properties cause shortening?** Existing controls do not separate length, syntax, salience, and information loss. Next experiment: compare content-matched rewrites with deletion and extractive baselines under independent fidelity review.
- **Can compaction preserve task quality reliably?** Small success differences and fixed captured runs do not establish noninferiority. Next experiment: repeated-seed, provider-controlled deployments with a prespecified quality margin.
- **When should an agent compact?** Deployment rewrites new blocks before every request, and overhead often removes time savings. Next experiment: compare rewrite frequencies and compressor context sizes while measuring success, total spending, and full task latency.
- **Does target–compressor style compatibility matter?** The proposed model-family explanation is untested. Next experiment: cross targets and compressors with matched rewrite lengths, assessed retention, and controlled serving settings (Appendix D.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports frequent conditional shortening and configuration-dependent savings, while mechanism and quality preservation remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes the numerical bodies of several referenced tables, particularly per-route results and per-configuration deployment costs and times; complete acquisition billing and elapsed task latency; independent large-scale fidelity assessment; and repeated-seed deployment outcomes. The reported code and frozen records were not inspected.
