# Ask, Don&#x27;t Equip: Question-Driven Coding Agents for Deep Kernel Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03517) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03517)


## One-sentence takeaway
KernelSeek’s human-steered, question-driven coding agent substantially outperformed a tool-rich autonomous baseline on attention-kernel optimization, but the study does not isolate questions’ causal effect or establish fully autonomous transfer (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Optimizing advanced GPU kernels requires coordinated changes across computation, memory movement, synchronization, host launch code, and routing. The authors argue that scripted agents restrict exploration, while open-ended agents waste trials and accept premature limits; adding documentation and profiling tools does not necessarily direct attention to the right bottleneck (§1).

The identified gap is **direction**: a measurable hypothesis about where performance remains unrealized, without prescribing the implementation.

## Approach and contributions

- **Question-driven optimization:** KernelSeek combines a general-purpose coding agent with questions at plateaus, claimed limits, suspicious wins, and correctness failures. Questions request measurements or challenge assumptions across system layers (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Mechanical evaluation and stopping:** A harness checks correctness against a seed and fp32 reference, measures scenario-level speedups, and can enforce a computed completion predicate through a Stop hook (§2.2).
- **Reusable steering assets:** The authors package questions into a bank and skill, and integrate hypothesis-shaped directives into evolutionary search. Human replacement by the bank remains untested (§§2.4–2.6, 6).
- **Artifacts and empirical analysis:** The paper reports dependency-free Hopper and Blackwell attention kernels, a comparison with the authors’ Arsenal baseline, and retrospective analysis of steering traces; it states that kernels, harness, bank, scripts, and traces are released (§§3–5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

Other passages report Arsenal as **0.50×**. | §5.3, Table 2; §3 |
| Efficiency comparison is qualified | KernelSeek used **40 human turns**, including **17 questions**, and reached its best after **68.0 hours elapsed / 41.3 active**; Arsenal used **133 model-driven steps**, reaching its best after **44.7 hours** within **62.4 hours** of activity. KernelSeek API cost was unavailable. | Table 2 |
| Hopper kernel beats upstream in aggregate | Correct on **182/182** scenarios; **1.16× FA3** geometric mean, with **135/182** scenarios at parity; **1.10×** the fastest per-scenario baseline among FA3, FlashInfer, and cuDNN. | §§4, 5.2; Table 1 caption |
| Blackwell coverage and comparison differ | Correct on **154 runnable scenarios**; performance comparison uses **124 scenarios also runnable by FA4**: **1.46× FA4**, **98/124** at parity, and **1.07×** the fastest available baseline. | §§4, 5.2 |
| Blackwell headline depends partly on exceptional cases | Excluding **six bf16-query/fp8-KV decode scenarios** yields **1.37× FA4** and **1.08× fastest available** over the remaining **118 paired scenarios**. | Appendix B.2 |
| Trace analysis supports a steering hypothesis | Across **162 human messages and 11,532 tool calls**, the authors report that every large jump followed a question changing the examined layer or challenging a limit. This is retrospective observational evidence. | §§5.1, 5.4 |
| Evolution remained within its directed layer | On Blackwell dense prefill, evolutionary search improved **0.29× to 0.78× FA4**; a subsequent question exposed a synchronous host copy, whose removal brought the dense envelope to parity. | §5.5; Appendix D, Figure 3 caption |

These results establish strong measured artifacts and a substantial gap between the two tested workflows. They do not establish that questions alone caused the gap, that fewer turns imply lower total cost, or that geometric-mean wins provide parity on every scenario. Timing includes dispatch overhead, particularly consequential for short kernels (Appendix F).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated comparison limits:** One run per arm, different front-ends, KernelSeek’s preceding development work, unrecoverable Cursor token costs, and an expert human steerer weaken causal and efficiency claims (§§5.3, 6).
- **Authors’ stated generality limits:** Both campaigns optimize attention with upstream implementations available. Neither a no-faster-reference campaign nor replacement of the human by the question bank was tested; many bank entries remain unexercised (§§5.6, 6).
- **Coverage limits:** Neither kernel implements backward attention. Blackwell lacks fp8-input and specified large dense-head configurations; Hopper has substantial losses on some supported configurations (§§4, 6).
- **Benchmark sensitivity:** Short-case wins include dispatch savings, and a paged fast path benefits from physically consecutive pages in the harness. Blackwell received only one full-suite run (Appendices B.2, F).
- **Analyst inference:** Questions, stopping enforcement, human persistence, subagents, and workflow differences are bundled; their individual contributions are unresolved. The proposed controlled ablation was not performed (§6).
- **Evidence consistency:** The supplied text differs on Arsenal’s aggregate result and contains stronger abstract coverage language than the detailed Blackwell accounting. Several referenced tables appear only as captions.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the bank replace expert steering?** The paper explicitly leaves this untested. Run its proposed fixed-model, fixed-budget comparison of equipped agent, harness-only agent, bank questioner, and human questioner (§6).
2. **Does steering transfer beyond attention and strong upstream bounds?** Existing evidence covers one operator family. Test another deep kernel using seed correctness and independently measured roofline or cycle bounds (§§5.6, 6).
3. **Which component produces the gain?** The evaluated workflow bundles several interventions. Ablate question steering and mechanical stopping separately with repeated runs and matched starting kernels (§§2.2, 5.3).
4. **Do gains survive production execution and fragmented caches?** End-to-end evaluation is future work, and page layout affects a fast path. Test integrated serving workloads with randomized physical page layouts (§6; Appendix B.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main method and kernel results, but causal attribution and generality remain limited.

Material unavailable information includes full contents of several caption-only tables, exact correctness tolerances, pinned commit identifiers, Cursor model identities and costs, measured human labor, and results from repeated controlled agent ablations. Release availability is asserted rather than independently verified.
