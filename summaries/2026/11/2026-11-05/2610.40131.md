# VideoMemBench: How Well Do MLLMs Remember in Video Conversations?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40131) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40131)


## One-sentence takeaway
VideoMemBench tests explicit cross-turn dependencies in video conversations, with the strongest evaluated model scoring 45.7% overall versus 86.1% for humans under the same 128-frame input setting (Table 2; Appendix D.2).

## Problem and motivation
Extended video conversations require models to reuse previously discussed scenes and earlier answers. The authors argue that existing video QA benchmarks largely use independent questions, so they cannot isolate whether dialogue history is necessary for answering later questions (§1; Table 1).

The target capability is **in-context video memory**: retrieving and using information already established within a conversation, while grounding subsequent answers in the video.

## Approach and contributions
- **A dependency-based benchmark:** Each video has a dialogue with a typed dependency graph. Episodic dependencies refer to previously discussed visual episodes; answer dependencies require information supplied by earlier answers. Graphs include multiple parents and distant references (§§3.1–3.3).
- **A construction and verification pipeline:** A planner specifies the graph and turn intents, a generator writes questions, and a judge checks dependency necessity, non-leakage, visual grounding, and plan fidelity. Eight experts review retained dialogues (§3.2; Appendix D.1).
- **Evaluation assets and diagnostics:** The benchmark contains 326 video dialogues and 2,476 turns, including 1,387 dependent turns. The authors evaluate 25 MLLMs and introduce separate episodic and answer-dependency scores, including chain crediting for answer dependencies (§§3.3–4.2). Dataset and evaluation release is claimed on page 1.

This is a benchmark and evaluation contribution; the paper does not propose a new memory architecture.

These results establish difficulty under the paper’s sampled-frame, retained-history protocol. **Overall is an arithmetic mean of three overlapping metrics, not ordinary question accuracy.** AnsDepAcc additionally requires every answer-dependency ancestor to be correct, so its gap from other metrics combines downstream difficulty with upstream errors (§4.2). Distance and parent-count analyses show associations; they do not independently establish causal memory effects.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section, but acknowledges chain-credit accumulation and benefits from denser visual sampling (§§4.3–4.4; Appendix E.1).

**Analyst inferences:**
- **Memory and perception remain entangled:** All video lengths receive the same frame budget, and additional frames improve scores. Some errors may reflect missing visual evidence rather than failed dialogue recall (§4.1; Appendix E.1).
- **Dependency comparisons are not fully controlled:** Distance and parent-count groups may differ in visual or reasoning difficulty; matched interventions are not reported (§4.4).
- **Dependency validation remains fallible:** Necessity relies on an LLM removal probe plus human review. No independent residual dependency-error estimate is reported (§3.2; Appendix C).
- **Human comparison has possible familiarity effects:** Annotators receive dialogues they did not review “where possible,” leaving some potential overlap (Appendix D.2).
- **Scope is bounded:** Frames remain accessible throughout each short dialogue; the evaluation does not establish persistent memory across sessions or streaming video performance (§§3.3, 4.1).

## Open research questions
- **How much error comes from missing frames versus failed recall?** Frame-count gains leave this unresolved. Compare fixed-budget sampling with evidence-window inputs while preserving identical dialogue histories.
- **Does dependency distance independently impair performance?** Existing distance groups are observational. Insert irrelevant intervening turns into otherwise identical dialogues and measure accuracy changes.
- **Can models recover from earlier wrong answers?** Oracle histories improve scores, but do not test correction mechanisms. Introduce controlled parent-answer errors and compare self-correction with explicit correction prompts.
- **How reliable are dependency labels?** Reviews are described without a residual error estimate. Run blinded parent-removal tests with independent annotators and report agreement and answerability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results; **medium** for attributing failures specifically to memory.

Material missing information includes uncertainty intervals, repeated-run variability, decoding settings, open-ended judge agreement with humans, quantitative review disagreement or revision rates, and independently verified release contents. The supplied text also does not clearly specify the Figure 4 dependent-turn aggregation rule beyond its plotted descriptions.
