# LiveFoley: Dynamic Real-Time Video-to-Audio Generation for Immersive Interactive Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05988) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05988)


## One-sentence takeaway
LiveFoley combines a trailing visual window with cross-patch audio denoising to approach offline temporal alignment while achieving a measured 156 ms startup latency on one A800 GPU (Table 1; Appendix E).

## Problem and motivation
Streaming video-to-audio generation must synchronize sound with incoming visuals while keeping delay and computation low. The authors identify two bottlenecks: reducing visual context damages alignment, while denoising audio patches independently limits acoustic quality. Their target is sound generation from external visual streams, including game engines and cameras, rather than audio coupled to a video generator (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trailing Sliding Window:** Retains the full historical segment context of frozen Synchformer, emits only trailing tokens, and pads missing initial history with the first frame. Hop size controls buffering delay without shrinking the encoder window (§3.2).
- **Unified Varhop Framework:** A transformer Synchronizer and latency-mode embedding let one generation checkpoint support several hop sizes, trading frontend computation for delay (§3.3).
- **Wider local denoising:** Default **History LocDiT** conditions on clean past patches without additional look-ahead. Optional **Future-Warmup LocDiT** jointly denoises the current and an approximate future patch, discarding and regenerating the latter at the next step (§3.5).
- **Supporting evaluation:** The authors construct VGGSound-Curated for frontend comparisons and conduct perceptual-delay and listening studies (§4.1; Appendices A, G). Public release of these assets is not established.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal alignment approaches the strongest reported offline baseline | On 8 s VGGSound evaluations, LiveFoley-48k has DeSync **0.48**, versus MMAudio-L **0.47**; streaming baselines range **0.67–1.03**. Its frontend look-ahead is **0.08 s**. Lower DeSync is better. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Audio quality remains metric-dependent | On VGGSound, LiveFoley-16k achieves FD_PaSST **92.48**, versus online WorldSonus **197.68** and offline MMAudio-L **74.16**; lower is better. LiveFoley-48k has the highest reported PQ, **6.26**, versus MMAudio-L **6.18**; higher is better. | Table 1 |
| Trailing windows preserve alignment as buffering decreases | On VGGSound-Curated with the V-AURA backbone, reducing frontend look-ahead from **0.32 to 0.08 s** changes trailing-window DeSync from **0.60 to 0.61**, versus chunked-window **0.67 to 0.88**. | Figure 1b; §4.3 |
| One checkpoint supports multiple hop settings | Unified versus expert DeSync is **0.47/0.47**, **0.46/0.45**, and **0.45/0.45** at hop sizes **1, 2, 4**, respectively, on VGGSound-Curated. | Table 3 |
| Past context helps some metrics; future warmup helps further | On VGGSound-Curated, History reduces FD_PANNs from Independent’s **21.08 to 17.55**, but worsens FD_PaSST from **154.62 to 158.04**. Future-Warmup reaches **16.88** and **143.92**, respectively. | Table 4 |
| Default startup latency is measured; other modes are extrapolated | History LocDiT at hop **1** produces first playable audio in **156 ms**, measured using a real-time frame simulator on one A800. Future-Warmup’s **236 ms** is estimated by adding look-ahead under unchanged per-patch computation. | Appendix E; Tables 6–7 |
| Listening results favor LiveFoley among tested online systems | Six raters evaluated **16 clips**. LiveFoley scores MOS-Q/MOS-A **3.23/3.58**, versus WorldSonus **3.04/2.96** and offline MMAudio-L **3.62/3.79**. | Figure 4; Appendix G |

The evidence supports strong short-clip alignment and competitive streaming audio quality, with controlled ablations supporting individual components. It does not establish statistical equivalence to offline models, universally imperceptible delay, or sustained real-time performance across deployment hardware. Frontend look-ahead and measured startup latency are distinct quantities.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged quality gap:** LiveFoley still trails offline MMAudio-L on several distribution, semantic-alignment, and listening metrics (§4.2; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—limited perceptual generalization:** The delay study uses six participants imagining first-person actions while watching clips. It does not directly test interaction or immersion during gameplay (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—deployment evidence is incomplete:** Only default startup latency is measured. Other configurations are extrapolated; sustained throughput, jitter, and long-stream quality measurements are not supplied (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—benchmark coverage is narrow:** Main objective evaluation uses short VGGSound clips. Frontend ablations exclude speech and visible faces, limiting their scope (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—alignment metric dependence:** Synchformer supplies both visual conditioning and the DeSync evaluator; an independent temporal evaluator would strengthen validation. Human alignment ratings provide some complementary evidence (§4.1; Appendix G).
- **Comparison and reproducibility limits:** SoundReactor results are quoted without an available official checkpoint and it is excluded from listening tests. Training details are substantial, but code, weights, curated split release, repeated-run variability, and subjective-score significance are not established (Table 1; Appendices C, G).

## Open research questions
1. **Does low startup latency translate to stable continuous operation?** Long-form streaming machinery is described without sustained performance measurements. Run extended interactive sessions measuring throughput, underruns, latency distributions, and quality drift.
2. **How well do the perceptual thresholds transfer to real interaction?** The study uses imagined actions and few clips. Test actual gameplay with more participants, varied events, and randomized delays.
3. **Can future warmup close more of the offline quality gap efficiently?** Its ablation improves quality, but deployment latency is extrapolated. Measure its actual runtime and compare alternative future-conditioning windows under matched compute.
4. **Are hop transitions themselves seamless?** Separate hop settings perform well, but transition-specific results are absent. Switch hop sizes within clips and measure boundary artifacts and synchronization around each switch.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported architecture and tabulated comparisons; **medium** in broad deployment and perceptual claims.

Material missing information includes release status, repeated-run uncertainty, measured latency outside the default configuration, sustained streaming performance, transition-specific Varhop evaluation, and larger interactive user studies.
