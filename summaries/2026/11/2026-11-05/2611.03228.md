# AnchorComplete: Recovering Complete Scene Meshes from Partial Point Clouds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03228) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03228)


## One-sentence takeaway
AnchorComplete combines point-conditioned generation with metric-scale refinement to complete indoor scene meshes, with reported benchmark gains whose interpretation is limited by metrics that do not penalize generated geometry outside input-view frustums (§§3–4; Appendix A.2).

## Problem and motivation
Partial reconstructions leave unseen surfaces unresolved, limiting uses that require complete geometry, such as rendering, collision detection, and simulation. The authors identify a gap between object-scale generative completion, visible-surface reconstruction, and scene completion methods that produce restricted-view point clouds or voxel grids rather than complete meshes (§1).

The central challenge is to infer missing geometry while preserving observations and retaining fine detail as scene extent increases.

## Approach and contributions
- **Partial-point conditioning:** A frozen Utonia backbone and trained Q-Former produce global anchor tokens for cross-attention. Local conditioning injects observed geometry into latent initialization and supplies visibility information throughout generation (§3.2).
- **Metric-scale coarse-to-fine generation:** A whole-scene prediction establishes layout; overlapping windows refine it at fixed physical scales. Observed regions follow input points, while unobserved regions inherit parent occupancy. Finest-level outputs merge into one grid before mesh extraction (§3.3).
- **Scene-completion formulation and evaluation:** Built on TRELLIS.2, the framework outputs geometry in the input coordinate frame without per-scene optimization. The authors construct comparisons against reconstruction, generation, and completion baselines (§§1, 4.1).

Training uses complete InternScenes and SAGE meshes with partial observations rendered from subsets of views (§§3.4, 4.1). The contribution is the conditioning and scene-scale adaptation, rather than the underlying pretrained generator.

These findings support the authors’ claim of better completion under their evaluation protocol. They do **not** establish uniformly accurate unseen geometry: CD_all combines frustum-restricted prediction accuracy with full-scene recall, and F-score likewise excludes outside-frustum predictions from precision penalties (Appendix A.2). Additional rooms and generation diversity provide qualitative evidence, without quantitative validation of correctness or uncertainty (§4.2; Appendix A.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Outputs contain geometry only, without modeled appearance. Upstream drift, scale errors, and systematic depth bias can propagate. Outdoor, unbounded, and substantially different scene distributions remain untested (Appendix A.6).
- **Author-demonstrated:** Synthetic image degradation causes upstream failures that AnchorComplete preserves rather than repairs; training uses clean geometry (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Outside-frustum geometry can improve recall without a corresponding precision penalty, so reported completion scores incompletely measure overgeneration (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is limited by indoor benchmark selection, exclusion of SCRREAM videos focused on a single corner, and unequal acquisition budgets in the source-generalization experiment (Appendices A.2, A.4).
- **Evidence availability:** Tables 1–3 appear only as captions in the supplied text. Full effect sizes, runtimes, and ablation values cannot be assessed. Code and checkpoints are promised rather than established as released.

## Open research questions
1. **Can the model repair unreliable anchors?** Severe upstream failures persist (Appendix A.5). Train with degraded-image-derived clouds and compare correction of systematic errors against preservation of valid observations.
2. **How accurate is geometry outside observed frustums?** Current precision excludes it (Appendix A.2). Add full-scene precision and overgeneration measurements alongside the existing completion scores.
3. **How far does metric-scale refinement generalize?** Refinement evidence covers a small held-out set, while outdoor and unbounded scenes remain open (§4.4; Appendix A.6). Evaluate increasing scene extents and new environments, measuring detail, boundary consistency, memory, and runtime.
4. **Does sample diversity represent useful uncertainty?** Diversity is illustrated on one partial cloud without calibration measurements (Appendix A.3). Generate repeated completions across held-out scenes and test whether variation predicts errors in unseen regions.
