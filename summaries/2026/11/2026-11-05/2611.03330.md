# The SIMPLE Law for Energy Cost of LLM Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03330) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03330)


## One-sentence takeaway

SIMPLE predicts GPU energy per generated token from architecture and sequence lengths using four shared fitted coefficients, achieving 13.252% mean MAPE on held-out LLMs within an H100/TensorRT-LLM evaluation (§6.2, Table 5).

## Problem and motivation

Tokens have unequal energy costs: processing a prompt incurs prefill cost, while each generated token expands the KV-cache accessed during subsequent decoding. The authors identify a gap between accurate models requiring separate calibration for each LLM and transferable models that insufficiently represent architecture or attention mechanisms (§§1, 3).

The practical aim is to estimate energy before benchmarking a target LLM and explain how architecture, reasoning budgets, and long agentic contexts affect consumption.

## Approach and contributions

- **Architecture-aware complexity derivation:** Extend prefill and decode FLOP/memory-operation expressions to MHA and GQA using hidden dimension \(d\), layer count \(L\), and KV-to-query head ratio \(R\) (§4).
- **Compact predictive model:** Simplify and aggregate those expressions into four fitted coefficients shared across LLMs, separating prefill amortization, KV-cache access, and constant-per-token computation (§5.2):
  \[
  E_{\mathrm{tok}}=\theta_0+\theta_1d^2L\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{in}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{out}}}
  +\theta_2dLR\left(\tau_{\mathrm{in}}+\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{out}}}{2}\right)
  +\theta_3dL.
  \]
  This remains a calibrated model; it eliminates **per-LLM** fitting within the evaluated hardware/framework setting.
- **Comparative validation and analytical applications:** Compare model granularities and baselines, derive the output length minimizing energy per token, and estimate reasoning and prefix-caching trade-offs (§§6–7). The energy traces are reused from SweetSpot rather than newly introduced (§6.1).

The measured evidence supports transfer among the evaluated architectures under fixed serving conditions. It does not establish universal hardware portability or validate the long-context use cases directly. Reasoning and agentic results combine SIMPLE predictions with externally reported accuracy or workload statistics.

The headline error reduction also depends on the comparator: SIMPLE improves over the evaluated transferable baselines but is less accurate than separately fitted SweetSpot.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope:** Evaluation excludes mixture-of-experts, attention variants beyond MHA/GQA, other accelerators/frameworks, and distributed inference. Coefficients are specific to H100/TensorRT-LLM (§8).
- **Authors’ stated workload limitation:** Batch size and concurrency are not explicit inputs. Measurements use throughput mode with fixed concurrency and batch limits (§§6.1, 8).
- **Derivation approximations:** No weight/cache quantization is modeled; FFN expansion is fixed at four; bias, normalization, softmax, and dropout costs are omitted (§4).
- **Analyst inference—extrapolation:** Measurements span input and output lengths of 64–4,096 tokens, whereas reasoning and agentic estimates extend beyond this range. Long-context accuracy is therefore unverified (§§6.1, 7.2–7.3).
- **Analyst inference—model-selection uncertainty:** Term ablations and grouping searches are described, but their individual results and whether selection was nested within cross-validation are not reported (§5.2).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Qwen2-1.5B has \(R=1/6\) in Table 4 but \(1/7\) in §6.1. The abstract’s reasoning-budget/IPJ summary also differs from Table 7’s model-specific results. Table 7 provides the clearer evidence.
- **Comparator boundary:** The “non-agentic” case in Table 8 uses the agentic request’s uncached 5K-token suffix, rather than the conversational median lengths discussed in §7.3.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the formula remain accurate for long contexts and cached prefixes?** These applications exceed the measurement range. Measure energy across context lengths and cache-hit rates, comparing both total predictions and phase contributions.
2. **How should batching and concurrency enter the model?** Both are fixed rather than modeled. Sweep them independently and test whether additional terms improve held-out serving-setting predictions (§8).
3. **Can the same functional form transfer across hardware and frameworks?** Only one pair is evaluated. Refit coefficients on another pair and test held-out architectures to separate coefficient changes from structural model failure (§8).
4. **Do estimated IPJ peaks match directly measured reasoning efficiency?** Current peaks combine borrowed accuracy with predicted energy. Measure accuracy and GPU energy together across reasoning budgets and tasks (§7.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in the reported within-setting results; **medium** in the extrapolated use-case conclusions.

Material missing information includes detailed simplification ablations, model-selection separation from validation, direct long-context/prefix-cache energy measurements, uncertainty on use-case predictions, and cross-hardware validation. The exact TensorRT-LLM version is also unavailable: its reference contains the placeholder “Version X.Y.Z.”
