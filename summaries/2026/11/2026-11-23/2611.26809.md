# Search From the Start: Search-Native Pretraining for Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26809) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26809)


## One-sentence takeaway

Pretraining QueryLM on documents augmented with inline search queries and retrieved evidence improves search-enabled QA over approximately compute-matched baselines after both tested fine-tuning recipes and makes the model more likely to follow supplied evidence, including false evidence.

## Problem and motivation

Language models have limited capacity to store facts, especially at smaller scales, and memorized facts can become outdated. Search gives models access to external knowledge that can be updated independently. The authors ask whether ordinary documents can teach search at pretraining scale while preserving next-token prediction, since search is typically introduced during post-training (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Scalable corpus augmentation.** A teacher marks useful insertion points and writes queries from the preceding text. A fine-tuned annotator then scores insertion positions and generates queries across Wikipedia and DCLM-Edu. Local BM25 retrieval over Sphere supplies evidence, and lexical overlap with the original continuation filters insertions (§§3.1–3.2, A.1).
- **Search-aware pretraining from scratch.** QueryLM learns to predict original text and search queries while conditioning on retrieved passages whose tokens are masked from the loss. The method uses a standard decoder architecture and supports flexible queries without an entity-based key–value store (§§2, 3.3, 4.1).
- **Controlled evaluation of search skills.** The study compares pretraining with two search-containing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) recipes, uses fixed-query tests to isolate evidence use, and varies retrieval source, response length, and insertion frequency (§§4.2, 5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Search-pretrained base models score higher on QA | DCLM-Edu 400M QueryLM scored **24.8% ± 0.8** versus **7.5% ± 0.4** for its compute-matched Standard baseline. Four-shot answer containment averaged over eight tasks, with Tavily retrieval and the baseline receiving QueryLM’s queries and results. | §§4.3, 5.1, Table 9 |
| The average QA advantage persists under both tested SFT recipes | At 1.4B on DCLM-Edu, QueryLM versus Standard scored **36.0% ± 1.0 vs. 33.1% ± 1.0** after Search SFT and **27.8% ± 0.9 vs. 20.4% ± 0.8** after standard SFT. Zero-shot, LLM-judged accuracy averaged over eight tasks in the Tavily setting. | Figure 5, Table 10 |
| QueryLM follows supplied evidence more strongly | After Search SFT, supporting and edited false evidence shifted the original-versus-counterfactual log-probability margin more strongly for QueryLM. With fixed queries and BM25 over Sphere, QueryLM also followed supplied answers more often in LLM-judged QA, especially under counterfactual evidence. | §5.2, Figure 6, §A.5.2 |
| Retrieval access affects comparisons with prior models | On average base QA answer containment, QueryLM led LMLM and Co-LMLM with Tavily. With matched retrieval corpora, Wikipedia QueryLM trailed LMLM on average and overtrained DCLM-Edu QueryLM was comparable to Co-LMLM. | §5.1, Figure 4, Table 9 |
| Closed-book and freshness results are more mixed | QueryLM base models scored lower on average on the closed-book general benchmark suite, with mixed results after SFT. On zero-shot, LLM-judged FreshQA, models performed similarly after Search SFT when both had search access. | §5.3, Tables 12–13 |
| Fewer insertions retain part of the QA gain | The 3.3B Top-2 variant, which keeps up to two insertions per document, scored between full QueryLM and Standard on average zero-shot, LLM-judged QA in the Tavily setting after both SFT recipes. | §6.4, Table 10 |

The reported `±` values are 95% confidence intervals bootstrapped over questions (§4.3). The matched comparisons support an average QA advantage under the tested recipes, and the fixed-query experiment shows that evidence use contributes independently of query writing. The abstract’s broader cross-size claim needs qualification. In Table 10, 1.4B QueryLM has a lower mean than 3.3B Standard after Search SFT, and Table 5’s compute matching applies within model sizes. The forced first lookup also leaves autonomous search initiation untested.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Gains depend on retrieval conditions. Training on summarized or oracle responses increased reliance on the original annotations without improving downstream QA under a different retrieval setting. Longer responses and more frequent insertions add processing cost, and the Qwen fixed-query comparison reverses QueryLM’s advantage at 3.3B (§§6.1, 6.3–6.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The search-enabled evaluation primarily measures query quality and evidence use after a forced first lookup. It leaves open whether the model searches selectively when lookup is optional. Stronger adherence to edited false evidence also leaves source reliability unresolved (§§5.2, A.4.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The training comparison estimates the effect of the full augmentation recipe at approximately matched model-training FLOPs. Original-document exposure differs, some learning-rate schedules differ, and corpus annotation adds separate work (§§A.2, 6.5).

## Open research questions

1. **Can models learn when a search is worth making?** The first lookup was forced. A next experiment could remove that trigger, mix questions with and without a need for retrieval, and measure accuracy and calls per question (§A.4.2).
2. **How much post-training would close the QA gap?** The authors tested two SFT recipes. A next experiment could vary search-trace volume and diversity for both starting checkpoints under a common total compute budget (§§4.2, 5.1).
3. **Can QueryLM distinguish reliable evidence from plausible misinformation?** The counterfactual probe measures adherence when passages support a designated answer. A next experiment could present conflicting sources with varied reliability and measure answer correctness and abstention (§5.2, §A.5.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method and the main QA comparisons in Tables 9–10. The supplied text omits numeric cells for several captioned results, including the response-length and insertion ablations in Tables 1–2 and the preference margins in Table 11. The prose describes the preference probe as sampling training-document sequences that QueryLM saw, while Table 11 calls the facts held out, leaving their exact holdout status unclear. The text reports training artifacts and gives cost formulas, but does not enumerate the collection contents or provide an end-to-end cost measurement covering annotation, pretraining, and retrieval.
