# Motion-Aware Reward Modeling for Instruction-Guided Video Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22495) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22495)


## One-sentence takeaway

A reward model trained with explicit RGB frame differences ranked the original edit first on every evaluated VE-Bench motion ladder, achieved a Spearman correlation of 0.57 with human quality ratings, and kept measured motion near its starting level during fine-tuning on two editing pipelines (§5.2, Tables 3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Instruction-guided video editing should preserve source motion when the requested edit leaves the action unchanged. Rewards based on temporal similarity can favor clips with repeated frames because suppressing motion makes adjacent frames more alike. The authors call the resulting optimization failure *motion collapse*: reward and measured prompt adherence can improve while motion declines (§§1, 3.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Diagnose motion collapse.** Build a same-duration ladder from each original model output using progressively stronger frame holds and a fully frozen variant. Compare reward rankings with human preferences and test whether optimizing the reward amplifies the bias (§3).
- **Give the reward explicit motion input.** Fuse tanh-normalized RGB differences from consecutive edited frames with source and edited video features at block 4 of a Qwen3-VL-32B backbone, then adapt it with LoRA (§4.2, Figure 1).
- **Train for motion fidelity and quality alignment.** Use Bradley–Terry preferences against both frame-hold and per-frame affine-jitter negatives. A correlation-based distillation term anchors quality scores to the frozen base reward. Evaluate on unseen suppression patterns and during editor fine-tuning (§§4.3, 5.2–5.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The temporal term increases preference for suppressed clips | On the VE-Bench ladder, a suppressed variant scored highest in 97% of cases (95% Wilson CI 92–99%), versus 62% after removing the temporal term. On the UniVideo ladder, capture fell from 100% to 83%. | §3.1, Table 1, Appendix B.1 |
| Humans favor the original edits | On pairs from 30 sources, human votes favored originals over hold-2, hold-4, and flow-swap variants at 96%, 98%, and 100%, respectively. Across 90 perturbation trials, excluding tied reward predictions, the proposed reward agreed with the human majority on 87%, versus 32% for the composite. Its flow-swap agreement was 77%. | §3.2, Table 2, Appendix B.4 |
| Optimization can trade motion for prompt adherence | In UniVideo latent optimization against the composite, the reported motion measure fell 55% during the temporal phase while CLIP-T rose from 0.61 to 0.72. | §3.3, Appendix B.6 |
| The proposed reward ranks originals above suppression variants | VE-Bench ladder capture was 0% (95% Wilson CI upper bound 3.1%). Pairwise preference for originals was 91% against segment freeze, 100% against burst freeze, and 95% against flow attenuation, all variants excluded from training. | §5.2, Table 4, Appendix C.1 |
| Ablations identify distinct component effects | Removing motion input reduced held-out hold-2 detection from 100% to 47%. Removing jitter negatives reduced held-out affine-jitter rejection from 100% to 53%. Removing distillation reduced Spearman correlation with human MOS on original VE-Bench edits from 0.57 to 0.40. The composite scored 0.55 on the same MOS comparison. | §5.3, Table 5, Appendix C.2 |
| Fine-tuning keeps measured motion near its starting level | Mean RAFT motion change on UniVideo’s genuine-motion subset was +1% with the proposed reward versus −61% with the composite. On eight CogVideoX-2b sources, it was +9%, versus +19% for the composite, −42% for VideoScore, and −88% for the RAFT-flow arm. | §5.2, Table 3, Appendices B.6 and C.3 |

The experiments support within-source ranking under the tested perturbations and reduced motion drift under the tested fine-tuning regimes. The MOS comparison with the composite is a point estimate, so statistical superiority remains unresolved. RAFT magnitude measures how much motion remains, leaving source-relative trajectory fidelity and human preference for the fine-tuned outputs unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The best-of-N result applies to the sampled natural candidates, and the optimization results depend on the reward–pipeline combination. The near-chance linear probe also leaves open whether other classifiers or backbone adaptations could recover mild-motion information (Appendices B.5–B.6).
- **Analyst inference.** The controlled perturbations cover several suppression and fabrication patterns, but training on frame holds and affine jitter leaves robustness to subtler natural editing failures uncertain. The lower agreement on flow swaps than on hold pairs highlights this gap (§3.2, Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Fine-tuning evidence comes from two pipelines, with eight CogVideoX-2b sources and the headline UniVideo comparison restricted to a genuine-motion subset. The findings therefore have limited reach across editors and optimization schedules (§5.2, Appendix B.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the reward distinguish an intended motion change from accidental collapse?** The diagnosis centers on edits where motion should be preserved. Test matched instructions on the same clips that alternately request unchanged motion, slowdown, or a new action, then compare reward rankings with human judgments.
2. **Does stable motion magnitude translate to faithful motion after fine-tuning?** The optimizer comparisons use RAFT magnitude, which leaves trajectory fidelity unresolved. Add source-relative trajectory measurements and blinded human comparisons of fine-tuned outputs across multiple optimization lengths.
3. **How well does the reward detect natural motion failures?** Most controlled negatives are constructed from existing edits, and the best-of-N result covers one sampled pool. Collect unmodified outputs from held-out editors, label natural motion defects with human raters, and measure within-source ranking accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported results for the evaluated settings. The supplied text lacks full table bodies, the motion-aware training and anchor-pool sizes, exact loss weights and reward-training hyperparameters, and verified access to the checkpoint, training data, or announced study assets.
