# From Points to Trajectories: Rethinking Multimodal Alignment for Physical Events

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24249) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24249)


## One-sentence takeaway

CounterPoint improves cross-modal retrieval of individual texture-sliding events by comparing synchronized, mean-centered trajectories, with gains concentrated in modalities that capture execution-specific dynamics (§§5.3–5.4).

## Problem and motivation

A multimodal aligner should retrieve the exact recording of a physical interaction across sensors, even when another execution shares the same texture, speed, force, or path. The authors call these repeats *twin events*. They argue that random negatives let models succeed using static context and can therefore hide whether pooled embeddings preserve transient cues such as a micro-slip registered in both sound and vibration (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Condition collapse.** The authors show that under ergodicity, representations formed by a weakly continuous function of the distribution of per-instant features converge to a condition-dependent limit. This class of representations is invariant to permutations of the features being pooled (§3.1, Theorem 1).
- **Event gap.** A Koopman-based decomposition separates condition, drive, and event components. Under the stated mixing and conditional-independence assumptions, normalized cross-modal covariance within a shared drive equals the limiting score advantage of a true match over its twin. For fixed encoders and a positive gap, the authors give an error bound proportional to the inverse window length (§§3.2–3.3, Theorems 2–3).
- **CounterPoint.** A plug-in branch linearly projects pre-pooling tokens, subtracts each stream’s temporal mean, and computes cosine similarity across matching timestamps. It adds a symmetric InfoNCE loss with twin negatives when available and combines trajectory and point scores at retrieval (§4, Fig. 1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| CounterPoint improved retrieval on both datasets | Across four aligners, held-out-texture Cluster A–H R@1 rose from at most 27.4% to at least 56.0%, and twin-ranking ACC rose from 56.9–58.5% to 80.8–81.1%. On held-out-subject HAVdb, mean R@1 across the three retrieval tasks rose from at most 9.9% to at least 87.4%. | §5.1, Table 1 |
| Gains tracked the measured event gap | On held-out Cluster data, the audio–haptic gap was 0.146 and the path-only visual gap was near zero. Across aligners, haptic-plus-audio to vision R@1 gained at most 0.3 percentage points on Cluster and at least 84.7 points on HAVdb, where vision tracked fingertip motion. | §5.4, Fig. 5, Table 1 |
| Longer windows favored trajectory scoring | On HAVdb at a fixed candidate count, increasing the training window from 1 to 16 s more than halved each point aligner’s A–H R@1, while TRIANGLE + CounterPoint rose from 38.6% to 84.3%. A separate fixed-encoder Cluster test reported increasing event ACC and approximately inverse-window margin variance. | §§5.2, 5.4, Figs. 4 and 7 |
| Time locking mattered more than twin-negative training in the reported ablation | On Cluster with TRIANGLE, full CounterPoint reached 58.2% A–H R@1, versus 47.6% for best-shift scoring and 37.3% for soft-DTW. A 40 ms candidate shift reduced it to 32.3%, omitting twin negatives yielded 55.7%, and using the trajectory score alone yielded 45.8%. | §§5.3, 5.5, Tables 3–4 |

A–H R@1 averages audio-plus-vision to haptics and haptics-plus-vision to audio. ACC measures whether the true event outranks its twin. The results support better exact-event retrieval across the tested backbones and sliding datasets. The finite-window experiments are consistent with the theory. The asymptotic guarantees depend on the assumptions in §3, and the temporal-baseline ranking comes from one dataset and one backbone.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-demonstrated boundaries.** Cluster’s path-only visual stream yields a near-zero event gap, and shifting candidate trajectories sharply reduces retrieval. The method adds 0.4% in parameters, while the reported query time rises from 34 to 145 ms for 1,000 events against one million candidates (§§5.1, 5.3–5.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Both datasets involve texture sliding. They also differ in drive control, visual signal, held-out split, and default window length, so their comparison cannot isolate which factor accounts for the larger HAVdb gains (§5.1, Table 1).
- **Analyst inference.** Theorem 1 covers continuous functions of feature occupation measures under ergodicity. The supplied results leave open how much event information an order-sensitive encoder could preserve in a pooled vector (§3.1, §5.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much clock error can CounterPoint tolerate?** Table 4 tests fixed shifts and permutation. Sweep offsets below one token and add controlled jitter and clock drift to measure R@1 and ACC.
2. **Does a positive event gap predict gains for other contact events?** Both datasets involve sliding. Record synchronized repeated taps or grasps under controlled drives and test whether the measured gap predicts twin-ranking gains.
3. **How much does the choice of temporal encoder affect pooled retrieval?** The collapse theorem applies to a specified representation class, and the reported shuffle diagnostic uses TRIANGLE. Compare an order-sensitive pooled encoder with CounterPoint under matched training data and embedding budgets.
