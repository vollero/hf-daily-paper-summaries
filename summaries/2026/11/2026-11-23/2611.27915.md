# StereoVLM: Binocular Vision-Language Models for Metric 3D Spatial Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27915) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27915)


## One-sentence takeaway

StereoVLM jointly learns stereo correspondence, disparity, metric depth, and language-grounded 3D localization, and its controlled evaluations show that valid image pairing improves benchmark performance and that disparity supervision transfers better than direct depth supervision to the paper’s unseen scenes.

## Problem and motivation

Vision-language models can describe scenes but struggle to recover physical distances and localize objects in metric 3D, capabilities needed for tasks such as robotic manipulation. The authors argue that single images leave metric scale ambiguous and that generic multi-image inputs make it hard to tell whether predictions use cross-view geometry or learned scene priors. Calibrated stereo supplies a geometric constraint, but the authors identify a gap in jointly learning low-level stereo geometry and high-level language-grounded reasoning. (§§1–2)

## Approach and contributions

- **StereoVLM.** Fully fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-Instruct to answer questions from ordered, rectified stereo pairs, with focal length and baseline supplied for metric reasoning. Training interleaves correspondence, disparity, depth, object grounding, and general multimodal instructions. FoundationStereo provides numerical stereo training targets. (§§3.2, 4, 5.1)
- **Stereo3D-Bench.** Introduces eight tasks spanning point geometry, object geometry, and object-level spatial reasoning. It scores against source-native geometry and adds an independently collected point-level out-of-domain split and controlled right-view interventions. (§3, Fig. 2, Apps. A–C)
- **Supervision and transfer experiments.** Compares disparity and direct depth under matched geometry-only training, then tests how stereo instruction tuning affects object-level reasoning, monocular and video benchmarks, and simulated manipulation. (§§5.2–5.4, App. D.6)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Disparity supervision improved out-of-domain depth | In matched geometry-only 2B runs, OOD depth δ₁ was **74.92%** from predicted disparity using known calibration, **45.89%** from direct binocular depth, and **51.00%** from direct monocular depth. Direct binocular depth improved main-split δ₁ from **77.91%** to **87.26%** over monocular depth. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| StereoVLM-8B improved point and object scores | On the Stereo3D-Bench main split with ordered stereo for both models, StereoVLM-8B versus GPT-6 Astra reached **95.17% vs. 75.96%** point-depth δ₁ and **66.98%** on the unweighted five-task object average, a **19.37 percentage-point** lead. Oriented 3D-box mIoU was **37.70% vs. 17.92%**. | §5.3, Table 4 |
| Correct pairing mattered within one checkpoint | For StereoVLM-2B, correct stereo versus single-left input with calibration retained reduced disparity MAE from **9.07 to 4.12 px**, raised depth δ₁ from **80.82% to 94.51%**, and raised the five-task object average from **40.26% to 64.43%**. Zero-right was similar to single-left. Duplicated and shuffled right views scored lower than single-left. | §§3.3, 5.3, Table 5 |
| Point supervision helped object-level tasks | Compared with the 2B variant trained without point-level geometry QA, the full model improved all five object metrics, including object disparity Acc@2px by **21.59 percentage points** and oriented 3D-box mIoU by **9.82 points**. | §5.2, Table 3 |
| Stereo instructions improved spatial transfer | With matched 2B training budgets, adding stereo instructions raised the five-benchmark monocular/video average from **47.94 to 51.48**. Under shared reduced supervision, correct-stereo and single-left training averaged **50.23** and **50.21**, respectively. | §5.4, Table 6, App. D.6.1, Table 14 |
| Stereo instruction tuning improved simulated manipulation | Under the same downstream stereo GR00T protocol, RoboCasa success was **42.4%** with stereo instruction tuning, **38.1%** with general instruction tuning alone, and **34.9%** from pretrained Qwen3-VL-2B. RoboCasa was excluded from representation training for this experiment. | §5.4, Table 7 |
| Integrated inference was faster than the tested modular pipelines | On an NVIDIA H200 at batch size one, StereoVLM-2B achieved **3.44×** and **1.43×** end-to-end speedups over the localization-based and direct-input FoundationStereo pipelines, respectively, while scoring higher on all five object tasks. | App. D.8, Table 17 |

The right-view controls show that a correctly paired image contributes to benchmark accuracy beyond the presence of a second image slot. Baseline perturbations also show that predicted depth responds to the supplied calibration. The model retains substantial single-view capability, so the relative contributions of geometric computation and learned priors remain unresolved. The transfer experiments support the combined instruction recipe, with similar monocular/video averages for correct-stereo and single-left training under shared reduced supervision. (§5.3, Apps. D.3, D.5–D.6)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors.** The study relies on calibrated, synchronized cameras and centers on controlled benchmarks and simulated kitchen manipulation. The authors identify real-world transfer, challenging visual conditions, and diverse robot platforms as open issues. (§6)
- **Analyst inference.** The in-house OOD split tests point geometry only, so it leaves cross-domain object grounding untested. The real-image point protocol also selects locations where native depth agrees with S2M2, limiting what the scores say about excluded regions. (Apps. A.2, B)
- **Analyst inference.** Point correspondence and disparity MAE exclude invalid outputs, so error comparisons depend on prediction coverage. Depth δ₁ counts invalid outputs as failures. (Table 4, App. C)
- **Analyst inference.** The transfer ablations add correspondence and disparity together, leaving their individual contributions unresolved. The geometry-only OOD comparison also changes both the learned target and the inference route, since the disparity variant uses explicit calibrated conversion. (§5.2, App. D.6)

## Open research questions

1. **Does object-level grounding generalize to unseen domains?** The OOD split covers only point geometry. Build an independently annotated object-grounding split with held-out scenes and camera rigs, then compare correct-stereo and right-view control scores.
2. **How much do correspondence and disparity supervision each contribute to transfer?** The reported transfer controls add both together. Run a matched factorial ablation that varies the two objectives independently while holding training budget and the remaining supervision fixed.
3. **Do manipulation gains transfer to physical robots with imperfect stereo?** Control was evaluated in RoboCasa, while the calibration stress test measured geometric predictions. Evaluate the matched policy initializations on physical robots and sweep synchronization and calibration errors while measuring task success.
