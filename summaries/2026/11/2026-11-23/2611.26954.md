# Learning Video Foresight Reasoning from Unlabeled Videos via On-Policy Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26954) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26954)


## One-sentence takeaway

Future-OPSD improves open-ended event prediction and multiple-choice next-action planning on the tested benchmarks by using a frozen teacher to score past-only student responses against both the actual future and an unrelated future, without answer or rationale labels during training.

## Problem and motivation

Video foresight asks a vision-language model to infer what may happen next from the video observed so far. The authors identify two gaps in prior training methods. Supervision often depends on annotated answers, descriptions, or external models, and training against an entire future clip can emphasize visual context that contributes little to the question (§1, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **On-policy self-distillation from video pairs.** The student generates a reasoning-and-answer response from past frames and a question. A frozen copy of the initial student scores that same response with access to the actual subsequent clip. Inference uses the past-only student. The authors describe this as the first application of on-policy self-distillation to video foresight (§§1–3.1, Fig. 2).
- **Foresight score and tractable training objective.** The score measures how specifically the actual future supports a response relative to the distribution of reference futures. The authors derive a KL-regularized lower bound that blends the teacher’s score difference between actual and independently sampled reference futures with a teacher–student distillation term. Training applies clipped, batch-centered immediate token advantages through PPO (§§3.2–3.5, Theorem 2).
- **Empirical evaluation.** The study tests two Qwen model families across prediction and planning, with comparisons against naive distillation and experiments on teacher strength, data scale, and the form of future evidence (§4).

The reported results support benchmark gains over Base and Naive OPSD, and Table 5 directly shows gains for matched teacher-input modalities. The authors also report cross-task transfer and preservation of general video understanding, but the supplied text lacks the per-benchmark scores needed to assess their size and consistency. The token case study illustrates the proposed mechanism on one example (§4.3, Fig. 4). It leaves open how often the contrast identifies decision-relevant evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The conclusions are limited to the datasets and models studied (§5). A higher lower bound need not raise the foresight score itself, and the practical PPO update approximates the sequence-level gradient by using immediate token rewards (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Training still uses question prompts from existing task datasets. The experiments demonstrate training without answer or rationale labels and leave performance with automatically generated questions or raw video alone open (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The open-ended results depend on one model judge in the reported protocol. The supplied text gives no human agreement or alternate-judge results. The GRPO comparison also uses different rollout counts, leaving performance at matched compute unclear (§§4.1, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference.** A random unrelated future can differ in scene as well as action. The single token-level example leaves the proposed distinction between useful content and incidental context under-tested across settings (§§3.1, 4.3).

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive is Future-OPSD to reference selection?** The main update samples one unrelated future per response. Compare random references with scene-matched references and multiple references at matched compute, measuring foresight scores on both benchmarks and examining token advantages.
2. **How much do the training questions contribute?** Both main runs use prompts from existing task datasets. Hold the video pairs fixed and compare dataset prompts with automatically generated questions and generic forecasting prompts.
3. **Do the open-ended gains persist under independent grading?** The reported protocol uses one model judge. Blind human raters and a second judge to model identity, then compare rankings and agreement separately for reasoning and answers.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text specifies the training method and includes the numerical modality ablation. It omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–4 and 6, detailed scaling and sweep values, seed variability and uncertainty estimates, and the referenced proofs and pseudocode. It announces a code release without establishing current code or checkpoint availability.
