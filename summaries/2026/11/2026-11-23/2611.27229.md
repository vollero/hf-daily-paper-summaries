# Prune to Expand: Training Prompt Enhancers with Less to Imitate

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27229) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27229)


## One-sentence takeaway

Training a text-to-image prompt enhancer on selectively pruned captions generally improved automated preference and image-quality scores over training on the original dense captions across four tested generators (§5.2, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

Short user prompts often specify the subject or goal while leaving composition and visual details open. Prompt enhancement fills in those details while aiming to preserve the request. Conventional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) uses dense captions as targets, assuming captions that produce better images also teach better prompt expansion. The authors test that assumption and argue that details useful for generating one particular image can be redundant when learning to elaborate a request with many valid interpretations (§§1, 3, 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Test of supervision quality.** The authors compare equally sized training subsets with sharply different direct generation gains and report only marginal downstream benefits from the high-gain subset. Preliminary pruning experiments show that an automated visual guard largely preserves performance, while unguarded pruning degrades it (§3, Tables 1–2, Figure 2).
- **Prune to Expand (P2E).** An LLM uses the short prompt and its paired dense caption to propose nested deletions of whole sentences, aiming to retain explicit requirements and useful visual structure. Surviving sentences keep their original wording and order. P2E selects the candidate with the greatest actual token removal within a per-example cap and falls back to the original caption if needed. The resulting pairs feed standard autoregressive SFT. Pruning runs offline using text alone and adds no inference step (§4, Figure 3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large direct gains yielded marginal training benefits | The high- and marginal-gain subsets each had 10,000 pairs. Mean direct HPS v2 gain from dense versus short prompts was **3.20 vs. 0.13** with Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo. Enhancers trained on those subsets scored comparably on HPS v2, PickScore, MANIQA, and VisualQuality-R1 over 200 held-out prompts with the same generator. | §§3.1–3.2, Table 1 |
| Visual guarding largely preserved performance during preliminary pruning | On the high-gain subset, unguarded LLM pruning degraded downstream performance relative to unpruned SFT. Guarded pruning largely preserved it, and aggressive deletions were rejected more often. | §3.3, Table 2, Figure 2 |
| P2E generally improved on unpruned SFT | The main evaluation used 800 held-out prompts, 200 per category, across four generators. Unpruned SFT → P2E HPS v2 was **26.98 → 27.21** on Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo General and **27.12 → 27.42** on BAGEL Design. BAGEL Design PickScore was **22.36 → 22.49**. | §§5.1–5.2, Table 3 |
| Unpruned SFT sometimes underperformed Raw PE | On BAGEL Design, HPS v2 was **27.34** for Raw PE, **27.12** for unpruned SFT, and **27.42** for P2E. On Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo Portrait, PickScore was **23.86** for Raw PE and **23.45** for unpruned SFT. | §5.2, Table 3 |
| A moderate cap performed best among tested settings | With Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo and metrics averaged across four categories, increasing candidates from **3 to 9** at a **40%** cap yielded no consistent improvement. With **9** candidates, the **40%** cap performed best overall among **20%, 40%, and 60%** caps. | §5.3, Tables 4–5 |
| Prompt-following benchmarks favored P2E over direct generation | The authors report a higher overall GenEval score on original prompt requirements and a higher average DPG-Bench score on dense inputs versus generation without enhancement. Exact scores are absent from the supplied text. | §5.3, Tables 6–7 |

The matched SFT comparison supports a benefit from changing caption targets in the tested pipeline. HPS v2 and PickScore are automated preference proxies, and the reported benchmark comparisons leave P2E’s semantic-fidelity advantage over unpruned SFT unresolved. The authors’ explanation that pruning reduces ambiguity in the training targets remains a conjecture (§6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** P2E depends on LLM judgments of sentence relevance. Sensitivity to the pruning model’s capabilities and persistence of the benefit at substantially larger training scales remain open (§6).
- **Analyst inference.** The high- versus marginal-gain study selects different examples using one generator and one gain metric. It tests whether that selection helps in this setting and leaves open whether other gain definitions would be more predictive (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The supplied ablations vary pruning extent and candidate count. A length-matched random deletion control would help isolate the contribution of relevance judgments from the effect of shorter targets (§5.3). Transfer across generators is reported for one enhancer backbone, and applicability to naturally authored user prompts remains unclear given the synthetic data construction (§5.1, LLM Usage Statement).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does semantic selection contribute at the same deletion rate?** The reported ablations vary the cap and candidate count. Compare P2E with random whole-sentence deletion matched on realized token removal.
2. **How sensitive is P2E to the pruning LLM?** The method relies on relevance judgments, and §6 leaves model capability untested. Swap pruning LLMs while holding caption pairs, deletion ratios, enhancer training, and evaluation fixed.
3. **Do the gains persist at larger training scales?** The main comparison uses one LSDIR-derived training set. Train matched pruned and unpruned variants at several dataset sizes and evaluate them on the same held-out prompts and generators.
4. **Does P2E preserve intent on naturally authored requests better than unpruned SFT?** The reported prompt-following comparisons use direct generation as the baseline. Compare both SFT variants on collected user prompts with blinded ratings of explicit requirement preservation.
