# Explicit Style Extraction for Personalized Long-Form Text Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27211) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27211)


## One-sentence takeaway

On LongLaMP’s English user split, the authors’ SP-Refine pipeline beat the same-sample retrieval baseline in 25 of 27 ROUGE and METEOR comparisons, while its single-call drafting stage recovered most of its performance (§§5, 7.2).

## Problem and motivation

Personalized long-form generation aims to complete a new writing task while matching the user’s style. Retrieval-augmented baselines supply relevant examples but leave the model to infer which habits to preserve, from sentence rhythm to rhetorical structure and formatting. The authors ask whether extracting those habits explicitly makes them easier to inspect and apply while holding retrieval fixed (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **StylePrism** organizes 28 stylistic aspects into seven categories. Its JSON profile records observed patterns, supporting quotations, and, where applicable, habits to avoid. The taxonomy also informs the style rubric (§3.2, Figure 2, Appendix B).
- **SP-Refine** uses four inference calls. It extracts a profile from four retrieved texts, drafts from those texts, revises with both the profile and the texts, then targets the weakest of five self-scored style criteria. The held-out reference is reserved for evaluation and oracle variants (§3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **Diagnostic evaluation** annotates held-out author texts for matches and departures, compares profile-only generation with single-call implicit inference, and tests revision and draft selection with access to the reference (§§6–7).

The benchmark results support the full pipeline over the authors’ LongLaMP baseline, while the SP-Infer comparison makes the added value of extraction and revision conditional on the generator and metric. The style scores capture a reference-anchored model judgment. Human-perceived author resemblance remains untested. The oracle’s phrase reuse makes its overlap gains unsuitable as an estimate of deployable style improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Evaluation covers English LongLaMP in the user setting with a fixed retriever and four samples. The baseline uses LongLaMP’s templates and retrieval with this paper’s generators and settings, limiting direct comparison with the original benchmark scores. Single-reference overlap captures style indirectly. The judge also serves as a generator and shares its style criteria with SP-Refine’s self-scoring. The annotations are illustrative, oracle controls use a subset, and closed APIs may change. (Limitations)
- **Analyst inference.** SP-Infer removes extraction and both revision calls together, while SP-Profile changes both the available context and the generation procedure. The reported comparisons leave the separate effects of the taxonomy, profile content, and targeted second revision unresolved (§§7.1–7.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Prompt formatting can contaminate the extracted profile. The example profile cites sample delimiters and field labels as evidence for the author’s visual structure (Appendices G–H).

## Open research questions

1. **Which part of explicit refinement adds value?** The ablations bundle extraction and revision. Starting from the same drafts, compare revisions using samples alone, samples plus a free-form profile, and samples plus StylePrism, with the number of revision calls matched (§7).
2. **When should a draft be revised?** Revision effects vary by generator, and the paper’s draft-selection oracle uses the held-out reference. Test a reference-free trigger based on draft–sample formatting mismatches against always revising and returning SP-Infer (§7.2–7.3).
3. **Do authors perceive the same style gains?** The judge anchors on a single reference, and the annotations cover individual examples. Run blinded paired comparisons with the original authors, asking separately about style resemblance and factual fidelity (§§4, 6 and Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and headline comparisons. The supplied text omits most numerical cells from the main, ablation, oracle, and per-criterion tables, limiting verification of individual settings. It also supplies no uncertainty intervals, human preference results, or measured latency and token costs for the additional calls.
