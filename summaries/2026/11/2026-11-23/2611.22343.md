# PURE: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Multi-Granularity Multimodal Identity Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22343) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22343)


## One-sentence takeaway

On PURE’s e-commerce benchmark, all ten evaluated multimodal models scored highest in attribute understanding, while visual difference localization and variant-level rule judgment remained weak (§4.2, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Two listings can describe the same underlying product design while differing in a variant attribute such as color or size. Seller-specific photography and wording complicate both decisions. The authors argue that aggregate matching accuracy obscures whether errors arise from missed evidence, misinterpreted attributes, faulty comparisons, or misapplied identity rules (§1, §3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Nested matching criteria and diagnostic tasks.** A base-level match requires agreement on the underlying design and core attributes. A variant-level match additionally requires agreement on specified variant attributes. PURE assesses these decisions through 1,457 multiple-choice questions across 14 tasks covering perception, understanding, reasoning, and judgment (§3.1, §3.2.4, Table 4).
- **Error-driven benchmark construction.** The authors select real listing pairs that both an embedding matcher and a generative multimodal model misclassify. Gemini 3.1 Pro assigns tasks and generates questions. Multiple models screen the candidates, and two domain experts must approve every retained scoring unit (§3.2, Figure 2, Appendix D).
- **Diagnostic evaluation.** The authors compare zero-shot capability profiles, measure sensitivity to removing images or titles, and relate benchmark scores to direct matching and external benchmarks (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Attribute understanding leads | Across ten zero-shot models, equal-weight overall task scores ranged from 47.1% for Qwen3.5-9B to 86.0% for Gemini 3.8 Flash. Understanding led for every model, and Judgment was weakest for most. C3 used all-four-correct group accuracy. | §4.1.2, §4.2, Table 2 |
| Image and title effects depend on the task | Relative to full inputs, removing images lowered mean accuracy by 8.8 percentage points on conflict detection (C2) and 17.5 points on base-level rule judgment (D1). Removing titles raised it by 2.7 and 8.2 points, respectively. On matching D1 pairs, title removal raised mean accuracy from 48.3% to 71.7%, while accuracy fell in every non-match answer category. All means are across ten models. | §4.3, Figure 3, Tables 6–7 |
| Thinking improved overall scores in paired runs | Enabling thinking raised overall PURE scores by 5.0–13.8 percentage points across six models evaluated on the same questions and inputs. Visual difference localization (C1) and grouped attribute grounding (C3) improved for all six. | Appendix C.1, Table 5 |
| Recall asymmetry reversed across granularities | On 330 base-level and 211 variant-level pairs reused from PURE, eight of ten models had higher match recall at the base level and eight had higher non-match recall at the variant level. Mean match recall minus non-match recall was +17.2 and −15.4 percentage points, respectively. | §4.4.1, Tables 3 and 8 |
| Model rankings aligned across evaluations | Across the same ten models, Spearman correlations between overall PURE scores and direct-matching balanced accuracy, M4Bench overall scores, and FGVQA overall scores were 0.952, 0.855, and 0.842, respectively. | §4.4, Tables 8–9 |

The results establish task-specific performance differences and sensitivity to the evidence shown under the tested prompts. The authors interpret the title-removal results as evidence that wording differences can prompt false conflict judgments. The ablation measures the effect of withholding evidence and leaves the underlying decision process unresolved. The external results show agreement in model rankings, while reuse of PURE pairs limits the independence of the direct-matching comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The source pairs may inherit the selection biases of the two matchers. The benchmark directly evaluates generative model responses, leaving embedding retrieval and end-to-end systems outside its scope. Generalization across platforms, languages, product categories, and non-commerce domains remains open (Limitations and Ethics Statement, p. 9).
- **Analyst inference.** Base and variant recall were measured on different subsets, so pair composition could contribute to the observed reversal (Table 8). The modality ablations cover only A3, C2, and D1, leaving title and image effects on variant-level rule judgment unmeasured (§4.3, Table 6).

## Open research questions

- **Does the recall reversal persist when the same pairs are judged at both levels?** The reported subsets differ. Evaluate both criteria on a common set labeled at both levels, with separate reporting for pairs that match only at the base level (§4.4.1, Table 8).
- **Which title differences trigger false conflicts?** Removing a whole title changes multiple cues at once. Test controlled rewrites that vary seller wording and identity-defining attributes independently while holding images and other evidence fixed (§4.3, Table 7).
- **Do the capability rankings transfer to representative traffic?** PURE starts from shared matcher errors in one setting. Apply the same taxonomy to randomly sampled pairs from the source setting and another platform, then compare task profiles (§3.2.1, Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported results and their settings. The supplied text leaves the source matchers’ identities and configurations, the source platform and category and language distributions, and the precise aggregation used for direct-matching balanced accuracy in the correlation unspecified. It also provides no uncertainty intervals for the reported correlations.
