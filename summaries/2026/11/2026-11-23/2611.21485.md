# Lost in the Hallway: Benchmarking Persistent Spatial Memory Across Revisits

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21485) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21485)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReSpaceBench shows that multimodal models often confuse distinct rooms across revisits, and targeted fine-tuning yields its largest gains in room association, with smaller gains in state tracking and reasoning.

## Problem and motivation

Robots and wearable assistants need to recognize the same physical room from new viewpoints, distinguish it from similar rooms, and update what they remember when objects move or appear. The authors argue that prior benchmarks often leave physical room identity implicit or count a room glimpsed from outside as a visit, making it difficult to isolate cross-visit re-identification errors (§1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **ReSpaceBench.** The benchmark contains 3,244 questions across 590 synthetic and real-home scenes. Its nine tasks cover room association, persistent state, and spatial or temporal reasoning. Controlled routes define visits by physical entry and include same-type room distractors and cross-session object changes. Metadata and realized trajectories determine answers, which undergo automated and human checks (§§1, 3.1–3.3, Figs. 2–3).
- **Comparative evaluation and diagnostics.** The authors evaluate 32 models and three agentic frameworks, collect a human reference on a shared subset, and analyze room-identity errors, agent traces, frame coverage, and ground-truth identity cues (§4, Appendix C.2).
- **RS-30K training data.** The authors report releasing 30,018 questions from ProcTHOR training scenes with separate templates and no scene, route, or video overlap with the benchmark. They test supervised fine-tuning on Qwen3-VL-8B (§3.1, Appendices A.2, C.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot performance trails the human reference | GPT-5.6 Sol leads zero-shot models at 50.5% nine-task macro accuracy on the full benchmark. On the shared 450-question mini subset, it scores 52.2% versus 90.1% for the mean of three human annotators with unrestricted replay. | §4.1, Table 2 |
| Wrong-room choices dominate association errors | Across six proprietary models on 466 T1 queries, choosing another visited room accounts for 66.5–88.3% of each model’s errors and occurs on 44.8–58.0% of visited-room queries. | §4.2, Fig. 4a–b |
| The tested agentic frameworks score below direct inference | On the mini subset, AVP and DVD each score 44.0% and VideoAgent scores 30.0%, versus 52.2% for direct GPT-5.6 Sol inference. Frameworks use Sol and Luna in their respective backbone roles. Trace review finds frequent room-identity errors in AVP and DVD and evidence omissions in VideoAgent. | §§4.1–4.2, Table 3, Fig. 4c |
| Coverage-aware sampling improves accuracy | On the mini subset with GPT-5.6 Sol and a fixed 128-frame budget, annotation-guided coverage-aware sampling scores 55.3%, versus 47.6% for uniform and 47.8% for event-weighted sampling. The separate 1-fps baseline scores 52.2%. | §4.2, Table 4 |
| RS-30K fine-tuning improves scores across scene sources | At 1 fps, Qwen3-VL-8B rises from 35.5% to 60.4% on the full benchmark. Association rises from 34.4% to 78.5%, and the real-home subset rises from 40.4% to 55.2% in task-macro accuracy. | Tables 2 and 6, Appendix A.3 |
| Ground-truth identity cues partly improve state tracking | On all 559 T7 questions, GPT-5.6 Sol scores 47.0% with the original input, 51.9% with room types and persistent room IDs, and 54.0% when object masks and instance IDs are also added. | Appendix C.2, Table 10 |

The authors interpret the T1 error pattern as evidence that recognizing a room as familiar is easier than identifying the specific room. The T7 intervention shows that supplying identity cues helps on that task, with substantial errors still present. The sampling result establishes a benefit when event annotations are available. Whether a system can recover that benefit from video alone, and whether the QA gains transfer to live embodied operation, remain open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Shared ProcTHOR design and furnishing styles may account for part of the larger in-source fine-tuning gain. RS-30K-only tuning also reportedly reduces performance on some external benchmarks. The appendix gives external scores for mixed-data tuning, while the affected benchmarks and scores for RS-30K-only tuning are unspecified (§4.1, Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The real-home transfer result covers 218 questions, with real cross-session changes sourced from headset recordings. That supports transfer within the benchmark and leaves performance in other building types or less structured walkthroughs uncertain (§3.2, Table 6).
- **Analyst inference.** Humans could replay the videos without restriction, while direct model inference used sampled frames. The score gap therefore combines capability with differences in visual access. A common frame budget preserved the top two model positions among the 11 models tested on the mini subset, though some intermediate ranks shifted (§4.1, Appendix C.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Association accuracy generally declines in bins with more visits per room, but the bins contain different questions. The reported comparison leaves the independent effect of adding revisits unresolved (Appendix C.4, Fig. 6).

## Open research questions

- **Can video-derived event boundaries recover the sampling gain?** Coverage-aware sampling used route annotations. At the same frame budget, compare a video-only entry and transition detector with uniform sampling and annotation-guided sampling (§4.2).
- **Which errors remain once room and object identities are known?** The T7 oracle supplies identity cues but leaves the remaining errors unresolved. Independently supply verified object observations and session ordering to test which additional cues improve state-change accuracy (Appendix C.2).
- **Do additional revisits independently make association harder?** The reported bins compare different questions. Construct paired routes with the same rooms and query views, vary the number of intervening revisits, and measure wrong-room errors (Appendix C.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark design and score comparisons. The supplied text leaves these details unavailable:

- Download locations, licensing, and packaging for ReSpaceBench and RS-30K.
- Confidence intervals and individual or agreement statistics for the human annotators.
- Benchmark-level scores for RS-30K-only fine-tuning on the external evaluations discussed in Appendix C.3.
