# Which Dwarf Is This? Doppeldwarf-306 and Near-Identical Instance Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27490) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27490)


## One-sentence takeaway

On Doppeldwarf-306, frozen DINOv2 achieves 93.1% leave-one-out top-1 accuracy, but falls to 81.8% on answerable queries when the query photographer’s images are withheld, where learned geometric re-ranking recovers accuracy to 85.5% (§§5.1, 5.3, 7).

## Problem and motivation

The task is to identify the same physical statue or fixed installation from a photograph, even when several candidates share a design. Small distinguishing details can disappear with changes in viewpoint or lighting. The author argues that landmark-heavy retrieval benchmarks underrepresent this setting and that photographer provenance is needed to test how much retrieval benefits from having the same contributor on both sides of a match (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark.** Doppeldwarf-306 contains 1,691 openly licensed Commons photographs of 306 Wrocław statues or installations, with source, photographer and license metadata and coordinates for 294 classes (§3).
- **Controlled evaluation.** The paper compares 13 frozen backbones across seven pretraining paradigms. It ranks each statue by its best reference image’s cosine similarity and uses a reference-count-matched control to study photographer overlap (§4, §5.1).
- **Geographic and verification tests.** The paper compares geographic and random candidate pools of equal size, permutes statue locations to test the role of the real layout, and evaluates soft distance weighting. It also tests established local matchers for shortlist re-ranking and compares two confidence scores for open-set rejection (§4, §§5.2–5.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen retrieval is strong in leave-one-out evaluation | Across 1,691 queries and 306 classes, top-1 is 93.1% for DINOv2, 94.4% for DINOv3, and 94.7% for C-RADIOv3, the highest point estimate among 13 checkpoints. | §5.1, Fig. 3, Table 2 |
| Accuracy falls as candidate pools grow | With the correct statue included and distractors sampled at random, DINOv2 loses about 0.8 top-1 percentage points per doubling over pools of 2–306 statues (95% statue interval for the change −1.0 to −0.6 points). | §5.2, Fig. 5 |
| Photographer withholding and reference scarcity both matter | On the same 1,157 answerable queries, DINOv2 top-1 is 94.2% with ordinary leave-one-out, 84.5% under random thinning matched on reference counts, and 81.8% with the photographer withheld. The reported control-to-withholding gap is 2.6 points (95% statue interval 1.0–4.3). | §5.1, Appendix C.2 |
| The real layout makes geographic pools harder than random pools | On 294 geolocated classes, geographic minus random top-1 for DINOv2 averages −0.8 points over pools of 5, 10, and 20 statues, versus +0.1 points under shuffled layouts. The permutation test reports p=0.005. | §5.2, Fig. 6, Table S16 |
| Soft distance weighting improves simulated retrieval | With the user simulated 25 m from the target and all 294 geolocated classes eligible, DINOv2 rises from 93.4% visual-only top-1 to 98.7% with distance weighting. The nearest-statue baseline scores 75.1%, and a ten-statue filter centered on the true statue scores 96.2%. | §5.2, Table S18 |
| Learned verification improves photographer-disjoint ranking | On 1,157 answerable queries, re-ranking DINOv2’s top ten with weights selected on other statue folds raises top-1 from 81.8% to 84.4% with DISK+LightGlue and 85.5% with LoFTR. SIFT’s gain is indistinguishable from zero. DISK+LightGlue raises DINOv3 from 85.2% to 87.2% under the same protocol. | §§5.3, 6, Tables 3 and S4 |
| The top-two margin reduces false acceptance | At 90% known acceptance, switching from top-1 cosine to the top-two statue margin cuts false acceptance from 38.3% to 19.6% for DINOv2 and from 21.6% to 7.7% for DINOv3. Unknown queries are created by removing every reference for their identity, and thresholds are calibrated leave-one-class-out. | §5.3, Fig. S5, Appendix E |

These comparisons support the reported gains on Commons photographs and under the simulated location protocol. The permutation test links the geographic penalty to the real assignment of identities to locations, while leaving sculpture appearance, shared backgrounds, and contributor patterns unresolved as explanations. Known acceptance counts some wrong identifications, so FA@90 alone cannot establish how often a guide gives a correct, accepted answer.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Every query is a Commons upload from the Wrocław corpus. Casual visitor photos remain untested. The phone-versus-camera comparison uses 51 phone images, has no intervals, and may be confounded by class and contributor differences (§6, Table S14).
- **Author-stated.** The photographer-disjoint evaluation excludes 125 classes with only one contributor. Its control matches image counts but preserves more candidate identities than withholding does (§4, Appendix C.2).
- **Author-stated.** Backbone comparisons are descriptive because training data, architecture, and preprocessing differ. The re-ranking cross-validation holds out statues for blend-weight selection after exploration of the benchmark (§4, §6).
- **Analyst inference.** The soft-prior gain cannot be assigned separately to retaining all candidates and ranking them by distance. A matched comparison that varies these two choices independently would be needed (§5.2).

## Open research questions

- **How well does the pipeline handle casual visitor photos?** The paper evaluates Commons uploads. Collect a prospective visitor-phone query set with known and out-of-gallery statues, then measure correct-and-accepted identification, false acceptance, and response time.
- **What causes the excess confusion among nearby statues?** Location shuffling leaves appearance, background, and contributor effects unresolved. Repeat the geographic comparison with statue-only crops and photographer-disjoint references, holding candidate identities fixed across image conditions.
- **How much of the soft-prior gain comes from keeping the full gallery?** The tested filter and soft prior differ in both candidate retention and scoring. Cross full versus restricted galleries with visual-only versus distance-weighted scoring under the same measured or simulated user positions.
