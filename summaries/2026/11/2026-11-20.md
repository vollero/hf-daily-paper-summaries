# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-20

## Executive summary

- Explicit response options gave two studies a way to separate decision quality from text generation. S1CogBench found that autonomous performance and prediction of human choices could diverge (`2611.00911`). A local tool-calling study also separated missing candidate values from errors in choosing among them (`2611.21053`).
- Checker design changed measured robot performance on fixed behavior. SimAudit found 61 verified instruction–checker mismatches across three simulated manipulation benchmarks, and repairs changed up to 9.1% of one policy’s episode verdicts (`2611.25179`).
- Several speed studies measured different parts of the inference stack. VSpark timed greedy robot-action decoding, NarrowSpec timed batch-one text decoding against full-width drafts, and SpecWM timed a video generation loop with an overlapped configuration that used more GPUs than Target-only (`2611.22666`, `2611.23835`, `2611.26211`).
- Two depth papers used pose-based reprojection at different stages. Accord corrected per-image depth scale during mapping, while Depth Pro 2 used cross-frame consistency during training (`2611.25279`, `2611.26603`).
- Readable model features and effective interventions answered different questions. The probing study measured the difference at a fixed perturbation budget, MC-Lens tested whether replacing visual concept subspaces redirected answers, and VETO tested whether suppressing text-only directions affected video QA under shift (`2611.19499`, `2611.26590`, `2611.25776`).
- Video forensics results covered distinct inputs and metrics. WeVisForensics reported mean binary detection accuracy across four benchmarks, while TwinD reported macro-average video-level AUROC on face crops across 19 datasets (`2611.21373`, `2611.25935`).
- Robot studies measured different parts of closed-loop behavior. AFFECT tested whether video predictions respond faithfully to changes in actions, Mind2Act separated reasoning and execution from their composition, and SAFI evaluated whether a policy could hold safely after taking over partway through a task (`2611.25980`, `2611.26444`, `2611.26239`).
- Evidence from recorded human activity supported different levels of inference. Squoosh Bench measured retrospective agreement with website A/B outcomes, while Twice-Told Critiques measured overlap in submitted reviews and left the cause of its detector-stratified association unresolved (`2611.25200`, `2611.26286`).

## Main results worth noting

- **Checker validity on fixed behavior.** SimAudit (`2611.25179`) found 61 verified mismatches across three simulated manipulation benchmarks. Replacing original checkers with repaired ones changed up to 9.1% of one policy’s verdicts on identical rollouts. The RoboCasa365 and RoboDojo rescoring was restricted to defect-bearing tasks.
- **Safe holding after takeover.** SAFI (`2611.26239`) reduced prohibited-task completion from 99.7% for the original OpenVLA-OFT to 1.3% after adaptation on selected LIBERO tasks where scripted holding passed a precheck. The authors recorded zero physical incidents or action-saturation episodes in those trials.
- **Draft cost versus acceptance.** NarrowSpec (`2611.23835`) reported decode-only speedups over matched full-width drafts of 1.06–1.12× on B200 and 1.13–1.44× on GB10 across four pairings at batch one and temperature zero. On B200, pooled acceptance-length losses were at most 3.0% for half width and 5.4% for quarter width.
- **Retrieval depends on the baseline and query.** On RepoPremium, whole-file BM25 reduced the dense lead, and LLM-generated identifiers erased the residual lead in point estimates across all four Python core pools. Fixed lexical–dense fusion improved on dense retrieval in the preregistered holdout (`2611.20455`).
- **Online procedural localization.** The joint model improved binary mistake-detection F1 over the modular pipeline from 80.7% to 84.1% at temporal IoU ≥ 0.5 on repartitioned EgoPER, with similar step-segmentation scores and the largest class-specific gain on slips (`2611.25567`).
- **Retrospective forecasting and shortlist yield.** UX-1 matched 83.96% of recorded outcomes versus 61.64% for the strongest general-purpose configuration on 318 blinded website tests lasting over 21 days. Its shortlist captured 118 of 137 recorded variant wins (`2611.25200`).
- **Robot reasoning and execution in combination.** Mind2Act World’s leading agent reached 12.0% final success on the combined Mind2Act view, compared with 52.7% on Mind and 39.0% on Act across simulated difficulty and scene settings. The task-view differences limit attribution of the gap to any single mechanism (`2611.26444`).
- **Readout versus intervention.** Across three instruction-tuned language models, the most effective truthfulness and sentiment steering directions lay closer to the class mean difference than the highest-AUROC probe directions when neutral-text next-token KL was matched (`2611.19499`).

## Open research directions

1. **Measure checker validity and its effect on stopping separately** (`2611.25179`, `2611.25005`, `2611.26444`). Stage reachable counterexamples, have independent reviewers adjudicate task completion, and rescore fixed episodes. Then randomize checker versions on fresh runs to measure changes in retries and stopping alongside changes in final verdicts.
2. **Test whether speculative speedups survive end-to-end deployment** (`2611.22666`, `2611.23835`, `2611.26211`). Sweep draft budgets within each domain at matched hardware and batch settings. Record request-to-output latency, target calls, accepted work, and domain-specific output quality, including physical-robot control outcomes for action decoding.
3. **Evaluate conditional fidelity in few-step distillation** (`2611.26629`, `2611.17194`). Hold the noisy state and prompt fixed, sample multiple teacher and student continuations, and measure mode coverage at that state alongside endpoint fidelity under a matched inference budget.
4. **Test if action-sensitive predictions improve executable behavior** (`2611.25980`, `2611.26617`). Use paired held-out action perturbations in the same scenes, compare predicted effects with observed effects, and then measure planning and execution success under a fixed proposal budget.
5. **Isolate the roles of training-time and online depth consistency** (`2611.25279`, `2611.26603`). Where the interfaces permit a shared system, cross reprojection supervision during training with online scale correction. Score boundary F1, track jitter, scale drift, and whole-map alignment separately on stationary and moving scenes.
6. **Stress-test decisions when no valid option is offered** (`2611.00911`, `2611.21053`, `2611.15777`). Pair intact and candidate-removed cases, add held-out tool schemas, and report candidate coverage, accuracy conditional on coverage, clarification quality, and unnecessary deferrals.
7. **Measure detector performance at frozen thresholds** (`2611.21373`, `2611.25935`). Calibrate on a disjoint development domain, then report false positives and false negatives by generator, authentic audio condition, and face visibility, with results for each detector’s input protocol.
8. **Separate skill selection from feedback noise** (`2611.26510`). Fix the candidate skill pool and training recipe. Repeat student training across seeds and evaluate on tasks excluded from all feedback, then compare whether skills selected at different evaluation budgets reproduce their gains.
9. **Validate retrospective A/B forecasts prospectively** (`2611.25200`). Freeze forecasts and shortlist rules before new tests launch on verified held-out sites. Report winner capture, missed wins, calibration by outcome class, and site-clustered uncertainty.
