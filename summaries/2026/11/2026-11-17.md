# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-17

## Executive summary

- **Controlled changes reveal what models actually use.** ViRA and DominoBench change the visual detail that determines the answer, while the metadata and expert-disagreement audits change a cue while holding task evidence fixed (`2611.21605`, `2611.20385`, `2611.04395`, `2611.16694`).
- **A passing patch can precede a successful handoff by many steps.** Termination Lag found that successful runs in its core SWE-bench settings spent a median 35–60% of steps after the first passing patch. On SWE-CoWork, the two leading agents passed about 63% of new-behavior tests but completed fewer than 8% of assignments (`2611.14960`, `2611.20218`).
- **Runtime checks protect different parts of a tool workflow.** SPILLWAY checks where a tool action may go and recorded zero executed redirections under the standard AgentDojo attack across 12 backbone–suite combinations. SilentGuard checks which API values can reach the final answer and reduced wrong counts chiefly through abstention on six benchmark faults (`2611.07222`, `2611.20595`).
- **Human video can help robot policies, with different evidence in simulation and on hardware.** SPAR reports gains under simulated distribution shift and mixed physical results. SALA reports lower held-out action error with more general human video and higher physical success with more task-specific video at a fixed robot-data budget (`2611.13685`, `2611.17110`).
- **Decoding gains depend on the workload.** BitDecoding-FP4 increases long-context serving throughput on B300, while LoopFill speeds greedy generation on looped models through speculative drafting and full-depth verification (`2611.09825`, `2611.19649`).
- **Communication gains are sensitive to the baseline.** Across 474 contrasts from eight selected studies, communicating teams averaged 4.2 percentage points lower accuracy than single-agent baselines matched on sample count or a declared inference budget (`2611.17610`).
- **Predictable integration and downstream value are separate tests.** Calpha routes chat-model residue states into a folding head but trails direct ESM2 conditioning on the reported longer targets. AllocFlow improves on its imitation checkpoint in one after-fee backtest, while the trading-loop study establishes that LLM reports changed allocations in replay and leaves incremental returns unresolved (`2611.20986`, `2611.19937`, `2611.11657`).

## Main results worth noting

- **Cue following and decision revision.** On CommonsenseQA, `2611.04395` reports 97–100% selection of the annotated wrong option after moving a perfectly predictive training cue, with 72–78% average accuracy when the cue was removed. In the five-model binary revision audit, `2611.16694` reports that expert-labeled dissent reduced accuracy by 1.9–16.1 percentage points relative to the initial answer. The incremental effect relative to anonymous dissent varied by model.
- **Visual evidence at different levels of assistance.** In a matched Qwen3.5-9B experiment at maximum resolution, `2611.21605` reports 63.3% ViRA-Bench set accuracy for CrossRPO versus 59.3% for per-version GRPO. On DominoBench-1K, `2611.20385` reports 57–61% unaided deciding-person accuracy versus 82–86% when both the deciding calculation and its inputs were supplied to four Claude models.
- **Defensive enforcement and its cost.** `2611.07222` reports zero executed redirections under the standard AgentDojo attack across 12 backbone–suite combinations and the highest mean task success under attack among the defenses evaluated. On six AgentOrchBench target faults, `2611.20595` reports a reduction in wrong counts from 74.0% to 1.7% across seven open-weight models, mostly through abstention, with clean accuracy holding at 84.9%.
- **First correctness and complete delivery.** `2611.14960` compares termination with the first passing patch under offline official tests. Successful core SWE-bench runs spent a median 35–60% of steps afterward, and 1–8% of ever-passing runs ended with a failing patch across the settings analyzed for regressions. On 101 SWE-CoWork assignments, `2611.20218` reports about 63% new-behavior test passage and under 8% full completion for the two leaders. The paired complete-spec intervention improved partial delivery without raising full completion for either tested model.
- **Decoding on distinct workloads.** Against BF16 KV, `2611.09825` reports 2.4–3.0× peak per-GPU serving throughput on B300 at 64K–128K inputs and an average accuracy difference within 0.5 points on two Qwen3 models. Against autoregressive decoding, `2611.19649` reports 5.13–5.85× mean greedy decoding speedups across three looped-model checkpoints and seven benchmarks.
- **Targeted intervention and expert assessment.** `2611.18919` reports that retraining with verified VR contacts raised simulated lift success from 24.0% to 45.2% on demonstrated persistent failures and from 23.2% to 33.6% on hidden hard cases, with no statistically significant change on held-out object families. In the Nigerian MRI cohort, `2611.21412` reports that neuroradiologists preferred MS-HVED to SegResNet for overall quality in 187 of 210 paired assessments.
- **Benchmark leads with different evidentiary limits.** `2611.17834` reports the highest macro F1 for PaperCritic-4B on the PaperSound-23K test set, with a paired interval against the strongest frontier baseline that includes zero. `2611.18981` reports about 2–6 points out of 100 in full-opinion grading gains across seven generators when available reference-cited statutes were supplied versus closed-book generation. The grades were produced by LLM judges.

## Open research directions

1. **Test when a coding agent should hand off.** Use offline snapshot correctness to train a completion monitor, then evaluate it on held-out tasks with official tests hidden at runtime. Compare with default stopping and final-outcome supervision on steps, retained solves, regressions, and verified handoff requirements (`2611.14960`, `2611.20218`, `2610.25525`).
2. **Validate confidence thresholds prospectively.** Freeze text and speech decision thresholds on development sources, then report realized error and coverage on held-out task families, languages, and audio corpora. Stratify by matched item and source to distinguish language or modality effects from corpus shift (`2611.06238`, `2611.18330`, `2610.28522`).
3. **Separate cue following from appropriate revision.** Cross annotation placement and asserted source credibility with independently verified new evidence that is helpful, irrelevant, or misleading. Measure corrections of initially wrong answers and reversals of initially correct answers separately (`2611.04395`, `2611.16694`).
4. **Distinguish seeing a detail from reasoning with it.** Independently vary crop resolution, access to exact geometric inputs, and access to the deciding formula. Score localization, intermediate states, and joint correctness across variants to locate the source of errors (`2605.09614`, `2611.20385`, `2611.21605`).
5. **Measure whether human video substitutes for teleoperation.** Cross robot-demonstration budgets with human-video budgets that include zero, and compare future-feature supervision with aligned latent-action supervision on the same repeated hardware tasks under viewpoint and appearance shifts (`2611.13685`, `2611.17110`, `2610.34356`).
6. **Replicate communication comparisons under enforced parity.** Compare communicating teams, independent ensembles, and longer single-agent reasoning on identical items. Log paired outcomes, tokens, latency, and tool costs to identify where communication changes the accuracy–cost trade-off (`2611.17610`).
7. **Validate substantive grading independently.** Have blinded domain experts grade both reference-matched and independently valid criticisms or legal arguments. Compare agreement with automated judges and test whether system rankings survive changes in reference coverage (`2611.17834`, `2611.18981`).
8. **Test long-video memory with controlled revisits.** Vary self-rollout duration, retained history, and teacher history supervision independently. Script disappearances and reappearances beyond 60 seconds, then score identity, scene state, throughput, and latency at matched settings (`2611.21339`, `2611.21479`, `2611.02782`).
9. **Check whether local resource decisions improve episode outcomes.** Replay matched states under policies with different reservation or reserve rules, then repeat complete episodes across additional worlds and independent policy lineages. Measure terminal value or final-goal success alongside local acceptance and progress metrics (`2611.17933`, `2611.19537`, `2610.36980`).

These conclusions depend on missing replications, incomplete tables, unmatched workloads, or outcomes outside the reported settings.
