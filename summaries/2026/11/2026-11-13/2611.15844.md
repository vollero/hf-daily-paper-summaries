# Open Vision Banana: A Fully Open Recipe for Generative Visual Perception

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15844) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15844)


## One-sentence takeaway
OVB adapts open image generators into unified segmentation and geometry models, with strong benchmark results driven partly by repeated color-legend voting, while retaining editing ability more consistently than text-to-image quality.

## Problem and motivation
Perception tasks can be expressed as image editing: a generator paints masks, depth, or surface normals as RGB outputs. The authors identify a reproducibility gap in Vision Banana, whose proprietary components obscure which training and representation choices drive performance. OVB investigates whether public generators and public training data support this approach and which design choices matter (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Open adaptation recipe:** Fine-tune FLUX.2-Klein 4B and 9B transformers using public perception data plus generation, editing, and identity replay. The VAE and text encoder remain frozen; deterministic decoders convert RGB outputs into predictions without task-specific heads (§§2.1–2.4).
- **Controlled design analysis:** Study color assignments, palettes, prompts, task mixtures, validity masking, and adaptation scope. Results show trade-offs and interactions rather than a universally best configuration (§4; Appendix E).
- **Legend ensembling:** Repeat segmentation with different color legends and combine decoded predictions by voting, without retraining (§3.4).
- **Released assets:** The authors report releasing data pipelines, training/evaluation code, and final, intermediate, and ablation checkpoints; release completeness was not independently checked (Abstract; §1).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. ZS means evaluation datasets are excluded from adaptation training; the tasks themselves are seen.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong semantic segmentation depends on ensembling | ZS OVB 9B achieves **73.9 mIoU** on Cityscapes val with **20 passes**, versus reported Vision Banana **69.9**; OVB’s mean individual-pass score is **65.6**. | Table 1; §3.1 |
| Larger label vocabularies benefit substantially | On COCO-Object’s 80 classes plus background, ZS OVB 9B achieves **53.0 mIoU** with **90 passes**, versus **38.7** mean individual-pass mIoU and CorrCLIP **49.4**. Each pass uses three class-group sub-passes. | Table 2; §3.1 |
| Diagnostic evidence implicates color binding | For final ZS OVB 9B on Cityscapes, standard readout improves **65.4 → 73.9 mIoU** from one to 20 passes; ground-truth oracle relabeling scores **85.9 → 85.6**. | Table 7; §3.4 |
| Query rewriting supplies much of reasoning performance | On ReasonSeg val, OVB 4B scores **41.2/51.2 gIoU** with raw queries for ZS/non-ZS models, versus **67.9/74.6** with Gemma4 rewriting. | Table 5 |
| Referring and instance segmentation remain behind Vision Banana | Non-ZS OVB 9B reaches **69.2 cIoU** on RefCOCOg val-UMD versus Vision Banana **73.8**; on SA-Co/Gold, OVB with an 8B presence gate and 11-pass voting reaches **39.6 cgF1** versus Vision Banana with its gate **47.5**. | Tables 4, 6 |
| Geometry gains vary across datasets | OVB 9B ZS → non-ZS improves aligned-depth δ₁ on NYUv2 **97.4% → 99.0%**, but drops on KITTI **95.3% → 83.2%**; NYUv2 normal mean error improves **19.85° → 13.51°**. | Table 8 |
| Generation retention is incomplete | On ImgEdit-Bench, 9B non-ZS matches base Basic average **3.89**, but UGE falls **4.03 → 3.68**. On GenAI-Bench, its VQAScore falls **0.8123 → 0.7857**, averaged over three seeds. | Tables 9–10 |
| Fine-tuning improves frozen-feature probes | OVB 9B versus its base improves COCO20k TokenCut CorLoc **52.5 → 56.3**, ScanNet correspondence R@5 **25.6 → 30.5**, and SPair-71k R@0.1 **59.3 → 61.9**, under the stated feature-selection protocol. | Table 11 |

These results support useful multi-task perception and a substantial inference-time voting benefit. They do not establish uniform superiority across tasks, equivalent inference costs, or comparable metric-depth performance: OVB uses ground-truth-based per-image disparity alignment, while Vision Banana reports metric depth (§3.5). Oracle relabeling is a diagnostic, not a deployable result.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Weaker perception in open base generators remains the main limitation (§6). Instance segmentation trails stronger systems, and generation quality involves trade-offs (§§3.3, 3.6).
- **Comparison scope:** ReasonSeg and instance results include auxiliary MLLMs; competing systems use different assistants. Published geometry specialists retain their original preprocessing and training settings (§§3.2–3.5).
- **Confounded training regimes:** ZS/non-ZS differences include training-mixture and geometry-supervision changes, so benchmark-data effects cannot be isolated (§3.1).
- **Limited causal certainty:** Reduced-budget ablations do not establish optimal full-scale settings. Only the complete combined geometry variant was repeated across seeds, leaving reference-run variation unresolved (Appendix E.2.2–E.2.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Many repeated generation passes make accuracy gains insufficient evidence of practical efficiency; deployment latency is not established in the supplied text.
- **Source limitation:** The source was truncated, omitting substantial implementation and evaluation material; some prompt bodies are also missing from the rendered text. This analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions
- **Can reliable color binding be learned without extensive voting?** Table 7 leaves a large gap between standard and oracle readout. Next experiment: compare binding-focused supervision with legend ensembling under matched inference compute.
- **Which task mixture works best at full scale?** Reduced-budget allocation results conflict with the selected final recipe (Appendix E.2.2). Next experiment: repeat full-scale allocation variants with matched data and multiple seeds.
- **Can perception improve without reducing generation quality?** All final variants lose GenAI-Bench VQAScore (Table 10). Next experiment: compare replay mixtures at fixed training compute and evaluate both perception and generation.
- **How much instance performance comes from the generator versus its surrounding pipeline?** Results combine presence gating, RGB decoding, refinement, and voting (§3.3; Appendix B.2). Next experiment: ablate these components under identical queries and compute budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main method and reported results, but truncation limits protocol assessment.

Material missing information includes complete training-source shares and overlap documentation, full evaluation definitions and splits, checkpoint-averaging details, exact rendered prompts, broad uncertainty estimates, and measured inference latency. Asset availability and reproducibility were not independently verified.
