# WorldDraft: If Code Builds the World, Can Video Models Just Render It?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16217) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16217)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorldDraft exposes failures to jointly preserve simulated dynamics and realize requested appearances in video generation, while first-frame visual cues improve both objectives on two tested frozen models (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
The paper studies draft-to-video rendering: a simulator supplies scene dynamics and camera motion, and a video model supplies the requested appearance. A useful renderer must preserve the prescribed world while binding target colors, materials, and lighting to the correct entities over time. The authors identify a gap between evaluations of natural-video editing or program-following and evaluations that jointly test structural preservation and persistent appearance correctness (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** WorldDraft contains 48 executable world families and 432 paired cases. Its factorial design crosses three draft granularities—abstract, simulation, and previs—with neutral, aligned, or conflicting appearances while holding dynamics and target prompts fixed. Six challenges cover multiple entities, staged dynamics, occlusion, camera motion, deformation, and hybrid interactions (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Separate scores measure world preservation (WP), rendering fidelity (RF), and correctness-gated appearance persistence (AP). Additional diagnostics measure granularity gains, conflict penalties, and source-color leakage; visual quality is reported separately (§3.4; Appendix B).
- **WorldDraft-Primer:** A training-free intervention adds object brackets and localized target-attribute/environment cues to the first draft frame using visual grounding and programmatic SVG drawing. The renderer receives no simulator state (§4; Appendix C).
- **Empirical characterization:** The authors compare 12 models and evaluate Primer against raw inputs and text prompt engineering on OmniWeaving and MiniMax-H3 (§5).

## Evidence and main results
Scores below use the paper’s scale; changes are score points unless explicitly marked as percentages.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Preservation and appearance performance diverge across models | Averaged over the full factorial grid, SolarRenderer has WP 63.28 and RF 69.97; Wan 3.0 has WP 44.37 and RF 80.40. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Richer drafts can improve structure while degrading appearance | Under neutral appearance, abstract-to-previs changes for OmniWeaving are WP +12.75, RF −14.47, AP −15.95. Seedance instead improves all three: +10.88, +2.51, +3.21. | Table 1; Appendix B.2 |
| Conflicting source colors frequently persist | At simulation granularity with conflicting appearance, source-color leakage is 64.17% for Wan2.1-VACE and 27.39% for Wan 3.0. | Table 1; Appendix B.3 |
| Primer improves both primary objectives on tested models | Full-grid OmniWeaving WP/RF rise from 61.61/66.37 to 64.60/70.93; MiniMax-H3 rises from 50.70/76.38 to 52.55/80.21. Both exceed their Text PE counterparts. | Table 2; §5.4 |
| Component ablation supports different roles for the cues | On MiniMax-H3 across the benchmark, removing attribute cueing reduces RF from 80.21 to 76.61 and AP from 73.84 to 70.53; removing object emphasis reduces count and bounding-box scores. | Appendix C.2; Fig. 12 |
| Automated QA broadly agrees with sampled human judgments | Against majority votes from five independent judges, agreement across six metrics ranges from 87.5% to 94.2%, averaging 91.0%; sample size is unspecified. | Appendix B.5; Fig. 11 |

The paired design supports comparisons of input conditions within these constructed worlds. The evidence demonstrates observed trade-offs, but does not establish an unavoidable trade-off or the authors’ proposed attention-based mechanism. Primer’s quantitative evidence covers two models. Human agreement validates sampled QA decisions, rather than every geometric metric or the composite ranking.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Primer depends on base-model capacity, can leave faint bracket artifacts, and does not eliminate synthetic shading biases in dense drafts (Appendix C.1). Composite gains and penalties also depend on baseline performance (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation is restricted to curated simulated families and short clips; longer-term persistence and broader scene generalization remain untested in the supplied results (Appendix B).
- **Reporting gaps:** The text provides no uncertainty intervals, repeated-generation analysis, human-validation sample size, complete inference settings, or runtime costs. Appendix C.4 announces a complete prompting specification but does not include it.
- **Internal inconsistencies:** §5.2’s “9 out of 10” granularity statement conflicts with Table 1, where all ten open-weight models have negative RF/AP gains. Appendix C.2 reports leakage of 26.75% without attribute cueing, below the full Primer’s 27.21% in Table 2, contradicting its claimed leakage increase and best-on-all-metrics conclusion.
- **Analyst inference:** Explanations involving cross-attention or immutable visual priors are interpretations; no direct attention analysis is reported (§§5.2–5.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Do Primer gains persist over longer videos?** Evaluation covers only the opening five seconds. Extend matched scenes and measure appearance correctness after later occlusions and interactions.
2. **Which changes in richer drafts cause appearance degradation?** Granularity changes meshes, context, and shading together. Independently vary these factors while fixing dynamics and target attributes.
3. **How broadly does Primer transfer?** Quantitative intervention results cover two models. Test the remaining benchmarked architectures with repeated seeds and paired uncertainty estimates.
4. **How reliable are metrics in difficult cases?** Human-validation sample size and subgroup errors are unavailable. Audit occlusion, deformation, and source-conflict subsets separately, including tracking and camera estimates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Benchmark design and headline comparisons are explicit, but reporting inconsistencies weaken some conclusions. Missing information includes release/download availability, full scripts and prompts, generation settings and repetitions, uncertainty estimates, human-audit sample size, detailed cohort exclusions, computational cost, and the supplementary per-granularity results referenced in Appendix B.2.
