# TurtLES: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Turbulent 3D Neural PDE Surrogates

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09567) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09567)


## One-sentence takeaway
TurtLES benchmarks neural surrogates on dense, irregular 3D turbulent-wake trajectories, finding that the tested CALM-PDE/ECHO implementation best balances rollout stability and spectral fidelity under the shared training protocol, while full-mesh inference remains expensive (§§4–5; Appendix L).

## Problem and motivation
Fast CFD surrogates need benchmarks that jointly test turbulent dynamics, complex geometry, dense irregular meshes, and sustained temporal prediction. The authors argue that existing datasets cover these requirements separately, leaving unclear whether models capture multiscale 3D turbulence rather than merely achieve low field errors on simpler flows (§1).

TurtLES targets that gap with geometry-dependent cylinder wakes whose simulated turbulence includes an active energy cascade. Its central motivation is to evaluate temporal stability, spatial scale fidelity, and computational cost together (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset and generation pipeline:** A reported 14 TB collection of 400 iLES trajectories, each containing 400 snapshots of pressure, density, and three velocity components at 2.9–10.5 million stored solution points. PyFR simulations span four super-ellipse shape families, aspect ratios from 1/3 to 3, Reynolds numbers from 1000 to 3900, and angles of attack from 0° to 45° (§3).
- **Reusable evaluation assets:** Fixed trajectory splits, standardized mini-TurtLES subsets, a debug subset, and per-case wake-probe spectra with computation scripts. The standard split measures interpolation within the sampled parameter distribution (Appendices H–J).
- **Initial benchmark and diagnostics:** Existing neural operators predict single-frame temporal residuals and undergo full-mesh autoregressive evaluation using field errors, bandwise spectral discrepancies, and GPU time. This is a dataset and evaluation contribution; the paper does not introduce a new surrogate architecture (§4; Appendix L).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated physical scope:** Only cylinder wakes in the shear-layer transition range are covered; transfer to other geometries and Reynolds regimes remains unknown (§5).
- **Author-stated reference and sampling limits:** Targets are iLES approximations. Temporal subsampling discards the highest frequencies, and stored trajectories are too short for converged statistics such as Reynolds stresses (§5; Appendix H).
- **Restricted generalization evidence:** The standard split is explicitly in-distribution interpolation; geometry-family and parameter extrapolation are not evaluated (Appendix I).
- **Implementation-dependent comparisons:** GINO did not converge under the stated budget and runs without mixed precision or compilation. Transolver++ processes independent tiles without cross-tile context and was developed for steady-state prediction (Appendix L). **Analyst inference:** These constraints limit conclusions about the underlying architectures.
- **Baseline identity and tuning:** “CALM-PDE” combines CALM-PDE convolutions, ECHO scaling improvements, and a UPT-style transformer. The shared setup says no additional hyperparameter tuning, while the GINO description mentions larger configurations being less accurate at equal wall-clock time; the extent of configuration selection is unclear (Appendix L).
- **Reference validation is imperfect:** Appendix G reports fluctuation-profile deviations, pressure discrepancies, and shifted wake features despite generally favorable agreement with reference data.

## Open research questions
1. **Does stability survive extrapolation?** The split tests interpolation only. A next experiment could hold out a complete shape family or high-Reynolds-number cases and repeat rollout and spectral evaluation (Appendix I).
2. **How much of the ranking comes from implementation constraints?** GINO is unconverged and Transolver++ loses cross-tile context. Compare longer-trained GINO and context-sharing Transolver++ variants while reporting both training and inference costs (Appendix L).
3. **Do stable rollouts reproduce long-term physical statistics?** Stored windows cannot establish converged Reynolds stresses. Generate longer reference trajectories for selected cases and compare surrogate stresses, shedding statistics, and mode occupancy (§5; Appendix G).
4. **Can inference cost fall without losing turbulent scales?** Current full-mesh acceleration remains modest. Test compression or query-decoding variants against the existing field, spectral, and GPU-time metrics (§5).
