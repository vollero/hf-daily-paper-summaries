# Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier? Rethinking Criteria Exposure in Embodied Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08318) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08318)


## One-sentence takeaway
In a controlled embodied coding-agent study, exposing the learner to verifier details improved scores on criteria-mismatched tasks, but that advantage disappeared when the same trajectories were judged by the authors’ instruction-faithful criteria (§4.2.1).

## Problem and motivation
Robot benchmarks can verify conditions absent from their language instructions, including hidden action orders, counts, and end states. A self-improving agent given those conditions may improve its benchmark score without better completing the instructed task.

The authors identify a measurement gap: prior systems expose different amounts of verifier information, but rarely isolate exposure experimentally or test whether its gains survive a change of verifier (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled exposure comparison:** A frozen GPT-6 Astra actor executes robot-control code; a Claude Opus 5.5 learner revises callable skills and memory. Both settings receive trajectories and success feedback; Criteria Exposure (CE) additionally supplies verifier code, scores, and stages. Learning uses five rounds on five development layouts per task (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Alternative evaluation criteria:** The authors audit RoboDojo instructions against verifiers and rewrite mismatched criteria. They judge identical trajectories with original and rewritten criteria, then separately train exposed learners against the rewritten criteria (§§3.2–3.3, 4.2).
- **Transfer analysis:** They distill task libraries and evaluate reuse on disjoint RoboDojo tasks, LIBERO-PRO, and RoboCasa365, with library-content and action-use analyses (§4.3; Appendices F–I).

The main contribution is an evaluation study and protocol for separating task improvement from verifier-specific improvement.

The strongest evidence is the paired rejudging: behavior stays fixed while the verdict changes. This supports verifier dependence in the tested system. It does not independently establish that the rewritten criteria perfectly represent task completion, or that exposure generally harms learning. Explanations involving physical reasoning and library overhead remain mechanistic interpretations rather than ablation-established causes (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors:** Instruction-faithful criteria are their own proxy for intended success; some definitions require judgment. Only RoboDojo receives rewritten criteria, and simulated success feedback is cleaner than human or VLM feedback (§5; Appendix C).
- **Authors:** Only two bundled exposure settings are studied. Verifier source, scores, and stages are added together; intermediate exposure levels are not isolated (§5).
- **Authors:** Test samples are small, distillation variance is unestimated, and proposed mechanisms rely on entries, skill calls, and action statistics rather than targeted ablations (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization across agent models is unestablished because the experiments fix one actor–learner pair (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** “Instruction-faithful” remains qualified: rewritten criteria retain both-arm return requirements, although the audit reports these are frequently absent from instructions (Appendices B–C).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer evaluates a processed library: distillation removes verifier-specific entries and uses different briefs across settings. It therefore does not isolate transfer of the original libraries unchanged (Appendix G).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The main text says task groups were fixed before results, while Appendix A.1 says grouping drew partly on learner diagnoses and failed rollouts; the precise timing is unclear.

## Open research questions
- **Which feedback component drives verifier dependence?** CE bundles source code, scores, and stages. A factorial experiment exposing each separately could distinguish their effects.
- **Do the findings survive independent task judgments?** The alternative verifier is author-defined. Blinded external review of sampled trajectories could compare human judgments with both criteria.
- **How robust is the effect across models and benchmarks?** One model pair and one rewritten benchmark support the central comparison. Repeat dual judging with additional actor–learner pairs and a second independently audited benchmark.
- **What causes the transfer differences?** Distillation variance and entry-level effects remain unresolved. Repeated distillations with grasp-rule and remeasurement-entry swaps could test the proposed mechanisms (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed protocols, paired counts, and consistent central results, but intended-task validity depends on the authors’ verifier judgments.

Material missing information includes independent validation of rewritten criteria, accessible released code and run artifacts, repeated learning and distillation estimates, component-level exposure ablations, and real-world or noisy-feedback evaluation.
