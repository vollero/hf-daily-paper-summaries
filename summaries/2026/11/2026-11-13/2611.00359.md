# Humanized but Still True? Auditing Facts and Detection in AI-Text Humanizers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00359) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00359)


## One-sentence takeaway
Humanizers frequently alter tracked numbers, names or quoted spans, while a zero-shot decision model detects humanized federal prose better than the tested baselines, with substantial scope and false-positive limitations (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Humanizer leaderboards measure detector evasion and embedding similarity, which can overlook changed numbers, missing names and altered quotations. The authors identify a gap in systematic, unit-level preservation audits and in testing decision models as detectors against humanized text with content-matched human controls (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unit-preservation audit:** A rule-based checker compares numbers/dates, person/organization names and quoted spans between sources and rewrites. It evaluates commercial leaderboard outputs and an open humanizer; this measures specified-unit retention rather than complete factual accuracy (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Fidelity–evasion tests:** Frozen hypotheses assess whether preservation declines as evasion improves across commercial tools and four released variants of one open model (§3.4).
- **Matched detection evaluation:** Human federal originals, deliberately AI-styled rewrites and humanized versions enable content-matched comparisons of Jev with four detectors. RADAR and formatting counterfactuals were added after review (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Reusable audit artifacts:** The authors report releasing the checker, per-item judgments, detector scores and table-recomputation script (§6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial alterations are common | All 11 tools altered at least one tracked unit in 35–65% of 20 eligible October sources per tool. Excluding quoted spans, 10 tools altered numbers/names in at least 20% of 19 eligible sources. | §4.1; Appendix B, Table 3 |
| Open-model preservation differs by unit type | Across four variants on federal drafts, number-unit losses were at most 2.8% and name losses at most 1.1%; variants reworded 12–14 of 18 quoted spans. Any-unit alterations affected 11–17 of 90 eligible drafts. | §4.1; Appendix B, Table 3 |
| Commercial fidelity–evasion hypothesis is unsupported | Across tools, unit retention versus mean human probability from seven commercial detectors had Spearman ρ = −0.46, 95% CI [−0.65, 0.08]. | §4.2; Fig. 1b |
| Released model variants show no stipulated monotone trade-off | From bf16 through Q8_0, Q3-QAT and IQ2_XS-QAT, retention was 0.972/0.967/0.964/0.964; evasion averaged over four detectors was 0.73/0.73/0.75/0.74 at evaluation-calibrated 5%-FPR thresholds. | §4.2 |
| Jev separates matched originals and humanized versions | On 92 federal originals versus 368 humanized rewrites, AUROC was 0.864, 95% CI [0.827, 0.902], with article-level resampling. Paired AUROC advantages were 0.211 over Binoculars, 0.176 over Fast-DetectGPT and 0.215 over post-hoc RADAR. | §4.3; Table 2 caption |
| Ranking performance does not imply a safe default threshold | Jev flagged 57% of humanized rewrites at an oracle 5% FPR; at its natural 0.5 threshold, it classified 24% of human originals as AI. | §§3.3, 4.3 |
| Perplexity detectors reverse ranking on newer stylized drafts | On 38 NEW2026 originals versus AI-styled drafts, Binoculars achieved AUROC 0.37 [0.28, 0.47] and Fast-DetectGPT 0.39 [0.29, 0.47]. Flattening whitespace did not remove this result. | §§3.1, 4.3 |
| Checker validation is encouraging but limited | Independent AI audit of 20 pairs gave precision 0.84 and recall 0.93; interpretation of ambiguous rules changed precision to as high as 0.90. No human annotation was collected. | §3.2 |

The evidence supports frequent **tracked-unit alterations**, not a corresponding rate of factual falsehood: many commercial quoted spans were templates or rhetorical wording. It supports Jev’s relative advantage in this matched federal setting, not general superiority across genres. Failure to support the trade-off hypotheses does not establish that no trade-off exists (§§4–5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Measurement limitations, stated:** The checker misses relations between values and referents, accepts some accidental value matches and excludes other factual categories. Strict quoted-span matching also counts benign rewordings (§5).
- **Validation limitations, stated:** Both audits used AI annotators; the initial annotator wrote the checker. False flags and rule disagreements remain (§§3.2, 5).
- **Narrow detection scope, stated:** Matched controls cover federal prose, one open humanizer and one decision model. Drafts intentionally exhibit stereotypical AI style; some human originals may themselves have received AI assistance (§§3.1, 5).
- **Comparison limitations, stated:** Low-bit variants differ in training as well as precision. Detection thresholds use evaluation originals; current-output supervised detectors and commercial classifiers were not tested on matched controls (§5).
- **Protocol and reproducibility qualifications:** Earlier evaluation-data contacts are disclosed; RADAR and formatting analyses are post hoc. Protocols and run receipts are available on request, rather than included in the reported public release (§§3.4, 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Commercial and open-model fidelity results cannot establish which tool class preserves facts better because their source domains differ (§3.1).

## Open research questions
1. **How closely do unit alterations track consequential factual errors?** The checker omits semantic relations and lacks human validation. Next experiment: a blinded human audit separating factual errors, harmless paraphrases and attribution errors (§5).
2. **Does Jev’s advantage generalize beyond stylized federal prose?** Matched evaluation covers one genre and rewriting regime. Next experiment: content-matched tests across genres and free generations, using thresholds fixed on separate development data (§§3.1, 5).
3. **What causes below-chance perplexity detection?** Whitespace flattening does not explain it, while length remains unresolved. Next experiment: independently vary rewrite length and style while holding source content fixed (§§4.3, 5).
4. **Does quantization itself change fidelity or evasion?** QAT and distillation confound the released-file comparison. Next experiment: compare standard quantizations with and without matched recovery training under identical prompts and sampling (§§3.1, 4.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods and narrative results support the main findings, but measurement validation and generalization remain limited.

Material missing information includes the bodies of Tables 1, 2 and 4, rendered figures, human-validated factual judgments, Jev’s training data, and independently calibrated deployment performance. The supplied text reports artifact availability but does not include the artifacts or archived protocols.
