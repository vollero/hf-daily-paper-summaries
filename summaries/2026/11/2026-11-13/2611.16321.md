# From Large Language Models to Efficient System One Models via Adaptive Computation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16321) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16321)


## One-sentence takeaway
NestOne improves shallow decision exits through shared training and self-distillation, while adaptive stopping saves layers with nearly unchanged accuracy on the largest benchmarks but a measurable macro-accuracy loss (Tables 3–4).

## Problem and motivation
LLM-based decision models select an option without generating text, yet typically retain the entire backbone and its inference cost. The authors ask where decisions form within that backbone and whether separately trained shallow models acquire complementary capabilities. They argue that understanding these questions can support input-dependent computation instead of assigning every decision the same depth (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Depth analysis:** Probes, logit lenses, integrated gradients, layer removal, and mean ablation distinguish early feature construction from middle-layer decision formation. Prediction comparisons suggest that smaller models mostly retain subsets of the full model’s capabilities (§3).
- **Nested training:** NestOne jointly fine-tunes shared prefixes and a shared “coda”—the final two layers plus decision head. Shallow exits receive gold-label supervision and self-distillation from the full exit (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Adaptive stopping:** Logistic gates predict exit correctness from hidden states before running the coda. Rejected exits reuse prefix computation; a held-out threshold controls stopping (§4.3).

The contribution is this combined analysis and training/inference design; the supplied evidence does not independently establish the authors’ broader novelty claims.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Middle layers dominate attributed decision formation | In full32, layers 15–23 contribute 73% of the final decision margin; early layers contribute 22%. | §3.1, Fig. 2c |
| Late body layers can be removed with little measured effect | Removing layers 24–29 from Intern-Decision-4B without retraining gives 90.1% accuracy, reported change +0.0 points, 95% CI [−0.9, +0.9]. | Table 1a |
| Shallow nested exits improve over separate training | Across three seeds, 12-layer accuracy is 64.4% versus 61.5%; difference +2.9 points, 95% CI [+0.7, +5.3]. The reported 16-layer difference is +1.8 points, CI [−0.1, +3.8]. | Table 3 |
| Deep exits remain close to separate models | Across three seeds, NestOne achieves 83.7% versus 83.5% at 24 layers and 84.0% versus 84.3% at 32 layers; both difference CIs include zero. | Table 3 |
| Fixed-depth latency and combined training time decrease | Batch-one latency is 34.7 ms at 24 layers versus 46.5 ms at 32. One NestOne run takes 80 minutes on one H200 versus 94 minutes for four separate models. | §5 setup |
| Adaptive savings depend on the accuracy aggregation | For seed 42, the state gate uses 21.6 or 20.2 mean layers versus fixed 24. Macro accuracy changes by −1.56 or −1.68 points, while decision-weighted accuracy on the four largest benchmarks changes by +0.08 or +0.03 points. | Table 4 |
| Shallow gains mainly preserve full-model successes | Across three seeds, the 12-layer nested exit loses 23.4% of decisions solved only by full32 versus 26.5% for M12; its unique correct decisions are 3.6% versus 3.8%. | Table 5 |

The evidence supports improved shallow exits and reduced computation on this benchmark suite. It does not establish statistical equivalence at deep exits, universal accuracy preservation, or measured end-to-end latency gains for adaptive inference. In particular, stability on the largest benchmarks coexists with worse macro accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** End-to-end latency, calibration under real traffic, larger models, and finer exit spacing remain future work (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Savings depend on traffic composition, and selected gate thresholds vary across fitting splits; deployment-like calibration is recommended (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is limited by evaluation on one backbone. Main exit comparisons use multiple seeds, but the detailed stopping-rule comparison reports seed 42 (Tables 3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** No component ablation isolates self-distillation from shared training or the shared coda. Prediction retention is consistent with the proposed mechanism but does not establish each component’s causal contribution (§5.3).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** The gate’s held-out accuracy tolerance does not guarantee the same test tolerance: the nominal 0.25-point setting loses 1.56 macro points (Table 4).
- **Reproducibility gap:** The supplied text does not establish code or checkpoint availability, specify the sub-model sampling schedule, or fully define the benchmark composition.

## Open research questions
- **Do adaptive layer savings translate into deployment latency savings?** Adaptive results primarily count layers, and latency remains future work. Measure complete inference latency across batch sizes and traffic mixtures (§§5–6).
- **Which training component produces the shallow-exit gains?** Component contributions are not isolated. Compare shared training with and without distillation and with shared versus separate codas (§§4–5).
- **Can calibration protect small benchmarks under distribution shift?** Small benchmarks account for most macro-accuracy degradation, and thresholds vary across splits. Test deployment-shifted calibration with benchmark-specific error reporting (§5.2).
- **Does decision localization generalize across backbones?** The analysis uses one backbone family. Repeat attribution, removal, and nested-training experiments on additional architectures (§§3, 6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides methods, controlled comparisons, confidence intervals, and explicit qualifications. Material gaps include adaptive end-to-end latency, complete benchmark definitions, component ablations, gate variability across seeds, the sub-model sampling schedule, and confirmed code/checkpoint releases.
