# Evidence-Guided Recovery for AI Agents with Ambiguous Tool Outcomes and Partial Execution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41453) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41453)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under a supplied finite provider model and shared safety shield, planner advice increased certified recovery completions from 9/58 to 30/58 model episodes, while standalone planning tied strong deterministic controls on completion (§§7.1, 7.8).

## Problem and motivation
A tool timeout can hide failure, successful execution, or an operation still in flight. Retrying can duplicate effects; stopping can leave approved work unfinished. Partial workflows also require reasoning about dependencies, compensation, authorization expiry, and remaining resources.

The authors target the decision gap after provider capabilities are fixed: which evidence to obtain and which obligations to continue safely. They distinguish actual goal satisfaction from completion justified by public evidence and stable against already accepted future effects (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Recovery contract and certificate.** Represent approved obligations as a dependency graph and maintain every latent state compatible with public observations. Check proposed actions across that full support, including authorization, duplicate effects, dependencies, compensation, and residual effects. Certification requires goals and safety to hold through every accepted finite-tail prefix; unknown-delay sentinels block certification (§§3–4).
- **Known-model decision interface.** Use bounded observation-contingent expectimax to choose queries, writes, waits, compensation, or handoff. Completion probability takes priority over obligation progress and cost. Search compares only fully completed common-depth layers and falls back to capability rules when needed (§4.2).
- **Controlled evaluation and auditing.** Separate deterministic policy comparisons from model-facing advice experiments, with an independently implemented Python effect-history adjudicator and exhaustive small-domain search checks (§§5–7).

The authors explicitly position planning, shielding, and compensation as established components; the contribution is their recovery-specific semantics, interface, and evaluation (§§2.4, 4.2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Planning advice improved model recovery | Across 58 matched model-by-assignment pairs spanning 19 developed families, certified completion was 30/58 with advice versus 9/58 with evidence prompting: +36.2 percentage points; registered family sign-flip p=0.0078. | §7.8, Table 6 |
| Both evaluated model bundles showed gains | gpt-oss-20b: 15/29 versus 5/29; GLM-4.7-Flash: 15/29 versus 4/29 certified completions, advice versus evidence prompting. Breakdown is post hoc. | §7.9, Table 8 |
| Advice reduced model interaction resources | In the same confirmation, advice used 272 versus 399 proposals and 202,508 versus 282,293 observed prompt-plus-completion tokens; planning computation was additional. | §7.8, Table 7 |
| Standalone search did not improve completion over strong controls | Joint planning, capability rules, verify–wait, and greedy each certified 28/37 assignments across 20 synthetic templates. | §7.1, Table 3 |
| Synthetic fee savings came with compute overhead | Joint planning versus capability rules: 188 versus 192 fee units, 103 versus 105 logical actions, and equal aggregate logical duration of 108 units. Current collection selection time totaled 9.53 versus 0.14 seconds on a shared host. | §§7.1, 7.4, Tables 3–4 |
| Safety results belong to the shared execution contract | Every active deterministic arm had zero recovery-introduced violations or false completion claims; three assignments per arm retained absolute historical violations. All 116 confirmatory model trajectories passed independent replay. | §§7.1, 6.5 |
| Small-domain implementation checks agreed | All 408 admissible root-action vectors matched independent enumeration across 63 task–horizon pairs; these were development diagnostics. | §7.5 |

The evidence supports the evaluated **advice interface under a common shield**, not a causal claim that deeper search itself produced the improvement. Rule-derived advice and legal-action-list interfaces were absent. The deterministic comparison establishes a resource trade-off, not superior completion or demonstrated monetary savings (§§1, 7.4, 8.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated model dependence:** Complete future attempt sequences provide substantially more predictive information than ordinary capability declarations. Omitted alternatives can invalidate safety premises without causing an observable contradiction (§8.2).
- **Author-stated transfer limits:** Held-out units were latent assignments within developed task families. Both model bundles used LM Studio; unseen families, independent runtimes, and third-party providers were not confirmed (§§6.5, 8.3).
- **Author-stated representation limits:** Shared mutable targets and cross-operation key aliasing are rejected. Batches expose individual operations; unknown-delay sentinels do not model all arbitrary late interleavings. Implementation limits are twelve operations and 4,096 initial support combinations (§§3.1, 3.4, 5.1).
- **Author-stated causal and economic limits:** Advice availability and its computation are bundled. Shared-host timings and synthetic fees do not establish deployment cost reductions (§§1, 7.4, 7.9).
- **Reproducibility limitation:** The new study artifact is retained locally for release review without a public identifier. The existing executor repository is a separate artifact (§8.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Independent replay strengthens confidence in results under the written specification, but cannot validate that specification against real provider behavior; the authors explicitly distinguish implementation independence from specification independence (§6.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does search outperform simpler advice?** The model experiment lacks matched rule-derived recommendations. Compare planner advice, capability-rule advice, and legal-action lists under identical evidence, budgets, and shielding (§1).
2. **Does the benefit transfer to unfamiliar workflows?** Families were used during development. Freeze the interface and evaluate entirely held-out families across an independent runtime (§8.3).
3. **How robust is recovery to incomplete provider models?** Observable contradiction handling does not detect every omitted alternative. Inject unmodeled delayed effects and correlated failures, then measure contradictions, unsafe execution, and uncertified outcomes (§§7.5, 8.2).
4. **When does planning pay for its computation?** Total model-study deployment costs are unavailable. Measure search time, provider costs, model usage, and end-to-end latency in matched sandbox executions (§§7.9, 8.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the scoped interpretation of the reported findings; operational generalization remains unestablished.

Material unavailable information includes the publicly accessible new recovery artifact, full table bodies omitted from the supplied rendering, independent-runtime and unseen-family results, matched alternative-advice experiments, real-provider validation, and complete model-study deployment costs.
