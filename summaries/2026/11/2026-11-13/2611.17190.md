# Consensus Capture in Reference-Based UAV Video Stabilization: A Benchmark on Dynamic Urban Scenes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17190) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17190)


## One-sentence takeaway
This benchmark finds that coherent vehicle motion can pull UAV registration toward traffic despite ordinary inlier statistics, while object masking eliminates measured capture in the tested cases (§7.3).

## Problem and motivation
Reference-frame stabilization aligns every video frame to one fixed frame so object positions remain comparable over time. Moving vehicles can supply mutually consistent correspondences that cause a robust homography estimator to follow traffic instead of the ground—“consensus capture.”

The authors identify an evaluation gap: conventional matching benchmarks lack independently moving content paired with background reference geometry, and inlier statistics measure fit consistency rather than alignment to the intended scene. Such errors propagate into vehicle positions and potentially their derived trajectories (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Two-part benchmark:** synthetic geometric and photometric perturbations with exact homographies, followed by real hovering-drone footage at offline and real-time compute budgets. Ten complete registration pipelines are considered, with site-disjoint tuning and controlled masking (§§3, 6–7).
- **Dynamic evaluation methodology:** background alignment, object-position error, motion-direction diagnostics, and a capture fraction measuring how much an estimate follows a moving group. Independent ground control points (GCPs) audit the pseudo-ground-truth estimator and directly score candidates (§4).
- **Case selection:** video-only measurements select hovers spanning moving-foreground coverage and coherence, with a stationary control; published trajectories validate the selection descriptors (§7.1).
- **Released assets:** authors report releasing benchmark code, annotated frames, GCPs, results, the hbb2obb conversion tool, and Songdo Vision OBB (§1.1). These are evaluation contributions; the deployed stabilization library is evaluated without modification (§3.2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Classical pipelines lead the static benchmark | At full resolution and 8,000 features, AKAZE achieves unmasked mAA **0.9839** over 0–5 px; RootSIFT, SIFT, and BRISK join its leading set under the authors’ comparison rule. Evaluation uses 20 scenes across 13 sites. | §6.2; Table 4 |
| Static masking has little accuracy cost | OBB masking’s largest mAA loss is **0.0024**, for XFeat; remaining measured pipelines change by less than **0.0013** in magnitude at the static operating point. | §6.2 |
| ORB exhibits measured capture | Unmasked ORB is captured on **28/304 group-bearing pairs (9.2%)** at ratio 0.18/1,000 features, versus **6/304 (2.0%)** at ratio 0.50/2,000 features; capture means following at least 10% of group displacement. | §7.3; Table 7 |
| Capture disappears under tested masks | No masked pipeline reaches the capture threshold in either regime; maximum masked capture fraction is **0.05** in real time and **0.03** offline. | §7.3; Fig. 8 |
| Large errors can retain typical inlier statistics | Real-time captured ORB estimates have median reference-relative corner error **18.8 px**; their median within-case/offset-bin ranks are **0.58** for both inlier count and ratio. | Table 7 |
| Independent points confirm some substantial errors | On the **four audited real-time ORB captures**, median GCP-based corner error is **4.3 px**, versus **1.17 px** for masked ORB on those frames. | §7.3 |
| Reference accuracy has a measurable floor | Across **1,064 points on 72 audited frames**, reference-estimator residuals have median **0.06 px** and 95th percentile **0.38 px**; GCP repeatability’s 95th percentile is **1.00 px**. | §7.2; Table 6 |

The evidence supports capture in these recordings and its removal under the tested masks. It does not establish a population capture rate or a universal average masking benefit. Importantly, the authors distinguish short-offset pulls toward traffic from long-offset breakdowns: both can exceed the capture threshold (§7.3). Their introduction reports that average masking benefits are nonsignificant or below GCP repeatability, but the detailed §7.5 results are missing from the supplied excerpt (§1, p. 3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Truncated source:** substantial middle content is omitted, including detailed later dynamic results; coverage is incomplete.
- **Authors’ scope:** evaluation concerns high-altitude hovers over Songdo intersections, with buildings and large canopy excluded. Conclusions are bounded by observed moving-foreground coverage (§§1, 3).
- **Concentrated descriptive evidence:** most real-time ORB captures occur in one case; capture rates are explicitly descriptive, not population estimates (§§4.9, 7.3).
- **Reference dependence:** masked SIFT pseudo-ground truth can favor related candidates and masking modes. GCP comparisons reduce this dependency, but small pulls remain unresolved at annotation repeatability (§§4.8, 7.3).
- **System-specific comparison:** pipelines come from the deployed library, rather than a comprehensive state-of-the-art selection. “Real-time” timing excludes decoding, object detection, box conversion, and warping (§§3.2, 6.2).
- **Conversion validation:** OBB fidelity is measured on its selection set, with partly unrevised detection boxes; authors identify both tuned-set optimism and reference slack (§5.1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** disappearance of capture does not isolate motion suppression from feature-budget redistribution, because ORB replaces masked features with background features (§3.2).

## Open research questions
- **How does capture scale with foreground coverage?** Current conclusions are coverage-bounded. Next experiment: apply the same protocol to lower-altitude or denser-traffic footage (§1).
- **When do oriented masks outperform horizontal masks?** Authors report little separation at tested densities and orientations. Next experiment: compare masks at equal suppressed area on oblique, densely queued vehicles (§§1, 5).
- **Can capture be prevented without annotated masks?** This benchmark measures deployed pipelines rather than alternative remedies. Next experiment: evaluate motion-aware or multi-model estimators using the same GCP and capture metrics (§2).
- **What downstream trajectory damage follows?** Registration-induced object error is measured, but the supplied text does not establish effects on speed or safety estimates. Next experiment: propagate captured and corrected registrations through identical trajectory processing (§§1, 4.2).

## Confidence and missing information
