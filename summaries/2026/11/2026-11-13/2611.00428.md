# Do Multimodal Models Decide Consistently on Equivalent Optical Spectra?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00428) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00428)


## One-sentence takeaway
Equivalent optical spectra produce substantial model decision changes, while training with answerable partial spectra improves evidence-sensitive abstention on the source dataset without establishing broad transfer.

## Problem and motivation
Scientific decision models should preserve answers when spectra change format or coordinate system and abstain when required measurements are missing. The authors argue that average accuracy and valid multiple-choice outputs conceal coordinate errors, evidence-position preferences, and indiscriminate abstention (§1).

The central gap is controlled evaluation of both physical equivalence and question-relative evidence sufficiency: missing irrelevant measurements should preserve answerability, whereas removing the relevant extremum should trigger abstention.

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired representation audit:** Deterministically labeled measured spectra are rendered as four numerical tables and two plots. Controls change question direction, evidence order, coordinate wording, and energy-table row order (§§3–5.1).
- **Evidence-sufficiency controls:** Each spectrum has full, answerable outside-cut, and unanswerable inside-cut views. These distinguish appropriate abstention from treating every truncated input as unanswerable (§3.3).
- **Controlled adaptation study:** d3-nano LoRA experiments vary representation coverage, training duration, and answerable truncations, with similarly sized added-data controls and individual seed reporting (§§5.3–5.4).
- **Evaluation artifacts:** The authors report releasing task definitions, per-response results, training-log summaries, and analysis code; other implementation artifacts require contacting them (Data and code availability).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinate changes can substantially reduce accuracy. | On gold core variants, Jev loses **41 percentage points** on eV tables relative to nm tables, using paired variants. | §5.1; Table 3 referenced |
| Matching wording to the coordinate does not reliably help. | With identical gold eV-table evidence, Jev gains **48 points** on compare questions; d3-nano drops from **59% to 34%**, a **25-point** loss. | §5.1; Table 5 referenced |
| Missing extrema often fail to trigger abstention. | On MOCLIP main middle-gap sufficiency requests, d3-nano, Jev, and Luna Decisions never abstain in any supported representation. Low-reasoning Luna abstains on **100%** of nm tables but **0%** of eV tables; each cell has **16 instances**. | §5.2 |
| Training representation coverage improves source-task performance. | MOCLIP compare accuracy on eV tables is **41–51%** after selected-epoch nm-only training, versus **94–100%** for the sample-matched four-table arm. Representation and wording coverage are confounded. | §5.3; Figure 3 |
| Answerable truncations counter the truncation shortcut. | Across ftC/ftCc’s **six seeds**, MOCLIP table scope discrimination is **+90 to +100 points**, with outside-cut accuracy changes of **−2 to +2 points**. Five of six matched original-task-data control seeds lose **27–99 points** on answerable outside cuts. | §5.4; Appendix C, Table 12 |
| The added-truncation advantage is dataset-dependent. | On gold tables, selected-epoch ftCc achieves scope discrimination of **+67 to +97 points**, versus **+70 to +75** for ftDc; the authors describe the arms as comparable. | §5.4; Table 12 |
| Stronger reasoning improves the tested configuration’s accuracy. | Low-reasoning generative Luna exceeds Luna Decisions by **28 points on MOCLIP** and **15 on gold** on matched core requests; internal checkpoint equivalence is unverified. | §§4.1, 5.5; Table 9 referenced |

Scope discrimination measures the difference between inside-cut and outside-cut abstention rates; it must be interpreted alongside full-view accuracy and outside-cut accuracy changes (§4.2).

The evidence supports representation-sensitive decisions and a source-dataset benefit from training on answerable truncations. It does not establish general physical understanding or reliable transfer. Agreement alone is insufficient because models can consistently choose the wrong answer (§§5.1, 6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Two narrow optical datasets, filtered clear extrema, fixed multiple-choice templates, and limited query intervals restrict generalization. MOCLIP sampling cannot support claims about narrow resonances (§7).
- **Dependence and uncertainty:** Gold includes repeated nominal recipes; its scope and main sets share spectra. Bootstrap intervals may understate uncertainty for a new experimental campaign, and exploratory comparisons lack multiple-comparison correction (§§3.3, 4.2, 7).
- **Adaptation confounds:** Only d3-nano is adapted, with a frozen vision tower. Representation and wording coverage are not fully separated; training updates and hardware differ across arms. Some training/development structures overlap (§§5.3, 7).
- **Interface comparison limits:** Service routes may use different checkpoints or inference implementations. Generative self-reported probabilities are not equivalent to decision-interface probabilities (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The strong MOCLIP scope results demonstrate behavior under these controls, but limited plot/task transfer and comparable gold controls weaken claims of a general evidence-sufficiency skill (§§5.3–5.4).
- **Reproducibility:** Rendering/model-calling code, raw outputs, and adapter weights are available only on request (Data and code availability).

## Open research questions
1. **Does evidence-sensitive abstention generalize to unfamiliar query intervals?** Only restricted intervals are tested, with boundaries partly associated with answer bands. A next experiment could hold out interval locations and widths entirely (§7).
2. **Which component causes energy-coordinate failures?** Row-order changes explain only part of the effect, while training mixes representation and wording coverage. A factorial experiment could independently vary row order, coordinate, spacing, and question wording (§§5.1, 5.3).
3. **Can adaptation transfer across optical systems and modalities?** Gold accuracy declines and plot comparison remains weak. A next experiment could compare table-only and mixed table/plot training while holding the training budget fixed (§5.3).
