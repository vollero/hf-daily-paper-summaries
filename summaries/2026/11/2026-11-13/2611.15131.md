# Duo: A Reciprocal World Model for Full-Duplex Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15131) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15131)


## One-sentence takeaway
Duo v1 combines autoregressive participation decisions with causal audiovisual generation for streaming conversation, with a small human evaluation showing stronger generation fidelity than content responsiveness (§§3, 7.1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing world models primarily simulate physical scenes while leaving reasoning and decisions to external controllers. Their proposed Reciprocal World Model (RWM) integrates decision-making with generated audiovisual behavior so participants can perceive, respond, interrupt, or remain silent while interaction continues (§1).

The technical gap is coordinating participation timing, language, and audiovisual synthesis without future-input leakage or a mismatch between recorded training histories and self-generated deployment histories (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Intent–Realization architecture:** A multimodal language model emits `<speak>` plus text or `<wait>` for each chunk. Its hidden states condition a joint audiovisual diffusion transformer alongside user media, a reference image, and generated history (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Causal timeline construction:** Timestamp-aligned inputs, chunk-contained visual patches, and module-specific attention masks restrict decisions and generation to available information (§§3.2, 4.2).
- **Dual rollout distillation:** The frozen Intent Module generates the final assistant turn within each training window, while Realization generates its own media history. Distribution matching trains a four-step streaming generator under these conditions (§4.3).
- **Supporting training and runtime:** Action balancing and history perturbations address participation failures; synthetic/recorded conversation pipelines provide aligned supervision; incremental decoding and GPU scheduling support streaming (§§4.1, 5–6).

These are the authors’ proposed contributions; the supplied experiments do not isolate their individual benefits.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| High aggregate human-check pass rate | Authors report 168/180 checks passed (93.3%) across 30 real-time interaction cases and six binary criteria; no comparator reported. | §7.1 |
| Generation fidelity exceeds semantic responsiveness | Language adherence, intelligibility, and identity stability passed all 90 combined checks; content responsiveness passed 22/30 cases (73.3%). | §7.1 |
| Cross-modal coherence is imperfect | Emotional consistency passed 96.7% of cases; lip–speech synchronization passed 90.0%, on the same 30-case evaluation. | §7.1 |
| Reported processing time excludes parts of response delay | Intent inference, generation, and overhead total approximately 330–650 ms; generation is reported at 768×576. First output additionally requires decoding and potentially up to approximately 708 ms of sampling wait. Runtime uses eight GPUs. | §§6.1, 6.3 |
| Incremental decoding can meet its shortest playback interval | Approximately 30 ms per latent versus a 41.7 ms shortest playback interval, conditional on generated latents arriving on schedule. | §6.2 |
| Fixed opening reference reduces synthesis drift in one example | In a shared-script 60-second, ten-block synthesis comparison, previous-frame-only conditioning loses 34.2 luma levels by block 9; adding the opening reference stabilizes near 99 luma. | §5.1, Fig. 9 |

The evidence supports preliminary conversational functionality and favorable fidelity ratings on the evaluated cases. It does not establish superiority over other systems, reliable interruption handling, or long-session robustness. The synthesis-drift comparison concerns training-data generation, rather than an end-to-end Duo comparison. Most showcase panels are explicitly script-conditioned data, so they do not independently demonstrate live capabilities (§7.2; Figs. 12–14).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Duo v1 is preliminary and supports basic audiovisual conversation; timing, long-session consistency, and misunderstanding recovery need broader evaluation (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Responsiveness failures include jokes, unusual remarks, and intent requiring joint interpretation of speech and visual context (§7.1).
- **Author-stated:** Camera-rotation weighting can also penalize intentional viewpoint changes or react to unreliable rotation estimates (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The small evaluation and absent reporting of case selection, evaluator counts, agreement, and uncertainty limit generalization (§7.1).
- **Analyst inference:** No reported component ablations establish how much dual rollout, action balancing, or causal visual encoding contributes.
- **Analyst inference:** Stage timings and a throughput target do not establish end-to-end latency distributions or sustained deadline reliability; GPU models are unspecified (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Scripted synthesis, reconstructed counterpart videos, and filtering out certain speech types may constrain coverage; their deployment impact is unmeasured (§5).

## Open research questions
1. **Does dual rollout improve long-session stability?** No isolated comparison is reported. Compare recorded-history, media-only-rollout, and dual-rollout training on extended live conversations, measuring drift and responsiveness.
2. **How reliably does Duo manage interruptions?** Interruption capability is described, but no dedicated timing evaluation appears. Test controlled interruptions at varied speech positions and measure yielding, overlap, and recovery.
3. **Can multimodal intent understanding close the responsiveness gap?** Reported errors involve pragmatic and audiovisual interpretation (§7.1). Evaluate matched cases with speech-only, video-only, and combined inputs.
4. **How do context resets affect continuity and latency?** Reset intervals trade history for memory and runtime (§6.3), without reported comparative results. Sweep reset intervals while measuring deadline misses and conversational consistency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture and reported results are explicit, but empirical validation is limited.

Material missing information includes training-data quantities and mixture proportions, detailed optimization settings, exact backbone sizes and GPU hardware, evaluation sampling and rater details, comparative baselines, component ablations, long-session measurements, end-to-end latency distributions, and concrete code/weight/data release access.
