# Actor, Judge, or Teacher? Understanding Decision Models in Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17167) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17167)


## One-sentence takeaway
A frozen decision model that acts poorly can still improve agent training when it scores each action’s contribution using the completed trajectory, as demonstrated with JEV on ALFWorld and WebShop.

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents need credit assignment across actions, but outcome rewards provide little differentiation within a trajectory. Step-level supervision can require environment-specific labels or repeated LLM judgments. The authors identify a gap: decision models are mainly assessed as action selectors, although poor acting may not imply poor retrospective supervision (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled role comparison:** Evaluate three dedicated decision models and three general-LLM variants as Actors and Judges, testing how prerequisite subgoals affect decisions (§§2–3.2).
- **Question–context analysis:** Cross **Value** (“will the task succeed?”) versus **Progress** (“did this action help?”) with **Prefix** versus **Hindsight** context. Goal masking and context expansion examine why acting and judging differ (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Teacher-based credit assignment:** Add frozen JEV scores, centered across actions in each rollout group, to GRPO outcome advantages with weight λ = 0.5. No step labels are required for this supervision, and the Teacher is absent at test time (§3.3, Eq. 2).

The contribution is chiefly an empirical role distinction and its training application, rather than a newly trained decision model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| An easier subgoal diverts action probability | Across 116 diagnostic states from 66 ALFWorld tasks, removing placement from the goal raises JEV’s probability on prerequisite-directed actions from 14.6% to 77.6%, holding other inputs fixed. | §3.1.1, Fig. 3a |
| Hindsight increases credit for prerequisite actions | On first actions of successful diagnostic continuations, JEV’s mean Progress score rises from 0.41 with Prefix to 0.59 with Hindsight; all six models show the same direction. | §3.2, Fig. 4 |
| Progress differentiates action types better than Value | On 462 ALFWorld trajectories containing milestones and no-ops, JEV’s within-trajectory milestone-minus-no-op gap is 0.36 under Progress versus 0.05 under Value, both with Hindsight. | §3.2.1, Fig. 5c |
| JEV improves the smaller policy | Qwen2.5-1.5B success: ALFWorld **72.8±3.6% → 82.9±2.9%**; WebShop **56.8±3.8% → 71.4±0.7%**, comparing reported GRPO baselines with Hindsight + Progress, averaged over three inference seeds. | Table 3 |
| The question matters during training | For the same 1.5B policy, Hindsight + Value achieves 69.3±0.7% ALFWorld success and 68.3±1.5% WebShop success, below Hindsight + Progress on both. | Table 3 |
| Gains extend to the larger policy | Qwen2.5-7B success: ALFWorld **77.6±5.2% → 83.8±1.6%**; WebShop **66.1±3.7% → 73.6±0.3%**, reported GRPO versus Hindsight + Progress over three inference seeds. | Table 3 |
| Teacher usefulness exceeds its acting performance | On the same ALFWorld evaluation tasks, JEV achieves 42.9% success as Actor; its supervised 1.5B policy achieves 82.9%. | §3.3.2 |

These results support the authors’ claim that acting performance alone is an inadequate screen for supervision quality. The controlled question/context comparisons support the usefulness of retrospective contribution scoring. They do not establish that the scores measure causal action contributions, or that the effect generalizes to other Teachers and longer tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments cover only two text-based environments with short episodes; longer software, tool-use, and assistant workflows remain untested (§7).
- **Author-stated deployment boundary:** Hindsight requires completed episodes; online filtering has only Prefix information (§7).
- **Author-stated opacity:** JEV and GPT Decisions have undisclosed adaptation procedures, preventing attribution of judging quality to particular training choices (§7).
- **Analyst inference—Teacher coverage:** Only JEV trains policies, so judging results across six models do not establish that all six are effective Teachers (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** GRPO results are imported from prior work, and reported repeated evaluations use inference seeds. The supplied text does not establish variability across independent training runs (§3.3, Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic selection:** Some Hindsight analyses use successful continuations, while action-type comparisons require both milestones and no-ops. These selected samples may not represent all rollouts (§§3.2–3.2.2).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Ranking changes and milestone separation support the proposed explanation, but do not independently prove causal credit assignment.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the method survive longer dependencies?** Short benchmark episodes leave this unresolved (§7). Next experiment: vary dependency depth and trajectory length while holding task family and training budget fixed.
2. **Does judging quality predict teaching quality across models?** Only JEV is tested as Teacher. Next experiment: train matched policies using all six Judges and compare judging metrics against policy gains.
3. **Can delayed online scoring recover Hindsight benefits?** Full trajectories are unavailable during online decisions (§7). Next experiment: compare Prefix, fixed-delay, revisable, and full-episode scoring under matched query budgets.
4. **Does Hindsight identify contribution rather than correlate with eventual success?** Outcome labels are masked, but later observations still reveal consequences. Next experiment: use matched continuations that alter whether the same action enables later progress.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Core methods and policy results are explicit, but the supplied retrieval omits several table bodies and prompt blocks.

Material gaps include exact benchmark split sizes, complete model/API identifiers, verbatim scoring prompts, per-run cost breakdowns, several actor-result tables, the meaning of Table 3’s ± values, and independent training-seed variability. Code availability is asserted by the authors but was not verified.
