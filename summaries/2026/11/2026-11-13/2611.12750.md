# The Narrative Gap: Can LLMs Help Us Navigate Diverse Narratives Across Languages?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12750) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12750)


## One-sentence takeaway
NarrativeBench finds that evaluated LLMs incompletely cover supplied multilingual narratives even when explicitly asked for diversity, although citation coverage alone does not establish faithful representation or improved user understanding (§§5, 7).

## Problem and motivation
LLMs increasingly mediate information seeking across languages and regions, where accounts of contested events differ in emphasis and interpretation. The authors identify a gap between evaluating factual conflicts or language-dependent answers and evaluating whether users can selectively request, exclude, and recover competing narratives (§§1–2).

They operationalize a narrative as a source perspective paired with a publication language, acknowledging that this simplifies disagreement within regions and languages (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Capability framework:** Three foundational capabilities—narrative inclusion, exclusion, and missing-narrative identification—and three higher-level capabilities covering diversity, robustness to confirmatory framing, and language parity (§4.1).
- **NarrativeBench:** A corpus of 1,333 articles, 180 narrative groups, 23 contested topics, 23 publication languages, and 460 queries, including directly sourced Reddit questions and adaptations (§3; Appendix A).
- **Controlled model audit:** Fourteen models under 15 configurations answer the same 4,800 cases per configuration across 24 query languages. Evaluation uses supplied documents rather than testing retrieval (§5; Appendix B.1).
- **Citation-based scoring and assets:** Narrative presence requires a citation to an associated article; inclusion/exclusion use F1, while other tasks use recall. Wrong-language responses receive zero. The authors report releasing data, responses, inputs, and scoring code (§4.2; Appendix A.5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Selective narrative control is weak | Across 15 configurations, mean inclusion F1 is 19.9% versus exclusion F1 of 49.3%; missing-narrative recall is 56.8%. Reported SEs are 0.6 percentage points each. | §5.1 |
| Explicit diversity requests leave substantial gaps | Mean recall is 44.3% under baseline prompting and 49.2% under diverse prompting, each with SE 0.7 points. Best configuration, Gemini 2.5 Pro, reaches 70.6% and 74.9%, respectively. | §5.2, HC-1 |
| Framing changes coverage without a uniform direction | Mean absolute neutral–confirmatory BP/DP recall gap is 3.23 percentage points across configurations. Adjusted signed comparisons include both positive and negative estimates. | §5.2, HC-2; Table 7 |
| Access differs across languages | Mean absolute English-relative gaps are 8.74 points across query languages and 7.51 points across target narrative languages, using the task pooling specified in §4.2. | §5.2, HC-3 |
| Some source languages are disproportionately omitted | Under diverse prompting, omission rates exceed English by 59.8 points for Tigrinya, 57.9 for Nepali, and 54.6 for Armenian, conditional on those languages being available. | §5.2; Figure 1E |
| Citation support is imperfect | In an audit of 45 cited sentence–document pairs across nine query languages, both annotators found at least partial support for 33 pairs; assessment used English versions. | Appendix C.1; Table 8 |
| Citation-count controls challenge interpretation | In 1,720 supplementary cases across 12 models, observed citation selection outperforms citation-count-matched random selection on inclusion, but underperforms it on the other four tasks. | Appendix C.2; Table 9 |

These results establish shortcomings under the paper’s supplied-evidence, citation-based protocol. They do not establish that every uncited narrative is absent semantically, that cited narratives are faithfully explained, or that broader coverage reduces polarization. Table 2 explicitly illustrates that greater coverage can accompany endorsement of a confirmatory premise.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Perspective–language grouping misses internal disagreement; localized-search sampling and text-only sources limit representativeness (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Coverage does not verify source reliability, resolve misinformation, or measure preservation of narrative meaning and emphasis (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Human information consumption and evolving conversations require separate user studies and multi-turn evaluations (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** Scores combine narrative selection with language compliance because wrong-language responses receive zero; aggregate deficits therefore cannot be attributed solely to narrative understanding (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Several reasoning comparisons also change output budgets or sampling settings, limiting causal attribution to reasoning effort alone (Table 6; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Translation validation covers only a subset of languages, and English-query plausibility ratings show weak inter-rater agreement, tempering claims of ecological validity (Appendices A.3–A.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 9 labels its scores as narrative F1, whereas the main protocol assigns recall to MNI, BP, and DP; the supplied text does not resolve this inconsistency (§4.2; Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does citation coverage track faithful narrative representation?** The small support audit does not assess complete responses’ causal and interpretive fidelity. Next experiment: native-language annotation of full answers against narrative-level claims (§7; Appendix C.1).
2. **Can diversity increase without amplifying misinformation?** Source verification is outside the benchmark’s principal scoring. Next experiment: jointly score perspective coverage, evidential support, and handling of misleading accounts (§7).
3. **Do coverage gains change what users consider?** No reported user study tests exposure, comprehension, or belief reinforcement. Next experiment: randomize multilingual users to baseline and diversity-focused answers, then measure source exploration and understanding (§7).
4. **Which intervention improves selection across languages?** Existing setting comparisons have mixed effects and some confounds. Next experiment: vary training objectives or reasoning settings while holding output budgets and sampling fixed (Appendix C.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main findings are explicit and supported by detailed appendices, but their interpretation depends on a coarse narrative definition and citation proxy.

Material unavailable information includes the numerical body of Table 1, plotted language-level estimates and intervals, a concrete release URL, full localized prompts and released files, detailed calculation of main-result standard errors, and comprehensive native-language assessment of narrative fidelity.
