# TTB: Test-time Baking for Efficient Rendering of Decoder-only View Synthesis Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17331) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17331)


## One-sentence takeaway
TTB fits scene-specific fast-weight MLPs to cross-view attention outputs, accelerating repeated novel-view rendering while approaching DVSM quality, at the cost of expensive scene prefilling and limited per-MLP capacity.

## Problem and motivation
Decoder-only view synthesis models produce high-quality images, but querying their scene KV-cache becomes slower as context grows. Replacing attention with test-time-trained MLPs improves rendering efficiency but loses quality. The authors target this gap by spending more computation once during scene reconstruction to make subsequent rendering faster (§§1, 3.3).

They also identify an input mismatch: conventional fast-weight updates receive image-and-camera tokens, whereas novel-view rendering receives camera-only tokens (§3.3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Attention-output baking:** Train scene-specific fast-weight MLPs against cross-view attention outputs instead of raw value projections; retain only the baked weights for rendering (§3.3.1).
- **Context pairing:** Split each context view into camera-only rendering tokens and image-plus-camera target tokens, aligning baking inputs with novel-view inputs (§3.3.2).
- **Convergence-oriented optimization:** Use a residual MSE target, multiple Muon updates, and feedback into context tokens; differentiate through the updates during outer-loop training (§3.3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Capacity scaling at inference:** Cluster context cameras and bake separate local copies, routing each novel view to one copy without retraining (§3.3.4; Appendix A.6).

The supplied results support a strong rendering-efficiency trade-off, especially for repeated dense-context rendering. They do not establish uniform quality equivalence to DVSM: some datasets and metrics retain gaps. “Constant complexity” concerns global scene retrieval with respect to context size; intra-view attention remains resolution-dependent, and duplicate routing searches over group centroids (Table 8; Appendix A.6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fixed fast-weight capacity causes saturation; local routing is heuristic; full TTB reconstruction remains quadratic in context size; single-shot use is less favorable (§4.3).
- **Scope ambiguity:** The limitations describe interpolation without unseen-view extrapolation, but Appendix A.3 samples some extrapolated targets and Appendix D.2 claims moderate extrapolation benefits. The supported extrapolation range is unclear.
- **Analyst inference:** Hard switching between local copies could affect trajectory consistency; the supplied text reports no boundary-continuity metric (Appendix A.6).
- **Comparison qualification:** LaCT has a deeper decoder than TTB, so their FPS difference cannot be attributed solely to baking (§4.2; Table 9).
- **Evidence availability:** Many numerical tables are missing their bodies, including the main DL3DV comparison and most ablations. Inner-loop ablation references also conflict between Table 5’s caption and §4.3’s Table 6 reference.
- **Reproducibility:** Training uses substantial hardware and fully differentiated inner updates with checkpointing; code/checkpoint release status and a complete timing protocol are not established (Appendix A.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can learned routing improve local-copy consistency?** Routing is heuristic and unblended. Compare hard routing with learned or blended routing, measuring quality, trajectory flicker, memory, and latency (Appendix A.6).
- **What is the best capacity allocation under a fixed resource budget?** Width and duplication are studied separately. Compare both at matched persistent memory and rendering latency across context counts (Appendix C.4).
- **Can cheaper baking targets preserve full-TTB quality?** TTB– reduces reconstruction cost but loses quality. Test sparse or approximate attention targets against full attention at matched end-to-end rendering budgets (§4.1; Appendix B.2).
- **How far can TTB extrapolate?** The stated scope and supplementary descriptions differ. Evaluate controlled camera offsets beyond context coverage, reporting quality against distance (§4.3; Appendices A.3, D.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several comparisons are well described, but important table bodies are unavailable.

Missing information includes complete DL3DV and ablation numbers, detailed efficiency measurements, uncertainty or repeated-run statistics, code/checkpoint availability, routing-boundary evaluation, and a clearly defined extrapolation range.
