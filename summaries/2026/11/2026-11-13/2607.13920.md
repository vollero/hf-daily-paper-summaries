# DeepStress: Stress-Testing Deep Search Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.13920) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13920)


## One-sentence takeaway

DeepStress exposes distinct search-agent failures under controlled evidence degradation, showing why answer accuracy alone can obscure unsupported answers, cautious abstention, and reliance on knowledge already stored in model parameters (§§5.1–5.2).

## Problem and motivation

Search agents depend on retrieved evidence, but standard accuracy evaluations obscure whether failures arise from misleading documents, ineffective searching, or answer-format problems. The authors identify a shortage of reproducible evaluations that directly control evidence quality rather than observe failures after retrieval (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Dynamic stress-testing environment:** DeepStress replaces retrieval with one synthetic document per search call, generated for the agent’s actual query. Independently sampled labels control apparent trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality; correct documents are grounded in dataset evidence (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Reliability-aware evaluation:** Five outcomes distinguish correct answers, confident incorrect answers, abstention, tool-budget exhaustion, and missing answer tags. The proposed RAS credits correct answers and rewards abstention or budget exhaustion according to evidence unreliability; CoPRAS divides weighted computational cost by accumulated RAS (§3.4).
- **Comparative diagnosis:** The study evaluates 12 model-and-harness systems on 200 questions from each of HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus across seven scenarios, producing 33,600 traces. Each scenario varies one quality axis while keeping the others fully positive (§4.3). Exploratory analyses examine search-query adaptation and verbalized concerns about evidence quality (Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

Unless specified otherwise, agent results below aggregate both datasets; degradation varies one axis while the other two remain fully positive, with a maximum of 100 search calls per question (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic labels are imperfect, especially factuality | Human evaluation of 50 documents: agreement with intended labels was 96.4% for trustworthiness and 85.5% for factuality; inter-annotator κ was 0.86 and 0.64, respectively. Relevance was not independently checked. | §3.3; Appendix A.1, Table 2 |
| Credibility cues have little effect on some agents | Search-R1 correctness remained between 78% and 80% across trustworthiness levels, without a clear abstention change. | §5.1; Figure 1a |
| Irrelevant evidence elicits different failures | When relevance falls from fully positive to zero, Search-R1’s incorrect-answer rate rises from 19.5% to 79.2%; AutoRefine’s missing-answer-tag rate rises from 18.2% to 61.9%. | §5.1; Figure 1b |
| Relevant false evidence can be harder to reject | SpiqaDR abstains in 15.2% of runs at zero factuality versus 38.0% at zero relevance; DR-Tulu produces incorrect answers in 59.1% versus 33.8%, respectively. | §5.1; Figure 1b–c |
| Reliability scoring changes the interpretation of degradation | Averaged across isolated-axis settings, zero quality yields correctness of 0.30 and RAS of 0.61. RAS credits abstention and budget exhaustion under unreliable evidence. | §§3.4, 5.1 |
| Degradation increases weighted cost | From fully positive to zero quality, average weighted cost rises from 10.9k to 20.5k, and TPC from 18.1k to 75.7k; CoPRAS reaches 36.8k. These are weighted token/search units, not measured monetary costs. | §5.1; cost definition in §3.4 |
| HotpotQA correctness need not demonstrate successful retrieval | With zero factuality and otherwise positive document attributes, GPT-5-mini (medium) reaches 60% HotpotQA correctness and every system reaches at least 10%; BrowseComp-Plus performance is almost zero. No-search baselines qualitatively support this dataset contrast. | §5.2; Figure 2; Appendix F.1 |

The evidence establishes heterogeneous behavior within this synthetic environment. The no-search baseline supports a parametric-knowledge explanation for some HotpotQA success, but does not establish benchmark contamination specifically.

RAS measures the authors’ chosen preference for avoiding wrong answers; its separation from accuracy is partly built into its definition. The authors also attribute robustness differences to training methods (§5.1), but **analyst inference:** these comparisons do not isolate training effects from model scale, data, or harness differences.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** One document per call and a single search loop simplify real retrieval. Findings may not transfer to richer systems with reranking, summarization, or sub-agents (Limitations).
- **Author-stated realism limits:** Experiments isolate quality axes rather than combine degradations. Synthetic documents may be cleaner and less diverse than web content; correct-document generation requires supporting evidence (Limitations).
- **Generation validity:** Human checking covers a small sample, and factuality agreement is imperfect. Relevance is assumed correct by construction rather than independently annotated (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Metric assumptions:** RAS treats abstention and budget exhaustion alike and includes apparent trustworthiness in its reliability product. Cost weights and tool budgets can change rankings (§3.4; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—confounding:** Systems differ in model size, training, decoding, and orchestration. DR-Tulu’s reasoning ablation also changes interaction patterns, limiting causal conclusions about reasoning itself (§4.2; Appendices C.2, D.2).
- **Analyst inference—conflict structure:** False documents are not constructed to agree with one another, while correct documents share grounding. Results under mixed factuality therefore do not establish robustness to coordinated misinformation (§5.2).
- **Reproducibility and judging:** Repository/checkpoint links are deferred. Final answers use an LLM judge without reported human validation in the supplied text; GPT reasoning summaries and open-weight full traces are not directly comparable (Appendices D–E, C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Do findings transfer to realistic retrieval?** Single-document synthetic returns leave this unresolved. A next experiment could return multiple real and synthetic documents with jointly varied quality issues.
2. **Can agents resist mutually consistent false evidence?** The mixed-factuality setting uses uncoordinated errors (§5.2). Compare coordinated false narratives against independent errors while holding factuality rates fixed.
3. **Does reliability-aware training improve behavior?** The paper proposes this direction but does not test it (§6). Train matched agents with correctness-only versus reliability-aware rewards, then evaluate on held-out degradations.
4. **How sensitive are rankings to evaluation choices?** Appendix B acknowledges scoring and cost dependence. Recompute rankings with separate abstention/budget-exhaustion credit and validate answer categories against human judgments.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported headline findings, but many referenced tables and figures lack their underlying values.

Material missing information includes complete per-system results and confidence intervals, numerical direct-inference baselines, several full prompts and document examples, accessible implementation/checkpoint links, human validation of final-answer judging, and reported variability across repeated stochastic runs.
