# Agent-R^2: Scaling Agents for Realistic Productivity Work via Real-Source Data Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08219)


## One-sentence takeaway

Agent-R² combines web-derived workflows, authentic files, and deliverable rubrics to train a 4B agent that reports 51.0% GDPval Pass@1 versus 48.0% for Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 under a common runtime (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Realistic productivity work requires agents to integrate heterogeneous files, execute long workflows, and produce usable deliverables satisfying detailed requirements. The authors argue that existing training data teaches these capabilities separately and lacks scalable supervision connecting professional intent, authentic file environments, and deliverable quality (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Real-source synthesis pipeline:** Extract file-oriented workflows from DLR-Web using O*NET occupational seeds; retrieve and inspect public attachments; then synthesize instructions and evidence-grounded rubrics. Qwen3.6-Plus handles synthesis, while Claude-Opus-4.5 generates expert trajectories and reference deliverables that undergo verification (§3.1).
- **Training collection and agent:** Produce approximately 4k tasks from 10k corpus samples, using 3k for supervised fine-tuning and a disjoint 1k for GRPO reinforcement learning of Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507. RL rewards rubric-scored deliverable quality rather than intermediate tool behavior (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Empirical and behavioral evidence:** Evaluate productivity work, document-grounded QA, and additional workflows; test authentic-file and rubric-reward contributions; analyze premature output, verification, error masking, and numerical traceability (§§4.3–4.5; Appendix B).

The distinctive contribution is the integrated synthesis recipe; SFT, GRPO, and model-based judging are adopted components.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger GDPval performance | Full-set Pass@1: Agent-R²-4B 51.0%, versus Qwen3-235B 48.0%; reported improvement over its base is 18.6 percentage points. Common sandbox; Gemini-3.1-Pro judge. | §§1, 4.2–4.3; Table 1 referenced |
| Transfer to document QA | OfficeQA official Markdown version: base → SFT → RL Pass@1 is 2.2% → 11.4% → 14.8%; Qwen3-235B scores 13.8%. Final-model Pass@3 is 26.8% versus 25.6%. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| RL gain exceeds additional imitation data | GDPval Pass@1: 3k-SFT 45.3%, 4k-SFT 45.5%, and 3k-SFT plus 1k-RL 51.0%. | Appendix B.3; Table 10 referenced |
| Authentic files and rubric rewards help | Matched 1k-task SFT comparison: real-file versus synthetic-file GDPval Pass@1 is 43.3% versus 41.4%. Removing rubric rewards from RL lowers Pass@1 from 51.0% to 49.5%. | §4.5; Table 4 referenced |
| Completion and shortcut indicators improve | Completion rate: base 86.8%, SFT 78.2%, RL 90.9%. Writing after an unfixed build-phase error: 55%, 16%, 11% of relevant error episodes, respectively. | §4.4; Figure 4; Table 3; Appendix C |
| Additional workflow transfer | On 132 GTA-Workflow tasks, Root Score is 4.52/10 versus base 1.53/10 and DeepSeek-v4-Flash 5.15/10. | Appendix B.4 |
| Judge ranking is stable in tested repeats | Across three judgments of the same GDPval deliverables per judge, Agent-R² leads Qwen3-235B under both Gemini-3.1-Pro and DeepSeek-v4-Flash in all six evaluations. | Appendix B.1 |
| Retained data still contains quality defects | Manual audit of 100 retained tasks: full workflow alignment 86%, cross-file consistency 95%, feasibility 85%; rubric-item validity 99.8%. | Appendices E.3–E.5 |

These results support improvements under the reported environments and provide controlled evidence for RL and authentic attachments. They do not establish human-rated workplace readiness or that reduced shortcut behavior causally explains the gains. GDPval’s Pass@k scores the first attempt producing a deliverable, whereas OfficeQA’s measures whether any attempt answers correctly; the metrics should not be conflated.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Acknowledged data limitations:** Authentic files do not guarantee compatible attachments or feasible tasks; the manual audit confirms residual inconsistencies and infeasibility (Appendix E).
- **Scope restriction:** Attachment retrieval excludes files exceeding 50 pages or 2 MB, and office formats are the primary focus (§§3.1, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation validity:** OfficeQA uses Markdown, so transfer does not directly demonstrate improved perception of original document layouts. GDPval relies on model judges; repeated judging supports ranking stability but does not establish human agreement (§4.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—behavioral measurement:** Shortcut metrics use automatic pattern matching. Numerical “traceability” means a number appears in inputs, which does not verify a derived calculation’s correctness (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—causal interpretation:** Cross-model effective-step correlations do not establish a benefit from longer trajectories. Within-model analyses instead associate longer trajectories with lower scores (Appendix B.5).
- **Evidence availability:** Several tables appear only as captions in the supplied text, limiting inspection of complete baseline results and training configurations.

## Open research questions

1. **Does improvement survive human evaluation?** Judge-repeat stability leaves human agreement unresolved. Next experiment: blinded professional assessment of paired GDPval deliverables.
2. **How much does task infeasibility affect training?** The audit finds infeasible retained tasks without isolating their effect. Next experiment: compare training on feasibility-filtered and size-matched unfiltered collections.
3. **Does transfer extend to complex original files?** OfficeQA is evaluated as Markdown and synthesis limits attachment size. Next experiment: evaluate original-format documents stratified by length, size, and cross-file dependency complexity.
4. **Does verification itself cause better outcomes?** Behavioral changes accompany training but are not independently manipulated. Next experiment: compare controlled verification interventions at matched tool budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the core method and reported comparisons, including useful ablations and audits. Material gaps include complete numerical contents of several tables, RL hyperparameters, training compute and synthesis costs, uncertainty across independent training runs, human evaluation, and confirmed dataset/model release details.
