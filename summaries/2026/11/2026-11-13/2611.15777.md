# SO-Arena: Benchmarking the Capability Spectrum and Reliability of System One Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15777) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15777)


## One-sentence takeaway
SO-Arena’s evaluation of 21 models shows that fast structured decision models have domain-dependent capability gaps, while strong benchmark performance does not ensure recognition of missing correct answers (§§4.1–4.2).

## Problem and motivation
System One models select structured outcomes without lengthy reasoning generation, making them attractive for routing, classification, action selection, and verification. The authors argue that aggregate accuracy inadequately captures task difficulty, interactive competence, evidence-supported correctness, and reliability when available options change (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evidence-audited benchmark:** 2,919 tasks drawn or derived from 40 public sources, spanning 11 domains and five capability families; references are reviewed against model-visible evidence and stored separately from inputs (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Difficulty and interaction coverage:** Domain-relative tiers in nine domains, interactive tool episodes, reference-route web tasks, and history-dependent completion verification. The benchmark contains 2,300 static and 619 environment-based tasks (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Separate capability and reliability metrics:** Capability equally weights domains using their hardest tiers, with specified exceptions. Reliability combines chance-adjusted option-order stability with missing-answer discrimination through a geometric mean (§3.3).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Capability, latency, and reliability measurements, plus analyses of backbone changes and controlled thinking modes. This is an evaluation contribution, not a new trained decision model (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| System Two references lead overall capability | Capability score: GPT-6 Sol **82.3**, Luna **79.7**, versus **70.2** for both GLiDE and KnowLine-31B; scores equally weight selected upper-tier domain results, not all-task accuracy. | Table 2; §3.3.1 |
| System One offers low measured latency | On **1,109 static single-question choice tasks**, mean latency was **113 ms** for GLiDE and **177 ms** for KnowLine-31B, each using one H100 80 GB GPU and its documented inference path. | Appendix C; Table 5 |
| Reasoning benefits depend on domain | On **120 medium/hard Math tasks**, StartLux Auto scored **50.8** with thinking disabled and **92.5** with thinking always enabled; Luna scored **100.0**. | Table 6 |
| Larger/newer backbones particularly help agent tasks | Across four paired model families, mean backbone gains were **25.6 points** on **182 medium/hard tool tasks** and **23.9 points** on **237 medium/hard web tasks**; corresponding StartLux thinking gains were **2.6** and **1.8 points**. | Tables 6–7 |
| Stability is relatively strong for most tested decision models | **14 of 18** System One models achieved chance-adjusted order stability **S > 0.8**, using original options plus **12 reorderings** per item; Jeva-4B has **S = 1** by construction. | §§3.3.2, 4.1.2; Appendix B |
| Missing-answer recognition separates similarly capable models | Kev-27B, Jev API 1.14, and JevAny-27B each exceeded all four capability-leading System One models in null discrimination; paired **95% bootstrap intervals** excluded zero for all **12 comparisons**, resampling **597 items 2,000 times**. | §4.2.2; Appendix D |

These results support evaluating capability and option-set reliability separately. They do not establish reliability across arbitrary distribution shifts, calibrated deployment abstention, or unrestricted agent behavior. The backbone comparisons also change model generation alongside size, preventing attribution to parameter count alone.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Free-text tasks are excluded; web interaction uses candidate menus and verified routes rather than unrestricted browsing (§3.2; Appendix A.5).
- **Author-stated label uncertainty:** Sentiment scores measure agreement with recorded ratings or annotations, which may be ambiguous. Some safety and completion-verification references are model-derived (Appendices A.1, A.3–A.4).
- **Author-stated sampling constraints:** Tiers are not calibrated across domains, some retrieval pairs share queries, and hard completion verification contains only 20 items derived from five base tasks (Figure 4; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Local GPU timing and network-inclusive API timing are different measurement conditions. The latency subset also excludes interactive workloads (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—reliability selection:** For two models, the better of two null-output formats was selected on the evaluation items, potentially favoring their reported discrimination. AUC measures separation rather than a usable abstention threshold (Appendix B; §3.3.2).
- **Internal reporting ambiguity:** The introduction ranks JevAny-27B eleventh in capability, whereas §4.1.2 ranks it eighth among 18 System One models. Ranking populations differ, and exact figure values are unavailable in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference—novelty boundary:** Related work already includes difficulty tiers and perturbations; the supported contribution is their particular integration with audited evidence, interaction, history verification, and separate reliability scoring (§5).

## Open research questions
1. **Does null discrimination translate into useful abstention?** The paper reports AUC-based separation, not deployment thresholds. Next experiment: choose thresholds on held-out data and measure error versus coverage.
2. **Do capability rankings survive unrestricted action generation?** Current agents select from supplied menus and web routes. Next experiment: compare menu-based and freely generated actions under matched goals and checkers.
3. **What independently drives backbone gains?** Paired comparisons change both size and backbone generation. Next experiment: hold training recipe and generation fixed while varying size.
4. **How robust are history-verification findings?** The hard subset is small, and thinking gains reverse sign when restricted to it (Table 7). Next experiment: expand independent base tasks and counterfactual histories, retaining task-level resampling.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Construction and principal capability results are well documented, but the supplied text lacks exact per-model reliability scores, full reliability-subset selection details, public benchmark/code release information, and independent reproduction. Figures are represented mainly by captions, limiting verification of plotted rankings and values.
