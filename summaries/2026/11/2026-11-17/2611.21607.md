# ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21607) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21607)


## One-sentence takeaway

With a shared action-head design and fixed training budgets within each benchmark, ScaleVLA’s frozen 552B vision-language backbone improved low-data learning on LIBERO, achieved the highest mean success under LIBERO-PRO perturbations, and completed more real-robot charger insertions than the tested smaller backbones (§§4.1–4.5, Tables 3, 7, and 8).

## Problem and motivation

Most vision-language-action policies use relatively small vision-language backbones. The authors ask whether representations from frontier-scale pretraining can help robots learn from fewer demonstrations, adapt to changed environments, and perform precise manipulation while keeping action inference fast enough for deployment (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Frontier backbone integration.** ScaleVLA extracts visual and text tokens from the frozen DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash causal encoder in one pass per action chunk. A GR00T N1.7–style action transformer, trained from scratch with flow matching, combines those features with robot state to generate continuous commands in four steps. The language decoder is skipped (§§3.1–3.2, Figure 2).
- **Controlled backbone comparison.** The authors compare source models from 2B to 552B, holding the action transformer, its initialization, robot training data, and optimization budget fixed within each benchmark. The feature adapter’s input width follows the backbone (§4.1, Table 1).
- **Deployment measurement.** The study measures client-side action-chunk latency for the sparse frontier backbone and reports physical task completion and insertion times separately (§§4.3, 4.5, Tables 6 and 8).

| Better mean robustness on LIBERO-PRO | Without retraining, ScaleVLA averaged 45.1% success across perturbation settings versus 31.3% for the best smaller backbone. On Object-suite environment perturbations, ScaleVLA reached 31.2% versus the best smaller result of 32.4%. | §4.2, Table 3 |

The experiments show that the tested DeepSeek backbone can improve success under a shared action-training setup, including when the number of demonstrations is limited. The authors attribute the gains to stronger pretrained representations. The comparisons leave the effect of parameter count alone unresolved, and the physical timing results describe successful reference-layout trials.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** The low-data sweep uses normalization statistics from the full training set. The smaller backbone also improves with more optimizer steps, so the sweep measures demonstration efficiency at a fixed update budget. Each policy uses one training seed (Appendices A, C, and D).
- **Author-reported.** ScaleVLA uses more GPUs and longer inputs in the RoboCasa latency comparison. The authors did not isolate the effect of token count or verify feature equivalence between the timing and success-evaluation deployments (§4.3, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference.** The largest backbone also changes model family and switches from dense to sparse architecture, so the experiments cannot attribute gains to size alone. The 552B label counts total source-model parameters, and the policy runs only the causal encoder (§3.1, Table 1).
- **Analyst inference.** The physical test covers one task on one bimanual setup. ScaleVLA’s success margin over π₀.₅ + Action Head amounts to one trial in each layout setting, so the comparative advantage remains uncertain (§4.5, Table 8).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gain comes from scale within a model family?** The frontier comparison changes both family and architecture. Compare multiple sizes within one family with matched pretraining recipes, feature readouts, and action-side training (§4.1, Table 1).
2. **Does the low-data advantage persist with subset-only preprocessing and longer training?** The sweep uses full-data normalization, one nested subset sequence, and a fixed update budget. Repeat it with independently sampled subsets, subset-only normalization, multiple training seeds, and longer update sweeps (Table 7, Appendices A and D).
3. **Does the insertion advantage carry over to other contact-rich tasks?** The physical evidence comes from charger insertion alone. Compare the same backbones on several physical insertion and assembly tasks using matched layouts and repeated trials (§4.5, Table 8).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported architecture, protocols, and tabulated results. Material gaps include training-seed variability, the active parameter count for the executed encoder path, measured observation age and buffer gaps during asynchronous control, and confirmation of ScaleVLA code, checkpoint, or demonstration release.
