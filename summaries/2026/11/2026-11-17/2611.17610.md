# More Agents, More Samples, or More Thinking? Untangling Coordination, Ensembling, and Test-Time Compute in Multi-Agent LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17610) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17610)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 474 contrasts from eight studies, communicating multi-agent LLMs averaged 4.2 percentage points lower accuracy than single-agent baselines matched on sample count or a declared inference budget (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Comparing a communicating team with a single greedy pass credits the team for any gains from drawing more samples or spending more inference compute. That makes it hard to tell whether communication itself earns its cost. The author identifies a gap in prior evaluations: studies control different resources, and their results had not been synthesized in one accounting framework or systematically compared against both greedy and sampled baselines for the same multi-agent systems (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evaluation framework.** Parity-Aware Compute Evaluation (PACE) tracks thinking tokens, total tokens, monetary cost, latency, sample count, and tool scaffolding. Its baseline ladder distinguishes greedy passes, sample-matched comparisons, comparisons matched on a named resource, and Pareto sweeps (Figure 1, §4).
- **Cross-study synthesis.** The paper extracts 516 unique multi-agent configurations from nine studies, compares baseline categories, audits how results change when the same systems face sampled baselines, and fits an exploratory multilevel model that accounts for clustering by study (§5, Tables 1–3).
- **Formal analysis.** The paper applies established results to redundant evidence, correlated votes, and answer revisions, and derives the token overhead of replaying full conversation histories. The conformity threshold comes from a stylized model with constant transition rates (§3.4, Appendix A).
- **Reusable evaluation assets.** The author reports releasing a reviewer checklist, evaluation card, trace-auditing utilities, hidden-profile task generator, extraction tables, and reproduction code (§6.3–6.4, Appendix D, Open Science and Audit Trail).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-agent systems generally trailed sampled or budget-matched baselines. | Mean multi-agent minus baseline accuracy was −5.1 percentage points across 360 sampled-baseline contrasts and −1.2 points across 114 thinking-token- or iteration-matched contrasts. The combined mean was −4.2 points across 474 contrasts in eight studies, with 352 of 474 (74%) tying or trailing. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| The greedy-baseline result depended more on the largest study. | Across 358 single-pass contrasts from five studies, mean accuracy difference was −3.2 points, shrinking to −0.5 points when Zhang et al. was omitted. The combined sampled and budget-matched mean remained negative at −2.9 points after the same omission. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Switching to a sampled baseline reduced the apparent multi-agent advantage. | Across 318 paired contrasts in five studies, replacing the greedy baseline with a sampled baseline reduced the accuracy difference by 3.8 points with studies weighted equally. The shift was negative in every study. | Eq. 5, §5.3, Table 2 |
| The exploratory pooled estimate stayed negative across sensitivity checks. | For the 474 sampled- or budget-matched contrasts, the pooled log odds ratio was −0.195 (CR2 95% CI [−0.320, −0.070]). Every leave-one-study-out estimate stayed negative. Intervals included zero under strong assumed correlations spanning disjoint benchmarks within studies. | §5.4, Tables 3 and 5 |
| Debate and mixture-of-agents led in one comparable-compute setting. | On MMLU-Pro with Llama-3.1-70B, debate and mixture-of-agents led 10-sample self-consistency by 1.3 and 2.7 accuracy points, respectively. Compute was approximated from Pareto fronts. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| The extracted contrasts offered no clear evidence of a universal capability threshold. | An exploratory segmented regression on log odds ratios across 235 configurations in four studies found no clear break at 45% single-agent accuracy. | §5.6, Appendix E, Table 13 |

The evidence supports a negative average accuracy difference against the evaluated stronger baselines. The paired sampling gap measures how much stronger the sampled baseline was because the multi-agent score cancels. A causal estimate of communication would require tighter controls within studies, and Pareto dominance would require checking resource use alongside accuracy for each contrast (§§3.3, 5.3–5.5). The redundancy proposition bounds information gain under its assumptions. Accuracy for compute-limited models remains an empirical question (§3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** The corpus was assembled without a systematic search, and the analysis was conducted without preregistration. One study contributed 180 of 516 configurations. Matching conditions are partly confounded with study, and the exploratory model relies on few clusters and assumed outcome correlations. Some cells contain only 20 items, and the choice of primary architectures creates an analytical degree of freedom (§§5.4, 5.7, Open Science and Audit Trail).
- **Author-reported.** The Jwalapuram et al. baseline rows use the best observed scores across system entries, which favors the single-agent side of those contrasts. Latency was uncontrolled throughout the corpus, and the proposed failure mechanisms were left individually untested (§5.2, Figures 4 and 10).
- **Analyst inference.** The larger negative mean for sampled comparisons than for budget-matched comparisons cannot establish that sample matching produces a larger penalty. The categories draw on different studies and control different resources (Tables 1–2, §5.5).

## Open research questions

1. **When does split evidence make communication worthwhile?** The hidden-profile generator validates the construction of the items, while model performance on them remains open. Compare debate, independent voting, and a single agent given pooled evidence under a common total-token budget, with both shared and split evidence conditions (§3.4, Appendix D).
2. **Do external verifiers improve debate specifically?** The SMFR result bundles specialized tooling with architecture. Give single-agent and debate systems the same verifier, vary whether verification is available, and measure accuracy, resource use, and correct-to-incorrect answer changes (§5.2, Appendix C.2).
3. **Does the positive MMLU-Pro result generalize under exact parity?** It comes from two configurations on one backbone at approximate compute parity. Repeat the frontier comparison across models and benchmarks with enforced token budgets and paired item outcomes (§5.2, Table 1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The descriptive findings are traceable to the supplied tables and sensitivity analyses. Generalizing beyond the selected studies remains uncertain. The supplied text lacks paired item-level outcomes for estimating correlations, per-entry Jwalapuram baseline scores, a verified AGIEval item count for Zhang et al., and complete per-contrast resource measurements for testing Pareto dominance (§§5.2–5.4, Appendix C.1).
