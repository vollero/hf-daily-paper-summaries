# LynnReal-Hand: Unlocking World Interaction with Hand Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21479) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21479)


## One-sentence takeaway

LynnReal-Hand uses hand-localized training and a compressed video decoder to achieve 92.49 MANO Dice on in-domain HandBench with three denoising steps, while its optimized 540p deployment streams at a reported 18.03 FPS on one H200 (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Text and camera controls let users direct what they see, but provide limited control over how they grasp, move, or release objects. The authors identify three obstacles to hand control in video world models. Hands occupy too few pixels to receive strong supervision from global losses, causal generators can compound errors from their own prior frames, and high-resolution decoding can remain slow even after denoising is accelerated (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Aligned data and evaluation.** The authors curate 100K real and synthetic egocentric interaction clips with synchronized captions, camera poses, and rasterized MANO hand trajectories. They introduce HandBench with 200 sequences across ten sources, including H2O and TACO held out from training (§5, Appendix B).
- **Interaction-Preserving Training.** A bidirectional latent video model adds hand-localized latent, LPIPS, and DINOv2 losses to global flow matching. During causal self-rollout distillation, a hand-conditioned video teacher guides the full scene at higher noise levels, while gradients from a hand-blind image teacher are restricted to regions outside the hand mask (§§3.2, 3.4).
- **Few-step streaming.** The authors adapt the bidirectional model with AnyFlow and LoRA, first using clean history and then training on the student’s own generated history. Three flow-map updates per chunk, a two-chunk attention window, and a local temporal position reset bound the attention cache and positional range (§3.3, Appendix C.4).
- **Ultra-Fast-VAE.** The decoder keeps the native H3 latent interface and compresses attention and feed-forward branches. Ridge refitting, operator distillation, and hand-weighted temporal losses recover detail and reduce chunk-boundary flicker in cache-free decoding (§4, Appendix C.5).

The benchmark and ablations support hand-following improvements under prescribed controls. MANO Dice measures projected silhouette overlap on frames with reference hands, leaving physical contact and object-state correctness outside the metric (Appendix A.1). The ablations support the tested training changes on their respective subsets. The streaming figures characterize an optimized deployment with a different precision configuration from the Fast quality evaluation (Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** The two-chunk window lacks explicit long-term 3D memory, which may impair object permanence after objects leave view. The authors identify physical contact priors for closed-loop dexterous robot learning as future work (§7).
- **Analyst inference — comparison scope.** Base, Fast, and streaming Hand2World differ in resolution and inference configuration, and baselines differ in access to corpus fine-tuning. The reported speed and quality differences therefore combine several factors. The text also leaves the relationship between Fast’s reported chunk latency and throughput unexplained (Tables 1–2, Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference — evaluation scope.** Out-of-distribution testing covers two held-out sources, and the Stage III ablation selects clips with hands visible throughout. These settings leave robustness to extended occlusion and other unseen sources uncertain. Dice excludes frames without reference hands, so it cannot quantify spurious hands on those frames (§5.2, Table 6, Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does explicit spatial memory improve object permanence during streaming?** The paper describes bounded attention and identifies memory as a limitation, but reports no dedicated re-entry test. Compare the two-chunk model with a memory-augmented variant on controlled object exit and re-entry sequences, measuring identity, geometry, and latency (§7, Appendix C.4).
2. **Does hand alignment translate to correct contact and object motion?** The reported Dice metric measures silhouette overlap, and the contact evidence is qualitative. Evaluate both variants on held-out clips with annotated contact events and object poses, measuring contact timing and object trajectory error alongside Dice (Figs. 6, 9–13, Appendix A.1).
3. **Which deployment choices account for Fast’s quality and speed trade-offs?** The reported quality and runtime configurations differ, and the streaming baseline uses a different resolution and chunk size. Vary resolution, decoder, and precision individually on the same H200 and clips, recording first-chunk latency, steady-state FPS, FVD, Dice, and Cam-BG (Tables 1–3, Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and tabulated results.
