# Correcting Persistent Failures in Point-Cloud Grasping Policies using Human Demonstrations in Virtual Reality

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18919) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18919)


## One-sentence takeaway

In simulation, retraining with verified VR grasp contacts raised lift success from 24.0% to 45.2% on demonstrated persistent failures and from 23.2% to 33.6% on hidden hard cases, with no statistically significant change on held-out object families (§§V-B, V-E).

## Problem and motivation

Offline grasp generators rank candidate grasps using learned scores that can diverge from actual lift success. Reinforcement learning (RL) can improve average lift success while leaving particular object–pose combinations persistently unsolved. The authors identify two practical gaps: deciding which failures merit scarce human demonstration time and extracting guidance that transfers from a human hand to a parallel-jaw gripper (§§I–II).

## Approach and contributions

- **Failure mining and contact verification.** A point-cloud policy starts from a Contact-GraspNet behavior-cloning warm start and trains with one-step PPO. Repeated failures are replayed and screened across final RL policies before being sent to VR. A person supplies two fingertip contacts, and the simulator checks whether the robot gripper can lift with those contacts under execution noise (§§III-A–E, Fig. 1).
- **Success-gated imitation.** Retraining starts from the same warm start and mixes policy rollouts with targeted replay and demonstration executions. Executed demonstrations train the critic, and the actor imitates the two contacts only when the demonstration lifts the object. Imitation fades to zero, and the approach angle remains a policy decision (§III-F).
- **Evaluation of retention and transfer.** The authors compare against the warm start and RL alone, score demonstrated and hidden hard cases separately, and test both conditions on held-out object families (§§IV-C–D, V-A–E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| RL improved held-out lift success | On 24 held-out YCB families, flat and tilted success was 83.1% and 80.2% for RL alone, 58.6% and 62.7% for the warm start, and 55.1% and 50.3% for top-ranked Contact-GraspNet. Reach filtering raised Contact-GraspNet’s flat score to 64.3%. | §V-A, Table II |
| Retraining improved demonstrated cases | At iteration 150, lift success on 59 scored demonstrated cases was 45.2% with RL + demos, 24.0% with RL alone, and 37.3% with the warm start. The authors report that the difference from the warm start was statistically nonsignificant. | §§V-B–C, Tables II–III, Fig. 7 |
| Retraining recovered hidden-case performance | At iteration 150, lift success on 71 hidden cases was 33.6% with RL + demos, 23.2% with the RL seeds used to select cases, 26.1% with a fresh RL-alone seed, and 33.1% with the warm start. | §§V-B–C, V-J, Tables II–III |
| Held-out performance was similar across RL conditions | Flat and tilted lift success was 85.2% and 80.9% with RL + demos versus 83.1% and 80.2% with RL alone. The per-object differences were statistically nonsignificant. | §V-E, Table III |
| The demonstrator found robust contacts efficiently | Across 82 cases assigned to demonstration, one author obtained robust contacts for 58 cases in 113 attempts over 32 minutes. Random search solved 54 cases at 32 attempts per case, with a paired comparison of \(p=0.57\). Two random attempts per case solved 26–28 cases across seeds. | §§IV-C, V-H, Table V |
| Sorting and learning-speed tests provided limited evidence for added benefit | The simulated arm-sorting comparison used one seed per RL condition and found no significant difference between them in any scene. Held-out performance was initially lower with demos and similar by the next checkpoint. | §§V-F–G, Table IV, Fig. 6 |

The floating-hand results support the combined retraining recipe on selected failures. Hidden-case performance returned to approximately the warm-start level, and almost all hidden cases used a demonstrated object or another instance from a demonstrated family. The comparison leaves the individual effects of targeted replay and human supervision unresolved. The held-out results establish no detectable change under this protocol, and physical-robot performance remains untested (§§IV-D, V-B–C, V-E, V-J).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported selection and confounding.** The original RL policies helped define the hard set, favoring the other conditions in comparisons against those policies. A fresh RL seed narrows the hidden-case gap. Targeted replay, contact supervision, and imitation changed together, leaving their individual effects unresolved (§V-J).
- **Author-reported statistical and baseline limits.** Case-level tests do not capture between-run variation, there were three seeds per RL condition, and reported tests were uncorrected for multiple comparisons. Contact-GraspNet scores also shifted with the viewpoint used to regenerate labels (§§V-I–J).
- **Author-reported scope limits.** One author supplied a single demonstration round. The study used simulated contacts, uniform object mass and friction, and broad point-cloud coverage. The simulated arm experiment used fused depth views and simulator-provided object segmentation (§§IV-C, V-F, V-J).
- **Analyst inference.** The demonstrated-case score excludes cases the demonstrator marked impossible, while the hidden-case score includes all assigned cases. Comparing the gain sizes across splits therefore mixes a difference in demonstration exposure with a difference in which cases were scored (§IV-C).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gain comes from human contacts?** Replay and supervision changed together. Compare ordinary PPO, targeted replay alone, and matched human-contact and machine-contact supervision using the same cases, verification rule, and training budget (§V-J).
2. **Do corrections transfer to hard cases in entirely undemonstrated families?** The hidden split contains only two such cases. Mine failures with independent RL seeds and reserve whole object families from demonstration collection (§§V-B, V-J).
3. **Does the benefit survive realistic sensing and contact dynamics?** Training used broad surface coverage and all evaluations were simulated. Compare single-view and fused-view inputs on a physical Franka arm across objects with varied mass and friction (§§V-F, V-J).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The prose provides the main lift-success comparisons and evaluation protocol, but the supplied rendering omits table bodies, including the full training settings in Table I and the exact bootstrap intervals in Table III. The text reports code availability but does not establish release of the mined cases, demonstration records, or trained checkpoints. Matched component ablations, independent demonstrators, and physical-robot results are unavailable (§I, §§IV-B, V-B, V-J).
