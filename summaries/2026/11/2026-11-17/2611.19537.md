# Continuation-Guided LLM Evolution of Program Policies and Goal Compilers in Craftax

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19537) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19537)


## One-sentence takeaway

In Craftax, a continuation-guided feedback cascade raised final-floor arrival from 0.29% to 50.49% along one policy lineage, and five frozen goal compilers trained on short tasks averaged 67.23% success on unseen seven- to eight-stage tasks without deployment-time LLM calls (§§3.1, 3.3).

## Problem and motivation

Whole-episode feedback gives coding agents little information about late-game failures when the policy rarely reaches those states. Local milestones provide more frequent feedback, but an edit can improve immediate progress while leaving the agent with less health, equipment, or time for the rest of the episode. The paper also addresses the cost of starting a new synthesis run whenever the objective changes, even when the existing policy already has useful skills and world knowledge (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Continuation-guided policy search.** Coding agents propose edits to an executable policy. The harness progresses from episode outcomes and floor pass rates to paired rollouts from saved floor and goal-entry states, plus simulator audits of hidden-state predictions. Decision edits are checked under single invocation, repeated use, and fresh resets. The paper audits where local scores diverge from endpoint gains (§§2.2–2.3, 3.1, Appendix D.2).
- **Policy factorization with behavioral checks.** The workflow extracts strategic rules into an editable program over a fixed Craftax interpreter that supplies memory, models, and skills. The interpreted and specialized builds are checked against the reference action and skill label at every tested decision (§§2.4, 3.2).
- **Reusable goal compilation.** Coding agents search for a Python emitter that translates structured tasks into control rules. A fixed parser, linker, reserve filter, and independent monitor supply task semantics and enforcement. The selected emitters compile unseen goals without task-specific rollouts or LLM calls (§§2.5, 3.3).

The held-out results demonstrate transfer within the supplied task grammar and behavioral parity on the tested worlds. The matched-budget policy controls provide some evidence for the harness design, while the single adaptive lineage leaves each regime’s independent effect unresolved. The Shared–Per-task comparison is descriptive, with no declared equivalence margin (Appendix B.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Feedback changes are confounded with ancestry in one human-directed development history, and each matched-budget policy control is a single search. The interpreter embeds Craftax-specific knowledge, and parity is empirical. Goal transfer uses one engine and a supplied grammar, with one direct search per baseline task (§3.4, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference.** The transfer study measures how well the emitter adapts inherited skills under fixed progress and reserve semantics. Transfer to deeper floors, diamond goals, or a different environment remains untested (§3.3, Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The main experiment leaves the independent contribution of belief accuracy unresolved because the projectile-model repair and safety filter were evaluated together (Appendix D.2).
- **Text ambiguity.** §2.5 describes active reserves as added coordinatewise, while Appendix C.1 describes a coordinatewise maximum. The supplied text leaves the rule for successive maintenance stages on the same resource ambiguous.

## Open research questions

1. **Which feedback regimes independently improve policy search?** The single adaptive lineage confounds regime and ancestry. Run replicated, matched-budget searches from common checkpoints, varying one feedback regime at a time.
2. **How far does compiler transfer extend beyond the tested goal distribution?** The parser accepts deeper floors and diamond goals, while the test generator excludes both. Evaluate the same frozen emitters and controls on held-out tasks that vary floor depth and resource type separately.
3. **How sensitive is the Shared–Per-task comparison to search variability?** Each task received one direct search. Repeat direct search with independent contexts per task and prespecify an equivalence margin if equivalence is the target claim.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the headline rates and reported evaluation settings. The supplied text omits the primary Shared–Guards bootstrap interval bounds and several referenced table bodies, limiting checks of detailed cohort and checkpoint comparisons. It also leaves the reserve aggregation rule ambiguous and provides no replicated full policy lineages or within-task replications of direct search.
