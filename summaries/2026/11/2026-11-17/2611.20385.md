# DominoBench: Evaluating Multi-Step Physical Reasoning from Still Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20385) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20385)


## One-sentence takeaway

On DominoBench-1K, four Claude models identified the person whose simulated fate flips with 57–61% unaided accuracy from a still image and 82–86% when given the deciding calculation and its inputs.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether vision-language models can predict how a multi-step physical chain reaction unfolds from a single initial image. A plausible description can miss a small change that reverses the outcome, a distinction that matters when predictions inform physical actions. The authors identify a gap in prior evaluation: few benchmarks combine static images, multi-mechanism dynamics, and controlled edits that cross a quantitative tipping point. (§§1–2)

## Approach and contributions

- **Simulator-verified minimal pairs.** The generator builds settled 2D rigid-body scenes with three to five mechanisms. It varies one placement parameter by one to three grid steps and retains pairs where only one person’s fate flips and both outcomes pass simulation stability and solvability checks. DominoBench-1K contains 854 items in 427 pairs. (§3)
- **Diagnostic evaluation.** Four cumulative hint levels run from H0, with no added hint, through a generic nudge and a pointer to the deciding step, to H3, which supplies the calculation and its inputs. A separate text-only mode supplies exact scene geometry. (§§3–4, Appendix I)
- **Scoring and release.** DominoScore combines chance-corrected deciding-person accuracy across mechanisms using a weighted geometric mean with frozen weights. The paper reports releasing three benchmark sets, the scene generator, seeds, trajectories, and the evaluation harness. (§§1, 3–4)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Unaided accuracy drops on the outcome that flips | On DominoBench-1K in image mode at H0, four Claude models achieved 39–47% exact, 57–61% deciding-person, and 15–16% both-twins accuracy. The naive chain-reaction and everyone-survives baselines scored 14% and 25% exact, respectively, and both scored 50% on the deciding person and 0% on both twins. | §5, Table 1 |
| Supplying the calculation improves paired predictions | For the same four Claude models on DominoBench-1K in image mode, H3 raised deciding-person accuracy to 82–86% and both-twins accuracy to 34–49%. | §5, Table 1 |
| Cross-provider results show a similar pattern | On DominoBench-124 in image mode, GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra went from 72% and 70% deciding-person accuracy at H0 to 82% and 83% at H3. The Gemini models went from 52–71% to 87–92%. | §5, Table 1 |
| A pointer can help, and exact geometry gives modest unaided gains | For three Claude 5.5 models on DominoBench-124, H0 deciding-person accuracy was 57–59% from images and 60–64% from text, with gains of at most 6 percentage points. In image mode, Sonnet 5.5 rose from 58% at H0 to 73% at H2. | §5, Figure 3 |
| Gains vary among open-weight models | On DominoBench-1K in image mode, five larger open-weight models improved from 50–53% deciding-person accuracy at H0 to 76–79% at H3. The three small open-weight models tested there gained little or nothing. | §5, Table 1 |

The authors interpret the gains as evidence that the main bottleneck is deriving the deciding quantity. The results establish that supplying the calculation **and** its inputs improves predictions for several tested models. Because H3 bundles the setup with quantities such as simulator-measured speeds, the gains cannot identify how much error comes from estimating those inputs, choosing the calculation, or carrying it out.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** Scenes use a single 2D line-art style and categorical contact labels. The study measures neither human accuracy nor models’ unaided estimates of intermediate quantities. Model coverage varies by split, and the reported Claude image/text comparison uses DominoBench-124. (§§4, 6)
- **Analyst inference.** Retaining only pairs where the idealized calculation agrees with simulation makes H3 a controlled test of calculation use, while limiting conclusions about scenes where that idealization breaks down. (§3, Appendix E)
- **Analyst inference.** Scores combine outcome errors with failures to produce a valid answer. The paper reports substantial unfinished-response rates for some open-weight models, so their low scores cannot be attributed to physical reasoning alone. (§§4–5, Appendix I)

## Open research questions

1. **Which part of H3 drives the improvement?** The paper supplies the formula and inputs together. On the same pairs, compare formula-only, intermediate-values-only, and combined hints.
2. **How accurately do models estimate intermediate quantities unaided?** The study scores final fates without measuring those estimates. Elicit the deciding mechanism and arrival speed at a specified point, then compare the estimates with simulator logs alongside outcome accuracy.
3. **How much of the difficulty do humans share?** The paper reports no human baseline. Give human participants the same stills and rules, show each twin independently, and score deciding-person and both-twins accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the benchmark design and the aggregate comparisons reported in the prose. The supplied text includes table captions but omits the numerical cells of the main and supplementary leaderboards, leaving many per-model accuracies, intervals, and rung-by-rung comparisons unavailable. Human performance and unaided intermediate-state accuracy were unmeasured, and the reported hint comparisons leave the separate effects of formulas and supplied inputs unresolved.
