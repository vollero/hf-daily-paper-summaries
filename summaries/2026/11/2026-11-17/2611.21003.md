# K2-Horizon: A Frontier Fleet of Open Source Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21003) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21003)


## One-sentence takeaway

K2-Horizon releases six language models with training artifacts across their development lifecycle, and its sparse 36B-A4B model trails a dense model trained on the same corpus by 1.2–3.7 points across four base-model benchmarks while activating roughly one-seventh as many backbone parameters per token (§1, §2.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that capable open-weight models rarely come with the data, intermediate checkpoints, and development decisions needed to reproduce training or study where capabilities emerge. K2-Horizon addresses that gap across six model sizes, with an emphasis on reasoning, coding, and tool use from memory-constrained devices to multi-GPU servers (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Architecture.** The authors introduce Grouped RMSNorm to bound normalized activations within feature groups. The 36B-A4B model adds Mixture-of-Value Attention (MoVA), which routes each token through a subset of nonlinear value projections and caches their combined output. It also uses a softplus attention gate that can attenuate or amplify attention outputs (§§2.1–2.3, Figure 2).
- **Pre-training and data.** Four models share a 22T-token corpus and a common recipe with model-specific learning rates. The authors test whether normalized loss curves align at production scale, report producing about 10T synthetic pre-training tokens, and introduce agent trajectories at the start of context extension (§§1–1.1, Table 2, Figure 4).
- **Post-training.** The flagship trains separate reinforcement-learning experts for search, knowledge work, tool use, scientific reasoning, and instruction following with abstention. It merges their weights with ISO-RAM and applies targeted supervised fine-tuning (§1.1, Released Artifacts).
- **Inference and release.** Uno uses distilled diffusion adapters for decoding without a separate draft model. The release inventory lists final weights, stage checkpoints, logs, pre-training datasets, data pipelines, and the xLLM, RL360, and Eval360 codebases. It lists Uno adapters for the 7B and 0.9B models (§1, Released Artifacts).

The same-corpus comparison supports a model-level trade-off between benchmark scores and active parameter count. Its practical latency and cost implications remain unmeasured in the supplied comparison, and the models differ in architecture and peak learning rate. Figure 4 shows similarity in the shape of the observed loss curves, with endpoint alignment imposed by normalization. The flagship rankings apply to the named benchmarks and comparators. The excerpt provides too little protocol detail to assess broader transfer or the robustness of those rankings.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Expert-level scientific reasoning remains a flagship weakness on HLE and CritPt (§1). Long-horizon training made smaller models prone to repetitive loops, and one attempted math RL schedule caused entropy collapse for the 0.9B model (§1.1).
- **Author-stated.** The MoE learning-rate sweep varied batch size and training horizon across expert counts, limiting what can be attributed to sparsity. The flagship loss monitor also required manual intervention when failures recurred after resuming from the same checkpoint (§§3.1.1, 3.2.1, 3.3.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The supplied comparison cannot isolate MoVA from softplus gating or the other architectural and optimizer differences. The excerpt also gives no matched control for estimating the benefit of the synthetic data or of introducing agent trajectories during mid-training (§§1.1, 2.2–2.3, Tables 1–2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much do MoVA and softplus gating each contribute?** The sparse model uses both. Train a factorial set of sparse variants on identical batches with matched optimization, then compare pre-training loss, downstream scores, and decoding cost.
2. **Does introducing agent trajectories during context extension improve later tool use?** The excerpt describes the schedule without a timing control. Hold total trajectory exposure and training budget fixed, vary when the trajectories are introduced, and compare held-out agent tasks.
3. **Where does Uno deliver its largest speedup?** The excerpt omits the conditions behind the reported maximum. Compare adapter-enabled and standard decoding for the same checkpoints across hardware, batch sizes, and output lengths, measuring throughput and checking the claimed losslessness.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied source was truncated in the middle. It contains the architecture, release inventory, and much of the training analysis, but omits the full benchmark tables and protocols, the data mixture and decontamination details, the reward-hacking and safety results, the math research review evaluation, and the detailed Uno experiments. The artifact inventory records the authors’ release claims. Artifact access and end-to-end reproduction remain unverified.
