# When Expert Disagreement Hurts: Prestige and Selectivity in LLM Decision Revision

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16694) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16694)


## One-sentence takeaway

With task evidence fixed, expert-labeled disagreement reduced accuracy by 1.9–16.1 percentage points from the initial answer across all five main-benchmark models, while the incremental effect of the expert label relative to anonymous disagreement varied by model (Figures 2–4, Table 32).

## Problem and motivation

When an LLM reconsiders an evidence-grounded decision after another assistant disagrees, a reversal can either correct an error or discard a correct answer. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that count source following or report final accuracy without separating ordinary reconsideration, the effect of dissent, and the incremental influence of a source’s claimed expertise (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Paired two-pass audit.** Each model answers a Yes/No question from an excerpt. The same question, excerpt, and initial answer feed three revision branches: neutral reflection, anonymous opposition, and expert-labeled opposition. The two opposing sources give the same answer (§§4–5, Figure 1).
- **Accuracy accounting.** The audit estimates the expert label’s effects on reversal and correctness separately. If initial accuracy is \(a\), and the reversal rates for initially wrong and correct answers are \(b\) and \(h\), revision changes accuracy by \((1-a)b-ah\). With \(h>0\) and \(0<a<1\), accuracy improves only when \(b/h>a/(1-a)\) (§3.2).
- **Controls and reusable evaluation materials.** The authors test identical rationales without filler, asserted reliability, agreement, and a prompt-level evidence gate. They also check sensitivity to incomplete excerpts and document clustering, and describe an offline reproduction package (§6, Appendices F, G.2, H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Expert-labeled dissent reduced accuracy across the main five models. | On the 1,989-item benchmark, valid expert-branch accuracy fell 1.9–16.1 percentage points (pp) from Pass 1. All five models reversed initial errors more often than correct answers, yet all fell below their own break-even ratios. Claude-Opus-4.8 cleared the threshold and gained 0.4 pp on a separate 250-item pool with a different prompt protocol. | §6.1, Figures 2–3, Table 6 |
| The incremental label effect differed across models. | In the padded main audit, expert minus anonymous changed reversal and accuracy by +6.90 and −5.19 pp for GPT-4o-mini, and by −1.86 and +2.37 pp for Gemini-2.0-Flash. Claude-Sonnet-4 reversed 2.62 pp more often, with an accuracy change of +0.30 pp (95% CI −1.01 to +1.61). | §6.2, Figure 4, Table 32 |
| The source wording still mattered with an identical argument. | With identical rationales and no filler, the label increased reversal relative to anonymous dissent by 4.42–5.62 pp across three proprietary models, each with 249 paired items. Only GPT-4o-mini’s accuracy interval excluded zero (−4.82 pp, 95% CI −8.43 to −1.61). Four newer models had a question-clustered pooled reversal increase of 2.0 pp (95% CI 0.7 to 3.3), with no individually significant exact test. | §6.3, Tables 2 and 19 |
| Reversal tracked stated reliability. | In the padded control with the evidence and opposing answer fixed, all five tested models reversed least with a fallible expert description and most with an asserted track record. | §6.3, Tables 3 and 21 |
| Evidence gating had uneven results. | In the padded answer-only subset, the gate reduced correct-to-wrong transitions from 171 to 76 across three proprietary models. In the no-padding rationale test, only Claude-Sonnet-4 showed a statistically significant reduction toward incorrect targets, from 24.7% to 20.2% (paired exact \(p=0.013\)). | §6.5, Tables 15 and 18 |

The no-padding comparison isolates the effect of adding the expert-description sentence on decisions made with a fixed rationale. Those requests use the stored initial answer to select the opposing target and omit it as a prior assistant turn, so the evidence for an isolated wording effect comes from separate requests rather than live conversational revision. The main audit measures revision of a shared initial answer, with filler differences contributing to its prompt contrasts. The gate results come from different prompt setups, leaving the effect of adding an argument on gate effectiveness unresolved (§§4.3, 6.3, 6.5, Appendix H.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** The tasks are binary and answer-first, with fixed evidence and scripted source cues. Open-ended decisions, multi-turn exchanges, newly supplied evidence, and authority earned through observed performance remain outside the evaluation. Estimates are tied to endpoint, date, prompt, and decoding settings (§8).
- **Author-reported checks.** Excluding the 48 main-benchmark law items with incomplete excerpts left the principal contrasts essentially unchanged. Restricting the sample to medicine and science reversed the sign of Claude-Sonnet-4’s expert-minus-anonymous reversal effect. Control tests were exploratory and unadjusted for multiplicity (§§5, 6.2, Table 33).
- **Analyst inference.** Rationale validation checked the assigned answer direction, leaving argument quality as a possible source of variation. The relatively low parseable-output coverage for Gemini-3.6-Flash makes its complete-case estimates sensitive to how excluded items would have behaved. Reported bootstrap intervals capture item or document sampling uncertainty, leaving independent-run variability largely unmeasured (Appendix F, Tables 16 and 25, Appendix H).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the label effect persist when the model has just given its answer in the same conversation?** The clean wording comparison omitted the prior assistant turn. Repeat it with the initial answer replayed, varying only the source sentence (§6.3, Appendix H.1).
2. **When does experience with a source outweigh its claimed expertise?** The reliability ladder uses asserted performance. Show models calibrated records of source accuracy, vary the expertise label independently, and measure both correction and harm (§6.3, §8).
3. **Can a gate preserve corrections when dissent brings new evidence?** The study holds evidence fixed. Cross gated and ungated prompts with independently verified supporting, irrelevant, and misleading evidence, then report both conditional reversal rates (§§6.5, 8).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported paired benchmark results and the binary accuracy accounting. The supplied text does not include the raw trial records, executable artifact, numerical missingness bounds described in Appendix H, or complete per-request timestamps for all historical runs. It also provides no fresh evaluation on the repaired evidence-complete excerpts (Appendix G.2, Table 31, Appendix H).
