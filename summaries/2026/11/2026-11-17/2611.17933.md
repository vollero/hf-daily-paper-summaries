# OutfitterBench: Evaluating LLM Agents on Long-Horizon Resource Allocation and Commitment Coordination

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17933) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17933)


## One-sentence takeaway

In OutfitterBench’s simulated rental shop, GPT 6.1 Sol was the only evaluated agent to beat the fixed Calendar policy in both worlds on terminal net value creation, and even Sol accepted an overlapping booking for its sole long-runtime lantern (§§5.1–5.2).

## Problem and motivation

Accepting a reservation commits equipment for a future interval, while returns, cleaning, and uncertain walk-in demand change what the same fleet can support. An agent must keep existing commitments feasible and judge whether a new rental, booking revision, or fleet purchase is worth the capacity it displaces. The authors argue that continuing-business benchmarks give less attention to these two demands jointly (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Rental simulator.** OutfitterBench couples advance reservations and uncertain walk-ins through a shared fleet, a capacity-limited cleaning queue, and a fixed equipment-capital ceiling. Agents make decisions for 200 days, followed by 14 settlement days. The score measures terminal cash plus eligible resale value less outstanding liabilities (§3, Fig. 2, §4.3).
2. **Matched-world evaluation.** Seven LLM configurations use the same business interface and are compared with Calendar, a fixed policy that screens bookings for projected capacity and positive direct contribution using public information (§§4.1–4.2).
3. **Decision diagnostics.** The authors combine state-based measures computed without an LLM judge, trajectory analysis, matched booking comparisons, and local walk-in replays to examine feasibility and economic trade-offs (§4.4, Apps. D–E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sol alone beat Calendar in both worlds | All 14 LLM runs finished, and 10 created positive net value. Mean day-214 net value creation across two equally weighted seeds was +2,556.78 CNY for Sol and about +1,120 CNY for Calendar. Calendar beat four configurations in both worlds. Luna and MiMo each beat it in one world. | §5.1, Fig. 1, App. C.1, Table 5 |
| Projected shortages and new conflicts distinguished two groups | Across the two seeds, Sol, Opus, and Luna had projected shortages on roughly 6–9% of pending booking-days, versus 21–34% for DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, and MiMo. Relevant successful confirmation calls created a new projected gap for an existing booking roughly 2–5% versus 14–20% of the time, respectively. | §5.2, Fig. 4A–B, Apps. D.2–D.3 |
| Comparable fulfillment could yield different returns | With comparable capital occupancy, mean net service contribution was 1.38 CNY per 100 capital-CNY-days for Sol versus 0.96–0.97 for Opus and Luna. Opus charged less in 75 of 77 fully delivered booking pairs matched to Sol on seed, request, dates, equipment, quantities, and service. Its total agreed rent on those pairs was 30–37% lower than Sol’s. | §5.3, Fig. 4C, App. E.2.1, Table 12 |
| Some walk-in limits left room for additional rentals | With a three-day turnaround allowance, a small relaxation of one product’s limit yielded positive estimated contribution without a new projected booking gap on 31.25% of sampled dates for Luna versus 12.50% for Sol. Rates average two runs per agent, with 40 fixed dates per run and each replay holding that day’s stock, prices, commitments, and demand fixed. | §5.3, Fig. 4D, App. D.5, Table 10 |

The runs establish the observed ranking in these worlds and show that higher booking volume or reliable fulfillment can coexist with lower business value. The diagnostics describe projected coverage and realized decisions, so their individual effects on terminal value remain unmeasured. The matched bookings cover completed transactions, and the walk-in replays estimate same-day contribution under existing configurations. Seasonal profit from changing those limits remains unknown (§§5.1–5.3, §7).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Customer behavior, operating events, and costs follow simulation rules, and transfer to other markets or real operations is untested. Two world seeds with one run per model–seed pair leave ranking stability uncertain. The fixed business tools and fresh daily sessions limit conclusions about other agent setups (§7).
- **Analyst inference.** The runs compare model configurations at their assigned reasoning settings. They leave the effect of reasoning effort unresolved (§4.1). The reported experiments and public release use different weather inputs, so exact reproduction of the reported world trajectories from the public release alone is uncertain (App. B.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How stable are the rankings across worlds and repeat runs?** The study has one run per model–seed pair. Repeat each configuration on the original seeds and additional seeds under matched budgets, then report variation in terminal value and rank (§4.1, §7).
2. **Can a capacity check reduce booking conflicts while preserving valuable admissions?** The paper records conflicts but does not test a preventive intervention. Compare the same agents with and without a pre-commitment coverage check, measuring new gaps, fulfillment, and terminal value (§5.2, §6).
3. **Do narrower walk-in restrictions improve seasonal value?** The local replays stop after the sampled day’s walk-ins. Run full seasons with the same policy under its original limits and under interval-specific limits, then compare terminal value and merchant-caused failures (App. D.5, §7).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported within-benchmark comparisons and **low** for ranking stability beyond the evaluated runs. The supplied text includes per-seed terminal outcomes in Table 5, but several other tables appear only as captions, including the full model usage, financial decomposition, detailed gap rates, capital productivity, and matched-rent totals. Exact scenario parameters for customer budgets and preferences and results using the public-release weather input are also unavailable in the supplied text (§3.4, Apps. B–E).
