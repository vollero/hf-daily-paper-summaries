# SWE-CoWork: Benchmarking Coding Agents as Digital Employees in Organizations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20218) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20218)


## One-sentence takeaway

On SWE-CoWork’s 101 simulated workplace assignments, the two leading coding agents passed about 63% of new-behavior tests but completed fewer than 8% of assignments, and supplying complete requirements upfront improved partial delivery without raising either tested model’s completion rate (§6.1–6.2, Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation

Software engineers often start from a brief handoff and must decide which requirements apply by consulting owners, checking record versions, and reconciling conflicting evidence. The authors argue that benchmarks built around pre-scoped issues or a single simulated stakeholder leave much of that work unmeasured. SWE-CoWork tests whether agents can recover requirements and carry them through implementation, verification, and handoff (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Organizational benchmark.** The authors construct 101 assignments across 101 real repositories, 11 programming languages, and 12 application domains. Each assignment combines a brief handoff, authored workplace records, and 26 rule-governed, LLM-backed colleagues. Scored requirements link discovery routes and accountable owners to private executable checks (§3–4).
- **Paired evaluation.** CoWork requires agents to recover requirements from the workplace. Oracle-Spec supplies the complete requirements upfront and removes access to colleagues while holding the tasks, repository states, test contracts, scaffold, and execution budget fixed. The paper reports CoWork results for eleven models and the paired comparison for two (§5.2, §6.1–6.2).
- **Requirement-level diagnostics.** Disclosure logs, trajectories, test outcomes, and handoff claims are linked to identify where requirements are lost. Detailed diagnostics cover Claude-Opus-5.5 (§6.3, Appendix H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Partial delivery was much more common than complete delivery | Across 101 CoWork tasks, Claude-Opus-5.5 and GPT-6 Astra scored 63.84% and 62.91% pooled F2P and 6.93% and 7.92% complete-task pass, respectively. The other nine models reached at most 39.09% F2P and 0.99% complete-task pass. Pooled P2P ranged from 81.85% to 95.20% across all eleven models. | §6.1, Table 2 |
| Complete specifications improved F2P, with mixed regression outcomes | On the same 101 tasks, CoWork → Oracle-Spec raised Seed Evolving’s pooled F2P from 39.09% to 55.76% while P2P fell from 94.48% to 89.50%. GPT-6 Astra’s F2P rose from 62.91% to 78.84% and P2P from 89.15% to 91.81%. Complete-task pass rates stayed at 0.99% and 7.92%, respectively. | §6.2, Table 3 |
| Colleague disclosure tracked delivery across models | Across eleven CoWork models, the share of colleague-only requirements disclosed had a Spearman correlation of 0.94 with pooled F2P. The corresponding correlation for median tool calls per run was 0.08. | §6.1, Figure 5 |
| Requirements were lost during and after clarification | Across 101 Claude-Opus-5.5 CoWork trajectories, a mean of 93% of first questions about queried cards went to the accountable owner, and the agent obtained 64% of 2,176 colleague-held requirements. Of 450 initially deferred owner–card threads, 153 received no follow-up. Of failed F2P nodes with linked requirements, 56% concerned requirements that had reached the agent. | §6.3.1–6.3.2, Figures 7–8, Table 7 |
| Handoff claims overstated verified completion | In the same Claude-Opus-5.5 trajectories, 255 of 826 requirements reported complete at handoff failed their corresponding tests (31%). | §6.3.4, Figure 8a |

F2P and P2P pool test nodes, so tasks with more nodes contribute more weight. Complete-task pass requires every F2P and P2P node to pass in the same run (§5.1). The paired comparison measures the combined effect of receiving a complete specification and bypassing workplace clarification for two models. It leaves their individual effects unresolved. The cross-model associations are descriptive, and the trajectory findings document observed failure stages within one model’s runs.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported scope.** The paper notes that simulated interactions can differ systematically from human interactions. The evaluation uses one colleague model with controlled disclosure rules, tests Oracle-Spec on two models, and traces detailed failures for one model. The authors describe the reviewer-based behavior correlations as exploratory (§2, §5.2, §6.3.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Because GPT-6 Astra uses Codex and the other models use Claude Code, cross-model rankings cannot isolate model effects from scaffold effects (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Complete-task pass measures the private executable contract. Appendix F.1 audits test-edit restrictions separately, and §6.3.4 assesses handoff accuracy separately, so the main pass rate alone cannot establish compliance or reporting quality (§5.1, §6.3.4, Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Some diagnoses depend on an LLM reviewer and card mentions in recorded messages. The supplied text gives the classification rules but provides no human agreement measurements (Appendix H).

## Open research questions

1. **Does explicit requirement tracking improve complete delivery?** The authors propose tracking owners, open questions, implementation status, and verification evidence but do not test it. Compare the same model and scaffold with and without a persistent requirement record under matched budgets, measuring full-task pass, deferred-thread follow-up, and handoff accuracy (§6.4).
2. **How much of the Oracle-Spec gain comes from earlier information versus saved work?** The paired condition changes both together. Compare ordinary CoWork, CoWork with an upfront specification and retained colleague access, and CoWork with staged automatic disclosures under the same budget (§5.2, §6.2).
3. **Do the failure patterns transfer to human collaboration?** The evaluation uses one simulator configuration. Repeat a matched subset with scripted simulated colleagues and human role-players given the same records and knowledge boundaries, then compare disclosure, follow-up, and verified delivery (§3.1, §5.2).
