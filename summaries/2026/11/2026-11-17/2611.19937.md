# AllocFlow: End-to-End Portfolio Trading Action Generation from Raw Market Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19937) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19937)


## One-sentence takeaway

AllocFlow generates joint long–short weights from hourly market bars, and group-relative reinforcement learning raised its Calmar ratio from 0.955 after imitation to 4.043 in the authors’ after-fee Binance perpetual-futures backtest (Table 1, §§1 and 4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation

Portfolio managers must choose positions jointly because sizing, overlapping exposures, and rebalancing costs determine whether a predictive signal has economic value. The authors argue that training forecasts or alpha signals separately from allocation can misalign learning with portfolio outcomes. Direct policy learning faces a related challenge: historical data supply noisy returns and no unique optimal allocation, so exploration from scratch must discover workable combinations of directions and sizes (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Joint portfolio generation.** A frozen Kronos encoder compresses each asset’s hourly market history. A cross-asset flow Transformer conditions on those representations and generates a full vector of signed target weights. The decoder enforces dollar neutrality before rounding and allows gross exposure to vary (§3.1, Figure 2).
- **Strategy-guided refinement.** Conditional flow matching and auxiliary sizing and ranking losses initialize the head from causal strategy demonstrations, principally AgenticAlpha. Flow-GRPO then compares candidate portfolios at matched gross exposure over the same forward 24-hour return path and anchors updates to the frozen imitation policy. The contribution is the adaptation of these methods to joint portfolio generation (§§2, 3.2–3.3).
- **Controlled portfolio comparisons.** The authors compare the imitation policy with its RL continuation on the same ledger and test alternative heads, teachers, encoders, and rewards (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results

The main backtest uses hourly Binance perpetual futures from November 2, 2024 to December 31, 2025. The fixed-notional ledger applies targets after one bar and charges 5 basis points per unit of absolute traded weight (§4.2, Appendices C.1 and C.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| RL improved return and reduced drawdown | From imitation to RL on the main ledger, annualized net return rose from 27.79% to 51.38%, additive maximum drawdown fell from 29.10% to 12.81%, net daily Sharpe rose from 0.954 to 2.192, and Calmar rose from 0.955 to 4.043. Average daily turnover rose from 1.143 to 1.976. RL results are means across five training seeds. | §§1, 4.2–4.3, Table 1 |
| The refined policy led the reported portfolio comparisons | On the main backtest, Calmar was 4.043 for AllocFlow, 1.733 for its AgenticAlpha teacher, and 1.720 for the strongest listed external alpha baseline. External methods used a common signal-to-allocation pipeline. | §§4.3, 5.2, Tables 1 and 9 |
| Tested optimization and head alternatives had lower Calmar | On the main after-fee ledger, the parameter-matched diagonal Gaussian head reached at most 1.92 under the same group-relative objective. Direct differentiation of the same reward from the same imitation checkpoint reached at most 1.74. Flow-GRPO reached 4.043. | §5.1, Table 2b, §5.5, Appendix C.5 |
| Refinement improved results across demonstration strategies | After-fee Calmar improved from imitation to RL for all four tested teacher initializations. The random-start control lost 75% annualized on the same ledger and omitted the frozen imitation reference terms. | §5.2, Table 4 |
| Performance persisted on a later holdout | The same trained policy, evaluated without retraining from January through June 2026 with contract-level funding settlement, reached a Sharpe of 2.06 and a Calmar of 4.90. | §4.3, Appendix C.2, Table 12 |

The same-architecture comparison supports the value of the refinement stage for this policy and backtest. The alternative-head and optimizer results support the tested configuration within the reported settings. The later holdout extends the evidence in time within the same market. Live execution, capacity, and transfer across markets remain untested in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated boundaries.** Training ranks fixed, gross-normalized books over a forward return window. Evaluation rebalances hourly at policy-selected exposures and deducts turnover fees. The base policy assumes price-taking and receives no current holdings as input. Rounding can leave residual net exposure, and dollar neutrality alone provides no factor neutrality or drawdown bound (§§3.1, 3.3, Appendices A.2 and C.1).
- **Analyst inference on attribution.** The random-start experiment changes both initialization and reference regularization, so its failure cannot isolate which change accounts for the result. The Gaussian comparison uses diagonal covariance, leaving the benefit of flow generation relative to a correlated non-flow policy open (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference on robustness.** The evaluation covers one venue and a high-volume subset of one instrument class. The fixed-notional ledger does not test market impact or capacity. Native baseline comparisons can also differ in fills, rebalancing, and asset availability. The holdings-conditioned and execution variants are reported as single runs, so their seed robustness is unknown (§4.2, Appendices B.4 and C.1–C.3).

## Open research questions

1. **How much improvement comes from imitation versus the frozen reference?** The random-start control changes both. Run a matched-compute, multi-seed comparison of imitation with and without reference terms, alongside random initialization without reference terms (§5.2).
2. **Does the flow head outperform a non-flow policy that can sample correlated positions?** The parameter-matched Gaussian control samples with diagonal covariance. Compare against a full- or low-rank covariance policy using the same encoder, demonstrations, reward, and ledger (§5.1, Appendix B.5).
3. **How sensitive are results to execution costs and scale?** The reported ledger uses proportional fees and assumes price-taking. Replay identical targets across order sizes using spread, slippage, and market-impact estimates, and report net performance by size (§§3.3, 4.2, Appendix C.3).
