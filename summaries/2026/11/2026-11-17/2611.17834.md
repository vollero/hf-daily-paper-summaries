# PaperCritic: Training AI Reviewers to Assess the Soundness of Papers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17834) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17834)


## One-sentence takeaway

PaperCritic-4B uses a review-level F1 reward to balance well-founded, substantive criticism with reference coverage and achieves the highest reported macro F1 on the PaperSound-23K test set, although its paired confidence interval against the strongest frontier baseline includes zero (§§5–6, Tables 3 and 13).

## Problem and motivation

Growing AI conference submission volume strains the supply of qualified reviewers. The authors argue that holistic review imitation and score prediction combine several review goals and provide limited supervision about which individual criticisms are valid and consequential. They focus on whether a paper’s evidence supports its main claims (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Point-level objective.** Precision counts the fraction of generated criticisms with correct premises that raise a material concern about a main claim or its evidence. Recall counts distinct high-quality reference concerns matched at equal or greater specificity (§3, §5.2).
2. **PaperSound-23K.** The dataset contains 23,215 human-written and model-generated review points from 989 ICLR submissions. An LLM labels soundness, claim linkage, and severity, with expert agreement checked on a subset (§4, Tables 1–2).
3. **PaperCritic training.** Supervised fine-tuning interleaves high-quality points across claims. Online reinforcement learning with GRPO then optimizes review-level F1 using separate soundness and substantiveness reward models and an LLM reference matcher (§5, Figure 2).

The evidence supports improved performance under the reported grading protocols and transfer relative to the Qwen base model. PaperCritic’s in-domain F1 lead comes with lower recall than DeepSeek and Opus under GPT-5.4 grading. The transfer comparisons leave its standing against frontier models open, and the exploratory expert study leaves its effect on real peer-review decisions unmeasured (Tables 3–5, Appendix A.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The objective focuses on soundness and excludes external-citation review points. Reference recall measures coverage of known concerns, so valid new criticisms can go unmatched. Novelty, clarity, and acceptance recommendations fall outside the training objective (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** GPT-5.4 contributes to annotation, soundness reward-model distillation, and primary evaluation. This creates a risk of shared grading preferences. The alternate grader partially addresses that risk, while the expert preference check covers a small, expert-selected set (§4.1, Table 7, Appendices A.3 and E).
- **Analyst inference.** In-domain prompts, context limits, output lengths, and parsing differ across model groups, so Table 3 compares entire evaluation configurations. The bootstrap measures test-paper sampling uncertainty from single runs, leaving run-to-run variability unmeasured (Appendices D.1 and F).
- **Analyst inference.** Transfer metrics capture different behaviors. Code and legal precision require reference matches, story feedback measures error detection on corrupted inputs, and the Nature-family evaluation retains references that may require supplementary material unavailable to the model (Appendix D.2).

## Open research questions

- **How often does PaperCritic find valid concerns absent from the references?** Reference recall leaves this open. Have blinded domain experts assess unmatched points from several models and measure how many add distinct, well-founded concerns (Limitations).
- **Does the in-domain lead persist under matched inference conditions?** Prompt, input, and parsing differences leave their influence unresolved. Rerun the comparison with matched visible context, output constraints, and parsing, then repeat generation across seeds (Appendices D.1 and F).
- **Does separating soundness from reference matching improve transfer?** The paper tests reward ablations in-domain, while some transfer protocols count only reference-matched points as precise. Evaluate the same ablations on transfer benchmarks using independent expert judgments for both matched and unmatched critiques (Table 6, Appendix D.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the description of the method and reported benchmark results. The supplied text does not establish public access to the dataset, weights, or training code. It also leaves training-seed variability, frontier comparisons on the transfer benchmarks, and effects on real peer-review workflows unmeasured.
