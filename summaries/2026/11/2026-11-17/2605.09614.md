# Reflection Anchors for Propagation-Aware Visual Retention in Long-Chain Multimodal Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.09614) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09614)


## One-sentence takeaway

RAPO trains vision–language models at high-entropy reasoning steps using a contrastive visual-dependence objective, improving average accuracy across the reported benchmark suite while retaining standard autoregressive decoding (Table 1, Appendix F).

## Problem and motivation

As a vision–language model generates a long reasoning chain, later tokens can rely increasingly on the textual prefix and lose sensitivity to the original image. Final-answer rewards provide no direct signal for retaining visual evidence along the chain. The authors identify a gap in both inference-time visual reinjection and grounding-oriented reinforcement learning: neither gives an explicit rule for choosing where a local change to native decoding could influence later reasoning (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Information-theoretic formulation.** The authors define visual gain as the change in conditional mutual information between the image and the generated suffix after intervening at a single token and returning to the native decoder. Under stated assumptions, a lower bound on the optimal gain combines next-token entropy with divergence from an image-marginalized suffix reference (§3, §4.1, Appendix B).
- **Training algorithm.** Reflection-Anchor Policy Optimization (RAPO) selects the highest-entropy positions on each training rollout and applies policy-gradient updates there. Its auxiliary objective rewards branching-room-weighted KL divergence between ordinary predictions and predictions under a mask that blocks image tokens and earlier anchors, averaged over a short window. The paper reports GRPO and DAPO variants (§4.2, Figure 3, Appendix H).
- **Mechanism tests.** The authors compare anchor selectors and masking strategies and use visual perturbations to test whether selected positions affect downstream predictions and final answers (§§5.3–5.4, Appendices D–E).

The benchmark results support average accuracy gains under the reported protocols. The masking experiments show that some selected positions influence later predictions and final answers. The experiments leave the theoretical mutual-information gain unmeasured and do not quantify how much of the accuracy improvement comes from retaining task-relevant visual evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors.** Native entropy and finite-window chain-masked KL are practical proxies for the ideal selector and suffix divergence. The authors expect smaller gains when the base model already retains visual evidence well. They leave broader real-world tasks and finer-grained faithfulness tests open (§§4.1–4.2, Appendix J.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Several main configurations use a one-token training window, which directly scores only the anchor. The claim about later propagation in those configurations therefore depends on separate diagnostics. The theoretical result concerns one-step interventions with rollback and relies on score separation and, for joint selection, local-target realizability (Table 4, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference.** The older-backbone comparisons include baselines with different training histories. The main table also lacks a plain DAPO control for the older backbones, limiting attribution of RAPO-D gains there. Table 3 reports variation over sampled evaluation responses, leaving training-seed variability unquantified (§5.1, Tables 1 and 3, Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does entropy identify the positions with the largest actual downstream visual gain?** The selector ablations rank strategies by accuracy, while the oracle gain remains unmeasured. On a bounded visual QA task, enumerate a set of compatible images, estimate one-step rollback gains, and compare their ranking with native entropy (§4.1, Figure 6, Appendix E).
2. **Do beyond-anchor training terms improve faithfulness across backbones?** The window sweep centers on Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, while other main configurations use one-token windows. Compare anchor-only and multi-token objectives at matched training cost across backbones, then measure whether answers change appropriately when task-relevant image details change (Table 2(b), Table 4).
3. **How much does RAPO add to plain DAPO on the older backbones?** Table 1 reports RAPO-D without plain DAPO for those groups. Train both with matched data, hardware, and wall-clock budgets, repeat across seeds, and evaluate on the same benchmark subsets (Table 1, Appendix H).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported method and benchmark comparisons. The supplied text leaves material gaps in training-seed uncertainty, measured oracle gain, direct DAPO controls for the older backbones, and whether observed accuracy gains carry over to finer-grained visual faithfulness tests.
