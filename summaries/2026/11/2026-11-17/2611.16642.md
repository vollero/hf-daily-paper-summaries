# Dataset Distillation via Drifting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16642) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16642)


## One-sentence takeaway

Directly optimizing augmented synthetic samples to match per-class distributions in frozen feature spaces improves ImageNet-1k coverage and ResNet-18 training accuracy over the main baselines, while diffusion-based baselines retain an edge in several training settings.

## Problem and motivation

Dataset distillation learns a small synthetic dataset that can stand in for a much larger real one. The authors argue that optimizing synthetic data for a classifier can concentrate examples around features useful for its decision boundary and neglect variation within classes. They aim to preserve that variation while retaining the ability to train useful classifiers at ImageNet scale (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Direct per-class drifting.** The paper adapts an existing generative-modeling objective to optimize a fixed set of samples directly. In a frozen encoder’s feature space, a distance-weighted field attracts synthetic views toward real examples and repels them from other synthetic views, including views of the same sample. Synthesis uses no classification loss (§§2–3.2).
- **Joint optimization of augmented views.** The authors optimize several views of each sample per step so a small set can span more of the real distribution through augmentation. They use a progressively grown Laplacian pyramid as an image prior and adapt it to audio and video (§3.3, Fig. 2, §4.4).
- **Distributional evaluation alongside classifier training.** The paper applies existing coverage, nearest-neighbor, and linear-probe metrics in multiple feature spaces. It also compares distilled outputs across encoders as a way to visualize differences in their representations (§§4.2–4.3, Table B.6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Drifting improves hard-label training over the main noise-initialized baseline | At 10 images per class, a ResNet-18 trained from scratch reaches **27.0 ± 0.2%** ImageNet-1k validation top-1 accuracy with noise-initialized MoCo-v3 drifting versus **11.7 ± 0.3%** for G-VBSM, the strongest baseline in that group. Values are mean ± SD across three training seeds. | §4.1, Table 1, Appendix D |
| Coverage gains extend to encoders other than the one used for synthesis | At 10 images per class on ImageNet-1k, noise-initialized DINOv3 drifting reaches **77.0%** coverage in SigLIP2 and **83.4%** in MoCo-v3, versus best matched-initialization baseline scores of **9.7%** (SC-DD) and **9.4%** (WMDD), respectively. Coverage uses cosine fifth-neighbor neighborhoods and a crop-expanded synthetic pool. | §4.2, Table 2, Appendix D.4 |
| Diffusion remains stronger in a hard-label setting | At 50 images per class, CaO2 reaches **44.3 ± 0.1%** ImageNet-1k validation top-1 accuracy for hard-label ResNet-18 training, versus **41.1 ± 0.4%** for the strongest drifting variant, real-initialized DINOv3. Values are mean ± SD across three training seeds. | Table B.2, Appendix D |
| Training-time views matter | For noise-initialized DINOv3 on ImageNet-1k at 10 images per class, moving from one deterministic resized view to 10 augmented views per synthesis step raises SigLIP2 coverage from **1.0% to 77.0%** and hard-label ResNet-18 validation top-1 accuracy from **0.2 ± 0.0% to 21.3 ± 0.5%**. Accuracy values are mean ± SD across three training seeds. | Table A.1, Appendix D |
| Audio and video beat a herding baseline on frozen-feature tests | At five samples per class, drifting versus herding yields linear-probe and cosine 1-NN accuracies of **51.4% vs. 45.6%** and **52.6% vs. 39.9%** on FMA small, and **70.3% vs. 66.6%** and **65.8% vs. 60.4%** on HMDB51. Both tests use mean-pooled features. | §4.4, Table 3 |

The shared ImageNet-1k training recipes support direct comparisons under the reported protocol. Coverage measures how often synthetic views reach real validation neighborhoods in the chosen feature space. Fidelity of the raw samples, transfer to other student architectures, and usefulness for end-to-end audio or video training remain unestablished. The authors also report that the coverage advantage is much larger than the probe-accuracy advantage, with RDED edging ahead in two 1-NN comparisons at 10 images per class (§4.2, Table 2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated compute limit.** ImageNet-1k distillation at 10 images per class takes roughly **250 GPU-hours** in total, with hardware-dependent timing. Classwise runs are easy to parallelize, and the estimate excludes hyperparameter sweeps, additional backbones, and larger budgets (Appendix F).
- **Author-noted measurement dependence.** The authors report higher coverage when the synthesis encoder also evaluates the samples and exclude those scores from rankings. Their explanation for CLIP’s distinctive outputs is tentative (§§4.2–4.3, Table B.6).
- **Analyst inference.** The audio and video results establish a gain over one coreset baseline in frozen-feature tests. Broader baseline comparisons and student-training experiments would be needed to assess whether the ImageNet training gains generalize to those modalities (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The main augmentation sweep changes the number of views under one ImageNet-1k configuration. It leaves the individual effect of repelling views of the same sample unresolved (Table A.1, §3.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much coverage comes from the stored images themselves?** The reported metric includes random crops. Evaluate identical distilled sets with center crops alone and with progressively larger crop pools across held-out encoders (Appendix D.4).
2. **Does repulsion between views of the same sample drive the augmentation gain?** The view-count sweep leaves that mechanism active. Keep view count and compute fixed, then toggle within-sample repulsion and measure coverage and hard-label accuracy (§3.2, Table A.1).
3. **Do the audio and video sets support end-to-end student training?** The paper evaluates frozen features against herding. Train audio and video students from scratch on drifted samples and matched-budget coresets using the same recipes (§4.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method and the reported benchmark comparisons. The supplied text does not establish whether executable code or full distilled datasets were released. It also omits audio and video uncertainty estimates and detailed evaluation splits, plus hardware-matched runtime comparisons against baselines.
