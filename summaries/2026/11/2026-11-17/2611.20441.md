# Abstention Direction in Large Language Models: Mechanistic Interpretability of Abstention Across Query Ambiguity, Knowledge Limits, and Safety Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20441) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20441)


## One-sentence takeaway

In Qwen3-4B, activation directions estimated from matched prompt pairs changed middle-layer abstention behavior for ambiguous queries, knowledge limits, and safety risks, with asymmetric spillover suggesting partially shared representations (§§6–7).

## Problem and motivation

Language models need to withhold answers when a request is ambiguous, exceeds their knowledge, or poses a safety risk. The authors identify a gap in prior mechanistic work: existing studies examined individual perspectives or compared subsets, leaving open whether all three share a representation under a common intervention protocol. They also argue that directions estimated from independently sourced prompt sets can capture incidental differences between those sets (§§1–2, §4.1, Appendix A.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Matched counterfactual prompts.** The authors construct “slot-flipped” pairs that preserve a shared template while editing the qualifier or topic, entity, or requested action and object. Kimi-K3 generates and verifies the pairs, with rule-based filtering before verification (§4.2, §5.1, Appendix B).
- **One intervention framework for three perspectives.** For each layer and perspective, the authors normalize the difference between mean final-token activations for prompts that call for abstention and prompts that permit answering. They test these directions through inference-time steering against no-intervention, independently sourced, and random-direction controls (§3, §6, Appendices C and E).
- **Behavioral and geometric comparison.** The study measures within- and cross-perspective effects, then compares direction alignment and the separation of prompt classes across layers (§7).

## Evidence and main results

Abstention rate (AR) measures abstention on prompts that call for it. Over-abstention rate (OAR) measures abstention on answerable prompts. The main evaluation uses 128 held-out slot-flipped pairs per perspective, with the same pairs used for both direction-construction methods. Reported means and standard deviations reflect three generation runs with different sampling seeds (§5.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Middle-layer steering changed abstention across all three perspectives | In Qwen3-4B thinking mode, adding the matched direction at \(m=+16\) raised AR above the no-intervention baseline at some layers for ambiguity and knowledge limits, and raised OAR for all three perspectives. Safety AR stayed at its baseline ceiling of 1.0 under addition. Subtraction at \(m=-16\) lowered AR at some layers for all three. | §6.1, Fig. 3a |
| Template-matched directions produced larger effects | Slot-flipped directions produced larger AR and OAR shifts than independently sourced directions on the same held-out pairs in both Qwen3-4B modes. A single-run random-direction control showed no clear systematic shift in either metric. | §§6.1–6.2, Figs. 3–4, Appendix E |
| Thinking and non-thinking modes showed similar layerwise trends | In Qwen3-4B, mean Pearson correlations across the six matched-perspective panels at \(m=\pm16\) were 0.94 for AR and 0.93 for OAR. | Appendix F, Fig. 16 |
| Cross-perspective effects were asymmetric and directions partially aligned | In Qwen3-4B, ambiguity steering changed knowledge-limit AR with little effect on safety abstention, while knowledge-limit and safety steering each affected both other perspectives. For the selected directions, cross-layer cosine similarities were +0.163 for ambiguity (layer 20) and knowledge limits (19), +0.190 for knowledge limits (19) and safety (17), and +0.092 for ambiguity (20) and safety (17). | §7.1, Figs. 3a and 7 |
| Prompt classes separated most clearly for safety | In Qwen3-4B, layerwise Fisher discriminant ratios for the two prompt polarities peaked at 1.15 at layer 23 for knowledge limits and 2.65 at layer 21 for safety. Ambiguity showed no notable middle-layer rise. | §7.2, Fig. 8 |

The interventions establish that the tested activation directions can change judged abstention on the tested prompts. The authors interpret the cross-perspective effects and positive cosine similarities as evidence of partial sharing. The experiments leave unresolved whether a single shared mechanism explains those effects and whether steering improves answer quality or the balance between appropriate abstention and over-abstention.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Evaluation covers two models, English, and selected triggers within each perspective. Direction estimation and testing use separate pairs from the same construction distribution, leaving transfer to other prompt distributions untested. The semantic meaning of each direction remains unresolved (Limitations).
- **Author-stated.** Kimi-K3 both generates and verifies the pairs, which can preserve model-specific biases despite rule-based checks. The single response judge detects abstention and leaves correctness and fluency unscored. Early-layer interventions frequently degraded output, and those layers were excluded from the main analysis (§6.1, Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The baseline comparison uses a slot-flipped test set, so the larger effects could partly reflect a closer match between direction-estimation and evaluation distributions. Fictional entities may also elicit different behavior from obscure real entities that the model does not know (§§5.1, 5.3.2, 6.2).
- **Analyst inference.** Figure 4 selects representative layers for the largest observed contrast, so its dose-response curves describe selected layers. The supplied text gives no independent validation of those layer choices (§6.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the directions generalize beyond template-matched prompts?** Both splits use the same construction process. Freeze directions and layer choices before evaluating on independently authored prompts, including obscure real entities and naturally ambiguous questions.
2. **Does a common component account for the cross-perspective effects?** Pairwise cosines and asymmetric spillover leave the mechanism unresolved. Estimate shared and perspective-specific components on one split, then test their effects separately on held-out ambiguity and knowledge-limit prompts with norm-matched controls.
3. **Does changing abstention improve response quality?** The judge scores only whether a response abstains. Use blinded human ratings of factual correctness, clarification usefulness, appropriateness of abstention, and fluency alongside AR and OAR.

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text supports the main Qwen3-4B trends and the reported geometric values. Exact per-layer AR and OAR values, the full Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct curves, and Table 1’s pre-filter counts are unavailable in the supplied Markdown. Public availability of code, constructed pairs, and learned vectors is unspecified.
