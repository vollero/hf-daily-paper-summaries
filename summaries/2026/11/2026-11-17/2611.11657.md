# Inside the Trading Loop: Markets, Models, and Moves with LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11657) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11657)


## One-sentence takeaway

A deterministic momentum portfolio beat SPY in one broker-recorded paper-trading window with a larger drawdown, while retrospective replays showed that LLM reports could change allocations without establishing an incremental return benefit.

## Problem and motivation

The authors ask how to turn company-news and macroeconomic assessments into stock weights, cash reserves, and orders in a portfolio where each trade changes the holdings available at the next decision. They identify a gap between generating specialist assessments and specifying which fields can affect a shared capital budget. Explicit decision rules let them test how much influence each analyst has while keeping the core portfolio engine fixed. (§§1–3)

## Approach and contributions

- **Implemented trading loop.** Two Claude Haiku 4.5 specialists produce structured sentiment and macro reports. A deterministic coordinator selects stocks by momentum, adjusts weights with composite scores, and sets cash reserves. A stateful executor converts targets into orders and feeds holdings into later decisions. Unusable replies trigger deterministic fallbacks. (§§3.1–3.4, Fig. 1)
- **Comparison of allocation authority.** The authors document the fallback-only paper account and replay three configurations: L0 uses fallbacks, L1 admits analyst scores, and L2 additionally lets the macro stance set cash. Next-session replays probe sensitivity to assessment timing, and constant-cash controls test whether exposure can explain the sizing arm’s result. (§§3.2, 4, 5.2, 5.4)
- **Tests of when calls matter and what replies must contain.** The authors test a pre-call gate based on distance to cash thresholds, compare full and short response contracts, and use role ablations and synthetic checks to examine which decisions an analyst field can affect. (§§5.5–5.6, Apps. C, E–F)

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The fallback-only account earned a higher raw return with greater risk. | March 11–August 28, 2026, broker paper account versus matched adjusted-close SPY: cumulative return **34.5% vs. 14.4%**, maximum drawdown **26.8% vs. 6.3%**, and lower Sharpe for the account. | §5.1, Fig. 4 |
| Benchmark outperformance persisted under next-session assessment use. | March 12–August 31, 2026, same-session L0–L2 replays returned **55.5%–58.7%** from initial capital versus **14.8%** for cost-adjusted SPY. All three lagged arms finished above SPY and QQQ and above their own same-session endpoints. | §5.2, Fig. 5, App. J.2 |
| A simple cash control came close to stance-based sizing. | The **5% constant-cash** arm used baseline stock scores and finished near L2 under both timing rules. All displayed **95% paired-bootstrap intervals** for same-session policy return differences versus L0 included zero. | §5.4, Fig. 7, Tables 23–24 |
| A pre-call threshold gate captured most advisory allocation changes. | A cutoff calibrated on the first **60** replay dates captured **98%** of the summed L1–L0 allocation distance on **68%** of the later **59** dates. This was direct capture from fixed daily comparisons. | §5.5, Fig. 8, App. F |
| Shorter reports improved parsing but changed some decisions. | Short sentiment replies yielded fewer parse failures on matched rich-input cases at the same **500-token** cap. Minimal and production macro contracts disagreed on stance on **57 of 117** jointly parsed dates (**49%**). | §5.6, Fig. 9, App. E, Table 7 |
| Company sentiment had little influence at the tested allocation settings. | Across **119** reconstructed dates, the sentiment-only arm changed no selected membership versus the lexicon, neutral, or FinBERT baselines. Mean half-L1 target-book distance was **0.07–0.10 percentage points of equity**. | App. F, Table 8 |

The account supports a descriptive finding about the fallback portfolio in this window. The replays show how the tested interfaces change allocations under specified inputs and execution assumptions. The similar constant-cash result leaves exposure as a plausible explanation for L2’s endpoint advantage. Incremental LLM returns, risk-adjusted superiority, and prospective trading performance remain unestablished. (§§5.1–5.5, 6.2)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported scope and risk.** The evaluation covers one paper portfolio, one historical window, and one primary LLM provider. The strategy has a single-name cap, with sector exposure and aggregate volatility left largely unconstrained. Prospective post-update and live-capital outcomes were unevaluated. (§§3.1, 6.1–6.2, App. A)
- **Author-reported reconstruction limits.** Some same-session fills precede their input snapshots. The next-session test addresses that mismatch while changing signal age and holdings. Original headlines were replaced, some price archives were unavailable, most model responses were collected after their decision dates, and the primary macro calendar was stale. The deterministic replay exceeded the recorded account by **25.8 percentage points** over overlapping dates. (§4.2, Apps. B, H, J.2)
- **Author-reported reproducibility limits.** The packet supports checks of derived allocations. Raw archives are restricted, sentiment replies are available on request, and exact order trajectories require the proprietary strategy package. (App. A)
- **Analyst inference.** The initial basket comparison cannot isolate rebalancing because membership, weights, and exposure vary together. The cash control also leaves stock inputs and available actions unmatched, so the return comparison cannot isolate the informational value of the macro stance. (§§5.3–5.4)

## Open research questions

1. **Do macro reports improve returns beyond their effect on exposure?** The constant-cash control approximates exposure and uses different stock inputs and actions. Run a prospective paper-account comparison with identical stock scores, cash action sets, and execution clocks, using a deterministic cash policy calibrated to match average exposure. (§5.4)
2. **Does threshold gating preserve portfolio performance while reducing calls?** The temporal split measured allocation capture, and gated trading paths were retrospective. Compare always-call, pre-calibrated threshold-gated, and equal-budget input-gated arms prospectively, recording returns, drawdown, and request costs. (§5.5)
3. **Can a shorter contract improve parsing while preserving allocation decisions?** The tested minimal macro contract changed stance labels despite similar numeric scores. Reissue matched prompts repeatedly under several fixed-cap contracts and compare parse success, within-contract variation, and resulting cash targets. (§5.6, App. E)

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied text clearly identifies the main outcomes and their evaluation settings. Several referenced tables appear only as captions, so exact per-arm lagged returns, full risk summaries, and matched sentiment parse-failure rates are unavailable here. Exact historical provider inputs, full production strategy code, and prospective LLM-enabled trading outcomes are also unavailable. (§§5.2, 5.6, 6.2, Apps. A, J)
