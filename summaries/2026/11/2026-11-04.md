# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-04

## Executive summary

- **Complete outcomes remain harder than intermediate competence.** Embodied Agent Arena (`2610.00854`) reports that visual judgments and executable actions frequently fail to produce complete embodied success. RoboColosseum (`2611.02497`) likewise finds that competitive rankings can coexist with poor task completion.
- **Agent state deserves explicit evaluation.** Switch-SWE (`2611.02017`) reports an average **11.5-percentage-point resolution loss** across seven configurations when unfinished coding tasks are interleaved. Structured resumption memory recovers **5.5–15.5 points** on two OpenCode models.
- **More detailed supervision is not uniformly better.** In movie-trailer editing, decayed turn-level rewards help relative to outcome-only GRPO, while fixed process rewards undermine completion (`2610.35497`). Token-adaptive distillation (`2610.40574`) and local reasoning preferences (`2611.04814`) report benefits in different settings; these do not establish a general winner.
- **Executable environments are a substantial part of the contribution.** WebHarbor (`2611.03634`), terminal-depth synthesis (`2611.01927`), and ToolSpace (`2611.05036`) validate generated tasks through execution and replay. Passing those checks still leaves semantic validity and external transfer to establish.
- **Efficiency claims require complete accounting.** Routing (`2611.05275`), context compaction (`2611.05306`), harness optimization (`2611.02079`), and speculative decoding (`2611.02501`, `2611.05345`) pursue different savings. Replay-based estimates, omitted optimization costs, and missing application latency measurements limit comparisons.
- **Scientific workflow search shows promise with bounded evidence.** MoLRSI reports experimental molecular-discovery outcomes (`2610.39714`); an MSA-search agent reports improved frozen-AF3 interface predictions using confidence feedback (`2611.03183`). Neither establishes unrestricted autonomous discovery.
- **Visual preservation and faithful execution remain separate questions.** Selective latent editing preserves designated tokens (`2611.02568`, `2611.04303`), while YANAMI (`2611.05280`) finds that understanding dialogue state does not guarantee rendering it correctly.
- **Several diagnostics expose failures hidden by ordinary scores.** These include text-preserving action attacks (`2611.04166`), silent gradient stalls (`2611.04449`), revision-insensitive reviewing (`2611.02244`), and joint multimodal forgetting–utility trade-offs (`2611.04467`).

## Main results worth noting

- **Task resumption:** `2611.02017` reports an **11.5-percentage-point average resolution drop** between matched uninterrupted and switched runs across seven coding-agent configurations. Its RCM recovers **5.5–15.5 points** on two OpenCode models. The benchmark inserts four unfinished interruption tasks; uncertainty and RCM overhead are unavailable.
- **Arabic ASR:** `2611.02476` reports **23.86% macro-average WER** for independently initialized recognizers using the weakly labeled corpus, versus **28.62%** for the strongest complete third-party comparator across five GigaSpeechBench dialects. This supports reusable supervision in that evaluation, without establishing universal Arabic ASR superiority.
- **Fixed-model legal adaptation:** Under revised rubrics, `2611.04035` reports held-out legal-task all-pass rates of **30.0%→55.0%** for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and **35.0%→46.0%** for Qwen3.8-Flash. Persistent repository edits change; model weights remain fixed. Repeated-run uncertainty and independent judge validation are missing.
- **Human-video robot transfer:** `2611.05163` reports average performance increasing from **2.1% to 60.0%** across two robots on tasks lacking task-specific robot demonstrations. This is a fixed-camera tabletop result, with supporting robot data used elsewhere in the pipeline.
- **Tool-use training:** `2611.05036` reports its 35B backbone’s seven-benchmark average increasing from **23.0 to 41.0**, reaching **43.2 after RL**. These are the authors’ aggregate scores; absent full metric definitions and uncertainty prevent finer comparison.
- **Hybrid computer use:** `2610.40747` reports **66.0% OSWorld-Verified success under a 50-step budget** for its 4B agent. The retained evidence supports this endpoint, but does not isolate the five-stage curriculum’s individual contributions or provide a complete matched-comparator table.
- **Action safety beyond readable rationales:** `2611.04166` reports new open-loop driving collisions in **52.6% of annotated scenes** relative to clean inputs while generated text remains exactly unchanged. The demonstrated setting uses white-box digital perturbations, not physical closed-loop attacks.
- **Real-time control:** Across the tested LOLPlay configurations, `2611.03685` reports a maximum **25.0% task success** and no benefit from high versus low reasoning effort. Continuous gameplay, observation delay, and response ceilings constrain interpretation.
- **Optimization diagnostics:** In small MoEs, `2610.37914` reports roughly doubled first-layer balancing-bias span with backbone Muon relative to its controlled optimizer comparisons at shorter-run-tuned rates. `2611.04449` separately supports a causal chain from near-zero embeddings to clipping overflow and lost batch gradients in tested gated architectures.
- **Negative and qualified findings matter:** Fixed process rewards can undermine editing completion (`2610.35497`); MCU migration can add cost without removing Linux interference (`2611.01598`); and none of sixteen models identifies a perpetrator in five sampled suspect-identification tasks (`2611.04785`). These findings apply to their stated protocols, not entire research areas.

These outcomes use different tasks, metrics, and budgets. Their numerical magnitudes do not define a common ranking.

## Open research directions

The following are editorial experiment proposals grounded in limitations explicitly identified by the supplied analyses.

1. **Measure whether partial embodied progress predicts completion.** Motivated by `2610.00854` and `2611.02497`, repeat matched tasks with standardized budgets and independent progress scoring. Test whether progress predicts eventual success across embodiments and task compositions.

2. **Isolate what resumption memory must preserve.** For `2611.02017` and `2611.05306`, cross history routing, structured state memory, and compaction frequency while varying interruption load. Measure completion, forgotten requirements, regressions, total tokens, and latency.

3. **Compare localized supervision at matched total budgets.** For `2610.35497`, `2610.40574`, `2611.03233`, and `2611.04814`, hold rollout exposure and supervision cost fixed. Independently vary reward granularity, schedule, teacher selection, and prefix selection, using validation-selected checkpoints and repeated seeds.

4. **Audit executable verifiers against semantic defects.** For `2611.01927`, `2611.03634`, and `2611.05036`, inject omitted requirements, invalid shortcuts, and inconsistent states into accepted tasks. Independent human adjudication and held-out solvers should measure false acceptance beyond replay success.

5. **Establish whether retained experience improves later learning.** For `2610.39714`, `2610.39813`, and `2611.04035`, compare frozen, accumulating, and deliberately mismatched memories over ordered task streams. Separate better execution from reduced cost or improved effectiveness of subsequent adaptation.

6. **Test efficiency in complete deployments.** For `2611.02079`, `2611.02501`, `2611.05275`, `2611.05306`, and `2611.05345`, charge optimization, routing, judging, retrieval, compaction, and verification. Measure application success, latency, memory, and deployment-volume break-even points.

7. **Disentangle speculative precision from rejection frequency.** For `2611.02501` and `2611.05345`, force identical decision sequences into different verification slots while varying cache precision. Jointly measure token mismatches, later decision reuse, coding outcomes, and runtime.

8. **Validate preservation at the final output.** For `2611.02568`, `2611.02737`, `2611.04303`, and `2611.05280`, test repeated edits and corrections using independent region-level or attribute-level checks. Report decoded drift separately from latent invariance and instruction understanding.

9. **Separate proxy improvement from scientific utility.** For `2610.39714`, `2611.03183`, and `2611.03806`, compare adaptive selection with matched-budget fixed portfolios on held-out targets. Validate confidence-based choices against structural or experimental outcomes and report unsuccessful selections.

10. **Test video-derived action representations under controlled shifts.** For `2611.04870`, `2611.05163`, and `2611.05377`, vary camera, appearance, and embodiment separately under matched adaptation budgets. Measure action-label fidelity, physical task success, rollout drift, and recovery rather than video quality alone.
