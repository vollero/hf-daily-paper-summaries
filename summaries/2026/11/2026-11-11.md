# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-11

## Executive summary

- **Evaluation conditions materially change apparent agent capability.** Embodied First Exam (`2611.12177`) reports 77% success with privileged simulator access versus 30% under sensor constraints for its strongest agent on the same 391 hard tasks.
- **Plausible delivery is an unreliable substitute for verification.** FINCOSMOS (`2611.07464`) reports no accepted deliveries across 150 initial financial-agent sessions, although simulated users approved 35.
- **Evidence provenance matters beyond final answers.** BioAgent-DF (`2611.01527`) finds that corrupted numerical tool states can change evidence choices and assay allocations without changing aggregate outcomes. WikiTrace (`2611.14320`) reports fewer memory-induced failures when lessons remain linked to source executions.
- **Physical video generation and its evaluation need separate measures.** PhysOPSD (`2611.12411`) reports improved physical-plausibility scores using simulation-derived training guidance; PhyRubric (`2611.13576`) shows why event depiction must be checked before judging the physics of that event.
- **Training-time prediction can support simpler deployment.** DexForesight (`2611.11166`) transfers spatial dynamics into policy latents, while MC-JEPA (`2611.13572`) uses realized peer futures to supervise standalone asset representations. Their evidence concerns different tasks and does not establish a common performance advantage.
- **Efficiency claims require full accounting.** The dense-reward GRPO study (`2611.13560`) finds that counting generated tokens and GPU time reverses Monte-Carlo rewards’ apparent advantage in its tested mathematical-reasoning settings.
- **Restricted weight changes remain a productive experimental interface.** Deletion Tuning (`2611.10866`) reports competitive adaptation at its largest tested scales; Tree-Ising (`2611.14210`) targets reasoning-loop repair through sparse BF16 edits. These are distinct objectives.
- **The collection supports qualified findings rather than a single leaderboard.** All 39 supplied analyses are compacted, and many describe missing tables, limited uncertainty estimates, or incomplete source coverage.

## Main results worth noting

- **Access-controlled robot evaluation — `2611.12177`.** The strongest evaluated agent achieves **77% success with privileged access versus 30% under sensor constraints**, on the **same 391 hard simulated tasks**. Privileged access also permits unlimited resets, so this comparison does not isolate sensing alone.
- **Acceptance versus approval — `2611.07464`.** FINCOSMOS reports **0 accepted deliveries across 150 initial sessions**, compared with **35 simulated-user approvals**. These are different evaluation endpoints: approval does not certify strategy fidelity, data validity, or chronology.
- **Joint forecasting throughput — `2611.11912`.** LO-World reports **over 25 generated future frames per second with both occupancy and LiDAR decoding on one RTX 4090**. Quality improvements are reported against its unimodal variants; the retained evidence does not supply a matched throughput comparator.
- **Context-based robot learning — `2611.12885`.** RoboScript reports **83.4% success across clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 settings**, using demonstration initialization and simulator rehearsal without weight updates. This aggregate should not be compared directly with Embodied First Exam’s different tasks and access regimes; a matched-budget baseline is not retained.
- **Cache reduction — `2611.03142`.** DepthGraft reports **roughly one-third less KV cache**, alongside improved language benchmarks relative to the evaluated baselines. Missing full tables and implementation settings prevent a precise quality–latency comparison.
- **Reward efficiency — `2611.13560`.** In the tested mathematical-reasoning runs, Monte-Carlo dense rewards’ apparent advantage over outcome-only GRPO reverses under generated-token and GPU-time accounting. Prompt-selection gains are small and largely disappear with longer training or baseline retuning.
- **Evidence-linked memory — `2611.14320`.** WikiTrace improves average benchmark performance and reduces regression relative to **the same procedural insights without source evidence**. The comparison supports the package; it does not isolate raw trajectories from compilations and prompt changes.
- **Adaptive video reasoning — `2611.13907`.** EVAThink reports better **six-benchmark average accuracy** and fewer generated tokens than **always reasoning**. Output-token savings are not yet measured end-to-end latency savings.
- **Local causal sufficiency — `2611.13885`.** The study evaluates **all 4,096 subsets of 12 selected neurons** and finds smaller, sometimes disjoint sufficient subsets. This establishes non-uniqueness within the selected intervention setting, not recovery of complete model circuits.
- **Execution proof scope — `2611.14083`.** Sable’s bit-exact guarantee covers its specified integer inference function, including sampling and stopping. It does not establish equivalence to the original BF16 model, answer correctness, or low-cost serving.

## Open research directions

These are editorial proposals motivated by the supplied evidence, not demonstrated cross-paper results.

1. **Separate event depiction from physical correctness.** For `2611.12411` and `2611.13576`, evaluate base and adapted generators on identical captions with blinded human checks. Report event adherence, physical assessability, and plausibility conditional on the event appearing.

2. **Measure whether computational recovery improves scientific outcomes.** For `2611.01527`, compare reference-restored and control assay allocations prospectively, using independent measurements and replication. Recovering the reference execution alone cannot settle biological benefit.

3. **Compare intermediate feedback at equal total cost.** For `2611.12200` and `2611.13560`, match GPU-hours and count prediction, branching, replay, warm-up, and discarded samples. Report both task success and environment interactions.

4. **Disentangle memory evidence from memory instructions.** For `2611.14320` and `2611.12885`, independently vary lessons, source trajectories, compilations, and inspection prompts under equal context and call budgets. Track gains on previously failed tasks and regressions on previously solved ones.

5. **Preserve useful timing without substituting it for progress.** For `2611.14488`, test tasks combining genuine waiting requirements, event counting, and legitimate return-to-state actions. Compare time indexing, event indexing, and hybrid memory on clean and perturbed episodes.

6. **Audit sparse edits for broad side effects.** For `2611.10866` and `2611.14210`, compare deletion, value edits, and conventional adaptation at matched edit footprint and optimization cost. Measure target gains, unrelated capabilities, retention, memory, and inference latency.

7. **Test scientific selectors beyond their existing feedback pool.** For `2611.12184` and `2611.13348`, run prospective held-out campaigns at fixed verification budgets, varying prior-outcome availability. Measure discovery yield and selection errors rather than only predictor quality or judged novelty.

8. **Translate compact representations into deployment evidence.** For `2611.03142`, `2611.10649`, and `2611.12426`, use method-appropriate matched workloads to report quality, resident memory, latency, and preprocessing cost. Storage or reconstruction improvements alone do not establish end-to-end benefit.

9. **Validate robustness under changes users actually make.** For `2611.13425` and `2611.13995`, test equivalent anatomical prompt reformulations and additional restoration domains, respectively. Report task quality alongside prompt sensitivity or adversarial sensitivity, with repeated training runs.

10. **Test safety gates under ongoing world dynamics.** For `2611.14115` and `2611.12177`, measure harm, safe completion, false blocking, and gate latency under matched paused and realtime execution. This would reveal whether additional checking remains useful when deliberation itself consumes physical time.

## Coverage and uncertainty

**All 39 current papers were covered.** Fifty historical candidates were supplied; four historical papers were retained in supported cross-day edges.

Taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical connections were all available. They were treated as proposals and checked against the retained analyses. Several detailed numerical claims and historical mechanisms from those aids were omitted because their supporting passages were not visible in the endpoint evidence.

**Every current analysis and every historical evidence item contains a `COMPACTED` marker.** All are therefore partial evidence. Omitted spans were not used to reconstruct results, ablations, citations, or dependencies.

The collection log reports unavailable Hugging Face Markdown for nine papers: `2611.12885`, `2611.13001`, `2611.13348`, `2611.10866`, `2611.10015`, `2611.13885`, `2611.14516`, `2611.14116`, and `2611.13995`. Analyses were nevertheless supplied for all nine; these failures concern an input channel, not complete absence of paper evidence.

The retained current analyses explicitly report truncated or omitted source coverage for `2611.01527`, `2611.12177`, `2611.12200`, `2611.13001`, `2611.13560`, `2611.14083`, and `2611.14488`. Missing table bodies, uncertainty estimates, resource accounting, and release verification recur across the collection.

Confidence is lowest for broad causal explanations, industrial or clinical transfer, general physical understanding, universal representation quality, and end-to-end efficiency inferred from proxy metrics. Historical edges remain medium confidence. No publication priority, citation relationship, influence, or technical dependence is inferred from service observation dates.
