# Sable: A Small-Field Sumcheck Argument for Bit-Exact Inference of Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14083) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14083)


## One-sentence takeaway

Sable reports single-GPU proofs of bit-exact integer LLM inference, including sampling and stopping, with substantial proving overhead, accuracy loss, and large proofs currently suited to open-weight auditing.

## Problem and motivation

A hosted model’s output alone does not establish which committed weights, arithmetic, sampling seed, or stopping policy produced it. The authors identify three obstacles to cryptographic verification: defining exact inference semantics, preventing integer constraints from wrapping in a small prime field, and proving traces too large to retain in memory (§1).

The guarantee concerns Sable’s specified integer function \(F_{\mathsf O}\), rather than equivalence to the original BF16 model. Its accuracy therefore requires separate evaluation (§1.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Exact inference semantics:** \(F_{\mathsf O}\) specifies integer normalization, attention, rounding, greedy or seeded sampling, and stopping for Qwen2-style transformers, with Qwen3 extensions for QK-norm and mixture of experts (§§3.6, 5).
- **Constraints with explicit integer bounds:** Gadgets over the 31-bit KoalaBear field enforce ranges intended to make field relations equivalent to integer relations. The paper supplies no-wrap and determinism lemmas (§6.7; Appendix A.6).
- **Combined constraint and lookup proofs:** A “domain” merges zerochecks with the leaf layer of LogUp-GKR. Lookup chunks remain smaller than the field characteristic, preventing multiplicities from cancelling modulo that characteristic (§§7.2–7.3).
- **Streaming and commitment design:** Two model passes recompute witnesses layer by layer; mixed-height Merkle trees and mixed-length BaseFold batch tensor openings. GPU-resident transcripts and CUDA graph replay reduce execution overhead (§§7.1, 7.5; §1.1).
- **Preparation for recursion:** The authors reduce verifier hashing and implement a BLAKE3 constraint circuit. Complete recursive compression and zero knowledge remain future work (§§1.1, 12).

These are the authors’ stated contributions; several underlying proof primitives are explicitly inherited from prior work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full-model proving on one GPU | Qwen2.5-32B, all 64 layers, RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell: 92.5 s for one 4,096-token sequence; 253 s for four such sequences; 398 s for one 16,384-token sequence. | Abstract |
| Qwen3 support | On the same stated single-GPU prover, 4,096 tokens take 121.8 s for Qwen3-32B and 84.7 s for Qwen3-30B-A3B. | Abstract; §13 |
| Significant proving overhead | Proof time is reported as 41–50× the time to compute the same tokens in the authors’ serving engine for \(F_{\mathsf O}\). | Abstract; §13 |
| Normalized improvement over zkLLM | Authors report 40–64× less proving time per parameter and token; detailed comparison settings are omitted by truncation. | Abstract; §13 |
| Large proofs and costly verification | Across the three evaluated models, proofs are 106–477 MiB; a verifier with a compiled core takes 19–56 s on one CPU core. | §4 |
| Integer accuracy loss | On WikiText-2, greedy tokens match BF16 at 86.3% of positions and perplexity is 8.3% higher; discussion associates this gap with Qwen2.5. | Abstract; §12 |
| Q/K calibration helps Qwen3 | Per-group Q/K exponent splitting removes 53% of the perplexity gap for Qwen3-30B-A3B and 23% for Qwen3-32B, without changing proof cost. Both integer models are within 1.6 points of BF16 on MMLU and 1.7 points on GSM8K. | Abstract; §5.7; §13 |
| No observed domain violations in the reported Qwen3 scan | Tested real-text prompts, adversarial sequences, and every vocabulary token as a one-token prompt reached no undefined-function condition. The scan covers prompt positions, not generated positions. | §11.9 |
| Claimed soundness is conditional | Abstract reports round-by-round error \(2^{-103}\), counting query proof of work. Analysis assumes random-oracle behavior and collision resistance; the exact commitment composition requires the authors’ own theorem-composition argument. | Abstract; §§8, 8.2 |

The evidence supports feasibility for the specified integer models and evaluated hardware. It does not establish BF16-equivalent outputs, private verification, universal input coverage, or a directly controlled speed advantage over prior systems. Much of the detailed evaluation and its tables is absent from the supplied source.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Privacy and deployment:** Proofs expose trace evaluations; repeated weight openings can reveal closed weights. Batch verifiers see every prompt and output. The authors consequently restrict the current system to open-weight auditing (§4).
- **Accuracy and partiality:** Quantization changes predictions, and \(F_{\mathsf O}\) is undefined when specified ranges fail. The authors explicitly acknowledge that the input scan cannot exclude adversarial failures (§§5.7, 11.9, 12).
- **Incomplete compression:** Recursive and zero-knowledge layers are unfinished. The BLAKE3 circuit’s trace commitment has not yet been measured (§12).
- **Security-analysis qualification:** The authors know of no published theorem covering their exact combination of mixed-length batching, list decoding, and two folds per oracle; they provide a composition argument (§8.2).
- **Calibration and reproducibility:** Calibration uses a short English technical text. Checkpoint conversion uses floating point and may differ across hardware at rare rounding ties, although the committed integer artifact fixes inference semantics (§5.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation on three Qwen-family models provides limited evidence for generalization to other architectures. Prior-system comparisons also involve different proof semantics and context lengths (§§2, 12).

## Open research questions

1. **Can accuracy improve cheaply?** Calibration helps Qwen3, but residual losses remain (§12). A next experiment could compare Q/K calibration and wider down-projection inputs using matched task-generation settings, measuring accuracy and actual proving time together.
2. **What is the complete cost of private, compressed proofs?** Only initial recursion components exist (§12). Implementing the recursive verifier and hiding layers would permit end-to-end measurements of proof size, latency, memory, and composed soundness.
3. **Can adversarial inputs force honest aborts?** Existing scans cover finite inputs and exclude generated positions (§11.9). A targeted search over prompts and autoregressive continuations could measure which range condition fails first.
4. **Can the commitment analysis be formalized as one theorem?** The exact composition lacks a directly applicable published theorem (§8.2). A machine-checked analysis covering all three modifications would address this qualification.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and its scope, but the source was truncated; this analysis does not provide complete coverage.

Missing material includes most detailed runtime, comparison, engineering-ablation, and accuracy tables; complete benchmark protocols and uncertainty estimates; the remainder of the security analysis; and parameter-table contents. The claimed code release was not inspected.
