# Blind Variation, Selective Retention: Improving Creative Idea Generation in AI Scientists

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12184) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12184)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that restricting task information during ideation, then screening implementations with a model trained on prior same-task outcomes, improves creative proposal generation and discovery performance while reducing empirical evaluations.

## Problem and motivation
AI scientists can produce effective solutions while repeatedly exploring familiar mechanisms. The authors argue that detailed task descriptions can anchor ideation, whereas prompting for novelty and retrieving literature may offer insufficient diversity. They address two separate needs: generating unusual ideas that work and preserving promising candidates before costly verification (§§1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Blind Variation (BV):** Limit task context during ideation and literature search; restore the full specification for implementation. Experiments vary context information across proposer models and scientific tasks (§§2–3; Appendix D.1).
- **Selective Retention (SR):** Fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B-Base with a scalar head using prior same-task proposal outcomes. A pairwise logistic loss separates top-utility candidates from sampled remaining candidates, rather than predicting every candidate’s exact performance (§4.2; Appendix B.4).
- **Combined workflow and assessment:** Integrate BVSR into SimpleTES, test component contributions, and validate LLM-based creativity judgments with experts. Creativity combines generative rarity, normalized empirical utility, and judged non-obviousness; the underlying creativity definition is adopted from prior work (§§2, 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Less task information favors creative exploration | Across five proposer models and 12 tasks, with 112 proposals per model/task/information level, lower information generally increases the relative frequency of top-1% creativity and utility proposals; mean originality and non-obviousness decline as information increases. | §§3.2–3.3; Fig. 2 |
| Full information weakens the BV prompt | Under matched proposer models, candidate budgets, and reasoning configurations, top-1% mean creativity falls from 0.530 with BV to 0.462 with full information; mean non-obviousness falls from 0.713 to 0.609. | §3.4; Table 1 discussion |
| SR preserves most exceptional proposals while halving verification | Evaluated on 5,783 proposals after training on a separate 40,733 proposals from the same tasks: discarding 50% retains 79% of top-5% creativity proposals and 82% of top-5% utility proposals. Authors report better retention than prompting, ICL, and SFT baselines. | §4.2; Fig. 3 |
| BVSR improves end-to-end discovery | With Kimi-K3 in SimpleTES, four parallel trajectories, 40 proposals per iteration, and 10 iterations, BVSR achieves higher final task scores on all five application tasks and surpasses the original final scores with fewer verifications. Exact improvements are absent from the supplied figure text. | §5.1; Fig. 4 |
| Concrete benchmark gains | Spatial-gene scaled Kendall score: 0.918 versus SimpleTES 0.906 and SPARK-X 0.853. Anomaly-detection composite score across four tabular datasets: 0.679 versus SimpleTES 0.629 and Isolation Forest 0.491. A two-fitted-parameter scaling law achieves held-out R² of 0.967. | §5.2; Table 2 discussion; Table 3 |
| Components are complementary in tested ablations | BV alone and SR alone improve final scores over SimpleTES; their combination performs best on gene-expression analysis and scaling-law discovery. | §5.3; Fig. 5 |
| Expert ratings support non-obviousness gains | On 150 above-baseline BV/analogical-reasoning proposals, human–automatic correlation is r=0.56 (95% CI 0.45–0.67); mean human ratings are 3.64 versus 2.97 on a 1–5 scale, difference 0.67 (95% CI 0.33–0.99; p=0.0006). | §5.4; Appendix F.2 |

The evidence supports improved benchmark exploration and candidate screening in the tested workflows. It does not establish literature-level novelty, transfer of SR to unseen tasks, or lower total computation after including selector training and prior verification costs.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no dedicated author limitations section. Relevant author-stated qualifications and analyst inferences are:

- **Measurement scope:** Originality explicitly means rarity within a generator’s sampled proposals, not novelty relative to scientific literature. Utility is normalized against the best candidate observed in these experiments (§2).
- **Imperfect judgment:** Human validation supports, but does not fully confirm, automatic scoring. Semantic grouping agreement is 84% for LLM-grouped pairs and 73% for separated pairs; non-obviousness validation covers only above-baseline BV and analogical-reasoning proposals (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** SR training and testing use the same tasks. End-to-end evaluation uses one proposer and component ablations cover two tasks; broader transfer remains unestablished (§§4.2, 5.1, 5.3).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency:** Fewer verified proposals need not imply lower total cost; the reported verification-count curves do not account for the prior outcome collection and selector training (§§4.2, 5.1).
- **Theory is conditional:** The originality argument assumes information strengthens a shared task signal; the best-utility result additionally assumes higher-utility ideas are less aligned with that signal (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does SR transfer to new tasks?** Only same-task training is evaluated. A next experiment could hold out entire tasks or domains and measure exceptional-candidate retention.
- **Which information should ideation retain?** Contexts vary in amount and content, leaving their separate effects unresolved. Compare length-matched contexts that selectively reveal task identity, metrics, or constraints.
- **When does BVSR save total resources?** Verification counts omit training and historical-data costs. Measure end-to-end compute and elapsed time while varying available prior outcomes.
- **Does BV improve literature-level novelty?** The originality metric measures sampling rarity. Conduct blinded expert comparisons against retrieved prior methods alongside empirical utility testing.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central findings and several exact results, but omits Table 1’s full entries, Table 2’s full comparison, and most numerical figure data. Selector optimization details, repeat-run uncertainty for discovery gains, the operational choice of BV information level, total resource accounting, and public code/model/data availability are not established.
