# PhyRubric: Rubric-Grounded Diagnostic Evaluation of Physical Commonsense in Text-to-Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13576) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13576)


## One-sentence takeaway
PhyRubric turns physical-commonsense evaluation into evidence-backed rubric checks, exposing widespread failures to depict requested events and training a compact judge with improved—but still modest—agreement on physical plausibility (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing text-to-video benchmarks rely on subjective holistic ratings that obscure which physical expectation failed. They propose separating semantic adherence (SA)—whether the requested event appears—from physical commonsense (PC)—whether the depicted event is plausible—while making both scores traceable to explicit observations (§1).

This distinction matters because omitting an event can yield apparently plausible video without demonstrating the requested physics (§4.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Diagnostic evaluation framework:** Construct each caption’s rubric before viewing videos and reuse it across generators. Semantic items check required facts; physical items specify expected behavior, visible failure signs, severity, and violation type. Fixed rules convert timestamped item verdicts into scores; absent events can make PC unassessable (§3.1).
- **Benchmark and training assets:** PhyRubric-Bench contains 245 captions and 2,433 human-verified videos across 34 categories in mechanics, optics, heat, electricity, and acoustics. PhyRubric-Train contains 947 scene-disjoint captions, 3,788 automatically annotated videos, and 45.9K item verdicts (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Rubric-free inference judge:** PhyRubric-Judge fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-8B using rubric-derived supervision. At inference, it receives a caption and 16 sampled frames and predicts scores, PC assessability, and a timestamped diagnosis without requiring a rubric (§§3.3, 4.1).
- **Diagnostic findings:** The authors identify event omission, severe physical failures, and misleading PC scores from avoidance as recurring patterns (§4.2).

## Evidence and main results

|---|---|---|
| Strongest generators still pass fewer than half of cases | Domain-averaged joint performance—SA ≥ 4 and PC ≥ 4—was 44.20% for Seedance 2.5, 36.20% for Veo 3.1, and 14.93% for leading open-source HunyuanVideo-1.5-8B. | §4.2; Table 1 discussion |
| Event depiction is a major bottleneck | Across benchmark videos, 50.8% scored SA = 2; 25.0% never showed the event. Among fully depicted events, 74.1% had plausible physics. | §4.2; Figure 5 |
| Physical scores are strongly bimodal | Among assessable videos, 52.9% scored PC = 5, 40.8% scored 1–2, and 6.3% scored 3–4. | §4.2; Figure 5(a) |
| Judge improves soft-score correlation | Against benchmark human labels, SA Pearson r was 0.61 versus 0.49 for Qwen3-VL-32B; PC r was 0.32 versus 0.23 for VideoPhy-2-AutoEval. Gemini 4 Argon reached 0.60 and 0.31, respectively. | §4.3; Table 2 discussion |
| Held-out generalization has limited reported degradation | Unseen-generator correlation dropped by 0.03 SA and 0.06 PC; leave-one-domain-out drops were at most 0.04 SA and 0.09 PC. | §4.3; Table 3 discussion |
| Human verification supports annotation consistency | Reviewers changed 2.8% of SA and 5.6% of PC verdicts; double verification achieved 95.3% verdict agreement and Cohen’s κ = 0.87. | Appendix A.4 |

| Diagnosis-driven rewriting improves adherence without demonstrated physics gains | On 100 selected failing Wan2.2-T2V-A14B captions, rewriting raised mean SA from 2.55 to 3.19 and SA pass rate from 11% to 31%; resampling reached 2.76 and 18%. No PC difference was significant. | Appendix C.4; Table 15 discussion |

The evidence supports useful diagnosis and stronger agreement with this benchmark’s human labels. It does not establish broad physical understanding or reliable clip-level physics assessment: PC correlation remains modest. “Matching Gemini” is specific to these settings; Appendix C.1 reports different strengths across metrics and no significant hard-correlation gaps.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The framework is presented as an analysis tool rather than a definitive standard (§1). It evaluates short, explicitly specified causal events and qualitative physical expectations, excluding actual audio and quantitative dynamics (§3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—causal interpretation:** Event avoidance does not uniquely establish missing physical knowledge. Instruction following or prompt formulation could contribute; rewriting improves SA without significantly improving PC (Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference—annotation dependence:** Training labels are unverified agent outputs. Benchmark reviewers inspect preannotations and also authored the rubrics, leaving possible anchoring and shared interpretation effects (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison asymmetry:** Baselines use different frame sampling and prompts. Gemini’s soft score comes from self-reported probabilities in a second run, unlike token-probability scores for local generative judges (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—sampling and scoring effects:** One generation per caption limits conclusions about sampling variability. Severity caps and multiple-failure penalties may contribute to the observed PC bimodality (§3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** §4.2 reports 16.9% jointly passing videos, while Appendix C.1 gives a 14% human joint pass rate; the supplied text does not explain the difference.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the rubric improve independent human reliability?** High agreement is reported under rubric-guided verification, without a matched holistic-rating comparison. Next experiment: randomize independent annotators between protocols and compare agreement and error detection.
2. **How much does sparse frame sampling limit the judge?** The annotation agent uses temporal zooming, while the judge sees uniform frames. Next experiment: compare uniform and adaptive sampling on human-verified brief violations at matched compute.
3. **Does the judge transfer beyond the benchmark’s prompt structure?** Held-out tests retain its short, explicit-outcome event design. Next experiment: evaluate longer, multi-event and implicit-outcome captions with independently constructed rubrics.
4. **Can diagnoses improve physics beyond better prompting?** The rewriting experiment improves adherence but finds no significant PC gain. Next experiment: compare diagnosis-driven generation interventions across generators, using blinded human verification.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methods and supporting analyses, but most referenced tables contain captions without numerical bodies. Material gaps include complete per-model results, full taxonomy entries, actual prompt templates, systematic diagnosis-accuracy measurements, annotation cost, and most uncertainty estimates. Asset availability is claimed in the paper but was not independently checked.
