# The Surprising Effectiveness of Deleting Weights in LLM Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10866) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10866)


## One-sentence takeaway
Deletion Tuning learns binary masks over frozen LLM gate-projection weights, reaching accuracy comparable to full fine-tuning at the largest tested scales across three post-training regimes, with compact adapters and stronger retention in the reported GRPO experiments (Tables 1–6; Figure 2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether post-training requires writing new weight values or can instead work by selecting pretrained weights to delete. Existing pruning primarily preserves capabilities, while full fine-tuning (FFT), LoRA, and sparse weight tuning learn new numerical values. The proposed alternative targets capability acquisition, small task adapters, and reduced forgetting (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Deletion-only adaptation:** Freeze the base model and learn one scalar logit per gate-projection weight. Training uses sigmoid masks with increasing sharpness; deployment keeps each pretrained value or sets it to zero. Sparsity is learned rather than fixed (§4).
- **Practical mask training:** Initialize logits using Wanda importance scores, optimize them with SFT, GRPO, or self-distillation fine-tuning (SDFT), and store the deployed mask using bit packing and zstd compression. Wanda initialization and the weight regularizer are adopted from prior work (§4).
- **Cross-regime evidence:** Evaluate capability acquisition and adapter storage across Llama and Qwen backbones, including a mixture-of-experts model; additionally measure prior-task retention under GRPO (§5.1–5.6).
- **Geometric analysis:** Compare learned updates using stable rank, spectrum drift, and principal-weight overlap. The authors interpret similarity to FFT as a possible explanation of effectiveness, rather than a demonstrated causal mechanism (§5.5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Larger-model GRPO accuracy is comparable to FFT | Best-checkpoint validation accuracy: Llama8B GSM8K **83.55% vs 83.24%**; Qwen7B six-benchmark SimpleRL-Zoo mean **52.32% vs 51.89%**; Qwen14B **55.43% vs 54.87%**; Qwen3-30B-A3B **58.54% vs 58.43%**. | Tables 1–2 |
| Smaller models show meaningful deficits | Under GRPO, Llama1B GSM8K accuracy is **54.44% vs FFT’s 60.50%**; Qwen0.5B SimpleRL-Zoo mean is **17.64% vs 19.01%**. | Table 1 |
| Very few weights are deleted in the documented GRPO runs | Final deleted fractions span **0.0347%–0.0459% of all parameters** across the six backbones in Table 1. | Appendix B, Table 7 |
| Stored adapters are much smaller | Llama8B GRPO mask: **8.4 MB**, versus **30,633 MB** for FFT and **10.0 MB** for rank-1 LoRA. Sizes use compressed masks and fp32 numerical weights, excluding deployment-discarded overhead. | Table 1; Appendix A |
| Sampling coverage exceeds the base model | With **128 samples per problem at temperature 1.0**, pass@128 is **99.62% vs 95.29%** on Llama8B GSM8K and **80.08% vs 74.01%** on Qwen7B SimpleRL-Zoo. | Table 3 |
| Learning improves substantially beyond one-shot Wanda pruning | Llama8B GSM8K validation accuracy: **83.55%** trained mask, **60.65%** best Wanda mask, **60.27%** base. Wanda selects its best result across eight sparsity levels. | §5.3; Table 4 |
| GRPO retention is stronger | Across three Llama scales, Deletion Tuning stays within **one percentage point** of base mean accuracy on six prior tasks throughout training; FFT ends **1.3–3.1 points** below base, and LoRA/MTH lose up to **5.4 points**. | §5.4; Figure 2 |
| SFT results depend on scale and suite | Llama3B mean accuracy: MMLU-Pro **22.28% vs FFT’s 21.19%**, BBEH **11.39% vs 11.39%**. Llama1B BBEH remains lower: **5.65% vs 8.35%**. Each subject/subtask is trained separately. | §5.1; Table 5 |
| SDFT achieves similar exact-match accuracy | Qwen7B ToolAlpaca: **69.07% vs FFT’s 68.04%**; Science: **70.61% vs 71.20%**. | Table 6 |
| Update geometry resembles FFT | At saved GRPO checkpoints for Llama8B and Qwen7B, Deletion Tuning’s spectrum drift is within **1.4×** FFT’s; principal-weight overlap is **0.43–0.46**, versus FFT’s **0.41–0.46**. | §5.5; Figure 3 |

These results support deletion-only adaptation in the tested settings. “Matches FFT” denotes similar reported point estimates; the text provides no uncertainty estimates or equivalence tests. Pass@128 demonstrates increased coverage under finite sampling, not that those solutions were impossible for the base model. The detailed sub-0.05% deletion evidence covers the six GRPO runs in Appendix B; corresponding fractions are not supplied for SFT, SDFT, or the larger GRPO models.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One trainable logit per masked weight makes training memory higher than LoRA’s, despite compact deployment storage (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Sequential continual learning, mask merging, and structured masks remain future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Retention evidence concerns GSM8K GRPO on three Llama scales and an aggregate prior-task suite; it does not establish retention under SFT, SDFT, or repeated task acquisition (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Best-checkpoint validation reporting, without reported seed variation or confidence intervals, limits conclusions about small accuracy differences (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Geometry is observational at selected checkpoints. It does not establish why deletion works causally (§5.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Storage savings depend on the stated fp32 baseline accounting and compression conventions; inference speed and measured training-resource savings are not reported (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Wanda-only pruning is a useful control, but its noise-free initialization differs from the trained method’s noisy initialization. The supplied experiments do not isolate the necessity of Wanda, gate-only targeting, or mask sharpening (Appendix E; §4).

## Open research questions
- **Does retention persist across sequential tasks?** The paper tests single-task adaptation rather than continual task sequences. Next experiment: train successive masks and evaluate every previously learned task after each stage.
- **Why does the accuracy gap shrink with scale?** The reported trend spans different backbones without isolating its cause. Next experiment: compare model sizes within one family under matched data, training budgets, and deletion budgets.
- **Which design choices are essential?** Initialization, target layers, and sharpening are not independently ablated. Next experiment: compare Wanda versus random initialization, gate versus other projections, and annealed versus fixed sharpness.
- **Can compact storage translate into lower training cost?** Training retains many mask logits and uses more memory than LoRA. Next experiment: restrict trainable candidate positions and measure peak memory, runtime, accuracy, and retention (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and descriptive comparisons; **medium** in broad equivalence, causal explanations, and continual-learning implications.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, per-benchmark retention results, measured training memory and runtime, inference-speed measurements, deletion fractions outside the six documented GRPO runs, and confirmed availability of code or trained masks.
