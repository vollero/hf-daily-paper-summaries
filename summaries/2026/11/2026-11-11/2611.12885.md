# World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12885) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12885)


## One-sentence takeaway
RoboScript turns one demonstration plus simulator practice into reusable text memory without updating model weights, achieving reported success of 83.4% across clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 settings, with more limited evidence for physical robot transfer (Table 1; §5.7).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address how robots can acquire manipulation skills without retraining an action policy for each task or distribution. They identify a gap in demonstration-only in-context learning: a recorded example provides no outcomes from acting in the learner’s current world. Their proposed solution adds rehearsal and feedback while storing learned task knowledge in context rather than weights (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **World In-Context Learning (WICL):** A frozen foundation model initializes context from a demonstration, rehearses on training instances, revises its context from outcomes, and freezes it before evaluation on disjoint instances. Transfer is argued to depend on shared task semantics and behavior effects across worlds (§3).
- **RoboScript infrastructure:** Modular perception, action tools, model services, and access controls implement WICL. Depth supplies geometry; model-assisted perception identifies objects; action receipts report achieved effects. Automated skill building precedes task learning (§4.1; Appendices B–C).
- **Semantic Task Memory and reflection:** Structured text stores object roles, workflows, relative grasp/place specifications, and lessons. Deployment adds arm selection, completion verification, and recovery. Separate reflection rounds produce guidance for subsequent unseen evaluation blocks (§§4.2–4.4).

The contribution is the integrated manipulation workflow and its evaluation; the authors explicitly identify earlier experiential language agents as close relatives (Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong benchmark performance | GPT-6.1-Sol: **83.6% clean, 83.2% randomized, 83.4% mean**, from 10 episodes per task per setting across 50 tasks. Reported episode-level 95% Wilson interval: 81.0–85.6%. | Table 1; §§5.1–5.2 |
| Advantage concentrated under randomization | Reported 4D-WAM baseline: 81.5% clean, 41.8% randomized, 61.7% mean with 50 clean demonstrations/task; RoboScript uses one demonstration/task plus practice. Mean gap: 21.7 percentage points. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Robustness across tested models | Randomized/clean success retention is 95.2–100.8% across three RoboScript models, versus at most 65.1% for listed trained policies. Rehearsal uses clean scenes only. | Table 1; §§5.1–5.2 |
| World interaction improves demonstration-derived memory | On 10 tasks, paired evaluation gives 78.5% mean with the full method versus 69.0% for demonstration-written memory without world interaction; reported McNemar p = 0.003. | Table 2; §5.4 |
| Recovery contributes materially | On the same deployment-ablation tasks/seeds, removing recovery reduces mean success from 78.5% to 69.0%. | Table 2; §5.4 |
| Privileged supervision ablation shows no observed loss | Relearning on 10 tasks gives 86.0% with full supervision and 86.5% without privileged demonstration state or failure diagnoses; success signals and demonstration trajectory keypoints remain. | Table 2; §5.4; Appendix D |
| Reflection helps, with diminishing returns | Across all 50 tasks, guidance rescues minus breaks **18, 7, and 1 episodes** over successive rounds, each using 10 new clean episodes/task and paired guidance/no-guidance evaluation. | Fig. 9; §5.5 |
| Limited physical transfer succeeds | Adapted simulated-ALOHA memories achieve 10/10 Click Bell, 7/10 Grab Roller, 7/10 Move Playingcard Away, and 6/10 Stack Bowls Two on PiPER, without renewed demonstrations or rehearsal; execution is planned offline. | §5.7; Fig. 11 |
| Deployment is expensive and slow | Sol averages 54.7 calls and 2.33 million tokens/episode; median deployment duration is 6.4 minutes. Reported API cost for learning 50 tasks plus 1,000 evaluation episodes is $1,911. | Table 3; §5.6; Appendix D |

These results support the effectiveness of the complete system on the tested benchmark and its particular randomizations. They do not isolate semantic memory as the sole cause: perception, generated skills, planning, and recovery also contribute. The comparison is not matched for interaction or computation budgets, and physical results do not establish online recovery or broad real-world generalization.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** API service variation can shift success by several points; inference is slow; reflection gains diminish; deformables and tightly coordinated contact remain challenging (Appendix F).
- **Evaluation scope:** Main results use one simulator benchmark. Physical evaluation covers four tasks with offline action sequences, unlike the online simulated deployment loop (§5.7).
- **Comparison limits — analyst inference:** Baselines come from leaderboards or published reports with different episode counts. “One demonstration” also includes substantial simulator practice and a skill set built across all tasks; it is not a matched total-data or compute comparison (§5.1; Table 3).
- **Information-boundary ambiguity:** Broad claims that every actor uses only deployable data conflict with Table 5 and Appendix C.6, which place S1 actors at the privileged tier. Deployment restrictions are described more consistently.
- **Mechanism claim — analyst inference:** Stable knowledge-failure counts support the authors’ semantic-transfer interpretation, but do not prove appearance-independent knowledge or transfer under arbitrary physics changes (§5.3). Learned-world-model rehearsal is proposed rather than evaluated.

## Open research questions
- **How much world mismatch can memory tolerate?** Shared behavior semantics are assumed (§3). Test frozen memories under controlled changes to friction, mass, geometry, and contact dynamics.
- **Can physical deployment retain online recovery?** Hardware trials use offline sequences (§5.7). Compare offline execution with online anticipation/recovery under deliberately introduced disturbances.
- **What is the best success–cost tradeoff?** Current deployment has substantial latency and token use (Appendix D). Compare smaller models, shorter memories, and fewer verification calls under fixed resource budgets.
- **Does rehearsal extend to deformable and contact-intensive tasks?** These remain unresolved in Appendix F. Evaluate cloth, cables, and contact-rich handovers with explicit perception and skill ablations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed protocols, ablations, uncertainty estimates, and costs, but supports narrower conclusions than some general transfer claims.

Material information not established includes verified release contents, a complete accounting of skill-building resources, detailed physical-adapter construction and tuning effort, matched-budget baseline experiments, and resolution of the S1 privilege inconsistency.
