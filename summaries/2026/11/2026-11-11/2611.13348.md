# ProTEUS: A mutation-resolved world model for closed-loop protein evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13348) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13348)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProTEUS combines mutation-focused protein representations with experimental feedback to improve variant prediction and enzyme engineering, although detailed results support stronger gains for levansucrase than the abstract’s broad claims imply.

## Problem and motivation
Protein engineering must search large combinatorial mutation spaces with limited experimental screening, while interactions between mutations complicate extrapolation to higher-order variants. The authors argue that evolutionary plausibility often differs from the activity an engineering campaign seeks to optimize, and that existing iterative methods lack sufficiently informative variant representations for learning from small experimental datasets (Introduction, pp. 3–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Mutation-focused representation:** MutationFormer integrates aligned sequence, MSA, and structural features through mutation-biased attention. DMS pretraining learns a shared representation across assays; mutation-bias and modality ablations support these design choices (Results, pp. 5–6; Methods, pp. 33–35).
- **Fast structural inputs:** A Boltz-1-initialized, two-step coordinate predictor uses differentiable Gauss–Seidel projection to enforce specified geometric constraints. This component builds on the cited projection work rather than establishing every underlying technique as new here (Results, p. 7; Methods, pp. 36–38).
- **Closed-loop nomination:** Initial sequence/MSA likelihood rankings use modality weights learned during pretraining. Later rounds combine zero-shot ranks, random-forest predictions, and experimental evidence. Crucially, iterative evolution refits the random forest while keeping ProTEUS representations fixed; neural fine-tuning is a separate evaluation procedure (Methods, pp. 35–43; Table S2).
- **Experimental integration:** An agent coordinates candidate generation and feedback and supplies LLM rationales. Those annotations did not affect rankings or batch selection in the reported campaigns, so their contribution to engineering performance is untested (pp. 8–9).

## Evidence and main results

All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong fit within pretraining assays | Held-out variants from the pooled 182-assay corpus: Spearman **0.933**, MSE **0.115**; PoET-2 Spearman **0.861**. This split holds out variants, not protein backgrounds. | p. 5; Fig. 2B; p. 32 |
| More functional data improves prediction | With architecture and evaluation sets fixed, an **80-fold** training-data increase reduced test MSE **0.262 → 0.115** and increased Spearman **0.842 → 0.933**. | p. 5; Fig. 2C |
| Mutation bias and multimodal inputs help | Separate ablations: removing mutation bias reduced Spearman **0.840 → 0.606**; sequence-only versus all modalities yielded **0.807 versus 0.938**. | p. 6; Fig. 2D |
| Better initial nomination under the tested definition | Across **13** held-out single-mutant assays, top-**10** selections had fitness at or above the assay mean in **75.4%** of ProTEUS selections versus **64.6%** for adapted MULTI-evolve rank fusion. | p. 6; Fig. 2F; p. 36 |
| Better supervised and simulated single-mutant performance | Within-assay fivefold fine-tuning: mean Spearman **0.549 versus 0.367** for ProteinNPT. Simulations on **10** datasets, **30** variants per round for **five** rounds: final mean normalized best fitness **0.741 versus 0.555** for EVOLVEpro. | p. 6; Figs. 2G–H |
| Faster structure generation with specified validity | Reported CASP15 median speedup **9.4×** versus 200-step Boltz-1; AlphaFold 3 mean runtimes **7.81–10.81×** higher across five atom-count bins. Projected structures passed all seven checks across six benchmarks. | p. 7; Fig. 3C–D; p. 38 |
| Better higher-order extrapolation | Across **40 dataset–K comparisons** from **15 datasets**, training on ≤K substitutions and testing on >K gave mean Spearman **0.448 versus 0.295** for MULTI-evolve; ProTEUS won **80%** of comparisons. | p. 8; Fig. 3F |
| Levansucrase improved substantially | **90 variants**, three batches: G23A:Q34N:G240W reached **14.03× WT screening activity**, versus **4.50×** for the best initial single mutant. | pp. 9–10; Fig. 4; Table S5 |
| ALDH improved, below the abstract’s headline | **150 variants**, five batches: final quadruple mutant reached **6.85× WT total screening activity**; the final-round best had **7.90× concentration-normalized activity**. Equal-protein purified assays supported improvement without reporting an exact fold change. | p. 11; Fig. 5C; Table S5 |
| Laboratory embedding evidence is mixed | Final-round levansucrase readouts showed no advantage over simple comparators. ALDH preceding-batch prediction: Spearman **0.265**, 95% CI **−0.10–0.59**, permutation **P = 0.158** on **30 test variants**. | Supplementary Text, p. 46; Table S7 |

The benchmarks support improved prediction and selection under the evaluated settings, and the experiments demonstrate useful enzyme variants. They do not establish prospective superiority over competing laboratory strategies, reliable laboratory prediction across batches, or universally accurate physical structures. Zero-shot performance is also metric-dependent: ProTEUS ranked third by NDCG and second by top-10% recall across the held-out assays (Supplementary Text, p. 46).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Internal headline discrepancy:** The abstract claims greater-than-tenfold gains for both campaigns, and the introduction describes a 100-sequence budget. Detailed ALDH results instead report 150 variants and gains below tenfold (pp. 2, 4, 10–11; Table S5).
- **Author-stated data limitations:** Mutation measurements are noisy and sensitive to assay conditions; the authors describe the two enzyme campaigns as preliminary validation (Discussion, pp. 12–13).
- **Analyst inference—generalization scope:** The strongest pretraining correlation measures interpolation within assays. Moreover, 20 of 38 held-out reference proteins retain structurally related pretraining matches despite low sequence identity, so evaluation is not uniformly across unseen folds (p. 32; Supplementary Text, p. 45).
- **Comparison qualifications:** The initial MULTI-evolve comparator changes its published nomination procedure to rank fusion. Evolution simulations use one ProTEUS run versus multiple comparator runs, leaving ProTEUS seed variability unresolved (pp. 36, 42).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No matched prospective laboratory baseline or campaign-level ablation isolates benefits from representations, structural prediction, or nomination heuristics. Agent rationales were annotations only (pp. 9, 39–43).
- **Validity is operationally bounded:** “Physical validity” means satisfying seven specified checks to numerical tolerance; it does not establish complete physical correctness. Figure 3D’s caption and plotted speedup labels also describe different comparator references (pp. 26–27, 37; Table S3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does ProTEUS improve prospective experimental efficiency over simpler methods?** The laboratory campaigns lack matched competing strategies. Run parallel campaigns with equal assay budgets using ProTEUS, EVOLVEpro, and an additive model.
2. **Do candidate-specific structures improve engineering outcomes?** Modality ablations support structural context, but do not isolate the fast predictor’s downstream value. Compare WT-only, candidate-specific, and omitted structural inputs under identical nomination settings.
3. **How robust is extrapolation to unfamiliar folds and mutation positions?** Some held-out proteins retain fold similarity, and difficult higher-order comparisons were excluded (pp. 39, 45). Evaluate fold-separated targets and explicitly test substitutions at positions absent from training.
4. **Can embeddings reliably predict laboratory activity across rounds?** Exploratory laboratory readouts were negative or inconclusive (p. 46). Preregister repeated cross-batch evaluations against training-mean, one-hot, and parent-activity baselines.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed methods and supplementary results support the central benchmark findings, but headline inconsistencies and limited prospective controls weaken broader conclusions.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes exact numerical zero-shot macro-Spearman values, complete per-assay benchmark results, ProTEUS variability across repeated simulation seeds, full training hyperparameters and total computational costs, comprehensive laboratory replicate/error estimates, and an exact purified-ALDH fold improvement. Code and model availability are stated but unverified.
