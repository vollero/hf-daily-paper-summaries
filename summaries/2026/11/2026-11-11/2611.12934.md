# EarthRecord: From Pixels to Land Change with Multimodal Agents over Four Decades of Satellite Imagery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12934) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12934)


## One-sentence takeaway
EarthRecord finds that leading multimodal agents can analyze raw satellite archives, but struggle to map land change and distinguish sensor alterations from actual ground change.

## Problem and motivation
Land-change analysis requires selecting observations, masking clouds, scaling measurements, mapping imagery, and reconciling sensors across decades. The authors argue that existing benchmarks largely provide curated or preprocessed inputs and that final-answer accuracy alone cannot distinguish regional prior knowledge, pixel-based computation, and reliable interpretation of ground change (§1; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Raw-record benchmark:** 238 questions at 56 sites using Landsat, MODIS, VIIRS, and Sentinel-2 observations spanning 1984–2026, plus supporting geospatial data. Questions cover ten tasks and distinguish computable, recognition, and insufficient-evidence cases (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Evidence-source controls:** Closed-book evaluation removes the archive; 22 instrument pairs alter one satellite while preserving the reference ground condition; eight placebo pairs remove observations randomly (§2.5).
- **Agent evaluation:** Twelve multimodal agents use the same offline tools and resource limits. The main metric, **pass 3**, requires the conclusion to be correct in all three runs—not merely once. References combine method agreement, included and withheld products, and reviewed visual interpretations (§§2.3–2.4; §3.1).

The contribution is a benchmark and diagnostic protocol; the paper does not introduce a trained land-change model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Leading agents complete most benchmark analyses consistently | Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6.1 Sol, and Sonnet 5.5 achieve **81.0–85.4% pass 3** on 226 computable and recognition questions; reported intervals overlap. | §3.2 |
| Mapping is substantially harder than variable computation | Six leading models achieve **92.0% pass 3** on 98 record-variable questions versus **61.1%** on 78 imagery-mapping questions referenced to withheld products. Adjusting for model and task, mapping has correctness odds ratio **0.31**, 95% interval **0.14–0.66**. | §3.2; Figure 4; Appendix B.4 |
| Archive access improves computable answers | Across all twelve models, pass 3 on 153 computable questions rises **4.6–60.8 percentage points** over closed book. | Table 4; §4.1 |
| Some recognition failures reflect early abstention | On 41 recognition-mapping questions, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, and GPT-6.1 Terra abstain in **46.3%, 58.5%, and 66.7%** of runs; abstaining recognition runs mostly inspect no image. | §3.2; Appendix B.6 |
| Sensor alterations overturn previously reliable conclusions | Of **200 model–pair cases** correct on the original in all runs, **107 (53.5%)** become incorrect after alteration; placebo changes affect **8/66** eligible cases. | §4.2; Appendix C.4; Table 17 |
| Changed directions usually follow the alteration | **186/204 runs** with differing stated directions move in the alteration’s direction. Only **8/228 wrong runs** in moved cases attribute a break to the instrument. | Appendices C.4–C.5 |

These results support the authors’ diagnosis of mapping and sensor verification weaknesses under this protocol. They do not establish independent field accuracy: references are products, computational consensus, or reviewed imagery. Nor does a correct altered-pair answer prove sensor understanding; the authors explicitly separate answers explainable by closed-book success or a no-change reference (§§2.3, 2.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated resource scope:** The sandbox supplies general geospatial and classical machine-learning libraries on CPU. The authors identify pretrained vision models as a next step, so results do not establish performance with such assistance (§6).
- **Author-stated perturbation scope:** Instrument pairs test clear-cut, cross-sensor-detectable breaks, rather than subtle drift; all use computable questions (§2.5; §6).
- **Analyst inference—reference validity:** Product agreement and author-reviewed, AI-drafted interpretations are imperfect substitutes for independent ground observations. Questions near decision thresholds were replaced, limiting evidence about ambiguous boundary cases (§2.3).
- **Analyst inference—benchmark-development dependence:** The first model’s pilot informed reference auditing, removal of unstable questions, and selection of pass 3; that model was rescored rather than rerun. This complicates strict symmetry with the other models’ frozen-bank evaluations (§2.4).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic scope:** Failure coding uses sampled runs and submitted summaries, with partial author checks. It supports reported-behavior analysis rather than complete reconstruction of each agent’s reasoning (Appendices B.8, C.5).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Sites were selected for thematic signal and manually reviewed, and recognition questions concentrate in forest, fire, and urban themes; broad geographic coverage does not establish representative deployment performance (§§2.1–2.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Can pretrained vision tools close the mapping gap?** Their benefit is proposed but untested (§6). Compare identical agents with and without a fixed segmentation/change-mapping tool, measuring pass 3 and abstention.
2. **Can routine cross-sensor checks detect subtle drift?** Current pairs use clear breaks (§6). Test a mandatory reconciliation workflow against smaller, gradual alterations and unchanged controls.
3. **Can agents abstain more selectively?** Some decline answerable mapping questions before viewing imagery (Appendix B.6). Test required image inspection before abstention, tracking both recovered correct answers and newly introduced errors.
4. **How sensitive are scores to analytical definitions?** Different winter definitions reverse one snow conclusion (§3.3). Compare explicitly defined and underspecified question variants across repeated runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main findings and protocol, but many tables contain captions without their underlying values. Missing material includes exact model versions/settings, complete per-model scores and uncertainty intervals, full question/reference specifications, and executable evaluation details. Asset links are supplied, but release availability is not established: the conclusion describes release as forthcoming.
