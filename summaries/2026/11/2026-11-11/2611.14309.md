# MMDM: Measuring Massive Multitask Decision Making

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14309) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14309)


## One-sentence takeaway
MMDM benchmarks text-based, single-pass decision models and reports that strong single-choice accuracy can coexist with weak complete-set ranking, constraint handling, and probability quality (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Decision models assign probabilities directly to request-defined options. The authors argue that existing evaluations rely heavily on public test material and insufficiently combine long contexts, large option catalogs, and interacting decision conditions. This can obscure both generalization and weaknesses relevant to agent workflows (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 5,448 questions across 16 categories, combining source-preserving rewrites, semantic recompositions, and newly constructed mechanisms. Inputs contain a state, query, and options with optional criteria (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Single-select accuracy, complete-set multi-select ranking accuracy, Separation–Balance F-score (SB-F), Soft Brier error, and normalized expected-score error distinguish selection from probability quality (§3.1).
- **Construction and validation:** Executable checks, option-only shortcut probes, five-model screening, and human review of borderline cases support label verification (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Comparative study and assets:** Evaluation of 28 decision models identifies task-specific weaknesses; the authors state that they release the dataset and evaluation toolkit (§§4–5).

The reported matched-cohort results support a distinction between selecting correctly and assigning accurate probabilities. They also show that multi-select difficulty persists without catalog reduction. However, multi-select accuracy tests ordering at the **reference set cardinality**; it does not establish that models independently infer how many options to return (Appendix C.2). Scaling curves describe deployment conditions rather than isolated causal effects of length or option count.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation uses fixed textual states and option sets, excluding multimodal inputs and interactive trajectories. Described tools and workflows are not executed (Limitations; Ethics Statement).
- **Author-stated:** Multi-select questions concentrate in a few categories, and soft targets predominantly concern rubric scoring. Subjective human-assessment variation remains underrepresented (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Rewriting and recomposition do not eliminate possible prior exposure to public source material (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Model-specific truncation, option caps, and recursive grouping complicate aggregate comparisons. Native probability metrics can use different valid-response subsets; matched analyses partly address this (§4.1; Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Model screening may influence the retained task distribution. Human agreement is reported for borderline cases, rather than an independent audit of every automatically accepted item (Appendix B.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Can models infer complete-set size?** The evaluator uses reference cardinality, so autonomous set-size recovery remains untested. A next experiment could require explicit set outputs and separately score cardinality and membership.
2. **What causes scaling losses?** Released bins vary in task composition and capacity handling. A controlled experiment could vary irrelevant context length and catalog size around otherwise matched decisions (§4.5).
3. **How much does rewriting reduce contamination effects?** Source exposure remains possible. Compare matched source-preserving rewrites with newly constructed, independently verified mechanisms (Limitations).
4. **Do these scores predict interactive workflow success?** Tasks evaluate recorded or simulated decision points. Test the same prerequisites and revision rules in executable multi-step environments (Ethics Statement).

## Confidence and missing information
