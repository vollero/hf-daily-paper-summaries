# MACaQ: Fast, Model Agnostic Calibrated Quantization for Low-Precision Weights and Activations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10649) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10649)


## One-sentence takeaway
MACaQ combines layer-local online transforms with Hessian-aware FP4 refinement to accelerate calibrated quantization, with reported gains in quantization time, inference throughput, and memory efficiency, although several evaluation tables are missing from the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation
Weight–activation quantization reduces inference memory and computation, but the authors identify two practical obstacles: expensive calibration optimization and architecture-specific work to propagate basis transforms between layers. MACaQ retains lightweight activation transforms inside each linear layer, trading some inference overhead for independent calibration, simpler integration, and streaming quantization. (Introduction)

## Approach and contributions
- **Layer-local MXFP4 pipeline:** Apply per-channel scaling and a block-Hadamard rotation online; absorb their corresponding weight-side transforms offline. Independent layers enable streaming quantization without modifying neighboring operators. (Method: Problem Formulation; Quantization Procedure)
- **Hessian-weighted exponent selection:** Screen candidate shared block exponents using diagonal Hessian-weighted reconstruction error, then fix the selected exponents during refinement. (Method: Hessian-Weighted Exponent Selection)
- **Flip-Refinement:** Starting from round-to-nearest codes, greedily select improving moves to adjacent FP4 levels. Exact quadratic gain calculations and incremental gradient updates avoid repeatedly recomputing the objective. (Method: Flip-Refinement; Algorithm 1)
- **Implementation and extension:** The authors describe Triton kernels integrated with vLLM and extend refinement to complete codewords in llama.cpp weight-only formats. Scaling, Hadamard transforms, and coordinate descent have precedents acknowledged in Related Work; the contribution is their specific formulation and integration here. (Introduction; Related Work; Appendix 0.C)

The supplied numerical evidence supports a strong time–quality trade-off on Llama-3-8B and practical streaming quantization of the large MoE model. It does not independently establish the full cross-model quality ranking: Tables 1–6 and 8 have captions but no data bodies. Generative-language results and component ablation magnitudes therefore cannot be assessed. Image results establish a reported fidelity comparison to BF16, rather than a demonstrated improvement in prompt correctness or human preference.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Strict locality prevents cross-layer error compensation; MoE experiments quantize only experts; online-transform overhead reaches approximately **8%** on Qwen3-0.6B. (Limitations)
- **Author-stated:** Better layer reconstruction objectives need not yield better model perplexity. In the vector-quantized extension, scalar refinement performs best at 14B, and scale refinement improves calibration loss without improving the reported 4B perplexity comparisons. (Appendix 0.C: Results)
- **Analyst inference:** Hyperparameters selected on Llama-3-8B with WikiText training data may not transfer equally well to other architectures or calibration domains. Cross-domain sensitivity is not established. (Implementation Details)
- **Analyst inference:** Hardware portability remains uncertain: experiments use one GPU specification, without an explicit device model. Missing table bodies and absent release links also limit reproducibility. (Experiments; supplied text)

## Open research questions
1. **Can limited cross-layer correction improve quality while preserving fast quantization?** Locality excludes this capability; compare MACaQ against a lightweight second pass at matched total quantization time. (Limitations)
2. **How robust is calibration transfer?** WikiText supplies language calibration and hyperparameter selection; vary calibration domains and budgets while measuring held-out perplexity and generative accuracy. (Perplexity; Implementation Details)
3. **Does image fidelity translate into task quality?** The reported diffusion comparison measures similarity to BF16; add prompt-compliance evaluation and blinded human preference tests. (Image Diffusion)
4. **Which refinement objective best predicts downstream quality?** The vector extension shows objective–perplexity mismatches; compare held-out reconstruction criteria and downstream generation scores under matched refinement budgets. (Appendix 0.C)

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The algorithm and several numerical results are explicit, but material gaps include most table bodies, full generative scores, ablation magnitudes, numerical image metrics, exact GPU identity, diffusion calibration details, scale-clamping bounds, and public code/checkpoint availability.
