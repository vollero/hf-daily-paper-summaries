# Market-Conditioned JEPA: Learning from Unpredictable Market Shocks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13572) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13572)


## One-sentence takeaway
MC-JEPA uses other stocks’ realized future embeddings during training to improve standalone per-stock representations, with stronger evidence for exposure, risk, and liquidity gains than for return prediction (§§3–4; Appendix E).

## Problem and motivation
Per-asset prediction cannot anticipate all market-wide shocks from a stock’s history. The authors argue that treating these shocks as noise weakens supervision for market exposure and obscures asset-specific signals. Existing cross-asset approaches often target supervised returns and require peer information at inference; the proposed goal is cross-asset supervision during training with independent per-asset inference (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Theoretical motivation:** Under a linear latent-factor model, conditioning on the true realized factor makes both exposure and the asset-specific component necessary for optimal prediction. Unconditional prediction requires only their combined conditional mean (§3.2, Proposition 1; Appendix A).
- **Training method:** A Transformer context encoder predicts EMA target-encoder embeddings. MC-JEPA adds a leave-one-out mean of other assets’ future embeddings, routed through a low-dimensional factor and asset-specific loadings derived from the context representation. Conditional and unconditional losses share the base predictor; only the context encoder remains for downstream inference (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Empirical and interpretability contributions:** Frozen-representation probes, alignment controls, historical backtesting, and synthetic experiments assess the conditioning mechanism. Scale-restored predictor loadings provide label-free beta rankings, although raw loadings do not directly measure beta (§§4.2–4.5; Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results

All real-data probe results below use the chronological 2023–2025 test period; trained encoders are evaluated over three seeds (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad improvements over JEPA | Authors report higher RankIC on all nine main targets and best scores on six, covering returns through five days and all three liquidity targets. | §4.2; Table 1 referenced |
| Return superiority is incomplete | Ret 20d RankIC is **0.1389** for MC-JEPA versus **0.1454** for TS2Vec, using frozen-encoder ridge probes. | §4.2 |
| Statistical support varies by task | Against per-asset JEPA, **7/9** main-target gains pass unadjusted **5%** Newey–West tests. Ret 5d has **t=1.8**; no minute-return horizon is significant against JEPA. | §4.2; Appendix E, statistical-significance discussion |
| Alignment and factor structure matter | Different-date conditioning fails to reproduce return/exposure gains; the unfactored predictor scores below MC-JEPA on every main target. | §4.3; Table 3 referenced |
| Historical portfolio improvement | CSI300 long-only strategy using Ret 20d predictions, net of **5/15 bp** buy/sell costs: authors report highest AER and Sharpe and lowest MDD. Versus JEPA, excess return rises by **1.8 points** and MDD falls from **0.132 to 0.117**. | §4.2; Table 2 caption; Appendix D.3 |
| Exposure ranking needs scale restoration | Label-free loading projection reaches Beta 20d RankIC **0.368** after price-scale restoration, versus **−0.060** raw and **0.374** for a supervised representation probe. Trailing-beta features are stronger at **0.525**; adding the representation reaches **0.539**. | §4.4; Appendix F |
| Real-data denoising is modest | At training end on July–December 2022 validation data, default **K=1** conditioning removes **4.5±0.9%** of unconditional prediction error across horizons. | Appendix E; Figure 4 |
| Synthetic recovery improves | Across factor predictability settings, held-out-asset probe R² rises from **0.51–0.68 to 0.69–0.80** for exposure and **0.62–0.78 to 0.79–0.85** for the asset-specific component; five seeds. | §4.5; Figure 2; Appendix B |

These results support useful representations and an alignment-dependent training benefit in the tested market. They do not establish universal forecasting superiority: return significance is uneven, several exposure and daily-risk baselines perform better, and the synthetic study uses an exact factor model with observed factor/signal histories. The reported denoising measurements also leave part of the return improvement unexplained (Appendices B, E–F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Other markets, asset classes, and bar frequencies remain untested; A-share trading characteristics may affect results (§6).
- **Author-stated theory gap:** The proposition assumes a linear model and the true factor, while implementation uses an estimated peer condition. Averaging also relies on limited correlated idiosyncratic noise and non-cancelling factor effects (§§3.2–3.3, 6).
- **Task tradeoff:** The return-selected single factor underperforms larger factor counts on exposure/risk; additional daily-risk targets favor other methods (§4.3; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Foundation models are frozen, and some receive only close and volume rather than full OHLCV. These comparisons establish performance under the stated extraction protocol, not every adaptation strategy (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—deployment nuance:** The encoder operates independently, but the label-free exposure projection uses the date’s mean loading across stocks. That diagnostic therefore includes cross-sectional aggregation (Appendix F).
- **Evidence limits:** Significance tests are explicitly unadjusted for multiple comparisons. Backtest MDD uses cumulative arithmetic returns, and Sharpe excludes a risk-free rate (Appendices D.3, E).

## Open research questions
1. **Does conditioning transfer across market structures?** Generalization is untested (§6). Repeat the chronological protocol in another equity market and asset class.
2. **Can scale-preserving targets produce direct beta estimates?** Raw normalized loadings fail to rank beta well (§6; Appendix F). Compare normalization schemes against supervised probes and trailing-beta estimators.
3. **What explains return gains beyond denoising?** More removed error does not yield better returns (Appendix E). Match conditional error reduction across factor capacities, then compare downstream returns.
4. **Can richer factors improve risk without sacrificing returns?** Larger factor counts exhibit a task tradeoff (§4.3). Test separate shared-factor heads for return and risk objectives under matched compute.
