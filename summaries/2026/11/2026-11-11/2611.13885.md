# Are Causal Neuron Circuits Unique and Transferable?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13885) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13885)


## One-sentence takeaway
Exhaustively testing selected neuron groups shows that answer-changing interventions often admit smaller, sometimes disjoint sufficient subsets, while transfer and apparent content specificity depend on the task and intervention.

## Problem and motivation
Changing a model’s answer by intervening on a neuron group establishes causal influence, but does not establish that every neuron is necessary, that the group is unique, or that its effect generalizes. The paper measures these missing properties in pretrained language models and tests whether random-neuron controls can distinguish question-specific information from generic activation signals (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Exhaustive analysis within selected groups:** Rank neurons using first-order estimates and measured single-neuron effects, freeze 12 candidates, and evaluate all 4,096 subsets. Measure additivity, interactions, minimal sufficient subsets, disjoint alternatives, and transfer (§3).
- **Intervention-sensitive interpretation:** Compare zero ablation, mean ablation, resampling, and source-prompt patching. Capital-question experiments demonstrate that strong ablation effects can coexist with negligible effects from replacing activations with those from other questions (§4).
- **Cross-family two-hop experiments:** Patch first-hop activations into composed questions in Qwen3-1.7B, Llama-3.2-1B, and Gemma-3-1B, examining smaller sufficient subsets and transfer to rephrasings versus different chains (§§5–6).

The contribution concerns selected neuron groups; it does not recover complete circuit connectivity or test the motivating example’s original neurons (§1).

## Evidence and main results

Here, Δ/m₀ denotes intervention-induced margin reduction divided by the clean answer–alternative margin. Brackets give reported 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Ablation influence need not imply question-specific content | On 10 Qwen3-0.6B capital questions, selected-neuron zero ablation gives Δ/m₀ = 0.27 [0.26, 0.30], versus −0.00 [−0.00, 0.00] for random neurons. Resampling from questions with the same correct letter gives 0.00 [−0.01, 0.01]; with the other letter, −0.01 [−0.02, −0.00]. | §4; Fig. 1 |
| Capital-task effects generalize to held-out questions | The discovery-frozen group gives Δ/m₀ = 0.24 [0.22, 0.26] on 10 new questions, versus −0.01 [−0.01, 0.00] for the random group. | §4 |
| First-hop patching frequently favors the bridge | Full-group patching flips the answer–bridge margin in 10/11 Qwen, 10/11 Llama, and 8/10 Gemma chains; greedy text begins with the bridge in 9/11, 9/11, and 8/10. Matched random patching yields no flips. | §5.2 |
| Full groups are often unnecessary | Among chains flipped by the full group, smallest flipping subsets range from 1–6 neurons for Qwen, 1–8 for Llama, and 1–7 for Gemma. For Lion King specifically: 4, 8, and 7 neurons. | §5.3; Fig. 3 |
| Disjoint sufficient subsets occur within selected groups | Disjoint minimal flipping subsets exist in 3/10 Qwen, 7/10 Llama, and 7/8 Gemma full-group-flipped chains. These alternatives were assessed by margin, not generation. | §5.4 |
| Subset effects are approximately additive | Patching-group additive-fit R² is 0.90 [0.83, 0.92], 0.89 [0.70, 0.93], and 0.84 [0.58, 0.92] for Qwen, Llama, and Gemma; corresponding slopes are below one. | §5.5 |
| Transfer is stronger within a chain | Rephrasing subset-effect Spearman correlations are 0.98, 0.98, and 0.97. Median other-chain Δ/m₀ is 0.18, 0.09, and 0.11, versus own-chain 1.52, 1.89, and 1.82, respectively. Other-chain tests use each target chain’s own first-hop source. | §5.6 |
| Confidence association is exploratory | Across full-group-flipped model–chain cases, disjoint alternatives occur in 13/15 with clean margin below 4, versus 4/13 at or above 4. The cutoff was selected after inspecting results. | §5.4; Fig. 4 |

The evidence establishes local sufficiency and non-uniqueness **within the selected candidates**, rather than identifying globally necessary mechanisms. Rephrasing correlations establish stability of effect rankings, not necessarily identical generated answers. Furthermore, unrelated-chain activations also substantially disrupt Study 2 answers, so the patching effect cannot be attributed exclusively to matching bridge content (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Pilot-scale models and question sets; only the last prompt position is intervened on. The motivating larger model and original neuron group are untested (§8).
- **Author-stated selection bias:** Single-neuron and first-order selection may miss purely synergistic groups. Minimality and uniqueness are conditional on the chosen candidates (§8).
- **Author-stated controls and precision:** Only one random group per question; Study 2’s bfloat16 noise floor reaches 0.50 logits, potentially hiding small interactions (§§3.5, 8).
- **Author-stated outcome limits:** First-token margins and generation-prefix checks do not establish complete answer behavior. Disjoint alternatives lack generation validation (§§5.4, 8).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer uncertainty may be understated because bootstrap resampling does not adjust for rephrasings or source–target pairs sharing a chain (§3.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Competence filtering restricts conclusions to chains already answered correctly with sufficient margin; the experiments do not establish behavior on initially unsuccessful chains (§5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Do alternatives survive broader candidate searches?** Selection may exclude synergistic neurons. Compare current groups with groups selected using joint interventions, then enumerate feasible subsets.
- **Does confidence predict alternatives independently of chain identity?** The reported association uses a post hoc cutoff and confounds chains with margins. Prospectively vary confidence within fixed chains and repeat subset analysis (§5.4).
- **How much patching influence is bridge-specific?** Unrelated sources also disrupt answers. Compare matched bridges, mismatched bridges, and unrelated sources using both logits and complete generations (§5.2).
- **Do these patterns persist at larger scale and across positions?** Current experiments cover small models and one position. Repeat the protocol in larger checkpoints with interventions at earlier prompt positions (§8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this summary of the supplied text; the paper itself supports narrower, pilot-scale conclusions.
