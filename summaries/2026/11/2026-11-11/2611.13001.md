# One Table, Many Queries: Benchmarking Generalist Tabular Models Across Generation, Imputation, and Prediction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13001) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13001)


## One-sentence takeaway
OTBench finds that tabular generation, imputation, and prediction share utility-related capabilities, but objective benefits depend on the backbone and training context, and generalists’ utility advantages do not extend uniformly to distributional fidelity.

## Problem and motivation
Generation samples complete records, imputation recovers missing features, and prediction infers a target. The authors frame these as different queries to the same tabular distribution, yet existing task-specific evaluations leave unclear which capabilities overlap, whether their training objectives help one another, and when one model can replace specialists (§§1–2).

The practical gap is evaluation alignment: shared datasets alone are insufficient without explicit information-access contracts, comparable method support, and separate measures of fidelity, reconstruction, dependence, and predictive utility (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified benchmark:** OTBench contains 50 selected OpenML datasets and six separate structural-causal-model datasets, with shared outer train–test splits and explicit task contracts. It retains 20 capability axes rather than producing a universal score (§§2–3; Table 3).
- **Three complementary analyses:** Aligned method-rank correlations measure capability co-occurrence; matched objective interventions measure changes from adding objectives; fixed-method profiles compare deployable generalists against declared specialist portfolios (§§4–6).
- **Controlled objective study:** Five backbones are trained under all seven nonempty generation/imputation/prediction objective combinations on five datasets with three seeds, holding each backbone’s configured budget fixed across arms. Single-addition effects quantify objective additions; cross-addition effects quantify departures from additivity (§5).
- **Reusable evaluation assets:** The authors report public benchmark code, configurations, query adapters, and evaluation routines. The contribution is an evaluation framework and empirical findings, rather than a new model architecture (reproducibility statement, p. 14; §H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical imputation and prediction rankings align. | Under target-observed MCAR imputation at 30% missingness, numerical point accuracy correlates with classification hard decisions at pooled Spearman ρ = 0.50 and regression mean absolute error performance at ρ = 0.59; both preserve direction under effective single-method deletions. | §4; Figure 2 |
| Generation utility and fidelity relate differently to prediction. | On aligned method–dataset support, generation TSTR-gap performance correlates positively with classification hard decisions (ρ = 0.32), while generation fidelity correlates negatively (ρ = −0.19); both preserve direction under method deletion. | §4; Figure 2 |
| Including the recipient’s own objective gives the largest pooled gains. | Across the matched seven-arm study, self-inclusion is positive and exceeds both auxiliary effects on every displayed quality axis. Eleven self-inclusion estimates pass the complete robustness gate; imputation/prediction self-inclusion can also change inference policy. | §5.1; Figure 3; §E.2 |
| Prediction supervision offers an uncertain synthetic-utility benefit. | P→G TSTR-gap SAE is +0.092 normalized rank units, with 95% bootstrap interval [−0.041, 0.225]. No off-diagonal SAE passes the complete cross-backbone robustness gate. | Figure 4; §§E.2–E.3 |
| Objective gains need not add. | Seven G×I interactions anchored on P pass the robustness gate and are negative, including generation fidelity CAE = −0.468 and imputation numerical point accuracy CAE = −0.475, on the normalized seven-arm rank scale. | Figure 5; §E.4 |
| Some generalists outperform the declared utility portfolio. | On 26 common datasets, worst-task percentile gaps versus ARF/DiffPuter/Random Forest are +0.157 for TabICLv2 (95% CI [0.078, 0.229]) and +0.142 for TabPFN-3 ([0.051, 0.227]). | Figure 8; §§6, F.3 |
| Fidelity still favors the evaluated specialist portfolios. | The numerical-fidelity portfolio outperforms every generalist on its 11-dataset cohort. On the three-dataset categorical cohort, generalist-minus-portfolio mean gaps range from −0.396 to −0.261, with all bootstrap intervals below zero. | §6; Figure 8 |

These results establish capability-specific relationships and objective effects under the declared benchmark systems. Correlations do not establish transfer; rank changes are not raw accuracy gains; and the portfolio findings concern particular fixed triples and cohorts. Auxiliary transfer remains exploratory, while the negative interactions establish sub-additivity under the implemented systems rather than identifying its mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Truncated evidence:** The supplied source omits part of the middle appendices. This analysis therefore does not cover the complete paper.
- **Restricted intervention population:** The authors test five backbones and five datasets, including only one regression dataset, with some unavailable evaluations (§8).
- **Training–inference confounding:** Imputation/prediction self-inclusion and some interactions change conditioning policy. Fixed budgets also redistribute updates among objectives, preventing clean attribution to losses alone (§§8, E.1).
- **Contract and support dependence:** Rank analysis uses target-observed imputation; controlled transfer hides the target. Axis comparisons and profiles use differing available cohorts, with only three datasets in the categorical profile (§8).
- **Mechanisms remain untested:** Feature predictability, gradient alignment, objective overlap, and capacity competition are proposed explanations, not measured mechanisms. Matched pretraining and decoder ablations are also absent (§§5, 8).
- **Analyst inference:** Rankings and frontier membership should be read as conditional on the selected metrics, aggregation rules, and competitors; they do not establish universal model superiority (§§3, 8).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The abstract reports 46 registered methods, whereas Table 7 lists 45 selected identities; the supplied text does not explicitly reconcile these counts.
- **Deployment validity:** The authors state that privacy diagnostics provide no formal guarantee and aggregate performance establishes neither demographic fairness nor suitability for high-stakes use (ethics statement, p. 14).

## Open research questions
1. **Do auxiliary objectives help when training and inference are separated?** Conditioning-policy switches confound some comparisons. A next experiment would cross objective combinations with independently fixed inference policies (§8).
2. **What causes sub-additive objective effects?** Fixed budgets cannot distinguish overlapping information from update competition. Compare fixed total compute against fixed per-objective updates while measuring gradient alignment (§§5.2, 8).
3. **Can pretrained generalists improve categorical recovery and joint fidelity without losing utility?** These remain observed bottlenecks, but their architectural causes are unresolved. Run matched decoder and conditional-sampling ablations with frozen pretraining (§§6, 8).
4. **Do the findings generalize beyond the narrow intervention cohorts?** Regression transfer and categorical portfolio evidence have limited dataset coverage. Repeat the matched studies on broader regression and categorical populations (§8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central utility–fidelity distinction and the qualified objective-effect findings, but source truncation prevents complete coverage.

Material unavailable or unresolved information includes the omitted appendix material, complete per-dataset raw results, the reconciliation of registered versus selected method counts, and independent verification of the reported code assets. The paper also leaves optimization mechanisms and pretraining-versus-adapter contributions unresolved.
