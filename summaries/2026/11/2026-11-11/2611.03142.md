# DepthGraft: Structural Regularization through Hierarchical Cross-Layer KV Reconstruction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03142) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03142)


## One-sentence takeaway
DepthGraft reconstructs deep-layer keys and values from distributed shallow–middle source pairs, reporting improved language-model benchmarks and roughly one-third less KV cache, with ablations supporting a benefit from cross-layer gradients (§§3.4, 4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that deep Pre-LN Transformers dilute earlier information through the residual stream, limiting useful depth scaling. Explicit cross-layer connections can address this but add computation and memory traffic. Existing KV-sharing methods primarily target inference efficiency; the authors identify an underexplored question: how sharing topology changes backward gradient flow and KV representation geometry (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Gradient-based interpretation:** Every KV reuse connection also sends gradients directly to source KV projections. The authors propose that useful sharing concentrates keys into common retrieval directions while expanding the value content subspace. Effective-rank measurements and conditional spectral analysis support this interpretation (§3.2; Appendix C).
- **Hierarchical reconstruction:** Divide depth into approximately equal shallow, middle, and deep stages. Each deep layer combines stride-aligned shallow and middle KV using learned channel-wise coefficients. This distributes reuse gradients across sources rather than concentrating them on global anchors (§3.4).
- **Efficient implementation:** Deep layers store no independent KV cache. Key coefficients are tied within RoPE rotation pairs; prefill processes only the final prompt token through the deep stage after source caches are complete. Suitable PP/VPP placement keeps source–reuse dependencies local (Appendices B, F).

## Evidence and main results
All values below are author-reported; benchmark gains are absolute score points unless stated otherwise.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Dense-model gains | Average benchmark accuracy/pass-rate score improves over matched baselines by +0.75, +0.85, and +2.42 points at 0.5B, 1B, and 1.5B parameters. | §4.2, Table 1 discussion |
| MoE gains across attention variants | GQA average gains: +1.05, +2.29, and +3.61 points at 3B, 9B, and 16B total parameters; 9B MLA gains +3.68 points. | §4.2, Table 2 discussion |
| Strong individual benchmark gains | On 16B GQA MoE, MMLU-Pro accuracy improves +7.00 points, CMMLU +6.61, C3 +6.41, and GSM8K +5.84 versus its baseline. | §4.2 |
| Cross-layer backward paths help | In the 16B A3B ablation, shallow-copy average score is 42.86 with detached reuse versus 45.30 with gradients enabled; GQA baseline is 42.38. | §4.3, Table 4 discussion |
| Two-source reconstruction helps further | In that ablation, DepthGraft averages 47.56 versus 45.30 for shallow-copy and 43.58 for two-stage single-source copy. | §4.3 |
| Cache and prefill savings | Approximately one-third less KV cache under equal per-layer KV dimensions. At 128K context with 2K generated tokens, TTFT falls 26.8% for 9B MLA and 28.8% for 16B GQA; TPOT overhead stays below 1% across tested 8K–128K contexts. | §4.2; Appendix F.1 |
| Long-context gains | After 32K continued training, 16B A3B RULER averages are 94.8 versus 86.8 at 4K and 82.4 versus 76.2 at 32K, DepthGraft versus baseline. | §4.4, Table 6 discussion |
| Gains persist after SFT | Matched SFT on 16B A3B models yields average scores of 52.00 for DepthGraft versus 48.52 for baseline. | Appendix D.1 |

The matched-training comparisons support improvements in the tested settings, and the detach ablation specifically supports a contribution from backward reuse paths. Effective-rank and attention-sink observations are consistent with the proposed mechanism (§3.2; Appendices E.1–E.2). **Analyst inference:** these findings do not isolate representation geometry as the sole cause of downstream gains or establish universal superiority over other topologies.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical scope:** Appendix C is a local, conditional analysis requiring spectral alignment and assumptions about update covariance; it does not guarantee monotonic effective-rank changes under arbitrary training.
- **Author-stated systems scope:** Communication locality requires suitable PP/VPP placement; imperfect depth divisibility can introduce imbalance (Appendix F.3).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** The corpus is primarily proprietary/private. The supplied extraction includes table captions but omits table bodies, including configuration and hyperparameter tables, preventing a complete audit of training budgets and comparisons (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** YOCO/FusedKV match DepthGraft’s reuse ratio, whereas CLA/MLKV use three-layer sharing groups. Consequently, the topology comparison does not hold cache reduction constant across every method (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and scope:** No repeated-seed results or uncertainty estimates are supplied. Quality evaluation extends through 32K, whereas latency is measured through 128K; the latter does not establish quality at that length (§§4.2, 4.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does KV geometry causally mediate the gains?** Diagnostics and detach ablations support the interpretation but do not isolate it. Next experiment: independently control key/value gradient paths and test whether geometry changes predict benchmark gains.
2. **Which topology works best at a fixed cache budget?** Stage ablations favor the proposed layout in one main setting. Next experiment: sweep source distance and fan-in across scales while matching cache, training tokens, and compute.
3. **Do quality gains persist beyond the tested context range and model scale?** Larger pretraining is future work, and quality beyond 32K is unreported. Next experiment: train larger matched models and evaluate retrieval and reasoning at longer contexts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported outcomes are clear, but full table values, training-token budgets, detailed model configurations, efficiency hardware/kernel settings, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability are unavailable in the supplied text.
