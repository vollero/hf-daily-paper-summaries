# DyCraft: An Agentic System for Long-Horizon 4D Scene Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40754) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40754)


## One-sentence takeaway
DyCraft combines verified 3D storyboards, motion authoring tools, and coding agents to generate long animations, reporting fewer geometric violations and stronger human preference than two baselines on DyBench’s 24 tasks (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Long animations require consistent geometry, contact, staging, and motion across many frames. The authors observe that general coding agents often script entire animations in one pass, producing intertwined errors that are difficult to repair. They identify a gap between existing static scene or short animation systems and long, multi-shot scenes involving characters and interacting objects (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Verified pose-to-pose workflow:** Expand prompts into scene and motion briefs, construct the scene, verify key 3D storyboard states, then author connecting motion. This adapts traditional animation practice into an agent workflow (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Integrated authoring and verification harness:** Extend general coding tools with articulated CAD authoring, motion guidance, simulation support, visual inspection, and per-frame checks for penetration, support, and joint limits. Failed checks normally require repair, with exceptions allowed as a fallback (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Character and motion infrastructure:** Provide searchable rigged assets, optional motion-capture references, and retargeting helpers; these support animation rather than requiring characters to be built from scratch (Appendix A.2).
- **DyBench:** Introduce 24 long-horizon, multi-shot text-to-4D tasks spanning robot, human/animal, and physical-mechanism scenes (§4.1; Appendix C.1).

The contribution is principally a system and workflow integration; pose-to-pose animation, CAD, simulation, and motion capture are existing ingredients.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Human judgments favor DyCraft | Preferred over VIGA in **89.7%** and bare agent in **72.3%** of binary judgments across realism, physical plausibility, and motion quality. Study: **109 raters**, **1,538 recorded judgments**. | §4.3; Appendix B.2 |
| Lower penetration rate | Across **24 films**, mean fraction of frames with a dynamic mesh penetration exceeding **5 mm**: DyCraft **13.0%**, bare agent **80.1%**, VIGA **74.9%**, subject to stated exclusions. | §4.4 |
| Lower floating rate | Across **24 films**, mean fraction of dynamic body-frames without a valid ground-support path: DyCraft **2.7%**, bare agent **15.7%**, VIGA **26.1%**. | §4.4 |
| Components have qualitative support | Removing prompt rewriting, storyboarding, motion tools, or verifiers produces planning or plausibility defects; numerical ablation results are absent from the supplied text. | §4.5; Fig. 8 |
| Demonstrated scope includes varied long animations | Authors report coherent minute-long scenes involving characters, rigid bodies, cloth, and liquids; comparisons illustrate omitted events and weaker baseline motion. | §4.2; Fig. 7 |

All experiments use GPT-6-Astra within Codex (§4). These results support improved judged quality and the specified geometric metrics on DyBench. They do not establish complete physical correctness, performance on other agent models, or quantitative gains attributable to individual components. The preference percentages aggregate judgments across questions rather than measuring objective task completion.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported residual failures:** Penetration remains around held objects and layered parts (§4.4).
- **Metric scope:** Penetration evaluation excludes static construction overlaps, connected structural parts, liquid/effect meshes, and consumed food. Support is graph reachability; **analyst inference:** this cannot by itself establish balance, force consistency, or realistic dynamics (§4.4).
- **Limited generalization evidence:** Evaluation covers one underlying model and one introduced benchmark. **Analyst inference:** transfer to other models and task distributions remains untested (§§4–4.1).
- **Component attribution:** Supplied ablations are qualitative, limiting conclusions about effect sizes and component interactions (§4.5).
- **Resource and comparison uncertainty:** Runtime, token usage, compute budgets, repeated-run variability, and baseline access to equivalent character/motion resources are not specified.
- **Incomplete reporting in retrieved text:** Referenced tables lack their numerical bodies; full rewriter instructions, benchmark prompts, and baseline guidance are announced but absent. Section 4.3 also points to Table 2 despite Table 1 being captioned as the preference table.

## Open research questions
- **Which components deliver the largest gains?** Qualitative ablations do not quantify contributions (§4.5). Next experiment: repeated, budget-matched ablations measuring preference, violations, and task completion.
- **Do geometric checks improve dynamic correctness?** Current metrics address penetration and support connectivity (§4.4). Next experiment: independently evaluate balance, contact forces, and causal responses on generated scenes.
- **Does the workflow transfer across coding agents?** All reported experiments use GPT-6-Astra (§4). Next experiment: evaluate the same tasks and harness with multiple models under matched resource budgets.
- **How much do retrieved assets and motion references contribute?** These resources support character animation, but their contribution is not isolated (Appendix A.2). Next experiment: compare matched runs with and without retrieval and motion-capture access.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the system and aggregate findings, but omits uncertainty estimates, per-task numerical results, quantitative ablations, compute costs, repeated-run details, complete prompts/instructions, and confirmed code or asset release status.
