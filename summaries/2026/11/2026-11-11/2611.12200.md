# Learning to Act with World Models by Valuing What Comes Next

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12200) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12200)


## One-sentence takeaway
WM-PPO improves sparse-reward text-agent training by using PPO’s critic to value world-model-predicted successors, turning outcome-calibrated comparisons into auxiliary policy advantages and critic targets while deploying only the policy (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Terminal success rewards provide little guidance about which intermediate actions establish prerequisites or prevent completion. Predicting the next observation supplies dynamics information, but does not establish whether that change helps the current policy succeed. The authors address this gap by applying PPO’s existing critic to predicted successors of both executed and unexecuted candidate actions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Success-oriented consequence evaluation:** A separate world model learns next-observation prediction from the agent’s executed transitions. PPO’s critic values predicted successors for sampled alternatives, producing “transition values” (§3.1).
- **Two learning channels:** Candidate rankings generate auxiliary advantages within PPO’s clipped objective; their mean becomes a fixed auxiliary critic target. Real returns remain the critic’s main supervision (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Outcome-calibrated reliability:** Real branches compare alternative actions from a shared state. Recent outcome differences determine margin-bin weights shared by both channels, reducing feedback from unreliable comparisons (§§3.4–3.5).

The contribution is this training combination; successor valuation and model-based value targets themselves are situated within prior work (§6). The policy, critic, and world model use separate LoRA adapters on a frozen backbone, without a teacher or simulator-state access; only the policy runs at evaluation (§3.5).

## Evidence and main results

Results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher ALFWorld success than PPO | Final iteration 36, policy-only greedy success averaged over three runs: WM-PPO **87.5%** versus PPO-160 **63.5%**; post-warm-up real steps **102k versus 105k**. | §4.2; Table 2 caption |
| Higher ScienceWorld success than PPO | Final iteration 60, three-run mean greedy success: **52.9% versus 39.3%** for PPO-160, **p<0.001**; real steps **128k versus 113k**. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 2 caption |
| Strongest tested baseline remains competitive | ScienceWorld greedy success: WM-PPO **52.9%**, best of three SO-PPO settings **46.5%**; authors report **+6.5 points**, **p=0.004**. Rounded displayed rates imply 6.4 points. | §4.2 |
| Critic-target benefit depends on environment | Adding the value channel gives **+5.8 greedy percentage points** on ScienceWorld, **p<0.001**; ALFWorld is **87.5% versus 88.4%** without it, not significant. | §4.2 |
| Comparisons improve a policy acting alone | ALFWorld diagnostic: one offline PPO-clip epoch using consequence-based labels raises temperature-1 policy-only success **57.1%→65.3%**, **p=0.0003**; majority-vote labels yield **56.9%**, with matched data and updates. | §2.3; Fig. 1c |
| Reliability is conditional | Late ScienceWorld training: largest-margin preferences agree with better outcomes in **84%** of branch pairs with differing outcomes. Conversely, final critics rank true-successor pairs near chance: **47–54%** on two sets of states from 60 procedural evaluation games, using five continuations per candidate. | §5.1; Fig. 5a; Appendix F.4, Fig. 16 |
| Interaction efficiency costs more training compute | At comparable real steps, iterations take **1.3–1.6×** PPO-160’s wall-clock time and **2.7–3.8×** its GPU-hours. | §4.3 |

The evidence supports improved policy success and interaction efficiency in the tested settings, with structural controls supporting the auxiliary comparisons (§5.2). It does not establish general critic-ranking accuracy or compute efficiency. Real-step budgets are approximate: ScienceWorld uses more interaction than PPO-160, and the accounting excludes shared warm-up and prefix replay calls (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Main evaluation uses one 4B backbone, two text environments, and a 40-action limit. Branching requires reproducible prefixes or saved states (§7).
- **Author-stated estimation risks:** One greedy prediction omits outcome uncertainty; longer-term effects depend on the critic. Prediction errors can bias both channels, and comparisons cover only sampled actions (§7).
- **Author-stated sampling issue:** Branch actions are sampled conditional on differing from the chain action, without correcting the update for that conditioning (§7).
- **Analyst assessment:** Strong training-margin agreement should not be generalized to arbitrary evaluation states, given the near-chance final-critic diagnostic in Appendix F.4.
- **Limited robustness evidence:** Gate ablations use one seed; some baselines receive fewer runs. Significance tests are uncorrected for multiple comparisons (§4.1; Fig. 9).
- **Behavioral qualification:** Behavioral analyses use non-bit-exact greedy re-runs; pooled improvements do not hold in every run, and premature-focus behavior varies by task type (Appendix F.5).
- **Reproducibility and coverage:** Release is promised rather than established. The supplied source is truncated, with substantial appendix material and table bodies unavailable.

## Open research questions
1. **Does calibration work in stochastic environments?** Deterministic replay and single predictions limit current evidence (§7). Compare sampled successor distributions and saved-state branching under controlled transition noise.
2. **How can small-margin decisions receive useful feedback?** Many receive little weight (§7). Test additional continuations or uncertainty-aware calibration specifically on these decisions.
3. **Why do strong training comparisons coexist with weak final-critic rankings?** Appendix F.4 leaves this unresolved. Evaluate successive checkpoints on a fixed shared candidate pool with more continuations per action.
4. **Are gains retained under equal compute budgets?** Training costs exceed PPO-160’s (§4.3). Compare success at matched GPU-hours, including replay and warm-up costs.
