# Visual Similarity in Adult Word Extension and Artificial Word Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14116) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14116)


## One-sentence takeaway
Frozen visual embeddings predict adult word extension and artificial word-learning difficulty, but teaching-example composition adds explanatory information, and the evidence does not establish a shared human–machine mechanism.

## Problem and motivation
The paper asks how much visual similarity explains which objects share a word, and what the composition of teaching examples contributes beyond similarity. Its identified gap is an independently defined, reproducible similarity measure applicable to the exact images adults saw and to a controlled artificial learner (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adult reanalysis:** Compute frozen DINOv2-small distances between test images and teaching-example centroids, then model word extension using public adult responses, controlling for taxonomic level and teaching condition (§2).
- **Controlled learner study:** Compare prototype, nearest-neighbor, and logistic-regression learners on naming words assigned to single categories or two-category unions; separate embedding distance from WordNet relatedness (§3). The paper summarizes these experiments, with fuller details attributed to earlier project work.
- **Cross-system tests:** Distinguish alignment between embedding geometry and human similarity/confusability from prediction of learner difficulty using human-derived distances; add an exploratory CVCL encoder comparison (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Greater visual distance predicts less adult extension | Across 520 people and all 24 test items, adjusted log odds decrease by **−3.226 per distance SD**, 95% CI [−3.288, −3.164], Holm *p* < .001. Target-domain-only estimate: **−3.16**, CI [−3.30, −3.02]. | §2.4; Table 2 |
| Teaching composition contributes beyond the measured distance | Descriptive superordinate-item selection: **1.1%** after three subordinate examples versus **77.5%** after three superordinate examples; respective mean cosine distances **.892** and **.845**. Exploratory superordinate-only models find condition improves fit given distance. | §2.4; Fig. 1a |
| DINOv2 learners find distant category unions harder | Prototype naming accuracy in 3-way, 5-shot episodes: native **94.3%** [92.2, 96.1]; near **84.7%** [83.2, 86.1]; mid **65.4%** [63.7, 67.2]; far **53.0%** [51.0, 55.0]. Intervals bootstrap 30 episodes; chance ≈33%. | §§3.1–3.2; Fig. 2 |
| Embedding distance predicts pair-level learner difficulty beyond the semantic control | Across 100 CIFAR-100 pairs, perceptual-distance coefficient **β = −.212**, *p* = 1.87 × 10⁻⁷, partial R² = .245; WordNet distance adds no detectable prediction, *p* = .660, partial R² = .002. | §3.2; Fig. 3 |
| Embedding geometry partially aligns with human measures | Spearman ρ: THINGS **.523** over 3,400 pairs; ImageNet-HSJ **.501** over 1,390; CIFAR-10H **.795** over 45. All reach the permutation-test resolution limit; reported Holm *p* = .0005 is a bound. | §4.2; Fig. 4 |
| Human distances do not conclusively predict learner difficulty | Controlling semantic distance: THINGS **β = −.008**, *p* = .374, n = 61; ImageNet-HSJ **β = −.034**, uncorrected *p* = .039, Holm *p* = .079, n = 32. Authors classify H2 as inconclusive. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Encoder robustness is mixed | CVCL descriptively reproduces CIFAR-10 tier ordering, but CIFAR-100 pair distance does not predict accuracy: **β = .0047**, CI [−.018, .028], *p* = .688. Its baseline naming accuracy is lower: **.618 versus .850** with DINOv2. | §4.2; Fig. 5 |

The adult results establish an adjusted association, not a causal effect of visual similarity. The artificial results show that the learner’s own geometry can produce a difficulty gradient. Human representational alignment does not establish equivalent learning predictions or a common mechanism; the authors explicitly distinguish these claims (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ limitations:** Human data are secondary and correlational; taxonomic level and distance are structurally related. The all-item adult coefficient is dominated by taught-domain versus other-domain differences (§§2.4, 5.1).
- **Exploratory evidence:** Composition interactions and post hoc models require caution. Trial order varies between experiments and is confounded with other design differences (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Restricted learner scope:** Frozen encoders, final-layer CLS features, CIFAR images, shallow learners, and two-category words limit generalization. Tier intervals measure episode variability rather than generalization over category pairs (§§3.1, 5.1).
- **Limited cross-system tests:** Human-distance prediction samples are small; human measures mix perceptual and conceptual information. CVCL fails to reproduce the pair-level effect (§4).
- **Modeling and reproducibility:** Adult models omit by-image distance slopes; preprocessing alternatives were not tested. The internal analysis plan was not externally registered, and its hash does not establish timing (§§1, 3.1, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Null semantic-control results provide limited evidence about broader semantic effects because the learner lacks semantic input, the measure is narrow, and within-superclass pairs are sparse (§§3.2, 5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Do humans show the same category-union difficulty gradient?** Human extension and artificial naming use different tasks. Next experiment: teach children or adults near/mid/far unions using the learner’s exact images and measure held-out naming (§6).
- **Which representation properties produce the pair-level effect?** CVCL reproduces tiers but fails the pair-level test. Next experiment: compare encoders, intermediate layers, and patch features under matched learning settings (§6).
- **What teaching information exceeds centroid distance?** Composition predicts extension beyond this measure. Next experiment: manipulate example diversity while matching centroid distance and taxonomic level (§§2.4, 6).
- **Can human distances predict difficulty with adequate coverage?** H2 remains inconclusive with limited pair samples. Next experiment: expand category-pair coverage and collect similarity judgments and learning outcomes for the same images (§§4.2, 6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings and their stated qualifications; causal and cross-system interpretations remain limited.
