# Is Your Robot&#x27;s Memory Just a Clock? Temporal Shortcuts in Memory-Augmented VLA Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14488) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14488)


## One-sentence takeaway
Controlled simulation interventions show that time-indexed VLA memory can mistake idle intervals for task progress, while event indexing substantially reduces—but does not eliminate—the resulting early stops.

## Problem and motivation
Memory-dependent manipulation requires tracking completed events, such as repeated picks, rather than merely observing the current scene. The authors identify a shortcut: regularly paced demonstrations let elapsed time predict progress, and uniformly sampled memory exposes that cue through both timestamps and frame occupancy.

Existing benchmark success can therefore conceal weak event counting. The paper tests whether physically inconsequential pauses and motions cause policies to report progress that never occurred, termed **phantom progress** (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic intervention protocol:** Inject pauses, wrist fidgets, and returning air-grasps; compare events visible to memory against physically identical events whose frames and elapsed timestamps are hidden (§§3–4).
- **Mechanism analysis:** Rescale timestamps, retrain matched Clock/NoClock models, and manipulate memory-slot contents to distinguish explicit clock dependence from effects of time-sampled frame occupancy (§5; Appendices E–F).
- **Comparison across memory designs:** Test whether vulnerability follows temporal sampling, pixel-change selection, or sub-goal indexing, with additional probes of independently developed policies (§5.4; Appendix G).
- **Test-time event-index intervention:** Collapse stillness and qualifying state-return loops, then recompute memory positions without retraining. The authors present this as a causal diagnostic intervention, not a new architecture or deployable detector (§§2, 6; Appendix I).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Pauses sharply reduce counting success | Released FrameSamp-Modul, two 5-second pauses, 50 paired episodes per task: PickXtimes **47→16**, SwingXtimes **44→10** successes. | §4; Fig. 2 |
| Failures predominantly indicate phantom progress | Same pause setting: **34/34** PickXtimes failures and **36/40** SwingXtimes failures were early stops; stage-logged rerun reproduced outcomes. | Appendix B |
| Explicit timestamps influence stopping | PickXtimes, released FrameSamp-Modul, unchanged frames and no events, 50 paired episodes: multiplying timestamps by **1.5** reduced success **47→18**, with every failure an early press; scaling by **0.5** instead induced extra repetitions. | §5.2; Fig. 4 |
| Removing timestamps does not solve the problem | NoClock under two 5-second pauses achieved **21/50** PickXtimes and **14/50** SwingXtimes successes; its no-event results were **42/50** and **47/50**. | §5.3; Appendix F.3–F.4 |
| Demonstrations support a temporal shortcut | Time-only completed-repetition prediction achieved **89%** accuracy on PickXtimes and **80%** on SwingXtimes, using five-fold cross-validation over demonstrations, roughly twice the majority-label baseline. | §5.1; Fig. 3; Appendix D |
| Event indexing substantially improves robustness | Same Clock checkpoint, 50 paired episodes: pause success **17→34** on PickXtimes and **10→30** on SwingXtimes; returning air-grasp success **13→43** on SwingXtimes. | §6; Table 30 |
| Gains extend to held-out probes | Released FrameSamp-Modul, simulator-contact detector, seven held-out probe settings with 50 paired episodes each: pooled success **118→243/350**; **6/7** probes significant, with one-way air-grasp the exception. | Appendix I.4; Table 34 |
| Tested pause augmentation sacrifices clean performance | Same-machine control versus strong augmentation, 50 episodes: no-event success **32→24** on PickXtimes and **46→19** on SwingXtimes; visible-pause success **16→25** and **11→10**, respectively. | Appendix H.1; Table 28 |

The paired visible/hidden controls, timestamp interventions, and slot-content controls support a causal role for memory representation in the studied policies. They do not establish that every time-indexed policy fails identically: SimpleMemVLA shows task-dependent pause sensitivity, and some hidden conditions retain unexplained deficits (Appendices B.1, G).

Real teleoperation data establish a time–progress correlation, not real-robot reliance on that correlation or deployment failure (Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Detailed causal analysis centers on MME-VLA; two independent policies receive narrower probes. All policy experiments use simulation and scripted events (§7).
- **Detector realism and evaluation:** Silhouettes and forces come from simulation, sensor noise is unmodeled, and tolerances were developed on test-split episodes before being frozen for held-out replication (Appendix I.1–I.2).
- **Incomplete fix:** Event indexing can erase genuine repetitions, misses some non-returning excursions, and provides little significant recovery for randomized micro-stops. Duration-dependent tasks require preserving elapsed time (Appendix I.6).
- **Mechanism remains partly unresolved:** The specific feature of identical occupied slots that NoClock uses is unknown; linear decodability does not demonstrate policy use (Appendix F.5–F.7).
- **Variance and exploratory evidence:** Most trained variants have one seed, although Clock/NoClock receive a second-seed confirmation. Training and evaluation variation complicate small differences. MIKASA counting results failed the authors’ competence gate and remain exploratory (§7; Appendices A, J).
- **Analyst inference:** The failed augmentation variants do not rule out better data-level remedies; the authors did not train with simulator-rendered faithful moving null events (Appendix H.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can event indexing work with real sensors?** The detector relies on simulator stand-ins. A next experiment could replace these with calibrated silhouettes and measured forces, then test noisy real-robot pauses and fidgets (Appendix I.2).
- **What exactly does NoClock read from null slots?** Occupancy controls identify a causal effect but not its internal feature. Independently varying slot order, block continuity, and frame similarity could distinguish candidate mechanisms (Appendix F.7).
- **Can memory retain genuine repetitions while ignoring incidental loops?** State return can collapse legitimate progress. Compare contact-aware indexing with task-conditioned event tracking on clean counting episodes and returning null events (Appendix I.6).
- **Can faithful augmentation preserve counting and handle micro-stops?** Tested augmentation fails, while rendered moving nulls were not tried. Train with physically valid simulated null events and varied pacing, then evaluate clean counting and unseen randomized stops (Appendices H.2, I.6).
