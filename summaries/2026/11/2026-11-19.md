# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-19

## Executive summary

- ALIVE and BLIP-Live train video assistants to decide when to speak using the stream observed so far. ALIVE also handles changing requests and asks for missing information. BLIP-Live explicitly penalizes redundant replies (`2611.21366`, `2611.21553`).
- LogT, NexSight, and AVTR make question-relevant visual evidence part of the evaluation. NexSight reports better temporal evidence recall at a fixed frame budget, while the answer-accuracy advantage over fixed windows remains uncertain (`2611.19200`, `2611.22790`, `2611.24161`).
- The shopping study found that higher requested reasoning effort often raised stated confidence, with only one low-to-high accuracy gain surviving multiple-comparison correction. AnyS1 found cost and latency savings when a small model handled selected workflow decisions under warm calibration (`2611.22033`, `2611.23751`).
- Two quantization studies tie deployment decisions to task outcomes. On one laptop, moving from a 1.90 GB to a 3.00 GB code model file raised energy per solve without a statistically significant solve-count gain. QDA improved reasoning benchmark averages within fixed precision and serving-kernel constraints (`2611.17605`, `2611.23388`).
- AnisoFlow lowered FID on a matched pixel-space backbone by changing the prediction target. DWD tested a separate final pixel head, which replaces a standalone decoder in the tested latent models (`2611.17767`, `2611.22287`).
- StrideWorld found that steady motion under a held command can coexist with scene deformation or missed endpoints after command changes. WorldRuler tests a separate question: whether visible rule execution survives save and reload (`2611.24408`, `2611.24869`).
- Unfinished-Bench found larger increases in rule violations under persistence when the rules were in internal documents. IDEA and PIPETTE report defensive results under distinct evaluations of GUI actions and untrusted tool output (`2611.17460`, `2611.24102`, `2611.24362`).
- BiCAT and HINT-WAM report improved simulated manipulation success using different training and action representations. Their RoboTwin 2.0 task selections and evaluation protocols limit direct comparison (`2611.23499`, `2611.24789`).

## Main results worth noting

- **Rule placement and persistence.** On matched Unfinished-Bench workplace scenarios across 12 open models, harness-driven persistence raised the average violation rate by 11.4 percentage points for internal-document rules and 3.8 points for the same rules stated by the user, relative to runs without that persistence condition (`2611.17460`).
- **Timing and excess replies.** BLIP-Live-Qwen scored 49.08 PAUC-F versus MMDuet2’s 34.01 on ProactiveVideoQA. This metric combines quality-weighted timeliness with firing precision and counts every emitted reply in the precision denominator (`2611.21553`).
- **Useful retrieval credit.** ECA-Horizon improved strict macro exact match over matched outcome-only GRPO by 1.03 percentage points at 3B and 4.80 at 7B across seven Search QA benchmarks. The likelihood-based credit is applied during training (`2611.23824`).
- **Prediction target on a matched backbone.** AnisoFlow reduced 600-epoch FID from 1.90 to 1.55 on class-conditional ImageNet 256 × 256 with the same JiT-H/16 backbone. The default targets and AnisoFlow gave comparable FID in the two tested latent models (`2611.17767`).
- **Restore versus execution.** On DeepStateBench, changing the start state while retaining Agent S3’s pixel policy raised end-to-end completion from 0% to 10.4%. The 27.5% StatePortal result also used an application hook to execute the target action (`2611.23257`).
- **Search cost in simulation.** CriticNPU reported best-or-tied-best latency in 74% of prefill and 87.5% of decode settings, with about half as many simulator calls as Bayesian optimization and greedy search. Both the latency outcomes and the search comparison come from the simulated design study (`2611.24733`).
- **Consolidation cost with logs already available.** Chronological expert replay took 9 hours versus 29 hours for MOPD + GRPO on eight H100s and gave the highest reported overall student score, 58.6 across 16 held-out math, search, and SQL benchmarks. The clock starts after the experts have been trained (`2611.24059`).
- **Manipulation under a matched starting policy.** HINT-WAM raised mean simulated LIBERO-90 success from 78.88% under supervised fine-tuning to 85.18% on held-out initial configurations. Its RoboTwin evidence and BiCAT’s ten-task RoboTwin result answer separate protocol-specific questions (`2611.24789`, `2611.23499`).

## Open research directions

1. **Test when better visual retrieval improves the answer.** Replay identical retrieved frames to systems trained with single-frame and multi-frame supervision. On annotated long-video questions, score evidence recall and answer accuracy separately, especially for counts that span several windows (`2611.22790`, `2611.24161`).
2. **Measure intervention timing under changing requests.** Use identical causal streams with overlapping, edited, and canceled requests. Report correct timely replies, duplicates, and missed interventions by request state, with human checks on a held-out subset (`2611.21366`, `2611.21553`).
3. **Measure the value of confidence-based deferral.** On the same held-out candidate lists, compare direct choice, thresholded fallback, and higher reasoning effort. Report accuracy at matched acceptance coverage, calibration, latency, and episode cost (`2611.22033`, `2611.23751`).
4. **Quantify which improvements a cheap screen misses.** Freeze candidate pools and sweep the screening budget before independently reevaluating selected and rejected candidates. For skills, use repeated held-out rollouts. For quantization, add long-generation correctness and truncation rates to proxy rank agreement (`2611.23068`, `2611.23388`).
5. **Check whether restored worlds behave correctly after the first action.** Replay identical action sequences from saved and uninterrupted states. Check restoration and subsequent transitions separately, with dense human event annotations where brief visible effects may be missed (`2611.23257`, `2611.24869`, `2611.22673`).
6. **Isolate action representation and hindsight supervision.** On the same simulated tasks, split, and demonstration budget, vary per-arm coupling and future-frame teacher supervision independently. Repeat policy training and report success and action latency by task (`2611.23499`, `2611.24789`).
7. **Evaluate authorized completion when approval is available.** Vary whether an authorized approver can be reached in matched Unfinished-Bench scenarios. Track protected-action violations, verified authorized completions, and compliant stops as distinct outcomes (`2611.17460`).
8. **Find out whether retained short replies improve dialogue models.** Train matched models on Kezek and baseline tracks under both full-recording and matched-coverage conditions. Score backchannels and turn-taking on held-out conversations, stratified by overlap and speaker loudness (`2611.16933`).
