# WebHarbor: Docking Real Websites for Evolving GUI Agent Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03634) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03634)


## One-sentence takeaway
WebHarbor provides task-scoped, human-audited replicas of real websites for reproducible GUI-agent evaluation and RL, with one trained 4B agent outperforming its live-web-trained reference on the reported transfer benchmarks (Tables 6 and 9).

## Problem and motivation
Live websites introduce content drift, bot detection, access restrictions, and difficult resets, while many controlled or synthetic environments offer limited website coverage, visual content, or authenticated workflows. The authors seek environments that combine realistic browser interactions with repeatable state and offline execution (§§1–2).

The identified gap is the joint provision of broad coverage, image-rich interfaces, explicit login, and persistent state changes—not merely functional website generation (Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Environment and task suite:** 153 Dockerized website environments containing 3,518 tasks, including account management, bookings, purchases, and other persistent operations (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Construction pipeline:** Tasks scope reconstruction; coding agents implement local interfaces, assets, databases, and workflows; humans audit fidelity, correctness, and task validity. New tasks drive further environment expansion (§§3.1–3.3; Appendix B).
- **Evaluation infrastructure:** Frozen SQLite seeds support controlled resets; task-specific verifiers inspect answers, browser evidence, and database changes, with a separate agent judge providing audits (Appendix E.2).
- **Training demonstration:** WebHarbor-4B reuses OpenWebRL’s SFT initialization, MM-GRPO algorithm, browser harness, and GPT-4.1 reward while replacing the task pool and execution environments. This is an environment contribution rather than a new RL algorithm (§4.1; Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial authenticated and stateful coverage | Of 3,518 tasks, 1,117 require authentication (31.8%), 1,253 change persistent state (35.6%), and 1,888 require cross-page reasoning (53.7%); classifications are LLM-based. | Table 2 |
| Improved live-web transfer | Excluding invalid trials, WebHarbor-4B scores 81.2% on WebVoyager and 70.4% on Online-Mind2Web, averaging 75.8%, versus 70.6% for OpenWebRL-4B and 53.6% for their shared SFT initialization. | Table 6 |
| Improved transfer to an independent environment family | On the 226-task WebArena subset, WebHarbor-4B achieves 28.8% success versus 20.4% for OpenWebRL-4B; without automatic login, scores are 26.1% versus 16.8%. | §4.1; Table 9 |
| Modest official gains on the local benchmark | On 306 WebHarbor test tasks, WebHarbor-4B achieves 30.0% verifier success versus 29.1% for SFT and 19.7% for OpenWebRL-4B. Agent-judge rates are 41.5%, 35.5%, and 25.7%, respectively. | Table 8 |
| Relative rankings preserved in a small comparison | Across four agents on WebVoyager and corresponding WebHarbor sites, all six pairwise rankings agree; Spearman correlation is 1.000 and Pearson correlation is 0.802. Replica scores are 4.4–18.8 points higher. | Table 5 |
| Repeatable execution has measured operational latency | Mean initialization, reset, and verification take 7.43, 6.68, and 4.04 seconds, including data-transfer and Orchard-management overhead. | Table 7 |

These results support the usefulness of the suite for training and controlled evaluation, with transfer beyond its training environments. They do not isolate whether fidelity, authentication coverage, task composition, or another environment property causes the gains. Live-web baselines in Table 6 are largely imported results, and invalid-trial exclusion leaves uncertainty about directly comparable task denominators.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Replicas cover audited tasks rather than complete services; interactive maps and real-time behavior may use approximations or static substitutes. Synthetic performance does not guarantee equivalent live-web behavior (Limitations; Ethics Statement).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Verifiers and model judges can reject valid alternatives or miss subtle failures. Only one 4B model is trained, without systematic comparisons across RL algorithms, SFT data, or harnesses (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No reported component ablation separates human review, imagery, authenticated tasks, and environment breadth. Shared optimization settings narrow the comparison but do not isolate these factors (§4.1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** Test tasks were selected partly through verifier–judge agreement, and the agent judge can inspect verifier code and verdicts; agreement therefore offers limited independent validation (§3.4; Appendix E.2).
- **Internal inconsistency:** The prose claims GPT-6-Astra exceeds 95% under both judging measures, but Table 8 reports verifier rates of 86.7% and 83.0% for its two harnesses. The table does not support the stated saturation claim (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—fidelity evidence:** Asset counts and selected visual examples establish implementation breadth, but do not quantify representative visual or behavioral fidelity against original services (Table 4; Figures 1 and 6–8).

## Open research questions
- **Which environment properties produce transfer gains?** The paper lacks component ablations. Train matched policies while varying authentication tasks, image fidelity, and human refinement separately.
- **Does the benefit generalize across model scales and families?** Only one 4B model is trained. Repeat matched live-versus-replica training across several architectures and sizes.
- **How accurate are the evaluation signals?** Agreement-based task selection and judge access to verifier outputs limit independence. Run blinded human audits on both retained and rejected tasks, including alternative valid solutions.
- **How well do replicas support workflows outside their construction tasks?** Reconstruction is explicitly task-scoped. Evaluate newly authored workflows and navigation paths before expanding the environments.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text documents the pipeline, training recipe, and principal results in detail, but some conclusions exceed the comparisons shown.

Material missing information includes exact live-web valid-task denominators for each model, uncertainty estimates for most headline scores, matched training-cost comparisons, detailed component ablations, quantitative fidelity audits, and independent human estimates of verifier accuracy. Public asset availability is reported by the authors but was not independently checked.
