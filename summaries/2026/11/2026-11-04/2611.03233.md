# Axion 1.0: Open Agent Foundation Models, Simplified

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03233) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03233)


## One-sentence takeaway
Axion 1.0 reports competitive scientific reasoning and tool-use performance from two data-focused SFT stages followed by RL, although its comparisons do not establish that more elaborate training pipelines are unnecessary (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Agent-model training increasingly combines many specialized stages, making reproduction and attribution of capability gains difficult. The authors also identify incomplete disclosure of data construction and training procedures in otherwise open models. They investigate whether carefully constructed data and a compact training sequence can deliver broad agent capabilities while exposing the development recipe (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Model-aware reasoning data:** Generate harder scientific problems from public seeds, then strengthen problems the base model solves using composition, parameterization, range changes, or case analysis. Independently verify reference answers and retain correct solution trajectories (§2.1).
- **Verifiable tool-dependent data:** Construct questions through deterministic filtering, unique-entity selection, and computation over structured sources. Withhold construction databases, remove questions solved without retrieval, and collect verified tool-use trajectories (§2.2).
- **Progressive training:** Apply self-contained reasoning SFT, tool-dependent SFT, then GRPO. RL rewards correct answers and dependency-ordered intermediate progress, with a penalty for failing to submit an answer (§3).
- **Open development recipe:** The authors claim release of model weights, data-construction procedures, prompts, filtering, verification, and training code. Infrastructure combines long-trajectory SFT with asynchronous RL; TITO and routing replay are adopted consistency mechanisms rather than presented as newly invented algorithms (§§1, 5–6).

## Evidence and main results

All scores below are author-reported. Their harness reports the best single run from three runs per benchmark, or five for BMB; externally sourced baselines may use different settings (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| HLE results depend materially on website access | Full-HLE scores for 35B / 122B: **52.2% / 59.0%** with restricted websites; **59.1% / 64.8%** without those restrictions. | §4.2; Table 1 caption |
| Competitive information seeking at 35B | SEAL-0: Axion-35B **62.2%**, Agents-A1 **56.4%**; cross-source execution settings may differ. | §§4.1–4.2 |
| Strong FrontierScience Research result | Axion-35B / 122B: **56.7% / 66.7%**, versus Agents-A1 **40.0%** and GPT-5.6 Sol **63.3%**. | §4.2 |
| Commercial models remain ahead on BMB | Human-difficult subset: Axion-122B **41.7%**, GPT-5.6 Sol **47.1%**, Claude Fable 5 **46.1%**; benchmark-approved websites only for the authors’ evaluations. | §§4.1–4.2 |
| SFT produces substantial checkpoint gains | For 35B, HLE-130 rises **47.7% → 59.2% → 66.2%** from base through Stage I and Stage II; BMB changes **23.5% → 17.6% → 35.3%**. | §4.3; Table 2 caption |
| RL has mixed effects | For 35B, Stage II → RL improves SEAL-0 **53.2% → 62.2%**, while FrontierScience Research declines **58.3% → 56.7%**. | §4.3 |
| SFT training cost is quantified | Both SFT stages together: approximately **90 GPU-hours** for 35B and **240 GPU-hours** for 122B, using **32 GPUs** and a **128K-token** context window. These figures exclude RL and data construction. | §5.1 |

The evidence supports the effectiveness of this particular recipe across the evaluated reasoning and information-seeking settings. Sequential checkpoints show associated gains, but do not isolate individual data operations or establish superiority over equally resourced alternative pipelines. HLE-130 is a separate text-only subset and must not be compared directly with full HLE (§§4.1, 4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ qualifications:** Cross-source baseline comparisons are indicative, not strictly controlled; checkpoint comparisons are not component-removal ablations; model-size comparisons mix architectures and RL exposure (§§4.1, 4.3–4.4).
- **Authors’ qualifications:** Routing-replay attribution is confounded by asynchronous policy staleness and context condensation, and downstream benchmark effects vary (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Best-run reporting without run distributions or uncertainty estimates limits assessment of typical performance and small score differences (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied experiments do not isolate model-aware strengthening, the SFT ordering, or intermediate-progress rewards, leaving their individual necessity unresolved (§§2–4).
- **Analyst inference:** A compact stage count does not establish low total development cost: reported SFT costs omit data generation and RL, whose infrastructure requires separate rollout, trainer, and service resources (§5).

## Open research questions
1. **Which data-construction ingredients drive gains?** The checkpoint comparisons do not isolate task strengthening or closed-book filtering. Next experiment: remove each ingredient separately while matching training tokens and compute (§§2, 4.3).
2. **Does intermediate-progress reward improve RL?** No final-answer-only reward comparison is supplied. Next experiment: compare the proposed reward against outcome-only GRPO with identical task pools and rollout budgets (§3.3).
3. **How robust are the reported advantages?** Best-run reporting and differing website access leave typical performance uncertain. Next experiment: evaluate models in a shared harness with matched access rules and report all-run means and uncertainty (§§4.1–4.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative supports the method and selected results, but table bodies and figure values are missing despite their captions being present. Material unavailable information includes complete benchmark matrices, detailed hyperparameter tables, dataset counts and source inventories, run-level variability, total RL/data-generation cost, and concrete release locations or licensing terms.
