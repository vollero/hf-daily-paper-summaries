# Muon Makes the First Mixture-of-Experts Layer Harder to Balance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37914) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37914)


## One-sentence takeaway
In small MoE language models, Muon on the backbone roughly doubled the first layer’s balancing-bias span at rates tuned on shorter runs, while reducing its learning rate or centering router inputs substantially mitigated the effect (§§5.1–5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Expert loads can appear balanced because routing biases compensate for shared preferences, concealing how strongly the router favors particular experts. The author studies this hidden balancing burden and whether Muon should optimize the router as well as the backbone. The identified gap is that existing recipe reports disagree about router optimization without controlled comparisons, while prior optimizer studies do not measure the balancing bias itself (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic:** Uses bias span—the largest minus smallest expert bias—to measure the compensation required within each layer, alongside overload, input cosine similarity, and load churn (§3).
- **Controlled attribution:** Separately assigns Muon or AdamW to routers and backbone components in models with 55M and 108M active parameters, using comparisons paired by initialization and training batches (§4).
- **Mechanistic and mitigation evidence:** Tests whether shared router-input directions explain early overload through input-centering probes, learning-rate changes, and faster bias updates. Also compares router optimizers with the backbone held on Muon (§§5.2–5.6).

These are primarily empirical diagnostics and findings; batch centering is a short-run intervention rather than an established deployment method (§6.2).

## Evidence and main results
Unless otherwise specified, span values average logged measurements after the 500-step warmup; each experiment compares three paired seeds (§§3.4, 4.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| First-layer balancing burden increased despite better loss | Small model, 1B tokens: first-layer span **0.194 with Muon versus 0.084 with AdamW**; final validation cross-entropy lower by **0.078** with Muon. | §5.1; Fig. 1; Table 3 discussion |
| The effect followed the backbone | Small-model ablation, 1B tokens: first-layer span **0.198/0.175** with a Muon backbone and Muon/AdamW router, versus **0.099/0.095** with an AdamW backbone. | §5.2; Table 4 discussion |
| Centering removed early overload in small-model probes | First 3,000 steps, seeds 3–5: early busiest-expert load fell from **3.30–4.66×** fair share to **1.11–1.16×**; step-3,000 loss changed by **−0.0012 to +0.0009**. | §5.3; Fig. 2 |
| Lower Muon rate removed most excess span | Small model, 1B tokens: halving Muon’s rate reduced excess first-layer span over AdamW from **0.103 to 0.024** and lowered final validation loss versus full-rate Muon by **0.008–0.012** across seeds. | §5.4 |
| Imbalance persisted in the larger model | 108M-active-parameter model, 3B tokens: Muon increased first-layer span by **0.152**, or **112%**, versus AdamW. During the final 10,000 steps, overload remained **2.2–2.8×** under the two Muon-backbone partitions versus **1.07×** under AdamW. | §5.5; Appendix C.4 |
| Moving only the router to AdamW did not help at tested scale | With a Muon backbone, warmup churn increased at every seed on both sizes and with added balance loss; final validation loss increased by **0.001–0.004**. | §5.6; Table 6 discussion |

The evidence supports a backbone-dependent first-layer effect under the tested recipes. Centering provides intervention evidence that the shared input direction contributes to early overload. It does not explain every large span: partial-backbone configurations produced substantial spans without similarly strong shared directions (§5.3). Nor does increased span establish worse language modeling or measured throughput loss: Muon improved validation loss, and these models had no capacity limit (§§5.1, 6.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One architecture family and training dataset, two small model sizes, one batch size and warmup, sigmoid routing, and no capacity limit. The larger model also changes relative expert width, so it is not a pure scaling comparison (§6.3).
- **Learning-rate sensitivity:** Rates were selected on shorter runs. Half-rate Muon performed better on the longer small-model runs; AdamW was not retuned at that horizon, and lower-rate large-model runs were not performed (§6.3).
- **Statistical uncertainty:** Three seeds per comparison, no correction for multiple comparisons, and some single-seed exploratory measurements. The author explicitly warns that reported ranges understate uncertainty (§§4.4, 6.3).
- **Intervention limits:** Centering was tested only in the first router for the first 3,000 steps. Batch centering makes token routing depend on batch composition; running-mean centering remains untested (§6.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Loss-spike co-occurrence with near-ceiling span is insufficient to establish that span or overload caused the spikes (§§5.6, 6.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the effect persist after tuning for the full training horizon?** Short-run tuning materially affects the result. Next experiment: retune both optimizers at the final token budget, including lower Muon rates on the larger model (§6.3).
2. **Why does the whole Muon backbone create a shared direction?** Partial-backbone ablations do not isolate the responsible interaction. Next experiment: vary shared-expert optimization independently alongside attention and routed-expert optimization (§6.1).
3. **Can running-mean centering improve full-run routing?** Only batch centering in short probes was tested. Next experiment: compare running-mean centering, batch centering, and unchanged routing throughout training (§§6.2, 7).
4. **Does overload create practical costs at scale?** The experiments measure neither distributed expert bottlenecks nor capacity-related token dropping. Next experiment: repeat comparisons with distributed experts and explicit capacity limits, measuring throughput, dropped tokens, and loss (§6.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-setting findings; **low** in extrapolation to production-scale training.

The supplied retrieval includes detailed prose but omits numerical table bodies and plotted graphics, preventing inspection of every reported seed range. Full-horizon retuning, long-run centering results, downstream task evaluations, measured distributed-training costs, and a causal explanation for the shared direction are unavailable.
