# What We Keep of What We Forget: Replacement Operators and Routing Semantics in Training-Free MoE Expert Pruning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41366) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41366)


## One-sentence takeaway
Fixed-mask experiments show that training-free MoE pruning depends strongly on preserving the router’s trained gate scale, while a fitted low-rank replacement improves some models without reliably preserving generation under general-text calibration.

## Problem and motivation
Expert pruning reduces the memory needed to store MoE language models without retraining. However, removing experts requires both choosing a pruning mask and deciding what happens when routing would select a removed expert. The authors argue that published comparisons usually change both decisions, obscuring the contribution of replacement behavior and gate scaling (§1).

The identified gap is a controlled comparison across replacement families, router conventions, selection masks, and evaluation protocols. The authors acknowledge earlier work separating selection from workload assignment; their novelty claim concerns the breadth of this controlled comparison.

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled replacement study:** Hold masks, calibration tokens, and evaluation fixed while comparing constants, input-dependent maps, survivor-based replacements, compressed expert copies, and rerouting. The main models are OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, and Moonlight; Granite supplies an additional routing test (§3).
- **Tilted novice:** Replace a removed expert with its mean output plus a low-rank, clipped input map fitted through closed-form reduced-rank ridge regression using streamed statistics. The constant replacement is its rank-zero case. The authors explicitly claim a new application and formulation, rather than a new regression estimator (§§1, 3).
- **Routing semantics:** Compare rerouting with the original router’s gate scale against rescaling over survivors, identifying an empirical “trained-scale rule.” Preserving the original scale also requires retaining removed experts’ router rows (§§3, 5).
- **Evaluation analysis:** Cross selection masks and operators; examine generation, answer bias, calibration domain, parameter budgets, and quantization. Paired item-bootstrap intervals and Holm correction support the principal comparisons (§§3–6). A reporting checklist is announced, but its full contents are unavailable in the truncated source.

These results support treating replacement and routing semantics as explicit algorithmic choices. They do not establish a universally best operator, preservation of open-ended generation, or equal runtime efficiency. The statistical intervals primarily capture test-item uncertainty, and several domain-specific comparisons are exploratory.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope:** One-shot, training-free pruning of the tested base models; only two endpoint gate scales are compared. Gate-scale contrasts cover multiple choice and perplexity, not generation (§§1, 5.3).
- **Calibration uncertainty:** The principal calibration slices are disjoint portions of one stream, and refits usually retain the original mask. This measures operator variability conditional on a mask, rather than uncertainty across the entire pruning pipeline (§3).
- **Limited replication:** Granite and calibration-domain experiments use one relevant calibration draw; mask crossing covers only half-pruning on two models. Several copy, generation, and fitted survivor-operator results remain exposed to calibration noise (Appendix I.2).
- **Comparison scope:** MoRA, ConMoE, and COMPEL are reimplemented components with documented deviations. Their results should not be read as evaluations of complete published pipelines (§3; Appendix H.6).
- **Measurement risks:** Near-chance performance and fixed-answer bias complicate high-pruning results. Post-hoc equivalence bounds are not prespecified equivalence tests (§§3, 6.2; Appendix H.8).
- **Analyst inference:** Architecture and training differences prevent attributing model-specific operator rankings solely to router convention. Memory comparisons also leave meaningful compute differences (§§4.1, 5.3).
- **Source limitation:** The supplied text is truncated, so this analysis does not provide complete paper coverage.

## Open research questions
1. **Can intermediate gate scales outperform the trained endpoint?** Only endpoints were tested (§5.3). Sweep interpolation scales under fixed masks and evaluate both multiple choice and generation.
2. **What predicts whether tilt, neuron copies, or rerouting will win?** Rankings vary across models, and expert reconstruction quality does not consistently predict accuracy (§4.3). Compare these operators across additional architectures with matched storage and measured latency.
3. **Can calibration preserve several generative domains simultaneously?** General-text calibration damages generation, while task-specific calibration changes masks and capability tradeoffs (§§6.1, 6.4). Test mixed general, mathematics, and code calibration with repeated mask construction.
4. **Why does rerouting lose more relative benefit under quantization?** The authors suggest a precision-related mechanism but do not test it (§6.5). Vary replacement precision and expert precision separately at matched storage.
