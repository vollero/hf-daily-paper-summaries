# FinForesight: A Diagnostic Backtesting Benchmark for Deep Research Systems in Financial Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01621) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01621)


## One-sentence takeaway

FinForesight finds that stricter retrieval controls bring resolved post-cutoff financial backtests closer to live forecasting scores in a narrow US evaluation, while exposing historical score advantages and prediction sensitivity (§§4.3–4.5).

## Problem and motivation

Historical forecasting evaluations can reward remembered outcomes or retrieved information published after the simulated forecast date. Live evaluation avoids direct access to unresolved outcomes but delays feedback. The authors identify a need to test whether controlled historical backtests can provide timely, credible feedback for financial research agents—and to distinguish forecasting performance from calculation ability and input sensitivity (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark and generators:** Four outcome-based tasks—valuation, return ranking, event direction, and future fundamentals—plus separate counterfactual and calculation diagnostics across Chinese, US, and Hong Kong markets. The suite contains 1,119 records; code and benchmark data are reported publicly available (Abstract; §§3.2, 4.1).
- **Time-controlled evaluation framework:** Instances depend on declared model knowledge cutoffs. T1 covers pretraining-era outcomes, T2 resolved post-cutoff outcomes, and T3 forecasts recorded before resolution. Mediated tools log evidence access; retrieval policies range from unrestricted search to point-in-time controls plus an LLM leakage filter (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Temporal comparisons, retrieval interventions, prompt rewrites, and numerical perturbations probe sources of apparent forecasting performance. Overall averages only the four forecasting tasks; capability diagnostics remain separate (§§3.3, 4.1; Appendix A.7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Historical scores usually exceed post-cutoff scores | T1 exceeds T2 in 25 of 27 RANK/EVT/FUND comparisons: three models × three tasks × three markets, using FinanceHarness; all RANK and FUND gaps are positive. | §4.3; Table 2 discussion; Appendix B.2 |
| Retrieval controls improve aggregate backtest–live agreement | Across nine US model–task pairs with FinanceHarness and matched one-week windows, mean absolute T2–T3 gap falls from **0.1203** with search to **0.0618** with PIT and **0.0285** with PIT plus filtering—approximately **76%** below search alone. Only T2 retrieval settings vary against a shared T3 reference. | §4.3; Appendix B.2, Table 6 discussion |
| Filtering is not uniformly the closest policy | Filtering gives the closest agreement for every EVT/FUND comparison, but PIT alone gives the smallest RANK gap for Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. | Appendix B.2 |
| Wording changes individual predictions more than numerical variants | On T2 EVT/FUND across three markets, two models and two harnesses yield Prompt Change Rates of **16.83–23.67%**, versus Perturb Change Rates of **7.00–9.17%**; every configuration’s mean signed Score Delta has magnitude below **0.0026**. | §4.5; Appendix B.4 |
| Perfect capability diagnostics do not ensure stronger forecasts | Claude Sonnet 4.5 with Claude Code scores **1.0000** on CF and CALC, but forecasting Overall is **0.6292**, compared with **0.6797** using FinanceHarness. Overall equally averages the four pooled forecasting-task scores. | §4.2; Appendix B.5; Appendix A.7 |
| Model and harness strengths depend on the task | Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite leads Overall and EVT within each research harness; Phi-4 leads VAL. FinanceHarness has the highest mean Overall across shared models, but does not consistently win for individual models. | §4.2; Table 1 discussion |

These results support controlled T2 backtesting as a useful diagnostic proxy in the evaluated settings. They do not establish universal backtest–live equivalence. The authors interpret T1 advantages as evidence of historical knowledge effects; **analyst inference:** comparisons across different periods do not independently prove memorization. Likewise, a small signed robustness delta can conceal offsetting gains and losses across individual predictions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Historical evidence reconstruction is approximate; current pages, snippets, metadata, and archives may differ from information actually accessible at the forecast date (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Cross-market differences in disclosures, language coverage, vendors, and release timing prevent clean causal conclusions about market difficulty (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** T2 remains a retrospective proxy requiring empirical validation against T3 (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** The central retrieval-policy comparison is limited to matched one-week US windows, three models, FinanceHarness, and RANK/EVT/FUND. Its generality across markets, harnesses, horizons, and VAL remains unestablished (§4.3; Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Leakage-detection precision/recall formulas are supplied, but measured filter accuracy is unavailable. Improved score agreement alone does not demonstrate complete leakage removal (§3.3; Appendix B.1).
- **Evidence availability:** The retrieved text includes table captions and narrative summaries but omits table bodies, limiting independent verification of configuration rankings and per-comparison results.

## Open research questions

1. **Does T2–T3 agreement persist across markets and horizons?** The strongest comparison uses short US windows. Repeat prospective paired evaluations across CN/HK, longer horizons, additional harnesses, and VAL (§4.3).
2. **Does filtering remove leakage without discarding useful admissible evidence?** Filter accuracy and evidence-retention measurements are absent. Audit retrieved pages against independently verified historical snapshots and measure both leakage detection and retained evidence quality (§3.3).
3. **Can T2 feedback improve subsequent live forecasts?** The authors propose this as future work. Compare systems developed with controlled T2 feedback against matched systems without that feedback on locked prospective T3 instances (§5).
4. **How consequential is prediction instability despite stable mean scores?** Individual outputs change substantially while signed mean deltas remain small. Measure absolute score changes, worst-case degradation, and repeated-run variability under the same interventions (§4.5; Appendix B.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative clearly supports the benchmark design and reported aggregate findings, but missing table bodies constrain verification.

Material unavailable details include per-regime and per-market sample counts, exact live evaluation dates, statistical uncertainty and repeated-run variability, measured leakage-filter accuracy, the filter’s exact prompt and decoding settings, robustness thresholds and valid-pair counts, and comparable runtime or tool-use budgets.
