# Can AI Reviewers See Your Efforts to Improve Your Paper? A Large-Scale Study of Paper Revision Trajectories

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02244) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02244)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across eight AI reviewers, most annotated major manuscript improvements receive unchanged scores, while unsupported user opinions can erase observed revision advantages, although unchanged scores alone do not prove failure to recognize improvements (§§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
Do AI review scores reflect substantive improvements authors make between manuscript versions? The authors argue that prior work on feedback quality, local edits, and rhetorical rewriting does not establish whether independent scores track scientific improvements in real revision trajectories. They address this gap by separating revision content from overall-quality judgments rather than assuming later versions are better (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Revision dataset:** PaperTrajectory-2026 contains 6,959 accepted-paper trajectories and 9,092 revision pairs from ICLR, ICML, ACL, CVPR, and ECCV. Retained versions are more than 30 days apart; 9,066 pairs have completed annotations (§3.1).
- **Separate change and quality annotation:** GPT-6 Astra compares complete manuscripts and appendices across 14 revision dimensions, supplying evidence and an independent overall-quality judgment. Five graduate-level researchers validate a sampled subset (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Trajectory analysis:** The study characterizes the evidence and framing changes associated with major improvements and compares early versus late revisions (§4).
- **Reviewer evaluation:** Eight models independently review anonymized versions without revision history. The authors analyze score responsiveness, reviewer agreement, revision-type associations, held-out prediction, and sensitivity to praise or criticism (§§5–7; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Validation supports improvement presence more strongly than its degree | Humans rated 2,760/2,800 change descriptions accurate (98.6%); every assigned evaluator confirmed some improvement for 74/75 major-labeled pairs (98.7%). Exact quality-category agreement on duplicated pairs was 62.0%, versus 88.0% for improvement/no improvement. | §3.3; Appendix B.5 |
| Major improvements involve broader changes | Major-improvement pairs average 7.9 substantially changed dimensions versus 3.1 for minor improvements. | §4.2; Table 8 referenced |
| Later revisions are narrower | Among 1,712 papers with multiple transitions, first-to-last mean major-change dimensions fall from 5.6 to 2.9; major-improvement share falls from 22.5% to 11.2%. | §4.3; Figure 3(c) |
| Most major improvements retain their score in pooled results | Across reviewers’ available assessments from 1,484 major-improvement pairs, 55–78% retain their earlier OA. The majority-unchanged pattern also holds for each reviewer on its available human-confirmed subset. | §5; Figure 4; Appendix E.3 |
| Score-direction agreement is weak | On 1,414 common revisions, pairwise Cohen’s κ ranges from −0.071 to 0.170; 385 receive an increase from one reviewer and a decrease from another. | §6.1; Table 1 |
| Revision indicators offer limited prediction | Paper-grouped five-fold evaluation using twelve varying indicators yields AUC 0.51–0.64 and relative Brier-loss reductions of −1.6% to 3.3% against training-prevalence predictions. | §6.2; Figure 6; Appendix F.6 |
| Criticism can remove an observed revision advantage | Among GPT-5.5’s complete-condition pairs with a positive neutral gap, criticism of the revision produces a strict reversal in 54.3% and a tie in 43.5%. | §7.1; Figure 7(a) |
| Opinion stability varies substantially | With criticism of the revision and praise of the earlier version, Astra reverses 7.1% of its positive neutral gaps and ties 45.8%; GPT-5.5, Sonnet, and Haiku reverse about nine in ten or more. | §7.2; Figure 7(b) |

The evidence establishes limited responsiveness of discrete scores under the tested independent-review protocol, weak directional agreement, and substantial framing sensitivity for several reviewers. It does **not** establish that unchanged scores necessarily reflect unnoticed improvements: the paper presents a case where a reviewer acknowledges added evidence but retains its rating because other concerns remain (§5). Pooled results also mask conference differences; some reviewer–conference settings have higher-score rates above half (Table 13).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ caveat—quality-label strength:** Human confirmation accepts either slight or clear improvement, so it validates improvement presence rather than the automatic “major” designation. Subsequent analyses use automatic labels (Appendix B.5).
- **Authors’ caveat—generation variability:** Each manuscript is reviewed once per condition. Selecting positive neutral gaps can select unusually large gaps that would shrink on another neutral review; repeated neutral reviews are needed to quantify the additional instability attributable to opinions (Appendix G.1).
- **Authors’ caveat—observational associations:** Revision coefficients do not estimate causal effects of edits. Feature selection was exploratory, and multiplicity correction excludes that selection process (Appendices F.3–F.4).
- **Authors’ caveat—uncertainty scope:** Prediction intervals hold fitted predictions and folds fixed, excluding refitting and fold-assignment variability. Opinion comparisons use different positive-gap subsets for different reviewers (Appendices F.6, G.3).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Accepted papers with qualifying public arXiv histories cannot establish behavior for rejected papers, other disciplines, or closely spaced revisions (Appendices B.1–B.2).
- **Analyst inference—annotation dependence:** Astra supplies quality labels and is also a tested reviewer. Sampled human validation provides a check, but fully independent human quality judgments are unavailable for the full review sample (§3.3; Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **How much opinion instability exceeds ordinary review variability?** Single reviews and positive-gap selection leave this unresolved. Repeat neutral, praise, and criticism reviews for each version and compare their ordering-change distributions (Appendix G.1).
- **Would paired, evidence-grounded reviewing improve responsiveness?** Reviewers never see both versions or change annotations. Compare independent scoring with paired assessment, both with and without verified revision evidence (§5).
- **Do findings generalize beyond accepted AI papers?** The corpus excludes other populations. Replicate the protocol on rejected submissions and other disciplines with independently validated improvements (Appendix B.1).
- **Which specific scientific improvements causally affect scores?** Broad co-occurring revision indicators provide associations only. Construct controlled manuscript variants isolating individual evidence changes while holding presentation fixed (§6.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main findings and several sensitivity checks, but many referenced tables contain captions without numerical bodies, and Appendix H contains headings without the promised full prompts.

Material unavailable details include complete per-reviewer pooled estimates and opinion denominators, full regression coefficients, exact prompt wording, comprehensive model-generation settings, and independently human-assessed quality labels for the full review sample. A project link is supplied, but artifact availability and contents are not established by the text alone.
