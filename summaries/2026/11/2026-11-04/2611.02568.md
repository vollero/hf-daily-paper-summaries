# SeMo3D: Unifying 3D Generation, Editing, and Completion via Selective Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02568) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02568)


## One-sentence takeaway
SeMo3D unifies image-guided 3D generation, local editing, and completion by synthesizing selected latent tokens while retaining context tokens, learning without paired editing/completion data and reporting strong results on three benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation
Regenerating an entire asset for a local edit or completion can alter content that should stay fixed. The authors identify separate task-specific models, imperfect preservation, paired-data requirements, and limited conditioning as barriers to unified 3D creation (Sec. 1).

Their proposed common formulation is to synthesize a designated region: the whole asset for generation, the target region for editing, or the missing region for completion. Editing here covers local additions, deletions, and replacements, excluding global style transfer (Sec. 1, footnote 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Selective modeling:** Partition spatially aligned latent tokens into selected and context sets. Generate only selected tokens and reuse context tokens across sparse-structure, shape, and texture stages. Exact preservation applies to tokens; asset-level preservation depends on latent reconstruction fidelity (Secs. 3.1–3.2; p. 3, footnote 2).
- **Region-selective denoising:** Apply noise and training loss only to selected tokens. Random full-region and local-box partitions teach all three tasks from ordinary assets without paired editing/completion supervision (Sec. 3.3).
- **Omni3DiT:** Extend pretrained Trellis2 models with selected/context role embeddings, text cross-attention, point tokens in the sparse-structure stage, and compositional condition dropout. Image guidance is required at inference; text and points are independently optional (Secs. 3.4, 7).

These results support the reported framework on the evaluated tasks and guidance settings. They do not isolate architecture gains from richer inputs: baselines receive their supported modalities, while SeMo3D often receives more modalities. Generation points and additional completion points are sampled from ground truth, making these evaluations tests of guided reconstruction fidelity rather than unconstrained creation (Secs. 8.1–8.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Structured-latent resolution misses fine structures such as thin fronds; the CLIP text encoder struggles with complex compositional prompts, illustrated by fruit becoming texture instead of separate objects (p. 9; Fig. 10).
- **Preservation qualification:** Exact preservation is guaranteed for context tokens. The claim of near-equivalent asset preservation relies on the representation’s reconstruction fidelity (p. 3, footnote 2).
- **Analyst inference—deployment gap:** Ground-truth-derived points and benchmark edit regions provide cleaner guidance than users or agents may supply. Robustness examples vary mask size and point count/distribution, but do not quantify tolerance to incorrect points or misplaced masks (Secs. 8.1–9).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Table 4 tests conditioning and architectural components, but provides no direct alternative-training-objective ablation isolating region-selective denoising.
- **Analyst inference—evaluation limits:** Editing FID/FVD compare edited outputs against source renders, so they may reward source similarity alongside quality. Agent benefits are illustrated without aggregate success rates or uncertainty estimates (Secs. 8.1, 8.3; Fig. 9).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is synthesis to erroneous guidance?** Reported robustness covers size, count, and distribution changes rather than incorrect inputs. Next experiment: perturb point locations and mask positions, measuring edit success and outside-region damage.
- **How much does selective denoising contribute independently?** Its training objective lacks a direct comparative ablation. Next experiment: hold architecture, data, and conditioning fixed while comparing selective and whole-asset denoising.
- **Does token preservation prevent visible boundary artifacts?** Exact token reuse does not itself establish decoded boundary quality. Next experiment: measure seam geometry and texture continuity over repeated edits.
- **How reliable is the complete agent workflow?** Demonstrations lack aggregate task outcomes. Next experiment: evaluate a fixed instruction suite with edit success, region-selection accuracy, latency, and cost against direct coding.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported benchmark values; **medium** in broader superiority and deployment claims.

Material missing information includes code/checkpoint availability, evaluation uncertainty or repeated-seed results, inference hardware for the reported runtime, aggregate agent evaluation, and quantitative tests of noisy user guidance and long edit sequences.
