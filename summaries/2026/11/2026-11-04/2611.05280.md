# YANAMI: A Large-Scale Multimodal Dataset and Benchmark for Multiturn Stateful Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05280) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05280)


## One-sentence takeaway

YANAMI supplies large-scale training data and a benchmark for tracking evolving image–text dialogue states, with BAGEL fine-tuning gains and evaluations showing that correctly understanding a state does not guarantee faithfully rendering it.

## Problem and motivation

Image creation and editing often require retaining earlier decisions, resolving references, preserving constraints, and replacing outdated requirements after corrections. The authors argue that existing multimodal datasets largely emphasize single-turn tasks or multi-turn sequences whose turns remain independently solvable, providing insufficient supervision for evolving dialogue states (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **YANAMI-400K:** 402,639 constructed dialogues containing 2,047,236 images and 2,643,291 user–assistant turns. Four scenarios cover multi-image fusion, image editing, world modeling, and visual reasoning; each training dialogue exercises at least four of six state-management capabilities (Appendix A.3, A.6).
- **Construction pipeline:** Public-dataset images feed model-assisted dialogue construction, automatic coherence and image–text verification, and iterative repair. Benchmark candidates additionally receive exhaustive human verification by three annotators per sample (§3.1; Appendix A.2–A.5).
- **YANAMI-Bench:** 400 balanced dialogues, 1,600 state-understanding questions, and 8,467 atomic visual checks distinguish Dialogue State Understanding (DSU) from Visual Realization (VR). A conditional analysis tests realization on state properties all compared models understand correctly (Appendix A.5, B.6).
- **Empirical validation:** BAGEL fine-tuning tests the training corpus’s utility, while benchmark experiments examine accumulated context, self-generated history, and understanding–realization failures (§4).

The authors claim priority as the first large-scale resource of this kind; the supplied evidence does not independently establish that priority. Dataset and prompt releases are promised rather than confirmed (Reproducibility Statement).

These results support training utility for BAGEL and expose distinct understanding and rendering failures under the defined protocol. They do not isolate the benefit of explicit state supervision from additional training data or compute. Percentage improvements and declines reported only in prose are not clearly identified as relative changes versus percentage-point differences.

Cross-group aggregate rankings also need caution: UMM scores weight DSU, State Consistency, and Visual Quality at 4:4:2, whereas generation-only scores weight the latter two at 6:4 (Appendix B.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Coverage is limited to four scenarios and six capabilities. Additional languages, modalities, longer horizons, broader interactive studies, and more fine-tuned architectures remain future work (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The supplied text reports no matched-data or matched-compute ablation removing explicit state dependencies, so gains cannot be attributed specifically to stateful supervision.
- **Analyst inference—deployment validity:** Main results replace earlier generated images with ground truth. Self-History tests expose additional failures, but keep textual history fixed, limiting evidence about fully interactive deployment (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Models receive different image organizations and sometimes native output resolutions. Missing evaluation records are excluded; GPT-Image-2 uses 948 valid outputs and Nano Banana 2 uses 939 (Appendix B.4–B.5, D.1).
- **Analyst inference—leakage and evaluator coverage:** SHA-256 checking establishes no exact image duplicates between training and benchmark, but does not establish absence of semantic overlap. Judge validation covers three models, and correlation alone does not demonstrate unbiased scoring (§3.1, §4.3).
- **Evidence availability:** Many referenced tables lack their numerical bodies, and the prompt-template headings contain no actual prompts in the supplied retrieval.

## Open research questions

1. **Does explicit state supervision drive the gains?** No controlled construction ablation is supplied. Compare full YANAMI training with equally sized, compute-matched dialogues whose cross-turn dependencies are removed.
2. **Do gains generalize across architectures?** Only BAGEL is fine-tuned here, and Appendix G identifies broader architectural validation as future work. Repeat training on additional UMMs using matched budgets and evaluations.
3. **What reduces the understanding–realization gap?** Correct answers still coexist with visual failures on shared subsets. Compare ordinary generation against generation conditioned on an explicit verified state representation.
4. **How robust is sustained interactive use?** Main evaluation supplies correct visual histories, while Self-History holds text fixed. Run longer rollouts with adaptive user corrections and separately measure understanding, realization, and recovery.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Dataset construction, scoring, and Table 4 results are well described, but several major quantitative claims cannot be checked against complete tables.

Material missing information includes full benchmark and editing results, numerical realization gaps, exact judge correlations and uncertainty, rendered construction/evaluation prompts, training duration and total compute, controlled ablations, and confirmation that promised assets have been released.
