# Near-Zero Token Embeddings Silently Stall Training in Gated Sequence Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04449) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04449)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors identify how near-zero input embeddings silently erase batch gradients in gated language models and demonstrate separate embedding clipping for checkpoint adaptation and a gate offset for more stable pretraining.

## Problem and motivation
Unseen tokens in untied input embeddings receive no input gradient but still shrink under AdamW weight decay. When these tokens later appear, zero-at-origin gates or queries can prevent historical context from restoring their residual scale, allowing repeated Pre-RMSNorm operations to amplify gradients. FP32 global clipping then overflows and zeroes current-batch gradients despite finite losses (§§2–4).

The identified gap is the connection between under-trained tokens, gated readout behavior, and silent optimization failure—particularly during adaptation to low-resource languages and chat templates. Earlier work discussed rare tokens and embedding decay without this complete failure chain (§6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnosis and census:** Audit embedding tables and tokenizer reachability, derive conditional gradient-scaling behavior, and test the mechanism through embedding rescaling, causal gate interventions, and optimizer-update decomposition (§§2–4; Appendices A–D).
- **GroupClip:** Clip input embeddings separately from backbone parameters, using an FP64 or rescaled embedding norm. This preserves backbone batch signal while allowing affected rows to recover (§5.1).
- **GateFloor:** Replace the recurrent output gate with `SiLU(z) + δ`, providing nonzero readout at zero input. The authors evaluate this architectural change during pretraining and separately recommend disabling embedding weight decay (§5.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reachable near-zero rows occur frequently in surveyed untied gated checkpoints | At embedding norm `<10⁻⁶`: 20/38 untied gated/hybrid repositories (52.6%), versus 0/101 tied repositories; census covers 291 repositories and 280 distinct tables. | §2.1; Fig. 3a; Appendix F |
| Controlled decay supports the proposed origin | Four small Mamba models cross weight tying with embedding decay; unseen untied rows follow the predicted decay, while tying or disabling embedding decay preserves normal scale. | Appendix A |
| Forward readout restoration supports the mechanism | On a native near-zero Nemotron-3-Nano-4B token at a non-initial position, opening only the first gate forward while retaining backward derivatives reduces embedding-gradient infinity norm from `2.1×10³⁵` to `6.71`. | §3.2; Fig. 4b; Appendix B |
| Overflow removes fresh batch signal, although parameters still move | In seed-0 Nemotron Telugu update decomposition, overflow-step backbone movement has median L2 norm `0.245`, entirely matching zero-gradient momentum/decay coasting; fresh-batch displacement is exactly zero. | Appendix D; Table 7 |
| GroupClip eliminates measured stalls and improves Telugu perplexity | Nemotron-3-Nano-4B, 1,000 FineWeb-2 adaptation steps, three seeds: baseline loses current gradients on 19.8% of steps and reaches FLORES-200 PPL `13.44±0.17`; GroupClip has zero stalled steps and PPL `13.04±0.19`. | §5.1; Fig. 6a; Table 2 results |
| Mongolian benefits include faster early adaptation | Falcon-H1-1.5B, same three-seed training design: step-100 PPL falls from `40.40` to `37.47`; final PPL is `15.68±0.11` with global clipping versus `15.58±0.10` with GroupClip. | §5.1; Fig. 6b |
| GateFloor suppresses probe gradients with comparable perplexity | A 370M hybrid trained on 1.57B FineWeb-Edu tokens, single seed: at input norm `10⁻³⁰`, reported gradients fall from `1.6×10²⁸` to `0.13`; validation PPL is `22.45` baseline versus `22.28` GateFloor. | §5.2; Fig. 6c; Table 3 caption |
| Measured GroupClip overhead is small in the tested configuration | One H100/Nemotron configuration, 15 timed steps after warmup: median step time `2.88 s` global versus `2.84 s` FP64 GroupClip; both peak at `69.7 GB`, although FP64 clipping creates a transient `3.1 GB` copy. | Appendix D |

These author-reported results connect controlled interventions to actual optimizer behavior and adaptation outcomes. They establish the failure and remedies in tested configurations, rather than a universal failure of gated models. Architecture, sequence position, embedding training, and clipping precision materially affect exposure (§§3–4). GateFloor’s large-scale effectiveness remains unestablished.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** GateFloor pretraining reaches only 370M parameters with one or two seeds per configuration, despite broader checkpoint probes and larger adaptation models (§7).
- **Analyst inference—census generalization:** Popularity-based selection, targeted family additions, excluded unreadable checkpoints, and mirrored tables limit interpreting repository percentages as population prevalence (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—origin attribution:** OLMoE tokens absent from the sampled corpus are not proven absent from its entire pretraining corpus; the controlled experiment supports the mechanism more directly (§2.2; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** GroupClip adaptation is tested on two model-language pairs. GateFloor’s position-zero recovery relies on nonzero Mamba-2 convolution bias in the evaluated architectures (§5; Appendix E).
- **Theory boundary:** The tight scaling relation requires non-degeneracy and non-decreasing cumulative gains, alongside Appendix B’s standing boundedness assumptions; it is not unconditional (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Measurement boundary:** The no-overhead claim rests on one short H100 timing configuration; FP64 clipping still allocates transient memory (Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **Does GateFloor retain quality at larger scales and longer training horizons?** The largest run is small relative to audited checkpoints (§7). Next experiment: matched multi-seed, billion-parameter pretraining with gradient probes and downstream evaluations.
- **How broadly does GroupClip improve adaptation?** End-to-end evidence covers Telugu/Nemotron and Mongolian/Falcon (§5.1). Next experiment: additional architectures, languages, and template-heavy instruction-tuning datasets with identical batch sequences.
- **What ensures position-zero recovery without convolution bias?** The demonstrated GateFloor mechanism uses that bias (§5.2; Appendix E). Next experiment: factorial gate-offset and convolution-bias ablations at initial and non-initial positions.
