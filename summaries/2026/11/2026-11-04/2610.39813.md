# EvoTeam: Multi-Agent Collaboration via Evolving Teams

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39813) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39813)


## One-sentence takeaway

EvoTeam evolves reusable agent teams and team-specific coordination rules while sharing individual skills across teams, reporting the best mixed-benchmark results across six metrics, although incomplete tables and limited ablations constrain verification and causal attribution (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Improving individual agents does not necessarily improve responsibility assignment, information handoffs, or verification within a team. The authors identify two obstacles to learning across heterogeneous tasks: adapting everything within one shared team structure and reusing coordination rules outside their applicable contexts (§1).

Their preliminary Meta-Team study reports worse target-task performance after cross-task evolution than target-only evolution. Example traces suggest mismatched team structures and inappropriate stopping rules as potential explanations, rather than establishing these as exhaustive causes (§2; Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Reusable team library:** An LLM selector chooses a team by collaboration requirements, roles, and verification needs; a composer creates a provisional team when necessary. A Chairman recruits a task-specific subset from its roster (§3.1).
- **Team-scoped coordination:** Each team retains its own interaction rules. Agents apply relevant rules as soft handoff guidance, while the Chairman assigns and can reassign subtasks; rules do not impose a fixed workflow (§3.2; Appendix D.4).
- **Three-level experience updates:** Separate reflectors revise team rosters and descriptions, team-specific rules, and individual prompt patches or skills. Rules remain scoped to teams, while individual guidance remains shared. Persistent updates are frozen before holdout evaluation (§3.3; Appendices A, D.7, G.5).

The distinctive contribution is this organization and scope of reusable experience, rather than reflection or task-dependent agent recruitment alone. The authors also supply individual- and mixed-benchmark evaluations, transfer experiments, and case analyses (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results

Unless specified otherwise, results use GPT-5.6-Luna, disjoint holdouts, and avg@3: three independent rollout scores per test instance (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest reported mixed-benchmark aggregate | After evolution on a shuffled 116-instance stream, average native score was **59.58 versus 55.55 for Meta-Team**, a **4.03-point** advantage; authors report first place on all six metrics. Metrics combine accuracy, success/resolution rates, and LCBS on a common 0–100 scale. | §4.2; §6; Appendix B.1 |
| Strong individual-benchmark performance | Average score **58.85**, leading four of six metrics; GAIA answer accuracy **78.00 versus 73.00 for Meta-Team**. | §4.2 |
| Cross-backbone gains over unevolved MAS | On GAIA, DeepSeek→GPT achieved **77.00 versus 72.00** accuracy; GPT→DeepSeek achieved **76.00 versus 58.00**. Both LoCoBench tasks also improved in each direction. | Table 2; §4.3 |
| Transfer remained near same-backbone performance | Across GAIA and two LoCoBench tasks with either evaluation backbone fixed, cross-backbone scores were within **4.00 points** of same-backbone scores. | Table 2; §4.3 |
| Rules and team organization helped in main ablations | Under mixed-benchmark evolution, adding rules improved GAIA accuracy by **10.00 points** and LOCA success by **8.75**; retaining teams without rules improved those metrics by **6.00** and **2.50**, respectively. | §4.4 |
| Ablation benefits were not universal | Appendix reports LoCoBench Refact LCBS **66.40 without rules versus 65.65 with the full framework**. Its evolution setting is not explicit in that paragraph. | Appendix C.1 |
| Competitive reported task cost | Under mixed evolution, EvoTeam scored higher at lower per-task cost than Meta-Team and ReCreate on GAIA and BeyondSWE DepMig; numerical costs are absent from supplied text. | §4.5; Figure 5 caption |

These results support effectiveness within the selected benchmark settings and transfer between the two evaluated backbones. They do not establish universal positive transfer or isolate team-specific scoping from the general benefit of learned rules. The aggregate also combines different native metrics; it is not a single task-success rate.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Multimodal interactive collaboration is not systematically evaluated. Fixed model parameters leave success dependent on the backbone’s ability to interpret stored guidance (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism isolation:** Reported ablations remove rules or both teams and rules; no matched comparison of shared versus team-scoped rules, or separate removal of agent-level evolution, is reported (§4.4; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—order robustness:** Mixed evolution uses one fixed shuffle. Three evaluation rollouts do not establish robustness to different evolution orders or independently evolved libraries (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference—partial isolation:** Shared skills and recruitment profiles remain possible channels for cross-team interference; only interaction rules are explicitly isolated (Appendices E.1, G.4).
- **Reporting constraints:** Several tables and figures appear only as captions, including the main results table and resource-budget table. Figure references for ablations are inconsistent, and no confidence intervals or significance tests are supplied.
- **Case-study scope:** Selected traces illustrate plausible mechanisms. The authors explicitly caution that Meta-Team’s post-evaluation note overwrite cannot explain the preceding task-score difference (Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does team scoping itself prevent negative transfer?** Existing ablations remove rules rather than changing their scope. Compare matched shared-rule and team-scoped-rule systems with identical update budgets.
2. **How sensitive is evolution to task order?** Only one mixed-stream shuffle is reported. Repeat evolution over multiple shuffles and clustered task orders, then evaluate the same holdouts.
3. **When do learned rules hinder execution?** The refactoring ablation favors removing rules (Appendix C.1). Audit rule applicability errors and test selective retrieval against injecting the entire team rule set.
4. **Does the method extend to multimodal interaction?** The authors identify this as unevaluated. Test visual interactive tasks with multimodal traces and measure both completion and handoff-evidence quality.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and several results are clearly described, but numerical verification is incomplete.

Material missing information includes full main-result tables, numerical costs and resource limits, complete prompt contents, uncertainty estimates, evolution-order replications, team-library growth statistics, and confirmation that evolved artifacts accompany the claimed code release.
