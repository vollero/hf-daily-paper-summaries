# Is Forgetting Unified? Exploring Concept Unlearning in Unified Multimodal Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04467) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04467)


## One-sentence takeaway
UNIFORGET substantially improves joint suppression of concept generation and visual identification on two unified multimodal models, but stricter evaluation exposes losses in visual utility, generation quality, and multi-concept scalability (Table 2; §§6.2–6.3).

## Problem and motivation
Unified multimodal models can both generate images and identify their contents. The authors ask whether editing one pathway also suppresses the same concept in the other, while preserving useful descriptions and unrelated capabilities. Their identified gap is that generation-only or understanding-only evaluations cannot establish joint forgetting (§§1–2).

The intended application is suppressing sensitive, proprietary, or copyrighted visual concepts. However, the paper evaluates behavioral suppression rather than verified removal of information from model weights (§8; Ethics Statement).

## Approach and contributions
- **Cross-pathway evaluation formulation:** Separates pathway-specific forgetting, joint forgetting, and utility-preserving joint forgetting. Identity answers must omit the target while supplying the correct generic category without refusal (§2).
- **UUBENCH:** Pairs generation and visual-understanding tasks for 30 targets and 60 related neighbors across characters, landmarks, and objects. Its 720 reference images have disjoint training and held-out splits; joint metrics operate on aligned records (§3; Appendix A).
- **UNIFORGET:** First applies closed-form, preservation-regularized edits to attention key/value projections, redirecting target prompts toward category-generic anchors. It then trains LoRA adapters for name-free identification, informative descriptions, preference ranking, and neighbor preservation, with model-specific stabilization objectives. A calibrated fraction of the adapter update is merged into the edited checkpoint (§4; Appendix B).
- **Diagnostic analysis:** Tests component contributions, update strength, multi-concept scaling, and selected prompt/image perturbations (§6).

The authors claim the first systematic study of this joint setting; the supplied text does not independently establish priority (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Generation forgetting transfers weakly to identification | TIME achieves generation Target Forget (TF) of **100.0% / 96.7%**, but understanding Direct Forget (DF) of only **24.0% / 30.7%**, on BAGEL / Janus respectively. GD and KL each achieve **0.0% TF** on both models. | Table 2; §5.2 |
| Coordinated editing improves joint forgetting | On UUBENCH, UNIFORGET achieves Unified Forget of **80.0% / 92.7%**, versus **31.3% / 41.3%** for TIME + GD, on BAGEL / Janus. UNIFORGET TF is **100.0%** on both. | Table 2 |
| Utility-aware success is substantially lower | UNIFORGET Unified Strict Forget is **47.6% / 58.0%**, versus **28.8% / 31.0%** for TIME + GD. Target-description utility is **72.2% / 75.6%**, below Base **96.7% / 96.2%**. | Table 2 |
| Neighbor identity retention masks quality losses | UNIFORGET neighbor retention is **98.1% / 92.9%**, but strict neighbor retention, which also requires coherence and preserved quality, is **84.6% / 73.2%**. | Tables 1–2 |
| General capabilities incur measurable costs | Base → UNIFORGET GenEval: **72.7% → 63.4%** on BAGEL and **78.9% → 70.1%** on Janus. MMBench: **88.4% → 87.8%** and **79.1% → 77.2%**. BAGEL MME-C falls **85.5% → 77.7%**. | Table 3 |
| Stronger understanding updates sacrifice utility | BAGEL merge strength **ρ = 0.4 → 0.6** raises DF **80.0% → 93.3%**, while target utility falls **72.2% → 55.6%**. | §6.2; Figure 3 |
| Multi-concept scaling degrades preservation and identification forgetting | In branch-specific sweeps from **1 to 30 concepts**, generation TF remains **100.0%**; neighbor retention at 30 concepts retains **81.5% / 41.8%** of its single-concept value. Understanding DF falls **80.0% → 51.2% / 92.7% → 37.1%** on BAGEL / Janus. | §6.3; Figure 4 |
| Robustness depends on pathway and attack | Generation TF remains **100.0%** under three tested prompt interventions on both models. Understanding name-exposure suppression is **61.7% / 97.5%**; forced-short suppression is **100.0% / 77.3%** on BAGEL / Janus. These understanding scores measure name omission, not full DF or strict joint success. | Tables 12–13 |

These results support improved **behavioral joint suppression within the evaluated setting**. Unified Forget requires generation suppression and valid generic identification on the same paired record; Unified Strict Forget additionally checks generation quality, name-free visual description, and target-description utility. Neither metric includes neighbor preservation, which is evaluated separately (Appendix A.3).

The evidence does not establish irreversible deletion, broad adversarial robustness, or utility preservation without meaningful cost. Reported results are means of three measurements, but uncertainty intervals are absent (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only two 7B models and 30 targets are evaluated; larger models and broader domains remain untested (§8).
- **Author-stated measurement limitation:** Automated metrics and LLM judges may disagree with humans; no certified weight-level deletion is established (§8).
- **Observed failures:** Multi-concept performance deteriorates, prompt sensitivities differ between models, and the telescope example loses useful descriptive detail (§§6.3–6.4; Table 11).
- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** Reused generation and identity records create dependence between joint pairings, explicitly acknowledged in Appendix A.3. Without intervals or per-run dispersion, the stability of differences is unclear.
- **Analyst inference—comparison reproducibility:** UNIFORGET is specified in detail, but comparable baseline adaptation and tuning recipes are not supplied. This limits assessment of baseline parity (§5.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—configuration ambiguity:** Table 8’s weight coordinates are not explicitly mapped to the loss coefficients in Appendix B.3. The text also reports seed 42 alongside three repeated measurements without clarifying what varies between repetitions.

## Open research questions
1. **Can joint forgetting scale without severe interference?** Branch-specific scaling degrades retention and DF, while simultaneous multi-concept joint metrics are not reported (§6.3). Next experiment: evaluate fully composed checkpoints across increasing concept counts using both joint metrics and neighbor utility.
2. **Does suppressed identity remain recoverable?** The study measures behavior and excludes certified deletion (§8). Next experiment: probe identity recovery through controlled relearning and additional query formats while tracking retained utility.
3. **Can robustness improve without description collapse?** Prompt attacks expose model-specific weaknesses, while stronger updates reduce utility (§§6.2, 6.4). Next experiment: add adversarial prompt supervision and compare full DF, strict joint success, and collapse on held-out attacks.
4. **How well do automated judgments match human assessments?** Human agreement is not reported (§8). Next experiment: independently annotate a stratified sample of target, neighbor, and failure cases and compare predicate-level agreement.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, metric definitions, and consistent central comparisons, but material gaps remain: uncertainty estimates, human-judge validation, full baseline recipes, exact repetition semantics, complete concept/image inventories and provenance, checkpoint-selection criteria, and confirmed public availability of code and benchmark assets.
