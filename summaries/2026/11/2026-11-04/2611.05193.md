# Factorized MLLM Embeddings for Composed Image Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05193) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05193)


## One-sentence takeaway

FactEmbed trains factorized MLLM latents to reconstruct target-image details before retrieval alignment, reporting improved composed image retrieval with inexpensive reranking on CIRR and FashionIQ (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Composed image retrieval (CIR) searches an image gallery using a reference image and text describing a desired modification. The model must distinguish attributes to change from those to preserve. The authors argue that directly training a single MLLM embedding with contrastive supervision provides insufficient guidance for resolving this composition before retrieval (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Target imagination through factorized latents.** Learnable tokens precede [EOS]. An intermediate decoder reconstructs target DINOv2 patch features from these latents, conditioned on a coarse target-layout prior during training. Competitive spatial masks encourage specialization in coherent regions without dense annotations (§3.1).
- **Connecting decomposition to retrieval.** Optimal-transport matching supports factor-level InfoNCE alongside global [EOS] InfoNCE. Target-side spatial distillation transfers decoder masks into deeper backbone attention; staged training first establishes reconstruction before joint optimization (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Efficient complementary embeddings.** One forward pass produces [EOS] and factor latents. [EOS] retrieves candidates; cosine similarity from mean-pooled latents supplements the score for the top-100 shortlist, requiring two stored vectors per image (§3.3).
- **Modified triplet augmentation.** Building on ReCALL, contrastive captioning conditions on the original target to distinguish mined hard negatives, with duplicate filtering and post-training. This supplements the core representation method (Appendix F).

These author-reported results support a useful combination of reconstruction, retrieval alignment, and reranking within the tested settings. They do not independently establish general compositional reasoning or large-gallery efficiency. The supplied extraction contains table captions but omits numerical table bodies, preventing verification of complete rankings and absolute scores.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** A fixed latent count ignores input complexity; mean pooling sacrifices some full-factor information; evaluation covers only CIRR and FashionIQ (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Contrastive captioning distinguishes each mined target from the original target, but can leave ambiguity among sibling hard negatives, potentially introducing false negatives as augmentation scales (Appendix F.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Reconstruction visualizations provide suggestive evidence of semantic specialization, but their training-time target-layout conditioning limits what they establish about unaided target imagination. Appendix A explicitly acknowledges the prior’s contribution to visual similarity.
- **Analyst inference:** The latency experiment uses a small gallery and excludes offline gallery encoding. It supports low shortlist-rescoring cost in that setting, rather than establishing total indexing or production-scale retrieval costs (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Augmentation changes both prompting and the captioning model; the supplied comparison does not isolate their individual contributions. Caption-quality reversal judgments also use the captioning model itself (Appendix F.1).

## Open research questions

- **Can latent count adapt to query complexity?** Fixed capacity is an explicit limitation. Compare adaptive and fixed latent sets under matched storage and compute budgets, stratifying queries by edit complexity (§5).
- **Can compressed factors retain full OT accuracy?** Mean pooling leaves a measured accuracy gap. Compare learned compression with mean pooling and full-factor matching across gallery sizes (§4.2; §5).
- **Does factorization transfer beyond these benchmarks?** Broader multimodal generalization remains unverified. Evaluate a frozen trained model on additional domains and retrieval tasks (§5).
- **Can augmentation avoid ambiguity across all sibling targets?** Current captioning conditions on only the original target. Compare joint-candidate captioning against the current pipeline as hard-negative counts increase, with independent human validity judgments (Appendix F.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and selected results are clearly described, but numerical table bodies and plotted values are absent. The supplied text does not establish run-to-run variance, statistical significance, current code/checkpoint availability, or completed release of augmented triplets. Exact baseline scores and some numerical units therefore remain unverifiable.
