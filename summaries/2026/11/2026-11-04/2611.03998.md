# Dory1.0-4B: A Looped Normalized-GPT

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03998) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03998)


## One-sentence takeaway
Dory1.0-4B uses shared recurrent layers to trade inference compute for stronger coding, agentic, and long-context performance from one checkpoint, while reducing generated tokens on evaluated agentic tasks (Table 2; Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Longer generated reasoning traces increase latency, KV-cache memory, and serving cost. The authors explore complementary scaling through latent recurrence: repeatedly applying shared layers increases computation per token without adding parameters. Their target is a fully post-trained language model that supports different inference budgets without retraining or switching checkpoints (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Elastic-depth model and training recipe.** A normalized Transformer combines input, shared recurrent, and output blocks with sliding-window and full attention. Training introduces variable loop counts after initial non-looped pretraining and uses truncated backpropagation through only the final recurrent invocation. Total pretraining reaches approximately 6.3T tokens including context extension (§§2–3).
- **Difficulty-aware post-training.** Extended supervised fine-tuning precedes RLVR, where profiled prompt difficulty biases harder prompts toward deeper execution while preserving fixed loop quotas (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Deployment assets and evaluations.** The authors report BF16 and quantized checkpoint releases, evaluate last-loop KV caching, and demonstrate shallow drafting with deeper self-verification (§§5–6).

The paper attributes several underlying techniques—including variable-loop training, last-loop caching, and quantization-aware distillation—to prior work; its contribution includes their integration and evaluation in Dory rather than establishing each technique as new (§1).

These results support adjustable capability within a fixed checkpoint and measured efficiency benefits in specified settings. They do not establish universal task improvement, superiority at matched inference cost, or equivalent end-to-end application speedups. The aggregate ranking also depends on the selected metrics, equal weighting, and Elo normalization.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Reported scope:** Base-model performance tends to saturate after four loops; extrapolation to deeper execution is evaluated only for the base model, while post-training results stop at four loops (§3.5).
- **Reported mismatch:** Last-loop caching exposes earlier-loop queries to previous tokens’ final-loop keys and values, unlike training, and incurs some accuracy losses (§6.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** The curriculum ablation uses an earlier SFT checkpoint. Its gains do not directly quantify the curriculum’s effect on the final checkpoint (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—cost comparisons:** Token-based compute estimates omit other serving costs, and self-speculative request timings exclude tool execution and evaluator processing (§§4.4, 6.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Most Table 2 results share a protocol, but Qwen3.6’s SWE-Bench score is self-reported. Confidence intervals are not supplied for the main benchmark comparisons (§4.3; Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—reproduction and modality:** Training blends reference other model recipes without fully specifying their contents here; document evaluation is text-only (§§3–4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does difficulty routing help the final SFT checkpoint equally?** The reported ablation uses an earlier checkpoint. Repeat curriculum versus random routing from the final SFT checkpoint with matched data, quotas, and training budget (§4.2).
2. **Can inference-time routing improve the accuracy–latency trade-off?** The paper reports fixed-depth evaluation rather than a validated adaptive serving policy. Compare learned per-request routing against fixed loop counts under matched latency budgets (§§4.3–4.4).
3. **Can training reduce last-loop cache losses?** The cache policy introduces a documented training–inference mismatch. Fine-tune with that policy and compare accuracy and memory against both existing cache configurations (§6.1).
4. **Do the serving optimizations combine effectively?** Last-loop caching and self-speculation are evaluated separately. Test their combination with quantization, measuring task accuracy, peak memory, and complete application latency (§§5–6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported architecture and descriptive results; causal and deployment conclusions remain narrower.

Material missing information includes complete training-data composition, contamination checks, uncertainty estimates for main scores, matched-cost baseline comparisons, hardware details for self-speculative measurements, and joint evaluation of the serving optimizations.
