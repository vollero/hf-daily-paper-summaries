# ParetoSaddler: Pareto-Guided Optimization of Agent Harnesses for Quality and Cost

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02079) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02079)


## One-sentence takeaway

ParetoSaddler separates intermediate harness promotion from final deployment selection, achieving higher quality and lower token cost on two agent benchmarks by retaining statistically supported quality–cost trade-offs (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

Automatic harness optimization modifies prompts, tools, middleware, and control logic while keeping model weights fixed. The authors identify a gap in staged optimization: applying deployment preferences during preliminary screening can reject intermediate updates that enable later improvements—for example, fixing premature termination may initially increase execution cost. Stochastic agent executions also make mean quality–cost gains unreliable promotion signals (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Separate promotion from deployment selection.** Candidates can advance through credible improvement in either objective; deployment preferences govern final selection. All development-evaluated candidates remain available, including currently dominated alternatives (§4).
- **Combine repeated execution with Bayesian screening.** The method reweights observed executions using a Bayesian bootstrap, preserving each execution’s paired quality and cost. Promotion requires sufficient bootstrap support for quality improvement or cost reduction, subject to local regression allowances (§4, Eqs. 6–8).
- **Instantiate and evaluate the framework.** Built on AutoSaddler, ParetoSaddler is evaluated under Pareto-set selection and quality-constrained cost minimization, with ablations of promotion rules, repetitions, and screening (§5).

The contribution is this integration into staged harness optimization; Pareto optimization, repeated evaluation, and Bayesian bootstrapping are acknowledged prior techniques (§2).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. Main test evaluations use domain-disjoint splits; quality means normalized stage-0 rubric score on ClawMark and Pass@1 on GAIA2. Costs are USD of LLM tokens per task under the paper’s accounting convention (§5.1; Appendix A).

The evidence supports improved benchmark operating points and an empirical benefit from the tested promotion and screening protocols. It does not establish global Pareto optimality, statistically significant superiority across repeated searches, or net lifetime savings after optimization overhead. Individual patch explanations remain observational; the authors explicitly caution against treating them as isolated causal estimates (Appendices D, H).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated uncertainty limitation:** bootstrap support captures execution variation on fixed screening tasks, not task-sampling uncertainty. With three executions, rare outcomes may be missed and borderline promotion decisions may be sensitive to observed runs (Appendices A, L).
- **Authors’ stated scope limits:** ClawMark covers initial-stage tasks only. Privacy mechanisms, fairness, harmlessness, safety, and deployment readiness are not evaluated (§8; Appendix A).
- **Compute confounding:** the repeated-execution ablation uses more task executions in its three-run condition, so it does not isolate evidence quality under equal compute (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—limited robustness evidence:** evaluation uses one framework instantiation and two benchmark/model configurations. Only one additional GAIA2 PS-Pareto optimization run is reported; test repeats of a fixed harness do not substitute for independent optimization runs (§5.1; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Meta-Harness receives a larger maximum execution budget but uses different proposal counts and evaluation structure. This supports a protocol comparison, rather than a tightly compute-matched component comparison (Appendix A).
- **Sensitivity evidence is retrospective:** threshold analysis replays archived proposals and counts promotions; it does not test how different thresholds alter subsequent searches (Appendix E).

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptive evaluation improve reliability per dollar?** Fixed-task bootstrapping excludes task-sampling uncertainty, and repetitions are costly. Compare fixed evaluation with adaptive task selection and repetition allocation under equal execution budgets (Appendix L).
2. **Does repeated evidence outperform broader search at equal compute?** The current repetition ablation is not compute matched. Compare additional rollouts per candidate against additional candidates across independent searches (Appendix D).
3. **Which intermediate candidates deserve preservation?** The tested gate rewards aggregate quality or cost progress; instance-level improvements and behavioral novelty remain alternatives. Compare these gates under matched evaluation budgets and final deployment criteria (Appendix L).
4. **When do deployment savings repay optimization overhead?** Lower task costs coexist with higher optimizer cost per patch. Measure total optimization expenditure and deployment-volume break-even points across repeated runs (Appendix G).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reports substantive results and ablations, but many numerical tables appear only as captions.

Material missing information includes complete table entries and standard deviations, exact split assignments, numerical token price cards, full resource-usage totals, and executable code availability. These omissions prevent independent assessment of uncertainty, complete baseline comparisons, and end-to-end economic benefit.
