# BEND 2.0: Benchmarking the Capabilities Needed to Design DNA

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03806) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03806)


## One-sentence takeaway
BEND 2.0 shows that genomic models can improve selection of experimentally assayed DNA candidates, but strong aggregate predictions conceal substantial weaknesses in context specificity and mutation interactions.

## Problem and motivation
DNA design requires choosing edits that produce a particular response in a particular biological context. Overall prediction accuracy can obscure whether models distinguish cell-specific effects, genetic backgrounds, or non-additive mutation interactions. The authors identify a gap between existing genomic representation benchmarks and the capabilities needed to guide these choices (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Capability-focused benchmark:** Evaluations organized around sequence priors, functional prediction, biological context, genetic interactions, and design (§2).
- **Controlled prediction comparisons:** Frozen-embedding linear readouts are compared with native outputs, simple baselines, specialist models, and experimental references. Related examples are grouped during splitting, and correlations generally give genes or regulatory elements equal weight (§2; Appendix A).
- **Retrospective design evaluation:** Models select edits or enhancers from measured libraries while candidate outcomes are withheld from selection. Objectives include matching a target effect and controlling activity across cells (§7).
- **Reusable evaluation assets:** The authors report providing code, fixed splits, setup instructions, and hosted benchmark data (Reproducibility statement).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Representation access changes measured sequence-prior performance | On 512 GRCh38 windows, NTv3 650M post loss falls from **2.199 to 1.375 bits** when an additional 12% of positions are masked. | §3; Fig. 1 |
| Functional post-training improves splicing prediction | OpenSplice held-out-gene macro-Spearman for NTv3 650M rises from **0.187 pre-training checkpoint to 0.564 post-training checkpoint**. AlphaGenome embedding readout scores **0.650**, its native output **0.621**, and SpliceAI **0.674**. | §4; Table 1; Fig. 2A |
| Readouts transfer across assays and genes | OpenSplice-trained readouts retain predictive accuracy on **4,618 COMPASS variants in 88 excluded genes**, without COMPASS fitting or selection; AlphaGenome and NTv3 650M post outperform the source-trained k-mer baseline. Exact transfer scores are not stated in the prose. | §4; Fig. 2B |
| Average effects are easier than cell contrasts | Across **128 Siraj elements**, native AlphaGenome correlation is **0.532** for mean effects versus **0.398** for HepG2–K562 differences; NTv3 650M post readouts score **0.492 versus 0.256**. | §5; Fig. 3 |
| Combined-effect accuracy conceals weak interaction prediction | On **110 COMPASS genes**, NTv3 650M post correlations are **0.500** for double-mutant effects and **0.046** for interactions. Replacing double effects with additive predictions changes correlation to **0.493**; interaction replicate correlation is **0.171**. | §6; Fig. 4B; Table 4 |
| Model rankings reduce retrospective testing needs | Across **331 targets in 110 Siraj elements**, ten selected edits contain a match within one combined standard error for **62%** of targets with native AlphaGenome and **59%** with NTv3, versus **38%** under uniform selection. | §7; Fig. 5B; Appendix F.1 |
| Cell-selective choices help, with substantial remaining headroom | At equal target/off-target weighting across **127 Siraj loci**, utility is **0.384** for calibrated native AlphaGenome and **0.515** for its embedding readout, versus **0** for no edit. Experimental selection exceeds native AlphaGenome by **0.603** on matched evaluation subsets. | §7; Table 1; Appendix F.3 |
| Multi-cell gains do not imply successful expression programs | Selecting ten enhancers per pattern across **16 Atlas patterns**, AlphaGenome’s embedding readout gains **0.833 log₂ utility units** over random selection among originally pattern-matched designs, but mean utility remains **−0.096**; repeat-based experimental selection reaches **+0.673**. | §7; Fig. 6; Appendix F.4 |

These results establish useful retrospective ranking and transferable functional information under the specified splits. They do not establish prospective success on newly generated DNA, uniform population generalization, or strong interaction understanding. Experimental references are comparison procedures rather than universal ceilings; multi-cell ranges reflect two measurements rather than confidence intervals (Appendices A.1 and G).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Experimental reliability varies, and difficult tasks often have less consistent measurements. Embedding dimensionality and different input context lengths may affect model comparisons (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Benchmark holdouts do not establish absence from foundation-model pretraining (Appendix A). Performance should not be assumed to generalize across populations or biological settings (Ethics statement).
- **Scope:** Design evaluates selection within existing measured libraries, rather than prospective synthesis and testing of novel sequences (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** GTEx contrasts test a favorable, outcome-defined setting because the comparison tissue is chosen using measured expression. They do not establish performance on arbitrary requested tissue contrasts (§5; Appendix D.1).
- **Evidence boundary:** The gene-excluded COMPASS transfer test and interaction test use different exclusions: the latter retains shared genes after removing exact sequence overlaps (Appendices C and E.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can interaction learning improve independently of overall effect prediction?** Limited decoder fine-tuning improves effects but not clearly interactions (Appendix E.3). Next experiment: train with an explicit interaction objective and evaluate on held-out loci.
- **Do retrospective selection gains survive prospective testing?** Current design choices come from assayed libraries (§7). Next experiment: synthesize previously unmeasured model-selected candidates and matched controls.
- **What drives the remaining multi-cell selection gap?** Better measured candidates exist, yet model-selected mean utility remains negative (§7). Next experiment: compare direct utility training with activity regression under identical family splits.

## Confidence and missing information
**High** confidence in the reported findings and benchmark scope. Material unavailable information includes several numerical results contained only in figures or omitted table bodies, complete model extraction settings and seeds deferred to code, verified pretraining overlap, and prospective biological validation. The abstract’s “eight tasks” and “five experimental datasets” are not explicitly reconciled with the additional evaluations described in the appendices.
