# Say the Same, Act Differently: Diagnosing and Steering Text-Action Divergence in Reasoning VLAs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04166) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04166)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors show that white-box visual perturbations can change two reasoning VLAs’ actions while preserving their generated text exactly, including inducing new open-loop driving collisions in 52.6% of annotated scenes (§3.2).

## Problem and motivation
Reasoning vision-language-action models expose readable rationales alongside continuous control outputs, making text a possible safety-monitoring channel. The authors ask whether unsafe actions can arise while that channel remains unchanged. Earlier attacks discussed in the paper alter the reasoning itself; the identified gap is action divergence under word-for-word identical rationales (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **TNAS diagnostic:** At a shared text/action interface, estimate an action-sensitive hidden-state direction, then remove its projection onto gradients of clean-token negative log-likelihood and token-margin losses. The residual preserves these two losses to first order—not necessarily every property of text generation (§2.1).
- **Controlled mechanism tests:** Compare TNAS, unprojected action directions, text gradients, and random directions under matched text-drift budgets and equal intervention norms (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Visual-input steering:** Add alignment with the TNAS residual to a text-preserving adversarial objective. Evaluate full-image and localized digital perturbations using fixed-prefix action readouts, free-generation text equality, and driving collision checks (§3).

These results establish counterexamples to treating unchanged rationales as sufficient evidence of unchanged or safe actions in the tested settings. They do not establish physical attack success, closed-loop collision rates, or manipulation task failure. Manipulation exact match and action displacement are separate cohort summaries, rather than a reported joint safety outcome.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-defined scope:** Attacks require white-box access to processed visual tensors. Localized patches remain digital; printing, camera capture, and physical robustness are not evaluated (§3.1; §3.3).
- **Author-defined safety proxy:** Driving collisions use predicted trajectories against recorded actors that do not react to the attacked ego plan. These are open-loop geometric hazards (App. A.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited text neutrality:** Orthogonality concerns two aggregate loss gradients at the clean state. It does not guarantee globally unchanged token distributions or generated strings; free-generation evaluation supplies the stronger empirical evidence (§2.1).
- **Analyst inference—estimator qualification:** Alpamayo uses a leading singular vector, but InstructVLA uses one Gaussian-output vector-Jacobian product. The latter is action-sensitive without being demonstrated to be the maximally sensitive direction (App. A.4–A.4.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evaluation covers two checkpoints and fixed cohorts. Hook selection partly uses evaluation examples, and manipulation displacement is measured before clipping, thresholding, and unnormalization (§2.1; App. A.2.1, A.4.1).
- **Reproducibility constraint:** Runnable attack pipelines and optimized tensors are explicitly withheld (App. A.9).

## Open research questions

- **Does divergence persist during execution?** Logged-scene scoring cannot capture reactive traffic or controller feedback. Next experiment: evaluate identical-rationale attacks in closed-loop driving simulation and manipulation rollouts.
- **How much does the text-span definition matter?** TNAS removes only two aggregate gradients. Next experiment: compare token-level or broader text-sensitivity bases at matched action displacement.
- **Does explicit residualization improve input attacks beyond action alignment alone?** Unprojected action directions nearly match TNAS in hidden-state probes, while a corresponding input-attack ablation is not reported. Next experiment: replace TNAS alignment with unprojected action-direction alignment under identical budgets.
- **Can action-channel monitoring detect these failures?** The authors recommend direct action verification but do not evaluate a defense. Next experiment: measure detection and false-alarm rates for trajectory or action-consistency monitors on paired clean and attacked inputs.
