# When Fine-Grained Credit Assignment Helps and Hurts: Multimodal Agentic Reinforcement Learning through Video Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35497) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35497)


## One-sentence takeaway
In this movie-trailer editing setup, decaying turn-level process rewards improves Fine Cut performance over outcome-only GRPO, while fixed process rewards undermine completion and token-level self-distillation provides little benefit or severe degradation (Table 3; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Multimodal editing agents must connect delayed artifact quality to many interdependent perception, planning, and tool-use decisions. The authors ask when trajectory-, turn-, or token-level learning signals help, identifying limited comparative evidence across tasks and training stages under a shared setup (§1).

Video editing provides tasks with different interaction horizons and editing requirements, although those differences also complicate attributing results specifically to horizon.

## Approach and contributions
- **Stagewise task suite:** A film-to-task construction pipeline produces Content Selection, Narrative Sequencing, Rough Cut, and Fine Cut tasks. The experimental dataset contains 716 training tasks from 43 films and 81 validation tasks from six separate films (§4; Table 2).
- **Comparison of learning recipes:** Qwen3.5-9B is trained for 50 updates using outcome-only GRPO, GRPO with fixed or decayed process rewards, or on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). Process judgments come from Gemini 3.8 Flash; subjective outcome judgments use Gemini 3.1 Pro (§5; Appendix C.1).
- **Training-dynamics analysis and mitigation:** The authors document rising process rewards alongside declining outcomes under fixed weighting. Their mitigation reduces the process weight from 0.5 to zero over updates 20–30 (§6.2; Appendix C.1).
- **OPSD failure analysis:** A frozen initial-policy teacher receives either reference answers or next-action suggestions. Disagreement and gradient diagnostics reveal sensitivity to that information, without establishing the cause or an effective remedy (§6.3; Appendix C.5).

These are comparisons of specified training recipes, rather than an isolated test of credit granularity: OPSD also changes the objective and rollout allocation.

## Evidence and main results

All final validation scores below average four sampled evaluations per task across the 81-task held-out set. Overall scores weight task types by their task counts (Appendix C.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome-only RL improves the base policy | Overall score rises from **0.284 to 0.413**, with gains on every task type. | Table 3 |
| Decayed process rewards give the best reported overall result | Overall **0.447**, versus **0.413** for outcome-only GRPO and **0.334** for fixed PRM. | Table 3 |
| The largest additional benefit is on Fine Cut | Decayed PRM scores **0.293**, versus **0.201** for outcome-only GRPO; authors report a **45.4% relative gain**, using unrounded scores. | Abstract; Table 3; Appendix C.3 |
| Shorter-task improvements are limited | Decayed PRM versus outcome-only GRPO: CS **0.804 vs. 0.821**, NS **0.552 vs. 0.500**, RC **0.348 vs. 0.356**. | Table 3 |
| Fixed process rewards accompany budget exhaustion | Across validation rollouts, truncation is **44.1%** with fixed PRM, **0.3%** with outcome-only GRPO, and **6.2%** with decayed PRM. | Table 5 |
| Fine Cut contains more mixed turn quality | On the base policy’s second validation repetition, judged positive/negative-turn mixing is **0.651** for FC versus **0.297–0.390** for the other types. | Table 4; §6.1 |
| OPSD depends strongly on teacher information | Overall score is **0.277** with reference answers and **0.044** with turn suggestions, versus base **0.284**. | Table 3 |
| Suggestion-conditioned OPSD has larger optimization signals | Across 50 retained updates, mean KL is **0.0279 vs. 0.0019**, and mean pre-clipping gradient norm **1.177 vs. 0.084**, compared with reference-conditioned OPSD. | Table 13 |

The evidence supports task-dependent benefits from the tested process-reward schedule and documents failure patterns in these runs. It does **not** establish that longer horizons cause the benefit, that larger gradients cause OPSD collapse, or that finer-grained supervision generally fails. Task metrics differ, so absolute scores across task types are not a shared difficulty scale (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Process-reward conclusions concern LLM-judged GRPO+PRM on trailer editing; extension to other credit-assignment methods remains untested. OPSD remedies remain unresolved (§8).
- **Unequal training exposure:** OPSD samples one rollout per task versus eight for reward-based methods. Equal updates therefore do not match trajectories or generated tokens (Table 9; Appendix C.1).
- **Limited replication:** Four evaluation repetitions vary rollout sampling, not training seeds. The reported configurations use one seed and individual training runs; uncertainty intervals are not supplied (Table 8; Appendix C.1; Figure 4).
- **Analyst inference—horizon is confounded:** Fine Cut differs in action requirements, audiovisual content, and scoring as well as length. Its stronger PRM gains cannot isolate a horizon effect (Table 1; §4.1).
- **Judge validity:** Process judgments use bounded retrospective windows and lack authenticated audio attachments; protocol validation does not guarantee semantic correctness. Subjective outcomes lack a reported human-agreement study (Appendices C.2 and C.6).
- **Restricted task composition:** Stages start independently from reference upstream states; full end-to-end editing and error propagation are outside the evaluation (Appendix A.3).
- **Behavioral proxies:** Reflection markers measure lexical reconsideration, and returned images measure attachments rather than distinct frames or understanding (Appendix C.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does horizon itself determine the value of process rewards?** Task type and horizon vary together here. A next experiment could vary editing length within one task type while holding tools and scoring fixed.
- **How robust is process-weight decay?** Only the reported fixed weight and decay schedule establish the result. Compare several schedules and adaptive completion-based weighting across training seeds.
- **Can OPSD improve with matched resources and validated guidance?** Its weaker results combine different objectives, rollout counts, and teacher information. Match sampled-token budgets and compare independently checked suggestions with reference conditioning.
- **Does concentrated disagreement identify useful supervision?** High-KL turns dominate reference-conditioned disagreement, but usefulness is unverified. Compare token averaging with turn weighting, alongside independent assessment of teacher advice (Appendix C.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed recipes and internally consistent descriptive results, but causal and generalization claims remain limited.

Material missing evidence includes training-seed variability, uncertainty estimates, matched-budget OPSD comparisons, human validation of judge scores, broader schedule and teacher-quality ablations, and end-to-end editing evaluation. Code and model links are supplied, but their availability and contents cannot be verified from the paper text alone.
