# Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05275) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05275)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across diverse model and robot-policy pools, routing delivers substantial reference-relative cost savings, while learned quality gains remain small and recover little of the observed oracle headroom.

## Problem and motivation
General-purpose AI systems can select among specialized models, generators, agents, and robot policies, but must balance output quality against execution cost. The authors identify a gap in routing research’s predominantly language-model focus: whether routing works across modalities, how much observable information helps, and when cheaper candidates preserve sufficient quality (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified routing theory:** Select candidates by expected quality minus weighted resource cost. The framework connects efficiency optimization to reference-relative quality constraints, distinguishes observable routing opportunities from outcome noise, and provides independent-data certification of an aggregate quality floor (§2; Appendix A).
- **Omni-modal evaluation matrix:** The authors report 374,401 scored runs from 452 candidate configurations across 25 benchmarks, using shared inputs within candidate pools. Tasks cover understanding, generation, agents, and manipulation (§3; Appendix B).
- **Learned-router study:** Capability classifiers map inputs to candidate-selection tables; outcome routers predict each candidate’s normalized score. A robot router additionally reads camera frames and robot state. Experiments examine training targets and artifacts caused by unmatched fixed baselines (§4).
- **Cost-sensitive evaluation:** Price-path routing, cascades, verifiers, forecasts, and memory are assessed using recorded outcomes and costs, supplemented by fresh verifier and forecast calls. The claimed novelty is the combined framework and cross-capability study; several mathematical ingredients and router families build on prior work (§§5–6).

The evidence supports efficiency gains relative to the specified references and modest learned quality improvements in selected panels. It does not establish that all oracle headroom is predictable, statistically reliable quality gains across modalities, or exact quality preservation by fitted zero-loss routes. The headline **8.82×** efficiency gain is the arithmetic mean of four heterogeneous testbed ratios, combining dollar and policy-time resources; it is not an end-to-end system speedup (§5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The theory fixes the state distribution and does not cover states changed by earlier routing choices, such as model-dependent SciCode histories. Long-horizon step-level routing remains unexplored; certification requires reference outcomes on every certification input (Appendix D).
- **Oracle optimism:** The realized oracle includes stochastic outcome variation unavailable to an input-only router. On repeated AA-LCR evaluations, its margin disappears when outcomes are averaged (§3).
- **Limited learned-router coverage:** Seven understanding benchmarks are omitted from learned-router studies despite accounting for much of the matrix’s inputs. The headline cross-modal and robot results use one training seed (Table 2; §3).
- **Analyst inference—deployment gap:** Major cascade studies use released scores as oracle verifiers and replay recorded outcomes. Practical verification is separately tested on a smaller coding subset; paid verification can eliminate dollar savings (Appendices C.1, C.4).
- **Aggregate guarantees:** Certification assumes independent, identically distributed calibration inputs from deployment. It does not guarantee subgroup preservation: certified code routing loses quality in a median eight of 21 test cells (Proposition 5; Appendix C.2).
- **Reference sensitivity:** Replacing always Opus with the stronger CellBest reference reduces the reported zero-loss efficiency advantage to 3.18×. Dollar savings also do not imply latency savings (§5.2; Appendices C.2, C.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix B reports differing totals for tasks and decisions without fully reconciling them. Evaluation panels also differ, so scores should be compared within matched panels (Table 2; Appendices B.1–B.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How much oracle headroom is predictable?** Most benchmarks lack repeated outcomes sufficient to separate stochastic variation from input-dependent complementarity. Repeat every candidate on shared inputs, then evaluate routing against expected-outcome rather than single-run oracles (§3).
2. **Do outcome targets generalize better across modalities?** A controlled outcome-versus-winner comparison is provided only for robots. Compare both targets with identical inputs, backbones, seeds, and tie-breaking treatments across generation and agent tasks (§4.3).
3. **Can quality certification survive distribution shifts?** The guarantee assumes deployment-matched independent data. Test certified routes under shifted task mixtures, robot randomization, and changing candidate performance, measuring aggregate and subgroup floor violations (Proposition 5; Appendix C.2).
4. **Can practical verification preserve cascade savings?** Oracle verification dominates the broad replay settings, while paid judging can be uneconomical. Run live cascades with deployable verifiers and charge all calls, routing overhead, and latency (Appendix C.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods, proofs, and appendices, but generalization beyond the evaluated settings remains uncertain.
