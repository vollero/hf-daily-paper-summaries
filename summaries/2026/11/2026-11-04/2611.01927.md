# Is Your Long-Horizon Benchmark Really Deep? Measuring and Synthesizing Depth in Terminal Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01927) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01927)


## One-sentence takeaway
Turn-capped evaluation finds most solved synthetic terminal tasks shallow, while the authors’ execution-grounded generation pipeline produces substantially deeper tasks and trajectories that improve a small model on two terminal benchmarks (§§4.2–4.6).

## Problem and motivation
A terminal agent can batch many predictable operations into one script, so a long uncapped trajectory need not require sustained interaction with the environment. The authors argue that existing turn counts mix task demands with model habits, while generated difficulty labels poorly reflect observed success (§§1, 4.3).

The missing distinction is between **work**, the operations needed when the environment is known, and **depth**, the sequential interactions needed to discover information and choose subsequent commands. This matters for both evaluating persistent agents and generating useful training interactions (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Measurement and audit:** Define idealized work \(W^*\) and depth \(D^*\). Estimate work \(W\) using reference-solution lines and depth \(D\) using the smallest successful stated turn cap across two models. Audit six existing datasets (§§2, 3.1, 4.2).
- **Task generation:** Depth generates segments inside real software environments, executing each segment before recording its solution and checks, then building the next segment from the resulting state. Fresh-container replay determines acceptance; removal experiments subsequently assess whether checks detect key steps and dependencies (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Benchmark and training demonstration:** Construct 105 tasks, evaluate six models, and fine-tune Qwen3-8B on successful trajectory prefixes, split to include continuation after context compression (§§3.5, 4.4–4.6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Most solved synthetic tasks require few interactions | Of 296 solved synthetic tasks across four datasets, 278 (94%) pass with \(D\leq3\); solved means DeepSeek passes at least one of two uncapped runs. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Depth tracks difficulty beyond work | Across 105 solved TB2/TW tasks, rank partial correlation between \(D\) and public pass rate, controlling for \(W\), is −0.31. Uncapped turns \(N\), controlling for \(D\), correlate at +0.06 (95% CI −0.18 to +0.26). | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Constructed tasks have greater measured depth and work | Among 54 solved Depth tasks, median \(D=40\) and median \(W=315\) lines; existing datasets have median \(D=1\)–2. Of those 54 tasks, 44 have \(D\geq40\). | §§4.2, 4.4; Table 1 |
| Merging instructions usually does not reduce measured depth | In 25 sampled solved tasks, 17 show no decrease: eight pass at the original cap and nine fail within it. Seven others drop one cap level and one drops two. The merged protocol uses DeepSeek alone. | §4.4; Appendix H |
| Checks usually detect omitted key steps | Removing a segment’s key step causes its checks to report failure in 598/604 completed replays (99.0%); next-segment checks fail in 421/500 (84.2%). | §4.4; Appendix F, Table 8 |
| Evaluated models remain below half passing | Two uncapped runs per task: pass@1 ranges from 17% for GLM-5.3-Flash to 47% for Claude Sonnet 5.5; GPT-6.1 Sol scores 44%. Denominators exclude unavailable results for the latter two models. | §4.5; Table 2; Appendix I |
| Fine-tuning transfers to single-instruction benchmarks | Qwen3-8B non-thinking SFT uses 4,941 samples from 1,272 runs. With three evaluation runs per task, pass@1 rises from 2.6% to 4.9% on TB2.1 and from 3.4% to 9.1% on TBLite versus the same-mode base model. | §4.6; Figure 7 |

These results support the authors’ distinction between operation volume and required interaction within the tested setting. They establish an association between measured depth and difficulty, plus transfer from the generated trajectories. They do not establish intrinsic depth independent of the solver and harness, or isolate depth as the cause of the training improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ measurement qualification:** \(D\) depends on the models, harness, and one initial environment state; it is an estimate rather than a bound on idealized \(D^*\). Waiting for commands and reading truncated outputs also contribute to depth (§2; Appendix B).
- **Selection and success conditioning:** Depth is unavailable when DeepSeek fails both uncapped runs; the second measurement model only attempts to lower estimates on DeepSeek-solved tasks. Sampling favors previously solvable tasks and excludes substantial dependency requirements. Label reweighting supports the audit’s direction but cannot characterize excluded tasks (Appendices B–C).
- **Work is a proxy:** Solution lines depend on coding style and need not represent minimum work. Token and character recounts support similar dataset rankings, but TMax uses model-generated commands because reference solutions are unavailable (Appendix D).
- **Dependencies are imperfectly enforced:** Key-step removal does not always break later segments. Removal tests are post-generation diagnostics, not acceptance requirements; incomplete replays are excluded (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Model rankings use unequal task coverage because refusals and empty responses are excluded. Existing tasks and Depth tasks also receive different time limits, complicating direct difficulty comparisons (Appendices B, I).
- **Analyst inference—training attribution:** The supplied text reports no matched-data comparison against shallower trajectories or ablation of compression training. The gain therefore cannot be assigned specifically to depth (§4.6; Appendix J).

## Open research questions
- **How stable is depth across solvers and harnesses?** Two-model robustness is examined, but \(D\) remains protocol-dependent. Repeat measurement with additional solvers, more trials, and varied output and waiting limits.
- **Does depth itself improve training transfer?** The reported SFT experiment changes the full training-data package. Compare deep and shallow trajectories matched for work, domains, tokens, and teacher models.
- **Does measured depth persist across equivalent environment states?** \(D^*\) considers multiple possible states, whereas evaluation uses one. Vary instruction-unspecified versions, configurations, and file contents, then compare depth rankings.
- **How much do verifier gaps affect observed difficulty?** Some removed key steps leave subsequent checks passing. Strengthen those checks and reassess depth and model success on the affected tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial experimental detail and robustness checks, but depth remains an empirical proxy with restricted coverage.

Material missing information includes a task/code/checkpoint release location, generation-model identity and full prompts, SFT optimization settings, numerical uncertainty for the transfer gains, and matched-data training ablations.
