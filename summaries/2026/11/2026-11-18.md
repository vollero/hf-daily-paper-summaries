# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-18

## Executive summary

- Three video generation papers target different costs. MPEG-Diffusion (`2611.12069`) reduces denoising work, PanDWM (`2611.19857`) distills a four-step panoramic generator, and FastFrames (`2611.23226`) reduces response latency by changing RGB decoding and cache refinement.
- In clinical data, Glyph (`2611.08922`) reports replication of prognostic associations across retrospective cohorts. Breath-Dx (`2611.15348`) finds that selected electronic-nose screening comparisons score lower on held-out collection weeks than on random splits.
- Two studies show why aggregate evaluation can miss important failures. Serving changes sometimes rivaled 8-bit quantization in their effect on individual decisions (`2611.15671`), while nominal 90% rotation cones calibrated on synthetic images covered only 9.0–15.4% of sunlamp images in two fine-tuned arms on SPEED+ (`2611.20321`).
- Structured records of harness edits improved held-out coding continuation over carrying the harness alone (`2611.18856`). In a separate gameplay study, three of six frozen models improved on held-out puzzles by designing practice and revising player context (`2611.23219`).
- On-policy distillation changed how agents sought feedback and used tools, with a trade-off between conversational and household accuracy under selective supervision (`2611.19244`). In a separate multimodal recipe, reinforcement learning after specialist distillation raised mean benchmark accuracy from 75.2% to 75.9% (`2611.23034`).
- Executable checks support two different software training pipelines. RepoFactory (`2611.21152`) validates repository reconstruction tasks with runnable tests, while AuXSpec (`2611.21421`) uses executable Lean postconditions to train specification generators.
- Robot studies cover distinct parts of the learning and control pipeline. ResWAM (`2611.19559`) isolates the effect of predicting visual feature changes in simulation. ForceHarness (`2611.21099`) reports a large gain on one physical arm setup. HubEgo (`2611.21938`) provides first-person work recordings, with downstream policy gains still unmeasured.

## Main results worth noting

- **Denoising and response latency measure different portions of the video pipeline.** `2611.12069` reports 4.8× faster denoising than full-resolution Wan2.1 14B at 720p on one B200, with DOVER lower by 0.025. `2611.23226` reports 36 ms per response at 480p on one B200. Its quality comparison with Causal Forcing++ is qualitative in the retained evidence.
- **Synthetic calibration failed under directional lighting.** For the two main fine-tuned arms in `2611.20321`, nominal 90% synthetic-calibrated rotation cones covered 9.0–15.4% of SPEED+ physical mockup sunlamp cases.
- **Visual target choice mattered at matched compute.** `2611.19559` reports a 2.9-percentage-point increase in mean RoboTwin 2.0 success for predicting future-minus-current features compared with predicting full-future features. The matched comparison was in simulation.
- **The contact interface changed physical task success.** `2611.21099` reports GPT-6 Astra completing 54/60 zero-shot trials with ForceHarness versus 2/60 in the comparison condition across six tasks on a Franka FR3 without a wrist force sensor.
- **Both software training studies report held-out gains.** On NL2Repo, `2611.21152` reports 5.1 accuracy points over supervised training without environment evolution and 22.6 over the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B base. On held-out AuXSpec-Test, `2611.21421` reports 84.3% SpecGen pass@4 after training versus 14.3% for its 27B starting model.
- **The serving baseline matters for quantization attribution.** On two Clef decision models on one GB10, `2611.15671` finds that both 4-bit builds flipped more top-1 choices than any measured engine, restart, batch, or kernel variation. Q8_0 sometimes fell within the measured serving variation.
- **Matched examples expose a multilingual visual gap.** In `2611.21644`, every one of 25 evaluated models scored lower on average when charts and tables were rendered in non-English languages versus English, with the question and requested answer held in English.

## Open research directions

1. **Disentangle clinical transfer effects** (`2611.08922`, `2611.15348`). Freeze the concept extractors or classifiers before evaluation. Compare held-out sites for Glyph and collection periods crossed with devices for Breath-Dx. Report performance by cohort or condition and record changes in recruitment and acquisition.
2. **Measure a common video latency–quality trade-off** (`2611.12069`, `2611.23226`, `2610.20556`). On a compatible backbone and fixed hardware, vary spatial compute, decoding and cache changes, and output commitment separately. Report denoising time, control-to-visible-response latency, and quality under the same controls.
3. **Audit executable verifiers with independently written cases** (`2611.21152`, `2611.21421`, `2610.40256`). Use blinded reviews of correct alternatives, incomplete solutions, and plausible incorrect solutions. Report false acceptance and rejection, including specification tasks with several valid outputs.
4. **Isolate what carries over in frozen-agent improvement** (`2611.18856`, `2611.23219`). Cross inherited edit history with target-stage updates while matching context and rollout budgets. Test held-out target gains and retention of source-task performance.
5. **Test visual residual supervision on physical robots** (`2611.19559`). Compare residual and full-future feature targets with identical architectures and budgets across multiple training seeds on physical FR3 tasks. Separate task success by contact type and report end-to-end control latency.
6. **Measure error turnover under serving changes** (`2611.15671`). Repeat batch-size comparisons within a pinned-kernel process and across fresh processes for both Clef models. Report paired flip directions alongside accuracy and distinguish restart variance from batch effects.
7. **Audit visual evidence use after distillation** (`2611.19244`, `2611.23034`). Use matched rollouts in which relevant evidence arrives late or tool feedback changes a valid action. Score whether the model waits for evidence and whether its final answer is supported, alongside task accuracy.
8. **Separate pose accuracy from uncertainty transfer** (`2611.20321`). Compare calibration procedures at fixed backbone and augmentation settings, then report empirical coverage and cone size separately across held-out lighting conditions.

## Coverage and uncertainty

- **Current papers.** All 39 supplied papers are covered. Every `PAPER_ANALYSIS` block is compacted and was treated as partial evidence. The retained text frequently omits full tables, run-level uncertainty, and release details.
- **Collection failures.** Hugging Face Markdown returned HTML for nine papers. Supplied analyses were still available for `2611.18856`, `2611.19244`, `2611.19559`, `2611.20321`, `2611.21791`, `2611.22275`, `2611.22565`, `2611.23140`, and `2611.23144`.
- **Downstream stages.** Taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical connections were all available. Four same-day and eleven cross-day edges were retained after checking the supplied endpoint evidence. The proposed GameRealm-to-π⁴ training dependency was omitted because the retained π⁴ excerpt cuts off its training-data description. The historical M3OPD–Reframe edge was also omitted because the retained Reframe excerpt leaves the claimed student-rollout and repair details incomplete.
- **Historical evidence.** The 50 supplied candidates use compacted cached analyses. No listing summaries were supplied. The wider service history is outside the supplied candidate pool.
- **Lower-confidence conclusions.** AURA’s detailed memory mechanism, FastFrames’ numerical quality parity, the reconciliation of Multi-Branch Transformer’s throughput summaries, and the exact frame count for GameRealm remain unresolved. The GameRealm–GameVerse evaluation is an editorial proposal contingent on asset access. Clinical deployment, physical machining fidelity, and HubEgo’s downstream policy benefit remain unverified in the supplied evidence.
