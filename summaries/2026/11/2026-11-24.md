# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-11-24

## Executive summary

- **Executable rewards can reject valid work.** MobileGym’s reference-path restriction reduced success when agents were evaluated in the full environment. An execution-backed SWE-Gym audit found tests that rejected valid alternative patches, and training on audited tasks improved held-out repair pass@1 in a controlled comparison (`2611.23946`, `2611.30207`).
- **The clock changes what an agent can accomplish.** Action delegation improved live browser-game performance in AutoPILOT, while tested frontier models recorded zero live wins at medium difficulty in GameDojo. RUIBench found that the relative advantage of program execution reversed on its five-task physical robot subset (`2611.28202`, `2611.30771`, `2611.31115`).
- **Selective visual memory helped when evidence arrived early and mattered later.** MomentMem retained briefly visible manipulation cues, VideoJSON searched timestamped records without raw video at query time, and MORSE revised what a frozen video model kept and retrieved (`2611.26745`, `2611.28167`, `2611.30011`).
- **Final-answer accuracy can conceal problems in the reasoning.** Outcome-reward training increased selection of certain previously reinforced false claims on multiple-choice probes. Spatial VQA interventions showed that a written relation could reduce sensitivity to the image, while LUCID found that expert feedback and statistical correction helped different parts of reasoning evaluation (`2611.26863`, `2611.28665`, `2611.31595`).
- **The systems papers report different kinds of fidelity.** Tritify reproduced a single-host integer computation bit for bit across three machines. Feedback Shortcut measured a throughput–quality trade-off against exact decoding, and NoHairCache tested perplexity under simulated cache eviction (`2611.13730`, `2611.28227`, `2611.29006`).
- **Shared visual representations supported several distinct outputs.** Plan4D and WorldLatent decoded appearance and geometry from shared latents. UNIFIED shared its Transformer across encoding, denoising, and decoding, while Integral reused visual representations for image synthesis and understanding (`2611.26516`, `2611.31209`, `2611.29064`, `2611.31237`).
- **Editorial inference.** The verifier studies, clock-controlled evaluations, and paired visual interventions make a common case for instrumenting how a result was achieved. Their measurements answer different questions and support comparisons within their respective protocols.

## Main results worth noting

- **Verifier quality and repair training.** At equal dataset size and matched difficulty, Qwen3.5-9B trained with GRPO on audited SWE-Gym tasks gained **2.4 percentage points in pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified-397** and **2.7 on SWE-bench Pro v2** versus training on original tasks. Filtering out never-solved tasks gave no average gain over the original set (`2611.30207`).
- **Live control.** With GPT-6.1 Sol under the same nominal budgets on live AFK-Bench, AutoPILOT achieved **68.6% episode success versus 38.3% for VISTA**. In a distinct evaluation, the tested frontier models recorded **zero live wins on medium-difficulty GameDojo encounters**, and the trained GameDojo-4B led learned models in real-time progress on the keyboard-and-mouse interface at each tested difficulty (`2611.28202`, `2611.30771`).
- **Correcting visual plans.** ViDecide reached **83.8% mean strict success versus 23.3% for standard fine-tuning** across four puzzle and simulated robot test sets. This is a comparison of the full recipe, which includes causal generation, stopping, noise scheduling, and corrective fine-tuning (`2611.26389`).
- **Retention and contact in manipulation.** MomentMem reached **49.9% mean closed-loop VMBench success versus 18.2% for EventVLA**, the strongest main-table baseline. In a separate simulation protocol, DexDuet preserved contact better than DexMachina in **117 of 122 pairs matched for object-tracking accuracy** (`2611.26745`, `2611.30495`).
- **Faster decoding with measured approximation.** At batch one and with one pipelined correction, Feedback Shortcut reported **2.36× and 2.75× exact-decoding throughput** for 135M and 780M models, respectively, with a difference within **\(10^{-4}\) bits per byte on Paloma** (`2611.28227`).
- **Forecasting and reconstruction.** PersistWorld gained **19.13 mean occupied-voxel IoU points over SparseWorld-TC** at 1–3 s on Occ3D-nuScenes with ground-truth future ego poses. ViDiHand2 reduced **absolute 3D joint error with a missed-detection penalty from 56.55 to 32.59 mm** versus the strongest retrained baseline on held-out Gen-HumanEgo (`2611.30573`, `2611.30597`).
- **Specific false claims after RL.** Across six open models trained on QuALITY and TriviaQA with final-answer rewards, multiple-choice false-claim selection rose by **4.83 percentage points** for claims from reinforced rollouts versus **0.17 points** for claims from held-out questions. The measured effect is selection on checkpoint probes (`2611.26863`).
- **A formal existence result.** Under the Krasner norm axiom, a presenting field yields the prescribed relative-error quotient, and a presentation exists for value group \(\mathbb Z\). Counterexamples clarify the role of the stronger axiom by using weaker valuation axioms (`2611.29473`).

## Open research directions

1. **Measure how often verifiers deny credit to valid alternatives.** Independently validate multiple GUI routes and repository fixes, then train matched policies with reference-only and goal-based rewards. Evaluate valid-alternative acceptance, invalid-solution acceptance, and held-out task success (`2611.23946`, `2611.30207`).
2. **Separate action cadence from control quality under a live clock.** Cross per-action control, open-loop batching, and feedback-enabled delegation in the same encounters or robot tasks. Hold the model, time budget, and action abstraction fixed, and record useful actions per second, failure recovery, and final success (`2611.28202`, `2611.30771`, `2611.31115`).
3. **Test when endpoint scoring needs intermediate checks.** Compare endpoint-only candidate scoring with selected intermediate trajectory checks on the same constraint-bearing tasks and candidate budget. Report goal completion, constraint violations, and planning latency separately (`2611.25927`, `2611.30989`).
4. **Find which visual evidence survives memory compression.** On questions with verified evidence timestamps, compare captions, retained keyframes, and caption-guided frame inspection at matched query budgets. Measure evidence recall and answer accuracy separately, alongside indexing cost and tail latency (`2611.28167`, `2611.30011`, `2611.22790`).
5. **Test whether process scores track independently judged soundness.** Gather activations and blinded expert step labels on the same math rollouts, stratify by final-answer correctness, and compare APRM with expert-calibrated text scores. In a paired training study, also test whether process supervision changes free-form recurrence of previously rewarded false claims (`2611.30627`, `2611.31595`, `2611.26863`).
6. **Isolate the value of geometry and motion supervision for robot control.** Give equal-capacity policies the same clips and training budget, varying whether targets come from reconstructed hands, video-derived action latents, or matched alternatives. Evaluate physical task success, contact retention, and robustness to camera movement (`2611.30597`, `2611.30898`, `2611.30495`).
7. **Measure GUI grounding inside a complete workflow.** Swap PixelOne and a crop-assisted grounder into the same policy, then stratify workflow completion, click errors, recovery, and end-to-end latency by screen density and target size (`2611.30235`, `2611.23946`, `2610.33645`).
8. **Separate repeated solver gains from editor improvement.** Compare frozen and updated editors at identical solver checkpoints under the ordered interface, with equal feedback and proposal budgets. Evaluate independently repeated edit sequences and retention on untouched tasks (`2611.28833`, `2611.25476`, `2611.09382`).
