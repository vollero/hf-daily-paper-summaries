# AutoCompress: Automated Hardware-Guided Model Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08290) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08290)


## One-sentence takeaway
AutoCompress jointly searches global and per-layer LLM structure using deployment-specific cost models, improving reported benchmark accuracy over an adapted Minitron baseline while meeting tested hardware budgets (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Parameter counts do not reliably predict serving latency, throughput, or memory, leaving practitioners to manually translate deployment requirements into pruning targets. The authors also argue that uniform layer dimensions restrict capacity allocation and that weight-plus-cache memory estimates omit substantial runtime allocations (§1; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint architecture search:** An importance-ordered, weight-sharing supernet and Gumbel-softmax router jointly select global hidden size and Mamba head count, plus per-layer expert counts and FFN widths, including layer removal. Distillation and resource penalties guide selection (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Serving-aware cost models:** Measured layer runtimes estimate latency; explicit accounting covers weights, caches, workspaces, runtime buffers, and calibrated residual memory (§3).
- **Automated compression pipeline:** The selected architecture is extracted from reordered parent weights and recovered through distillation. The contribution combines deployment constraints with heterogeneous search; importance ordering, distillation, and Gumbel-softmax are acknowledged prior techniques (§§2.1–2.4, 7).

These author-reported results support improved quality under the tested budgets relative to **Minitron++**, the deployment-adapted baseline. They do not establish superiority across pruning methods or deployment environments. Numerical H100 throughput measurements and detailed cost-prediction errors are unavailable in the supplied figure captions and prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Additive latency estimates can miss architecture-dependent kernel selection, cache behavior, and overhead (§3.1). The expensive large-scale case study omits Minitron++ (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Results cover a narrow model family and serving engine; transfer to other architectures, engines, or changing workloads is unestablished (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The baseline differs in both search procedure and architectural flexibility. Its uniform dimensions and retained layers prevent attributing gains solely to differentiable search (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The initialization ablation changes initialization and warmup together, so their separate effects remain unresolved (§6.1).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Large-scale recovery allocations of 68B and 48B tokens do not sum to the stated 120B total. Displayed means imply a 2.84-point difference rather than 2.85; rounding could explain the latter (§5.3).
- The large-scale mean mixes an index with percentages, limiting interpretation of “95% retention” as a uniform accuracy measure (Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **How much improvement comes from heterogeneous structure versus the search algorithm?** The baseline changes both. Compare search methods within identical homogeneous and heterogeneous spaces (§4.3).
- **How robust are selected models to workload shifts?** Cost models target fixed deployment settings. Evaluate unchanged models across context lengths, concurrency levels, and cache configurations (§3).
- **Does the method generalize beyond hybrid Nemotron models?** Supplied experiments do not establish this. Repeat matched-budget comparisons on a different architecture family (§4.1).
- **Does long-context task quality survive compression?** Long-context serving feasibility is reported, but task-specific quality at those deployment lengths is not established. Evaluate coding or retrieval tasks at the target contexts (§§4.1, 5.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline comparisons are clearly described, but the supplied text omits table bodies and numerical figure contents. Material gaps include complete candidate values, absolute benchmark scores, detailed architectures, timing-model errors, exact H100 throughput, training-data composition, uncertainty across repeated runs, and code/model release availability.
