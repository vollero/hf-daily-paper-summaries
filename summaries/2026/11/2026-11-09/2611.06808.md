# LLMs Don&#x27;t Know What They Know: Benchmarking Temporal Self-Knowledge for Financial Backtesting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06808) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06808)


## One-sentence takeaway
On dated U.S. financial facts, models can correctly answer outcomes they deny knowing, and historical-date instructions largely change knowledge claims without reducing accuracy, undermining these safeguards against backtest leakage (§§4.4–4.5).

## Problem and motivation
Historical financial forecasts can benefit from outcomes already stored in an LLM’s parameters, inflating apparent predictive skill. Practitioners rely on vendor cutoffs, model self-reports, and instructions to answer from a historical date, but the authors identify a missing joint evaluation of factual knowledge and explicit knowledge claims on the same dated facts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Aligned benchmark:** FinDendro contains 2,226 U.S. financial facts spanning 1999–2026, covering macroeconomic observations, stock prices, and Federal Reserve outcomes. Historical values use first releases and prices adjusted back to their contemporary quotations (§3.1).
- **Paired answer-and-claim tasks:** Each fact produces four prompts: value-only, Recall with a knowledge claim, Predict with a knowledge claim, and Predict with a historical-date instruction. Shared candidates support direct comparisons across formulations (§3.2).
- **Controlled measurement and boundary estimation:** Numerical scoring excludes facts solved by previous-month persistence; policy scoring uses balanced accuracy. Rolling factual accuracy and claim rates yield separate behavioral knowledge and claim boundaries (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Safeguard audit:** Experiments test whether reported cutoffs, disclaimer filtering, and historical-date instructions separate known outcomes from predictions, with smaller open and point-in-time models as comparisons (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy and knowledge claims diverge | GPT-5: 76% Task 1 accuracy on 1,340 controlled facts versus 38% Recall knowledge claims across all 2,226 facts; chance accuracy ≈0.27. These measures use different denominators. | §§3.4, 4.2; Table 4 |
| Claim boundaries can lag behavioral boundaries | Llama-3.3-70B’s measured knowledge ends at 2021Q2, while claims continue to 2023Q1: seven quarters. GPT-5’s boundaries both fall at 2024Q3. | §4.3; Figures 1, 3 |
| Disclaimers retain substantial factual accuracy | Grouping controlled Task 1 answers by separate Recall claims, GPT-5 scores 95% on claimed-known facts and 61% on disclaimed facts. Frontier within-period gaps range from 0.08 to 0.39; all reported 95% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Filtering does not reliably remove in-window knowledge | GPT-5 retains 46% of controlled in-window facts after excluding claimed-known facts; retained accuracy is 0.75, versus 0.33 after its measured boundary. | §4.4; Table 6 |
| Historical-date prompting mainly changes claims | GPT-5’s Predict knowledge-claim rate falls from 0.39 to 0.01, while factual accuracy changes from 0.76 to 0.75. Claim rates use the full sample; factual accuracy uses controlled facts. | §4.5; Table 4 |
| Dated “predictions” remain more accurate inside the knowledge window | On controlled Task 3 facts, GPT-5 labels 99% of in-window answers as predictions; their accuracy is 0.82, versus 0.39 after its boundary. | §4.5; Table 7 |
| Point-in-time controls show no detectable boundary signal | Candidate-likelihood scoring yields accuracy of 0.25–0.30 before and after training cutoffs; differences are ≤0.041, with all 95% bootstrap intervals including zero. | §4.7; Table 8 |

The evidence establishes that disclaimers and date instructions do not reliably identify unknown outcomes in these tasks. Knowledge claims still contain useful information: claimed-known facts generally receive more accurate answers. However, correct multiple-choice answers do not prove memorization, and the study does not establish leakage-free periods or quantify effects on actual trading returns.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One economy, monthly observations, and three fact families limit transfer to other regions, frequencies, and text-based events (Limitations).
- **Author-stated identification risk:** Persistence controls remove only a simple forecasting strategy; trend extrapolation can still produce correct answers without recall (Limitations).
- **Boundary sensitivity:** GPT-4o’s estimated claim–knowledge gap changes from one to six quarters under an alternative threshold; estimates become unstable near chance (§4.3).
- **Author-stated reproducibility limits:** API-router versions may change, each prompt receives one response, and multiple-choice claims may differ from free-form or probabilistic self-reports (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate accuracy–claim differences are not direct calibration errors because accuracy uses controlled facts while claim rates use the full sample (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Near-chance results from small point-in-time models cannot determine whether sufficiently capable models with strict training cutoffs would provide useful controls (§4.7).

## Open research questions
- **How much above-chance accuracy reflects recall rather than inference?** Persistence controls leave richer extrapolation strategies unresolved. Next experiment: compare against stronger forecasting baselines using information available at each historical date.
- **Do findings transfer to other claim formats?** The study uses paired multiple-choice claims. Next experiment: repeat the aligned evaluation with free-form answers and probabilistic knowledge estimates.
- **Can behavioral boundaries guide real forecasting evaluations safely?** The paper tests factual probes and a simulated dated backtest, without establishing downstream trading effects. Next experiment: compare prospective forecasting performance with historical performance across estimated boundaries.
- **Would larger point-in-time models yield detectable boundaries?** Tested controls remain near chance. Next experiment: apply the same probes to more capable models with documented historical training restrictions.
