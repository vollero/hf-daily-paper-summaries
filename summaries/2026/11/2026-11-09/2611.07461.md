# The Last Mile to the Real World: Agent-Native Primitives as Reusable Building Blocks for Solving Real-World Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07461) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07461)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors propose reusable resources that apply themselves through local LLM reasoning, with strong results across individual task families, while controlled evaluation of composition across families remains a proposed protocol.

## Problem and motivation

Agents can retrieve tools, code, proof methods, and documents yet still fail to apply them correctly. The authors identify three gaps: adapting a resource to the current context, passing requirements between dependent steps, and tracing failures to the work that needs revision (§1).

Their central argument is that reusable resources should carry the competence to apply themselves, reducing the burden on a central agent that otherwise reconstructs this competence for each task.

## Approach and contributions

- **Agent-native primitives:** Each primitive pairs a resource with a resident LLM, a discoverable interface, and outputs comprising results and messages to other primitives. Exact implemented procedures can replace the resident LLM where appropriate (§3.2).
- **HEELO framework:** Selects and composes a task-specific graph, executes and verifies it, revises implicated work, and admits improved primitives through a separate validation path (§3.3).
- **Eight instantiations:** Tool, Code, Math, Knowledge, Agent, Dev, Navigation, and Harness Primitives specialize the abstraction for different tasks (§3.4). The paper unifies evidence from eight companion manuscripts rather than presenting every result as a new integrated-system experiment (Appendix D).
- **Assets and deployment:** The authors report ToolFace, CodeFace, Lean-checked Method Contracts, the LEGO-REPO reconstruction benchmark, and the heelo.dev action platform (§1; Appendix D). Public release and access conditions are not established in the supplied text.
- **Conditional theory:** Results characterize demand-bounded activation, task-conditioned versus fixed composition, and retrieval-pool coverage ceilings under explicit assumptions (§3.5).

All entries below are author-reported results.

|---|---|---|

The strongest causal evidence comes from matched-backbone and budget controls within families. These results do not establish one uniform gain across tasks: metrics, budgets, and comparators differ. Appendix E specifies a controlled cross-family evaluation protocol; it does not report its outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Coverage limitation:** The source is substantially truncated, preventing complete assessment of methods, results, and limitations.
- **Author-stated scope:** LEGO delivery is normalized test performance, not binary repository success; field ablations add information cumulatively, making attribution order-dependent (Appendix D.2).
- **Author-stated scope:** Math selection uses annotated official-solution methods. Downstream gains combine selection and contract content, and most proofs remain unsolved (Appendix D.3).
- **Author-stated scope:** Knowledge QA uses one LLM judge that is also an evaluated backend. Grounding checks are model judgments, and audits find partially supported or unsupported learned units (Appendix D.4).
- **Author-reported inconsistency:** An Agent Primitives cost table repeats Organizer accuracies that disagree with its sensitivity table (Appendix D.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Bounded activation does not imply bounded discovery cost; whole-library assessment can still grow with library size (§3.5; Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Family-level successes and the platform comparison do not isolate the benefits of general cross-family interoperability. Appendix E explicitly proposes the necessary controls.

## Open research questions

1. **Does cross-family composition beat comparable asset access?** Appendix E supplies a protocol without outcomes. Run paired API-migration tasks requiring both source understanding and code changes, comparing HEELO with monolithic, executable-reuse, and fixed-composition controls.
2. **Do local checks remain reliable after composition?** Appendix E warns that composed requests may change a verifier’s input distribution. Measure false passes using independent hidden checks on multi-family tasks.
3. **When does library growth stop being economical?** Activation bounds exclude discovery costs. Increase irrelevant library entries while holding demand and relevant coverage fixed; measure total cost, latency, recall, and success.
4. **How robust are primitives to resource drift?** Appendix E proposes schema and dependency changes without reported results. Introduce controlled updates and measure invalidation, revalidation cost, and recovery.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied sections and evidence ledger support the framework description and selected results, but the source is truncated.

Material missing information includes complete experimental tables and uncertainty estimates, detailed heelo.dev task and grading records, full lifecycle cost accounting, artifact release/access details, and completed controlled cross-family evaluation results.
