# LowRAM: Low-Rank Keys and Amortised Values for Composable KV Cache Compression at Test-Time

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07025) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07025)


## One-sentence takeaway
LowRAM combines context-fitted key-to-value predictors with low-rank key compression, reporting strong cache savings on long-context benchmarks while exposing trade-offs between adaptation cost, accuracy, and context length (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
KV caches grow with context length and batch size, constraining decoding memory and throughput. The authors identify within-head key–value predictability as an underused compression axis alongside token eviction, quantisation, and low-rank approximation. Their central motivation is asymmetric compression: predict values from keys, while exploiting low-rank structure to compress the keys themselves (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Value cache amortisation:** Fit an independent two-layer MLP per layer and KV head to reconstruct values from keys during prefill. Store predictor parameters and residual corrections for the tokens with the largest reconstruction errors. The base language model remains frozen (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Asymmetric integration:** Combine the new value representation with existing xKV cross-layer low-rank key compression. Prediction uses reconstructed pre-RoPE keys; positional rotations are reapplied for attention. Newly generated KV entries remain exact (§3).
- **Efficiency and composition:** Use a FOMAML initialisation to reduce context adaptation, and adapt landmark-guided selective reconstruction to avoid rebuilding the full cache during decoding. Evaluate composition with eviction and quantisation (§§3.3, 4.4).

The novelty rests chiefly in context-adapted, per-head value prediction with selective residual storage and its integration into LowRAM; xKV, FOMAML, and landmark retrieval are adopted components.

## Evidence and main results

All performance recovery figures below are relative to the corresponding uncompressed baseline unless identified as task accuracy.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Current-layer keys are the strongest tested prediction source for values | On Mistral-7B LongBench, the source–target comparison holds compression at 3× and fixes the residual budget; key-to-value prediction preserves the most downstream performance among tested configurations. | §3.1, Figure 3 |
| Moderate full-cache compression preserves much benchmark performance | At 3× compression, Mistral-7B recovers 98.8% on RULER 4K and 99.4% on LongBench; Llama-3.1-8B recovers 97.9% and 98.5%, respectively. These results use 150 adaptation updates and full reconstruction. | §§4.2–4.3; Table 10 |
| Value amortisation works independently of key compression | With exact keys, Llama-3.1-8B retains 98.2% on RULER and 99.3% on LongBench at 4× **value-cache** compression; configuration uses 300 updates without warm-starting or the Slim branch. | §4.5, Table 5; Appendix D.1 |
| Meta-initialisation sharply reduces fitting overhead | Value-amortisation-only TTFT is reported as 1.3× baseline with 2 updates, versus 26.3× with 150 and 55.5× with 300; measured at 4K tokens, batch size 4, with exact keys. | §4.3; Appendix F, Figure 6 |
| Faster operation has an accuracy cost | At 4× compression on Llama-3.1-8B, meta-initialisation plus selective reconstruction recovers 92.4–96.8% on RULER 16–64K and 92.9% on LongBench >10K. Meta-initialisation costs 1.4–4.4 recovery points on RULER and 2.4 on LongBench relative to 300-update adaptation. | §4.3, Table 2 |
| Selective reconstruction improves peak throughput at long contexts | Llama-3.1-8B LowRAM-SR at 4× compression reaches roughly 5× the peak throughput of exact KV attention at 262K tokens. The baseline remains faster at small batch sizes. | §4.3, Figure 4 |
| The composed stack reaches aggressive compression | On Mistral-7B LongBench prompts >10K tokens, LowRAM-EQ achieves 26.9× compression with 94.5% recovery. Standalone LowRAM achieves 9×/90.7%; eviction 10×/91.6%; quantisation 7.5×/92.3%. | §4.4; Table 11 |
| Residual selection matters materially | On Llama-3.1-8B RULER NIAH-Multikey-1 at 4K, full LowRAM at 4× with 2-update meta-initialisation obtains 98% accuracy using error-ranked residuals, versus below 40% with random selection, over 200 samples. | Appendix D.1, Figure 5 |

These results support the usefulness of asymmetric compression in the evaluated settings. They do **not** establish that the strongest accuracy, lowest TTFT, and highest throughput occur in one configuration. In particular, the 1.3× TTFT figure isolates value fitting and excludes key factorisation; the extreme-compression result concerns one model and a filtered benchmark subset.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Long generations are unevaluated. Generated KV entries remain exact, so sustained generation would require periodic suffix compression (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Fixed predictor storage makes short contexts less suitable; eviction can worsen this by shrinking the retained sequence without shrinking predictor parameters (§§4.4–5; Figure 8).
- **Author-stated:** How much of the mapping transfers across contexts, and whether difficult tokens remain difficult across contexts, is unresolved (§6).
- **Observed scope:** Quantisation does not improve every setting: adding it lowers Llama LongBench recovery across the tested LowRAM ratios (§4.4). Qwen compression ratios cover only its full-attention-layer cache, not whole-model memory (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Broad deployment conclusions remain limited by evaluation on three model families and the concentration of efficiency results on Llama and extreme-compression results on Mistral. Benchmark averages also cannot establish uniform preservation across individual tasks.
- **Supplied-text gap:** Several result and configuration tables appear only as captions, limiting inspection of complete comparisons and reproduction settings.

## Open research questions

1. **Can compression remain stable during long generation?** The paper explicitly leaves this unevaluated (§6). A next experiment could periodically compress generated suffixes while measuring memory growth, refresh latency, and quality over extended outputs.
2. **How transferable is the learned mapping?** Shared versus context-specific structure remains unresolved (§6). Compare warm-started, meta-initialised, and fully adapted predictors on distinct text domains under matched fitting and memory budgets.
3. **Can residual allocation better reflect downstream importance?** Reconstruction error is an imperfect downstream proxy (§3.1), although error ranking beats random selection in the tested ablation. Compare error-ranked, attention-aware, and combined selection at equal byte budgets across multiple tasks.
4. **When does full adaptation repay its cost in serving?** Context reuse is motivated but no reuse-count break-even measurement is supplied (§5). Measure total latency and throughput across increasing query counts per shared prefix.
