# Knowing When to Teach: Learning from Teachable Prefixes in Multilingual Reasoning Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08179) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08179)


## One-sentence takeaway
TOPSD improves multilingual mathematical reasoning by distilling only adaptively selected student prefixes from which a reference-conditioned teacher can still recover the correct answer, outperforming the reported baselines across three Qwen3 scales (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy self-distillation supervises student-generated reasoning with predictions from the same underlying model given reference solutions. The authors identify **Teacher Drift**: as incorrect student reasoning accumulates, the privileged teacher can begin following those errors despite knowing the reference solution. Their analysis suggests this happens earlier in lower-resource languages (§4.1; Figure 2).

The identified gap is supervision reliability along a trajectory: strengthening the teacher with English references does not establish that its feedback remains useful at every student state (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Characterize Teacher Drift.** The authors jointly examine increasing teacher–student likelihood alignment and declining correct-answer recovery as student prefixes grow (§4.1).
- **Select trajectory-specific prefixes.** TOPSD stops supervision before the first sustained crossing of a cumulative teacher-to-student likelihood-ratio threshold; defaults are threshold 0.9 and four consecutive positions (§5.1; Appendix A).
- **Verify recovery before distilling.** A frozen teacher greedily continues the selected prefix; only prefixes producing a verified correct final answer receive supervision. Teacher continuations are verification probes, while training targets remain teacher distributions on original student states (§5.2).
- **Balance the objective.** Reverse-KL distillation is averaged over supervised tokens within trajectories and equally across eligible languages (§5.3; Appendix B).

The experimental setup jointly trains LoRA adapters on multilingual data, with the teacher receiving the English problem and reference solution (§6.1; Appendix A).

Avg@3 averages correctness across three sampled completions; Pass@3 records whether any is correct (Appendix A). These results support selective supervision in the tested settings. They do not establish that every retained token receives correct guidance, that likelihood alignment itself causes recovery failure, or that gains extend beyond mathematics.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Other reasoning domains, model families, reference-source languages, and settings without English reference solutions remain unexplored (Limitations).
- **Author-stated variability:** Benefits may depend on existing language capabilities; aggregate improvements need not imply equal benefits across languages (Limitations; Ethical Considerations).
- **Analyst inference:** Final-answer verification does not validate each intermediate reasoning step or teacher distribution (§5.2; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text does not report uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variation, or a compute-matched baseline comparison. Verification requires additional generation, so accuracy gains alone do not establish efficiency gains (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Resource groups partly depend on base-model reasoning performance, limiting interpretation of group differences as effects of language-resource availability alone (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Does TOPSD transfer across domains and model families?** The authors explicitly leave this open. Test the same selection and verification procedure on another model family and a nonmathematical task with checkable outcomes.
- **How necessary are English references?** Other source languages and reference-quality levels are unexplored. Compare matched references across languages and controlled quality degradations.
- **Does the likelihood signal identify unreliable supervision specifically?** The fixed-prefix comparison supports adaptivity, but token-level guidance quality remains unverified. Compare likelihood cutoffs with annotated error locations and alternative selection signals under matched supervision budgets.
- **What is the accuracy–compute tradeoff?** Verification overhead lacks a matched baseline comparison. Measure total training compute and accuracy against OPSD/COPSD given equivalent budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are explicit, but the supplied retrieval includes table captions without table bodies.

Material missing information includes complete per-language and baseline scores, exact AIME and fixed-prefix results, full sensitivity results, uncertainty estimates, drift-analysis sample sizes, threshold sensitivity, verification acceptance rates by language, comparative compute costs, and code/checkpoint release availability.
