# Video-Coder: Reinforcing Agentic Coding for Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09894) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09894)


## One-sentence takeaway
Video-Coder trains an agent to iteratively program and render 3D video drafts that guide a fixed video generator, improving reported physics scores while leaving uncertainties about dataset sizes, evaluation reliability, and broader generalization.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target failures in object counts, spatial relationships, occlusions, and physical motion in generated videos. They argue that executable 3D scenes offer explicit, editable control over structure and motion, whereas prior code-driven approaches largely use frozen models and fixed prompting pipelines without optimizing final video quality (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trainable coding agent:** A Qwen3.5-9B-based agent writes, executes, and revises Blender Python programs using execution errors and rendered frames. Its gray, untextured draft video guides a downstream generator; only the agent is trained (§§3.2–3.3). The authors claim novelty in training this kind of multimodal coding agent for video generation.
- **Data pipeline and harness:** Teacher-generated coding trajectories, synthetic reference videos, and filtering produce separate SFT and RL datasets. The harness integrates static checks, rendering, content checks, visual feedback, and clean re-execution of submitted programs (§§3.1–3.2). Dataset sizes are inconsistent across sections.
- **Agentic RL with dual rewards:** After SFT, GRPO combines binary execution success with a VLM video-consistency reward. The execution coefficient decays exponentially during training (§3.3). This reduces its weight; the formula does not guarantee that video reward dominates by training end.

The abstract states that data, models, and code are open-sourced; availability is not independently established by the supplied text.

These results support improvements on the reported physics evaluations and complementary benefits from both rewards. They do not establish general video-quality gains, statistical significance, or broad generator transfer.

The numerical reporting contains discrepancies: the abstract/introduction describe roughly seven PhyGenBench points, whereas Table 1 shows six on a 100-point conversion. Section 4.2 calls OmniWeaving’s gain “16.28 points,” which is inconsistent with its displayed scores. The table values above are therefore the clearest available evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated representation limitation:** The authors attribute weaker material performance to phenomena requiring fine appearance and material response that gray drafts omit (§4.2).
- **Restricted training scope:** Prompt construction excludes content poorly represented by geometric primitives and keyframed motion, including fine appearance and facial expressions (§3.1). **Analyst inference:** performance outside this filtered scope remains uncertain.
- **Inconsistent dataset specification:** Section 3.1 reports SFT-6k/RL-2k, while §§4.1 and 5 report SFT-10k/RL-4k. The actual training dataset sizes cannot be resolved.
- **Limited evaluation breadth:** Only two physics-focused benchmarks are reported, with transfer tested on one additional generator. No uncertainty estimates, repeated-run results, or human validation appear in the supplied text (§4).
- **Synthetic and model-judged supervision:** MiniMax-H3 creates reference videos and supplies RL rollouts; VLMs filter data, assign video rewards, and judge PhyGenBench (§§3.1, 3.3, 4.1). **Analyst inference:** dependence on synthetic references and automated judges leaves alignment with human assessments unresolved.
- **Unisolated mechanisms and cost:** No ablation isolates visual feedback, revision turns, or clean re-execution. Training uses eight H100 GPUs plus sixteen for rollout generation for approximately three days; inference latency and cost are unreported (§§4.1, 4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **How much does iterative visual revision contribute?** Table 3 isolates training and rewards, but not interaction. Compare single-pass coding, execution-only feedback, and visual-feedback revision under matched budgets.
2. **Does transfer extend beyond the tested generator?** Only OmniWeaving supplements MiniMax-H3 in the reported transfer evidence. Evaluate unchanged drafts across additional generators with matched prompts and human assessments.
3. **Can richer drafts improve material phenomena?** The authors identify missing appearance/material information as a weakness (§4.2). Compare gray drafts with drafts encoding material properties while holding the agent and generator fixed.
4. **Do automated scores track human judgments?** The pipeline relies on synthetic references and model judges without reported human validation. Run blinded human comparisons on held-out prompts and measure agreement with benchmark and reward scores.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and main table comparisons are clear, but internal inconsistencies weaken reproducibility and confidence in headline reporting.

Material missing information includes the correct dataset sizes, exact appendix prompt contents, numerical sensitivity-plot values, rendering resolution/frame rate, filtering thresholds, evaluation sample counts, uncertainty estimates, inference costs, and independently confirmed release contents.
