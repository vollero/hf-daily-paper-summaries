# PCBRouteAgent: How Close Are LLM Agents to Becoming PCB Routing Engineers?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08175) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08175)


## One-sentence takeaway
PCBRouteAgent combines heuristic routing guides, native KiCad execution, and multimodal LLM replanning to improve reported PCB routing completion and efficiency, although large-board failures and evaluation caveats limit claims of engineer-level reliability.

## Problem and motivation
PCB routing must connect required pads while respecting geometry and design rules, balancing wirelength, vias, and space needed by other nets. The authors identify a coordination gap: agents with native routing tools can execute local actions but still struggle to prioritize connections, allocate space, and recognize when failures require a different topology or layer assignment (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Explicit planning–execution interface:** Heuristics initialize minimum-spanning-tree connectivity, shorter-net-first ordering, rectangular corridors, and via-transition regions. A detailed executor realizes these guides using KiCad’s push-and-shove router (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Targeted LLM replanning:** After bounded local recovery fails, a multimodal LLM uses initial board images, structured geometry, updated connectivity, and execution feedback to patch endpoints, corridors, transitions, or order. Validation and checkpoints preserve accepted work (§§3.2.4–3.3.2).
- **Framework evaluation:** The authors evaluate two model backbones on existing PCBWorld-Bench splits and compare routing quality and computational cost. The contribution is the integrated framework and its reported results, rather than a new benchmark or routing engine (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
Clean pass requires complete connectivity and zero **error-level** KiCad design-rule violations; warning-level issues do not affect the score (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic boards reach full success | Both variants report 100% clean pass and 1.000 routability on 128 D2 boards, matching Freerouting and KiCadRoutingTools on completion. | §5, synthetic-board results; Table 1 cited |
| Small real-world boards improve | Opus reports 94.9% clean pass on 99 D3-A boards, 13.9 percentage points above PCBWorld PPO without finish; mean routability is 0.994 and mean DRV is 0.09. | §5, small-board results; Table 2 cited |
| Medium-board success has protocol caveats | Opus completes all 10 D3-B boards without error-level violations, versus reported Freerouting clean pass of 64%; results include response-failure retries and angle repair. | §§4–5; Table 3 caption |
| Large boards remain difficult | On 10 D3-C boards, Opus reports 40% clean pass versus Freerouting’s 30%, 0.949 mean routability, and 6.3 versus 32.0 mean DRVs. Six boards remain incomplete. | §5, large-board results; Table 4 cited |
| Same-backbone comparison favors the framework | With Haiku, PCBRouteAgent versus PCBWorld clean-pass rates are 100%/63.3% on D2, 92.9%/34.3% on D3-A, 80%/0% on D3-B, and 10%/0% on D3-C. | §5, framework effectiveness |
| Aggregate resource use decreases | Across 247 boards with Haiku, model calls fall from 22,529 to 172, tokens from 105.7M to 12.0M, API cost from $20.72 to $4.77, and cumulative per-board runtime from 36.4 to 2.6 hours. | §5, efficiency; Appendix C |
| Completion trades off against compactness | Opus uses 10.9% more wirelength than Freerouting on D3-B and 8.2% more on D3-C; D3-C mean via counts are 21.3 versus 21.4. | §5, physical routing cost |

The same-Haiku comparison supports the effectiveness of the **overall framework**, but does not isolate which components cause the gains. These results establish benchmark routing performance under the stated evaluator, rather than broad engineering readiness or validation of every signal-specific constraint discussed in the introduction.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated challenges:** Large-board completion and routing compactness remain unresolved; the conclusion proposes better spatial/layer planning, adaptive recovery budgets, and refinement (§6).
- **Comparison qualification:** D3-B is explicitly not a strict first-attempt estimate. Reported Opus outputs also receive guarded angle repair, and no full same-Opus PCBWorld comparison is provided (§4; §5).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No component ablations appear in the supplied text, so the separate value of images, LLM patches, heuristic guides, local search, and repair is unestablished.
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Medium and large subsets each contain only 10 boards, with fixed placement and two-layer designs. Generalization beyond this setting remains untested here (§4).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Freerouting’s reported 64% D3-B clean-pass rate is not readily interpretable as a binary pass fraction over 10 boards; the supplied prose does not explain its aggregation. Table bodies are absent, and some cross-references are inconsistent.
- **Analyst inference—state representation:** Replanning retains initial images while relying on feedback and connectivity summaries for changes; the effect of visually stale geometry is not evaluated (§3.2.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Which components drive improvement?** The framework comparison does not separate their effects. Ablate LLM replanning, images, A* recovery, and angle repair under matched budgets.
2. **What prevents large-board completion?** High average connectivity still leaves most D3-C boards incomplete. Categorize unresolved failures and compare adaptive spatial/layer planning against the current fixed-budget policy.
3. **Can completed routes become more compact?** Reported real-world wirelength exceeds Freerouting’s. Test post-routing refinement that preserves connectivity and design-rule validity.
4. **How robust are the gains under a uniform protocol?** Retries, repair, and mixed published/local baselines complicate comparison. Rerun methods with explicit attempt accounting, matched budgets, and repeated trials.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the architecture and headline results, but omits numerical table bodies and the actual prompt contents despite appendix headings. Also unavailable are component ablations, uncertainty estimates, detailed failure analyses, complete per-board outcomes, and clarification of the D3-B baseline aggregation and response-retry counts.
