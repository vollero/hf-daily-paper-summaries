# PREEN: Exemplar-Based Color Style Transfer with Smartphone Editor Presets

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36896) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36896)


## One-sentence takeaway
PREEN predicts editable color presets from content–reference image pairs, achieving strong fidelity on PST50 while exposing a strength control whose preferred setting differs between reference matching and standalone aesthetics.

## Problem and motivation
Exemplar-based color transfer should reproduce a reference’s look while preserving the content photograph. Raster-producing methods do not expose editing controls, whereas preset prediction produces compact, deterministic, user-editable outputs. The author identifies a supervision gap: large reference-conditioned datasets with expert target edits are unavailable, and prior sampled-preset supervision restricts the styles represented in training (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Teacher-supervised preset learning:** SA-LUT generates target edits for real image pairs. Optimization through a differentiable editor projects those targets into 29 slider values. A CLIP-conditioned regressor combines image embeddings with handcrafted color statistics to predict these pseudo-presets (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Image-level refinement:** A second phase trains LoRA adapters using RGB and CIELAB reconstruction losses. Separate tone and chroma trunks receive selectively routed gradients; identity samples and preset regularization encourage restrained edits (§3.4).
- **Controllable strength:** A bounded transformation scales chromaticity parameters while retaining predicted tone parameters, producing stronger presets without another network inference (§3.5).
- **Preference analysis:** A two-axis study distinguishes fidelity to expert targets, perceived reference matching, and standalone aesthetic appeal (§4.7).

The claimed novelty centers on the supervision recipe, strength primitive, and preference analysis; reference-conditioned preset prediction itself has prior work (§§1–2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest main-table fidelity scores | On 50 paired PST50 triples resized to 256×256, PREEN obtains PSNR **24.63 dB**, SSIM **0.914**, LPIPS **0.094**, HCorr **0.473**, and ΔE*ab **9.07**. NLUT, the next-best PSNR baseline, scores **23.43 dB**. PREEN leads the listed baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, but not HCorr. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Statistical support varies by metric | Paired Wilcoxon tests with per-metric Holm correction retain every PSNR advantage over published methods, adjusted **p≤.031**. ΔE*ab differences versus SA-LUT/NLUT are not significant, uncorrected **p≥.42**; LPIPS versus Deep Preset’s raster output is also not significant, **p=.077**. | Supplement §4 |
| Refinement improves color matching | On PST50, Phase 1 reaches **23.60 dB PSNR**; full refinement raises HCorr from **0.383 to 0.473**. MLP-only LoRA reaches HCorr **0.423**, supporting adaptation of the encoder as well as the head. | §4.4.1; Table 2 discussion |
| Stronger style matching can reduce aesthetic preference | Vivid beats default for reference matching in **73.4%** of judgments. Across its preset opponents, including default, Vivid’s pooled style-match win rate is **66.9% [62.2, 71.6]**, versus **43.3% [38.9, 47.9]** for aesthetics. | §4.7; Supplement §11 |
| Restraint explains part of the benchmark advantage | A half-strength SA-LUT raster blend reaches **25.09 dB PSNR**, above PREEN’s **24.63 dB**. Its test-time preset projection reaches **25.05 dB**, but requires teacher inference and optimization. The blend strength was selected by a reported sweep. | Supplement §5 |
| Smartphone preset prediction is sub-second | Quantized TFLite inference takes **283 ms per pair**, CPU-only on Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, using **8 threads** and the median of **20 warm runs**. Quantized PST50 PSNR is **24.34 dB**. | §4.8 |

These results support efficient, editable color transfer on the supplied benchmark. They do not establish superior expressivity or universal perceptual quality: restrained targets favor conservative edits, a calibrated teacher exceeds PREEN’s PSNR, and human preferences change with the evaluation objective. Device latency measures preset prediction; it does not establish a complete interactive editing pipeline’s latency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The fixed global editor cannot reproduce certain palette remappings, split-toned monochromes, or local effects such as grain, vignettes, and burns (§5; Supplement §7).
- **Author-stated:** Semantically disjoint references are untested, and the CLIP-dominated model footprint remains a deployment concern (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative generalization remains narrow: paired metrics cover PST50; DPST and in-the-wild evidence are qualitative (§4.3; Supplement §7).
- **Analyst inference:** Human-study conclusions concern subjective preferences with limited agreement. The reported Fleiss’ κ is **0.15–0.34**; pooled Vivid results include its own default as an opponent (Supplement §11).
- **Evidence availability:** Numerous tables appear only as captions in the supplied extraction, limiting independent inspection of complete ablations and confidence intervals.

## Open research questions
- **Can smaller encoders preserve color calibration?** Model compression is explicitly deferred (§5). Replace CLIP with lighter encoders and measure fidelity, memory, and latency on the same device.
- **How dependent is transfer on semantic compatibility?** Disjoint references are untested (§5). Compare matched and deliberately mismatched references using expert targets and human style judgments.
- **Can prediction close the editor’s remaining headroom?** Target-fitted oracle results exceed PREEN on color/tone metrics, although not LPIPS (Supplement §5). Test alternative regressors or editor-feedback refinement while retaining the same editor.
- **Can strength selection adapt to user intent?** The study finds different preferences for matching and aesthetics (§4.7). Compare fixed settings with user-selected or intent-conditioned strength in a new preference study.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and main findings, but complete table bodies for many ablations and supplementary comparisons are absent. Exact training-pair counts, full editor calibration rules, valid judgment counts by pairing, and public availability of code, weights, and pseudo-presets are not established.
