# "PRISM" Is Not All You Need: Cross-Topic Name Collisions and Scientific Paper Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01362) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01362)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the authors’ AI-related arXiv corpus, cross-topic name collisions increased sharply, while research clues improved paper identification but left measurable ambiguity, especially among same-topic namesakes (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
A shared scientific name can refer to distinct papers, making a remembered label insufficient for retrieval. The authors distinguish exact-label reuse across records from reuse within a method lineage, and ask how collision rates change across topics and how much research context restores identification (§1).

The identified gap is a joint measurement of historical name convergence and retrieval specificity, with candidate competition, document fields, and ranking methods evaluated separately (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Version-aware measurement:** Analyze 564,861 AI-related arXiv records submitted in 2020–2026, using original titles and abstracts. Extract leading title labels, normalize their spelling, and measure matching-label rates within and across abstract-derived topic clusters. Check sensitivity to encoders, topic resolution, author overlap, and label definitions (§3; Appendix A).
- **Controlled retrieval evaluation:** Compare shared-name targets with metadata-matched unique-name targets, using the same abstract-derived query construction. Separate full-index retrieval from ranking within complete namesake groups, and compare BM25, MiniLM, E5, and listwise language-model ranking (§4).
- **Reusable evaluation assets:** Provide a frozen corpus, candidate groups, queries, topic-error labels, baseline outputs, evaluator, and local query demonstration (§5). Supporting experiments examine how controlled descriptions affect model-generated names (Appendices B–C).

## Evidence and main results

Intervals below are reported 95% name-group bootstrap intervals.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-topic collision grew substantially faster than the within-topic point estimate. | Among 144,523 non-format-labelled records, 2026 versus pooled 2020–2022 growth was **4.33× [2.62, 6.47]** across topics and **1.12× [0.81, 1.57]** within topics, using 200 clusters. MPNet reproduced the pattern: **4.38×** versus **1.14×**. | §3.2; Fig. 3 |
| Intermediate-frequency labels account for most measured growth. | Fixed Zipf bands **[3,5)** contained **12.4%** of 2026 named records, **89.4%** of cross-topic matching pairs, and **98.0%** of the net population-rate increase. | §3.3; Fig. 4; Appendix E |
| Context leaves a substantial matched retrieval gap. | Full-index title BM25, with three abstract-derived terms: **59.4% Top-1** for 1,400 shared-name targets versus **85.1%** for 1,400 matched unique-name targets; gap **25.7 percentage points [22.5, 28.7]**. | §4.1; Fig. 6 |
| Context helps when candidates are fixed. | Within exact-name groups, name-only → contextual Top-1 increased **30.8% → 70.6%** for BM25 and **32.0% → 74.0%** for MiniLM. | §4.2 |
| Same-topic namesakes remain harder in a fixed-size comparison. | Two-candidate contextual BM25: **80.0% Top-1** with a cross-topic rival versus **66.9%** with a same-topic rival; difference **13.1 points [1.6, 25.0]**. MiniLM: **85.9% versus 61.0%**. | §4.3; Fig. 7 |
| Listwise ranking improves title-only namesake selection. | DeepSeek-Flash ranked all candidate titles for the same 1,400 contextual targets: **89.2% Top-1 [87.5, 90.9]**, **98.9% Top-10**, **0.933 MRR**; two invalid outputs received zero credit. | §4.4; Appendix D |
| Abstract indexing improves identification but retains a gap. | Full-index title-plus-abstract E5: **87.9% Top-1** shared versus **94.8%** unique; gap **6.9 points [4.8, 9.0]**. Exact-name selection reached **93.6% [92.3, 94.8]**. | §4.4 |

These results establish descriptive historical growth and retrieval difficulty under frozen populations and query rules. They do not establish why authors reuse names or how well systems handle naturally remembered reader queries. Candidate-restricted ranking and full-index retrieval also measure different stages of search.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-defined scope:** Extraction covers compact leading tokens followed by a colon in selected AI-related arXiv categories. Exact spelling groups differ from method lineages, and suffixes remain separate (Appendix A).
- **Author-described query constraint:** Clues come automatically from target abstracts. The abstract-indexing condition explicitly exposes their source, so its gains measure access to that evidence (§§4.2, 4.4).
- **Topic-boundary sensitivity:** Topics operationalize abstract similarity. Although aggregate conclusions reproduce across encoders, same-topic pair Jaccard agreement is only **0.301**, indicating materially different boundaries (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Matching year, category, and title length does not isolate a causal effect of name sharing; the authors retain actual lexical characteristics and candidate contents (§4.1).
- **Author-described uncertainty:** Bootstrap intervals quantify sensitivity to observed name groups, rather than every source of corpus or topic-assignment uncertainty (§3.1).
- **Causal attribution remains unsupported:** Actual author LLM use is unobserved; the writing-style analysis is explicitly associational (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Do the gains hold for reader-authored clues?** The benchmark uses source-derived terms. Collect remembered queries from readers and evaluate them against the frozen targets and candidates.
2. **Can interfaces elicit better same-topic distinctions?** Same-topic rivals remain harder (§4.3). Test a clarification interface requesting a method component or application against the existing three-term condition.
3. **How far do the findings generalize beyond the extraction scope?** The corpus restricts categories and title structure. Repeat the protocol on additional disciplines and manually validated names outside leading-colon titles.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported descriptive findings within the defined benchmark; broader generalization remains uncertain.
