# Frontier3DBench: Benchmarking Coding Agents for 3D Creation across Objects, Scenes, and Interactive Open Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09017) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09017)


## One-sentence takeaway
Frontier3DBench evaluates coding agents across 145 object, scene, and interactive-world tasks, finding persistent weaknesses in reference reconstruction and coordinated activities despite stronger text-driven generation and local mechanism performance (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Existing benchmarks use different task scopes and protocols for asset creation, scene authoring, and executable worlds, making capabilities difficult to compare across construction levels. The authors propose evaluating editable, executable artifacts through appearance, structure, and behavior so that aggregate quality does not hide failures in fidelity, layout, or interaction (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified task suite:** 65 object tasks, 65 scene tasks, and 15 interactive-world tasks across nine categories. Objects and scenes use Blender; worlds use Unreal Engine. Tasks cover text generation, reference reconstruction, and scene editing (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Artifact-based evaluation:** Rendered judgments, native-scene measurements, and independent runtime tests assess visual fidelity, structural requirements, edit preservation, navigation, mechanisms, and persistent workflows (§2.3; Appendix A).
- **Comparative study:** Eleven model–harness configurations are evaluated across the full suite, with capability profiles, estimated API costs, and a human-ranking agreement study (§3; Appendix B). The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation framework rather than a new generation model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership depends on construction level | Across 145 tasks and 11 configurations, GPT-6.1-sol/Codex scores **66.02/100 overall**, with L1/L2/L3 means of **71.21/70.35/58.89**. GPT-6-astra leads L3 at **61.28**. Overall weights are **30%/30%/40%**. | §§2.3.4, 3.2–3.3 |
| Reference reconstruction scores below text generation | Every configuration shows this ordering for objects and scenes. Text-to-object scores span **75.93–84.05**; the best multi-view and single-view reconstruction scores are **69.61** and **66.26**, respectively, on the benchmark’s 0–100 task scales. | §3.2 |
| Local interaction success does not extend to coordinated activity | Across **165 L3 submissions**, mean interaction subcomponent scores are **83.08/100** for local mechanisms, **20.82** for motion clearance, and **30.26** for state/workflow checks. | §3.4; Appendix A.2.3 |
| Automatic rankings agree with human reference-fidelity judgments | Eight raters rank four candidates for each of **45 reference-conditioned L1/L2 items**, yielding **360 rankings**: mean Kendall **τᵦ = 0.727**, **79.2%** top-one agreement, and **44.7%** exact-order agreement. | §3.7; Appendix B.3 |
| Estimated spending does not consistently track score | Sol scores **66.02** at approximately **$1.01/task**, versus Astra’s **63.85** at **$5.58/task** and Opus’s **62.29** at **$21.17/task**. Costs average final successful generation attempts across all tasks and exclude infrastructure and judging. | §3.5; Appendix B.2 |

These results establish benchmark-specific differences between configurations and between measured capabilities. They do not isolate model ability from harness effects. Generation and reconstruction also use different scoring formulas, so their score gap supports a practical benchmark finding rather than a controlled estimate of intrinsic task difficulty (Appendix A.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-disclosed cost limits:** Costs exclude earlier attempts, judging, tool use, infrastructure, cache storage, and taxes. Partial usage records make estimates for DeepSeek, both Kimi configurations, and GLM lower bounds (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—configuration confounding:** Models use different harnesses without a reported harness-controlled comparison, limiting attribution of rankings to models alone (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** GPT-6-astra helps develop instructions, judges visual quality, and is itself evaluated. Hidden generator identities reduce direct identification, but no alternative-judge sensitivity study is reported (§2.2.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—validation scope:** Human validation covers selected reference-conditioned object/scene candidates with enforced score separation; it does not validate text-generation or world-behavior scoring (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—ranking sensitivity:** Worlds receive substantial overall weight despite having fewer tasks, and their task score emphasizes appearance. The text reports task-bootstrap methodology but no numerical intervals or weight-sensitivity analysis (§2.3.4; Appendices A.1, B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can execution feedback improve complete activities?** Workflow scores remain low despite stronger local mechanisms (§3.4). Compare otherwise identical agents with and without continuous-route testing and state-trace feedback.
- **Which factors drive reconstruction failures?** Reconstruction trails generation, but the paper does not isolate perception, geometry construction, or scoring effects (§3.2). Use matched targets with controlled reference views and separate geometry and appearance measurements.
- **How stable are rankings across harnesses and judges?** The evaluation couples models to harnesses and uses one visual judge (§3.1; Appendix A.2). Cross models with shared harnesses and rescore fixed artifacts using independent judges and humans.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed scoring and protocols, but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 3 and 4, numerical confidence intervals, a release URL/license, full task-level annotations, exact model/harness revisions, and independent generation-run variability. These gaps limit auditing of rankings and reproducibility.
