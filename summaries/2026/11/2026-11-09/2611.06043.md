# Confucius4-R2T2: A Stable-Prefix Streaming ASR Model for Real Real-Time Transcription

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06043) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06043)


## One-sentence takeaway
R2T2 adapts Qwen3-ASR-1.7B to immutable streaming transcription using stable-prefix supervision and explicit waiting, reporting near-offline recognition quality and low audio-timeline emission lag, but without measured real-time serving performance (§§4–8).

## Problem and motivation
Streaming ASR often revises earlier text as more audio arrives, forcing captions, translators, and voice agents to redo work or potentially act on incorrect text. The authors target stable output together with accuracy and low latency: encoder causality alone does not guarantee immutable text, while inference-only commitment rules do not teach the recognizer when sufficient evidence has arrived (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **A specific LSP learning target:** Select the longest whole earlier hypothesis that is entirely a prefix of the same model’s full-context output. The authors establish monotonicity, endpoint consistency, and maximality within this restricted class of labels—not optimal accuracy or latency (§4.3; Appendix A).
- **Decoder-only streaming adaptation:** Annotate cumulative audio prefixes, add `Wait` for uncertain continuations, then apply supervised fine-tuning and GRPO using approximately 1,500 hours of proprietary Chinese/English audio. The encoder and projector remain frozen, with no added layers or heads (§§4–6).
- **Commitment and inference machinery:** Preserve committed text, optionally withhold a provisional suffix, reuse caches, bound retained context, and verify provisional tokens during prefill. The release includes model weights and code, according to the abstract (§7).
- **Streamability diagnostic:** Measure the area under normalized LSP-progress curves to assess candidate backbones or annotation teachers (§5.1).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest ranked combined recognition score | Authors report **5.76% total macro error** at **160 ms, U=1**, equally averaging English WER and Chinese CER percentages across **13 test sets**; offline and 2-second base references are excluded from rankings. | §8.2; Table 3 caption and accompanying prose |
| Large improvement over matched base streaming | At **160 ms, U=1**, authors report lower error on every main English/Chinese test set and a reduction of nearly three quarters in total macro error versus the base model; remaining gap to base offline recognition is approximately **0.6 percentage points**. | §8.2 |
| Subsecond emission timing | At **80 ms, U=0**, mean retrospective emission lag is reported below **0.5 seconds** in both languages, with some recognition-quality loss. This excludes execution and delivery time. | §8.3; Figure 6 discussion |
| Transfer beyond post-training languages | At **160 ms, U=1**, authors report lower recognition error than the matched base setting on all **18 test sets in 10 additional languages**. Metrics are CER for Japanese/Korean/Thai and WER otherwise. | §8.2; Appendix C.2 |
| Backbone responsiveness diagnostic | Qwen3-ASR-1.7B has the highest reported normalized streamability, **0.493**, among four compared models on **1,860 Buckeye utterances**, annotated at **80 ms** intervals. | §5.1; Figure 1 |

The supplied prose supports these reported findings, but the numerical table bodies and plotted values are absent, preventing independent checking of individual scores and margins. The results support recognition and emission-timing claims for the evaluated settings; they do not establish wall-clock real-time operation or downstream agent benefits. Immutable release follows the commitment policy, while the LSP proofs concern offline labels rather than guaranteeing correctness of learned predictions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ measurement caveat:** Lag excludes execution, queueing, transport, and rendering; keeping pace with incoming audio was not verified through runtime measurements (§8.1).
- **Selective lag scoring:** Only correctly matched units contribute, and high-error utterances are excluded. API buffering is undisclosed, making API audio-time estimates approximate (§8.1).
- **Missing causal attribution:** Reward-component effects require controlled ablations, as the authors acknowledge (§6.2). **Analyst inference:** The supplied results also do not isolate the benefit of this LSP definition from alternative prefix targets or separate SFT from GRPO.
- **Reproducibility and exposure:** Training audio is proprietary; evaluation data are excluded from post-training, but base-model pre-training exposure is possible (Appendix C.1).
- **Scope of guarantees:** LSP anchors to model output rather than truth and deliberately favors conservative emission. Flicker frequency is not measured; speech endpoint detection and final flushing remain separate operations (§§4.3, 8.3; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the system sustain real-time service under load?** Runtime was not measured (§8.1). Benchmark wall-clock latency, throughput, memory, and final flushing across stream lengths and concurrent users.
2. **Which supervision and training components drive the gains?** Controlled comparisons are missing. Compare identical training budgets using alternative prefix labels, SFT alone, and SFT plus GRPO.
3. **Does streamability predict adaptation success across backbones?** Its broader usefulness remains open (§5.1). Adapt several backbones with the same recipe and test whether initial streamability predicts recognition–lag outcomes.
4. **Can a stronger teacher improve commitment without harmful mismatch?** The authors use self-distillation and explicitly leave this open (§5.1). Compare self-generated and stronger-teacher targets under matched data and compute.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and evaluation caveats are clearly described, but quantitative verification is limited by missing table bodies and figure values.

Material unavailable information includes per-dataset scores, exact lag distributions and exclusion thresholds, full GRPO configuration and reward weights, identities of Commercial A/B, measured deployment performance, and controlled training/target ablations.
