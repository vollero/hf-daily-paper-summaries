# OmniLatent: Unified Latent for 3D Part Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09055) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09055)


## One-sentence takeaway
OmniLatent jointly generates textured geometry and surface part labels through a shared latent representation, reporting stronger mask-guided segmentation than compared methods and supporting separately fine-tuned interactive editing and amodal completion (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Editable 3D assets need coherent, addressable parts. The authors argue that segmenting meshes after generation makes boundaries vulnerable to geometry artifacts, texture ambiguity, and inconsistent granularity, while scarce part annotations limit standalone segmentation learning (§1).

Their proposed gap is a representation that transfers abundant geometry and appearance supervision into part decomposition while also supporting incomplete masks and sparse user input (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified representation and generation:** A voxel-query VAE shares latent tokens across SDF geometry, vertex deformation, RGB appearance, and object-local binary part IDs. Surface extraction precedes attribute queries, aligning labels with the decoded mesh. An image-conditioned MM-DiT trained with rectified flow generates these tokens, optionally guided by segmentation masks (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Progressive transfer:** Training introduces geometry, then appearance, then part supervision so scarce part annotations benefit from previously learned priors (§§3.2–3.3). This extends an existing voxel-query representation rather than introducing that scaffold itself.
- **Interactive adaptation:** A separately fine-tuned model embeds point coordinates and part IDs as prompt tokens for full segmentation, refinement, and subdivision (§3.4).
- **Completion adaptation:** Another fine-tuned model combines dynamic per-part token budgets, spatially anchored observed tokens, unanchored completion tokens, geometry cross-attention, and alternating local/global attention (§3.5).

## Evidence and main results

All numbers below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger segmentation on identical generated surfaces | PartObjaverse-Tiny-wt: mask-guided OmniLatent **74.18% mIoU**, versus strongest listed baseline P³-SAM **56.01%**, with every method evaluated on OmniLatent-generated meshes. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Mask guidance materially improves segmentation | On ground-truth watertight surfaces: **62.42% mIoU** without masks versus **76.53%** with masks. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Sparse prompts support segmentation and editing | Under the P³-SAM protocol using **10 prompts**, **67.32% mIoU** for full segmentation and **55.75%** for interactive segmentation; claimed to exceed Point-SAM and P³-SAM, whose scores are absent from the supplied text. | §4.3, Table 3 discussion |
| Better reported part completion | On **200 ObjaversePart-Tiny samples**, with full geometry and partial part masks: part-level CD **0.08** versus X-Part **0.11**; F-score at distance threshold **0.05** is **0.78** versus **0.71**. Objects are normalized to **[-1,1]**, and scores use the best of four quarter-turn orientations. | §4.4, Table 5 discussion |
| Completion anchoring and global attention help | In the same part-level completion setting, removing surface RoPE changes CD/F-score-0.05 from **0.08/0.78** to **0.15/0.64**; removing full attention yields **0.13/0.68**. | §4.4, Table 5 discussion |
| Shared latent modeling and CA-RoPE improve segmentation | PartObjaverse-Tiny-wt: full model **74.18% mIoU**, part-ID-only latent **67.69%**, and without CA-RoPE **69.20%**. The headline full-model result uses mask guidance. | §4.6, Table 8; §4.2 |
| Reported runtime advantage | Joint generation takes approximately **30 seconds at 8,192 latent tokens**, versus approximately **80 seconds** for a representative separate geometry/texture/segmentation pipeline; hardware and pipeline identities are unspecified. | §4.7 |

These results support the shared representation and selected completion mechanisms within the reported settings. They do not establish equivalent superiority for autonomous image-only generation: the main generated-surface comparison uses mask guidance. Geometry and appearance quality are described as competitive, but their numerical tables are unavailable in the supplied text (§4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Symmetric structures can receive inconsistent layouts; distinct components can merge; view-dependent masks can incorrectly split connected back-side regions. Autonomous quality depends on part-data diversity and semantic consistency (§5, Fig. 13).
- **Scope:** IDs are object-local rather than globally named semantic categories, and the encoding supports at most 64 parts (§3.1). Base outputs label surfaces without internal interfaces; completion requires a separate adaptation (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Completion robustness under severe missing evidence remains uncertain because training filters objects where four or more parts each require over 60% completion (§3.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Nearest-neighbor label transfer couples segmentation scores to geometric accuracy. Ground-truth-driven matching does not independently establish predicted part-count accuracy (§4.2).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Completion is described both as recovering internal part geometry and as completing a surface layout; the supplied description does not fully resolve its internal-geometry representation (§§3.2, 3.5).
- **Missing controls:** No numerical isolation of dynamic allocation or completion-token design is supplied. Binary-ID and pretraining comparisons are qualitative (§§4.4, 4.6). Runtime lacks a reproducible hardware comparison (§4.7).

## Open research questions
1. **How strong is autonomous segmentation on generated meshes?** The headline comparison is mask-guided. Next experiment: evaluate image-only and mask-guided generation on identical inputs, reporting segmentation, geometry, and part-count errors (§4.2).
2. **Does completion survive severe occlusion or incorrect geometry?** Training excludes some highly incomplete cases and inference uses generated geometry. Next experiment: sweep mask coverage and geometry corruption while measuring observed-region preservation and completed-part accuracy (§3.5).
3. **Which completion mechanisms drive the gains?** Surface RoPE and global attention are ablated, but dynamic allocation and unanchored tokens are not separately quantified. Next experiment: compare fixed/dynamic budgets and anchored/unanchored completion tokens under matched compute (§4.4).
4. **Can symmetry and connectivity constraints reduce editing failures?** Both remain explicit limitations. Next experiment: add each constraint separately and measure symmetric-part agreement, connectivity errors, and mIoU (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and several results are explicit, but most referenced table bodies are missing, leaving only narrative summaries.

Material gaps include complete baseline scores, geometry/appearance metrics, uncertainty estimates, training–test overlap checks for Objaverse-derived assets, compute hardware, public code/model/data availability, and precise internal-part decoding details.
