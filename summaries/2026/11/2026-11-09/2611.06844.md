# Where to Connect? Boosting MLLMs via Dynamic Gated Pathways across ALL ViT and LLM Layers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06844) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06844)


## One-sentence takeaway
DGP lets every LLM layer dynamically fuse features from every ViT layer, improving the reported image–language benchmarks while leaving universal superiority and larger-scale generalization unproven (Tables 1–2; Appendix F).

## Problem and motivation
Most MLLMs supply one fixed visual-layer representation, although different vision layers retain different levels of detail. The authors argue that encoder-side fusion and predefined cross-layer connections cannot adequately match visual granularity to each LLM layer’s needs. Their target is adaptive access to the full visual hierarchy throughout language-model depth (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **All-to-all visual access:** Each LLM layer receives a weighted fusion of every ViT layer’s patch features through a layer-specific projector and gated residual update. Initial penultimate-layer visual tokens are retained (Algorithm 1).
- **Context-conditioned routing:** A glimpse token placed before the answer queries projected visual-layer summaries through shared cross-attention; separate routing MLPs produce each LLM layer’s softmax weights (§3.2).
- **Training and analysis:** Batch-averaged routing entropy is encouraged during pre-training and disabled during instruction tuning. Ablations, routing interventions, and visualizations examine the contribution of selective fusion (§3.3; Table 4; Appendix E.3).

The distinguishing contribution claimed by the authors is the combination of all-to-all connectivity and routing conditioned on the current LLM-layer state, rather than adaptive gating alone (§2.2).

These results support selective visual-layer fusion in the tested settings. They do not establish that every connection is necessary, that DGP dominates every competitor, or that routing weights fully explain semantic reasoning. The authors’ “architecture-agnostic” interpretation extends beyond the limited framework coverage demonstrated.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Resource constraints limit validation on larger LLMs, broader encoder families, and larger multimodal corpora (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** DGP jointly trains the LLM during both stages (§4.1). The supplied results do not include an explicitly matched baseline isolating this training choice or a parameter-matched control.
- **Analyst inference—connectivity:** Table 4 tests uniform fusion, routing, and entropy regularization, but does not isolate all-to-all connectivity against a learned sparse alternative with matched capacity.
- **Analyst inference—intervention ambiguity:** Table 12 repeats the baseline and uniform-fusion rows from Table 4 exactly despite describing inference-time interventions without retraining. Their implementation and relationship to the earlier ablation need clarification.
- **Analyst inference—efficiency:** Generation lengths, latency, and training cost are unavailable. Consequently, Table 3’s slightly lower 13B FLOPs cannot establish intrinsically cheaper computation.
- **Author-proposed explanation:** Encoder unfreezing may destabilize visual-layer semantics; Table 10 shows performance changes, but does not directly measure this proposed mechanism (Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Is full connectivity necessary?** No matched sparse-routing ablation is reported. Compare dense DGP with learned top-k pathways under matched training and parameter budgets.
2. **How much improvement comes from routing versus training changes?** Joint LLM optimization and added capacity remain incompletely isolated. Retrain baseline and static-fusion controls with the same optimization schedule and matched capacity.
3. **Does routing adapt across generated answer tokens?** The query is a pre-answer glimpse token, and inference-time recomputation or caching is not detailed. Compare fixed prompt-conditioned routing with routing recomputed from generated-token states.
4. **Does the method scale beyond the tested frameworks?** Appendix F leaves larger models and corpora unresolved. Evaluate the same controlled comparisons across model sizes and encoder families, including latency and training cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial methods, results, and ablations, but lacks repeated-run uncertainty, complete evaluation/scoring protocols, matched training and capacity controls, detailed autoregressive caching behavior, and generation-length controls for efficiency measurements. Code availability is announced; repository contents and checkpoints were not inspected.
