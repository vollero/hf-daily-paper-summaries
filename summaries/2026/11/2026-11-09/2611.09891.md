# CPU-ARC: Agentic Reasoning Caching for on-CPU Reasoning Reuse

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09891) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09891)


## One-sentence takeaway
CPU-ARC converts individual LLM reasoning traces into reusable Python programs supported by a small language model, reporting comparable benchmark accuracy and 16.3–291.1× lower invocation costs, although construction uses golden labels and deployment efficiency is not directly measured (Table 2; §2.2.1).

## Problem and motivation
Repeated LLM requests often require the same reasoning with different wording or parameters. The authors argue that existing caches either reuse relatively rigid conversation artifacts or still require substantial new LLM generation. They propose separating the discovery of a solution from its execution, so subsequent instances can reuse executable logic (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable reasoning caches:** A builder converts a source transcript into parameterized Python “semantic functions,” each representing a logical operation. Construction includes decomposition, parameter identification, planning, implementation, and judge-guided revision (§§2.1–2.2.1).
- **Semantic composition and invocation:** Functions and values are retrieved through natural-language descriptions. A small target model handles extraction and elementary language tasks; CPU code performs the computation. Entry-point retrieval selects a cache, with source-model fallback on misses or errors (§2.2.2; Figure 1).
- **Compilation and target specialization:** Stable function networks can compile into standalone Python with direct calls and dictionary accesses, while necessary language-model calls remain. The authors also fine-tune a small target model from invocation logs and synthetic examples (§§2.2.3–2.2.4; Appendix D).
- **Evaluation:** The paper measures answer accuracy, source agreement, perturbation robustness, and API-priced construction and invocation costs on mathematical, scientific, and academic-question benchmarks (§3).

These results support reuse within constructed problem neighborhoods. They do not establish an independent replacement for frontier reasoning on unseen problem classes: construction tests against both transcripts and golden labels (§2.2.1). The authors attribute some accuracy gains to deterministic execution avoiding source-model slips, but provide no isolating ablation (§3.6). Reported savings are token-price calculations rather than measured CPU latency, energy, or complete deployment costs.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** This is exploratory benchmark work; production-agent deployment remains a goal. Generalizing overlapping semantic functions into a common function is explicitly unaddressed (§§1, 4).
- **Analyst inference—label-assisted construction:** Golden labels guide root-function validation, so source-versus-cache accuracy compares different information conditions. It cannot isolate faithful transcript conversion from correction enabled by answer supervision (§2.2.1).
- **Analyst inference—conditional robustness:** Perturbations are restricted to initially correct caches and filtered by model judges. Parametric answers are model-generated references rather than independent benchmark labels (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—correlated evaluation:** Source, builder, perturbation generator, and judges use the same model family; separate judge instances do not establish independent error behavior (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference—training overlap uncertainty:** Target fine-tuning uses logs harvested from benchmark construction and evaluation. The text does not specify sample-level separation between this training material and subsequent reported evaluation (Appendix D).
- **Evidence gaps:** No direct competing-cache experiments, semantic-dispatch ablations, measured compilation speedups, cache-miss/error analysis, or hardware-level efficiency measurements are reported. Code inspectability and narration demonstrate auditability, rather than universal correctness guarantees.

## Open research questions
- **Does accuracy hold without golden-label supervision?** Construction currently checks golden answers. Rebuild caches using transcripts alone, then evaluate on independently held-out instances.
- **Can retrieval safely reject nearby but incompatible problems?** The paper reports accepted perturbation performance without false-hit measurements. Test mixed workloads containing valid variants, subtly changed assumptions, and unrelated queries; measure incorrect hits and fallback rates.
- **What does semantic composition contribute?** Its benefits are not isolated. Compare semantic dispatch with fixed-name functions and monolithic generated programs under matched construction budgets.
- **Do savings survive full deployment accounting?** CPU execution and edge suitability lack hardware measurements. Benchmark end-to-end latency, memory, energy, maintenance, and total cost across reuse frequencies, including target training and fallback.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, aggregate results, cost assumptions, and training hyperparameters, but leaves important evaluation controls unclear.

Missing information includes evaluated sample counts and selection for each benchmark, perturbation counts, uncertainty estimates, construction failures and budgets, retrieval thresholds, target-training/evaluation separation, independent verification, hardware measurements, and public code/model/data release locations.
