# Static Recovery Is Not Dynamic Stability: Dynamics-Aware Benchmarking of Protein Motif Scaffolding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07723) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07723)


## One-sentence takeaway
Among 55 selected statically successful protein-scaffolding model–problem pairs, only 31 passed the authors’ heuristic MD-support screen and eight passed every check, showing that refolding success does not guarantee reference-consistent dynamics (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Protein motif scaffolding builds protein structures around functional residues. Standard benchmarks assess motif recovery through sequence design and refolding, but a recovered static structure may lose its geometry, contacts, or scaffold organization during molecular motion.

The authors identify two gaps: small, manually curated motif panels and success criteria that omit conformational ensembles. They therefore broaden static evaluation and separately test the dynamical behavior of selected successful designs (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **BILBY-PRO benchmark:** 220 cases from 174 PROSITE motif-pattern entries, selected for recurring structural conservation in experimental structures. Exports include motif structures, source structures, residue annotations, and generation specifications (§2; Appendix A.1).
- **Comparative static evaluation:** Six existing generators are evaluated on MotifBench and BILBY-PRO, separating target coverage from the yield of unique successful scaffolds (§3).
- **BILBY-MD framework:** Reference-based, explicit-solvent all-atom simulations assess motif drift, scaffold drift, motif contacts, and motif-dihedral free-energy landscapes. The reported MD study uses original MotifBench designs, **not BILBY-PRO designs** (§4; Code and Data Availability).

The contributions are benchmark construction and evaluation, rather than a new scaffold generator.

The baseline screen uses absolute reference-relative RMSD mean differences of at most 1 Å for motifs and 2 Å for scaffolds; contact Pearson ≥0.8 plus normalized contact error ≤1.0; and both PCA- and TICA-landscape correlations ≥0.3 (Appendix D).

These results support treating static recovery, unique-solution yield, and simulated dynamical fidelity as complementary measurements. They do not establish biochemical function, experimental stability, or a definitive model ranking on matched targets. The three-of-four rule also permits an “MD-supported” design to fail the scaffold-stability check.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** MD thresholds are heuristic; design trajectories may miss slower instabilities; reference trajectories are longer; selecting highest-TM-score candidates favors structurally familiar candidates; and ESM3 has missing landscape projections (§4.5).
- **Scope and contamination:** BILBY-PRO favors annotated, experimentally resolved families and near-contiguous motifs. Training overlap cannot be excluded, so it is not a strict out-of-distribution benchmark (§4.5).
- **Reproducibility discrepancy:** The public benchmark includes contig and identifier corrections, but historical evaluations were not recomputed (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—model comparisons are confounded:** Models’ MD summaries cover different solved subsets, so favorable averages cannot isolate model quality from target difficulty (Figures 2–3).
- **Analyst inference—reporting needs reconciliation:** Table A.4 lists 15 solved Proteina targets, while Figure 2 includes 12 Proteina pairs in MD. That figure also includes eight ESM3 pairs, versus four in the contact and landscape figures. Missing measurements are acknowledged, but pair-level exclusions and their effect on support labels are not fully documented.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty remains:** Independent simulation repeats and convergence evidence are not reported in the supplied protocol. RMSD mean differences summarize drift relative to a reference; they do not by themselves demonstrate folding stability or functional dynamics.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the static–dynamic mismatch extend to BILBY-PRO?** Its reported evaluation is static only. A next experiment could apply BILBY-MD to a stratified sample covering compact, multi-conformational, and multi-segment cases.
2. **How robust are support labels to sampling and simulation choices?** Threshold sensitivity is tested, but trajectory-duration asymmetry remains. Run independent repeats with matched design/reference durations and assess label stability.
3. **Does restoring biological context rescue interface motifs?** The authors attribute several failures to omitted partners, without testing that mechanism. Compare otherwise matched simulations with and without the relevant partner or assembly context.
4. **Do MD checks predict experimental utility?** The study does not establish biochemical function. Test matched designs with differing MD outcomes for structural stability and motif-specific activity.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text strongly supports the central static–dynamic distinction, but exact support rates and model comparisons depend on selection, missing measurements, and heuristic criteria.

Material missing information includes the numerical BILBY-PRO structural-conservation cutoff; complete static success and novelty filters; pair-level MD inclusion and missing-data handling; simulation-repeat and convergence evidence; detailed contact and landscape estimation settings; and experimental validation.
