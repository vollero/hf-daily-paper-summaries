# P^2-MOPD: Accelerating Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation via Parameter Priors

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03953) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03953)


## One-sentence takeaway
On four language benchmarks at Qwen3-1.7B and 4B scales, jointly learning student weights and coefficients over frozen expert task vectors improves recovery and reduces time to the final quality of MOPD from the same initialization (§§4.2–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates domain experts by scoring student-generated responses with the corresponding teacher. This requires costly rollouts and teacher scoring, yet can recover domains unevenly or erode capabilities already present in the initialization (§1).

Training-free task arithmetic provides a useful starting point but does not supervise the merged model’s behavior. The authors identify a gap between using expert parameters once for initialization and continuously learning how those parameters should guide distillation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint parameter-prior optimization:** Each student projection is represented as \(W_m=B_m+\sum_k\alpha_{k,m}\tau_{k,m}\), where task vectors are frozen and both full student weights and mixing coefficients remain trainable. The existing MOPD objective updates both channels; unconstrained student weights permit movement outside the task-vector span (§3.2).
- **Practical implementation:** The optimizer maintains composed weights, applies the ordinary weight update, then adds the coefficient-induced task-vector increment. Both channels use the same gradient; exported checkpoints require no additional inference machinery. Equivalence to explicitly optimizing \(B\) requires zero weight decay (§3.3; Appendix B).
- **Mechanistic evidence:** Projection analyses and interventions support the authors’ explanation that coefficients enable substantial movement along the lagging medical expert’s direction, while full weight updates adapt other capabilities beyond the span (§4.3).
- **Empirical contribution:** Matched-initialization comparisons measure recovery, domain balance, and wall-clock cost, with ablations of coefficient learning, granularity, initialization, and learning rate (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
Recovery measures the fraction of the base-to-teacher score gap closed; average recovery weights the four domains equally. Unless indicated otherwise, results below are three-training-seed means, with benchmark scores evaluated using avg@3 (§4.1).

| Higher raw benchmark average | Final mean score across the four benchmarks: **71.43% vs 68.78%** at 4B; **58.72% vs 56.55%** at 1.7B, against MOPD (TA init). | Table 1 |
| Better worst-domain recovery | Final worst-domain recovery: **89.42% vs 47.22%** at 4B; **88.01% vs 32.03%** at 1.7B, against MOPD (TA init). | Table 1; Figure 3 |
| Faster recovery in steps | At step 50, P²-MOPD reaches **92.3% / 90.5%** average recovery at 4B / 1.7B, exceeding the control’s step-100 recovery in every paired seed. | §4.2; Appendix G |
| Lower time to control’s final quality | First measured crossing occurs at **0.31× / 0.55×** the control’s total training wall-clock at 4B / 1.7B. These are upper bounds because evaluation occurs at spaced checkpoints. Full-length training costs **1.24× / 1.09×** as much. | §4.4; Table 4 caption |

The evidence supports faster, more balanced recovery within this protocol. Improvements concentrate on MedQA; FinQA is slightly lower than the control in most paired comparisons (§4.2). The interventions strengthen the proposed mechanism, but do not establish universal superiority or that ordinary weight optimization could never reach these solutions under different settings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Reported scope:** Evaluation covers one model family, two scales, four domains, and a fixed training budget. The authors omit the conversational-agent domain because its simulator requires a paid API (§4.1).
- **Reported sensitivity:** A larger coefficient learning rate underperforms the control later in training; layer-level tying performs poorly at 4B but not 1.7B (§5; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Three-seed evidence covers the principal methods, while several other baselines and ablations lack equivalent reported replication. Confidence in their relative rankings is consequently weaker (Table 1; §5).
- **Analyst inference—metric dependence:** Recovery depends on domain-specific base-to-teacher gaps. Equal raw-score changes can produce different recovery changes; raw averages help contextualize the headline metric (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—cost portability:** Task-vector streaming increases actor-update time substantially. Overall savings depend partly on rollout generation dominating the measured workload (§4.4).
- **Evidence availability:** Many referenced tables appear only as captions in the supplied text, limiting inspection of full ablation and hyperparameter details.

## Open research questions
- **Does the advantage persist beyond the fixed budget?** The paper does not establish eventual convergence of the control. Extend matched-seed runs and compare recovery at equal wall-clock budgets.
- **Does the method generalize beyond this expert bank?** Other model families, expert counts, and domain combinations are untested. Repeat matched-initialization experiments while varying expert diversity and task-vector alignment.
- **What predicts stable coefficient settings?** Granularity and learning-rate effects vary by scale. Run replicated joint sweeps and test whether task-vector norms or gradient conflicts predict failures.
- **When does streaming overhead erase the savings?** Cost evidence comes from the reported eight-GPU setting. Vary rollout lengths, hardware, and expert-bank size while measuring time to fixed quality.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed matched controls, seed comparisons, and interventions supporting the central result. Material gaps include full bodies of Tables 2–12, GPU model and complete hyperparameters, replicated uncertainty for several ablations, longer-budget outcomes, and evidence outside the evaluated model family and domains. Code availability is author-reported and was not verified.
