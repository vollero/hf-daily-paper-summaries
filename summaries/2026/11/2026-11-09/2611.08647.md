# IWIBench: Evaluating Gradient-Free Model Driven Intra-weight Injection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08647) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08647)


## One-sentence takeaway

IWIBench finds that model-directed, gradient-free weight edits can change lexical associations and output labels in a fixed Gemma target, but generalization to structured output and answer-length defaults remains weak under its constrained evaluation (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether a language model can persistently change another model’s behavior by editing weights while preserving unrelated capabilities. The authors identify a gap in evaluating the entire editing attempt: permitted weight access, feedback budgets, withheld-task performance, and collateral damage. They position IWIBench as the first controlled benchmark for this setting; that priority claim is not independently established by the supplied text (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Behavioral editing benchmark:** Ten task families cover lexical associations, classification-label recoding, language and formatting defaults, factual updates, withholding, and resistance to false suggestions, each paired with preservation controls (§2).
- **Restricted editing protocol:** Editors receive objectives, practice examples, token information, and a tensor manifest. Four tools support bounded weight reading, weight writing, generation, and teacher-forced inspection; gradients and code execution are unavailable (§3.1).
- **Isolated evaluation and scoring:** Only the submitted patch transfers into fresh terminal-evaluation processes. Reward multiplies positive normalized target improvement by retention of baseline-correct controls, then averages equally across families (§§2.1, 3.2).
- **Comparative results and assets:** Ten editor configurations are evaluated on the same thirty episodes. The authors report releasing implementation, task definitions, reinforcement-learning environments, traces, and outputs (§§1, 3.1, 6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported macro reward is 0.4601 | 6.1-sol medium versus 0.4250 low and 0.4551 high; equal-family scoring over thirty episodes on Gemma 3 4B IT. Pristine target: 104/551 successes. | §4.1 |
| Alias acquisition and recoding are comparatively tractable | 6.1-terra and 6.1-sol low/high reach 9/9 alias targets; all three 6.1-sol settings reach 112/112 recoding targets. These counts do not alone establish full control retention. | §4.2 |
| Structured defaults resist terminal generalization | No editor succeeds under the required JSON convention. One Astra trajectory extracts all 64 names correctly but fails JSON formatting on all 64 terminal targets despite practice success. | §§4.3–4.4 |
| Answer-length improvement is limited | Only 5.6-terra earns positive reward: 3/9 targets versus pristine 2/9, retaining all 195 baseline-correct controls; family reward 0.1111. | §4.3 |
| More reasoning helps withholding but does not consistently improve aggregate performance | 6.1-sol withholding successes rise from 2/9 to 6/9 to 9/9 across low, medium, and high effort; corresponding macro rewards are 0.4250, 0.4601, and 0.4551. | §§4.1–4.2, 5.3 |
| Successful local edits can preserve tested controls | An illustrative Astra lexical-inhibition patch passes both terminal targets and retains all 66 baseline-correct controls. | §4.4 |

These results establish selective editing success under the reported target, tools, and budgets. They do not establish general behavioral rewriting, preservation beyond the tested controls, or transfer to other target models. The authors’ suggestion that the capability was specifically taught is an interpretation of model differences, not a demonstrated training-history result (§6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Narrow evaluation — analyst inference:** One target checkpoint, three selected episodes per family, and one recorded run per configuration per episode limit generalization and assessment of run variability (§3.1; Figure 2 caption).
- **Incomplete retrieved results:** Tables 1–3 and C1–C2 appear only as captions. Full rankings, family scores, control breakdowns, and resource totals therefore cannot be reconstructed from this text.
- **Selection procedure unclear:** The supplied episode-selection section points to preservation controls without explaining how the thirty episodes were selected (§2.3).
- **Preservation is conditional:** Retention measures only initially correct controls and pools groups by item count. Positive-gain clipping also hides deterioration within reward alone, although the authors report signed changes separately (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Observed brittleness:** The authors document practice-to-terminal formatting failure and factual-edit spillover into historical answers (§§4.4–5.2).
- **Internal reporting inconsistency:** Section 5.1 calls Astra the best-performing model, whereas §4.1 ranks 6.1-sol medium first. The supplied text does not reconcile this wording.
- **Causal explanations remain tentative:** Neither adaptive revision nor specifically taught capability is isolated through a controlled ablation (§§5–6).

## Open research questions

1. **Do results transfer across target models?** Only Gemma 3 4B IT is tested. Repeat matched task families and budgets across additional checkpoints and architectures.
2. **What causes structured-output generalization failure?** Practice formatting succeeds in one trajectory while terminal formatting fails. Vary extraction wording systematically and compare embedding/output-only edits with edits to other tensors.
3. **Does adaptive revision cause the stronger editors’ advantage?** Trajectories suggest this, but no ablation establishes it. Compare feedback-enabled editing with fixed edits under matched access budgets.
4. **Can scoring better protect nearby and override behavior?** The authors propose increasing penalties for these failures (§5.4). Train editors with alternative control weighting and evaluate on withheld target and preservation tests.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The protocol and several numerical findings are explicit, but the supplied text lacks substantive table contents, uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variability, a clear episode-selection procedure, and full control/resource breakdowns. Reported asset availability and claims about training origins cannot be verified from the supplied text alone.
