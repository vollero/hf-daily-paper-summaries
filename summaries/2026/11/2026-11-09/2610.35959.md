# AgentBaton: Efficient Model Collaboration in Agentic Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35959) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35959)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgentBaton learns when to transfer a running task from a capable model to an efficient model, reporting 65.9% average API cost savings across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, with uneven accuracy preservation and local inference assigned zero cost (Abstract; §§2, 4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents repeatedly invoke expensive models even as the capabilities needed change during execution. The authors argue that routing from the initial request misses this changing task state. They investigate which handoff direction, timing, and history-transfer format can reduce cost while retaining accuracy (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled handoff analysis:** Compare one-time handoffs in both directions and two history interfaces: full conversation context or an inspectable execution logfile. The receiving model inherits the workspace (§§2–3).
- **Learned readiness detector:** Fine-tune Qwen 3.5 2B on 4,000 trajectories from 2,000 SWE-rebench V2 tasks, retrospectively annotated by Codex. Readiness requires a substantive artifact, concrete execution feedback, and localized remaining work. The detector checks after each capable-model turn and triggers a permanent handoff (§4.2; Appendix B).
- **Evaluation:** Test the same detector across model pairs, compare against routing and fixed handoff policies, and combine handoffs with reasoning-effort settings (§§4.3–4.5). Model selection itself is outside the detector’s role (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Aggregate savings do not imply uniform accuracy preservation | Authors report 65.9% average API cost reduction across 11 pairs and two benchmarks versus capable-only execution; 12 of 22 pair–benchmark settings improve accuracy or lose less than 5% relatively. | Abstract; §4.3, Table 3 discussion |
| Capable-first handoffs can preserve accuracy | On DeepSWE, fixed-progress GLM-5.3→Qwen at 30% trajectory progress matches the 61.1% capable-only accuracy with 82.4% lower API cost. | §3.2, Figure 3 |
| Learned handoffs can retain near-baseline accuracy | On DeepSWE, Kimi→Qwen achieves 72.6% versus 73.5% capable-only accuracy, with 72.9% lower API cost. | §4.3, Table 3 discussion |
| Some handoffs improve accuracy | On DeepSWE, Opus→Luna achieves 76.1% versus 67.3% capable-only accuracy, with 70.5% lower API cost. | §4.3 |
| Evaluated per-turn routing is less favorable | Against reproduced MTRouter with Qwen as receiver, AgentBaton reduces API cost by 46.7–86.6%, matching or improving accuracy in all four evaluated settings. | §4.4, Table 4 discussion |
| Reasoning-effort sweep expands the observed frontier | On DeepSWE, Kimi→Luna at high source effort reaches 79.6% accuracy at $1.60/task; the best single-model accuracy in the sweep is Opus at xhigh effort, 75.2% at $8.90/task. | §4.5, Figure 1 |
| Detection has modest measured latency | Across 360 real-input evaluations spanning 2K–121K tokens, median latency is 338 ms and the 95th percentile is 1.11 s on one H100. | §4.2 |

These are author-reported results. They support favorable trade-offs for selected settings, rather than guaranteed accuracy preservation or superiority over every routing strategy. API accounting assigns locally deployable models zero cost (§2). Table captions are supplied, but their numerical bodies are absent, preventing independent inspection of the complete comparisons and aggregate calculation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Reported variability:** Accuracy preservation varies across pairs and benchmarks. Appendix A explicitly reports less consistent checkpoint results on Terminal-Bench and non-monotonic accuracy with later handoffs.
- **Accounting boundary:** Local inference is assigned zero cost (§2). **Analyst inference:** reported API savings do not establish equivalent savings in total compute, hardware, energy, or deployment cost.
- **Annotation validity:** Labels reflect retrospective readiness judgments rather than measured receiving-model success; continuation outcomes are excluded from annotation evidence (Appendix B). **Analyst inference:** readiness supervision is a proxy for successful transfer. Annotator agreement and label validation are not reported.
- **Scope:** Evaluation uses two benchmarks and one shell-based scaffold (§2). **Analyst inference:** generalization to other interfaces and domains remains untested here.
- **Comparison limits:** Only four MTRouter settings are summarized (§4.4). Claims about the difficulty of frequent switching go beyond direct evidence of universal failure.
- **Missing treatment details:** The supplied training description does not explain how trajectories labeled “no clear handoff” enter training (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does annotated readiness predict actual continuation success?** Labels exclude receiving-model outcomes. Next experiment: run multiple receivers from annotated and neighboring checkpoints and measure transfer success against readiness labels.
2. **Do savings survive realistic local compute accounting?** Local execution is priced at zero. Next experiment: include receiver and detector compute, hardware amortization, and end-to-end latency in the cost–accuracy comparison.
3. **How well does the detector transfer beyond shell-based engineering workflows?** Current evidence covers two benchmarks under one scaffold. Next experiment: evaluate the unchanged detector on a different agent interface and task domain.
4. **Can recovery improve settings that lose accuracy?** AgentBaton never returns control after transfer. Next experiment: compare permanent handoff with a limited fallback triggered by failed validation, measuring added cost and recovered accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose explains the method and several concrete results, but omits numerical table bodies and readable plot data.

Material missing information includes complete per-setting results, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variability, the aggregate savings calculation, annotation reliability, training treatment of no-clear-handoff trajectories, train–evaluation overlap checks, explicit detector cost accounting, and confirmed availability of code, weights, and annotations.
