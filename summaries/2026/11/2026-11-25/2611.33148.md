# CoT-Proxy: Cross-Format Transfer for Distilling Long-Horizon Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33148) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33148)


## One-sentence takeaway

Fine-tuning reasoning models on teacher actions alone improved thinking-enabled task success on AppWorld and SWE-bench Verified, and adding brief reasoning summaries raised mean success further in every reported setting, including with a closed teacher.

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon agents must choose effective actions over many tool interactions. The authors study how to teach that policy to a post-trained reasoning model while retaining its existing single-turn capabilities. Standard trajectory distillation supervises the teacher’s reasoning and actions, but closed APIs can hide the full chain of thought. The authors ask whether observable actions, optionally supplemented by summaries, provide enough supervision. (§1)

## Approach and contributions

- **Action-only cross-format transfer.** Prefill an empty reasoning block during training, apply loss only to the parsed action, and render earlier turns in the same format. At inference, the student generates its own reasoning before acting. (§2, Fig. 1)
- **CoT-proxy supervision.** Add a short summary between the prefilled empty block and the action. The open teacher’s summaries are generated offline from its reasoning. The closed teacher’s summaries come from its API. (§2, Appendix A)
- **Capability retention and recipe checks.** Combine LoRA fine-tuning with a KL penalty toward the initial student on fixed, out-of-domain generations. The authors test replay source, summary placement and content, and whether to retain the teacher’s visible prose. (§§2–3, 5, Appendix D.2)

The results establish that action supervision can improve performance when the student switches from an empty reasoning block during training to generating its own reasoning at inference. The shuffled-summary comparison supports a role for turn-specific content. Task success and reasoning-style measurements leave open how closely the student reproduces the teacher’s decisions and how broadly the method preserves prior capabilities. (§6, Appendix D.2)

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported constraints.** The closed-teacher SWE-bench runs used a single seed because of cost, plus a larger, success-filtered corpus and a different pass count. The closed teacher’s hidden reasoning also precluded a full-CoT baseline. (§3, Table 2)
- **Analyst inference.** The SWE-bench results cannot isolate teacher choice from dataset size, success filtering, and training passes. The main replay and formatting ablations center on Qwen3-4B in AppWorld, so claims that particular recipe choices are necessary have limited scope. (§§3, 5)
- **Analyst inference.** Separate training in each environment tests generalization to held-out tasks within that environment. Transfer to a new environment or student family remains untested in the supplied results. Single-turn retention is measured on three benchmarks and one held-out replay set. (§3)

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the closed-teacher SWE-bench gain comes from teacher choice?** The collection and training recipes differ. Compare both teachers on the same training tasks with matched trajectory counts, success filters, passes, and multiple seeds.
2. **Which part of the summary format drives the gain?** The placement ablation also changes the loss mask and supervision of the closing reasoning token. Vary placement and delimiter supervision independently while holding summaries, actions, and retained turns fixed.
3. **How broadly does replay preserve capability?** The retention ablation centers on one student and training environment. Repeat the replay comparison across both student sizes and environments, varying replay source and fraction and measuring retention on tasks outside the replay domains.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the direction of the reported benchmark gains and the core method.
