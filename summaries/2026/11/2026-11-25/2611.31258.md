# RECAP: Training Long-horizon LLM Agents with Context Compaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31258) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31258)


## One-sentence takeaway

RECAP trains Qwen3.5-4B to preserve its behavior after summarizing interaction history, improving accuracy over task-reward-only GRPO by 4.6 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus and 4.3 points on SWE-bench Verified at a 32K working-context limit (§5.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon agents can exhaust their working context before finishing a task. Replacing older interactions with a summary lets the agent continue, but a summary can affect decisions many turns or compactions later. The authors identify a credit-assignment gap in task-reward-only training, where a terminal success signal gives little direct feedback about which summaries preserved the information the agent needed (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Behavior distortion objective.** On continuations sampled with compacted context, RECAP measures token-level KL divergence from the same policy conditioned on the pre-compaction context. Both versions see the same continuation prefix (§4.1, Fig. 1).
- **Two learning signals from one objective.** Low estimated distortion rewards the summary that produced the continuation. On-policy self-distillation gives individual action tokens feedback from the pre-compaction teacher. The implemented continuation update detaches the sampling process and optimizes a surrogate gradient (​​§4.2, Appendix A.2).
- **Joint training on existing rollouts.** RECAP combines both signals with the task-success advantage in GRPO. The authors report that teacher scoring uses the same sampled trajectories, requiring no additional rollouts or environment interactions (§4.3, Appendix B.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher accuracy at matched context budget | With Qwen3.5-4B at 32K, RECAP scored 62.8% vs. 58.2% on BrowseComp-Plus (+4.6 points) and 51.1% vs. 46.8% on SWE-bench Verified (+4.3 points) against task-only GRPO with compaction. Accuracy was averaged over four evaluation runs. | §§5.1–5.3, Tables 1–2 |
| Smaller gap to the long-context reference | On BrowseComp-Plus, 32K RECAP was 1.0 percentage point below 128K GRPO without compaction. On SWE-bench Verified, 32K RECAP scored 51.1% vs. 55.5% for that reference. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| More efficient trajectories | Against task-only GRPO at 32K, mean tool calls per trajectory fell from 23.4 to 19.9 on BrowseComp-Plus and from 158.9 to 145.6 on SWE-bench Verified. The authors also report fewer cumulative tokens per trajectory and higher accuracy among trajectories that triggered compaction. | §5.2, Fig. 2, Table 2 |
| Both learning signals gave the highest accuracy | In the 32K ablation, summary reward alone scored 56.7% and 47.9%, and self-distillation alone scored 60.3% and 47.1%, on BrowseComp-Plus and SWE-bench Verified respectively. Combining them yielded 62.8% and 51.1%. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Gains persisted under tighter working contexts | With a fixed 128K total interaction budget, 32K-trained RECAP maintained 60–63% BrowseComp-Plus accuracy at working windows of 16K–64K and beat both GRPO variants at 16K. On SWE-bench Verified it beat 32K-trained GRPO at every tested window. | §5.4, Fig. 3 |

The matched comparison and ablation support an accuracy benefit from combining the two signals in the tested setup. The tool-call and token results measure execution efficiency. The authors hypothesize that better post-compaction behavior reduces redundant interactions, but the experiments do not isolate that mechanism or quantify training compute.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope.** RECAP is evaluated on one model across two domains. The authors leave broader model scales, task domains, longer horizons, and more varied information densities and representations for future work (§6).
- **Author-stated implementation boundary.** Distortion credit ends at the next compaction or task termination, and later distortion does not train earlier summaries directly. The continuation update also omits the gradient through the distribution of visited prefixes (Appendices A.2, B.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The compacted-trajectory comparison may condition on different subsets for each policy, since a policy’s own behavior determines whether it reaches the compaction trigger. Figure 2(c) therefore provides descriptive evidence about those trajectories, with the matched overall benchmark comparison giving the clearer estimate of the performance difference (§3, §5.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the benefit transfer across model sizes?** RECAP is tested on one model. Train several model sizes with RECAP and matched task-only GRPO, then compare accuracy and trajectory cost under the same context budgets (§5.1, §6).
2. **Would credit across compactions improve early summaries?** The current distortion signal stops at the next compaction. On tasks that regularly require repeated compaction, compare segment-local credit with a variant that also credits earlier summaries for later distortion (Appendix B.2).
3. **When does preserving pre-compaction behavior help or hurt?** The teacher is the same policy with more context, and summary reward alone has mixed results. Compare matched training variants that apply distortion supervision to all continuations or only to successful ones, and measure both task success and behavior distortion (§§4.1, 5.3).
