# Clinical3D-Bench: Evaluating and Improving Spatial Intelligence of Multimodal LLMs in Hospital Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32094) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32094)


## One-sentence takeaway

On simulated hospital walkthroughs, fine-tuning VideoJev-4B raised macro accuracy from 45.5% to 73.9%, and GeoJeV’s scene-specific 3D computations raised it to 75.7%, with only a small gain on route planning (Table 2, §5.3).

## Problem and motivation

A hospital assistant using egocentric video may need to identify which bed a device belongs to, interpret left and right from a patient’s perspective, or judge whether equipment can fit through a passage. The authors argue that general spatial benchmarks give limited coverage to these judgments and that patient privacy and the cost of measuring hospital geometry make real-world benchmark construction difficult (§1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Benchmark and training data.** Clinical3D-Bench contains 3,368 multiple-choice questions across eight types on 569 clips from 373 simulated layouts. Answers come from the underlying 3D scenes. Paired states change one scene condition to test whether models update their answers. Physicians screened the layouts. Clinical3D-45K supplies 44,750 training questions from layouts disjoint from the benchmark (§3, Appendix D).
2. **Model evaluation and diagnostics.** The authors evaluate 35 zero-shot MLLMs, fine-tune two, and measure sensitivity to answer-changing scene states and to swapping the 3D input across clips (§4, §5.1, Appendix E).
3. **GeoJeV.** Linear heads on a frozen, fine-tuned VideoJev-4B select a 3D operation and its arguments from the model’s question state. Object-track queries, free-space search, or a learned bird’s-eye-view map reader compute scores from a scene memory reconstructed from RGB video. GeoJeV adds those scores to the model’s option scores (§5.2, Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot accuracy was low | On the full benchmark, the top open models, InternVL3-14B and InternVL3.5-14B, tied at 45.7% macro accuracy. Gemini 3.6 Flash led proprietary models at 53.6%. The test-fitted answer prior scored 40.8%. | Table 2, §4.2 |
| Patient-relative directions exposed a systematic error | On the two-choice Patient frame task, 27 of 32 open zero-shot models scored below 40% accuracy against 50% chance. Qwen3-VL-32B scored 13.4% and chose the image-side answer on 93.4% of the 241 bedside items where image and patient sides were opposite. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Fine-tuning helped across held-out layouts | On the full benchmark, VideoJev-4B rose from 45.5% to 73.9% macro accuracy and from 17.0% to 93.7% on Patient frame. Route and Passability remained its weakest types at 58.3% and 66.0%. Spatial-MLLM SFT reached 69.9% macro accuracy. | Table 2, §4.2 |
| Fine-tuning reduced excess answer repetition | Across 204 answer-changing groups, VideoJev-4B’s unchanged-answer rate exceeded the same-subtask answer-habit reference by 8.8 percentage points zero-shot and 1.5 points after fine-tuning, averaged over three fine-tuning seeds. | Appendix E, Table 9 |
| Most tested 3D inputs showed little scene dependence | On the full benchmark, swapping 3D input changed macro accuracy by at most 0.4 percentage points for BEV tokens, local BEV reads, and ground-truth BEV. The geometry head lost 1.4 points with swapped input and improved by 0.6 points over plain fine-tuning with its own input. | §5.1, Table 3 |
| GeoJeV improved accuracy and relied on scene-specific evidence | On the full benchmark, GeoJeV raised macro accuracy from 73.9% to 75.7% over three seeds, a gain of 1.8 percentage points (paired layout-bootstrap 95% CI, +0.7 to +3.0). Another layout’s scene memory reduced it to 70.4%. Passability rose from 66.0% to 69.8%, and Route rose from 58.3% to 59.5%. | §5.3, Tables 2 and 4 |
| Physicians led on the matched subset | On the same 400-question Clinical3D-Bench-mini subset, five physicians scored 79.0% macro accuracy, GeoJeV scored 75.5%, and the best zero-shot model, Gemini 3.6 Flash, scored 57.0%. | Appendix E, Table 7 |

The fine-tuning gains carry across rendered training clips and photorealistically transformed test clips from held-out layouts produced by the same generator. GeoJeV’s shuffle result supports the authors’ claim that its predictions depend on the reconstructed scene. Transfer to real hospital footage, unfamiliar question forms, and actual navigation remains untested. The physician comparison uses the same questions but different viewing conditions, including full clips and unlimited replay for physicians (Appendices D–G).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** All scenes are synthetic, static, and unoccupied. Training and test layouts share generators, assets, and templates. GeoJeV depends on estimated geometry and argument heads trained on template-derived targets (Appendix G).
- **Author-stated.** Camera registration checks pose consistency. Question evidence is checked on the source renders, and physicians screened one transferred clip per layout, leaving per-question evidence preservation after transfer unverified (§§3.3–3.5).
- **Author-stated.** The official score retains questions flagged for possible text shortcuts. The authors also flag possible downward bias for the two Qwen3-VL models used to refine question formulations (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference.** Cross-model rankings reflect differences in viewing budgets and decoding settings as well as model capability. The GeoJeV and 3D-input shuffle experiments also use different donor-selection rules, which limits direct comparison of their effect sizes (Appendices E–F).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the gains transfer to real hospitals?** Both splits use the same synthetic generator. A next experiment would evaluate zero-shot, fine-tuned, and GeoJeV variants on held-out real walkthroughs with independently verified answers and measured clearances (Appendix G).
2. **Did fine-tuning teach a general patient-relative frame transformation?** The reported gain comes from a template-based benchmark whose visible bedside cues systematically oppose the patient’s side. A next experiment would balance viewpoints around the bed and use independently written prompts (§4.2, Appendix G).
3. **What limits route and clearance accuracy?** GeoJeV makes a small route gain, and its straight-route questions go to a map reader that checks neither the marked path nor overhead clearance. A next experiment would vary reconstructed versus ground-truth occupancy and predicted versus ground-truth endpoints, then test an explicit swept-volume clearance check on straight routes (§5.3, Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported benchmark results and method summary. The supplied text lacks real-hospital performance, per-question audits of evidence preservation after appearance transfer, and end-to-end GeoJeV latency or compute measurements. It lists a benchmark dataset link, while the release status of GeoJeV code and weights is unspecified.
