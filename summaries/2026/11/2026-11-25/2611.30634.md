# Rewirable Circuits in Transformers with Loom Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30634) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30634)


## One-sentence takeaway

LoomFormer lets attention heads change which earlier heads they read from at each token, and its 2.8B model matches Pythia-12B on average zero-shot accuracy and exceeds it in five-shot evaluation at the same 300B-token training budget (Table 2, §5.2).

## Problem and motivation

Standard Transformers choose which tokens to attend to, but their learned projections fix how earlier head outputs feed later heads’ queries, keys, and values. The authors argue that this makes it hard to select different circuits without interference or duplicating heads. Existing depth-wise attention and residual-stream methods route representations after head outputs have been mixed, leaving a gap in per-token, head-level routing (§§1–2, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Head-level depth attention.** Each head reads compressed outputs from earlier heads to build its queries, keys, and values for causal attention across tokens, then writes its own output for later heads to read (§§3.1–3.2, Fig. 4).
- **A fixed-size matrix stream.** LoomFormer stores these writes in a per-token matrix that accumulates information across layers. The vector stream generates normalized, gated read queries and write keys, so each read uses a fixed number of slots as depth grows (§§3.1–3.2).
- **A wiring-capacity analysis.** The authors derive a head-count bound for node-gated architectures on an exact routing task. They show that the same-token matrix stream can realize any wiring pattern involving at most as many writers as memory slots, given suitable read and write vectors (§2, Theorem 2.4, §3.3, Proposition 3.1).

The results support a quality gain in the reported Pile-based comparisons. The compute-to-quality ratios are interpolated from scaling curves, so they do not directly establish wall-clock savings to reach a target loss. The authors attribute the larger few-shot gain to heads spending less attention on sinks. Appendix H shows that freezing read queries reduces the target-token probability on one in-context prompt, which supports a causal role for dynamic reads in that example. The benchmark-wide role of reduced sink attention remains unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ scope limits.** Active writers can interfere when they share slots. The node-gating bound covers exact, interference-free direct routing under its stated assumptions and excludes routing through FFNs. The appendix gives an FFN construction but leaves its minimum cost open (§3.3, Appendices A.2 and A.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The ablations are confined to the Medium model and Pile validation loss, while the mechanistic comparisons use 2.8B checkpoints on held-out Pile text. How those findings transfer across scales, corpora, or tasks remains uncertain (§§5.3, 5.5).
- **Analyst inference.** LoomFormer-XL also changes the vector-stream FFN activation for stability. Without an activation-matched comparison, the XL result does not isolate the effect of loom attention (Appendix D.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does less attention to sinks explain the few-shot gain across tasks?** The paper reports aggregate sink statistics and one read-query intervention. Apply controlled read-query and sink-attention interventions separately across the five-shot suite and measure per-task accuracy (§5.3, Appendix H).
2. **How should slot count scale with model width and depth?** The reported sweep covers only the Medium model. Repeat it across scales and compare validation loss and measured throughput at matched parameter budgets (§5.5, Fig. 7).
3. **Do the gains persist with sparse or linear attention across tokens?** The authors propose the combination, and the reported model uses causal dot-product attention. Train matched pairs with and without loom attention under the same sequence attention mechanism and compare loss and throughput across context lengths (§3.2, §6).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported settings and table values. The supplied text does not establish training-seed variability or uncertainty intervals for the main quality comparisons, the exact size and sampling seed of the FLAN subset, or public availability of full training code and LoomPythia checkpoints. Appendix C provides architecture pseudocode.
