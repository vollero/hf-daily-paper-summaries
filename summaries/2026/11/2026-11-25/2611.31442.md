# Small Learned Projections for Semantic-Reversal-Aware Retrieval with Spectral Acceleration: A Four-Domain Empirical Study

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31442) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31442)


## One-sentence takeaway

Domain-specific linear projections of frozen E5-Large-v2 embeddings yielded zero observed top-10 trap hits and 93–99% top-10 paraphrase retention on the cases used to train and select them across four domains, while transfer was partial and legal query rewording reduced paraphrase retrieval (§§6.1, 6.3, 6.7).

## Problem and motivation

A dense retriever can rank a clause alongside a near-copy that reverses its meaning through a small edit, such as a negation, quantity change, or change in patent claim scope. This matters when users need the original meaning for compliance, clause lookup, or answering a question. The paper asks whether a small projection over a frozen encoder can keep specified reversals out of the top 10 while retaining correct paraphrases, with a separate question about serving the projection over large pools (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Training recipe.** Train a linear projection on triples consisting of an anchor, its meaning-reversing trap, and a correct paraphrase. A cosine-preservation loss maintains pairwise embedding geometry, while a hinge loss favors the paraphrase over the trap. The authors identify the deterministic recipe and duplicate-check gate as contributions and describe the loss ingredients as standard metric learning (§§1, 3.1–3.6).
- **Four-domain evaluation and release.** Build matched-shape trap corpora across legal, medical, patent, and financial text, measure all matched and mismatched lens–corpus combinations, and release the trap sets, trained projections, generators, and exact-search harness under AGPL-3.0 (§§3.2, 6.3, 9; Appendix A).
- **Spectral serving.** Replace the dense spectral build with a boundary-conditioned Laplacian solve. A fixed set of corpus boundaries lets queries reuse a factorization while changing the boundary values. The implementation and graph construction are proprietary (§3.9).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Exact search suppressed declared traps | HN@10 was 0.0000 in all 12 domain–pool settings at roughly 5k, 50k, and 100k documents. HitC@10 was about 0.93–0.99. At roughly 5k, unprefixed raw E5 cosine had HN@10 of 0.8127–0.9967 across the four domains. | §6.1, Table 1 |
| Dimension reduction alone did not match the trained lens | At matched output dimensions, Gaussian random projection and distractor-only PCA left HN@10 at 53–97% across the four 5k pools under exact search, versus 0.0000 for the trained lenses. | §6.6, Table 6 |
| Transfer was partial | At 5k, all 12 mismatched lens–corpus pairs improved on the target domain’s raw cosine. Eleven had HN@10 of 0.44–0.85. The Legal lens on Patent reached 0.2957 versus 0.9967 for raw cosine, missing the preregistered off-domain criterion of HN@10 ≥ 0.30. | §6.3, Table 4, Fig. 3 |
| Rewording reduced legal retrieval utility | Across 194 eligible cases, the shipped lens had HitC@10 of 0.7938 and HN@10 of 0.0052 with query-only rewording against the original corpus. Joint query-and-corpus rewording yielded 0.9845 and 0.0155, respectively. On the second query-only variant, HitC@10 was 0.8093 for the shipped lens and 0.7784 for the lens fine-tuned on the first variant. | §6.7 |
| Spectral serving retained trap suppression at the largest pools | At roughly 100k documents in each domain, HN@10 was 0.0000 and the roughly 300-query suite took 0.6–1.1 s on a laptop CPU, averaging about 2–4 ms per query with query encoding excluded. Across the sealed, matched Legal pools, spectral serving was 116–143× faster than the three-stage cascade, with HitC@10 lower by 1.0, 0.3, and 4.0 percentage points at 5k, 51k, and 100k, respectively. | §§6.2, 6.5, Tables 2–3, Fig. 5 |

The exact-search results establish observed suppression on the fitted cases in the measured distractor pools. The cases were reused across sizes, and in three domains the pools were nested, so adding documents could only preserve or worsen a trap’s exact rank. These measurements provide no independent estimate of failure rates on unseen reversals. The timing establishes a retrieval-only suite average and a speedup against the Legal cascade. A same-pool timing comparison with full-pool exact search or original dense SpectRAG was absent (§§6.1–6.2, 6.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope.** The evaluation uses constructed, length-matched clauses and one encoder configuration. Natural retrieval failures, long-form contradictions, other encoders, and sparse or hybrid baselines remain untested. Legal rewordings are mild by design and preserve the edited tokens, with full semantic equivalence unverified (§§2, 6.7, 7.2–7.3).
- **Authors’ comparison caveats.** Exact and spectral search used differently built pools, so their HitC@10 differences cannot isolate the backend effect. The accelerator’s implementation is proprietary, and its near-linear complexity depends on graph or solver conditions. The Legal dimension follow-up used one seed and changed the warm-start history (§§3.9, 6.2, 6.4).
- **Analyst inference.** Because the declared cases were used for training and selection, fitting those cases remains a plausible explanation for the zero-hit result. PCA and random projections rule out dimension reduction alone as an explanation in the tested setting, while the relative roles of the preservation and hinge losses remain unresolved (§§3.6, 6.1, 6.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the lens suppress unseen reversals within a domain?** The headline evaluation reuses fitted cases. Train on one set of source clauses, then measure HN@10 and HitC@10 on held-out clauses with both familiar and held-out reversal types (§§6.1, 7.2).
2. **Can training improve utility on genuinely new query phrasings?** The Legal fine-tune improved retrieval on a fitted wording, with no gain on the second variant. Evaluate independently authored paraphrases against a fixed corpus and reserve all test wordings before training (§6.7).
3. **What serving advantage remains against full-pool exact search?** The same-pool timing used the cascade baseline. Run both backends on identical projected vectors and queries, and measure retrieval quality, offline setup cost, and single-query latency (§§3.9, 6.2, 6.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The supplied prose gives clear headline results and comparison settings, and Appendix A provides artifact hash prefixes. The supplied text omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–6, so exact pool sizes, complete per-cell scores, and full runtime rows are unavailable. The proprietary graph construction and sealed run logs, same-pool exact-search timing, and independent within-domain generalization results are also unavailable. Section 6.7 reports one trap hit on the original phrasing in its Legal subset, while §6.1 reports zero on the main declared-case Legal runs. The supplied text leaves the difference in evaluation setup unresolved.
