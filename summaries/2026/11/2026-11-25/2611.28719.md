# KMed-31B Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28719) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28719)


## One-sentence takeaway

KMed-31B uses task-specific specialists and multi-teacher delta distillation to improve unadjusted HealthBench scores over Gemma-4-31B-IT in both response modes while keeping medical multiple-choice accuracy close to or above the base model (§§4.2, 5.4).

## Problem and motivation

Institutions that keep patient data on premises need models that can handle medical exams, case diagnosis, and clinical consultations within local hardware constraints. The authors identify a post-training trade-off: improving consultation responses can reduce exam accuracy. They attribute this to differences in expected outputs and available feedback. Exam answers and diagnosis labels support answer matching, while several responses to a clinical inquiry may be acceptable and require judgment against multiple criteria (§§1, 2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task taxonomy and specialist training.** The recipe groups tasks into open-ended medical QA, multiple-choice medical QA, diagnosis prediction, and clinical inquiry. The first three use answer-matching reinforcement learning. The inquiry specialist uses supervised fine-tuning, fixed-rubric RL, and then dynamic-rubric RL. The Gemma thinking branch omits the open-ended QA specialist (§§2.1, 3–3.1).
- **Synthetic Health Dialogue (SHD).** The authors build consultations for lay users and clinicians, grounded in common primary-care presentations. The simulator tracks information the user knows privately so the assistant must respond to potentially incomplete context (§2.2.3).
- **Diversified ROPD (D-ROPD).** The authors extend rubric-based on-policy distillation by generating rubrics from current policy rollouts and fixed responses from several distinct teacher models (§3.1, Figure 5).
- **Multi-teacher On-policy Delta Distillation (MOPD 2).** The authors combine multi-teacher distillation with a token-level log-probability delta between each task specialist and a frozen reference model, evaluated on the student’s own rollouts (§3.2, Figure 4).

The evidence supports a favorable balance between conversation scores and exam accuracy for the tested checkpoints. The ranking depends on the scoring protocol and response length, while the integration comparisons leave some training differences uncontrolled. Clinical utility and on-premises performance remain unmeasured in the supplied evaluation. The price–performance figure assigns KMed the base model’s API price as a proxy (§§4.1, 5.1, 6, Figure 1, Appendix A.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The evaluation leaves out electronic health records, clinical tool use, and patient-care studies. HealthBench uses a different grader from the original reports, and unadjusted scores favor longer responses. Some integration comparisons use different initializations and training lengths, and most configurations were trained once. Some external medical-model scores also come from different or incompletely documented protocols (§§4.1, 4.3, 6).
- **Analyst inference.** The sequential D-ROPD gain leaves the contribution of teacher diversity unresolved because the paper provides no matched single-teacher online-rubric control. The context-awareness improvements are consistent with SHD’s design, but the supplied text provides no matched ablation of the simulator’s private-information mechanism (§§3.1, 5.5–5.6).
- **Analyst inference.** The Qwen result supports transfer to a second family, but family, response mode, specialist coverage, and the reference-checkpoint setup differ from the non-thinking Gemma comparison (§§3, 3.2, 5.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does teacher diversity itself improve clinical inquiry?** The sequential results leave this open. Compare D-ROPD with single-teacher ROPD using the same number of references, prompts, training budget, and multiple seeds (§§3.1, 5.6).
2. **How much of the cross-task balance comes from the delta objective?** Mix-RL and distillation use different initializations and training lengths. Compare Mix-RL, MOPD, and MOPD 2 under matched starting checkpoints and compute budgets across seeds (§§5.1, 6).
3. **Can shorter answers retain the clinically useful gains?** Length adjustment changes the Hard ranking. Compare models under a common response budget and ask blinded clinicians to score accuracy, completeness, and safety (§§5.7, 6, Appendix A.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The prose specifies the pipeline and headline comparisons. The supplied text omits full table rows, including complete benchmark results and the hyperparameters referenced in Table 8, as well as the verbatim Appendix A.3 prompt. It also leaves training-seed variability, measured on-premises hardware and latency requirements, clinical outcomes, and access to weights, code, and SHD data unestablished.
