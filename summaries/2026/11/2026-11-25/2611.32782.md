# Measuring Layer-wise Intrinsic Dimensionality of FFNs in LLMs via PCA

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32782) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32782)


## One-sentence takeaway

At a mean reconstruction cosine threshold of 0.99, middle-layer FFN activations required a larger fraction of PCA directions than edge layers in 76% of 58 scanned LLMs, and allocating pruned widths from each teacher’s own profile beat uniform widths on the five-task average for all four main distillation teachers (§§3.2, 4.2–4.4).

## Problem and motivation

Transformer LLMs typically use the same FFN intermediate width at every depth. The paper asks whether layers differ in how many activation directions they use and whether those differences can guide a fixed pruning budget. The gap identified in §1 is a lack of both large-scale measurement and controlled tests of whether a measured profile improves on uniform width.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Measurement protocol.** Fit centered PCA to activations immediately before each FFN down-projection, using roughly 100,000 unlabeled SlimPajama tokens per layer. Estimate \(d_{\theta,l}\) by interpolating the rank at which mean reconstruction cosine reaches \(\theta\). At the default threshold of 0.99, \(\sigma_{99}\) is the across-layer standard deviation of \(d_{0.99,l}/D_l\), where \(D_l\) is the original FFN width (§2).
2. **Empirical characterization.** Profile 58 open-weight LLMs across 14 families and 70M–72B parameters, compare calibration corpora and activation sites, and track dispersion through pretraining and selected post-training stages (§3, Appendix B).
3. **Controlled width allocation.** Use the measured ranks to set layer widths, then retain neurons by activation magnitude. Compare PCA-guided, uniform, and inverted allocations under approximately matched FFN budgets and the same pruning and distillation recipe within each teacher (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Middle-heavy profiles recur | At \(\theta=0.99\), the middle third had a higher mean normalized rank than the edge thirds in 76% of 58 models. | §3.2, Figs. 2 and 5 |
| Calibration preserves profile shape better than absolute level | On Qwen3-8B with six roughly 100K-token corpora, per-layer Pearson \(r\) was 0.953–0.990 among five English corpora and 0.941–0.993 between Chinese SkyPile and the English corpora. Chinese calibration shifted mean normalized rank by +0.064. | §3.1, Fig. 1 |
| Dispersion evolves during pretraining | \(\sigma_{99}\) rose from the first checkpoint to a later peak in 20 of 21 trajectories across six families. Ten trajectories declined by more than 10% after their peak. | §3.3, Fig. 3 |
| Dispersion is fairly stable in selected post-training comparisons | Relative \(\sigma_{99}\) changes stayed within 10% in the reported general and domain-specific post-training, continued-pretraining, and annealing comparisons. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B was 26% below its base. | §3.4, Table 6 |
| PCA-guided widths lead the main distillation comparison | At step 3K under approximately matched FFN budgets, PCA-guided > Uniform > Inverted on the unweighted five-task average for all four teachers. PCA-guided gains over Uniform were +2.50 pp on Qwen2.5-3B, +2.28 pp on Qwen3-8B, about +0.9 pp on Gemma-3-4B, and +0.52 pp on LLaMA-3-8B. Qwen2.5-3B and LLaMA-3-8B results average three training seeds. | §§4.1–4.4, Table 1, Abstract |
| Alternative allocations give mixed results | At step 3K, explained-variance PCA was comparable on Qwen2.5-3B and trailed cosine PCA on Gemma-3-4B. SlimLLM trailed cosine PCA on both. In a single matched-budget transfer run, a Qwen3-8B profile applied to LLaMA-3-8B scored below Uniform on the five-task average. | §4.3, Table 3, Appendices E and G |
| The gain survives selected training changes | Qwen2.5-3B retained the ordering with language-model cross-entropy in place of distillation. After two epochs of identical SFT on Qwen3-8B students, PCA-guided beat Uniform by +11.0 pp on ARC-Challenge Chat, +4.9 pp on generative MMLU-Redux, and +3.0 pp on GSM8K under zero-shot chat evaluation. | §§4.5–4.6, Table 4 |
| Non-uniform widths carry a serving cost in the tested configuration | On one V100 using vLLM with CUDA-graph decoding, the trained Qwen2.5-3B PCA-guided student delivered 592.83 output tokens/s versus 626.30 for Uniform, a 5.3% throughput penalty at a 0.4% parameter difference. | Appendix F |

The matched-budget comparisons support the measured profile as an allocation signal under the tested pruning and training recipes. The authors report larger gains for the higher-dispersion teachers, but four main teachers leave the predictive relationship uncertain. The calibration correlations support stability of profile shape on Qwen3-8B. The shift in mean rank shows that absolute widths can still depend on calibration data.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Family, architecture, data, and training recipe vary together in the public checkpoints, so the cause of family-level dispersion remains unresolved. The four main teachers provide limited evidence for predicting gains from \(\sigma_{99}\). The pruning runs cover 1.57–4.2B training tokens, leaving the cited 70–100B-token regime untested (§5).
- **Author-stated.** The measure is an upper bound on the dimension of the best subspace under the cosine criterion because PCA optimizes explained variance. The reported serving implementation used no per-shape kernel tuning (§2, Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference.** Cross-language calibration was tested on one model, downstream SFT on one teacher’s students, and the cross-model transfer on one source–target pair with one training run. These checks support their specific settings (§§3.1, 4.6, Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference.** The three 1B-class teachers all had low dispersion and yielded gaps below 0.6 pp on the five-task average, leaving size and dispersion entangled in that regime. The longer LLaMA-3-8B evaluation-seed check reused the same trained weights for each allocation, leaving long-horizon training-seed variability unmeasured (§4.4, Appendices D and H).

## Open research questions

1. **Does \(\sigma_{99}\) predict pruning gains within a fixed architecture and scale?** Family and recipe vary among the main teachers. Train same-size variants within one architecture and compare PCA-guided and uniform allocation under a shared pruning recipe across the resulting dispersion range (§§4.4, 5).
2. **Are high-rank layers more sensitive to width reductions?** The paper changes widths jointly, leaving individual layer sensitivity unresolved. Prune one layer at a time by the same relative amount and test whether the resulting loss increases track normalized rank (§5).
3. **Do the gains persist at longer recovery-training horizons?** The reported runs fall below the cited Minitron-scale token budget. Extend the matched-budget PCA-guided, Uniform, and Inverted runs to that regime and compare learning curves across training seeds (§5).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported measurement protocol and headline comparisons in their stated settings. The supplied text includes table and figure captions but omits full numeric table bodies and the supplementary profiles for all 58 models. It also leaves unclear how ranks outside the stated 10%–90% interpolation grid are estimated, which matters for reproducing the roughly 91% mean width reported for randomly initialized Qwen2.5-3B (§§2, 3.2).
