# Model Carving: Training Transformers That Optimize Their Own Topology

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27962) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27962)


## One-sentence takeaway

Model Carving learns which Transformer sublayers to remove during pretraining, yielding lower validation bits per byte at matched training FLOPs and 8–34% fewer inference FLOPs per token than the tested full-depth baselines (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Transformer training typically fixes the architecture before optimizing the weights. The authors argue that repeating the same attention and MLP pattern at every layer can waste compute, and that architecture search and post-training compression make structural decisions in separate optimization stages. They ask whether a single run can learn which sublayers justify their cost and use the saved compute to train on more tokens within the same budget (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Joint weight and topology learning.** Each attention or MLP application writes to the residual stream through a learned scalar gate after RMS normalization. The model also learns connections across depth. In weight-tied Transformers, each loop has its own gates, so a shared sublayer can run in one loop and be skipped in another (§2.1, Figure 1).
- **An online estimate of what saved compute buys.** The trainer scores the same batch with current and earlier weights, then fits a power law to forecast how much extra tokens would reduce loss. That estimate sets a logarithmic gate penalty. When a gate reaches zero, the trainer removes the application in a subsequent graph compilation and spends the freed FLOPs on more tokens (§§2.2–2.4).
- **Evaluation across standard and looped models.** The authors compare against full-depth and approximately inference-matched shallower models, test pruning and distillation baselines, and characterize which sublayers survive across scales and symbolic tasks (§§3–5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lower validation loss and inference cost | Across the tested standard and looped pairs, carved models had lower held-out bpb at matched training FLOPs and at matched token counts before cooldown. Inference used 8–34% fewer FLOPs per token than the corresponding full-depth models. | Table 1, Figures 2 and 4 |
| Largest fitted training-compute advantage in the looped family | At looped 1.3B, final validation bpb was 0.683 carved versus 0.704 full-depth at matched training FLOPs. The fitted full-depth curve required 1.71× the compute to reach the carved loss. Across the tested budgets, fitted compute-optimal losses also favored carving in both families. | §3, Table 1, Figure 5, Table 14 |
| Generation gains depended on the sampling budget | At nearly matched inference FLOPs, the standard 2.7B carved model drew 24 samples per prompt versus 16 for full-depth and improved the fraction solved on all five benchmarks, with paired 95% intervals above zero. At 16 samples each, it scored lower on TriviaQA and LAMBADA, with paired intervals below zero. | Table 2 |
| Higher measured decoding throughput | On one H100 at an 8,192-token context, the standard 2.7B carved model reached 1.45× full-depth throughput at the largest common batch and 1.61× when each model used its largest tested batch in the compiled-loop timing. | Table 10, Appendix D |
| Stronger than the tested compression baselines | At standard 1.3B, carving reached 0.727 validation bpb and 31.2 CORE-22 at matched total training FLOPs and approximately matched inference FLOPs. The three pruning variants reached 0.742–0.761 bpb and 27.6–28.1 CORE-22. Carving also beat all reported distilled students on both metrics, with teacher training and forward-pass costs excluded. | §4.3, Tables 12–13 |
| Small symbolic circuits, with variable accuracy | On modular addition, all three carved seeds retained two of 24 applications, both attention. Held-out exact-match accuracy was 99.8%, 100%, and 76.4%. | Figure 6, Table 19 |

The strongest evidence is for the validation-loss and inference-cost trade-off under the tested recipes. Downstream gains are less uniform. Several CORE-22 intervals against full-depth include zero, and the generation advantage in Table 2 depends on spending the saved FLOPs on more samples. The compute multipliers come from fitted loss curves, and the symbolic runs demonstrate that small circuits can work without establishing that those circuits are minimal.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The study uses from-scratch language models up to 2.7B standard and 1.3B looped. Removal is irreversible, happens mostly early while the forecast relies on its prior, and stops during cooldown. The deployed graph is static, and the pruning objective prices FLOPs at whole-sublayer granularity (§8, Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference.** The DenseFormer-style controls sometimes achieve lower validation loss than carving while using more inference FLOPs. The comparison supports the efficiency benefit of removal, while leaving how much of the full-depth loss improvement comes from learned connections versus gate-driven removal unresolved (§5.1, Table 6).
- **Analyst inference.** Final-loss replication is narrower than the topology comparisons because several second-seed runs stopped early. The pruning comparison covers one standard model size, and each distillation entry represents one training run (Tables 12, 13, and 15).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the gains hold at larger scales and on other corpora?** The main pretraining study uses FineWeb-Edu within the reported size range. Train larger standard and looped pairs on a second corpus at matched FLOPs and compare validation loss and inference cost.
2. **How much benefit comes from the final topology itself?** The learned masks are predictable across sizes, but the supplied experiments do not compare training those masks from initialization with discovering them online. Train fixed carved masks, matched random masks, and online-carved models with the same residual connections and training FLOP budget (§5.2).
3. **Can a schedule-aware pressure improve removal decisions?** The current forecast assumes a constant learning rate, and pressure turns off during cooldown. Compare the existing method with a forecast that includes the cooldown schedule, measuring final bpb, inference FLOPs, and variation across seeds (§§5.5, 8).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported comparisons within their stated settings. The supplied text does not confirm public availability of training code or carved checkpoints. It also lacks completed second-seed results for several larger pairs and fixed-topology-from-scratch comparisons that would isolate the value of discovering the mask during training.
