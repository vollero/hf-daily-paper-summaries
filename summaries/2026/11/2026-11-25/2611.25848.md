# Interpretable Prototype-Conditioned Adaptation for Weather Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25848) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25848)


## One-sentence takeaway

ProtoCast uses sparse, patch-specific mixtures of weather-pattern prototypes to improve short-range forecasts on two benchmarks and trace how individual feature corrections affect forecast error (§§4.2–4.5).

## Problem and motivation

Locations with similar current conditions can evolve differently, and variables at the same location have distinct but interdependent dynamics. The authors argue that conventional forecasters encode these patterns implicitly, making their influence on local predictions difficult to trace, while sample- or task-level prototype methods leave open how to adapt individual locations and variable groups (§1, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Local matching by variable group.** Each group has its own prototype bank. At each patch, ProtoCast combines historical feature means, endpoint changes, and cross-variable forecast context derived from the input history. Cosine matching with entmax produces sparse weights shared across forecast leads (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Prototype-specific corrections.** The weights mix low-rank operators that produce lead-specific residuals. Each residual decomposes into weighted prototype contributions before gated cross-variable fusion, so individual terms can be removed and their effects measured (§§3.5–3.6, 3.8).
- **Tracked refitting during training.** The forecaster and correction operators train jointly from scratch. Before each epoch, the method refits the matching space and prototypes, using previous sample assignments to initialize prototype slots and maintain their association with learned operators. The banks are fixed at inference (§3.7, §S1.2).

| Typhoon tracks | Mean Haversine error against ERA5 pressure-derived centers was lower at every 1–12-hour lead for Doksuri versus Met 2 Net over 10 evaluated starts, and for Mawar versus TAU over 16 starts. | §4.3, Fig. 5, §S1.5, Fig. S15 |

The evidence supports short-range accuracy gains under the reported protocols and shows that selected prototype terms can improve local forecasts within the frozen model. The history and permutation tests link matched patterns to useful learned corrections. These experiments leave atmospheric causation, operational track skill, and performance beyond the tested grids and horizons unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-identified open scope.** Transfer across regions and seasons is future work. The authors propose reuse as a way to reduce retraining, but report no transfer experiment (§5).
- **Analyst inference.** The two downsampled benchmarks and 12-hour horizon limit generalization to finer grids or longer forecasts. Track evidence covers two storms with different comparators and a center search guided by historical JMA position information. The reference centers come from ERA5 (§§S1.4–S1.5).
- **Analyst inference.** Detailed ablations and the three-seed check cover TyphoonTracking, with the seed check limited to two internal controls. Run-to-run variability for the broader baseline rankings remains unresolved (§§S2.1–S2.7, S2.14).
- **Analyst inference.** Deletion tests select a local contribution by magnitude and probe its effect under fixed routing. The weaker cloud-cover effects and mixed regional temperature results against norm-matched perturbations limit claims about how consistently the selected contribution helps (§§S2.8, S2.13).

## Open research questions

1. **Do prototype banks and operators transfer across regions or seasons?** Section 5 leaves transfer untested. Hold out a region or season and compare frozen reuse, bank-only refitting, and full retraining at matched target-data budgets.
2. **Do lead-specific prototype weights improve later forecasts?** ProtoCast shares weights across leads, while deletion effects vary by lead and variable (§§3.4, S2.13). Compare shared and lead-specific matching at similar capacity and report per-lead errors on both benchmarks.
3. **Does the track advantage persist across more storms?** The paper evaluates two selected storm windows against different baselines (§S1.5, §S3.3). Evaluate the same baselines across a held-out storm cohort and quantify uncertainty across storms.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and reported within-benchmark findings. The supplied text omits many cells from the main comparison and supplementary tables and has conflicting main-text table and figure numbering. Exact storm-track error values by lead, broader repeated-run uncertainty, and availability of ProtoCast code or checkpoints are unconfirmed. The abstract lists a project page.
