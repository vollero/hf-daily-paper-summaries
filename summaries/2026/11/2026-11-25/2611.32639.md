# ReFocus: Progress-Guided Adaptive Practice for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32639) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32639)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReFocus alternates full-task reinforcement learning with targeted practice at recurring bottlenecks, raising mean task success over StructRL from 96.2% to 97.8% on LIBERO-Long and from 45.8% to 51.3% on RoboCasa365 Composite-Seen (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon manipulation policies must continue from states produced by their own earlier actions, where deviations from demonstrations can accumulate. Binary terminal rewards give the same feedback to failures at different stages, and restarting the full task limits opportunities to practice later bottlenecks. The authors also identify a reward-design gap: uniform per-subtask credit gives more weight to stages split into more subtasks (§1, Appendix A).

## Approach and contributions

- **Stage-balanced progress rewards.** ReFocus gives each stage of a task DAG an equal share of the progress reward and divides that share among its subtasks. Each completion earns credit once, after prerequisites are credited. The same completion records support bottleneck diagnosis (§3.1).
- **Adaptive practice windows.** An LLM scheduler uses validation progress traces, rollout images, and practice history to select an entry stage, target stage, and interaction budget. Local RL combines restored-entry practice, normal-start practice through the target, and restored-entry rollouts that continue toward full-task completion. Verified milestones subdivide the target subtask’s reward (§3.2).
- **Alternating updates to a shared policy.** Both simulation phases use PPO to update the same actor and critic. Normal-start evaluations and practice costs inform subsequent windows. On hardware, the authors adapt RECAP with progress-augmented returns and upsampling of recorded bottleneck segments (§3.3, Algorithm 1, Appendix D.2).

Figure 12 shows that Local RL increased exposure to selected stages in the analyzed RoboCasa365 run. The authors interpret the success gains and ablations as evidence that progress rewards and targeted practice complement each other. ## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Mid-episode restoration is impractical on physical hardware, so the robot study uses recorded-rollout post-training. The scheduler operates between training rounds. Intervention during task execution is proposed as future work (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference.** Generalization remains uncertain because the main evaluation uses one base VLA policy, a seen-task RoboCasa365 split, and one physical robot on three tasks. Simulator progress and milestones depend on task-specific checks (§§4.1, 4.4, Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Matching PPO updates can leave fresh-interaction and replay costs unequal, as Appendix C.4 acknowledges. The RoboCasa365 seed check measures evaluation variability with fixed checkpoints, leaving training-seed robustness unclear (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The reward-feedback ablation holds the Local RL configuration fixed, but changing the Global RL reward can change subsequent window choices. The hardware study reports value visualizations for progress rewards, while its task-success table reports the two modifications together (§4.3, Table 4, Appendix D.3, Table 5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does adaptive window selection improve success at equal interaction cost?** The window-selection ablation matches PPO updates and acknowledges that simulator costs can differ (Appendix C.4). Compare fixed, rule-based, and LLM-selected windows under matched fresh-interaction and replay budgets.
2. **How sensitive are gains to the task decomposition?** Reward allocation is invariant to subdividing subtasks within a fixed stage, while alternative stage partitions remain unevaluated (§3.1). Train with several valid partitions of the same tasks and compare diagnoses, selected windows, and full-task success.
3. **Which hardware modification drives the success gains?** Table 5 evaluates progress-augmented returns and segment upsampling together. Run a factorial ablation on the same recorded rollouts and evaluate each variant on normal-start physical trials.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported method and benchmark comparisons. Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes RoboCasa365 variability across independent training runs, an interaction-cost-matched window-selection comparison, separate hardware success rates for the two RECAP modifications, and confirmed public availability of code, checkpoints, or recorded rollouts.
