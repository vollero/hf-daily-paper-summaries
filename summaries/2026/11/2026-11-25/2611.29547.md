# Align the Pair, Not the Sample: Representation Alignment for Faster and Better One-Step Generato

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29547) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29547)


## One-sentence takeaway

On class-conditional ImageNet, RePrime improved one-step generation and reached Priming’s quality with fewer training updates by aligning corrupted inputs to their own clean images, while all tested alignment targets for pure-noise inputs reduced recall.

## Problem and motivation

One-step generators produce images in a single network evaluation, but training from scratch still requires costly reconstruction and distribution matching. The authors ask where to apply a representation loss in Priming. Corrupted training images have known clean counterparts, but inference starts from pure noise, where there is no corresponding real image. The practical question is whether alignment learned during reconstruction carries over to generation and offsets the frozen teacher’s training cost (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Loss placement.** RePrime applies the existing REPA cosine loss to intermediate patch features during reconstruction. A lightweight projector aligns them to frozen DINOv2 features of the same clean image. The projector is discarded after priming, and optimal transport (OT) post-training uses the original ConvNeXt and ResNet-MAE feature spaces (§§3.1–3.4, Fig. 2).
- **Analysis and controls.** The authors show that when targets are conditionally independent of inputs within a class, a class-constant direction minimizes the population alignment loss. They test the role of instance correspondence by substituting a random same-class image or shuffling the original image’s target tokens, then use a ridge probe to study whether semantics learned during reconstruction carry over to noise inputs (§§3.3, 4.2–4.3, App. A).
- **Joint training.** RePrime-J interleaves aligned reconstruction and OT updates through shared weights. It can generate directly after joint training and supports optional OT refinement (§§3.4, 4.7).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are from class-conditional ImageNet 256×256. The evaluation protocol uses 50,000 unguided samples generated with one network evaluation (§4.1, App. C).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Generative-path alignment reduced coverage | Across 17 B-scale configurations with 2.5k OT updates, EMA recall fell from 0.452 to 0.149–0.252 and FDᵣ⁶ worsened from 14.71 to 14.92–19.62. Precision met or exceeded baseline in 16 configurations. The paired-path counterpart reached recall 0.469 and FDᵣ⁶ 12.89 under the same post-training protocol. | §4.2, Fig. 3, Table S3 |
| Image correspondence mattered | With 10 priming epochs and 5k OT updates, EMA FDᵣ⁶ was 22.10 without alignment, 21.47–21.62 with random same-class targets, 19.94 with shuffled tokens from the correct image, and 18.10 with intact same-image targets. | §4.2, Table S8 |
| Probe estimates of semantic routing tracked quality | Across 16 models after 2.5k OT updates, routing probe scores had Spearman ρ = −0.90 with online FDᵣ⁶. The feature gap between noise and corrupted inputs had ρ = 0.00. | §4.3, Fig. 5, App. F.1 |
| RePrime improved standard-budget scores | At 160 priming epochs plus 25k OT updates, Priming → RePrime FID was 1.373 → 1.303 for B and 1.174 → 1.155 for L. FDᵣ⁵ fell from 11.27 → 10.20 for B and 7.41 → 6.43 for L. Excluding DINOv2 from the metric still yielded improvements of 8.6% and 12.7%, respectively. | §4.4, Tables 1 and S4 |
| RePrime needed fewer priming epochs and OT updates | After 25k OT updates, 80-epoch RePrime-B matched 160-epoch Priming-B on FDᵣ⁵ (11.24 vs. 11.27). It also beat 120-epoch Priming-B on FID (1.404 vs. 1.415) at lower teacher-inclusive compute (102.3 vs. 105.0 EFLOPs). From 160-epoch initializations, RePrime-B surpassed Priming-B’s 25k-update FDᵣ⁶ of 9.51 after 6.25k OT updates. | §4.5, Fig. 7, Table S2 |
| Joint training produced a metric trade-off | At 200k joint updates, native RePrime-J-B improved FDᵣ⁵ over Joint-B (9.75 vs. 11.00) and had worse FID (2.47 vs. 2.06), using one sampling seed. After 25k refinement updates, RePrime-J-B reached FID 1.224 vs. 1.265 for refined Joint-B, while its own FDᵣ⁵ worsened to 10.60. | §4.7, Tables 3 and S6 |

The correspondence control supports the authors’ claim that the target’s image identity and spatial layout matter in this setting. The routing probe gives correlational evidence for their proposed mechanism. The weaker L-scale FID difference is within reported sampling variation, while the held-out feature metrics favor RePrime. The compute comparison establishes a FLOP saving at the reported operating point. End-to-end elapsed time remains unmeasured in the supplied text (§4.4, App. D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Evaluation is confined to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, the Priming architecture, and OT post-training. Most configurations were trained once. Targets that create their own instance correspondence remain open, and the frozen teacher adds compute (§5).
- **Analyst inference.** The class-prototype result concerns the alignment term in isolation. Its conditional-independence argument applies directly to random or pooled targets. OT-matched targets depend on the sample, and self- and EMA targets fall outside the proposition’s stated scope, so their recall losses are empirical findings (§3.3, App. A).
- **Analyst inference.** Teacher identity and overlap with the OT feature spaces vary together in the teacher ablation. The results favor DINOv2 among the tested encoders, while the general requirement for an outside-space teacher remains unverified (§4.6, Table 2).
- **Analyst inference.** The supplied results support treating FID 1.224 as a refined checkpoint result. The abstract associates the rounded value with the joint variant, while Table 3 and Fig. S9 show an additional OT refinement stage. The short placement sweep also uses a different ImageNet packing from the full-budget runs, limiting direct comparisons across those campaigns (App. B).

## Open research questions

1. **Can alignment on pure noise preserve coverage when the targets share an instance?** The tested targets do not establish this (§5). Compare alignment between two views of the same generation trajectory against unpaired targets and plain OT at matched compute, tracking recall and FDᵣ⁶.
2. **Does the placement result transfer beyond class-conditioned OT?** The paper uses one architecture and post-training objective (§5). Run a placement ablation on a text-conditioned one-step model with a different distribution-matching objective and compare fidelity and coverage at equal budgets.
3. **Where does coverage disappear under weak generative-path alignment?** The weakest setting loses output recall while the pooled interface diagnostics show no corresponding code collapse (App. F.1). Probe token-level diversity across later blocks and intervene at the decoder input to localize the loss.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported placement effect within the tested Priming setup. Confidence is lower for the proposed causal mechanism and transfer beyond that setup. The supplied text lacks end-to-end wall-clock comparisons, independent training replicates for most ablations, and confirmation that RePrime checkpoints are publicly available. It reports a code release with scripts for regenerating tables and figures, but artifact availability was not verified.
