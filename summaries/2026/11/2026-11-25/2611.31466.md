# AdProducer: Towards Agentic Advertising Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31466) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31466)


## One-sentence takeaway

On AdCreation-Bench, a trained agent that researches products and writes reference-image-grounded storyboards raised PG-Score over direct prompting from 17.3 to 51.3 with frozen MiniMax H3 and from 28.0 to 35.9 with frozen Veo 3.1 without retraining the agent (§6.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

A visually polished ad can still depict the wrong product, display incorrect specifications, or fail to show why a feature matters. The authors identify a gap in end-to-end video generation for real products: the system must find reliable product evidence and turn it into shots that visibly demonstrate the selling point (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Research-to-storyboard agent.** A Qwen3.5-9B agent uses web search, browsing, and image search to verify product details and select visual references. It specifies shot timing, action, camera movement, and image assignments, then passes the storyboard and selected images to a frozen video generator (§3.1, Fig. 3).
- **Training pipeline and joint reward.** The authors construct AdProducer-SFT and AdProducer-RL from teacher-generated research trajectories, verified product evidence, and rendered reference videos. They train the agent through supervised imitation followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization with equally weighted storyboard and rendered-video rewards (§§3.2–4).
- **Product-grounded benchmark.** AdCreation-Bench contains 305 briefs across eight domains with no product overlap with the training sets. PG-Score measures product grounding and creative execution. Ad-Score uses a judge outside the training loop to rate the finished ad holistically (§§4–5, Fig. 5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Trained agent outscored prompting workflows | With H3 on AdCreation-Bench, the trained 9B agent scored **51.3** PG-Score versus **34.7** for the best untrained agent, search-enabled Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. | §6.4, Table 5 |
| SFT accounted for most of the gain in the training ablation | With H3 on AdCreation-Bench, SFT raised PG-Score from **17.3 to 48.4**. Single-reward RL added **1.1–1.3 points** over SFT, while joint-reward RL reached **51.3**, a **2.9-point** gain over SFT. | §6.4, Table 6 |
| Human ratings favored AdProducer + H3 | In a blinded study with **15 participants**, **five methods**, and **eight briefs**, AdProducer + H3 ranked highest on all rated dimensions. | VideoScore2 improved with H3 | On AdCreation-Bench, direct prompting → AdProducer scores rose from **3.53 to 4.09** for text–video alignment, **4.24 to 4.32** for visual quality, and **3.96 to 4.11** for physical plausibility. | §6.3, Table 3 |

The same-renderer comparisons support a benefit from the full research-and-storyboarding workflow in this benchmark. The ablation shows a smaller incremental gain from joint-reward RL after SFT, measured with PG-Score, which also supplies the video reward. Ad-Score and human ratings provide corroborating evidence about perceived quality. Real-world attention, clicks, and purchases remain unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** The authors attribute the smaller Veo 3.1 gain to weaker adherence to reference images. They also report that AdProducer + H3’s opening hook scored slightly below Veo 3.1’s (§6.2). Their reward design explicitly accounts for noise from the frozen renderer (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference.** The direct-prompt comparison measures the combined effect of research, image references, and shot planning. The untrained workflow comparisons and reward ablation clarify parts of the pipeline, but the supplied results do not isolate text search, image selection, and storyboard structure within the trained agent (§§6.1, 6.4).
- **Analyst inference.** PG-Score uses the same judge and rubric as the video reward. Its aesthetic reference videos also come from MiniMax H3, creating a possible renderer-specific influence whose size is unknown. Ad-Score uses a different judge without the reference video (§§4, 5.2, Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference.** The human study covers a small set of briefs in a controlled viewing setting. It supports perceived ad quality within that sample. Audience response in a feed or campaign remains open (§§6.1, 6.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Which inputs drive the trained agent’s gains?** The reported comparisons do not isolate text evidence, selected images, and shot structure within the trained system. Run a factorial ablation that toggles each input while holding the renderer and briefs fixed.
2. **How much of the transfer gap comes from reference adherence?** The authors propose this explanation from the two renderer results. Render identical storyboards with and without matched images across several generators, repeat generations, and have blinded raters score identity and shot adherence.
3. **Do higher ratings predict viewer behavior?** Ad-Score and the human study measure judged appeal and stated intent. Run a randomized feed experiment with matched briefs and measure early retention and click-through.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the method and headline comparisons explicitly reported in the text. The supplied text gives captions for Tables 1–6 and selected figures from them, while full table cells, uncertainty across briefs or render runs, participant demographics, and format-fallback frequency are unavailable. The authors report releasing the code, models, and data, but the supplied text does not establish the contents of those releases.
