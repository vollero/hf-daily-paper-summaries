# MAGPIE: Agentic Multi-Reference Image Generation via Executable Scene Construction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32462) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32462)


## One-sentence takeaway

MAGPIE uses an agent to build and verify reference-linked Blender proxies before image synthesis, and the authors report that training generators to follow those proxies improves overall scores for both tested backbones on DreamOmni2Bench and SconeEval (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Multi-reference image generation must select the requested subjects, preserve their appearance, and arrange the right number of instances in the specified poses and relationships. The authors argue that direct generation leaves many composition errors visible only after synthesis, while text plans and bounding boxes can leave depth ordering, articulation, and physical contact unresolved. Prior executable scene methods mainly address text-driven generation, leaving instance-to-reference binding and transfer to reference-faithful synthesis less explored (§§1–2, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Reference-bound executable scenes.** A multimodal coding agent maps references and an instruction to an instance-level scene specification, then builds a simplified Blender scene using procedural components and shared anchors for interactions (§3.1, Fig. 2).
- **Geometry and visual refinement.** The harness measures visibility, camera-relative ordering, and designated contacts. A multimodal reviewer checks the render against the instruction and references, and the agent revises the program within a bounded budget (§§3.1, 9).
- **Proxy-conditioned training.** MAGPIE-Instruct-3K contains 3,187 examples pairing references and instructions with Blender programs, proxy renders, and target images. Flow-matching LoRA adaptation teaches the generator to take composition from the proxy render and appearance from the bound references (§§3.2–3.3, 7).

The benchmark results support the full pipeline in the tested settings. The same-data comparison shows a gain beyond ordinary fine-tuning, and the fixed-FLUX budget comparison shows that further refinement improves measured success. The individual effects of geometric checks and visual review remain unresolved. The human audit validates proxy structure, while final-image scores come from the benchmarks’ model judges (§6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported scope and cost.** Training excludes appearance-only edits without a meaningful geometric requirement. Iterative construction accounts for most of the added inference time, and abstract generation was the hardest group in the proxy audit (§§7.2, 10.1–10.3).
- **Analyst inference on training and inference.** Training proxies are constructed with access to target images and filtered for alignment. Inference proxies are built from references and instructions alone. The effect of this difference on generator robustness remains unmeasured in the supplied experiments (§§7.3, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference on attribution and evaluation.** The refinement ablation varies the full loop’s budget, leaving the value of each feedback channel unclear. The coding-agent comparison is qualitative, and the human audit covers DreamOmni2Bench proxies, leaving final-image rankings without a reported human cross-check (§§4.3, 6, 10.3, 11.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Which feedback channel earns its inference cost?** The reported budget study varies the combined loop. Compare geometry-only, visual-only, and combined refinement under matched time budgets, measuring proxy correctness and final-image success (§§3.1, 10.2).
2. **How much does target-assisted proxy construction matter for training?** The supplied experiments use target-assisted training proxies and target-free inference proxies. Train otherwise matched adapters on target-assisted and target-free proxies, controlling for example selection, then evaluate both with the same inference harness (§§7.2–7.3).
3. **Do human raters reproduce the final-image gains?** The human audit scores proxies, while model judges score final images. Run a blinded, paired human evaluation of MAGPIE, standard fine-tuning, and best-of-six outputs on subject identity, selection, and spatial relationships (§§6, 10.1, 10.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the method description and explicitly reported aggregate results. The retrieved text includes table captions and selected values in prose, but omits full numerical rows and figure contents. It does not establish public availability of the harness, dataset, or adapters, run-to-run variability, or uncertainty intervals for the reported gains. The main setting and usage statement also use different names for the construction agent, Qwen3.8-flash and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, leaving the exact identifier unclear (§§4.1, 6).
