# RoMo Hands: A Large Scale Richly Organized Text to Hand Motion Dataset

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31766) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31766)


## One-sentence takeaway

RoMo Hands pairs roughly 542,000 in-the-wild bimanual motion clips with five levels of captions and shows that, among four adapted generators, better text retrieval can coincide with substantially worse finger-motion smoothness.

## Problem and motivation

Text-conditioned hand synthesis needs data that captures fine finger articulation and two-hand coordination. The authors identify a gap between large body-motion corpora, which often treat hands as rigid end-effectors, and hand-focused corpora, which generally offer less footage or less detailed language supervision. Fine articulation matters for applications such as avatar animation and teleoperation, where jitter and finger collisions can be visible even when a gesture broadly matches its prompt. (§1)

## Approach and contributions

- **Dataset curation.** Extend RoMo’s web-video pipeline by recovering per-frame MANO poses with WiLoR, anchoring them to body-derived wrist trajectories, and filtering for hand visibility and finger articulation. (§3.1, Fig. 2)
- **Structured captions.** Use Qwen3.5-27B to produce five descriptions per clip, from a headline to a paragraph with finger and wrist details. Index clips under a pruned version of RoMo’s three-level action taxonomy. (§3.1)
- **Motion representation.** Introduce a 393-dimensional per-frame encoding with a wrist-midpoint root, inter-wrist yaw, joint positions and velocities, and 6D rotations. Use type-aware normalization and a fixed mean MANO shape. (§3.3, Appendix B)
- **Benchmark.** Retrain Kimodo M, MDM (DiP), MoMask, and MotionMillion on the shared representation, then compare learned text–motion scores with joint- and mesh-based physical metrics. (§§4.1–4.2)

| Human audit supports average caption fidelity | Across 370 ratings of 250 randomly sampled validation clips, mean scores were 4.16/5 for overall action match, 3.79/5 for hand-specific caption–motion faithfulness, and 3.53/5 for reconstruction quality. | §3.1, Table 11 |
| The representation improves top-1 retrieval in both ablated generators | With non-representation choices held fixed within each generator, MoMask R@1 was 0.547 with the 393-D encoding versus 0.395 for MANO-102 and 0.368 for CLUTCH-198. | Retrieval and physical metrics favor different models | On the RoMo Hands test protocol, MoMask had the highest generator R@1 point estimate at 54.00% versus Kimodo M’s 53.82%, with overlapping reported 95% intervals. Kimodo M had the lowest generator FID at 0.087 and intra-hand jerk of 22.9 m/s³ versus MoMask’s 265.2 m/s³ and the reference’s 14.3 m/s³. On the 256-clip mesh subset scored every fourth frame, Kimodo M had the lowest generator inter-hand penetration rate at 1.06% of scored frames. | Tables 3 and 9, Appendix C.2 |

The evidence supports evaluating retrieval and physical quality together for these configurations. The controlled ablation supports a representation effect on retrieval in two generators, with the FID benefit depending on the model. Table 14 measures retrieval on reference motions, leaving the best prompt granularity for generation open. The source-video audit tests the sensitivity of the reported semantic results to filtering the test set. Generalization after retraining on a strict source-video-disjoint partition remains untested in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations.** Occlusion and motion blur can still affect recovered poses. The human audit uses coarse ratings and provides no error measurement against independently captured 3D poses. The filters favor visible, active hands, and the dataset inherits the demographic and geographic composition of its source videos. (§5)
- **Authors’ limitations.** Semantic scores depend on a single RoMo-trained TMR evaluator. Baseline conditions differ because MDM uses a ground-truth motion prefix and a shorter horizon, MotionMillion uses a reduced decoder, and MoMask’s second stage was still improving. (§5, Appendix E.3)
- **Analyst inference.** Frame-strided mesh scoring can miss brief contacts, so MotionMillion’s reported zero self-penetration depth is limited to detections under that protocol. (§4.2, Appendix C.2)
- **Analyst inference.** The paper gives conflicting descriptions of high-frequency power. §4.1 describes velocity energy, while Appendix C.2 defines it from joint-position spectra. The supplied text leaves the implementation ambiguous.

## Open research questions

1. **Which caption detail works best for generation?** Table 14 isolates caption tiers on reference-motion retrieval, while the headline generator scores average across tiers. Generate from each tier for the same held-out clips and compare retrieval, physical metrics, and blinded judgments of prompt fidelity.
2. **Do the rankings transfer across datasets?** The source-video audit stays within RoMo Hands, and §5 lists cross-dataset transfer as future work. Evaluate the same generators on HandX or CLUTCH with matched skeleton conventions and a shared scoring protocol.
3. **Which representation channels drive the retrieval gain?** Appendix D reports a Kimodo M variant that removes velocity and heading together. Ablate those blocks independently and jointly, then measure semantic and physical effects in both Kimodo M and MoMask.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the main dataset and within-benchmark findings. Material gaps include independently measured 3D pose error, demographic and geographic breakdowns, exact curation thresholds and the full captioning prompt, cross-dataset generator results, and verified availability of the announced checkpoints and strict source-video-disjoint partition. The high-frequency power implementation also remains ambiguous.
