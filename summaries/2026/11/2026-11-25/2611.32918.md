# InstructReplay: Replaying Assistant Turns to Preserve General Behavior During Factual Fine-Tuning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32918) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32918)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across six model–domain settings, replaying the initial model’s own answers to general instructions reduced average forgetting on five benchmarks from 28.07 to 2.44 percentage points while yielding similar mean factual acquisition to standard fine-tuning (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation

Closed-book fine-tuning on factual question–answer pairs can improve answers about taught facts while degrading instruction following, reasoning, and coding. The authors find that these declines coincide with much shorter responses and that likelihood-scored MMLU changes little under standard fine-tuning. They identify an unresolved design question in prior replay work: how much retention depends on the context used for replay, who wrote the replayed answers, and whether training matches full distributions or individual tokens (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Assistant-turn replay.** The initial model answers 9,600 general instructions once using its native chat template. During factual fine-tuning, the student learns domain answers by cross-entropy and matches the frozen model’s full next-token distributions by forward KL at every saved assistant-response prefix. Each update uses equal numbers of domain and replay sequences, and inference uses the adapted model alone (§§4–5, Figure 1).
- **Diagnosis of forgetting.** The study contrasts generated-answer benchmarks with likelihood-scored MMLU and uses response lengths, constraint categories, and parser outcomes to characterize where performance drops (§3, Appendix J, Table 12).
- **Controlled replay comparisons.** The authors compare native-chat replay with unprompted beginning-of-sequence (BOS) replay and domain-answer KL, replace soft targets with sampled tokens on the same saved responses, and compare the model’s answers with dataset answers to the same prompts (§6.2, Table 4, Appendix H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Standard fine-tuning hurt generated answers most | Across six settings versus the initial model, the four generated-answer benchmarks fell by about 35 percentage points (pp) on average and likelihood-scored MMLU fell by 0.4 pp. IFEval’s mean per-setting median length fell from 190 to 20 words. | §3, Table 1 |
| Replay preserved general scores with similar factual acquisition | Across six settings, mean forgetting was 2.44 pp with InstructReplay versus 28.07 pp with Naive SFT. Mean acquisition on rephrased questions about taught facts was 15.49 versus 15.23 pp. InstructReplay had the highest acquisition-minus-forgetting point estimate against the seven adaptation baselines in every setting. | §§5.2, 6.1, Table 3 |
| Instruction context improved retention at a matched replay token budget | Mean IFEval decline versus the initial model was 2.6 pp with instruction replay and 14.2 pp with token-matched BOS replay, using the same forward-KL objective. Domain-answer KL lost 43.3 pp and constrained fewer positions. | §6.2, Table 4, Appendix H |
| Answer source mattered more than target form in the tested configurations | With identical prompts and token cross-entropy, mean forgetting was 3.1 pp for the model’s sampled answers versus 7.5 pp for dataset answers. With identical saved model answers, soft KL yielded 2.4 pp versus 3.1 pp for sampled-token cross-entropy at the shared coefficient. | §6.2, Table 4, Appendix H |
| Uncertainty depends on the interval construction | For the acquisition-minus-forgetting margin over the strongest baseline, corrected paired percentile intervals excluded zero in all three PMC settings and included zero in all three CommonCorpus settings. The more conservative simultaneous-band construction included zero in all six. | §6.3, Table 5, Appendix F |

The authors interpret these results as evidence that replay context and answer source matter more than soft versus sampled targets under the tested schedule. The token-matched BOS control supports the context comparison. The dataset-answer comparison also changes response length and formatting, so the contribution of each answer property remains unresolved. Response length tracks accuracy, and Gold replay’s MATH-500 responses matched the initial model’s median length while still losing accuracy. The evidence leaves the causal role of length open (§7, Appendix J, Table 24).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated evaluation limits.** Acquisition uses model judges without human calibration. Judge families overlap with those used to score RAFT’s training targets, invalid-vote rates vary by method, some questions cue parts of their answers, and publication dates cannot establish that related facts were absent from pretraining (Limitations, Appendix B.4).
- **Author-stated scope limits.** The study uses short runs on three similarly sized models in two domains, and settings share general benchmark questions. The benchmarks overlap in kind with the replay mixture. Safety behavior and long-context use remain untested, and likelihood-scored MMLU cannot detect generation failures (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference.** The small soft-versus-hard gap leaves equivalence unresolved. The variants share a coefficient, corrected score intervals include zero in every setting, and method-specific tuning remains open. The repeated runs reuse saved replay answers, leaving sensitivity to buffer resampling unmeasured (Appendices F–H, Table 18).

## Open research questions

1. **Does assistant-turn replay still help at larger scales or over longer adaptation?** The study covers short runs on similarly sized models. Repeat the standard fine-tuning and replay comparison across model sizes and training durations, tracking acquisition and forgetting at multiple checkpoints.
2. **Does replay protect abilities absent from its prompt mixture?** Safety behavior and long-context use were untested. Evaluate both with the original buffer and with targeted prompts substituted at a fixed replay token budget.
3. **How much of the answer-source effect comes from formatting or length?** The dataset-answer control changes both, and removing one formatting artifact failed to restore MMLU. Compare model and dataset answers after normalizing formatting and matching response-token budgets, with both likelihood-based and generated-answer evaluations.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the descriptive results within the reported settings. The supplied text includes endpoint scores, controlled comparisons, repeat runs, and uncertainty analyses. Material information unavailable includes human calibration of factual judgments, results for larger models and longer training, retention outside the tested skill families, the exact upstream CommonCorpus revision, and complete source and evaluation corpora. The paper reports supplementary verification materials and notes that full corpora were withheld because row-level redistribution permission was unresolved (Limitations, References, Appendix I).
