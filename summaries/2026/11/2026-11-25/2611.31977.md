# Distill to Loop: Turning Pretrained Diffusion Transformers into Looped Models with Adjustable Depth

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31977) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31977)


## One-sentence takeaway

Dooop converts pretrained diffusion transformers into shared-weight students with adjustable inference depth, and its two-loop SD3-medium student slightly exceeds the teacher’s four-metric average with about 37% of its parameters and 58% of its reported per-image FLOPs (§§5.1–5.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Denoising-step distillation reduces the number of sampling steps, while each remaining step still runs the full backbone. Looped transformers can reduce the work per step by reusing blocks, but the authors identify two gaps in prior looped diffusion models. They require training from scratch, and reaching baseline quality can require many loops or come at the cost of degraded quality beyond the training loop count (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Teacher-initialized loop distillation.** Dooop keeps selected input and output blocks and replaces the middle stack with a shared block group, initializing all student blocks from the teacher. Each loop’s target moves from the actual previous prediction toward the frozen teacher’s velocity. The final target is the teacher velocity itself (§4.1, Fig. 2, Eqs. 5–8).
- **Adjustable depth.** Random depth grids during training condition the shared blocks on continuous depth. At inference, the loop count sets how finely to traverse the same depth interval. Euler, Heun, and DPM++ can be used along depth independently of the denoising-time sampler (§4.1, §5.3, App. B.3).
- **Rollout training and loop guidance.** The training recipe can mix noisy data with states from a teacher–student sampler. At inference, loop guidance extrapolates away from an earlier loop’s prediction. Reading that prediction from the same forward pass requires one extra output-module evaluation (§§4.1–4.2, Eqs. 9–11).

The results support a quality–compute trade-off on the reported image benchmarks. The text-to-image aggregate averages four teacher-relative scores computed on the same GenEval images. A higher mean alone leaves individual metric outcomes and statistical uncertainty unresolved. The FLOP counts also leave wall-clock speedup unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** SD3-medium distillation still takes 100k training steps. The few-step result depends on a separately trained four-step teacher. Text-to-video transfer remains untested (App. E).
- **Analyst inference.** Rollout training is setting-dependent. The ratio ablation favors data-only training on ImageNet by gFID and a data–rollout mix on SD3-medium by GenEval and ImageReward (§5.5, Table 5).
- **Analyst inference.** The fixed depth interval makes different loop counts executable. The reported tests cover a finite range, leaving quality and convergence at arbitrary loop counts unresolved (§4.1, §5.2, Fig. 3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can conversion reach the same quality with less training compute?** The authors flag the long SD3-medium run. Compare the current objective with one that adds distribution matching, using matched training-compute budgets and the same evaluation protocol (App. E).
2. **Can step distillation be applied after loop distillation?** The four-step experiment starts from a separately trained CDM teacher. Apply step distillation directly to the multi-step looped student and compare against the reported CDM-derived student at matched inference cost (§5.4, App. E).
3. **Do the savings carry over to video generation?** The reported evaluations cover images. Convert a pretrained text-to-video backbone and measure temporal consistency, prompt alignment, and wall-clock cost across loop counts (App. E).

## Confidence and missing information

**Medium.** The method and headline comparisons are explicit. The supplied text includes captions and selected values for several tables whose full rows are absent, including Tables 1–3, 5, S1, and S6. This limits inspection of per-loop scores, baseline details, and training hyperparameters. Run-to-run uncertainty, wall-clock latency, peak memory, and released code or checkpoints are also unavailable in the supplied text.
