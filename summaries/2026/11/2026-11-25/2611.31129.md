# FATCAT: Fault Injection Attacks Targeting Chip-level Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31129) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31129)


## One-sentence takeaway

On two Jetson developer kits, the authors show that physical memory faults can weaken refusal in small language models and cause a SmolVLA-controlled arm to execute hazardous motions that pass its NaN check (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation

The authors identify a gap in model safety evaluations that assume faithful hardware execution. That assumption matters for edge devices because someone with physical access can disrupt inference even when the device restricts software access and protects model weights on disk. In robotics, a corrupted inference can change the motion a machine executes (§§1, 3.1, 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hardware safety evaluation.** FATCAT uses electromagnetic fault injection synchronized to device activity to study how transient and persistent memory corruption affects model behavior. The authors characterize the susceptibility of on-chip memory interfaces and off-chip DRAM (§§3–5, Appendix B).
- **Physical faults compared with software ablations.** The authors compare persistent weight corruption with software zero-ablation and measure both refusal behavior and task accuracy. They claim the first physical fault-injection demonstration that suppresses aligned-model refusal on production edge SoCs (§1, §5.2).
- **Executed robot behavior.** The study compares corrupted SmolVLA action plans with fault-free plans for identical observations and noise, then documents hazardous motions executed by a physical arm (§§6.2–6.6).

The experiments establish that faults can compromise refusal and physical control in the tested configurations. Table 5 measures whether each prompt produced an unsafe answer at least once within a retry budget. Per-attempt reliability and performance on proprietary models remain unestablished. The robot episodes demonstrate hazardous motion in the tested task, while injury incidence remains unmeasured.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported.** Fault outcomes are stochastic, and the margin between a behavioral change and a crash or incoherent output is narrow. Memory placement can change across cold boots, and proprietary-model target discovery was outside the evaluation (§§3.1, 3.3, 7.2).
- **Author-reported.** The transient software simulation uses a different runtime from the device experiments and models one corrupted block at a time. Many judged simulation results come from outcomes selected using keyword refusal scores, which undercount some Gemma refusals (Appendix A, Table 3).
- **Analyst inference.** The transient Orin–Thor comparison combines differences in hardware, serving runtime, weight format, and cooling, so the measured gap cannot be attributed to hardware alone. The robot evaluation covers one policy, arm, and task, and modifies how the rollout handles NaNs. Its campaign rates therefore have limited applicability to other deployments (§§4.2, 5.3, 6.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much protection does resident-weight integrity verification provide within an edge device’s latency budget?** Section 7.1 proposes verification without measuring its coverage or cost. Compare full and selective verification under controlled software corruption and report detection latency and inference overhead.
2. **Can an independent motion monitor catch corrupted plans that pass the NaN check?** Sections 6.2–6.6 report such plans without evaluating a separate monitor. Replay clean and corrupted action chunks in simulation to measure hazardous-plan detection and false stops.
3. **How stable are the unsafe-answer labels under expert review?** The two automated judges disagree most on heavily damaged outputs (§5.2.3). Have blinded human reviewers independently score a stratified sample of clean, unsafe, and ambiguous responses, then compare agreement and benchmark estimates.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the reported benchmark counts and the documented robot behavior within the tested settings. The supplied text does not establish per-attempt unsafe-answer rates for Table 5, human validation of the response labels, total trial counts for each Table 7 campaign, or measured effectiveness of the proposed defenses. The Artifacts section identifies video clips. It leaves the availability of analysis code, raw traces, and per-trial logs unspecified.
