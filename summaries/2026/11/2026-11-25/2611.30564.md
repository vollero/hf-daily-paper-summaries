# Seeing Is Locating: Spatial Awareness and Geographic Bias in Decision Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30564) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30564)


## One-sentence takeaway

On GeoDecisions, pretrained location priors improved decision-model accuracy on satellite and species images and reduced measured error clustering on iNat2018, while all six decision models gave lower subjective ratings in places with higher infant mortality (Tables 2, 4, and 5).

## Problem and motivation

Decision language models assign probabilities to candidate labels and are used to classify or rate inputs tied to real places. Useful geographic context can improve predictions, while geographically concentrated errors or socioeconomic patterns in subjective ratings can produce uneven outcomes. The authors identify a gap in prior evaluations, which studied geographic bias in generative language models and supervised geospatial classifiers but focused mainly on label presentation for decision models (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **GeoDecisions benchmark.** Adapts LocBench for geotagged image classification and an existing global location-rating benchmark for objective and subjective topics. It compares image and text input settings and introduces shuffle-corrected spatial bias scores that subtract a shuffled baseline while preserving the overall error rate (§§4.1–4.3).
2. **Comparative evaluation.** Tests six decision models zero-shot, with comparisons to their available base LLMs, supervised TorchSpatial classifiers, and previously reported generative-model bias scores (§§6.1–6.3).
3. **Location-prior fusion.** Multiplies image-only label probabilities by coordinate-conditioned scores from a pretrained Space2Vec-theory encoder and renormalizes over the candidates. The method assumes conditional independence of image and location given the label and requires no further training, although the encoder was trained on each target dataset’s training split (§5, Eq. 4, §6.1).

The results show that the usefulness of location depends on how it is supplied and what the task asks. They leave open whether the fusion gains transfer beyond dataset-specific priors and candidate sets. Negative B establishes an association with infant mortality under the tested prompts. The authors interpret similarities to base-model ratings as evidence that much of the bias is inherited. The comparison leaves the causal contribution of fine-tuning uncertain because the base LLMs and decision models use different scoring procedures (§§6.1–6.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Infant mortality is the sole socioeconomic proxy. The location prior is dataset-specific and was evaluated only for image classification. Image preprocessing, inference configurations, and rating-probability precision vary across models (Limitations).
- **Author-stated.** API cost restricted each closed-source image evaluation to 2,000 samples per dataset. The paper omits those models from the spatial bias comparison because the subsets are too sparse. Black-box contamination checks leave indirect pretraining exposure unresolved (§6.1, Table 4, Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference.** The iNat coordinates-only condition still receives an image-derived shortlist of 20 classes, which contains the true class for 92% of samples. Its accuracy therefore measures prediction from coordinates within that shortlist. Full-catalog, image-independent performance remains unknown (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference.** YFCC’s mostly US coverage limits the geographic scope of its clustering result. The use of one fixed permutation for the bias correction also leaves the sensitivity of small score differences to the shuffle unquantified (§4.1, Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Do fusion gains transfer to held-out regions?** The evaluated priors were trained on their respective benchmark training splits. Train the prior with entire regions held out, then compare image-only and fused accuracy and clustering within those regions.
2. **How much does the image-derived shortlist affect the iNat result?** The same visually selected candidate set is used across input conditions. Compare coordinates-only predictions under image-derived and image-independent candidate sets while matching candidate count and ground-truth inclusion.
3. **How stable is the social-bias finding across socioeconomic proxies and refusal patterns?** The benchmark uses one proxy, and GPT-6 Luna illustrates how narrow rating spread and refusals can shrink B. Recompute the evaluation with additional socioeconomic indicators and report rank correlation, rating spread, and answer rate separately.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported benchmark comparisons. Confidence is lower for causal explanations and transfer beyond the tested settings. The supplied text does not provide the models’ training corpora, comparable closed-source spatial clustering scores, uncertainty estimates for the main fusion comparisons, or results with alternative socioeconomic proxies or geographically held-out priors.
