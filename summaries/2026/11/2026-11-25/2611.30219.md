# RoomTokens: Masked Diffusion over Structured Tokens for Indoor Scene Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30219)


## One-sentence takeaway

On floor-plan-conditioned 3D-FRONT generation, masked diffusion over RoomTokens achieved the best aggregate spatial plausibility scores among the evaluated methods and recorded zero out-of-bounds placements, while furniture collisions still occurred (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

The task is to furnish a 3D room given its type and floor plan, choosing object categories, positions, sizes, and orientations that fit the architecture. The authors argue that object-vector methods encode object attributes but leave occupancy implicit, while top-down semantic maps make occupancy explicit but lose height and orientation cues and can merge touching objects of the same class. RoomTokens aim to preserve both spatial occupancy and the information needed to recover individual objects (§1, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **RoomTokens representation.** Each cell in a stack of horizontal slices stores a semantic class, a local instance color, and a body/front facing label where applicable. Greedy graph coloring assigns colors to separate adjacent same-class objects. The evaluated model uses three colors, an empirical choice for the dataset (§3.1, §4.1, Appendix E).
2. **MDiRT denoiser.** A shared U-Net with cross-slice attention predicts the three fields in one masked discrete diffusion process while clamping supplied architectural tokens. Within each reverse step, semantic updates guide attribute predictions, and validity projection enforces compatible field values (§§3.2–3.3).
3. **Evaluation and diagnostics.** The authors introduce Pair-Rel for comparing object-pair category and geometry distributions and opening clearance for measuring unobstructed door and window regions. They evaluate representation fidelity, component ablations, and final scenes assembled from retrieved 3D-FUTURE assets (§§3.4, 4.1–4.2, Appendices A and C).

The comparisons support improved aggregate layout plausibility on the tested split. The ablations support the combined representation and denoising design, and the placement intervention shows that yaw and scaling also affect boundary compliance (Table 10). The zero out-of-bounds rate is an observed result for the evaluated pipeline. The reported collisions and blocked doorways leave functional validity unresolved, and the source-layout decoding diagnostics do not measure yaw accuracy on generated scenes (Fig. 5, Table 9).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Fixed spatial resolution and height slices can miss fine geometry and narrow gaps. Training data are limited and uneven across categories and room types. Grouping, pose estimation, scaling, and mesh retrieval can introduce collisions during assembly (§5).
- **Analyst inference.** Aggregate gains can hide room-specific reversals. SemLayoutDiff had lower collision scores in dining rooms, and the living and dining diagnostic subsets overlap. Transfer beyond the three evaluated room types remains untested in the supplied results (Table 6).
- **Analyst inference.** Table 1 varies inference seeds with fixed checkpoints, while Tables 2–3 give ablation point estimates. The reported variation therefore leaves training-run sensitivity unresolved. The human study establishes preference within its tested sample and presentation protocol (§4.2, Appendix B).

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptive slices preserve narrow gaps while keeping generation costs practical?** The paper uses fixed slices and identifies resolution loss (§5). Compare fixed and adaptive grids at matched token budgets, measuring narrow-gap recovery, collisions, opening clearance, and runtime.
2. **How should the instance palette scale in denser rooms?** Three and four colors trade off Pair-Rel against collisions and clearance, and the combined cross-slice adjacency graph can be nonplanar (Table 3, Appendix E). Compare fixed and adaptive palettes on held-out layouts stratified by same-class adjacency, measuring instance merges and final scene quality.
3. **How much of the residual collision rate originates in layout generation versus mesh placement?** The placement ablation isolates yaw and scaling interventions but does not attribute each residual collision to a pipeline stage (Table 10). Track intersections across decoded boxes, posed meshes, and geometry-aware placement with the generated tokens held fixed.

## Confidence and missing information

**High** for the reported method and within-benchmark comparisons. The supplied text does not establish code or checkpoint availability, wall-clock training or inference costs, variability across independent training runs, or performance on external datasets. Appendix A describes what COL and NAV measure but leaves their exact computation and aggregation incompletely specified.
