# Can an Agent Trust Its Own Zoom? Cross-Magnification Provenance Verification for Whole-Slide Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29150) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29150)


## One-sentence takeaway

On PyraFault, ZoomTrace ranks correct spatial claims more effectively than Efficient LoFTR and recovers more substituted sources within the reference image, but its calibrated coverage drops sharply on CAMELYON16 and downstream verdict gains vary by agent and threshold (§§6.1, 6.3–6.4).

## Problem and motivation

A whole-slide image agent can correctly interpret a magnified crop and still attribute it to the wrong tissue location. Repeated structures make substitutions visually plausible, lower magnification can erase distinguishing detail, and the true source may lie outside the reference image. The authors identify a gap between finding a visual correspondence and establishing that it supports the claimed origin, especially when several locations look plausible or the source is absent (§§1–3, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **CMPV task.** Verify the claimed location of a magnified query in a trusted lower-magnification context, recover its source when it lies within that context, and abstain when evidence is insufficient (§3).
2. **PyraFault benchmark.** The authors report releasing 3.06 million query–claim pairs from 2,200 cases across five TCGA cohorts. Slide identities and pyramid coordinates establish source labels. Case-level splits, controlled substitutions, and label-preserving viewing perturbations support evaluation of distinct failure modes (Abstract, §4, Table 1).
3. **ZoomTrace verifier.** Resample the query to the context’s physical scale, retrieve windows with frozen UNI features, and refine the best geometric match with LoFTR and RANSAC. Estimate reliability from the winner’s margin over its strongest spatially disjoint retrieved rival. Only then compare the estimated source with the claim to verify, flag a mismatch, or abstain (§5, Fig. 2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

Claim-conditioned LoFTR scores 0.884 and 0.990. Among incorrect claims with in-context sources, recovery at IoU ≥ 0.5 is 97.26% versus 82.75% for Efficient LoFTR, counting missing estimates as failures. | §§6.1–6.2, Table 2 |
| The selective comparison depends on coverage. | At separately calibrated operating points, ZoomTrace reaches 0.985 conditional balanced accuracy at 34.39% coverage versus Efficient LoFTR’s 0.998 at 36.14%. In the full-coverage rank comparison, balanced accuracy is 0.980 versus 0.910. At the calibrated ZoomTrace operating point, coverage across absent-source fault types is 0.83–1.94%. | §6.1, Fig. 3, Tables 2–3 |
| Alignment and refinement contribute to localization and discrimination. | On the PyraFault test set, removing scale alignment yields 0.201 AUPRC and zero median source IoU. Removing geometric refinement yields 0.920 AUPRC and 0.590 median IoU. | §6.2, Table 4 |
| The reliability rule changes the trade-off across perturbations. | On identical PyraFault source estimates in a separate calibration run, winning-score confidence has higher AUPRC than the disjoint margin, 0.982 versus 0.969. Across four perturbation levels, the disjoint margin has a smaller coverage range, 0.023 versus 0.166. | §6.2, Table 5 |
| External evaluation exposes retrieval and calibration failures. | On 68,400 CAMELYON16 test pairs, Efficient LoFTR outperforms standard ZoomTrace. Increasing retrieved candidates from 10 to 50 raises ZoomTrace AUPRC from 0.676 to 0.792 and median localization time from 150 to 535 ms. With source-specific calibration, coverage is 1.3% and 1.1%, respectively. | §6.3, Table 7 |
| Middleware reduces corruption exposure, with uneven verdict gains. | At the threshold selected to limit clean withholding, exposure falls from 100% to 45.3–53.8% across three paired agent evaluations, and silent failures decrease for all three. Among episodes returning a verdict, Claude Code accuracy rises from 0.521 to 0.756, DeepSeek improves numerically from 0.755 to 0.829, and Codex remains at 0.987. | §6.4, Table 8 |

The controlled tests establish that independent localization can support claim discrimination and source correction. Continuous ranking, conditional accuracy, and coverage measure different outcomes. In particular, most absent-source cases produce abstentions, which the tested middleware passes through. The evidence leaves real-world fault prevalence, performance across additional slide sources, and routine clinical benefit unresolved (§§4, 6.1, 6.3–6.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ findings.** Retrieval degrades on CAMELYON16, and large clean-localization residuals widen the calibrated spatial tolerance and suppress coverage. Within TCGA, LGG also has a substantially larger residual tail than the other cohorts (§6.3).
- **Authors’ findings.** Adjacent drift can fall inside the spatial tolerance. Absent-source faults usually trigger abstention, and downstream verdict effects depend on the agent and operating point. PathAgent’s precomputed navigation features limit the observable effect of substituted pixels (§§6.1, 6.4).
- **Analyst inference.** Equal sampling of controlled fault conditions and fixed image geometry limit extrapolation of average precision and operational coverage to tool logs with unknown fault prevalence or different magnification pairs (§4).
- **Analyst inference.** ZoomTrace assigns maximum reliability to a positive match when the retrieved set contains no disjoint rival. A missed rival can therefore look like an unambiguous match. The reported external retrieval degradation makes this worth testing, but the supplied results do not quantify that edge case (§§5, 6.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can calibration recover useful coverage under source shift while meeting the target false-verification budget?** CAMELYON16 refitting gives low coverage, and transferred TCGA thresholds were not fitted to the target constraint. Compare residual-tail and confidence-aware calibration on held-out target cases, reporting false verification and coverage together (§6.3).
2. **Can candidate retrieval improve under source shift within the same matching budget?** Retrieving more candidates improves CAMELYON16 discrimination but increases latency. Compare alternative frozen encoders or spatially diversified retrieval at a fixed number of geometric matches, measuring source-window recall, AUPRC, and latency (§§5, 6.3).
3. **Should an agent retry a zoom when the verifier abstains?** The tested middleware passes abstentions through, leaving some corrupted crops available to the agent. Compare pass-through with a single re-extraction on paired episodes, measuring exposure, clean-view loss, verdict accuracy, and latency (§6.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**High** confidence in the task definition, method, and headline results explicitly reported in the supplied text. Full table bodies and supplementary protocols are unavailable. Missing details include complete baseline and VLM results, per-split counts, precise fault-mining and quality-control procedures, and full agent episode construction. The authors report releasing code and data, while the supplied text does not establish repository contents or independent reproducibility.
