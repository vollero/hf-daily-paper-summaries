# Fractal Neural Synthesis: Constant-Storage Procedural Weight Derivation via Complex Quadratic Boundary Dynamics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32393) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32393)


## One-sentence takeaway

Fractal Neural Synthesis regenerates a hidden layer from a 24-byte seed and, with an INT8 readout, beats quantized linear classifiers on four compact benchmarks while using 10.7×–129× less persistent model storage than dense FP32 MLPs, although seeded random Fourier features are smaller and more accurate on both digit benchmarks (§5.5, Table 5, Fig. 4).

## Problem and motivation

Dense networks store hidden weights explicitly, making persistent model storage grow with layer size and straining the Flash budget of small microcontrollers. Seeded random features already offer constant hidden-layer storage. The authors ask whether a similarly compact generator can also produce diverse hidden features that change predictably under small seed perturbations, making the seed amenable to local search (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Boundary-derived weights.** A seed comprising three Float64 values specifies a center and zoom near the Mandelbrot boundary. FNS samples a smooth escape-time field, standardizes and reshapes the samples into weights and biases, and materializes them in SRAM at boot. The paper compares their effective rank and local continuity with those of SHA-256, Fourier, and lattice-noise generators (§§3.1–3.3, 5.3).
- **Sampling geometry.** The paper compares a Cartesian grid, a coprime Rule-of-Thirds stencil, a Vogel spiral, and a three-scale Harmonic Tripod under a fixed seed. The stencils add no persistent model bytes under the paper’s accounting (§§3.3, 5.4).
- **Two training modes and a capacity bound.** Mode I searches one or more seeds without a stored readout and compares four gradient-free optimizers at equal evaluation budgets. Mode II fits a ridge readout on fixed nonlinear features and optionally quantizes it to INT8. For a fixed synthesis configuration, Proposition 1 bounds the hidden tensor’s Shannon entropy by the seed’s 192 bits and its conditional Kolmogorov complexity by those bits plus the decoder program length (§3.4, §4, §5.6).
- **Storage and timing evaluation.** The authors compare model bytes and accuracy against seeded, hashed, linear, and dense baselines, and measure boot synthesis and inference on a host CPU (§§5.5, 5.7).

The results support constant persistent storage for a single hidden-layer seed and a storage–accuracy trade-off relative to the tested dense MLPs. The perturbation result shows local stability under the tested seed step. On the same drift task, SHA-256 random shooting achieved higher mean tracking accuracy with 120 blind draws per drift step (§5.3). The dense-MLP comparisons do not establish accuracy equivalence, and the host timings leave microcontroller performance open.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** The seed bounds hidden-tensor information capacity, the Mode II readout grows with width and class count, and learned input scaling can add persistent bytes (§§3.4, 7). INT8 reduced FNS-Spiral accuracy from 91.00% to 58.50% on Two-Spirals under 10-fold CV (§5.2, Table 2). The authors also identify fixed-seed competition from RFF and the need for native MCU profiling (§7).
- **Analyst inference.** The fixed-seed CV results cover four compact datasets chosen for zero-byte input scaling, including stratified subsets of PenDigits and Satimage. They leave performance on full datasets and sensitivity to the Mode II seed unresolved. The multiscale comparison centers on one Misiurewicz anchor, so the joint rank and continuity result has limited support across other boundary regions or untested generators (§§5.3–5.5).
- **Analyst inference.** The reported Flash totals count model data. A device-level comparison would also need the synthesis code footprint and peak boot SRAM alongside latency and energy (§§3.4, 5.7).

## Open research questions

1. **Does tuning the boundary seed improve Mode II at the same model size?** The benchmark used a fixed seed, and seeded RFF led on both digit tasks. Use nested CV to tune FNS coordinates and select RFF seeds under matched search budgets, then compare held-out accuracy and model bytes (§§5.5, 7).
2. **Does local continuity improve search as the number of patches grows?** The drift result comes from a small 2D problem, and the optimizer comparison uses a single six-patch configuration. Sweep patch counts and compare accuracy against evaluations for local FNS search and seeded random shooting (§§5.3, 5.6).
3. **When does boot synthesis pay off on a microcontroller?** The timings come from warmed-up host NumPy. Compare native FNS and dense implementations on the same MCU, measuring prediction agreement, firmware Flash, peak SRAM, boot energy, and per-inference energy across duty cycles (§§5.7, 7).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose gives the main configurations and headline comparisons, while Tables 1–5 appear only as captions and the figures appear as captions with image placeholders. The full baseline rows, macro-F1 values, fold-level scores, exact split and subsample indices, and plotted values beyond those described in prose are unavailable here. The paper reports an archive for scripts, seeds, and telemetry. Native MCU measurements and complete firmware and peak SRAM accounting are also absent.
