# SpatialArena: Where Do Geometric Foundation Models Stand?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-11-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32816) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32816)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under SpatialArena’s shared evaluation protocol, feed-forward 3D/4D models lead video depth, while large, uncontrolled captures expose depth and tracking failures that public benchmark rankings only partly predict (§§4–5.1).

## Problem and motivation

Geometric foundation models estimate depth, camera pose, and scene structure from images, but published scores use different frame samples, resolutions, splits, and alignment procedures. The authors argue that these differences can obscure which models are better, especially when scoring predictions with unknown scale. Existing evaluations also seldom cover rolled views, wide depth ranges, dynamic scenes, or long recordings, and some test data overlap with model training data (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified benchmark.** SpatialArena evaluates 52 models and variants across five tasks on 23 public datasets plus the authors’ captures. It uses shared frame selections, separates RGB-only from camera- and depth-prior tracks, and excludes datasets flagged for training overlap from the aggregate ranking (§§1, 3.1, 3.5; Table 3).
- **New capture dataset.** Spatial Arena adds 30 uncontrolled indoor and outdoor scenes recorded with handheld LiDAR and three synchronized cameras. It provides camera poses, sparse metric LiDAR depth, and dense depth pseudo-labels rendered from LiDAR-supervised Gaussian splats (§3.2; Fig. 3).
- **Robust alignment and comparative studies.** Depth uses a deterministic Huber affine fit in the model’s native depth or disparity space on ground-truth-selected pixels. Trajectories use camera centers and orientations in a robust similarity fit, with all returned matched frames retained for scoring. The authors compare alternative alignments and analyze model failures across tasks (§3.3; §4; Appendices C, D.1–D.2).

The controlled alignment studies show that the scoring procedure can materially change errors even when predictions are fixed. The benchmark comparisons establish relative performance under the specified inputs, alignments, and coverage rules. The aggregate leaderboard also rewards task coverage by assigning zero to unsupported tasks. The authors attribute depth and tracking failures to factors including roll, depth range, and camera motion. The observational comparisons leave their individual causal effects unresolved (§5.1; §6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated.** Front and side views differ in content as well as roll, and only one walk contributes both types of VideoDepth clips. Release-date trends cover short periods, some models only partially cover Spatial Arena, and relatively few enter the auxiliary-input or large-range SLAM comparisons (§§5.1, 6).
- **Author-stated.** Sparse LiDAR leaves some regions where dense rendering may fail unchecked, including reflections and near-camera artifacts. MonoDepth selects well-reconstructed views and filters artifact frames. The proposed explanation for reconstruction errors was inferred from depth and pose analyses without measuring fused-point error by distance (§§3.2, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference.** Aligned scores leave raw metric-scale accuracy unresolved. Cross-dataset reconstruction gaps also combine differences in scene difficulty with differences in reconstruction processing and distance thresholds (§3.4; Appendix C).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the depth error comes from roll itself?** Front and side views vary in both orientation and content (§§5.1, 6). Rotate the same images by controlled angles, map predictions back to the original grid, and score on common valid pixels.
2. **How much do depth and pose errors each contribute to reconstruction failure?** The paper infers their roles without directly measuring fused-point error by distance (§5.1). Swap in reference poses and reference depths independently, then compare reconstruction error by distance band.
3. **Which long-sequence design choices reduce drift under a fixed memory budget?** The benchmark compares complete systems whose designs differ (§§5.1–5.3). Hold the backbone and recordings fixed, vary chunk overlap and loop closure, and measure drift, ATE, completeness, and peak memory.
