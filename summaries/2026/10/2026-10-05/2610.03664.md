# ProAR: Learning Prospective Reasoning with Autoregressive Video Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03664) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03664)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProAR combines predicted goal frames with training-only future-feature alignment to improve autoregressive video reasoning over a matched baseline, with stronger gains on selected visual tasks than on embodied simulation.

## Problem and motivation
Video-native reasoning requires generated trajectories to reach a goal through valid intermediate states. The authors argue that standard next-chunk training supervises local continuations without explicitly teaching eventual completion or future transitions, allowing errors to compound during irreversible autoregressive rollout (§§1, 3.1).

The identified gap is prospective guidance at two scales: an anticipated final outcome and an anticipated next stage, rather than visual plausibility or a longer prediction horizon alone (§§2–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Outcome guidance:** Jointly denoise each current chunk and an auxiliary goal frame, supervised by the training video’s final frame. An asymmetric attention mask lets current tokens use goal tokens while preventing current tokens from influencing the goal prediction. The goal is re-estimated from growing history during inference (§3.2).
- **Transition guidance:** Align noisy current-chunk features with stop-gradient clean next-chunk features using token-wise cosine loss and a lightweight predictor. Teacher forcing supplies both representations in one backbone pass; the predictor is removed at inference (§3.3).
- **Combined framework and evaluation:** Integrate both losses into a causal Wan2.2-TI2V-5B backbone and evaluate reasoning performance, training efficiency, component ablations, and simulated manipulation (§§3.3–4; Appendices A.1–A.5).

The claimed methodological contribution is this combination of sparse outcome supervision and dense transition supervision without an external feature encoder or additional backbone pass.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher aggregate visual-reasoning score | On 10 selected VBVR tasks, with 50 generated test examples each, mean continuous score rises from **0.663 Standard AR to 0.801 ProAR**; Wan2.2 SFT scores **0.736**. This is not binary success rate. | Table 1; §4.1; Appendix A.4 |
| Modest improvement in strict game completion | Across Maze, FlowFree, and Sokoban, each with 1,000 test samples, average SR rises from **50.97% to 52.97%**. ProAR’s task SRs are **82.70%, 30.20%, and 46.00%**, versus **79.50%, 29.40%, and 44.00%** for Standard AR. | Table 3; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Components improve aggregate performance together | VBVR mean scores: **0.663** baseline, **0.786** outcome only, **0.683** transition only, **0.801** combined. | Table 1; §4.4 |
| Faster progress by training-step count | ProAR reaches VBVR mean score **0.708 at 2,500 steps**, exceeding Standard AR’s reported **0.663 at 10,000 steps**. Transition alignment starts at step **7,500**, so this early gain precedes its activation. | §4.3; Fig. 4; §4.1 |
| Small embodied-simulation gain | Across 50 WorldArena manipulation tasks and 1,000 test trajectories, EWMScore rises from **60.43 to 60.95**, with **13 of 16** component metrics improving. | Additional compute remains measurable | Authors report **12%–14%** higher training cost; approximately **12%** higher inference time for four-frame chunks on NVIDIA B200, and approximately **8%** for eight-frame WorldArena chunks. | Appendix A.1 |

The controlled Standard AR comparisons support improvements under the reported training and evaluation settings. They do not establish universal task-level gains: ProAR trails Standard AR on two VBVR tasks, and transition-only guidance exceeds full ProAR’s Sokoban success rate (Tables 1, 3). The results also do not directly establish a planning mechanism or successful physical robot control. Comparisons against reported external baselines are less controlled than the matched AR comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Final-frame supervision can be weak when the final frame contains idle behavior or does not summarize a process-oriented objective. The authors propose adaptive milestones as future work (Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Experiments cover one backbone, a selected VBVR subset, three abstract games, and simulated manipulation. Transfer to other architectures, broader tasks, or physical deployment remains untested in the supplied text (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** No repeated-run variability, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported. Small differences, particularly the attention-mask and embodied aggregate gains, therefore have uncertain robustness.
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** VideoRLVR bidirectional results and WorldArena external baselines come from prior reports, whereas Standard AR is matched to ProAR (Table 3; Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency interpretation:** The convergence claim concerns optimization steps; each ProAR step costs more. The text provides relative overhead but no absolute end-to-end training times (Fig. 4; Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can informative milestones replace final-frame targets?** Appendix A.6 identifies weak supervision from idle or process-driven endings. Compare final-frame guidance with selected milestones on videos containing post-completion idle segments.
- **When do both components help strict completion?** Full ProAR improves aggregate performance, but transition-only performs better on Sokoban SR (Table 3). Run repeated component and loss-weight comparisons using success rate as the primary endpoint.
- **Does prospective guidance remain effective over longer rollouts?** The reported evaluations use specified video lengths and chunk sizes (Appendix A.1). Increase rollout length while separately measuring transition violations and final completion.
- **Do simulation-score gains improve executable behavior?** WorldArena evaluates generated videos, with no physical execution results supplied. Test generated trajectories through a fixed action-extraction and execution pipeline.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported comparisons; **medium** in robustness and broader generalization.

Material information unavailable includes repeated-run uncertainty, absolute compute timings, confirmed code/checkpoint availability, broader backbone evaluations, and physical robot execution results. Figure 4 labels the final AR score as **0.664**, while Table 1 and the accompanying prose report **0.663**; the digest uses the tabulated value.
