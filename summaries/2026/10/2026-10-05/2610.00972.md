# VeriHarness: Scaling Agentic Verification for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00972) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00972)


## One-sentence takeaway
VeriHarness improves long-horizon artifacts using the generator’s own model to investigate disagreements and challenge shared errors against workspace evidence, with reported average gains over a single rollout of 6.2 points for Gemini 3.5 Flash and 6.4 for Claude Opus 4.8 after revision (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Reports, spreadsheets, and multi-file deliverables require verification across sources, computations, and task requirements. The authors identify a gap in methods that vote over or judge candidate outputs without actively checking environmental evidence: agreement can preserve shared errors, while disagreement can expose correct alternatives (§§1–2).

The target setting uses the same model for generation and verification, with no reference answers or grading rubrics available during verification.

## Approach and contributions
- **Two complementary investigations:** A disagreement resolver checks competing claims; a consensus challenger tests shared values, interpretations, and omissions. Separate contexts produce evidence records for fresh-context adjudication (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Verification harness:** A file-based workspace, evidence tools, and reusable skills support selection and evidence-backed revision. Check priorities and stopping decisions are model judgments; the information-gain equations are qualitative guides, not computed optimization objectives (§3).
- **Skill evolution and released assets:** Development failures and per-item grader feedback improve skill libraries without changing model weights. The authors also report releasing verification code and rollout trajectories, artifacts, and grades; original benchmark inputs are excluded (§5; Appendices E, I).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement is an unreliable correctness signal | On Claude Opus 4.8 ten-rollout APEX pools, 34% of 917 consensus claims were incorrect; 74% of 775 disputed claims contained a correct candidate, while the most frequent value was correct in 47%. Correctness derives from rubric grades. | Figure 2; Appendix A |
| Selection beats evaluated primary baselines | Across five benchmarks, three-run unweighted native-score averages increased from 47.2 to 51.6 for Flash and 49.6 to 53.7 for Opus; VeriHarness selection led every primary baseline in each model–benchmark setting. | Table 2 |
| Revision adds gains beyond selection | Full-harness averages were 53.4 for Flash and 56.1 for Opus, with reported single-rollout gains of 6.2 and 6.4 points. Revision improved every model–benchmark setting. | Table 2 |
| Both investigations and skills contribute | With Opus and revision, average scores were 54.3 resolver-only, 52.6 challenger-only, 53.8 without skills, 55.3 in one context, and 56.1 for the full harness. | Table 4 |
| Evolved skills improve held-out performance | With Opus and full revision, empty-to-evolved libraries raised APEX scores from 38.6 to 49.6 and spreadsheet scores from 32.8 to 38.5; evolving human libraries raised 45.3 to 52.1 and 35.7 to 39.4, respectively. | Figure 4; Appendix I |
| Shared errors often survive through incomplete checking | Among unanimous APEX errors upheld by the Opus challenger, 70% involved checking a correct input or intermediate quantity while the graded error lay downstream. | Table 7 |
| Verification has measurable additional cost | Selection cost averaged $1.44 per task for Flash and $3.92 for Opus at list prices with cache-read pricing; these are verification costs, separate from generating the pool. | Appendix K |

These results support the combined harness in the evaluated workspace settings. They do not establish correctness guarantees or generalization beyond those settings. Benchmark scores measure delivered artifacts, so skill-evolution gains combine checking, adjudication, and editing effects. The averages mix different native metrics rather than representing one common success rate. Appendix I explicitly states that evolution point estimates do not establish statistical significance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Ten generated rollouts add cost, and multi-turn verification adds latency. The harness provides no calibrated scalar score per rollout; stronger-model verification is outside scope (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Skill generalization beyond the evaluated distributions, and improvement without development grader feedback, remain unestablished (Appendix I).
- **Evaluation scope:** Evolution covers only Opus on selected APEX and spreadsheet task categories. WorkBuddy’s security domain was excluded because API safety alerts prevented its evaluation (Appendices D, I).
- **Analyst inference:** LLM graders and model-mediated claim extraction/mapping can affect conclusions about correctness and shared errors; the paper uses these procedures in Appendices A, D, and J.
- **Analyst inference:** Equal rollout pools control generation cost, but the main comparison is not an equal verification-budget experiment. Cost analysis includes estimated baseline costs and cannot meter aggregation (Appendix K).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Released rollouts omit task descriptions, input workspaces, rubrics, and reference answers; reproducing environmental verification requires the original benchmarks (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
- **How should compute be divided between generation and verification?** Main experiments fix ten rollouts; Figure 6 varies only oracle selection. A next experiment could compare end-to-end quality under equal total budgets with different pool sizes and checking limits.
- **Do evolved skills transfer across domains and models?** Current evidence concerns within-distribution held-out subsets. Freeze learned libraries and evaluate them on another benchmark and verifier model.
- **Can challengers detect downstream shared errors more reliably?** Table 7 identifies intermediate-only checking as the dominant surviving-error category. Compare explicit dependency tracing with current challenger skills on annotated shared-error cases.
- **Can evidence records yield calibrated reward signals?** The authors leave rollout-level scoring unresolved. Test record-derived scores against independent grades for calibration and candidate ranking.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark findings; lower for broad generalization and causal explanations.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes exact evolution split sizes and task membership, statistical uncertainty for evolution gains, wall-clock latency measurements, complete evolved-library contents, and a total-budget-matched generation-versus-verification comparison.
