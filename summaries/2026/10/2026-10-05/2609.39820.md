# Learning from Runtime Feedback through Failure-Bank Self-Evolution for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39820) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39820)


## One-sentence takeaway

FailBank learns shield-free VLA policy updates from unexecuted safety corrections, improving reported mean success and cost on static-obstacle tasks, although statistical support and transfer vary across tasks and backbones (§§5.2–5.3; Appendix G.1).

## Problem and motivation

Runtime shields can reduce immediate contact while repeatedly redirecting an unchanged policy away from its goal. The authors call this persistent **policy–shield mismatch**: local safety corrections can obstruct task completion, particularly in constrained scenes (§§1–2).

The identified gap is how to turn runtime corrections into lasting policy improvement while screening unreliable supervision and preserving successful behavior.

## Approach and contributions

- **Observe-only supervision:** The policy controls collection rollouts; a fixed control-barrier-function (CBF) teacher uses privileged simulator geometry to propose—but never execute—translation corrections (§4.2).
- **Outcome-aware failure bank:** Admission filters corrections by validity, timing, and subsequent rollout evidence. Successful uncorrected actions become nominal-action “quiet anchors,” and admitted records accumulate across rounds (§§4.3–4.4).
- **Guarded adaptation:** Each round trains a fresh LoRA adapter from the original base using weighted first-action flow-matching supervision. Held-out full-chunk loss and action drift determine acceptance; deployment omits the teacher and privileged geometry (§4.5).

The contribution is this integrated feedback-to-update loop; CBFs, flow matching, privileged supervision, and LoRA are established components (Appendix A.5). Notably, the main π₀.₅ recipe assigns quiet anchors zero weight; positive anchor weighting is tested separately and used for π₀ (Appendix E.4).

| Quiet anchors reduce cost in a controlled ablation | π₀.₅ Level-1 onion, same bank and budget: anchor weight **0 → 0.2** preserves **89.0% SR**, while policy-induced CC falls **20.88 → 16.52**, paired **p=0.0039**. | Appendix E.7.3; Figure 5 |
| Corrections explain only part of one task’s gain | Level-2 apple controls: zero-residual SFT0 accounts for **11.3** of the full **26.2-point SR gain**; randomized-direction SHAM accounts for **12.0 points**. FailBank exceeds both when orders are averaged within state. | Appendix C.5 |
| Aggregate cost reductions lack consistent paired support | Ten-task policy-induced CC tests are null: **p=0.085** for π₀.₅ and **p=0.13** for π₀. π₀.₅ Level-2 cost significance disappears after excluding floor-task lemon. | Appendix G.1 |
| Dynamic transfer is not established | Static-trained adapter on two dynamic tasks: **6 improvements, 3 regressions, 27 ties**, pooled paired **p=0.5078**. | Appendix B.10 |

These results support descriptive aggregate gains and selected task-specific improvements. They do not establish uniformly safer behavior, consistent improvement across states, or general dynamic-scene transfer. Policy-induced CC is a diagnostic decomposition of official CC, not a safety guarantee (Appendix B.2). Aggregate BRS also provides no supported advantage over AEGIS on π₀ (Appendix B.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The implementation requires continuous flow-matching policies and focuses on static obstacles; moving hazards require an adapted teacher/interface (§6.3).
- **Counterfactual uncertainty:** Teacher proposals are unexecuted. Outcome-aware weights measure record utility, not the causal effect of executing a correction (§4.3).
- **Guard validity:** Acceptance thresholds are empirical guardrails, not calibrated safety bounds. Guard passage does not predict useful task improvement reliably; the adopted drift threshold changes no archived decision (Appendices E.7–E.7.3).
- **Evaluation qualifications:** π₀ excludes one rejected training order; several tasks have floor performance. Mean gains can coexist with inconsistent per-state improvements, and bootstrap intervals exclude training-order variance (Appendices B.3, G.1–G.1.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Privileged teacher geometry versus AEGIS’s visual grounding complicates attribution of the headline comparison solely to learning versus shielding. Privileged in-loop controls help, but their inactive projection counters limit direct verification (Appendix C.6).
- **Accumulation and metric selection:** Larger banks do not establish higher success. BRS was defined after inspecting several task outcomes and is unstable or undefined with small or zero base denominators (Appendices E.5, B.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Can feedback learning work without privileged geometry?** Collection currently uses simulator truth. A next experiment could train with perception-derived geometry under matched collection and evaluation conditions (§4.2).
2. **Which corrections causally improve behavior?** Attribution is partial and task-dependent. A next experiment could execute versus withhold selected proposals from matched states, then test resulting training targets across unseen tasks (Appendix C.5).
3. **Can acceptance guards predict deployment outcomes?** Current loss and drift checks are not validated predictors. A next experiment could prospectively test a fixed guard against independently held-out task success and cost (Appendix E.7.2).
4. **What enables dynamic-obstacle transfer?** Existing stress tests are inconclusive or null. A next experiment could compare snapshot and temporal-prediction teachers under matched training budgets and moving-hazard tasks (Appendix B.10).
