# EyeRobot 2.0: Active Gaze for Precise Manipulation without Wrist Cameras

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03710) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03710)


## One-sentence takeaway
EyeRobot 2.0 combines learned stereo fixation, foveated images, and fixation-relative actions to improve task-specific bimanual manipulation without wrist cameras, with its strongest advantage when wrist views are occluded (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Fine manipulation needs detailed, task-relevant visual feedback, but wrist cameras can be blocked by grasped objects and constrain gripper design. The authors ask whether a fixed stereo camera can provide comparable precision through coordinated gaze and local action representations. Their identified gap is fine-grained fixation from an already useful viewpoint, rather than head movement or viewpoint selection (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Hierarchical learned fixation:** A goal-conditioned gaze policy learns object tracking through PPO with geometric rewards derived from SAM3 masks and FoundationStereo depth. A separate target selector learns when to switch objects using a co-trained behavior-cloning policy’s action prediction error as reward; human gaze annotations are unnecessary (§3.1).
- **Fixation-centered perception and control:** Stereo views are computationally rotated through image warping from the static camera, then processed with multiscale centered crops. Gripper poses and action chunks use a rotating fixation-relative SE(3) frame (§§3.1–3.2; Appendix B).
- **Controlled manipulation evaluation:** The authors collect physical and simulated demonstrations and compare policies trained on identical demonstrations, including passive stereo, ego-plus-wrist, and component ablations (§§4–5).

The BC–RL loop builds on prior EyeRobot work; the claimed advances are coordinated stereo fixation, hierarchical target selection, and fixation-centric manipulation (§2.2).

These results support improved use of stereo observations within the tested task distributions. They do not establish general-purpose manipulation, unseen-object transfer, or learning from human videos. Distractor results also show that better target acquisition need not produce a comparable improvement in full-task completion.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Policies are task-specific, targets come from annotated object sets, head/neck movement is uncontrolled, and the method does not yet learn sub-object fixation (§6). Simulation covers simpler pick-and-place behavior and is not intended for sim-to-real transfer (§4).

**Analyst inferences from the protocol:**

- Real evaluations use one randomly selected trained seed; simulation headline results use the best seed. This limits interpretation of typical training reliability, although Table 7 supplies simulation seed statistics (§5).
- Test configurations remain within training regions or distributions, so broader spatial generalization is untested (§5).
- The monocular ablation removes both a stereo image and fixation depth, preventing separation of their contributions (§5.1).
- Action chunk durations differ between AVF and baselines; the authors justify this with simulation sweeps, but the comparison remains a system-level comparison (§5).
- Trial counts are inconsistent: Table 2 uses 64 tape trials, while general evaluation descriptions and figure captions specify 25. Figure graphics containing some headline results are not numerically reproduced in the supplied text.
- Training code and simulation code are promised, but their availability is not established (§4; Appendix B.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can fixation generalize across tasks and objects?** Task-specific prompts and policies leave this open (§6). Train a shared policy and evaluate held-out tasks and object identities.
- **Would sub-object targets improve precision?** Current supervision targets object centroids (§§3.1, 6). Compare centroid fixation with annotated insertion openings and tool contact points.
- **How much do binocular images and explicit depth each contribute?** The monocular ablation removes both (§5.1). Run separate image-only and depth-only removal ablations.
- **Does prediction-error reward reliably select useful gaze under distribution shift?** Reward optimizes demonstration action prediction, while evaluation remains within bounded distributions (§§3.1, 5). Compare it with task-success-based selection on shifted layouts and clutter.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method details and tabulated results are substantial, but aggregate reporting has ambiguities.

Material missing information includes complete numerical values behind Figs. 6–7, physical performance across training seeds, confidence intervals for physical comparisons, a reconciled tape trial count, and confirmation of code, model, or dataset releases.
