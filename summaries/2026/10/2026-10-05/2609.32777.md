# DEFINE: Exemplar-Guided Accent Control for Zero-Shot TTS

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32777) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32777)


## One-sentence takeaway
DEFINE separates voice and accent conditioning through audio exemplars, achieving accent-probe accuracy comparable to a TTS–conversion cascade on seen and out-of-domain English accents with higher speaker similarity, but weaker transfer on held-out accents (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Zero-shot TTS reference recordings carry both speaker identity and accent, making it difficult to request a familiar voice with a different accent. The authors attribute this coupling partly to training data where each speaker has one native accent. They identify a gap between explicit accent-label conditioning and post-synthesis conversion: neither naturally combines separate voice and accent exemplars with adjustable accent strength in one synthesizer (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Separate exemplar conditioning:** A voice prompt supplies speaker identity; a frozen XLS-R feature extractor and trained pooling/MLP encoder map separate accent clips into an accent vector. That vector shifts timestep and text embeddings in an F5-TTS backbone adapted with LoRA (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Prototype anchoring:** Learned prototypes for training accents supervise the exemplar encoder through cosine and contrastive objectives. Training mixes prototype and encoder conditioning, with additional accent-contrastive and adversarial speaker losses. Prototypes are discarded at inference, so accent labels are unnecessary then (§3.2).
- **Adjustable accent guidance:** An inference-time scalar weights the difference between accent-conditioned and accent-free predictions, trading accent strength against speaker similarity without retraining (§3.3; Table 2).

## Evidence and main results
ACC is accent-probe accuracy; SPK is ECAPA-TDNN cosine similarity; WER uses Whisper large-v3; UTMOS estimates speech quality.

DEFINE/cascade SPK: 0.624/0.600; WER: 0.067/0.056; UTMOS: 3.93/3.92. | Table 1; §4.1, p. 3 |
| Held-out transfer remains weaker | On 378 items at \(w=15\), DEFINE/cascade ACC was 0.127/0.175; SPK 0.654/0.603; WER 0.062/0.076; UTMOS 4.01/3.89. | Table 1, p. 3 |
| OOD guidance improves over accent-off synthesis | On 252 items at \(w=15\), DEFINE/cascade ACC was 0.087/0.083 and SPK 0.640/0.601. DEFINE’s paired ACC gain over accent-off was +0.040 [95% CI +0.004–+0.079]. | Table 1; §4.1, p. 3 |
| Guidance introduces a voice–accent trade-off | On seen accents, increasing \(w\) from 0 to 19 raised exemplar ACC from 0.065 to 0.214 while SPK fell from 0.694 to 0.604; ACC then fell to 0.212 at \(w=23\). | Table 2, pp. 3–4 |

The automated results support useful exemplar-based accent control with incomplete speaker preservation. The authors describe OOD performance as statistically indistinguishable from the cascade; this does not establish equivalence. Human preference supports the prototype-lookup conditioning pathway, but does not directly validate exemplar inference or unseen-accent perception.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ bottleneck analysis:** The encoder generalizes poorly across speakers, and exemplar conditioning trails label-based prototype lookup (§4.1).
- **Observed trade-off:** Stronger guidance reduces speaker similarity. A cross-attention variant attained higher probe accuracy only with severely degraded speech, illustrating that ACC alone can mislead (Table 2; §4.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited human validation:** Listening tests use prototype lookup and only three seen target accents; human evidence for the deployed exemplar configuration and unseen accents is missing (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement dependence:** The accent probe shares XLS-R features with the encoder. The authors offer countercomparisons, but an independent accent measurement would strengthen validity (§4, footnote 2).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Table 3 does not isolate the effects of synthetic augmentation or the speaker-adversarial loss. Their individual necessity remains unclear.
- **Analyst inference—efficiency evidence:** Sequence-evaluation counts are given, but measured latency, throughput, and memory comparisons are absent (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can encoder speaker overfitting be reduced?** Unseen-speaker prototype accuracy drops substantially. Next experiment: compare speaker-balanced training and stronger speaker-invariance objectives under fixed decoder and guidance settings.
- **Do listeners perceive exemplar-driven unseen accents?** Current human tests use seen-accent lookup. Next experiment: evaluate exemplar DEFINE against the cascade on held-out and OOD accents, rating accent, voice identity, and intelligibility separately.
- **How necessary is converted training data?** Seed-VC augmentation creates deliberately mismatched voice–accent pairs, but its contribution is not isolated. Next experiment: vary the converted-data proportion while holding training budget fixed.
- **Does accent control survive independent measurement?** Probe accuracy can rise on degraded audio. Next experiment: repeat comparisons with an independent accent classifier and blinded human judgments.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Methods and numerical comparisons are well specified, but perceptual evidence for exemplar-based generalization is limited.

Material missing information includes exact exemplar durations, complete training hyperparameters and loss weights, probe training/split details, full evaluation-grid construction, commercial prompt-generator identity, measured runtime, and confirmed repository/checkpoint contents.
