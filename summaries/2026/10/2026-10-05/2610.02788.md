# Skill2Real: Agentic Skill Learning for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robot Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02788) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02788)


## One-sentence takeaway
Skill2Real learns and freezes hierarchical robot-program memories in simulation, raising mean completion on four real UR5e tasks from 27.50% without learned skills to 78.75% with the full hierarchy under the same Astra Proposer and robot interface (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address transferring reusable manipulation knowledge from simulation to real robots without real-world task-policy training. Long-horizon tasks require reliable local actions, composition, and recovery despite differences in appearance, contact, dynamics, and embodiment.

Their identified gap is that representation-focused transfer methods may spend learning effort on task-irrelevant variation, while existing code-policy systems often compose supplied primitives rather than acquire validated local skills and task strategies from simulation experience (§1). This is the authors’ framing, rather than a directly tested comparison of learning-budget allocation.

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable knowledge as the transfer unit:** A foundation VLM generates programs over a shared robot API. Frozen skill memories transfer across domains; each backend supplies perception, calibration, and low-level execution (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Asymmetric Proposer–Verifier–Governor learning:** The Proposer acts from public observations; the Verifier translates privileged simulation outcomes into publicly grounded feedback; the Governor admits candidate memory updates using subsequent validation rollouts (§3.2; Algorithm 1).
- **Sequential skill hierarchy:** Stage I learns local “Cerebellum” skills. Stage II freezes them and learns “Brain” knowledge for task ordering, composition, and recovery. Skills contain guidance, code templates, and compact state descriptions; they condition program generation rather than add callable API operations (§3.3; Appendices C, E).

These are the paper’s proposed contributions; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority over all related systems.

The controlled Astra comparisons support a contribution from learned memories beyond the fixed test-time model and API. Cross-Proposer results support reuse across the tested models. However, the authors’ broader claim that learning gains are “orthogonal” to foundation-model capability is an interpretation, not proof of model independence.

Robosuite results demonstrate independent acquisition on another task suite, not transfer of LIBERO memories or transfer across physics engines. Appearance and lighting examples are qualitative; the dim-light recording ends before final equation acceptance (Appendices A.1, B.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limitations:** Performance depends on backend perception and control. Narrow-space manipulation exposes limitations of the current code interface, and VLM inference latency remains a target for improvement (§§5.3, 6).
- **Explicit experimental caveats:** Scores describe fixed libraries, without variability across independent training runs. Stage comparisons combine additional training with adding the Brain level; they do not isolate hierarchy at equal training budgets (Appendix B.1).
- **Ablation confounds:** Removing the Verifier removes both privileged diagnosis and its other contributions. Removing the Governor also removes admission-validation rollouts, so total rollout cost is unmatched (§4.2; Appendix B.6).
- **Comparison differences:** Published baselines use different models, seeds, retries, and development protocols; Zetta uses target-task development seeds, whereas Skill2Real excludes target-task skill construction (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Real-world generality remains bounded by four tasks on one UR5e configuration. Selected visual demonstrations do not quantify robustness across lighting, materials, or dynamics.
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Prompt templates are edited rather than historical runtime prompts; final skill files do not provide complete candidate-by-candidate learning transcripts (Appendices E.7, F).

## Open research questions
1. **Does hierarchy help at matched training cost?** Current stage comparisons confound structure and additional training. Compare hierarchical and single-memory systems with equal exploration, validation, and inference budgets.
2. **How much benefit comes specifically from privileged diagnosis?** The Verifier ablation does not isolate privilege. Compare privileged and public-only Verifiers while holding other roles and rollout budgets fixed.
3. **Can callable reactive policies close the position-perturbation gap?** Narrow-space failures motivate the authors’ proposed interface extension. Compare code-only execution with the same frozen memories plus an end-to-end policy tool.
4. **How stable is transfer across runs and real conditions?** Independent-training variability and condition-wise real robustness are unmeasured. Repeat training and evaluate frozen libraries across controlled lighting, placement, and dynamics changes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and within-protocol results; broader robustness and comparative superiority remain less certain.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes confidence intervals, independent-training variability, total validation and inference cost, latency measurements, numerical admission/stopping thresholds, complete backend implementations, condition-wise real-world success rates, and confirmation of a complete public artifact release.
