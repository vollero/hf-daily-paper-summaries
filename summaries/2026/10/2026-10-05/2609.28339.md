# Beyond Future Prediction: Denoising as Generative Adaptation for Robot Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28339) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28339)


## One-sentence takeaway
NowWAM jointly trains current-observation denoising and action prediction, achieving higher reported manipulation robustness and lower training cost than future-target co-training without generating future images at deployment (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Generative robot policies often adapt pretrained Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) through future-image prediction, even when deployment uses only current observations. The authors ask whether forecasting supplies the benefit or merely provides a generative adaptation signal. Resolving this could simplify training while preserving control performance (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Single-stream adaptation:** NowWAM corrupts the current observation latent along the pretrained denoising trajectory. That same stream supplies visual-language context to a separate action expert and receives visual velocity supervision alongside masked action regression. It removes the auxiliary future-image stream rather than replacing its target with another separate image (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Clean-observation deployment:** Training spans noise levels, but inference evaluates the clean endpoint in one forward pass, without a visual denoising rollout (§3.3).
- **Controlled evidence about adaptation:** Matched experiments separate pretrained initialization, temporal-target choice, and denoising supervision. The authors find similar past/future-target performance and poorer robustness from clean-only training or removing visual supervision (§4.4).
- **Transfer and efficiency:** Experiments cover manipulation robustness, few-shot cross-benchmark transfer, and both image-editing and pure text-to-image backbones (§§4.1–4.5).

## Evidence and main results

All values below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher robustness | Full LIBERO-Plus evaluation, 10,030 episodes: FLUX2-Klein NowWAM **87.7%** success versus future-target baseline **81.6%**, a **6.1 percentage-point** gain; Z-Image NowWAM **87.8%** | §§4.1, 4.3; Table 2 |
| Nominal performance preserved | Standard LIBERO: **98.4%** average success, reported as matching the future-target baseline | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Few-shot transfer improves | RoboCasa GR1: 24 public ID tasks, 100 demonstrations/task, 50 episodes/task; NowWAM **64.9%**, DIAL **58.3%**, Fast-WAM **56.0%** success; external recipes differ | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 |
| Pretraining supplies a strong prior | Matched non-EMA LIBERO-Plus subset, 1,923 episodes: initialization raises success **58.14% → 83.05%** with the Edit objective and **50.55% → 75.81%** without a visual objective | §4.4; Table 3a |
| Future targets are not uniquely advantageous | Same controlled protocol: future targets at t+16/t+32 yield **82.89%/83.70%**, versus **83.80%** for past target t−16 | §4.4; Table 3b |
| Both trajectory exposure and visual supervision help | Same controlled protocol: clean-only **77.48%**; uniform noise **83.46%**; low-noise-shifted sampling **84.97%**; noisy-current training without visual loss **78.73%** | §4.4; Table 3c |
| Lower joint-training cost | Same FLUX2-Klein/action expert, 2×H200, global batch 64, cached text/VAE encoding: visual tokens **784 → 392**, step time **2.85 → 1.63 s** (reported **1.8×** speedup), peak memory **78.9 → 74.3 GiB** | §4.5; Table 4 |

The matched ablations support the authors’ interpretation that denoising supervision on action-facing representations matters in these settings. They do not establish that future modeling is unnecessary for every control task. Main results use final EMA checkpoints and the full benchmark; ablations use non-EMA checkpoints and a subset, so their absolute scores should be interpreted separately (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Future modeling may still benefit explicit dynamics or long-horizon planning; the conclusion concerns the manipulation settings studied (§5).
- **Analyst inference—external validity:** The supplied evaluation covers simulation benchmarks, leaving physical-robot deployment unestablished (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison strength:** Equal RoboCasa target-demonstration counts do not imply identical training resources or recipes; the Table 1 caption explicitly notes differing external recipes.
- **Evidence gaps:** Tables 1–4 appear only as captions in the retrieved text; prose supplies selected results but omits full baseline and perturbation-category values. No training-seed variability, confidence intervals, or significance tests are supplied.
- **Efficiency boundary:** Timing excludes text/VAE encoding and establishes joint-training savings, not an end-to-end or deployment speedup (§4.5).

## Open research questions

- **When does future modeling help?** Long-horizon planning remains outside the demonstrated conclusion (§5). Compare matched current-denoising and future-target policies on tasks requiring explicit anticipation.
- **Does robustness transfer to physical robots?** Reported evaluation is simulated (§4.1). Test matched policies under physical camera, lighting, and object shifts.
- **How stable are the gains and noise-sampling choices?** Low-noise sampling improves the reported ablation, but its chosen shift parameter and variability are unavailable (§§3.2, 4.4). Sweep noise distributions across multiple training seeds and report uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and selected results are clearly described, but full table contents, statistical uncertainty, detailed training schedules, the selected noise-shift parameter, action-expert specifications, and code/checkpoint availability are unavailable in the supplied text.
