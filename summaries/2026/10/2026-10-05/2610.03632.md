# World Embedding Benchmark

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03632) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03632)


## One-sentence takeaway
World Embedding Benchmark separates physical information recoverability from video–language alignment, finding that physics-specific contrastive adaptation improves matching while worsening linear property regression in the evaluated models (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Video embeddings may recognize physical scenes without distinguishing the parameters governing them. The authors identify a gap between benchmarks for numerical dynamics and evaluations that jointly test video–language alignment and quantitative physical information recoverability under controlled conditions (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 8,000 simulated cases across 80 families in fluid mechanics, solid mechanics, dynamics, and optics/electromagnetism, with rendered videos, physical annotations, and template-generated descriptions (§2; Table 1).
- **Complementary evaluation:** Bidirectional text–video retrieval, frozen-embedding linear property regression, and binary video–description pair classification distinguish alignment, quantitative accessibility, and fine-grained discrimination (§2.2; Appendix C).
- **Adaptation study:** Continual contrastive training of LCO embeddings uses separate numerical parameter values within the same simulation families and within-family hard negatives, comparing physics-only training with mixtures containing general video–text data (§3.2).
- **Downstream demonstration:** Adapted embeddings retrieve reference videos for MiniMax-H3 generation, evaluated on the optics and thermal portions of PhyGenBench (§3.3; Table 3).

The contribution is the benchmark and empirical investigation; the simulation engines, embedding backbones, and video generator are existing components.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Pretrained embeddings struggle with fine-grained matching | Within-family binary accuracy spans 46.8–53.1% across pretrained embedding models; cross-family accuracy spans 66.1–86.2%. | Table 2, p. 6 |
| Adaptation improves retrieval | LCO-7B bidirectional Recall@10 rises from 4.9/1.6/5.7/1.9% to 17.9/9.4/21.0/9.4% for fluid/solid/dynamics/optics & EM, respectively; retrieval uses each full branch corpus. | Table 2; §2.2 |
| Classification gains favor coarse discrimination | LCO-7B within-family accuracy rises from 46.8% to 59.6%, while cross-family accuracy rises from 76.4% to 98.4%. | Table 2 |
| Adaptation worsens linear property recovery | Macro-average nRMSE across ten scalar tasks, reported as ×100, increases from 2.47 to 3.95 for LCO-3B and from 2.02 to 2.89 for LCO-7B; lower is better. | Table 2; Appendix C.2 |
| Video-only predictive representations support property regression | V-JEPA2 ViT-L reports macro-average nRMSE ×100 of 0.94 across the ten tasks, versus 1.41 for Omni-Embed-Nemotron-3B. | Table 2 |
| Direct prompting gives stronger within-family discrimination | Qwen3.5-27B reaches 61.3% within-family binary accuracy, compared with 53.1% for Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B. These are different models and evaluation interfaces. | §3.1; Table 2 |
| Retrieved references improve generation in the tested subsets | With MiniMax-H3 and top-1 references, average reported PhyGenBench score over optics and thermal is 0.60 without RAG, 0.59 with VideoCLIP-XL, 0.63 with base LCO-3B, and 0.66/0.65 with general-plus-physics/physics-only adaptation. | §3.3; Table 3 |

These results support a distinction between similarity-based alignment and linear accessibility of physical targets. They do **not** establish that adaptation destroys physical information: reduced linear probe performance could reflect changed accessibility. Likewise, the generation experiment supports utility in its tested setting, without establishing broad transfer or a causal relationship between benchmark retrieval scores and generation fidelity.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported constraint:** Retrieval continues improving without convergence within the allocated training resources; mixing general data slows training (§3.2; Figure 3).
- **Analyst inference—restricted transfer:** Adaptation changes numerical values but retains the benchmark’s families, engines, and caption templates. Generalization to unseen systems, renderers, or natural video remains untested (§3.2; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—representation shortcuts:** Videos expose physical fields through color maps and geometry; some sequences depict loading states or parameter scans rather than time. Probes could exploit these presentation cues. No rendering-invariance ablation is reported (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—physical fidelity varies by construction:** Several dynamics families use prescribed parameterized response relations, and the `reissnerShellModal` identifier uses a thin-plate generator. Simulator provenance alone does not establish equal fidelity across families (Appendix B.2.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Directly prompted MLLMs differ from embedding models in size and interface, so their advantage does not isolate the effect of embedding extraction (§3.1).
- **Reporting gaps:** Regression split details and the nRMSE normalization formula are absent. Figure 6’s results are not recoverable from the supplied text. Generation scoring details, sample counts, and uncertainty estimates are also unavailable.

## Open research questions

- **Can alignment improve without sacrificing property recovery?** Contrastive adaptation worsens nRMSE (§3.2). Compare contrastive-only training with joint contrastive and scalar-regression objectives using identical data and budgets.
- **Does adaptation transfer beyond familiar families and templates?** Training retains the evaluation families (§3.2). Hold out entire families and test paraphrased descriptions and alternative renderers.
- **Do probes recover physical behavior or visualization cues?** Field colors and parameter scans provide possible shortcuts (Appendix B.2). Re-evaluate after changing color maps, camera views, and normalization while preserving physical targets.
- **How robust is retrieval-assisted generation?** Evidence covers one generator and two PhyGenBench branches (§3.3). Compare learned retrieval with random and deliberately mismatched references across additional branches, reporting repeated-run uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The main task definitions and tabulated trends are clear, but the supplied text lacks regression splits and normalization, readable per-task probe results, detailed adaptation hyperparameters and checkpoint selection, generation scoring procedures and sample counts, uncertainty estimates, and independent release verification.
