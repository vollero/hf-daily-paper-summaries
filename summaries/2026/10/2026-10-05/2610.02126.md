# Local Support Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02126) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02126)


## One-sentence takeaway
Local Support Learning gates adapters using activation-density estimates, achieving near-base retention on tested Qwen language models while learning new tasks, without accessing original pretraining data (Secs. 4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Finetuning can overwrite pretrained and previously finetuned capabilities. The authors identify a geometric source of interference: a weight update changes the mapping of inputs beyond the distribution that produced it. They argue that replay and prior-data orthogonality constraints are impractical at pretraining scale, while existing weight constraints do not adequately prevent forgetting (Secs. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Local-support formulation:** Restrict each adapter’s effect to the current phase’s activation distribution. The single-matrix analysis connects support-estimation error to worst-case routing error under explicit density assumptions (Sec. 3; App. E).
- **Practical gating framework:** Train a standard adapter, then fit per-matrix positive GMMs on current-task activations. Apply the adapter when their likelihood exceeds that of a shared negative GMM fitted on generic reference text; optionally smooth decisions across tokens. Original pretraining data is unnecessary, but generic reference data is required (Sec. 4).
- **Efficient implementation and evaluation:** Random projection, diagonal covariances, and gate sharing reduce overhead. Experiments evaluate adaptation, retention, sequential learning, and alternative gates (Sec. 5; Apps. B, D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong adaptation with near-base retention | On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, LSL outperforms LoRA, OP-LoRA, and LwF on the reported adaptation–retention trade-off across chemistry, Igbo translation, and cybersecurity. Retention averages GSM8K, HumanEval, and IFEval; exact plotted coordinates are unavailable in the extracted text. | Sec. 5.1; Fig. 4 |
| GMM gating supplies most retention gains | In the component ablation, mean pretraining-task score rises from 55.3 for plain LoRA to 69.8 with GMM gating and 71.1 with smoothing: 76.6%, 96.6%, and 98.8% of base performance, respectively. OOD gate hit rate falls from about 16% to 5% with smoothing. | Secs. 4, 5.2; Fig. 11 |
| Retention persists across sequential phases | Translation → chemistry → cybersecurity preserves earlier gains with LSL. The authors report LoRA declines of 8% on translation after two subsequent phases and 5% on chemistry after one; the text does not clarify whether these are relative percentages or score-point changes. | Sec. 5.1; Fig. 5 |
| Locality ablations support the proposed mechanism | Union-of-Spheres achieves similar retention to GMM gating, whereas the MLP gate retains less. Stored gate memory is approximately 5.67 GB for Union-of-Spheres versus 13.8 MB for GMMs in this comparison. | Sec. 5.2; Fig. 9 |
| Modest measured overhead for one adaptation phase | On NVIDIA B200 with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: forward-pass latency is 497 ms for LSL versus 411 ms for LoRA; persistent adapter-plus-gate memory is 90.8 versus 77.0 MB; training on 1M tokens takes 13:45 versus 8:23, excluding one-time negative-GMM fitting. | Sec. 5.1; Fig. 8 |
| Tested robustness and scaling | Sweeps of learning rate, adapter rank, and batch size preserve strong retention; retention improves across tested Qwen sizes from 1.5B to 7B. Exact values are not supplied as numerical tables. | Figs. 6–7 |

These results support effective retention on the selected benchmarks and implicate update locality as a useful mechanism. They do not establish preservation of every pretrained capability, universal support recovery, or a full-network guarantee. Experiments average three seeds; multiphase evaluations cap each benchmark at 300 samples (App. D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Adapters cannot merge into base weights, so inference computation grows linearly with learning phases; parallelization can trade compute for latency (Sec. 6).
- **Author-stated:** Theory concerns one weight matrix, and performance depends on representations being adequately captured by GMMs. A misspecified density family can retain routing errors even with unlimited data (Sec. 6; App. E).
- **Author-stated:** Training keeps the new gate open, creating a train–inference mismatch; authors report no observed performance harm in their experiments (App. F).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence is confined to the Qwen family, selected language tasks, and one reported three-phase order, limiting conclusions about other architectures and long learning sequences (Sec. 5; App. D).
- **Analyst inference:** Temporal smoothing assumes task boundaries do not change at token frequency; rapidly alternating domains could challenge routing (Sec. 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark averages and the multiphase sample cap limit sensitivity to capability-specific or rare failures (Sec. 5.1; App. D).

## Open research questions
- **How does LSL behave over many phases?** Only a short sequence is evaluated, while inference cost accumulates. Next experiment: run a long sequence and measure retention, routing interference, memory, and latency.
- **What happens when task supports overlap?** Theory prioritizes the newest phase on overlapping activations, leaving conflicting-capability preservation unresolved. Next experiment: vary overlap between tasks with conflicting targets and measure both tasks’ performance (App. E.1).
- **How sensitive is routing to reference-data choice?** Generic reference text supplies the practical threshold, whose ideal construction remains unresolved. Next experiment: vary reference domains and sample sizes while holding adapters fixed (Sec. 4; App. E.3).
- **Does smoothing handle rapid domain switches?** Its motivation assumes temporal continuity. Next experiment: interleave domains within sequences and compare smoothed and unsmoothed gate errors (Sec. 4; App. G).
