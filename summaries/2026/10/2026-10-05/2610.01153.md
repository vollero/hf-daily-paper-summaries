# Looping Beyond Twice: A Scalable Recipe for Looped Mixture-of-Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01153) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01153)


## One-sentence takeaway
LOOM combines recurrent-state stabilization and loop-specific expert routing to improve MoE language models beyond two loops, with gains at approximately matched compute and larger gains when additional computation is allowed.

## Problem and motivation
Reusing Transformer blocks increases effective depth without duplicating attention or expert weights, but deeper MoE recurrence often degrades performance. The authors identify two obstacles: accumulating residual updates increase hidden-state variance, while shared routers repeatedly select similar experts. Their target gap is reliable deep recurrence that improves performance under compute-controlled comparisons, rather than merely spending more FLOPs (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnosis:** Activation-variance measurements and expert-load statistics associate deep-loop failure with unstable recurrent states and similar expert utilization across loops (Figs. 3–4). These are empirical observations, not a proof of exclusive causes.
- **Combined LOOM recipe:** Shared attention and expert weights use loop-specific routers. Residual scaling, normalized MoE outputs, and decreasing input-embedding re-injection stabilize recurrence. A **Looping Residual** maintains normalized exponential moving averages of attention outputs, with global and within-loop memories (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Training strategy and scaling evidence:** Segmented backpropagation supervises consecutive loop segments and detaches gradients between them while retaining forward state. The paper evaluates both compute-controlled looping and fixed-layout sweeps (§3.3; §4.2).

Residual scaling and input re-injection are explicitly attributed to prior work; the contribution is their MoE-specific combination with routing and residual-memory mechanisms, supported by scaling experiments (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Gains with exactly matched modeled layer cost | At 700M parameters and 10B tokens, four loops versus one reduce validation perplexity from **18.36 to 16.58** and raise seven-task average accuracy from **38.84% to 39.53%**; both have cost proxy **84**. Full backpropagation is used. | Table 2; §4.2.1 |
| Lowest perplexity in the near-iso-FLOP sweep | Five loops achieve **16.54** perplexity and **39.53%** average accuracy, versus the same one-loop baseline; cost proxy is **90 versus 84**, with top-k reduced from **26 to 4**. | Table 2 |
| Deeper recurrence works at smaller scales when compute grows | At approximately 100M parameters/5B tokens, twelve loops yield **19.26** perplexity and **39.11%** average accuracy versus **26.26/36.73%** for one loop. At approximately 350M/10B tokens, twelve loops yield **14.86/41.42%** versus **20.07/37.96%**. | Tables 1, 3 |
| Larger-scale performance peaks at nine loops | At approximately 1.7B parameters/60B tokens, nine loops achieve **7.77** perplexity and **47.7%** average accuracy versus **9.62/42.4%** for one loop. Twelve loops slightly worsen both aggregate metrics to **7.84/47.5%**. Compute is not matched. | Table 4 |
| Every tested component contributes at the ablation checkpoint | For the approximately 350M, nine-loop model at step **5,000**, full LOOM achieves **18.62** perplexity; removing one component produces values from **19.25 to 26.50**, with lower average accuracy in every variant. | Table 5 |
| Segmentation improves measured resource use | On the approximately 350M model at twelve loops, peak NPU memory is **12.6 versus 19.3 GiB**, measured with microbatch size **1**; reported training time is **12.6 versus 26.1 hours**, segmented versus full backpropagation. | Fig. 5; §3.3 |

Accuracy averages combine length-normalized accuracy for five tasks with raw accuracy for WinoGrande and SIQA (§4.1).

The evidence supports deeper recurrence in the tested architectures and a compute-controlled improvement over the non-looped baseline. It does not establish universal scaling, inference-speed benefits, or superiority over the named prior looped-MoE systems. Expert-load diversity supports the routing diagnosis, but does not directly demonstrate complementary computations for individual tokens.

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors report saturation with loop depth, slightly worse aggregate results beyond nine loops at the largest scale, and reduced microbatch sizes for the deepest full-backpropagation runs (§4.2; §4.3.2). There is no dedicated limitations section.

**Analyst inferences grounded in the experiments:**

- The near-iso-FLOP comparison changes both loop count and top-k; its simplified cost formula does not document a complete end-to-end training-cost accounting (§4.2.1).
- Evaluation covers one pretraining corpus, a fixed sequence length, and seven zero-shot commonsense tasks. Broader reasoning, long-context behavior, and other modalities are untested (§4.1).
- Component ablations use one model size, one loop count, and an intermediate checkpoint. Interactions and sensitivity to the fixed coefficients are not systematically evaluated (Table 5).
- Segmentation changes supervision and optimizer-step placement as well as gradient depth, so its gains cannot be attributed solely to reduced activation storage (Algorithm 1).
- No repeated-seed uncertainty or direct experimental comparison against the named prior looped-MoE methods is reported.

## Open research questions
- **Do gains persist under measured end-to-end compute matching?** The comparison uses an approximate layer-cost proxy. A next experiment could match total training FLOPs and report wall-clock throughput across loop/top-k configurations.
- **Does routing diversity produce distinct token-level computations?** Figure 4 measures aggregate expert loads. A next experiment could track per-token expert overlap and intervene on routing diversity while holding other components fixed.
- **Which part of segmentation improves optimization?** The method simultaneously changes gradient boundaries, intermediate losses, and optimizer steps. A factorial experiment could separate these choices at a matched update budget.
- **Does the recipe transfer to longer contexts and broader tasks?** Current evaluation uses the fixed pretraining sequence length and commonsense suite. A next experiment could test longer-context training and separately specified reasoning benchmarks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported settings and numerical findings; **medium** in the causal explanation and general scalability claims.

Material missing information includes repeated-seed variability, validation-set construction, exact evaluation prompts and harness details, full hardware configuration, complete compute accounting, coefficient-sensitivity experiments, and verifiable code or checkpoint release details.
