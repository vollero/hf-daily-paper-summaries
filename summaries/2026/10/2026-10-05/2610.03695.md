# Language Models that Play Chess and Explain Their Moves

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03695) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03695)


## One-sentence takeaway
Queen combines a chess-expert encoder with a language decoder and iterative search distillation to reach an estimated 2697 Lichess-anchored Elo while generating explanations whose conceptual accuracy remains a weakness (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Chess engines select strong moves but do not explain them in natural language; language models produce readable explanations but often recommend weak moves. The authors identify a gap in prior explanation models’ emphasis on isolated puzzles rather than full games, and argue that useful explanations require strong move selection and credible continuations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Expert-to-language architecture:** Queen connects the Lc0 BT5 encoder to SmolLM3-3B through gated cross-attention over multiple encoder layers. A four-stage QA curriculum teaches current-board understanding, legal interactions, future-state reconstruction, and future-position reasoning; encoder and decoder weights remain frozen during this adaptation (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Iterative explanation distillation:** After filtered GPT-5.6-Sol seeding, the model analyzes candidate successor positions. Stockfish guides recursive sampling and filtering, Qwen3.8-27B consolidates child explanations, and supervised fine-tuning distills these analyses back into the model over seven iterations (§3.3; Appendix B).
- **Three-part evaluation:** The authors assess recommendation accuracy through full games, continuation substantiation through principal variations (PVs), and prose coherence through an LM judge (§4).

The architecture builds on Flamingo, and the paper acknowledges concurrent expert-encoder/LM work; its claimed contribution is the combined adaptation and iterative explanation-training recipe (§§2–3).

These results support strong move selection and engine-validated continuations in the tested chess settings. They do not establish that prose faithfully explains the model’s internal decision process or improves human learning. Move disagreement with Leela rules out exact policy copying on the sampled positions, but does not establish explanation faithfulness. Cross-domain applicability remains an author proposal.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Conceptual hallucinations can propagate through consolidation; search distillation cannot teach verbal descriptions of motifs absent from its supervision (§4.3).
- **Author-stated:** Frontier-model costs constrain full-game evaluation, and encoder/curriculum ablations stop after seeding because iterative data generation is costly (§5; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The limited game count and two prescribed opening sequences constrain the precision and breadth of the Elo evidence; uncertainty intervals are unavailable (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** A single LM judge provides limited evidence of human-perceived explanation quality. The supplied text reports no human validation or educational outcomes (§4.3; Appendix C).
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** §3.3 describes oracle-move injection when all candidates are mistakes under the 10% threshold, whereas Appendix B uses a 5% cutoff for injection and 10% for child mistakes. Filtering descriptions also differ between retaining non-worse and strictly improved continuations (§3.3; Algorithm 1).
- **Scope:** Robotics and computer use are suggested applications, not evaluated domains (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Can conceptual hallucinations be reduced without weakening play?** The conceptual score remains low (§4.3). Next experiment: add verified motif supervision and measure coherence alongside Elo and NMR.
- **Does search consolidation improve performance beyond additional filtered SFT data?** The supplied ablations do not isolate this contribution through the complete training pipeline (§5). Next experiment: compare consolidation against matched-data, matched-compute filtered SFT.
- **Are explanations faithful and useful to learners?** Engine metrics and LM judging do not answer either question (§4). Next experiment: combine controlled intervention tests with a blinded human learning study.
- **Does the recipe transfer beyond chess?** Generality is proposed without cross-domain results (§6). Next experiment: apply the same adaptation and distillation stages to another stateful domain with an expert encoder and evaluator.
