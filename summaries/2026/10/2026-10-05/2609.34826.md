# WM-VLM: Probing Internal World Models for Interleaved Visual-Textual Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34826) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34826)


## One-sentence takeaway
WM-VLM improves synthetic mental-rotation accuracy over its supervised fine-tuned VLM backbone by generating intermediate visual embeddings, with corruption experiments supporting dependence on those embeddings within the tested settings (Tables 1, 4–5).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether a VLM can internally predict visual transformations and use those predictions for reasoning. They argue that text-only reasoning may lose spatial information, while external world models delegate prediction elsewhere and planning benchmarks confound state prediction with action selection. Their proposed diagnostic tasks provide verifiable intermediate states to examine prediction quality and downstream reasoning separately (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Internal latent world model:** WM-VLM extends Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with a shallow Mixture-of-Transformers generation branch. Textual mental actions alternate with predicted visual embeddings; subsequent reasoning consumes those embeddings. The default generation branch has four layers and shares global attention with the understanding branch (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Two-stage training:** First, rectified-flow training teaches the generation branch to predict intermediate embeddings while the backbone is frozen. Second, cross-entropy training updates the language decoder and generation branch using generated states; the vision encoder stays frozen (§3.4; §4.1.2).
- **Controlled diagnostic datasets:** Programmatically constructed Tetris-2D and Tetris-3D supply rotation questions, action/state traces, and verifiable answers, with held-out shapes or counts for generalization tests (§3.1; §4.1.1; Appendix B).
- **Dependence and design analyses:** Retrieval, state corruption, training curriculum, token count, and branch-depth experiments investigate whether generated states contribute to answers and whether better reconstruction translates into better reasoning (§4.2; Appendix C).

These are the paper’s proposed contributions; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority over all prior work.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large mental-rotation gains over backbone SFT | Answer accuracy (%), WM-VLM vs Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct SFT: 2D-ID **87.50 vs 48.25**; 2D-OOD **75.00 vs 46.20**; 3D-SC-ID **91.00 vs 71.75**; 3D-C-ID **66.25 vs 44.50**; 3D-OOD **71.80 vs 55.50**. All are dataset-fine-tuned comparisons. | Table 1 |
| Corrupting states sharply reduces accuracy | On 2D-ID, accuracy falls from **87.50%** to **14.00%** with zero embeddings and **23.50%** with randomized embeddings. On 3D-OOD, **71.80%** falls to **14.40%** and **20.40%**, respectively. | Tables 4–5 |
| Retrieval quality is positively associated with correctness, but imperfectly | Across the five Tetris splits, retrieval-correctness/answer-correctness φ coefficients range from **0.104 to 0.399**. On 3D-OOD, top-1 embedding retrieval is **13.40%**, while answer accuracy is **71.80%**. | Table 2 |
| Joint training fails in the tested configuration | Single-stage training with flow/CE weights **0.1/0.9** remains near chance; the staged model and backbone SFT exceed **40% accuracy** within comparable training steps. | §4.2; Figure 6 |
| Reconstruction quality does not reliably predict answer quality | Fewer visual tokens improve reconstruction metrics, but ID answer accuracy increases with token count and 2D-OOD accuracy peaks at **four tokens**. Increased branch depth also does not consistently improve answer accuracy. | §4.2; Figure 4; Appendix C, Figure 10 |
| Navigation gains are modest and do not lead the comparison | VSP-Nav accuracy after ThinkMorph-SN training: WM-VLM **50.00%**, backbone SFT **43.17%**, LatentUM **52.00%**. ThinkMorph/BAGEL reaches **82.50%** with additional proprietary interleaved training data. | Table 7 |
| Stronger text-reasoning models provide an important qualification | Unfine-tuned Qwen3.8-27B, question-only with think mode, scores **93.50%/85.40%** on 2D-ID/OOD, exceeding WM-VLM’s **87.50%/75.00%**. These differ in model size and training setting. | Tables 1, 6 |

The results support the usefulness of this architecture and training recipe on the supplied rotation tasks. Corruption establishes sensitivity to the latent channel, while retrieval provides associative evidence about its content. **Analyst inference:** neither alone proves that the model performs faithful geometric simulation; severe corruption also changes the input distribution. The evidence does not establish that visual intermediates are universally necessary, that WM-VLM leads across model scales, or that its gains transfer to embodied agents.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated future scope:** Embodied action/observation trajectories and world-model pretraining remain future work (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—narrow generalization:** Main gains concern synthetic rotations using one WM-VLM backbone. Navigation results are weaker, limiting claims about general planning (Tables 1, 7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Equal training steps for backbone SFT do not establish equal compute; WM-VLM adds parameters. ThinkMorph’s Tetris-3D result reflects failure to emit images and answers, rather than a successful reasoning comparison (§4.1.2; Tables 1, 8).
- **Analyst inference—task necessity:** Appendix B.3 reports coordinate-based textual solutions. Thus, the authors’ claim that intermediate images are essential should not be read as proof that every solution requires visual generation.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §4.1.1 describes 3D-OOD shapes or counts, whereas Algorithm 1 explicitly constructs OOD from held-out shapes. The pixel-medium discussion also contains incorrect table cross-references.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text gives no seed variability or confidence intervals, numerical Euler-step setting, complete per-dataset training schedules, or confirmed code/data/checkpoint release.

## Open research questions
- **Do the latents encode the correct transformation?** Corruption and retrieval do not fully resolve semantic fidelity. Next experiment: substitute plausible states from incorrect rotations and test whether answers follow the substituted geometry.
- **Does the method generalize across backbones?** WM-VLM is instantiated with one backbone, while stronger text models can solve some tasks. Next experiment: apply the same recipe across backbones with matched parameter and compute budgets.
- **Can staged training improve longer-horizon planning?** Navigation performance is mixed, and embodied training is deferred. Next experiment: vary trajectory length on action/observation datasets and measure state error and task success.
- **What latent quality matters for reasoning?** Reconstruction improvements do not consistently improve accuracy. Next experiment: compare matched reconstruction-quality models using rotation-sensitive state metrics and controlled latent interventions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Tables directly support the reported task gains and corruption effects; broader mechanistic and transfer claims remain uncertain. Material missing information includes statistical variability, exact sampling steps and training durations, complete baseline compute budgets, detailed OOD count construction, public asset availability, and numerical values for several figure-only ablations.
