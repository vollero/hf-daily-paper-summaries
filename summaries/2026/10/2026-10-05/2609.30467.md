# Where Does Retrieval-Based Open-Ended Evaluation Fail? Automatic Taxonomy Induction from Long-Form Medical Answer Factuality Verification

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30467) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30467)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors’ retrieval and verifier error taxonomies expose persistent evidence-quality and reasoning failures in medical factuality checking, although the narrow evaluation does not establish that these failures are fundamental to every retrieve-then-verify system.

## Problem and motivation
Retrieval-based factuality evaluation decomposes generated answers into claims, retrieves evidence, and assigns verdicts. Aggregate precision, recall, and F1 conceal whether failures arise from unsuitable evidence or faulty verification reasoning.

The authors identify a diagnostic gap: open-ended medical answers lack reference answers and annotated gold passages, so diagnostics requiring those resources cannot directly apply. Their case study examines clinician-identified errors in mental-health and prenatal-care answers (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Stage-specific taxonomies:** Five evidence-quality dimensions—entity match, clinical scope, trustworthiness, polarity, and chunking—and six verifier steps—selection, interpretation, inference, calibration, consistency, and grounding (§6; Table 8).
- **Human-guided automatic induction:** Clinician-informed seed codebooks guide LLM error-pattern extraction, followed by manual synthesis and human validation. The authors report saturation after six iterations. This avoids requiring gold evidence or reference answers, but the case study still uses clinician factuality labels (§§3.4, 6).
- **Comparative diagnosis:** Experiments compare four retrievers, six verifiers, static medical evidence, and web-search alternatives. The contribution is error analysis and an intermediate evaluation protocol, rather than a new fact-verification system (§§2, 5–7). The abstract states that code and data are released.

These results establish persistent failures in the tested configurations and show that intermediate judgments can rank systems differently from final verdict metrics. They do not establish a universal impossibility result. The authors’ description of Mistral’s higher recall as “lucky” is an interpretation of its poorer reasoning ratings, rather than a separately demonstrated causal mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Analysis centers on one open-ended dataset and the MedRAG-derived corpus. Missing passage dates prevent temporal-quality evaluation, and API budgets restrict automatic tagging to MedExpert-gold (Limitations).
- **Author-stated metric limits:** Without gold passages or reference answers, standard evidence precision/recall and reference-answer diagnostics are unavailable (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—selection and adjudication:** MedExpert-gold contains originally error-flagged sentences, many subsequently relabeled correct. Clinicians saw verifier votes and reasoning during adjudication, creating a possible anchoring risk. Appendix A.2 acknowledges that subset precision/F1 do not generalize; recall transfer is asserted rather than independently validated.
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic validity:** The small human-validation samples and unspecified agreement statistic limit confidence in judge-derived percentages. Visible reasoning traces also do not establish the model’s internal causal reasoning.
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** The abstract mentions three closed-ended datasets, while §3.1 lists four. §5.1’s prose conflicts with Table 5’s label percentages, and the supplied verifier-codebook table stops at its header.

## Open research questions
- **Does the taxonomy transfer across specialties?** The main open-ended evidence covers mental health and prenatal care. Apply the frozen codebooks to independently adjudicated answers from additional specialties.
- **Can scope- and polarity-aware retrieval improve error recall?** These remain prominent retrieval weaknesses (§6.2). Compare a taxonomy-guided reranker with Qwen3 using the same corpus, top-10 budget, and verifier.
- **How stable are automatic diagnostic labels?** Human validation is limited (§6). Compare multiple judges against a larger blinded clinician sample and report per-code agreement.
- **How much does adjudication affect rankings?** Clinicians saw model outputs during gold-set construction (Appendix A.2). Repeat adjudication with and without those outputs and compare labels and system rankings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied tables support the central performance comparisons, but broader structural conclusions remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes the complete verifier codebook and per-step rates, judge prompts and detailed induction procedure, agreement-statistic definition, uncertainty intervals, qualitative sample-selection details, full-data ablations, and corpus-overlap analyses referenced through empty links. The stated code/data release was not inspected.
