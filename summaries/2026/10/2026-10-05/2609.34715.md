# PDE-JEPA: Predictive Representation Learning of Latent Dynamics Modeling for Parametric PDEs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34715) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34715)


## One-sentence takeaway
PDE-JEPA combines predictive representation pretraining, latent trajectory alignment, and parameter-structured dynamics to achieve the lowest reported rollout error on eight of nine in-distribution PDE benchmarks and all five evaluated OOD benchmarks (Tables 1 and 4).

## Problem and motivation
Parametric PDE forecasting requires predicting physical evolution across changing coefficients, forcing, and boundary conditions. The authors identify a gap between learning informative representations and learning representations that support accurate recursive forecasting: JEPA improves frozen-state and parameter probes, yet vanilla JEPA has worse ID rollout performance than a reconstruction baseline on Wave-2D and Vorticity (§1, Figure 1).

The proposed goal is therefore to preserve physical information while adapting latent geometry and parameter responses for forecasting under unseen governing conditions.

## Approach and contributions
- **Predictive representation study:** The authors investigate masked latent prediction for parametric PDEs and distinguish probe informativeness from rollout accuracy. Their claim of being the first systematic study is an author claim, not independently established here (§1).
- **Physics-Aligned Latent Geometry (PAG):** A residual projector aligns temporal directional similarities in latent and physical trajectories. An anchor loss preserves proximity to pretrained features, while an auxiliary conditional predictor encourages predictability. The encoder stays frozen (§4.2).
- **Physics-Structured Latent Predictor (PSP):** The latent vector field separates shared evolution from state-dependent responses weighted by known governing parameters, with PDE-specific formulations. RK4 propagates the latent state (§4.3; Appendix C.1.1–C.1.2).
- **Staged physical reconstruction:** A decoder is trained on predicted latent rollouts with the preceding components frozen. Downstream encoding is frame-wise, and forecasting starts from the initial physical state (§4.1; Appendix B.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Strong reported OOD performance | Lowest relative L2 error across five benchmark families. Wave-2D: 0.157 versus LNS’s 0.610; Vorticity: 0.288 versus Zebra’s 0.320. | Table 4; §5.3 |
| PAG improves rollout | Vorticity relative L2 error falls from 0.086 to 0.040 ID and from 0.491 to 0.397 OOD when PAG is added to vanilla JEPA. | Table 2; §5.4 |
| Alignment changes representation geometry | Vorticity latent-to-physical turning-angle MAE reaches 6.3°, a reported 78% reduction; Wave-2D reaches 10.1°, a reported 71% reduction. Computed without a dynamics predictor. | Table 3; §5.4 |
| PSP adds OOD gains | On PAG-aligned representations, relative L2 error falls from 0.397 to 0.288 on Vorticity and from 0.321 to 0.157 on Wave-2D. | Table 2; §5.5 |
| PSP improves parameter-response agreement | Under matched initial conditions, Vorticity field-response cosine agreement increases from 0.626 to 0.708, and amplitude agreement moves from 0.846 to 0.987; the amplitude target is 1. | Table 5; §5.5 |

These results support the combined framework on the supplied benchmarks and incremental benefits of PAG and PSP on two systems. They do not establish universal extrapolation, exact recovery of physical operators, or guaranteed long-term stability. The abstract reports average improvements of 33.4% ID and 51.4% OOD, but the OOD aggregation procedure is not specified.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** PSP provides a physics-inspired decomposition; its branches need not recover analytical PDE operators (§4.3). Advection remains a case where CoDA performs better (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—OOD interpretation:** HeterNS combines viscosity interpolation and extrapolation, without separate scores in Table 4. Wave-2D’s OOD release includes ten boundary-support trajectories alongside 110 strict extrapolation trajectories (Appendix A.7–A.8).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Supplied module ablations cover Vorticity and Wave-2D. Separate geometry-loss, anchor-loss, and auxiliary-predictor ablations are unavailable, limiting attribution within PAG (Table 2; §4.2).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Combined and GS OOD errors differ between Table 4 and §5.3 prose. Vorticity ID errors also differ between Tables 1 and 2, without explanation.
- **Reproducibility:** The architecture/training hyperparameter table lacks its values in the retrieved text. Seed variability, uncertainty estimates, and detailed baseline training protocols are unavailable. Appendix C reports approximately 9,000 GPU hours across experiments, without a per-method breakdown.
- **Data-use ambiguity:** Combined has separate OOD training and validation splits, but their use in the reported evaluation is unclear (Appendix A.5).

## Open research questions
- **Which PAG objective drives improvement?** The supplied ablations add PAG as a package. Next experiment: remove geometry, anchor, and auxiliary dynamics losses individually under matched training budgets.
- **How robust is PSP to parameter dependence that its decomposition captures poorly?** The implementations encode PDE-specific coefficient structure. Next experiment: compare structured and unrestricted predictors on systems with nonlinear parameter interactions.
- **How far does extrapolation remain reliable?** Evaluation uses prescribed parameter ranges and finite trajectories. Next experiment: sweep parameter distance and rollout duration while measuring error and physical stability.
- **Does geometry alignment help reconstruction-pretrained representations equally?** The study motivates PAG through JEPA, but a matched cross-objective PAG comparison is unavailable. Next experiment: apply identical alignment and predictors to reconstruction- and JEPA-pretrained encoders.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and benchmark-level findings, but incomplete implementation reporting and numerical inconsistencies weaken precise reproducibility.

Material missing information includes full hyperparameter values, baseline training and adaptation protocols, seed statistics, probe measurements underlying Figure 1, referenced additional analyses, verified code/weight availability, the OOD averaging rule, and clarification of Combined’s OOD training/validation usage.
