# JEPA-TTT: Persistent Test-Time Training of Latent World Models for Planning under Dynamics Shifts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00722) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00722)


## One-sentence takeaway
JEPA-TTT persistently adapts a pretrained latent dynamics predictor from observations and executed actions, improving planning across eight fixed dynamics shifts without test-time rewards or goal images (Sections 3–4).

## Problem and motivation
A world model trained under one transition regime can mispredict action outcomes after changes in actuation or contact dynamics, causing its planner to choose poor actions. The authors investigate whether self-supervised adaptation can recover planning while preserving the pretrained visual representation and task objective (Sections 1, 3.1).

Their identified gap is persistent adaptation across episodes: existing JEPA planning methods primarily address pretraining, while the closely related AdaJEPA resets adaptation each episode and originally requires a goal image (Section 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent predictor adaptation:** Update the action encoder, autoregressive predictor, and prediction projection using latent prediction MSE; retain parameters, optimizer state, and experience across episodes. The visual encoder and latent projector remain frozen (Sections 3.3, A1.2).
- **Planning with a fixed task objective:** Fit a reward head from offline observation–score pairs, freeze it, and use CEM model predictive control to rank latent rollouts. Deployment requires RGB observations and executed actions, but neither environment rewards nor goal images (Section 3.2).
- **Dense replay and controlled evaluation:** Construct prediction windows at every raw-step offset and uniformly sample accumulated windows. Ablations separate additional optimization, temporal coverage, replay, and cross-episode persistence (Sections 3.4, 4.4).

The contribution is this adaptation and planning procedure plus its empirical analysis; the pretrained JEPA architecture and replay concept are inherited foundations.

Main planning results use three deployment runs and a fixed, deployment-disjoint set of 100 held-out episodes, evaluated every 50 episodes through episode 500. “Best” selects the maximum three-run mean across post-adaptation evaluations; AUC summarizes the full adaptation period (Sections 4.1, A1.2).

The evidence supports recovery under the specified shifts and is consistent with improved dynamics prediction helping planning. It does not establish general robustness to arbitrary deployment changes or real-world effectiveness. Prediction diagnostics use fixed behavior trajectories rather than exhaustively covering actions the adapted planner might consider (Section A5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only one persistent dynamics change is tested. Simultaneous visual or reward shifts, successive dynamics changes, and returning to training dynamics are unstudied; forgetting and transfer are therefore unmeasured (Section 6).
- **Conditional uncertainty:** Deployment runs share one pretrained model per task. Reported standard deviations capture adaptation variability, not independently pretrained-model variability (Section A1.2).
- **Comparison scope:** AdaJEPA is modified to use the paper’s reward head and goal-image-free planner; its episodic score estimate is reused for both metrics. PPO-TTT uses online oracle rewards and a different policy architecture. These results compare the specified implementations, with differing information and adaptation protocols (Section A3).
- **Task and reward-head limitations:** OGBench-Cube is evaluated as a Cartesian reaching task. PushT’s reward head underpredicts high-score states, and the training-dynamics planner achieves no strict successes (Sections A2.4, A6.3).
- **Analyst inference:** A growing buffer and repeated planning may constrain long deployments, but the text supplies no wall-clock latency or memory measurements. The persistence control also bundles parameter and optimizer-state retention, leaving their separate effects unresolved.

## Open research questions
1. **Does persistence survive repeated dynamics changes?** Retention is explicitly untested. Alternate dynamics regimes, return to the original regime, and measure recovery and forgetting (Section 6).
2. **Can bounded replay preserve the benefit?** The method retains all valid windows. Compare fixed-capacity, recent-window, and full replay under matched update budgets, reporting planning, memory, and latency (Section 3.4).
3. **What limits Reacher recovery?** Its gap to the offline reference remains substantial. Compare replay batch sizes, update schedules, and planning horizons while measuring latent MSE and planning scores (Tables A5, A8).
4. **How robust are gains across pretraining seeds?** Current runs condition on fixed pretrained models. Repeat the suite with independently pretrained models and separate pretraining from deployment variance (Section A1.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and controlled experimental findings; broader deployment validity remains unestablished.

Material missing information includes code/checkpoint release status, hardware and runtime measurements, replay memory consumption, variability across pretraining seeds, and performance under real-world or sequential dynamics shifts.
