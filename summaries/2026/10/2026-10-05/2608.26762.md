# Equal Ranking Quality, Different Decisions: Measuring and Reducing Order Dependence in LLM Scorers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26762) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26762)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors show that similarly accurate LLM scorers can make different decisions when candidates are reordered, and that penalizing score disagreement during fine-tuning improves decision stability while preserving aggregate ranking quality (§§6.1–6.3).

## Problem and motivation
LLMs that score several candidates in one prompt assign scores that depend on candidate order and companions. Reordering can therefore change threshold-retained documents, reader answers, or chosen/rejected preference pairs despite leaving the query and candidate pool unchanged (§§1, 3).

The authors identify a gap between evaluating ranking quality or ranking stability and measuring the stability of downstream decisions: a threshold consumes absolute scores, whereas nDCG primarily measures relative ordering (§6.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Decision-level evaluation:** Measure retained-set Jaccard overlap, reader verdict/answer flips, and preference-pair flips alongside output quality. Adapt a rank-instability index, τ-PSI, using pairwise Kendall correlations across random permutations (§§3, 5).
- **Order-consistency SFT (OC-SFT):** Combine teacher-score regression on one shuffled view with a penalty on each candidate’s score variance across shuffled views. The default uses two views and self-distillation; training preserves unrestricted shared context and single-permutation serving (§4).
- **Controlled comparison:** Compare explicit consistency against single-order and order-averaged distillation, permutation augmentation, DebiasFirst, calibration, and serving-time averaging across reranking, multi-document QA, and response ranking (§§5–6).

The distinctive contribution is applying consistency regularization to continuous candidate scores and testing the decisions that consume them; the authors explicitly acknowledge that consistency regularization itself is established (§2).

## Evidence and main results

Unless otherwise stated, primary trained comparisons use Qwen3-4B, self-distillation, three training-seed means, and ten random evaluation permutations (§5).

| OC-SFT reduces ranking instability | Reranking τ-PSI falls from 0.209 for single-order distillation to 0.083 for OC-SFT under the primary evaluation. | §6.3 |
| Reader decisions become more stable | Multi-document QA answer-flip rate is 0.125 for OC-SFT versus 0.149–0.164 for three other order-aware objectives; frozen readers consume the top five passages. The comparative table cells are absent. | §1 contribution 3; §§5–6.3 |
| Better partitioning improves quality without measurable decision gains | Off-the-shelf round-robin partitioning gains 0.052 nDCG@10 over contiguous windows; retained-set overlap, claim-verification flips, and preference-pair flips do not measurably improve. | §6.2; §D.1 |
| Stability gains extend across bases | OC-SFT has lower mean reranking τ-PSI than order-averaged distillation on all twelve bases. Visible cross-base results use seed 42 and 18 collections. | §H.4; Table 36 |

The evidence supports separating decision stability from ranking quality and supports OC-SFT’s advantage within the tested settings. It establishes attenuation rather than guaranteed invariance or universal quality preservation. The seed-42 Qwen3-4B τ-PSI values in Table 36 differ from the primary three-seed means, so those results should not be conflated.

The supplied source is truncated, and many tables retain captions without numerical bodies; several comparisons can therefore be assessed only through the authors’ prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers English text, no pools smaller than four candidates, and no reasoning-intensive retrieval or long-context corpora exceeding the studied prompt geometry (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated pipeline limits:** Consumers are frozen. Co-adaptation could reduce the measured benefit, and the effect of preference-pair instability on a subsequently trained model remains untested (Appendix A).
- **Incomplete invariance:** Training shuffles within windows while evaluation can also change companions. Marker-renaming sensitivity persists, and residual score variance can still change near-tied rankings (§4; §C.1).
- **Comparison and reproducibility risks:** Proprietary legal collections prevent reproducing the full aggregate; GPT-5.4 comparisons depend on provider behavior, fixed-order noise, and tie-breaking. External specialized rerankers have different supervision (§Ethics Statement; Reproducibility Statement; Appendix A; §G.5).
- **Analyst inference:** OC-SFT receives a consistency-weight search while some alternatives use fixed recipes, leaving tuning-effort comparability uncertain. The selected reranking weight reaches the tested grid boundary; larger weights were not tested (§B.3).

## Open research questions
- **Do more stable preference pairs improve downstream models?** Only pair selection is measured. Train otherwise matched downstream models using labels from single-order distillation and OC-SFT, then compare quality and variation across label permutations (Appendix A).
- **How much benefit survives consumer co-adaptation?** Frozen consumers cannot establish this. Refit readers or downstream consumers for each scorer and repeat decision-stability evaluation (Appendix A).
- **Can one objective stabilize multiple presentation channels?** Order training does not remove marker sensitivity. Compare order-only, marker-only, and combined consistency penalties on held-out marker schemes and prompt wording (§C.1).
- **Does the method transfer to reasoning and longer contexts?** Those regimes are untested, and wider serving eventually degrades. Evaluate reasoning rerankers and longer documents while controlling truncation and serving width (Appendix A; §G.4).
