# QuantWM: Temporally Consistent 2-Bit KV Cache Quantization for Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26425) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26425)


## One-sentence takeaway
QuantWM uses historical Query sensitivity and low-rank Key-error compensation to improve attention preservation under 2-bit KV-cache quantization, with reported visual-quality gains across five video models and up to 6.20× cache compression (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Historical KV caches support consistent autoregressive video generation but consume substantial memory. The authors find that existing 2-bit quantization can introduce flickering, blur, and artifacts despite nearly unchanged VBench scores (§§1, 3.1).

Their diagnosis is that Key quantization changes attention logits and selected historical tokens: Keys reportedly have smaller reconstruction errors than Values yet cause larger output degradation. The identified gap is that reconstruction accuracy and standard video benchmarks alone do not adequately capture these attention disruptions (§§3.2–3.3; Tables 1–2; Figure 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Quantization-sensitivity-aware clustering (QSAC):** Extends QVG’s existing centroid-residual representation. Historical per-head Query second moments weight Key channels; candidate centroids are then scored using sensitivity-weighted residual ranges to approximate quantization’s effect on attention logits (§4.1).
- **Principal-subspace attention compensation (PSAC):** Projects remaining Key errors onto dominant historical Query directions and restores those components during reconstruction. The implementation uses rank 8, BF16 bases, and INT8 correction coefficients (§4.2; §5.1).
- **Training-free streaming integration and diagnosis:** Updates statistics causally, compresses completed chunks, and reconstructs BF16 tensors through a fused decoder for native attention. The study also evaluates token-selection shifts alongside frame quality and VBench (§3; Appendices C.3, D.1).

Centroid-residual quantization itself is inherited from QVG; the proposed additions are Query-sensitive assignment and principal-subspace compensation (§§2, 4).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. The retrieved text contains table captions and narrative summaries, but no table cells.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Keys dominate degradation | With QVG, Key-only quantization reportedly worsens PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS more than Value-only quantization, despite smaller Key reconstruction errors. | §§3.2–3.3; Tables 1–2; Figures 2–3 |
| Better frame quality than QVG | On LongCat-Video, PSNR increases from 20.683 to 25.508 and LPIPS decreases from 0.0784 to 0.0332. Main evaluation uses 93-frame, 480p/720p videos; the narrative does not identify which resolution yields these numbers. | §5.2; Tables 3–4 |
| Fewer attention-selection shifts | On 480p videos across five models, average top-1 token-shift ratio versus BF16 decreases from QVG’s 51.55% to QuantWM’s 15.31%. | §5.3; Table 5 |
| Both components contribute | On HY-World 1.5, QSAC reduces token-shift ratio from 61.36% to 38.17%; PSAC without QSAC reaches 22.9%. Their combination reportedly performs best; its exact ablation values are unavailable. | §5.4; Table 6 |
| Substantial cache savings | For 93-frame generation, compression reaches up to 6.20× across models. LongCat-Video cache memory decreases from BF16’s 21.709 GiB to 3.625 GiB. | §5.5; Figure 6 |
| Latency depends on model | Relative to BF16, generation latency increases 5.44% on LingBot-World-v2 and 5.87% on HY-World 1.5, but decreases 17.78% on LongCat-Video; experiments use A800 80GB GPUs. LongCat’s improvement is attributed to reduced cache-offloading traffic. | §§5.1, 5.5; Table 7; Appendix D.2 |
| Longer-generation quality improves | For one-minute HY-World 1.5 generation, VBench Image score increases from QVG’s 0.4756 to 0.6242. Best average rank across five models is also claimed. | Appendix B.1; Table 8 |

These findings support improved preservation of the evaluated models’ behavior while reducing persistent cache storage. They do not establish complete equivalence to BF16, universal latency gains, or reliable measurement of flickering by VBench. The reported attention-shift reduction supports the proposed mechanism, but does not isolate token shifts as the sole cause of visual degradation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified measurement limitation:** VBench Temporal scores remain similar despite visible flickering; long-video evaluation nevertheless focuses on VBench ( §§5.2, B.1).
- **Author-identified systems qualification:** Latency depends on cache residency and offloading. Separate cold-start runs also make latency values across Tables 10 and 11 unsuitable for direct comparison (Appendices C.5, D.2).
- **Analyst inference—historical-statistics risk:** Past Queries may imperfectly predict sensitivity after abrupt scene or action changes. Such shifts are not separately evaluated in the supplied text (§4; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** Evaluation covers five models on A800 80GB hardware; behavior on memory-constrained devices remains unestablished (§5.1).
- **Evidence limitation:** Missing table cells prevent checking per-model results, average ranks, and complete ablations. Sample counts, uncertainty estimates, and the exact frame-metric reference/alignment protocol are unavailable.
- **Precision qualification:** “2-bit” describes residual storage; centroids, correction bases, coefficients, metadata, and active chunks use additional precision. Attention consumes reconstructed BF16 tensors (Appendix D.1).

## Open research questions
1. **How quickly do historical statistics adapt to abrupt changes?** The paper uses accumulated past Queries without a targeted distribution-shift test. Compare cumulative and rolling-window statistics during sudden camera, action, or scene changes.
2. **How reliably do token shifts predict perceptual flickering?** Reduced shifts accompany better quality, but VBench misses visible defects. Test their relationship using controlled sequences, human ratings, and explicit temporal-error measurements.
3. **Does long-horizon attention preservation persist beyond benchmark gains?** One-minute evaluation emphasizes VBench. Track token shifts and temporal artifacts throughout longer interactive trajectories.
4. **When does compression improve practical deployment?** Reported latency varies with offloading. Measure peak memory, latency, and feasible context length across GPU capacities and cache-residency configurations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and narrative results are clear, but quantitative verification is limited by absent table contents.

Material missing information includes full per-model metrics and timings, evaluation sample counts and seeds, uncertainty estimates, precise frame-quality comparison protocols, peak-memory accounting for temporary reconstruction buffers, and confirmed availability of runnable code and evaluation configurations.
