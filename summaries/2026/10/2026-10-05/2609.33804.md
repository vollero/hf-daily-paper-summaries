# MinkowskiPE: Minkowski Positional Encoding for Spatiotemporal Perception

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33804) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33804)


## One-sentence takeaway
MinkowskiPE uses coordinate-conditioned Lorentz transformations to make positional attention depend only on relative spacetime displacement, improving molecular and video forecasting over reported baselines and matched models without positional encoding (§4; Tables 2–4).

## Problem and motivation
Spatiotemporal forecasting requires jointly modeling when and where observations occur. The authors identify a gap between dynamical models with potentially restrictive physical structure and flexible attention architectures whose time–space coupling remains implicit. They propose adding an explicit geometric bias through positional encoding without prescribing a dynamical law (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Relative spatiotemporal encoding:** Learnable linear projections map joint time–space coordinates to scalar parameters for Lorentz boosts on two-dimensional query/key blocks. A Minkowski metric factor in the key transformation makes their ordinary dot product depend on relative coordinate displacement (§4.1, Eqs. 9–12).
- **Practical attention integration:** Different feature blocks learn different projection directions; the projection matrix is shared across layers and heads. Values remain unchanged, and the method preserves standard dot-product attention. The implementation uses FlashAttention through PyTorch on H200 GPUs (§4.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Mathematical characterization:** Appendix A characterizes the commuting generators required by the homomorphic construction. Appendix C establishes a separate obstruction: exact finite-dimensional unitary token-wise Galilean encodings cannot retain the relative spatial component under its stated assumptions.
- **Cross-domain evaluation:** The same positional formulation is tested in task-specific molecular and video architectures, including controlled removal of positional encoding (§5).

The ablations support an incremental benefit over removing positional encoding in these architectures. They do not establish superiority over alternative positional encodings, since no such controlled comparison is supplied. Parameter counts establish model size, not measured runtime or memory efficiency. The algebraic result concerns positional score dependence; it does not establish Lorentz symmetry of the data or the complete model (§3.2; §6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Geometry is fixed rather than learned; scalar projections restrict each block to a one-parameter transformation; evaluation covers only molecular and visual forecasting (§6).
- **Analyst inference—test selection:** Video checkpoints are selected using test-set MSE, followed by selection of the lowest-MSE seed. This makes the reported test performance vulnerable to selection optimism, even though the authors aim to match OpenSTL’s reporting protocol (Appendix B.3; §5.2).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Tables provide seed averages or selected runs without standard deviations or confidence intervals. Statistical reliability of small margins, especially on MISATO-100, is therefore unclear (Tables 1–4).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Molecular evaluation fixes the protein and predicts ligand heavy atoms; KTH is the only video dataset. These results do not establish performance for flexible proteins or broader video settings (§5; Appendix B).
- **Comparison provenance:** Single-trajectory and video comparisons partly use previously reported baseline results; multi-trajectory baselines are retrained. This limits uniformity of experimental control (Appendix B.2; Table 3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does Lorentz geometry outperform other positional mechanisms?** The ablation only removes PE. Compare alternative relative encodings in the same architectures with matched training and parameter budgets.
2. **Do gains persist with independent model selection and longer forecasts?** Video selection uses test MSE, and reported targets cover 20 future frames. Select checkpoints on validation data, then evaluate untouched tests across longer horizons.
3. **Can richer or adaptive geometry improve performance?** The authors leave multi-parameter and learned geometry open (§6; Appendix A). Compare commuting higher-dimensional constructions and adaptive geometry against the current blocks under matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the construction and reported benchmark results, but comparative generality remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes alternative-PE ablations, uncertainty estimates, runtime and memory measurements, verified code/checkpoint availability, independent-validation video results, and detailed baseline retraining configurations.
