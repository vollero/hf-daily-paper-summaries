# Rollout-Marginal Distillation for Long-Horizon Autoregressive Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37925) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37925)


## One-sentence takeaway
RMD independently scores chunks from autoregressive rollouts, then refines temporal coherence with video-level distillation, outperforming two baselines on approximately 60-second VBench-Long videos after training on five-second rollouts (Table 1; §§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Autoregressive video generators condition each new chunk on generated history, allowing small errors to accumulate beyond their training horizon. Self-rollout training exposes models to these errors, but the authors argue that joint video-level distribution matching distillation (DMD) can preserve historical artifacts because correcting them would disrupt temporal consistency (§1; Figure 2).

The identified gap is a supervision signal that corrects local visual quality despite degraded context, without requiring paired continuations for generated histories (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Rollout-marginal objective:** Match independently scored chunks to teacher-defined chunk distributions while retaining generated history for causal prediction. Initial and continuation chunks receive separate targets because their VAE latent statistics differ (§3.2, Equations 4–5).
- **Practical chunk supervision:** Adapt the Wan 14B teacher using LoRA on generated continuation chunks. Supervise initial chunks at random denoising exits and continuations only at the final exit to preserve temporal dynamics (§3.3).
- **Two-stage quality/coherence training:** Follow marginal training with lower-learning-rate video-level DMD refinement to improve cross-chunk consistency (§3.4).

The central novelty is changing DMD’s matching space from joint videos to rollout-induced chunk marginals. Causal initialization and checkpointed differentiable replay are adopted from prior methods (§§2–3).

## Evidence and main results

All aggregate comparisons use VBench-Long’s 944 prompts, five generations per prompt, 832×480 resolution, and 16 FPS (§4.1). Scores below are reported as **Total / Quality / Semantic**, with higher values better.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| RMD leads both baselines with chunk size 1 | Approximately 60 seconds: RMD **81.26 / 84.52 / 68.24**; Self Forcing **70.94 / 77.89 / 43.17**; Causal Forcing **69.88 / 78.34 / 36.03**. | Table 1; §§4.1–4.2 |
| RMD also leads with chunk size 3 | Same evaluation: RMD **81.48 / 85.03 / 67.24**; Self Forcing **77.44 / 81.99 / 59.22**; Causal Forcing **74.88 / 80.53 / 52.27**. | Table 1 |
| Quality remains stable across measured durations | Chunk size 1, same generated videos: RMD Quality **84.68 at 10 seconds → 84.52 at 60 seconds**; Self Forcing Total **79.12 → 70.94**. These are different metrics, not a direct comparison of decline magnitudes. | §4.3; Figure 5 |
| Marginal training improves appearance; refinement improves aggregate performance | Chunk size 1: video DMD only **72.44 / 78.68 / 47.50**; marginal only **80.64 / 84.90 / 63.59**; full RMD **81.26 / 84.52 / 68.24**. | §4.4; Table 2(a) |
| Refinement improves reported temporal metrics | Marginal only → full RMD: Subject Consistency **97.29 → 98.05**; Background Consistency **96.26 → 96.89**; Temporal Flickering score **99.06 → 99.41**; Motion Smoothness **98.79 → 98.96**. All are higher-is-better. | Table 2(b) |
| Asymmetric exits outperform uniform random exits | Chunk size 1: random exits **78.29 / 82.48 / 61.56**, versus full RMD **81.26 / 84.52 / 68.24**; the authors report visible jitter for random exits. | Table 2(a); Figure 6 |
| Much longer generation is illustrated qualitatively | A **500-second** example follows **five-second** training, using fully sliding context; it is excluded from aggregate evaluation. | Figure 1; §4.1 |

The results support improved benchmark performance and reduced degradation within the measured horizon. Ablations support the complementary roles of marginal scoring and temporal refinement, although refinement slightly lowers the aggregate Quality score. The 500-second example does not establish reliable performance at that duration across prompts. The proposed artifact-preservation mechanism is supported by illustrative score predictions and ablations, rather than a comprehensive causal test.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified tradeoff:** Independent chunk scoring leaves cross-chunk relationships unconstrained, producing flicker; uniform random exits can disrupt temporal dynamics (§§3.3–3.4, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Aggregate evidence ends at approximately 60 seconds and covers one teacher/student model family, two chunk sizes, and one benchmark suite (§4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Baselines share sliding-window generation without anchors, but the supplied text does not establish matched training budgets or report uncertainty estimates (§4.2; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility gaps:** Teacher-adaptation dataset size, LoRA configuration, adaptation schedule, hardware, and training cost are not specified. Inference overhead is claimed absent, but no latency measurements are reported (§§3.3, 4.1–4.2).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic isolation:** No reported ablation isolates teacher adaptation or separate initial/continuation targets from the remaining design choices (§4.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does stability persist across many very long rollouts?** Beyond the aggregate evaluation, evidence is one qualitative example. Next experiment: evaluate the full prompt set at progressively longer durations with uncertainty estimates.
- **How much does teacher adaptation contribute?** Its necessity is motivated by latent statistics but not separately ablated. Next experiment: compare adapted and unadapted chunk teachers under otherwise identical training.
- **How sensitive is the quality/coherence tradeoff to refinement strength?** Refinement improves temporal and semantic scores while slightly reducing Quality. Next experiment: sweep refinement duration and learning rates.
- **Does RMD generalize beyond Wan?** All experiments use one teacher/student family. Next experiment: repeat marginal and joint-DMD comparisons with another causal video architecture under matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark comparisons; **medium** in the causal explanation and broader long-horizon generalization.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, matched baseline training budgets, teacher-adaptation details, hardware and runtime measurements, quantitative evaluation beyond approximately 60 seconds, and independently verified release contents.
