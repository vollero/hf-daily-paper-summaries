# Can Computation from Earlier Problems Help LLMs Solve New Ones?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39394) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39394)


## One-sentence takeaway
STAIR learns how a frozen LLM reads stored attention states from completed problems, improving mean later-turn AIME 2025 accuracy by up to 11.67 percentage points over ordinary retained history, with uneven transfer to scientific reasoning (§4.2, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Independent problems often share a conversation, exposing each new problem to earlier questions and responses. The authors show that retained history can improve or degrade correctness and ask whether the internal computation from completed problems can be reused productively. Their identified gap is historical computation reuse across task switches, where each new problem supplies its own information (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Representation analysis:** Controlled replay separates problem effects, history effects, and their interaction. The interaction retains similar problem neighborhoods across distinct histories, despite accounting for a small share of displacement energy (§2, Appendix A).
- **STAIR:** A separate, read-only bank stores historical keys and values. Learned per-head Householder reflections redirect current queries; the difference between reflected and reference reads updates current prompt processing. The backbone remains frozen, and the auxiliary branch stops after prefill (§3).
- **Training and evaluation:** Only 12,288 reflector parameters are trained using backbone-generated mathematical responses, response supervision, and Native-distribution KL regularization. Experiments cover three Qwen models, four benchmarks, shared-history controls, and joint LoRA training (§§3.3–4.4).

## Evidence and main results
Avg@4 averages correctness across four sampled responses per problem; Pass@4 counts problems with at least one correct response (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| History changes correctness in both directions | Qwen3-4B Instruct, MATH-500 T4: 35 wrong-to-correct and 60 correct-to-wrong transitions among 2,000 paired responses; Avg@4 falls from 95.00% at Vanilla T1 to 93.75%. | §2.3; Table 6 |
| Interaction geometry recurs across histories | Two Qwen3-4B Instruct/MATH-500 replay groups, layer 19: eight-neighbor overlap is 48.06% and 46.50%, versus shuffled-identity controls of 11.73% and 12.08%; interaction energy fractions are 0.69% and 0.66%. | §2.2; Figure 2; Appendix A.3 |
| Largest continuous-session gains occur on AIME | Mean T2–T4 Avg@4, Native → STAIR: Qwen3.5-4B 40.56% → 52.22% (+11.67 pp before rounding); Qwen3.5-9B 43.33% → 49.44% (+6.11 pp); Instruct 34.44% → 38.06% (+3.61 pp). | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Transfer is uneven | GPQA-Diamond mean T2–T4 Avg@4, Native → STAIR: Qwen3.5-4B 75.25% → 75.38%, but Pass@4 89.39% → 86.87%; Qwen3.5-9B Avg@4 79.88% → 75.93%. | Table 2 |
| Shared-history controls favor full STAIR | Qwen3.5-4B, AIME T2, 30 problems and four matched trajectories: Native 21.67%, STAIR 48.33% Avg@4; Bank-free 36.67%, random reflector 37.22%, direct reflected read 41.67%, K–V permutation 33.06%. Random and permutation controls average three test-time seeds. | §4.3; Table 3 |
| STAIR complements LoRA in one tested setting | Instruct, AIME mean T2–T4: LoRA → LoRA + STAIR raises Avg@4 from 39.44% to 43.33% and Pass@4 from 58.89% to 63.33%, adding 12,288 trainable parameters. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Small parameter count does not eliminate runtime cost | Qwen3.5-4B, 16 fixed MATH-500 histories on one A800 80GB: full-bank paired median overhead is 567 ms prefill, 0.81 s for a fixed 256-token decode workload, and 6.60 GiB peak memory; bank reconstruction is excluded. | Appendix C.4; Table 13 |

These results support useful learned historical reading in the tested mathematical settings. The shared-T1 experiment isolates the current-turn intervention more closely than continuous sessions, whose later histories diverge between conditions. Representation recurrence is descriptive evidence; it does not establish that the measured interaction structure causes accuracy gains. STAIR also remains below Vanilla mean Avg@4 on AIME for every evaluated model (Table 2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers four-turn sessions within the Qwen family; mathematical training may contribute to domain-dependent transfer (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Bank storage and prefill costs grow with completed turns; selective retention remains proposed (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Shared-history ablations cover only Qwen3.5-4B on AIME T2. Random-reflector and direct-read controls modify a trained method at test time, so their deficits do not establish how independently trained alternatives would perform (Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** No confidence intervals or repeated-training-seed variability are reported for headline accuracy gains. The small AIME and filtered AMC23 evaluations limit precision (Appendix C.3).
- **Comparison boundary:** LoRA uses a different optimization schedule, and training compute budgets were not measured (Appendix C.1).
- **Measurement boundary:** Native replay varies visible content, historical K/V, and current-token positions together; it does not isolate their individual effects (Appendix A.1). Runtime measurements exclude bank reconstruction and full-answer generation (Appendix C.4).

## Open research questions
- **Can broader training improve scientific transfer?** GPQA gains are inconsistent (§§4.2, 6). Train math-only and mixed math/science controllers under matched budgets, then evaluate held-out domains.
- **Can useful reuse survive longer sessions at manageable cost?** Only four turns are evaluated, while banks grow (§6). Compare full banks with selective retention across longer sessions, measuring correctness, latency, and memory.
- **Which components remain necessary after matched training?** Some controls are test-time interventions (§4.3). Train differential-read, direct-read, and alternative layer-placement controllers independently across multiple seeds.
- **Does recurring interaction geometry predict useful history?** The representation analysis does not link neighborhood recurrence causally to correctness (§2). Test whether those measurements predict per-problem gains, then intervene on selected historical states.

**Confidence: high** for the reported method and experimental results; **medium** for generalization and mechanism.
