# Scaling Trajectories for Complex Tasks through Recursive Self-Rewrite

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02826) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02826)


## One-sentence takeaway
Recursive Self-Rewrite converts harness-assisted successes into verified training trajectories under a general terminal harness, improving reported benchmark performance over direct trajectory fine-tuning, although rewriting and increased training-data volume are not separately tested.

## Problem and motivation
Different execution harnesses help the same model solve complementary tasks, but their trajectories contain controller messages, workflow conventions, and stopping rules that may not transfer to a general harness. The authors aim to preserve useful planning, checking, and recovery behavior while removing dependence on specialized execution support (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Multi-harness discovery:** Run Qwen-3.8-27B with Terminus 2, StateM, and Recursive Self-Reflect Terminus (RSRT), then pool verified successes. RSRT uses pass/fail verifier feedback to trigger continued reflection and repair (§§2.1, 3.2).
- **Recursive trajectory rewriting:** The same model acts as planner, critic, and executor. The planner extracts procedural runbooks; deterministic checks and a model critic screen leakage; critique feeds runbook revision; accepted guides drive fresh executions under Terminus 2. Private guides are excluded from training histories, and passing executions undergo an additional leakage audit (§2.2; Fig. 1).
- **Training demonstration:** Fine-tune on verified rewrites plus original Terminus 2 successes, then evaluate without private runbooks or specialized discovery harnesses (§§2.2, 3.5). The contribution is this discovery-to-rewriting pipeline, rather than autonomous repeated model improvement (§4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery harnesses provide complementary coverage | On 2,929 tasks, the three-harness union solves 759 (25.9%), versus 565 (19.3%) for the strongest individual harness: 194 additional tasks, a 34.3% relative increase. Recorded rollout counts differ. | Tables 1–2; §3.3 |
| Complementarity survives equal attempt counts | On 1,247 tasks with 2,074 rollouts per harness, the union solves 352 (28.2%), versus RSRT’s 285 (22.9%): a 23.5% relative increase. | §3.3, p. 6 |
| Rewriting expands successful trajectory volume | Authors report 11,094 passing rewrites from 12,893 executions, approximately 86%, following discovery of 2,001 passing source trajectories. RSR training additionally includes 766 original Terminus 2 successes. | Table 2; §§3.4–3.5 |
| RSR improves pass@3 across all five benchmarks | Base / Direct SFT / RSR: TB2 **57.0 / 53.4 / 74.2%**; TB3 **0.0 / 5.4 / 9.5%**; TB4 **1.5 / 4.5 / 9.1%**; TBH **39.0 / 56.0 / 63.0%**; SWR100 **3.0 / 3.0 / 6.0%**. Evaluation uses three attempts under the general harness. | Table 5; §§3.5–3.6 |

The results support the combined pipeline as a useful training intervention for this model and evaluation suite. They do not isolate the effects of rewriting, additional samples, leakage screening, or recursive critique. The equal-rollout analysis controls attempt counts, but does not establish equal compute cost across harnesses.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-discussed:** Discovery rollout counts differ because of sandbox/task errors; extended RSRT execution can spend substantial effort on failed attempts (§3.3). Rewrites can fail despite identical guidance, and verifier success does not guarantee quality-audit acceptance (Fig. 5; §3.4).
- **Analyst inference — causal attribution:** RSR changes both trajectory construction and training-data quantity. No matched-volume comparison or planner/critic/recursion ablation is reported (§§3.4–3.7).
- **Analyst inference — limited generality:** Experiments use one base model and terminal tasks. Transfer to other models or interaction modalities remains untested (§3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §3.4 reports rewritten executions covering 975 unique tasks, although discovery successes cover only 759; the supplied text does not reconcile these counts.
- **Measurement uncertainty:** Confidence intervals and significance tests are absent. Table 5’s pass@3 task counts are rounded estimates; its TB4 base pass@3 of 1.5% alongside a 0.0% mean per-run rate is unexplained (§3.6).
- **Reproducibility:** Training hyperparameters, full prompts, the recursive retry budget, detailed audit criteria, and train–evaluation overlap checks are not supplied. The explanation of direct-SFT looping rests on inspection of three trajectories (§3.6).

## Open research questions
1. **Does rewriting help independently of data volume?** The reported comparison changes both. Next experiment: match training tokens and task coverage between direct SFT and rewritten SFT.
2. **Which pipeline components prevent leakage and improve learning?** Component effects and audit accuracy are unmeasured. Next experiment: ablate recursive critique and post-execution auditing, with independent blinded leakage review.
3. **How cost-effective is multi-harness discovery?** Equal rollout counts do not equalize execution time, and RSRT permits long continuation (§3.2). Next experiment: compare coverage and downstream gains under matched wall-clock or token budgets.
4. **Does RSR generalize beyond this model and achieve complete long-horizon solutions?** Only one model is studied, and LHTB has no completed tasks. Next experiment: repeat across model sizes and measure both process reward and full completion.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the pipeline and reported comparisons, but causal attribution and reproducibility are limited.

Material missing information includes training settings, compute costs, complete prompts and retry limits, audit validation, dataset/code availability, overlap controls, uncertainty estimates, and explanations for the task-count and metric inconsistencies.
