# From Gradients to Capabilities: Understanding Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02179) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02179)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the studied multi-teacher language-model distillation settings, loss weighting, Adam history, and numerical precision reshape teacher signals, while closer full-vocabulary gradient approximation does not consistently improve task performance (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation aims to combine specialist teachers’ capabilities in one student, but strong teachers do not guarantee balanced capability transfer. The authors identify a gap between supervision choices, their resulting gradients, optimizer updates, and eventual task gains—especially for response-length weighting and vocabulary truncation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The main experiments use Qwen3-1.7B and four RL-trained teachers sharing its initialization, specializing in mathematics, code, instruction following, and science. Controlled diagnostics hold student parameters, responses, and optimizer states fixed; training comparisons vary loss averaging, vocabulary supervision, and optimizer. SmolLM3-3B supplies complementary diagnostics (Appendices A, H).

- **Weighting analysis:** A covariance identity explains why balancing domain token contributions still favors longer responses within each domain; equal response averaging removes that length-weighting term (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Optimizer and precision diagnostics:** Moment resets and zero-gradient controls separate current supervision from Adam history; paired FP32/BF16 measurements distinguish widespread movement from apparent sparsity (§4; Appendices C, F).
- **Vocabulary-to-capability comparison:** Renormalized KL on shared student–teacher top-k candidates is assessed through coverage, gradient fidelity, and task scores. Its implementation addresses a teacher-query interface constraint; intersection supervision also has prior motivation, so the contribution is principally the controlled analysis rather than an established first invention (§5; Appendix D.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Adam attenuates averaging-induced directional differences | Six fixed Qwen batches: DT–DR mean gradient cosine **0.68**, versus **0.96** for one-step updates using identical saved Adam states. Diagnostics use top-64 gradients at PG-trained checkpoints. | §§3.1–3.2; Figure 2b; Appendix B.1 |
| Shared momentum masks teacher differences | At a fixed PG-trained Qwen state, own-domain teacher updates have mean pairwise cosine **>0.83** for PG and top-64. Subtracting the zero-gradient Adam step reduces increment cosine to **<0.01**; common prefixes raise it to approximately **0.24–0.35**. | §4.2; Figure 5b–c; Appendix F.1 |
| BF16 makes widespread movement appear sparse | Under domain response averaging, approximately **97%** of FP32 master weights change from initialization, versus **7–9%** after BF16 rounding; BF16 reaches **7–11%** when DT/GT runs are included. | §4.1; Figure 5a |
| SGD achieves higher reported mean scores | Qwen PG training, 500 steps: SGD versus Adam equal-weight four-benchmark mean scores (%) are **39.94 vs. 38.91** (DR), **39.46 vs. 38.63** (DT), and **39.26 vs. 38.79** (GT). Table caption specifies five training seeds. | §4.3; Table 1 caption; Appendix A |
| Gradient fidelity does not predict task gains consistently | Qwen top-64 gradient cosine to full KL exceeds **0.999**. At 500 steps with Adam, top-64 versus PG changes MATH-500 accuracy by **+2.6 percentage points** under DR but **−2.1 points** under GT; four-task mean changes are **+0.16** and **+0.21 points**, respectively. | §5.3; Table 1; Appendices A.2, F.3 |
| High average coverage can conceal truncation error | SmolLM top-64 mean retained student mass is **97.42%** initially and **98.56%** after DR training; weighted prefixes below 90% coverage comprise **8.42%** and **3.65%**. Trained diagnostic batches show **2.16–24.54%** relative gradient error against full KL. | §5.1; Figure 7; Appendix H.2 |

These results support the authors’ distinction between raw supervision, optimizer-mediated movement, and benchmark gains. They do not establish a universally best averaging rule, optimizer, or vocabulary budget. Full-vocabulary KL and Teacher-64 are single-step references rather than end-to-end training baselines (Appendix D.1). Fixed-prefix PG unbiasedness likewise does not establish equivalent training trajectories or capabilities (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evidence comes from limited model families and scales; larger-scale behavior may differ. The roles of reduced Adam momentum, clipping, and second-moment normalization remain unresolved (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** SGD’s score advantage does not isolate momentum as its sole cause: optimizer families and learning rates differ, and the supplied text reports no matched end-to-end momentum sweep (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** SmolLM capability comparisons confound loss, averaging, and optimizer choices, limiting attribution to vocabulary supervision alone (Appendices H.1, H.3).
- **Evidence availability:** Table 1’s numerical cells and uncertainty values are absent despite its caption and prose summaries. Tables 2–3 also lack their contents, limiting checks of data sizes and teacher construction.

## Open research questions
- **Would lower Adam first-moment decay improve capability transfer?** The authors propose this but report no training sweep (§7). A next experiment could vary β₁ while holding loss, averaging, data, and tuning budget fixed.
- **Why do nearly aligned vocabulary gradients produce different task outcomes?** Clipping and second-moment effects remain unresolved (§7). A factorial training experiment could vary these mechanisms across PG and intersection KL.
- **Can adaptive candidate budgets address low-coverage prefixes?** SmolLM exposes truncation error despite high mean coverage (Appendix H). Compare fixed top-k against coverage-triggered expansion, measuring gradient error, task scores, and scoring cost.
- **Do the findings generalize to larger or differently initialized teachers?** Current scope is limited (§7; Appendix A.1). Repeat the controlled diagnostics and training comparisons across scales and teacher initialization relationships.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central findings and diagnostic controls, but lacks complete result tables and plotted values. Material missing information includes per-setting uncertainty, exact training-pool sizes, full teacher GRPO configurations, statistical significance assessments, and confirmed code/model release availability.
