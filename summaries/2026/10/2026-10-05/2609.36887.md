# WEFT: Scaling Tool-Use Post-Training for General-Purpose Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36887) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36887)


## One-sentence takeaway
WEFT jointly constructs and repairs tool environments, tasks, and verifiers, with controlled experiments supporting improvements from execution-driven revision, diverse interaction settings, and atomic-task credit assignment rather than environment expansion alone.

## Problem and motivation
Tool-use training needs feasible tasks, correct state transitions, and trustworthy outcome checks. The authors argue that scaling executable environments alone leaves faulty tools, impossible tasks, and incorrect verifiers that can penalize valid agent behavior. Their proposed gap is reliable scaling of the complete interaction system: environment, task, agent harness, and evaluator (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Whole-system construction:** Synthesize stateful MCP environments, certify atomic tasks through execution, and compose them into dependency graphs with grounded initial states. Present shared objectives through Agentic and SimUser disclosure views and ReAct, OpenClaw, and Hermes harnesses (§3.1).
- **Execution-driven self-evolution:** Use traces, state differences, and checkpoint outcomes to attribute failures to the policy or defective components; revise selected components, revalidate dependencies, and collect fresh rollouts (§3.2).
- **Local sampling and learning:** Preserve verified trajectory prefixes while retrying failed atomic tasks for SFT. Filter RL tasks by agreement between executable and rubric-based scores, then compute group-relative advantages from binary atomic-task completion (§3.3.1–3.3.2).
- **MegaMCP infrastructure:** Share compatible tool-service processes while isolating rollout databases and workspaces; snapshots support retries, branching, and recovery (§3.3.3).

The claimed novelty is this integrated system-level treatment and its specific repair, sampling, credit-assignment, and serving mechanisms; the paper does not establish that every individual ingredient is new (§2–3).

The controlled comparisons support several component benefits under the tested settings. They do not establish universal superiority, equivalent training compute across external baselines, or general deployment speedups. The larger model’s main-benchmark regressions also limit the claim of consistently beneficial post-training across scales.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described conditions:** Local sampling and atomic-turn RL depend on verifiable, recoverable intermediate states and a fixed task order (§3.3). MegaMCP retains concurrent-session provisioning costs, and its larger primary latency comparison uses asymmetric warming conditions (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—verification validity:** Agreement between an LLM judge and executable checks does not independently establish correctness. The text provides no human-audited attribution accuracy or verifier false-positive/false-negative measurement (§3.2–3.3).
- **Analyst inference—ablation scope:** View and harness experiments fix tasks but do not state that total training trajectories or compute are matched. Self-evolution does not separately quantify backend, task, and verifier repairs (Table 2; Fig. 6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** External base-model results include an airline-domain setup modification; repeated-run uncertainty and statistical significance are not reported (Appendix B.2; Tables 1–3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text does not specify numerical verifier-correlation thresholds, complete SFT settings, total training costs, or public availability of code, checkpoints, and corpus.

## Open research questions
- **Are repairs correctly attributed?** No independent attribution audit is reported. Next experiment: human-label failures and measure attribution accuracy and repair success by component.
- **Does harness diversity help at equal compute?** Task count is controlled, but training-volume matching is unclear. Next experiment: compare mixtures with equal accepted tokens and teacher-rollout budgets.
- **How reliable are filtered verifiers?** Correlation filtering lacks independent correctness measurements. Next experiment: audit retained and rejected tasks using human judgments and deliberately perturbed executions.
- **Why does the larger model regress on the main benchmarks?** The reported base comparison uses external scores. Next experiment: reevaluate base and trained checkpoints under identical harnesses and run staged SFT/RL ablations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported results and method description, but causal and reproducibility conclusions remain limited by missing uncertainty estimates, independent verification audits, complete compute controls, filtering thresholds, and confirmed asset releases.
