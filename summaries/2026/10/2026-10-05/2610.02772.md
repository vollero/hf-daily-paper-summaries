# Improving Atomic-Fact Recall via Focused Views in Unstructured Knowledge Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02772) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02772)


## One-sentence takeaway
FOVEATED improves atomic-fact recall across all tested editor–backbone–benchmark combinations by perturbing preceding-context key positions during editing, with additional editing cost and some general-capability tradeoffs (Table 1; Tables 12, 14).

## Problem and motivation
Unstructured knowledge editing updates an LLM using a passage containing multiple facts. Existing editors can reproduce that passage yet fail to answer questions about individual facts without its context.

The authors identify **context-induced difficulty underestimation**: teacher forcing supplies longer ground-truth prefixes to later facts, lowering their initial prediction loss before they have been learned. They argue that this weakens the editing signal and helps explain context reliance—a learning-dynamics gap in earlier approaches (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Empirical diagnosis:** Pre-edit token losses decline with passage position on Qwen2.5 across the UnFine subsets, including after sentence shuffling. This supports the contextual-shortcut explanation (Section 2.2; Appendix D.1).
- **Focused-view editing:** For each target sentence, FOVEATED independently samples rounded-Gaussian positional offsets for the prompt and preceding sentences. It perturbs their key-side RoPE rotations while preserving query rotations and target-sentence key rotations; offsets are refreshed each optimization step and removed at inference (Section 3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Integration with two editor families:** Direct-optimization editors use token-weighted sentence losses across focused views. Locate-then-edit editors first write focused sentence targets into shallow layers, then fit the complete passage in deeper layers while preserving those targets. No externally generated atomic-QA supervision is required (Section 3.2).
- **Theoretical interpretation:** The perturbed loss decomposes into a mean-margin term and a nonnegative view-consistency term. Mean-margin reduction requires additional shortcut assumptions; the analysis does not guarantee improved recall in arbitrary models (Theorem 3.1; Appendices A.3–A.5).

The paired results establish improved lexical recovery of edited atomic answers in the tested single-request setting. They do not establish reliable semantic correctness, arbitrary-context robustness, or continual-edit stability. Capability preservation is mixed: Table 12 also shows small Qwen2.5 decreases, qualifying the main text’s “stable or improves” wording. Removing perturbations after editing supports the claim of no method-specific inference overhead (Appendix D.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged scope:** Correlated, continually evolving edits remain future work; experiments use single-request editing (Sections 4.1, 6).
- **Author-reported sensitivity:** Effective perturbation scales depend on the editor. Larger scales substantially damage FT-M holistic recall, whereas µKE is more tolerant over its tested range (Appendix D.4; Table 13).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evaluation covers two backbones and three subsets of one benchmark suite. Perturbation and writing ablations cover only Qwen2.5 FT-M and µKE on an UnFine-UnKE subset (Sections 4.1, 4.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** COIN⋆ omits original locality mechanisms, and FABLE is excluded because it uses additional supervision. Results therefore do not establish superiority over full COIN or QA-supervised editing (Section 4.1; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—metric limits:** Lexical recall can reward correct target phrases despite incorrect surrounding claims; Table 3’s SC Atlético example illustrates this possibility. General-task F1 is not a comprehensive test of unrelated factual preservation (Appendices C.2–C.3).
- **Theoretical boundary:** Spectral contraction holds for fixed pre-RoPE states; stronger margin and attention conclusions require explicit assumptions rather than following automatically for full forward passes (Appendices A.2, A.4–A.6).

## Open research questions
- **Does FOVEATED preserve interacting edits over time?** Continual dependencies are left open in Section 6. Test sequential reinforcing and contradictory passages, measuring retention, revision, and unrelated-fact preservation.
- **Do lexical gains translate into semantically correct answers?** Evaluation uses overlap metrics, and cases contain potentially incorrect surrounding content. Add blinded semantic assessment and adversarially paraphrased atomic questions.
- **Can editing cost be reduced while preserving gains?** Table 14 shows substantial added latency. Compare sentence sampling or batched focused views at matched editing-time budgets.
- **How broadly do the perturbation design and theory apply?** Ablations and shortcut assumptions are limited. Test additional backbones and editors while measuring empirical margin changes and confidence variation across views.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark improvements; **medium** in the causal explanation and broader applicability.
