# Rethinking Token Reweighting for SFT: Suppress, Reverse, and Extrapolate Learned Features

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33463) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33463)


## One-sentence takeaway
SCALE improves math and code results by learning entropy-guided gates over a frozen LoRA update, although headline scores use benchmark-selected gate ranges and its task-improvement guarantee depends on unmeasured alignment conditions.

## Problem and motivation
Standard SFT gives stronger corrections to low-probability demonstration tokens, potentially amplifying unreliable supervision alongside useful novelty. The authors identify a distinction between changing how an update is learned and controlling how a completed update is applied: nonnegative, fixed token weights cannot reverse the direct cross-entropy logit direction, whereas signed scaling of a frozen delta makes suppression, reversal, and extrapolation explicit (§§1, 4).

This is a component-level result, not a general impossibility claim about unlearning through shared parameters, differentiated weights, or other losses (§4.1; Appendices C, H.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Analysis:** A unified policy-loss formulation characterizes nonnegative weighted cross-entropy and expresses entropy minimization as signed action-level feedback (§§3–4).
- **Method:** SCALE first trains a standard LoRA adapter, then freezes the base, output head, and adapter. Independent linear gates use each module’s detached token representation to scale its delta through an identity-centered, bounded tanh mapping (§5).
- **Calibration and theory:** Only gate parameters are trained, minimizing normalized top-100 predictive entropy on demonstration response prefixes without CE or KL. A local task-value improvement theorem requires entropy–task alignment within gate-accessible directions; the implemented stopped-gradient variant needs an additional error condition (§§5.4–5.5; Appendix E).
- **Empirical contribution:** Range sweeps, fixed-range seed experiments, and inference interventions investigate when reversal and extrapolation help math and code (§6; Appendix G).

“Features” here means module-level delta contributions, not disentangled semantic units (§4.2).

These results support useful post-SFT delta calibration in the tested settings. They do not establish universal benefits from reversal, per-benchmark code superiority, or unconditional correctness gains from lower entropy. Fixed-range seed evidence strengthens the math finding, but headline comparisons remain oracle sweep results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation stops at 7B parameters and covers only Qwen models; gate ranges require empirical selection (Appendix I).
- **Author-qualified theory:** Entropy can reinforce incorrect predictions when the semantic margin is wrong. Alignment, stopped-gradient error, and vocabulary-tail effects are not directly measured; the guarantee is local and does not prove free-running accuracy gains (Appendices E, H).
- **Representational constraint:** A scalar gate scales an entire module delta and cannot independently control every semantic component. Useful independent interventions may be inaccessible to shared gates (Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference—selection risk:** Selecting headline intervals from evaluation-benchmark maxima can inflate apparent generalization relative to a separately validated, fixed selection procedure (§6.1; Figure 3).
- **Analyst inference—limited robustness evidence:** Three-seed results cover only the smallest math backbone; comparable seed evidence is unavailable for other models and code (Table 7).
- **Cost qualification:** Reported gate-training time excludes the preceding LoRA stage; complete SCALE training requires both stages (Appendix G.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Can gate ranges be selected without evaluation-benchmark oracle sweeps?** The authors identify automatic selection as unresolved. Next experiment: choose ranges on a separate validation split, then evaluate once on held-out benchmarks.
2. **When does entropy calibration reinforce errors?** Alignment and implementation-error terms remain unmeasured. Next experiment: compare entropy and externally verified task-value directions on correct versus confidently incorrect prefixes, including complete-gradient and stopped-gradient gates.
3. **Does the benefit transfer beyond small Qwen models?** Larger models and other families are untested. Next experiment: repeat fixed-range math and code comparisons across another family and a larger backbone with matched calibration budgets.
4. **How much does top-k truncation affect outcomes?** Conditional entropy does not control full-distribution tail effects. Next experiment: compare top-100, other support sizes, and full-vocabulary entropy while measuring accuracy and retained probability mass.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported comparisons; **medium** in broader generalization and the proposed entropy-based mechanism.

Material unavailable information includes release status of code/checkpoints, gate temperature, full decoding settings, detailed baseline hyperparameter matching, inference overhead, independently validated range selection, and multi-seed results beyond the smallest math model.
