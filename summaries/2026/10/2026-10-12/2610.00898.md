# When Do Biological Reasoning Models Use Their Biological Inputs?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00898) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00898)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across six evaluated biological reasoning models, benchmark accuracy and post-training gains do not reliably establish use of biological inputs: BioReason and BioReason-Pro largely follow predictive text despite receiving informative sequence representations (§§4.1–4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Biological reasoning models combine LLMs with sequence or transcriptomic representations and biological text. Accurate answers can come from annotations alone, making benchmark performance insufficient evidence that models extract information from their biological representations. The authors address the gap between **information encoded in an input**, **its contribution to predictions**, and **its portrayal in generated reasoning** (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **An input-use evaluation framework:** perturb one biological input while fixing others; construct conflicts between inputs from different entities; probe representations before and after projection; and check traces against supplied evidence (§3).
- **A comparative empirical audit:** apply these tests to six models across genomic, protein, and single-cell tasks, including post-training checkpoints and model scales (§§4.1–4.3).
- **An auxiliary-supervision experiment:** train BioReason to identify edited bases, then measure sequence sensitivity, disease-prediction transfer, and generalization to unseen genomes (§4.4; Appendix E).

The contribution is primarily diagnostic evaluation of existing models, with a targeted training intervention rather than a new general biological reasoning architecture.

These results support the authors’ distinction between predictive representations and their performance contribution. They do **not** establish complete non-use: BioReason-Pro changes GO sets for 46.8% of proteins after shuffling despite nearly unchanged Fmax (Table S3). Nor do they establish that predictive text causes underuse; the cross-model comparison is observational (Appendix B.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** findings cover specific tasks and six models; shuffling can produce unnatural inputs; linear probes establish predictability rather than how an LLM could exploit representations (§5).
- **Author-stated:** training-text availability is confounded with architecture, task, data, and scale; no controlled intervention isolates its effect (Appendix B.5).
- **Evaluation boundary:** several conflict analyses select examples correctly handled before intervention, limiting their interpretation to those selected settings (Appendix B.2).
- **Annotation risk:** most protein-property negatives mean absent GO annotation rather than confirmed biological absence. Experimental GO NOT conflicts provide an additional check (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** overlapping genomes and predictive gene text weaken claims of general biological reasoning from the auxiliary experiment; the unseen-genome analysis directly demonstrates this concern (Tables S8–S10).
- The temporal holdout’s Fmax level is explicitly not comparable with the human-proteome score because annotation density differs (Appendix A.1.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does predictive-text dropout cause stronger sequence use?** The comparison is observational. Retrain one architecture with controlled annotation-dropout rates while fixing data and optimization (Appendix B.5).
- **Can input-sensitive rewards improve downstream predictions?** The authors propose such objectives but do not test them. Compare accuracy-only RL with rewards for correct responses to biological-input changes (§5).
- **Can edited-base supervision generalize beyond seen genomes?** Current gains concentrate on training-seen genomes. Repeat training and evaluation with genome-disjoint splits and varying edit positions (Appendix E).
- **Can rationales become both marker-relevant and prediction-consistent?** Tested prompt variants leave low overall DEG precision. Evaluate training supervision for concise marker selection alongside matched annotation-only prompts (Appendix D.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**High** confidence in the reported descriptive findings; lower confidence in causal explanations of underuse.

Material information unavailable or unclear in the supplied text includes exact uncertainty intervals for several figure-only results, aggregate evaluation compute cost, and a clear denominator reconciliation for the C2S-Scale conflict pool versus the stated annotation sample. Code availability is stated, but its contents and reproducibility were not verified.
