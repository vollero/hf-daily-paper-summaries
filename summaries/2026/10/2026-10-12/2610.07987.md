# VisionWeave: Weaving Elastic Visual Representations as a Native Capability of MLLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07987) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07987)


## One-sentence takeaway
VisionWeave learns spatially adaptive visual-token granularity, with authors reporting 43.0% average token savings and 98.9% native-performance retention on Qwen3.8-27B across eight benchmarks, plus a 2.30× serving-throughput gain on a long-video workload (§1; §3.4).

## Problem and motivation
Dense, fixed-size visual tokens make high-resolution images and long videos expensive to process. Downsampling can lose detail, while fixed-budget pruning can remove information needed for document understanding and GUI grounding. The authors identify a gap in learning fine-scale, content-adaptive representations within native-resolution MLLMs while supporting modern serving infrastructure (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Mixed-granularity representations:** A gated spatial pooler constructs coarse tokens alongside native fine tokens. Each spatial block contributes either four fine tokens or one coarse token, positioned within a shared MRoPE coordinate system (§2.2.1).
- **Learned spatial allocation:** A router probes early, middle, and final ViT features through local and global cross-attention to select each block’s granularity. An inference threshold controls compression without retraining (§2.2.2; §3.5).
- **Three-stage self-distillation:** Training first learns coarse representations, then differentiable routing, then updates the pooler and LLM using the hard-routed sequences deployed at inference (§2.3; Table 1).
- **Serving integration:** SGLang extensions support half-integer positional coordinates and variable visual-sequence layouts while retaining batching and cache management (§2.5).

The authors claim novelty in establishing this combination as a native MLLM capability through large-scale training; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority (§1).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Near-native aggregate quality | Qwen3.8-27B, default threshold 0.5, eight benchmarks: 43.0% average visual-token savings and 98.9% native-performance retention; main comparison uses 512-token image/frame budgets. | §1; §3.2; §4.1; Table 4 referenced |
| Better than fixed-budget baselines | Qwen3.8-27B: average relative performance losses of 1.08% for VisionWeave versus 11.82% for FastV† and 12.46% for VisionZip, with baselines targeting 50% savings. | §3.2 |
| Gains persist at matched dataset savings | Eight-benchmark mean scores: VisionWeave/FastV†/VisionZip = 76.73/70.10/70.33 on 27B and 72.59/72.20/71.90 on 4B. Excluding ScreenSpotV2, 27B means are 74.63/73.86/73.85. | §3.2; Appendix E.3 |
| Spatial allocation matters | Shuffling routing scores while preserving each input’s coarse-selection count lowers eight-benchmark mean scores by 2.31 points on 4B and 5.36 on 27B, at 512-token image/frame budgets. | §4.1; Table 6 referenced |
| Pooling and final training contribute | At 75% savings after Stage 1 on 4B, gated pooling beats pixel-unshuffle on all eight benchmarks. On 27B, Stage 3 raises the benchmark mean from 73.15 to 76.73, but lowers RealWorldQA by 1.18 points. | §4.1; Fig. 7; Appendix E.1 |
| Compression is adjustable | On 27B Video-MME, lowering the threshold from 0.5 to 0.25 increases savings from 54.2% to 66.0%, with a 0.37-point score loss. | §3.5; Fig. 8 |
| Practical serving gains | Versus native 27B on LongVideoBench: 2.30× end-to-end throughput; P95 TTFT/TPOT reductions of 57.1%/59.6%. Setting: two A100-80GB GPUs, BF16, TP=2, 2 FPS, 256-frame cap, 1,024 tokens/frame, client concurrency four. | §3.4; Table 5 referenced; Appendix E.4 |

These author-reported results support useful compression on the evaluated tasks and deployment setting. Matched-savings and shuffled-routing comparisons strengthen the case that spatial allocation contributes beyond token count alone. They do not establish universal superiority: task rankings vary, the 27B aggregate gap is substantially influenced by GUI grounding, and serving acceleration is measured on one long-video workload.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Training cost limits architectural exploration, including alternative routers. The method currently supports only two granularities and is introduced through post-training rather than base pre-training (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer beyond the two evaluated Qwen backbones remains unestablished (§3.1).
- **Comparison boundary:** FastV† moves selection after the vision encoder; results therefore concern this serving-compatible variant rather than the original in-LLM procedure (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Dataset-level savings matching does not match each example’s budget, and the heuristic baselines do not isolate the effect of VisionWeave’s additional training (§3.2; Appendix E.3).
- **Evidence availability:** Several tables contain only captions in the supplied retrieval. Complete per-task results, training-mixture counts, and stage-specific hyperparameters cannot be checked. No uncertainty estimates or repeated-run results are supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Would richer granularity levels improve the trade-off?** Appendix A leaves multi-scale representations unexplored. Compare two-level and multi-level models under matched training compute and visual-token budgets.
- **How much router complexity is necessary?** Alternative designs remain unexplored because of training cost. Ablate feature depths and local/global attention while measuring quality, routing overhead, and serving throughput.
- **Do serving gains generalize beyond long video?** §3.4 evaluates one workload. Benchmark images, dense documents, and GUI inputs across concurrency and output-length settings.
- **Would routing conditioned on the question improve allocation?** The described router receives visual features, without textual conditioning (§2.2.2). Compare visual-only and question-conditioned routing on multiple questions per image.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline findings are clearly described, but important underlying tables are absent from the retrieved text.
