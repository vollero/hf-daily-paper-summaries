# GATOR: Generative and Agentic 3D Object Reconstruction From Casual Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11215) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11215)


## One-sentence takeaway
GATOR combines observation-conditioned 3D generation with an asset-initialized editing agent to reconstruct textured, scene-aligned objects from sparse images, improving benchmark reconstruction while showing mixed geometric effects from agent refinement.

## Problem and motivation
Casual photographs show incomplete objects amid clutter, with irregular viewpoints and uncertain camera, depth, and segmentation estimates. Simulation and scene editing require complete geometry, appearance, and scene-relative placement—not merely plausible visible surfaces.

The authors identify two gaps: generative reconstruction leaves local associations between image, mask, and geometry evidence insufficiently explicit, while agents building assets from scratch are slow and often simplify detailed structures (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Local modality mixing:** Within each image patch, attention fuses aligned RGB, target-mask, and pointmap tokens before cross-view structure generation. Scene context remains available, while masks identify the target (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Semantic guidance throughout generation:** Category embeddings guide sparse structure; caption tokens guide geometry and PBR appearance through stage-specific adapters. The cascade builds on TRELLIS.2, with projected image conditioning following Pixal3D (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Pose-aware asset generation and agent refinement:** Observation normalization preserves orientation; rendering-based optimization refines translation and scale. GPT-6-Astra then edits the generated mesh in Blender, reviewing matched renders and reverting harmful edits (§§3.1, 3.4–3.5).
- **Training and evaluation assets:** The authors develop S3 for synthetic cluttered-scene supervision and extend the ScanNet++ evaluation protocol. Public release of these assets is not established in the supplied text (Appendices B, D.4).

## Evidence and main results

CD and ADD-SB are normalized by reference diameter. Shape metrics use similarity alignment; ADD-SB evaluates placement without object-to-reference fitting, after camera-frame registration where needed. Appearance metrics compare aligned renders under shared lighting (§C).

These results support strong reconstruction performance in the evaluated settings and useful appearance or structural repairs. They do not establish uniformly beneficial agent edits, calibrated material recovery, or general simulation readiness. The indoor prose calls RecGen the CD runner-up, but Table 3 places GPT-6-Astra closer to GATOR; the distinction requires excluding the proprietary baseline.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Missing masks or catastrophic depth errors can harm reconstruction; refinement depends on a proprietary agent and adds up to ten minutes per object (§D.7).
- **Author-stated evaluation caveats:** ScanNet++ partial scans penalize completed surfaces absent from the reference. Appearance scores measure rendered-reference agreement rather than calibrated material recovery (§§C.3, D.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Annotation-assisted view selection, masks, and category labels leave fully automatic casual-image deployment untested (§B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Sequential ablations on HANDAL cannot fully separate component interactions; separately trained ablation models also differ from the main model (§D.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons vary in available views, text, and geometry estimators. Timing excludes export/import, uses estimated generation time, and averages quality over valid outputs (§§A.4, D.1–D.2; Fig. 5).
- **Analyst inference:** One rigid-body scene with assumed masses and collision approximations provides limited evidence about physical fidelity (§D.6).

## Open research questions
- **Can refinement reliably avoid geometric regressions?** Tables 4 and 7 show mixed effects. Compare visual review alone against review augmented with automatic geometric checks.
- **How robust is reconstruction to upstream failures?** The authors acknowledge catastrophic mask/depth failures without controlled failure curves. Sweep segmentation, camera, and depth corruption independently.
- **How much does annotation assistance contribute?** Current evaluation uses annotation-supported target selection and text. Compare these inputs against fully automatic target discovery and captioning.
- **Do simulation gains generalize?** Evidence covers one meeting room. Repeat matched pre/post-refinement simulations across scenes and collision approximations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, protocols, and numerical tables, but lacks uncertainty estimates, repeated-agent-run variability, training compute and duration, API costs, and confirmed code/checkpoint/data releases. Several narrative summaries overstate or simplify the accompanying tables.
