# Surfing the Activation Wave: Analyzing the Information and Geometry of LLM Hidden States for Speculative Decoding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12939) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12939)


## One-sentence takeaway
Entropy-guided hidden-layer selection improves speculative token acceptance over EAGLE3’s fixed layers on Qwen3-4B, but falls slightly below that baseline on Llama-3.1-8B, showing that its benefit depends on the target model.

## Problem and motivation
Speculative draft models condition on selected target-model hidden states, yet common selections use fixed layer positions. Exhaustive selection is combinatorial, and individual-layer analyses do not establish which combinations provide complementary information. The authors seek a practical selection criterion that balances informativeness and redundancy (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **ELSI:** Beam search scores layer sets using Shannon matrix entropy of concatenated hidden states, computed from their normalized squared singular values (§§3.1, 4.3; Algorithm 1).
- **ELSI-C:** An extension restricts expansion candidates using conditional CKA transition scores, then prunes candidates by entropy (§4.4; Algorithm 2).
- **Calibration filtering:** Retains tokens with the highest predictive entropy before constructing averaged layer representatives. Experiments retain the top 30%, use block size 32, and calibrate on 200 ShareGPT samples (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Representation diagnostics and downstream validation:** Depth-wise entropy, concatenation scores, and CKA compartments motivate selection; separately trained EAGLE-style drafts test the selected sets (§§5.1–5.6).

These contributions adapt entropy and CKA analysis to joint layer selection; the paper does not introduce those underlying measures.

The tables establish model-dependent improvements in accepted tokens, not measured throughput gains. Higher matrix entropy does not guarantee higher acceptance: Llama’s selected triples score higher on entropy yet trail EAGLE3. The authors attribute Llama’s smaller selection headroom to broad high-CKA regions; this is an interpretation rather than a demonstrated causal mechanism (§5.6; Figure 5).

The prose’s claim that the low-entropy control consistently has the lowest acceptance conflicts with Table 2’s aggregate results, where EAGLE2 is lower.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers two target models and EAGLE-style conditioning. Broader models, conditioning strategies, and applications remain future work (§6).
- **Author-stated deployment gap:** Smaller layer sets might improve throughput through a smaller projection, but edge-device experiments are deferred (§5.6.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No reported repeated-run uncertainty or significance tests establish whether the modest acceptance differences are robust (Tables 2–3).
- **Analyst inference:** Filtering reduces statistic variance, but no downstream filtered-versus-unfiltered draft comparison isolates its acceptance benefit. Appendix A compares CKA boundaries instead.
- **Analyst inference:** CKA-guided expansion has no demonstrated aggregate acceptance advantage over entropy-only search (Tables 2–3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Full training settings, selection runtime, and several search hyperparameter values are unavailable. Algorithm 2 also leaves the layer-zero transition-score boundary case unspecified.

## Open research questions
- **Does improved acceptance produce net decoding speedups?** Throughput is inferred rather than measured. Next experiment: benchmark latency and tokens per second, including projection overhead, on GPU and edge hardware.
- **When does entropy reliably rank useful layer sets?** Llama’s higher-entropy triples do not beat EAGLE3. Next experiment: train drafts for a broader sampled set of layer combinations and measure entropy–acceptance association across models.
- **Does token filtering improve downstream performance?** The paper establishes variance reduction without isolating acceptance gains. Next experiment: compare filtered and unfiltered calibration with matched training, multiple calibration samples, and retention fractions.
- **Can CKA improve selection under different budgets?** ELSI-C does not outperform ELSI on reported aggregate results. Next experiment: compare both selectors at matched search cost across candidate-pool sizes and larger layer sets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported table comparisons and algorithm descriptions; **medium** for the proposed geometric explanation and practical speed implications.

Material missing information includes measured throughput, selection cost, repeated-run variability, complete training/search settings, downstream filtering ablations, and an established code or checkpoint release.
