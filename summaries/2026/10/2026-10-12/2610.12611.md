# SoftwareGym: Scaling Computer-Use Agent Training with Diverse Synthesized Software Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12611) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12611)


## One-sentence takeaway
SoftwareGym synthesizes and repairs diverse desktop applications whose trajectories improve Qwen3.5-9B’s Pass@3 from 41.39% to 56.97% on the paper’s 244-task OSWorld evaluation without OSWorld training data (§4.1–4.2; Table 8).

## Problem and motivation
Computer-use agent training needs diverse, executable environments that support reliable multi-step workflows. The authors argue that integrating existing applications restricts coverage, while browser-hosted synthesis limits interaction depth; their identified gap is jointly achieving diversity, functional reliability, and scalable construction (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Environment synthesis:** A multi-agent pipeline expands software specifications into structurally distinct Avalonia interfaces, implements standalone applications, and combines source audits with fixed GUI tests and iterative repair. Layout-distance gates discourage duplicate interfaces (§3.1–3.2; Appendix B).
- **Verified task generation:** Tasks combine only capabilities supported by passed GUI checkpoints, with controlled difficulty, explicit initial states, and verifiable outcomes (§3.3).
- **Assets and training evidence:** The authors report releasing 182 applications across twelve categories and constructing SoftwareGym-Bench. They train Qwen3.5-9B through multimodal supervised fine-tuning on synthesized-software trajectories and evaluate transfer to native applications (§1; §4.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synthesized supervision transfers to OSWorld | Qwen3.5-9B base → SoftwareGym-only SFT: **41.39% → 56.97% Pass@3**, a reported **15.58-point** gain; 244 tasks across eight domains, three rollouts per task, 100-step budget, no OSWorld training data. | §4.1–4.2; Table 8 |
| In-domain benchmark performance improves | On 120 SoftwareGym-Bench tasks, base → SFT: **25.83% → 40.00% Pass@3**; EvoCUA-32B scores **35.00%**, while GPT-5.4 scores **74.17%**, under the same stated rollout protocol. | §4.1; Table 9 |
| Repair improves tested reliability | Across the 182-application library, mean GUI task pass rate rises **54.9% → 81.1% → 91.5%** over three versions, using fixed first-version tests and up to 50 interaction steps per case. | §4.1–4.2; Figure 6 |
| GUI feedback outperforms source-only repair | Paired, category-balanced **36-application** ablation: shared **50.4%** initial pass rate; final GUI-guided **83.1%** versus source-only **48.8%**. Reported advantage: **34.3 points**, **95% CI 28.2–41.0**. | Appendix C.2; Figure 7 |
| Explicit style design increases structural diversity | Ten blueprints per category per method across twelve categories: macro-average normalized tree-edit diversity, on a **0–100 scale**, increases **4.9 → 83.6** versus Simple Design. | Appendix B; Appendix C.1 |
| Function coverage is broad but incomplete | Across **121 applications in eight aligned categories**, category-union GUI evidence covers all **49 major modules** and **78.8%** of concrete native functions on average; module coverage needs only one verified child function. | §4.2; Figure 4; Appendix B |
| Training sources show complementary performance | SoftwareGym-only / OSWorld-only / combined SFT achieve **56.97% / 61.48% / 58.61% Pass@3** on OSWorld and **40.0% / 33.3% / 47.5%** on SoftwareGym-Bench. Single-source corpora are size-matched before length filtering; combined training concatenates both. | Appendix C.3 |
| Synthesis has measurable resource costs | Sample of **20 applications**: mean **237.3 minutes**, **14.8 million tokens**, **1,718 model calls**, and estimated **$70.76 per application**. | §4.2; Figure 5 |

These results support transfer under the reported protocol and the usefulness of GUI feedback for repair. They do not isolate interface diversity as the cause of downstream gains, establish universal application reliability, or substantiate savings against manual development with a quantified manual baseline in the supplied text. Combined-source gains also accompany a larger training corpus.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text has no explicit limitations section. Relevant boundaries and **analyst inferences** are:

- **Test-relative reliability:** Fixed generated tests support measured functionality, but may miss untested failures; task synthesis explicitly excludes unsupported capabilities (§3.2–3.3).
- **Limited generalization scope:** Transfer evidence covers eight OSWorld domains and one trained base model. Broader platform and model transfer remain unestablished (§4.1; Appendix C.3).
- **Benchmark separation is unclear:** Semantic-group splitting is described, but the text does not establish application-level separation between training and SoftwareGym-Bench (Appendix E.2).
- **Proxy measures:** Structural diversity measures component trees; visual fidelity checks design tokens rather than perceptual similarity. Neither directly measures human-perceived realism (Appendix B).
- **Selection and comparison risks:** Trajectories rely on model judges, source-specific selection, and screenshot sampling. Combined-source training doubles the available corpus, complicating attribution to complementarity alone (Appendices C.3, E).

## Open research questions
- **Does structural diversity itself improve transfer?** The paper measures blueprint diversity without a corresponding training ablation. Next: train on equally sized, equally reliable low- and high-diversity libraries.
- **How robust are repaired applications to unseen workflows?** Reliability uses fixed tests. Next: evaluate independent held-out tests and longer workflow compositions after repair.
- **Do mixed sources help beyond additional data volume?** Combined training uses both complete corpora. Next: compare mixed and single-source training at matched example counts and compute.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main numerical findings and core protocols are explicit, but causal attribution and benchmark separation remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes release locations and artifact accessibility, the trajectory-collection teacher and collection budget, application-level benchmark overlap, a quantified manual-development comparator, and downstream training-seed uncertainty. Several referenced tables appear only as captions, although important results are recoverable from prose and Tables 8–9.
