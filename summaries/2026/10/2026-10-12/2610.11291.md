# When Do We Need On-Policy Distillation? Distilling on Offline Student Rollouts Is Often Better

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11291) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11291)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the tested math-distillation settings, reusing initial-student rollouts usually beats continually regenerated rollouts, with larger benefits at lower teacher–student token overlap, provided the initial student produces meaningful trajectories (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) gives teacher supervision on responses generated by the current student, addressing training–inference distribution mismatch. However, repeatedly regenerated responses can lengthen and yield unreliable supervision. The authors ask whether continual sampling helps arbitrary teacher–student pairs, rather than assuming it is always necessary (§1).

The identified gap is a systematic comparison across differently aligned model pairs, including the accuracy and computational costs of freezing student rollouts.

## Approach and contributions
- **Semi-OPD:** Generate responses once from the initial student, cache teacher token log-probabilities, and train on that fixed dataset. Current-student log-probabilities and per-token advantages are recomputed at every update. It retains OPD’s sampled-token loss but changes the rollout distribution (§2.2).
- **Comparative study:** Evaluate across 17 teacher–student pairs spanning three model families, thinking and non-thinking modes, and models ranging from 1.5B to 235B parameters (§2.3; Table 3).
- **Selection evidence:** Relate relative performance to initial top-64 token overlap, a metric adopted from prior work rather than newly invented here (§§2.2–3).
- **Mechanistic investigation:** Ablate rollout source, trajectory coverage, rollout length, distillation objective, and initialization. The authors hypothesize that effective supervision requires balancing compatibility with both student and teacher distributions (§§4–5).

Relative to Lightning OPD, the proposed recipe omits teacher-rollout SFT when starting from sufficiently capable instruction-tuned students; the paper’s contribution also includes studying when frozen rollouts improve accuracy (§2.2).

These results support Semi-OPD as a strong baseline for the tested settings. They do not establish a universal overlap threshold or prove that teacher compatibility causes the observed gains. The authors’ claim that OPD is useful *only* at high overlap is stronger than the demonstrated correlation.

The headline **+13.6% accuracy** is not clearly reconciled with the tables: Table 1 implies a 13.6-point checkpoint-averaged difference, whereas Table 3’s largest last-step gain is 13.1 avg@32 points. Learning-curve gains, checkpoint averages, and final-step gains should therefore remain distinct.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundary:** Semi-OPD requires sufficiently good initial rollouts; the base-student experiment shows that it can underperform OPD (§5, Fig. 11).
- **Author-stated measurement caveat:** Self-correction word density measures a reflective or hedging style, not verified reasoning corrections (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** All training uses math prompts; coding transfer is illustrated for one pair. Broad task or modality generalization remains unestablished (§2.3; Fig. 10).
- **Analyst inference—comparison:** Some Table 1 baselines are imported from prior work. Training caps also differ between comparisons, including the largest cost advantage, so that advantage cannot be attributed solely to caching (Appendix A; Tables 1 and 4).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Longer responses and positively supervised-token fractions support the hypothesis but do not independently establish teacher-distribution drift as the causal mechanism (§4; Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Can overlap reliably select a method on unseen pairs?** The paper provides correlation without a validated decision threshold. Next experiment: fit a selection rule on some pairs and test it on held-out families and domains (§3).
- **When should a rollout cache be refreshed?** Fixed caching works well, but OPD can help aligned pairs. Next experiment: compare periodic and overlap-triggered refresh schedules against both endpoints (§§3–5).
- **What initial competence is sufficient?** The base-versus-instruction-tuned comparison establishes a boundary without quantifying it. Next experiment: vary initialization quality under a fixed teacher and measure rollout quality against distillation gains (§5).
- **Is the observed OPD equilibrium robust?** Warm-start experiments suggest convergence toward similar performance but leave its characterization open. Next experiment: vary rollout caps and update settings across additional pairs (§5, Figs. 8–9).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and principal comparisons, but lacks numerical plot data for most ablations, comprehensive uncertainty estimates, a validated overlap threshold, and an explicit code/model/cache release. Appendix E contains only its heading, so its referenced qualitative examples are unavailable. Several figure references are missing or inconsistent, including the positive-token discussion’s reference to Fig. 7 despite Fig. 6’s matching caption.
