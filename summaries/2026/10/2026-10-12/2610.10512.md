# Video-Conditioned Generative Joint 2D-3D Hand Motion Recovery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10512) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10512)


## One-sentence takeaway
JoHan jointly generates local 2D and 3D hand motion from monocular video, improving reported pose accuracy, acceleration error, and hand-recovery speed while remaining slightly behind leading methods in world-space trajectory accuracy.

## Problem and motivation
Occlusion and motion blur make frame-wise hand estimates unreliable and temporally inconsistent. The authors identify two gaps: existing generative recovery methods often inherit errors from intermediate pose estimates, and the temporal dynamics of their 2D conditioning trajectories receive limited attention. JoHan instead learns paired motion directly from video and uses generated 2D geometry to constrain ambiguous 3D reconstruction (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Video-conditioned joint generation:** Separate spatiotemporal generators evolve normalized 2D joints and local MANO pose/shape through a shared flow-matching process. Motion generators are trained from scratch; the DINOv3 visual backbone is pretrained and fine-tuned (§§2.1–2.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Directional geometric coupling:** At every flow step, predicted 2D coordinates and visibility guide corresponding 3D pose tokens. Reprojection supervision couples both branches geometrically (§2.2).
- **Geometric global recovery:** Refined, Kalman-smoothed detector boxes restore image-space coordinates; generated 2D–3D correspondences and camera intrinsics feed PnP to recover camera-space orientation and translation (§2.3; Appendix A.5).

The claimed novelty is this combination of joint video-conditioned motion generation and evolving 2D guidance, rather than flow matching, MANO, or PnP individually.

These results support improved local reconstruction and lower acceleration error in the evaluated settings. Ground-truth-box results help separate temporal gains from detector-box preprocessing. They do not establish superior world-space tracking, calibrated uncertainty, or generalization beyond the supplied benchmarks. Acceleration error measures agreement with ground-truth dynamics, rather than smoothness alone.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** World-space trajectory errors remain slightly behind leading results; JoHan combines camera-relative recovery with externally estimated camera motion instead of explicitly learning world-space motion (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Global accuracy remains sensitive to box localization and camera calibration. The box-refinement ablation demonstrates substantial dependence on denormalization quality (Appendix A.5).
- **Evaluation scope:** Main HOT3D results cover filtered right-hand frames from held-out Aria recordings, not the official test split or all device streams (Appendix A.4).
- **Comparison risk:** Some baselines use published results, others are reproduced; efficiency comparisons also use different visual-backbone sizes (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Evidence availability:** Tables 1–7 contain captions but no retrieved row bodies, and some prose cross-references misidentify the HOT3D table. Numerical analysis therefore relies on the surrounding text.
- **Unmeasured capability:** The generative formulation supports plausible alternatives in principle, but supplied results do not quantify sample diversity or uncertainty calibration.

## Open research questions
- **Can world-space recovery improve without losing temporal accuracy?** Trajectory errors remain worse despite local gains. Compare geometric recovery with a learned global-motion module under identical estimated and ground-truth camera trajectories.
- **Does generation capture useful ambiguity?** Diversity and calibration are unreported. Evaluate repeated samples under controlled occlusion using coverage, diversity, and pose-error measurements.
- **How robust is global positioning to degraded inputs?** Box refinement matters, but systematic sensitivity is unreported. Perturb boxes and intrinsics independently and measure camera-space errors.
- **How much efficiency comes from the backbone?** Compared systems use different encoder sizes. Repeat timing and accuracy comparisons with matched encoders and hardware.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method details and prose-reported results are substantial, but complete numerical tables are unavailable. Other material gaps include uncertainty estimates, sampling variability, controlled occlusion robustness, matched-backbone timing, exact per-baseline frame coverage, training wall-clock cost, and confirmation of released code or weights.
