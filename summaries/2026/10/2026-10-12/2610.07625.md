# Stateless Language Agents: Scaling Long-Horizon Automated Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07625) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07625)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that harness-owned research state, fresh role-specific contexts, and explicit experiment assignments let SLA outperform three research frameworks at full token budgets, although most comparisons are single runs and statelessness itself is not directly ablated (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

Long-running coding agents can spend increasing inference on replaying history, duplicating experiments, or repeatedly deciding to stop. The authors identify two coupled problems: retaining useful evidence without overwhelming context, and converting accumulated results into coordinated next experiments (§§1–2).

They argue that short evaluations and benchmarks that saturate early obscure these failures and can misrank systems whose design benefits accumulate over longer searches (§2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Harness-owned state with reconstructed contexts.** The harness retains candidates, outcomes, and experiment records. Every Advisor epoch and Worker trial starts a fresh session. Advisors receive summarized global evidence; Workers receive assignments, retained code, and local feedback (§3.1).
- **Explicit experiment coordination.** A stateless Advisor assigns distinct experiments to parallel Workers. Local candidates can persist below the global best, allowing alternative directions to mature; redirected Workers restart from the global best (§3.2).
- **Enforced implementation and controlled evaluation.** Filesystem restrictions, session clearing, and protected external evaluators enforce the architecture. Checkpoints support comparisons from identical research states, while evaluation measures quality and target-reaching efficiency across cumulative token budgets (§3.3; §§4.1–4.3; Appendix B).

The contribution is a research orchestration framework and its evaluation, rather than a newly trained model or benchmark.

These results support SLA’s measured advantages in the tested executable-search settings and show that evaluation horizon can change rankings. They do not establish universal superiority, statistical reliability across most tasks, or a causal benefit from statelessness alone: all component ablations retain stateless agents. Trace examples illustrate coordination mechanisms but do not quantify their prevalence or contribution (§5; Appendix D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated replication limits:** Most configurations have one run because long searches are expensive; replication covers Codex kernel comparisons and checkpoint continuations (§§4.1, 5).
- **Author-stated resource limits:** Main comparisons match tokens, and cost estimates exclude experiment execution and evaluation compute (§5).
- **Author-stated causal gap:** Ablations vary context and assignments, not conversation persistence (§5).
- **Author-stated scope:** Vague objectives and slow-to-evaluate goals remain untested; all evaluated tasks have executable evaluators (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Shared checkpoints isolate continuation effects from an SLA-generated state, but may not predict variants’ behavior from initialization. Only Worker isolation additionally receives a full-run ablation (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline adaptations and differing orchestration defaults limit attribution to any single design choice. EvoX is adapted from direct calls to coding agents; SwarmResearch retains adaptive fan-out (Appendix B.2).
- **Author-stated safety boundary:** Harness-owned state can still contain evaluator-gaming code; conversation resets do not remove this risk (§5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does resetting conversations independently improve search?** Existing ablations keep all agents stateless (§5). Compare persistent and reset sessions while holding reconstructed evidence, assignments, and budgets fixed.
2. **How robust are the broader task advantages?** Most task/configuration results are single runs (§4.1). Replicate FrontierSWE, SOL-ExecBench, and Structured-LWE comparisons with uncertainty estimates.
3. **What limits wider Worker pools?** The authors identify Advisor decision quality and fewer trials per lane as competing explanations (§5). Compare one versus multiple Advisors while controlling Worker width and trials per direction.
4. **Does SLA remain efficient with expensive or delayed feedback?** Such objectives are untested, and evaluation compute is excluded (§5). Measure quality against total cost and elapsed time while varying evaluator expense and latency.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported measurements clearly, but broader conclusions face limited replication and incomplete causal isolation.

Material missing information includes actual prompt contents—Appendix E contains only a heading—exact pinned commit identifiers, complete task resource/time limits, and confirmation that code, checkpoints, and full run logs are released. No direct stateless-versus-stateful ablation or total cost accounting including evaluator compute is reported.
