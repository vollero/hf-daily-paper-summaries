# Encore: Few-Shot Agentic Discovery of Manipulation Strategies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37359) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37359)


## One-sentence takeaway
ENCORE uses demonstrations as evidence for an iterative coding agent, accelerating manipulation-strategy discovery and achieving 96.3% sealed LIBERO-PRO perturbation success, although its demonstration-free counterpart reaches 94.9% (§4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Robot instructions often omit grasp geometry, contact order, and the intended final configuration. The authors argue that few demonstrations can reveal these details, but raw trajectories overwhelm coding agents while predefined summaries may discard decisive cues. Their proposed gap is a demonstration interface that preserves inspectable evidence for iterative policy construction (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Demonstration distiller:** Deterministically packages multi-view keyframes, gripper events, state trajectories, and actions without semantic strategy labels. Hardware packs also include event-centered frame strips (§3.1).
- **Policy-development harness:** A fresh agent inspects the pack, writes and repairs code through a fixed perception/action API, then freezes a program for evaluation on disjoint states. Tasks share no code (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix D).
- **Controlled empirical comparison:** Demonstration-count comparisons, images-only experiments, and case studies examine when evidence accelerates search, supplies missing goals, or biases discovery (§4; Appendices A–C).

The contribution is the evidence packaging and development workflow; code-based robot policies and iterative agent debugging are acknowledged predecessors (§2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Full packs often help contact-sensitive tasks, but not universally | RoboDojo stacking: full pack 43/50, images-only 0/50, K=0 10/50; swapping: 47/50, 15/50, 38/50. Six contact-precise tasks fail in every arm. | §4.5; Table 2 |
| Hardware feasibility demonstrated on two tasks | Five demonstrations per task; frozen handover and cup-inversion programs each succeed in 9/10 operator-varied, human-judged sealed trials. | §4.6 |
| Fewer revisions do not yield comparable time savings | Median across 60 perturbation cells: K=3 versus K=0 uses 4 versus 6 versions, 46.5 versus 53 development episodes, and 0.51 versus 0.55 agent hours. | Appendix B |
| Verification ablation supports iteration under an earlier protocol | Nine goal-suite tasks: full system 440/450 versus 117/450 without verification; both arms used an earlier brief containing a shared note file. | Appendix C; Table 5 |

The clean comparisons support the authors’ interpretation that demonstrations guide search, with larger effects on early discovery than final LIBERO-PRO success. They also show task-specific benefits and harms. The ASPIRE comparison evaluates different complete harnesses, so its margin cannot be attributed solely to demonstration distillation.

The statement “without demonstrations, no program succeeds” applies to the first RoboDojo group discussed in §4.5, not the entire benchmark: Table 2 reports demonstration-free successes on several other tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported:** Demonstrations can terminate search prematurely or transfer unsuitable behavior; the plate failure and tic-tac-toe pauses illustrate this (§§4.3, 4.5).
- **Author-reported:** Six contact-precise RoboDojo tasks fail across all arms, marking a capability limit (§4.5).
- **Protocol qualification:** The authors reject an earlier, larger demonstration advantage after removing a shared note file. Verification and fixture ablations retain that earlier protocol (§4.4; Appendices C–D).
- **Analyst inference:** One agent-development run per cell provides limited evidence about variability in policy discovery; many sealed episodes test the resulting program, not repeated independent synthesis (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Two hardware tasks with small, operator-varied evaluation bands provide limited evidence of broader hardware generalization (§4.6).
- **Internal ambiguity:** The protocol says benchmark success is unavailable during development, but Appendix F and Appendix G comments describe benchmark rejection or false outcomes during drawer development. The supplied text does not reconcile these accounts.
- **Runtime ambiguity:** The paper claims deployed programs make no model calls, yet the API includes VQA and the hardware policy explicitly queries a VLM. Whether “no model calls” means no coding-agent calls is unclear (§1; Table 1; Figure 6).

## Open research questions
- **How stable is the demonstration benefit across agent runs?** Each cell is developed once. Repeat independent synthesis on matched cells and report distributions of sealed success and discovery cost.
- **Which pack channels drive strategy discovery?** Images-only comparisons remove several channels together. Separately remove actions, poses, gripper readings, and event selection under the clean protocol.
- **Can agents avoid demonstration-induced premature stopping?** The plate cell shows harmful search bias. Test a matched-budget policy that reserves exploration attempts for strategies inconsistent with demonstrations.
- **Does hardware transfer extend to precise-contact failures?** Hardware evaluation covers only handover and inversion. Run controlled demonstration-count comparisons on additional insertion and articulated-object tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main numerical outcomes are well documented, but interpretation is limited by protocol ambiguities and single-run policy development.

Material information unavailable or unclear includes independent synthesis variance, exact per-episode API budgets, complete hardware development budgets and failure analysis, detailed human-judging criteria, the scope of runtime model calls, reconciliation of development success-feedback references, and verified completeness of the advertised release.
