# Recurrent Harness for Agentic Continual Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12668) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12668)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReHarness revises previously retrieved reflections using downstream feedback while grounding changes in their original trajectories, achieving the highest reported overall normalized gain across three continual-learning benchmarks and three fixed LLM backbones (§§3.3, 4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Agents operating over changing task streams must adapt without losing useful earlier experience. The authors identify a gap in existing harnesses: accumulated memories and reflections often persist without correction based on their later effectiveness, while other optimization methods require replay or validation data unavailable in strictly online settings. Consequently, overgeneralized or outdated guidance can repeatedly mislead an agent (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Backward revision of retrieved experience:** After each instance, a revision agent revisits each retrieved reflection using its original trajectory and the current outcome. New content must be supported by the original trajectory; current experience supplies feedback rather than new facts (§3.3, Equation 2).
- **A recurrent read–revise–write loop:** The decision agent retrieves reflections, the revision agent updates them, and the memory agent writes a new reflection using revised reflections as demonstrations. Model parameters remain fixed, and updated state assists subsequent instances without environment replay (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Empirical evaluation and mechanism analysis:** The authors compare against raw-history, memory, reflection, and online prompt-optimization baselines, then examine grounding, revision ablations, distribution-shift behavior, and resource consumption (§§4–5). These are evaluation contributions; no new benchmark is introduced.

## Evidence and main results

Normalized gain is improvement over the stateless backbone divided by the remaining reward headroom: `(method reward − stateless reward) / (maximum reward − stateless reward)` (§4.1). All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest overall performance | Overall normalized gain **0.240**; first or tied in **8 of 9** benchmark–backbone combinations across CL-Bench, MemoryBench, and MemoryArena with Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, and Gemini 3.7 Flash. Experiments use three independently sampled rollouts. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 referenced, cells unavailable |
| Significant aggregate advantages | Two-sided paired Wilcoxon tests over seeded task streams, with Bonferroni correction: adjusted **p < 0.05** against every baseline; **0.039** versus Top-K Raw and **0.043** versus Full Raw. | §4.2 |
| Fewer tokens, mixed monetary savings | One rollout across all CL-Bench tasks with Claude: ReHarness **≈41.57M** total tokens versus **≈52.66M** Top-K Raw and **≈142.12M** Full Raw. With caching and the stated pricing, costs were **≈$254**, **≈$85**, and **≈$314**, respectively; normalized gains were **0.217**, **0.165**, and **0.191**. | §4.2, Token Efficiency |
| Mostly source-grounded revisions | GPT-5.6-Sol claim evaluation on CL-Bench across all three backbones: **>95%** source-grounded claims, **0.8–2.1%** leakage, and **0.5–2.6%** unsupported claims. | §5.1; Table 2 referenced |
| Revision benefits future instances | On CL-Bench with Claude, ReHarness exceeds the no-revision variant on every task. Same-instance counterfactual replay improves further but violates the online evaluation protocol. Exact task gains are unavailable. | §5.2; Table 3 referenced |
| Stronger shift behavior | Highest average stability gain and highest plasticity gain for every backbone on CL-Bench. Stability concerns the first post-shift instance; plasticity concerns later instances before the next shift. Exact values are unavailable. | §5.4; Table 4 referenced |
| Revised demonstrations help new reflections | Removing revised-reflection demonstrations lowers CL-Bench normalized gain with Claude from **0.217 to 0.191**. | §5.5 |

The evidence supports improved aggregate online performance in the evaluated settings, with ablations supporting a role for backward revision. It does not establish superiority on every task or combination. Grounding is imperfect, and the counterfactual replay analysis demonstrates retrospective usefulness rather than an additional online result. Several tables are supplied only as captions, preventing independent inspection of their detailed values.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Noisy poker rewards weaken the feedback needed for useful revision; gains there are marginal. Retaining raw source trajectories also adds storage overhead (§§4.2, 6).
- **Author-discussed:** Larger retrieval/revision sets yield diminishing returns and eventually lower gains; the authors suggest unnecessary revision and potential reflection drift (§5.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Grounding compliance is supported by an LLM judge rather than demonstrated as a guarantee; reported leakage and unsupported claims remain nonzero (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Resource conclusions have limited scope because token and cost accounting covers one Claude rollout on CL-Bench. Fewer tokens do not imply lower cost than Top-K Raw (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The detailed revision ablations and retrieval-size sweep use CL-Bench with Claude, leaving their generality across other benchmarks and backbones unestablished (§§5.2, 5.5).
- **Comparison boundary:** GEPA-Online replaces GEPA’s validation-based search with sequential updates, so the comparison concerns this streaming adaptation (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can revision withstand noisy feedback?** Poker exposes weak reward-to-strategy credit assignment (§4.2). A next experiment could compare ordinary revision with confidence-gated revision under controlled reward noise.
- **Can source storage be reduced safely?** Raw trajectories remain necessary in the current design (§6). Compare full trajectories with compressed evidence records, measuring storage, grounding, and normalized gain.
- **Do the component gains generalize?** Detailed ablations are concentrated on CL-Bench with Claude (§§5.2, 5.5). Repeat revision and demonstration ablations across every evaluated benchmark–backbone combination.
- **Does grounding persist over much longer streams?** The reported grounding audit does not establish unlimited repeated-revision reliability (§5.1). Extend streams and track claim leakage, unsupported content, and reward as individual reflections accumulate revisions.
