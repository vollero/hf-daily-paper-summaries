# Humanity&#x27;s Sixth Sense: Benchmarking Intuitive Visual Reasoning in Multimodal Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08966) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08966)


## One-sentence takeaway

HSS tests implicit visual inference through human-vetted, open-ended image and video questions, with the strongest evaluated model achieving 53.6% pass@1 versus humans’ 93.1% (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

The authors identify a gap between recognizing visible content and inferring unstated causes, physical constraints, intentions, or consequences. They argue that existing benchmarks emphasize explicit perception, specialized problem-solving, or isolated reasoning domains rather than everyday visual intuition (§1). This capability matters for agents interpreting human environments, although HSS evaluates question answering rather than deployment behavior.

## Approach and contributions

- **Taxonomy:** Four domains—temporal/causal, physical/spatial, social, and abstract/contextual inference—divided into eleven subdomains (§2.2; Table 4).
- **Benchmark and curation:** HSS contains 522 tasks, comprising 288 images and 234 videos with 723 atomic rubric criteria. Human authors create questions, references, and rubrics; admission requires approval through three independent review rounds. Only 15.1% of initially authored tasks survive (§2.4).
- **Evaluation and diagnostics:** Free-form responses pass only when every mandatory criterion is satisfied. The study compares multimodal models and humans, audits alternative judges, and examines reasoning effort, frame budgets, and agentic inspection (§§3, C.4). Agentic harnesses are diagnostic interventions, explicitly not a new method contribution (Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large human–model gap | On 522 tasks, human pass rate is 93.1%; GPT-6-astra at maximum effort achieves 53.6% ±4.1, where ± denotes a 95% task-bootstrap interval half-width. Model pass@1 averages repeated attempts per task. | Table 1; §C.4 |
| Visual evidence is necessary | Removing media from GPT-6-astra at maximum effort lowers pass@1 from 53.6% to 6.6% on the same 522 tasks. | Table 1; §C.5 |
| Social inference is especially weak | Social understanding is the lowest-scoring domain for 21 of 25 models: mean accuracy 24.4%, versus 34.1% across the other domains. | §3.1 |
| Failure labels emphasize perception and latent inference | Among 8,573 failed first attempts from 24 models, 53% are labeled perception failures and 41% latent-inference failures. Labels come from Claude-Opus-5. | §3.2; Appendix D |
| Agentic inspection helps but leaves substantial errors | On a 388-task subset, Claude Code improves pass@1 by 16.5–20.5 percentage points and Codex by 4.9–7.5; the best setup reaches 59.3%. | §3.3; Appendix F |
| More reasoning is not uniformly beneficial | In a single-attempt sweep over HSS, GPT-6-astra retrodiction accuracy falls from 64% at high effort to 50% at xhigh effort. | §3.4; Table 10; §C.4 |
| Denser video sampling helps, with uncertain later gains | On 234 video tasks, GPT-6-astra gains 17.9 percentage points from a 10-frame to 500-frame cap; every budget spans the full clip. Later gains are poorly resolved by the reported intervals. | §3.4; Appendix H |
| Alternative judges preserve ordering | Regrading five models with three judges yields pairwise Spearman correlation 1.0 and item agreement of 96.2–97.5%. | Appendix I; Table 12 |

These results establish a substantial gap on HSS’s curated tasks and show that the tested interventions do not eliminate it. **Analyst inference:** They do not establish that visual intuition is a distinct cognitive mechanism in models, or that future compute scaling cannot close the gap. Automated failure labels describe inferred error categories rather than independently verified causes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Audio and transcripts are excluded; automated judging can introduce bias; public-web media may have appeared in training (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Social judgments are culturally situated. Reviewer consensus measures agreement with annotators rather than objective mental states or universal interpretation (ethics statement).
- **Analyst inference:** HSS operationalizes “intuition” through task selection and answer accuracy; no timed human evaluation is reported, so rapid, automatic processing is not directly established (§1; §3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Different native-video pathways, frame caps, and provider defaults complicate attributing leaderboard differences solely to model capability (§C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Failure labeling uses answers and references without reported media inspection or a human audit, limiting causal interpretation (Appendix D).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Agentic summaries use 388 tasks, while paired failure tables use 387 without explaining the difference. Judge agreement is summarized as κ≥0.92 in §3.4 but ranges down to 0.914 in Appendix I.

## Open research questions

- **Does HSS accuracy track rapid human intuition?** Timing is not reported. A next experiment could compare timed human responses and models under explicit latency budgets.
- **How culturally stable are social answers?** The ethics statement cautions against universalizing annotator consensus. Independent, demographically diverse panels could re-answer and re-review social items.
- **What repairs persistent spatial-depth errors?** Agentic inspection barely changes the corresponding failure count (Figure 4). Compare ordinary inspection with explicit geometric reconstruction under matched resource budgets.
- **Are failure categories reliable?** They are assigned automatically (Appendix D). Human reviewers with media access could audit a stratified sample and measure agreement.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central findings, but several referenced tables lack their numerical bodies and figure examples are not textually available. Missing material includes detailed agentic configurations and paired scores, frame-budget cells, full grading prompts, annotator demographics and task allocation, human response timing, and a human audit of failure labels. The text also mixes promised and stated code-release language, so release status is not established.
