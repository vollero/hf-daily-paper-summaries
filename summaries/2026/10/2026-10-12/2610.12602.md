# Self-Preservation in Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12602) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12602)


## One-sentence takeaway
Generated replay from supplied Task Banks improves retention on excluded benchmarks during short Qwen fine-tuning updates, while incoming-aware target selection helps only in the tested 4B setting and fixed-budget replay reduces new-task gains (§§3.2.2–3.2.5).

## Problem and motivation
Fine-tuning can degrade prior capabilities, but available preservation examples represent only part of a model’s knowledge. The authors ask whether organizing and selecting replay targets can protect capabilities beyond that partial inventory. Their identified gap is between generating more examples for known targets and choosing targets that provide broader protection (§§1–2.1).

Here, “self-preservation” means retaining task performance during adaptation; the experiments concern replay generation and target selection.

## Approach and contributions
- **Task Bank interface:** Entries contain task descriptions and optional demonstrations or prompt sources. A selector chooses entries, a frozen pre-update model generates replay, and the learner receives supervised fine-tuning on incoming and replay data. The main studies generate questions and answers; the fixed-allocation study generates answers to supplied questions (§2.2; Appendices A.7, C.1–C.3).
- **Composition and selection experiments:** The authors compare partial versus combined banks, and random versus bank-only versus incoming-aware selection. Evaluation tasks are excluded from replay construction and selection (§§3.2.3–3.2.4).
- **Preservation diagnostics:** Paired-answer Retention complements accuracy change, exposing previously correct answers lost despite offsetting gains. Optimization sweeps and fixed-allocation experiments characterize preservation alongside acquisition (§§3.2.1, 3.2.5–3.2.6).

The distinctive contribution is studying supplied-target organization and selection for protection beyond the bank; generated replay itself is acknowledged as prior work (§4).

## Evidence and main results

Retention is the percentage of initially correct answers that remain correct, averaged equally across the relevant tasks; it is not overall accuracy (Appendix A.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Near-stable accuracy conceals answer losses | Qwen3-4B, MedQA, 256 rows, one epoch, learning rate 1e-6, two seeds: across four held-out tasks with 512 paired items each, 3.56% of all items become incorrect and 3.61% become correct, yielding +0.05 percentage points (pp) net accuracy change. | §3.2.1; Figure 2 |
| Broad replay protects excluded tasks | Qwen3-4B, C-STANCE, one epoch at 1e-5, five seeds: mean held-out accuracy change is −4.34 pp for SFT, −3.99 pp for single-task replay, +0.86 pp for stored replay, and +1.07 pp for Task-Bank Self-Replay. Incoming gains span +27.74–+27.86 pp across these methods. | §3.2.2; Figure 3 |
| Combined banks improve mean retention | Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B, 1,024 MedQA examples, one epoch at 1e-5, five seeds: combined-bank held-out Retention is 88.70%/93.28%/96.01%, respectively, exceeding the better partial bank by 4.42/3.32/2.99 pp. Incoming gains are 0.45/0.60/0.28 pp below SFT. | §3.2.3; Figure 4 |
| Incoming-aware selection helps at 4B | Same MedQA protocol, selecting two of four targets: Qwen3-4B reaches 95.69% mean held-out Retention versus 92.94% for random and 93.42% for bank-only selection. Incoming gain is 0.04 pp above random; Retention is 0.32 pp below the full bank. No corresponding advantage appears at smaller sizes. | §3.2.4; Figure 5 |
| Fixed allocation exposes an acquisition cost | Qwen3-4B, MedQA, one epoch at 1e-6, two seeds, 16 updates: replacing 59 of 256 incoming rows with generated-answer replay improves mean seed-wise worst held-out accuracy change from −1.56 to −0.68 pp, while incoming gain falls from +1.17 to +0.49 pp. Stored replay yields −0.88 pp and +0.98 pp, respectively. | §3.2.5; Figure 6 |
| Lower learning rate can improve both outcomes | Qwen3-1.7B, C-STANCE, 1,024 examples, one epoch, 64 updates, two seeds: incoming gain and mean held-out Retention are +12.01 pp and 97.02% at 1e-7, versus +9.67 pp and 82.22% at 3e-6. | §3.2.6; Table 1 accompanying text |

These results support preservation transfer to the tested excluded benchmarks. They do not establish protection of the entire pretrained repertoire, a general scaling advantage, or a better acquisition–retention frontier. Recorded answer transitions establish behavioral change, not erasure of underlying knowledge (Appendices A.1 and G).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Qwen checkpoints, two incoming tasks, and short updates dominate evaluation. The sequential pilot has mixed results and does not establish sustained autonomous preservation (Appendices F–G).
- **Author-stated confounds:** Learner, selector, and generator strength are not independently varied. Fixed-allocation replay reduces incoming exposure; tokens and generation overhead are unmatched (§§3.2.4–3.2.5).
- **Author-stated uncertainty limits:** Small seed counts limit uncertainty analysis. Endpoint aggregates do not support paired significance tests; prompt variants and independent task omissions are untested (Appendices A.5, C.6, G).
- **Author-stated supervision risks:** Synthetic answers are not externally verified. Supplied-question replay requires verified demonstrations, and discovery filtering does not certify semantic novelty or competence (Appendices A.7, C.4–C.6).
- **Analyst inference:** The selection improvement cannot yet be attributed specifically to better interference prediction, because selector, generator, and learner capabilities change together (§3.2.4).

## Open research questions
- **Which component enables useful selection?** Component strengths are coupled. Next experiment: vary selector size while fixing learner, generator, replay allocation, and incoming data (§3.2.4).
- **Does broader coverage beat additional depth at equal cost?** Token and generation costs are unmatched. Next experiment: compare partial and combined banks with matched replay tokens, incoming exposure, and update counts (Appendices C.4 and G).
- **Can discovered targets add preservation value over supplied targets?** The expansion pilot lacks a fixed-bank control. Next experiment: compare validated additions, unguided additions, and extra within-bank replay under equal budgets (Appendix C.5).
- **Can retention accumulate across many updates?** The sequential pilot is too limited. Next experiment: run longer, multi-seed task sequences and track paired retention of both initial and newly acquired behavior (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly reports the main findings and caveats, but several referenced tables contain captions without their numerical bodies. Missing material includes complete per-task scores and uncertainties, pinned revision values from Table 3, selected target IDs, fully specified replay allocations for the main composition/selection studies, measured generation costs, and a verifiable public code/data release location.
