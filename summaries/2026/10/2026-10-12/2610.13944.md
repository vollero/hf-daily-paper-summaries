# InstantFusion: A Shared Latent Interface for Heterogeneous Image Generators

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13944) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13944)


## One-sentence takeaway

InstantFusion learns model-specific latent adapters that align states and denoising dynamics for cross-generator handoffs, with reported composition and acceleration benefits under shared-autoencoder and compatible-time assumptions (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation

Image generators differ in latent representations, architectures, and conditioning, making their intermediate denoising states difficult to exchange. The authors identify a gap between pair-specific latent mappings, which mainly align isolated states, and trajectory-stitching methods, which generally assume interchangeable latent spaces. A reusable interface could let independently trained generators share capabilities during sampling (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Compatibility observations:** Similar spatial self-similarity patterns and preserved coarse content after unaligned handoffs motivate learned alignment; these are qualitative observations rather than proof of general compatibility (§3.2, Fig. 2).
- **Shared latent interface:** Each generator receives a time-conditioned Latent AutoEncoder (LAE). Joint training combines reconstruction, cross-model state alignment, norm regularization, and alignment of one-step denoising displacements. Generator backbones remain frozen during interface training (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Transfer and downstream reuse:** A sender encoder and receiver decoder translate intermediate states. An adaptive handoff criterion tracks predicted-clean-state stability. Applications include cross-model composition, acceleration, preference composition, and cross-architecture on-policy distillation; the latter updates student LoRA parameters (§3.5, §4.3, Appendix B).

The distinctive contribution is joint state-and-dynamics alignment through a shared interface, rather than a separate learned translator for every model pair (§1).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; the supplied text contains table captions but omits table bodies.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| High single-trip reconstruction fidelity | Clean-latent round-trip evaluation reports examples with PSNR >31 dB and SSIM >0.94; fidelity declines with repeated trips. | §4.2, Table 1(a); Appendix D |
| Improved cross-model composition | Qwen guidance raises SD3/FLUX.1 GenEval-v2 by 12.92/13.68 to 35.06/36.54; CVTG-2K gains are 0.2806/0.1803 and OneIG-Bench average gains 0.0619/0.0591, respectively, over corresponding base models. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Faster hybrid generation | Qwen paired with Z-Image-Turbo/FLUX.2-Klein reduces reported latency from 18.84 s to 4.373/2.545 s; corresponding GenEval-v2 scores are 33.34/27.19 and OneIG-Bench averages 0.5386/0.5467. | §4.3, Table 3 |
| Compatibility with caching | Adding DiCache to InstantFusion reduces latency from 10.64 s to 5.70 s; GenEval-v2 changes from 32.70 to 32.46. Exact hybrid configuration is unspecified in the prose. | §4.3, Table 3 |
| Modest reported distillation gain | Qwen-to-SD3 on-policy distillation increases SD3 HPSv2 from 26.27 to 26.46. | §4.3, Table 1(b) |
| Preference composition | Combining GenEval-v2 and HPSv2 experts yields scores of 30.97 and 30.68, respectively; authors report superiority to velocity fusion and proximity to joint multi-reward GRPO, without comparator values in the supplied prose. | §4.3, Table 4 |
| Dynamics and architecture matter | Removing dynamics alignment reportedly causes OPD divergence; replacing time-conditioned blocks with standard convolutions reduces GenEval-v2 by 7.20 for Qwen–SD3 and 19.17 for Qwen–FLUX.1. | §4.4, Tables 5–6 |

These reports support useful transfer in the tested configurations. They do not establish statistical significance, universal compatibility, or preservation of standalone Qwen quality during acceleration: uncertainty estimates and complete comparator scores are unavailable. Section 4.6 specifies a separate latency protocol at 1024×1024 over 20 measured runs after three warm-ups, but its numerical table is absent.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** The method requires a shared autoencoder and compatible time parameterization. Hosting multiple frozen backbones increases memory and storage requirements (§5). Reconstruction errors accumulate through repeated translations (§4.2, Appendix D).

**Analyst observations:**

- The handoff rule is underspecified: Eq. 13 defines a normalized difference \(S_k^A\), while Eq. 14 switches using an undefined similarity \(s_k^A \geq \gamma\). Their relationship, threshold selection, and fallback behavior are unclear (§3.5).
- Appendix E.2 claims preservation across distinct autoencoder designs, conflicting with the shared-VAE formulation and stated limitation (§3.3, §5).
- Missing table bodies prevent inspection of full baselines, per-model reconstruction results, schedule variation, and training configurations.
- Qualitative timestep and permutation results do not establish trajectory-wide robustness across arbitrary generators (§4.5, Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions

- **Can the interface bridge different VAEs?** The current formulation excludes this (§5). A next experiment could compare shared-VAE and heterogeneous-VAE pairs using reconstruction and generation metrics.
- **Can new generators join without retraining existing adapters?** Minimal-adaptation incorporation is proposed as future work (§6). Freeze an established interface, train only a new LAE, and compare against joint retraining.
- **Does adaptive handoff outperform fixed schedules?** Structural consistency is shown qualitatively, but the criterion is ambiguous (§3.5, §4.4). Specify the score and compare threshold sweeps with fixed handoff steps on held-out prompts.
- **How much transfer can cascading tolerate?** Repeated reconstruction degrades despite reported consistency across three-stage schedules (§4.2, §4.5). Increase cascade length and measure quality loss alongside reconstruction error.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and several reported results are explicit, but verification is constrained by absent table bodies.

Material missing information includes complete baseline scores, uncertainty estimates, evaluation prompt counts and seeds, hardware and memory measurements, full training hyperparameters, precise handoff implementation, incremental-adaptation results, and clarification of the autoencoder inconsistency. Code availability is announced but was not verified.
