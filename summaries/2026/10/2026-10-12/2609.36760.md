# QuantMLA: Function-Aligned Dual-Path Quantization for Low-Bit MLA KV Caching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36760) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36760)


## One-sentence takeaway

QuantMLA learns offline-fused transformations for MLA’s content and RoPE caches, reporting near-BF16 accuracy with INT4 history and substantial memory savings, while its serving throughput gain comes from avoiding preemption in one cache-pressure workload.

## Problem and motivation

MLA compresses attention storage into a shared content latent and a separate positional RoPE key cache, but memory still grows with context length and batch size. The authors identify a gap in existing MLA quantization: content caches are typically compressed to FP8 while RoPE keys retain high precision, and the functional consequences of quantizing both paths remain insufficiently understood (§1).

The key motivation is that cache reconstruction error can misrepresent downstream damage: the two caches serve different functions and exhibit different attention-output sensitivity (§2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Dual-path error analysis:** The authors derive exact finite error routes and a local operator–error decomposition. Content errors affect matching, value aggregation, and their interaction; RoPE errors affect positional matching. Sensitivity and error direction help explain why RoPE errors can cause greater output distortion (§§2.3, B.2–B.3).
- **Function-aligned transformation learning:** Content uses learned orthogonal rotations and positive channel scales, optimized for attention-output reconstruction. RoPE uses rotations and shared scales within frequency pairs, with reciprocal query compensation, optimized for positional QK reconstruction. Both transformations preserve full-precision computation and fuse into parameters offline (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Joint low-bit caching:** The evaluated settings combine INT4 content with INT4 RoPE history (C4R4), or INT2 content with INT4 RoPE history (C2R4). The authors claim the first reported joint INT4 MLA caching; priority cannot be verified from the supplied text (§1).
- **Native execution:** A low-bit attention backend integrates unpacking and dequantization, reuses reconstructed content tiles, and preserves global full-history softmax. The C4R4 policy protects the first four and most recent 128 tokens in BF16 (§3.3; Appendix C.5).

## Evidence and main results

All numerical findings below are author-reported.

| C4R4 retains general-task accuracy | Equal-weight ten-task averages are within **0.26** and **0.13 percentage points** of BF16 for DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Moonlight-16B-A3B, respectively. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| C4R4 improves reasoning/code averages over adapted baselines | Seven-task averages are **62.01%** for LongCat-Flash-Lite and **53.75%** for GLM-4.7-Flash, exceeding the strongest same-precision baselines by **4.82** and **2.70 points**. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| C2R4 remains competitive | Ten-task averages are **64.42% / 72.70%** for DeepSeek-V2-Lite/Moonlight, **7.20 / 10.84 points** above the strongest same-precision baselines; GLM’s seven-task average is **51.43%**, ahead by **6.22 points**. | §4.2; Tables 1–2 |

| Transformations and protection both contribute | Full DeepSeek-V2-Lite MMLU accuracy follows **51.90 → 53.12 → 56.31 → 57.95%** when adding content transformation, RoPE transformation, then BF16 protection; BF16 scores **57.90%**. Gains depend on this cumulative order. | §4.3; Table 3; Appendix D.3 |
| Physical cache storage shrinks | C4R4 with sink4/local128 achieves **3.22× at 4K** and **3.59× at 128K**, including packed copies, metadata, protection buffers, and reverse mappings. | Figure 5(a); Appendix C.5 |
| Attention latency stays near BF16 | Synthetic single-query tests at batches **8/32**, contexts **1K–1M**, range from **7.42% lower to 4.29% higher** latency; differences are below **0.5% from 64K onward**. Writer and metadata construction are excluded. | §4.4; Figure 5(b); Appendix F.1 |

These results support low-bit accuracy retention and memory reduction in the evaluated configurations. They do not establish a general 5.168× inference speedup: the serving gain reflects avoided capacity-driven recomputation, whereas attention latency is approximately unchanged. The supplied text contains table captions and narrative results but omits the numerical table bodies, limiting independent inspection of full comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The serving gain is workload-specific, uses a historical BF16 baseline, and has no confidence interval across independent runs (Appendix F.2).
- **Author-stated theoretical boundary:** The positional-QK bound fixes content logits, values, queries, and valid-key sets. It does not bound all errors from jointly quantizing both caches; the normalized objective is a surrogate (Appendix C.3).
- **Author-stated execution boundary:** Native correctness checks use the same reconstructed cache as the reference, rather than original unquantized attention; shared-prefix ownership is outside validation (Appendices C.5, F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Accuracy at very long contexts remains unestablished: retrieval reaches 32K, while kernel timing reaches 1M (§4.2; Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The cumulative one-model MMLU ablation cannot isolate order-independent component effects or demonstrate objective superiority across models (Appendix D.3).
- Baselines are author-created MLA adaptations, not reproductions of the original full-model pipelines; downstream comparisons use seed 0 (Appendices C.4, D.2).
- The authors warn that preserved aggregate accuracy does not establish preserved safety, calibration, or failure modes (Ethics statement).

## Open research questions

- **Would joint optimization improve aggressive compression?** Separate learning leaves potentially nonzero cross-path error interactions (Appendix B.2). Compare separate and joint optimization under identical calibration budgets at C4R4 and C2R4.
- **How does accuracy behave beyond 32K?** Longer contexts appear in timing and serving evaluations, but not reported retrieval accuracy. Extend retrieval and reasoning tests across models at longer contexts.
- **How much throughput benefit persists away from capacity limits?** The reported workload triggers BF16 preemption. Run repeated paired serving experiments across request lengths and concurrency, including settings where both caches fit.
- **Which objectives and protection policies are necessary?** The reported ablation combines components cumulatively. Run factorial comparisons of reconstruction objectives, transformations, and protection-window sizes.
