# Video-RSI: Recursive Self-Improvement of Video Understanding Agents via Harness Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37950) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37950)


## One-sentence takeaway
Video-RSI uses a frozen language model to investigate video-question failures and revise its executable harness offline, with reported accuracy gains and lower inference-time frame usage across four benchmarks relative to its initial harness (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Video agents’ observation choices affect both answer quality and visual cost. Existing execution traces cannot reliably distinguish missed events, perceptual errors, and failures to use acquired evidence, making harness revisions difficult to diagnose. The authors address this gap by revisiting training videos before editing code and evaluating revisions for both accuracy and observation cost (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evidence-grounded revision:** The same frozen language model acts as Solver and Editor. Before editing, the Editor requests additional video observations, transcripts, OCR, or incumbent reruns to test competing failure explanations (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Cost-aware evolution:** Candidates revise prompts, tools, observation processing, memory, and execution control. A private selection gate accepts accuracy gains with bounded frame-cost growth, or sufficient frame savings with bounded accuracy loss (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Reusable harness changes:** Accepted revisions accumulate offline; the final harness is frozen for evaluation. Observed changes include timestamp-linked structured perception, relation graphs for evidence reuse, and selective observation routines (§4.5).

The authors position investigation and cost-aware selection as their distinguishing additions to existing frozen-model harness evolution (§2.3).

These are author-reported findings. The supplied text includes table and figure captions but omits their numerical contents, preventing inspection of absolute scores and complete comparisons. The evidence supports improved deployed accuracy–frame trade-offs in the reported settings, rather than lower total computation: frames count successful visual-model calls and exclude offline Editor observations (§§4.1, 4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Explicit scope caveat:** Frame savings do not measure total model computation (§4.5), and offline investigation costs are excluded (§4.1).
- **Observed transfer risk:** Development improvements do not reliably transfer at each accepted revision (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Generality beyond the tested model pairing remains uncertain: experiments use DeepSeek-V4-Pro and Qwen3.6-Plus with frozen weights (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Repeated selection on the fixed development set could produce selection-set adaptation despite private per-item outcomes; the paper does not quantify this risk (§§3.4, 4.1).
- **Evidence gaps:** The supplied text reports no repeated-run uncertainty or isolated ablations of structured perception, relation graphs, and selective observation routines. Their individual causal contributions therefore remain unresolved (§§4.3–4.5).
- **Comparison boundary:** Only VideoSeek and VideoHarness-RSI are reproduced; other baseline results come from original publications (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does evolution reduce total resource cost?** Editor observations and total computation are outside the reported metric. A next experiment could measure end-to-end evolution and deployment costs, then estimate the question volume needed to amortize evolution.
- **How stable are the gains across runs and selection sets?** The reported trajectory shows uneven transfer, without repeated-run uncertainty. Repeat evolution with independent seeds and video-disjoint selection splits.
- **Which retained changes drive improvement?** §4.5 describes several co-evolved mechanisms without isolating them. Disable each mechanism in the final harness and measure accuracy and frames.
- **Does the method generalize across model services?** Only one model pairing is reported. Repeat the matched investigation and gate ablations with alternative frozen Solver and perception models.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and experimental protocol are clearly described, but quantitative assessment depends on prose summaries.

Material missing information includes full Tables 1–2, Figure 3 values, absolute benchmark scores and frame counts, repeated-run uncertainty, numerical per-attempt resource limits, total evolution cost, and detailed prompts, code, and execution-check procedures.
