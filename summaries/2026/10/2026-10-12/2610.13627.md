# AMLworld-Compact: Importance-Weighted Downsampling for Cost-Effective LLM Evaluation and Error Diagnosis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13627) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13627)


## One-sentence takeaway
AMLworld-Compact reduces AMLworld evaluation targets by 271× and 611× through rare-class retention and weighted benign sampling, with improved sampling accuracy for two tested booster families but LLM findings constrained by ambiguous target identification (§§3.5–3.6, 3.4).

## Problem and motivation
AMLworld’s smallest test partitions each require over a million transaction-specific graph prompts. Class-proportional sampling leaves few illicit cases for recall and typology analysis, while balanced sampling changes unweighted precision and F1. The authors address the gap between affordable evaluation, preservation of rare cases, and estimation at full-test prevalence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation sampling protocol:** HT-Coreset retains every illicit transaction, reserves benign samples for high supervised scores, and samples remaining benign transactions from three difficulty terciles. Inverse inclusion probabilities estimate full-test confusion counts; recall is exact for fixed predictions, while precision and F1 remain potentially biased ratio estimates (§3.3).
- **Reusable evaluation assets:** AMLworld-Compact aligns case IDs, labels, typology annotations, GFP features, graph strings, and weights. A separate target-marked input version corrects target identification and time summaries but has no LLM evaluation (§3.5; Appendix B.2).
- **Sampling validation and error diagnosis:** The authors compare alternative samplers, hold out one booster family from construction, evaluate seven open-weight LLMs, audit lexical trace markers, and control for label-informed hybrid routing (§§3.6, 4–5).

The contribution combines established HT estimation with a task-specific sampling design and released assets; the paper does not introduce HT estimation itself (§2).

The evidence supports improved sampling accuracy for the tested booster families and descriptive diagnosis of the evaluated LLM outputs. Exact construction agreement follows from retaining every confusion-count contributor, rather than demonstrating predictor-independent accuracy. Near-baseline LLM scores do not establish statistical equivalence, and trace-audit percentages are not population estimates (§3.6; Appendices D.2, E.2, H.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:**

- Original inputs leave targets unmarked and omit 324/3,753 HI-Small and 297/2,268 LI-Small target transactions. The corrected version is unevaluated, so errors cannot be attributed solely to reasoning (§3.4; Appendix B).
- Main LLM testing covers two synthetic splits, one coreset size, and one neighborhood cap. Full-test LLM predictions and independent coreset draws were not evaluated (Limitations).
- File-order partitions overlap in time; task-reference thresholds use full-test labels. Archived GCPAL training overlaps the test set and remains in frozen construction scores, although excluded from primary task comparisons (§3.1; Appendix D.6).
- Trace analysis uses one run; judges receive no graph, and human Match agreement is low. These checks cannot validate graph grounding (§5.3; Limitations).
- TF1 includes additional predicted labels rather than only eight typologies. Exact historical scoring requires archived labels because the live parser does not reproduce every fallback (Appendix E).

**Analyst inference:** The Elliptic experiment broadens sampling evidence beyond synthetic AMLworld, but its construction-ensemble agreement does not resolve transfer to unrelated predictors or real-data LLM performance (Appendix D.8).

## Open research questions
- **Does target identification change detection?** Corrected inputs remain unevaluated. Run a paired original-versus-target-marked comparison with fixed models, demonstrations, and scoring; then separate the bundled input changes through ablations (Appendix B.2; §6).
- **Does sampling remain accurate for unrelated predictors?** Held-out validation uses two GFP boosters. Measure full-population predictions for an unrelated predictor and compare estimates across independent coreset draws and budgets (Appendix D.5).
- **Can trace checks verify evidence rather than vocabulary?** Current judges lack graph inputs. Test graph-aware annotation of claimed structures against answer correctness on a probability sample across runs (§5.3; Appendix H.2).
- **Can deployable routing add LLM value?** DT uses labels, and score-only gates are evaluation-selected with an incomplete search grid. Train routes without label-derived strata and evaluate on a separate chronological partition against an always-illicit routing control (Appendix G.2).
