# PolyOCR-Venus: Unified OCR Foundation Models for Text-Centric Visual Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37712) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37712)


## One-sentence takeaway
PolyOCR combines unified generative OCR training with competence-gated teacher distillation, achieving strong reported benchmark results while retaining task-specific weaknesses and dependence on external layout analysis for its strongest page-parsing configuration.

## Problem and motivation
The authors seek a single model that can recognize, localize, parse, extract, translate, and reason over visual text. They argue that specialized OCR systems fragment these capabilities, while general-purpose vision-language models offer unified interfaces but uneven OCR accuracy. Heterogeneous supervision also makes balanced training difficult (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified model family:** PolyOCR-2B and PolyOCR-9B adapt Qwen3.5 through a shared instruction-following, autoregressive interface. A specialized PolyOCR-2.7B combines a ViT encoder with a Qwen2.5 decoder for multilingual document parsing (§1, §8.1).
- **Capability-oriented data engine:** Approximately 60 million training instances combine collected resources, controlled synthesis, and model-assisted annotation, with agreement, structural, and source-grounded checks. A three-phase supervised curriculum shifts emphasis toward OCR reasoning (§3, §4.2; Table 2).
- **Competence-Guided Policy Optimization (CGPO):** Always-active GRPO is combined with reverse-KL on-policy distillation. Frozen calibration maps estimate teacher quality and teacher superiority from verifier-scored generations; these estimates determine each sample’s distillation weight. This calibrated routing is the specific methodological contribution beyond combining reinforcement learning and distillation (§2.4, §4.3).
- **OCRBench v2.1:** The authors revise annotations and task-specific metrics while preserving the original benchmark composition. Other named public benchmarks serve as evaluation assets; the text does not establish them as newly created contributions (§11).

These author-reported results support broad OCR capability and an advantage for CGPO over the tested post-training alternatives. They do not establish superiority on every task or statistical significance. Multilingual results use a separate specialized model, and pipeline parsing results include an external layout module. OCRBench v2.1 scores are explicitly incompatible with original v2 leaderboard scores (§11.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Sample-level routing can retain local teacher errors even when aggregate rewards are high (§4.3). Mathematical calculation, Chinese text recognition, and Chinese visual-text understanding remain below the strongest evaluated baselines (§5.3).
- **Author-stated future needs:** Better fine-grained perception, localization, reasoning, and real-world robustness remain priorities (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 9 lacks repeated-run uncertainty and separate ablations of teacher-quality versus superiority gating, so routing-component attribution and the reliability of small score differences remain unresolved.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text does not report data-engine or curriculum ablations, training–test overlap checks, or in-house KIE sample counts and split construction.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Figure 1’s caption associates OmniDocBench with the layout-guided pipeline, whereas Tables 3 and 6 distinguish direct parsing from the stronger pipeline configuration.
- **Reproducibility:** Some exact serialization, normalization, and judge-version details are promised for release rather than fully specified (§9). Model and benchmark release claims cannot be verified from the supplied text alone.

## Open research questions
1. **Which routing component drives improvement?** Table 9 evaluates CGPO as a whole. Compare quality-only, superiority-only, and combined gating under matched training budgets.
2. **Does frozen calibration remain valid as students improve?** Calibration uses SFT-student generations and stays fixed (§4.3). Measure calibration error throughout training and compare periodic recalibration.
3. **Can routing protect critical local content?** The authors acknowledge residual local teacher errors. Test token- or field-level gating on documents containing critical amounts and identifiers.
4. **How much benefit comes from data construction and curriculum?** Their individual effects are not isolated. Run matched-size data-source and sampling-schedule ablations across the same evaluation suite.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains detailed methods, protocols, and results, but lacks uncertainty estimates, key component ablations, full data provenance and overlap audits, in-house benchmark composition, calibration split size and fitted coefficients, CGPO duration and compute cost, and complete released implementation details.
