# Dex-One2Many: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from a Single Human Demonstration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12470) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12470)


## One-sentence takeaway
Dex-One2Many converts one human demonstration video into relational task stages that guide simulation RL, reporting 75% mean zero-shot real-world success on unseen configurations across five tasks (§1).

## Problem and motivation
Learning dexterous manipulation requires costly robot demonstrations, while human videos provide more accessible task examples. The authors identify a tradeoff: closely following demonstrated motions restricts generalization, but removing that guidance makes exploration difficult for high-dimensional, contact-rich, multi-stage RL. They aim to preserve task requirements while allowing different poses, grasps, and trajectories (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Relational task abstraction:** A VLM extracts ordered scene graphs using a predefined predicate vocabulary. Graphs encode required hand–object and object–object relations rather than demonstrated coordinates or trajectories (§3.1; Table 3).
- **Graph-grounded policy learning:** Predicates determine observations, dense rewards, and diverse stage-wise reset states. Synthesized grasps and backward reset generation are filtered for collision, stability, and task compatibility, allowing RL to practice intermediate stages (§3.2; Appendix C.3).
- **Integrated sim-to-real demonstration:** PPO training combines domain randomization, noisy observations, and an asymmetric actor–critic. Deployment uses multi-view object-pose tracking and a QP controller. These components draw on existing methods; the principal contribution is their integration around graph-grounded task learning (§3.3; Appendices C–D).

The evidence supports pose generalization within the evaluated tasks and zero-shot deployment on the tested robot. It does not establish generalization to new object categories, arbitrary task structures, or unseen embodiments without retraining. The embodiment results use separate policies. The training-scale comparison holds updates constant, so it does not isolate compute or sample efficiency. Figure 4 also marks an unavailable experiment due to hardware failure, but the supplied text does not identify that cell.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Estimated object poses and simulation–real geometry differences can degrade contact-rich performance (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Automatic reconstruction supports rigid objects; articulated objects require manually built assets because articulation structure may be hidden in a single video (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation breadth is limited to five tasks and one real arm–hand embodiment; other embodiments are tested only in simulation (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Sweep-only ablations leave component importance across other tasks unresolved. No reported ablation isolates graph-extraction accuracy, grasp diversity, or the added grasp-quality reward (§4.4; Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** “Single video” still requires supporting depth, simulation assets, and pretrained systems; CAD substitutions limit what the experiments establish about fully automatic reconstruction (Table 2; Appendix B.3).
- **Reporting limits:** Training-seed variability, confidence intervals, baseline implementation details, and release availability are not established in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
1. **How sensitive is learning to incorrect task graphs?** Extraction accuracy and error robustness are not evaluated. Compare automatically extracted graphs with corrected graphs and controlled predicate or ordering errors.
2. **Can training achieve comparable success with less simulation experience?** Smaller environment counts underperform at equal updates (§4.4). Compare methods at matched transition counts and wall-clock budgets, including adaptive stage sampling.
3. **How much of the real-world failure rate comes from perception versus control?** The authors identify pose and geometry errors but do not quantify their contributions (§5). Compare tracked poses with independently measured poses and controlled geometry perturbations.
4. **Can the pipeline extend to articulated objects?** Automatic articulation reconstruction remains unresolved (§5). Test joint inference against manually specified assets on articulated tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and principal experiments, but material gaps include exact Figure 4 values and its missing hardware-failure cell, uncertainty estimates, baseline training details, quantitative graph-extraction accuracy, exact unseen-pose distributions, total training compute and runtime, and confirmed code/model/asset releases.
