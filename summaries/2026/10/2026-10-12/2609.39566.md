# From Given to Gathered Evidence: Agentic Learning for Longitudinal Medical Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39566) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39566)


## One-sentence takeaway
CASE trains a shared compact vision–language policy to gather longitudinal patient evidence and synthesize answers, with reported gains over frontier models that cannot be fully assessed because the supplied, truncated text lacks numerical result-table entries.

## Problem and motivation
Clinical evidence is distributed across records, imaging sequences, measurements, and visits, so an agent must decide what to inspect before answering. The authors argue that fixed-context medical QA misses this acquisition problem and that teacher demonstrations expose learners only to teacher-selected investigation paths (§1).

CASE studies whether autonomous interaction can improve compact models beyond imitation in a patient-specific, longitudinal multimodal environment (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and evidence interface:** Diagnosis-derived multiple-choice questions are paired with patient environments containing clinical context, paired-visit MRI, segmentation-derived measurements, and generated reports. Agents choose tools, views, and cross-visit comparisons (§4).
- **Shared investigation policy:** One Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct policy implements Anatomist, Radiologist, and Consultant workflows through role prompts and tool registries; these are not independently trained collaborating agents (§5.1).
- **Post-training framework:** Screened teacher trajectories initialize tool use through rejection-sampling fine-tuning; GRPO then learns from policy-generated interactions using correctness, format, rubric, and outcome-linked synthesis rewards (§§5.2–5.5).
- **Distinctive synthesis penalty:** Ordinary and answer-privileged versions of the same frozen policy score the same realized trajectory. Their synthesis-region divergence becomes a detached trajectory reward penalty before group normalization, rather than a differentiable distillation loss (§5.4; Appendix I.3).

The paper acknowledges precedents for medical evidence gathering and agentic RL; its specific distinction is the privileged synthesis reward construction and longitudinal task structure, not those broader ideas alone (Appendix A.2).

The text supports the method specification and benchmark composition more strongly than its performance claims. It describes matched within-role interfaces and first-pass exact-set scoring, but supplies no populated main comparison tables. Appendix A.1 also says controlled baseline results “remain to be supplied,” creating ambiguity about result completeness. Reported evidence sensitivity does not independently verify clinical justification or establish transfer beyond this cohort.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Some diagnosis labels may be unobservable from available images; generated evidence and judges are fallible; broader clinical generalization and efficiency remain future work (§7).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** The rubric judge excludes images and full tool logs, so it evaluates the stated argument rather than visual validity. Lower privileged divergence can reflect hint insensitivity rather than improved reasoning (Appendices E–F, I.5).
- **Implementation risks disclosed:** Approximate insight masks can include unintended positions. Scoring failures can skip penalties or assign zero divergence; judge failures can produce neutral scores and alter linkage behavior (Appendices E, H).
- **Analyst inference:** Live Consultant memory within evaluation runs makes order sensitivity a material question; matched memory rules alone do not establish order invariance (Appendix B).
- **Evidence gaps:** Numerical tables, completed training-seed counts, failure frequencies, and proposed comparisons against other distillation update rules are unavailable. These omissions prevent assessment of effect size, robustness, and mechanism-specific superiority (Appendix F).
- **Coverage limitation:** The supplied source is truncated; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions
1. **Does CASE transfer beyond UK Biobank?** The authors explicitly leave this open (§7). Next experiment: evaluate a frozen policy on an external cohort with compatible longitudinal evidence and independently validated labels.
2. **Does OPSD help because of answer privilege or other conditioning changes?** The text proposes controls without providing their results (Appendix F). Next experiment: compare correct-answer, shuffled-answer, neutral-hint, and answer-only scoring conditions.
3. **Are improved insights visually grounded?** Text-only judges cannot establish this (Appendices E–F). Next experiment: blinded clinical review of sampled trajectories against retrieved images, including incorrect answers.
4. **Does top-128 coarsening preserve useful trajectory rankings?** High probability coverage does not bound conditional-tail divergence (Appendix I.2). Next experiment: compare full-vocabulary and bucketed scores and reward rankings on fixed held-out histories.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium** for the method and benchmark description; **low** for comparative performance magnitude and causal mechanism claims.

Missing information includes populated accuracy, ablation, uncertainty, and efficiency tables; completed seed counts; scoring-failure frequencies; clinical grounding audits; current release status; and material content removed by source truncation. Trajectory counts also conflict: the abstract states 14,734 interactions, whereas §4 reports 147,434 recordings and approximately 140,000 retained demonstrations.
