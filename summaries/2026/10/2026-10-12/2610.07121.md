# SoloQ: Calibration-Free Quantization for Diffusion Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07121) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07121)


## One-sentence takeaway

SoloQ enables calibration-free 4-bit diffusion language model inference through structured rotations and scale correction, with reported memory and speed gains on native NVFP4 hardware but model-dependent accuracy losses (§§4–5; Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

Diffusion language models repeatedly process tokens during denoising, increasing computation; block-diffusion models also accumulate persistent KV-cache memory. Weight-only compression therefore misses activation and cache costs (§1; Figure 1).

The authors identify calibration dependence as a weakness of existing quantization methods: activation distributions change across masking states and denoising steps, and calibration-set choice can alter downstream accuracy. SoloQ instead seeks a predictable representation that supports quantization without calibration samples (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured representation transformation:** K-RPBH extends randomized permuted block-Hadamard rotation with an orthogonal cross-block mixer to reduce residual energy imbalance and accommodate transformer dimensions unsupported by some existing rotations (§4.2).
- **Two calibration-free quantization paths:** SoloQ-C uses distribution-matched Lloyd–Max activation and E8P weight codebooks; SoloQ-N uses hardware-native NVFP4 with scales calculated directly from current tensors. These quantizers build on prior methods (§4.3).
- **Scale-only correction:** A scalar absorbed into existing scales restores the quantized vector’s projection along the original vector without extra quantized payload. It does not remove orthogonal quantization error (§4.4).
- **Commit-time KV quantization:** Previously committed blocks are compressed while the active denoising block remains in full precision. Keys use existing per-channel asymmetric quantization; values use the SoloQ activation path (§4.5).

The new contribution is the framework and its extensions for diffusion language models; random-rotation quantization, codebooks, and scalar correction have acknowledged precedents (§§2.2, 4.3–4.4). The authors claim the first calibration-free framework covering both full-sequence dLLMs and block-diffusion KV-cache quantization (§2.3).

The results support useful calibration-free quantization on the evaluated models and hardware. They do not establish universal superiority over calibration-based methods or lossless accuracy retention. The mathematical proposition establishes variance averaging in expectation, rather than an exact Gaussian marginal for every fixed structured rotation (§4.2; Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** Online activation rotations add overhead. Folding rotations into weights reduces that overhead but can degrade quantized accuracy, especially for block-diffusion models. SoloQ-C requires INT8 computation for its codebooks, while SoloQ-N’s full acceleration depends on native NVFP4 support (Appendix G).

**Comparison qualifications:** Dream TruthfulQA results from DLLMQuant/STaR-Quant are reported as unreproducible and have a different full-precision reference (Table 1 footnote). The FAIR-Calib comparison mixes externally reported results with the authors’ evaluation (Table 6). Evaluators differ across models, and Nemotron is evaluated at a controlled operating point rather than an exact reconstruction of its original paper (Appendix C).

**Analyst inference:** Aggregate averages can conceal concentrated damage: Nemotron SoloQ-N W4A4 IFEval falls from **97.27% FP16 to 60.85%**, under the Appendix C.5 evaluator (Table 2). No uncertainty estimates or repeated-seed results are supplied, limiting assessment of small improvements. FPGA gains also combine quantization with doubled compute capacity, so they do not isolate a precision-only effect (Appendix E).

## Open research questions

- **How stable is K-RPBH across random seeds and denoising states?** The proof concerns expected variance, and reported evaluations lack seed uncertainty. Next experiment: repeat rotation sampling and measure marginal mismatch and task accuracy across masking states (§4.2).
- **Can instruction-following degradation be reduced without calibration?** Nemotron retains mathematical performance better than IFEval performance. Next experiment: selectively increase precision in individual layer groups and measure IFEval alongside reasoning tasks (Table 2).
- **How do cache errors scale with context and commitment policy?** The cache is compressed at commitment, but the supplied results do not provide an accuracy sweep over context and block sizes. Next experiment: vary both while comparing KV precision and attention-sink protection (§4.5; Appendix D.4).
- **Can folding preserve accuracy while reducing rotation cost?** Existing folding causes model-dependent losses. Next experiment: selectively fold residual-stream rotations and compare accuracy against end-to-end latency (Appendix D.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, evaluation protocols, ablations, and limitations, but some comparisons are qualified.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, exact per-model numerical values and workload details behind Figure 4, numerical logit-KL curves from Figure 5, and independent verification of the announced code release.
