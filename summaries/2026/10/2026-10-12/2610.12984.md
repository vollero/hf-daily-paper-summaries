# Work2Skill: Capturing Human GUI Experience for Reusable Agent Skill Induction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12984) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12984)


## One-sentence takeaway
Work2Skill converts synchronized human GUI demonstrations into reusable textual skills, with reported execution gains across four models, while reuse evidence is limited to task changes within the same applications and transfer between two models (§§4.2–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
GUI agents can lack the procedural knowledge needed for unfamiliar software or organization-specific workflows. Human demonstrations provide that knowledge, but reusable guidance must distinguish workflow rules from instance-specific inputs.

The authors identify three gaps: ambiguous action semantics without structured UI evidence, inconsistent demonstrations and execution protocols across studies, and fixed inspection pipelines that can miss relevant evidence or overload context (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **WorkRecorder:** Aligns screen video, mouse/keyboard actions, keyframes, and available accessibility-tree (AX) evidence so demonstrated operations can be interpreted in context (§3.1).
- **WorkDemo-350:** Provides 350 screened, successful demonstrations covering 64 MyPCBench Bounded Action tasks and 286 curated OSWorld-Verified tasks. The authors claim it is the first synchronized multimodal desktop-demonstration dataset for reusable skill induction; priority is not independently established here (§3.2).
- **Agentic skill induction:** A tool-using agent alternates selective evidence inspection with drafting and revising `SKILL.md` procedures. The authoring policy emphasizes dependencies, generalized inputs, semantic UI targets, and completion checks. Skills are neither executed nor revised using downstream evaluation feedback during induction (§3.3).

Executors receive the generated skill text alongside the target instruction, without access to the source demonstration (§§3.3, 4.1). The abstract reports code and data availability.

## Evidence and main results

Score is mean evaluator output scaled to 0–100; SR is the percentage of tasks receiving full credit, distinct from OSWorld’s official mean-score “success rate” terminology (Appendix E.3). Results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Improved demonstrated-task execution | Across four models, versus No Skill: MyPCBench’s 64 tasks gain **4.67–10.54 Score points** and **6.25–25.00 SR percentage points**; the 286-task OSWorld subset gains **3.11–7.50 points** and **1.40–7.69 percentage points** | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Better adapted demonstration guidance | Versus the higher-scoring Aloha/D2T baseline per executor: **3.95–7.79 Score points** on MyPCBench and **1.65–6.96 points** on the OSWorld subset, under shared execution settings | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Same-application reuse | On 64 MyPCBench Jitter variants, frozen original skills gain **3.93–8.64 Score points**, **6.25–9.38 SR percentage points**, and use **2.49–5.44 fewer steps** than No Skill across four executors | §4.3; Table 3 |
| Transfer between two models | All GPT-5.5/Qwen3.5-9B induction–execution pairings improve over the same executor’s No Skill baseline; GPT-5.5-induced skills perform better under both executors | §4.4; Figure 4 |
| Multimodal evidence matters | On 64 MyPCBench tasks with GPT-5.5/high reasoning, Full Evidence scores **85.27** and fully solves **31 tasks**. Removing indexed images, UI annotations, or KeyLogs lowers Score by **5.02–10.63 points** and SR by **14.06–21.88 percentage points** | §4.5; Figure 5(a) |
| Flexible prompt improves utility at higher cost | Same model, evidence, tools, and authoring policy: Work2Skill versus Pipeline Prompt gains **3.97 Score points** and **6.25 SR percentage points**; steps fall **48.98→47.38**, with **12.7% higher induction token usage** | §4.5; Figure 5(b) |

These comparisons support the utility of generated guidance under the tested harnesses. They do not establish performance on the full OSWorld benchmark, unseen applications, or native Aloha/D2T execution systems. Execution-step and induction-token savings are not consistent across models (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Explicit scope:** OSWorld results cover a curated subset; Jitter changes requirements within existing applications; transfer tests only GPT-5.5 and Qwen3.5-9B (§§4.1, 4.3–4.4).
- **Explicit evidence limitations:** AX availability varies, snapshots may not match action-time state, and target association uses heuristic timing rules without measured accuracy bounds (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Explicit validation limitation:** Finalization checks structure and tool-use requirements, not semantic correctness or task success (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Aloha loses observation/reasoning/expectation fields, and D2T loses image-containing lines. Results therefore assess these text adaptations rather than complete original systems (Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference—ablation confounding:** “w/o KeyLogs” also removes image overlays; its effect cannot be attributed solely to raw keylogs (Appendix E.6).
- **Analyst inference—selection and uncertainty:** Successful-demonstration screening limits conclusions about noisy or failed recordings. The supplied text reports no repeated-run variability or confidence intervals (§3.2; Appendix E).

## Open research questions

- **Do skills survive interface changes?** Jitter preserves application context. Next experiment: freeze skills and test changed layouts, controls, or software versions (§4.3).
- **Which input annotations drive gains?** KeyLogs and overlays are removed jointly. Next experiment: independently toggle logs, captions, and pointer highlights (Appendix E.6).
- **How reliable is induction from imperfect demonstrations?** Only complete, successful recordings are retained. Next experiment: introduce omissions, failed actions, and AX mismatches, then measure skill errors and downstream completion (§3.2; Appendix A).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and aggregate results, but many tables and figures appear only as captions. Missing material includes complete per-model result cells, detailed evidence statistics, full prompts and generated skill content, complete retained task IDs, demonstrator characteristics, measured recording-alignment accuracy, and repeated-run uncertainty. Reported code/data availability was not independently verified.
