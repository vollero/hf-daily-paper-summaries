# Towards Physically Grounded Articulated 3D Assets: A Multi-Agent Pipeline and Benchmark

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13947) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13947)


## One-sentence takeaway
Artisan combines independent design, coding, and review agents with simulation feedback to substantially reduce sampled articulation collisions and improve stability, while downstream demonstration generation remains a significant bottleneck (Table 9; §E.2).

## Problem and motivation
Generating articulated assets requires jointly reconstructing appearance, geometry, joints, mass, and functional behavior. The authors argue that separate prediction pipelines and visual checks leave assets unsuitable for simulation: objects may interpenetrate, topple, or resist manipulation. They identify two gaps in prior agentic construction: self-evaluation by the construction agent and insufficient direct physical testing (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Generation pipeline:** Artisan extends Articraft with a Design Agent producing a blueprint and image-generated design board, a Coding Agent constructing executable Python assets, and an independent Review Agent requesting repairs without editing the asset (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Physical and functional tooling:** MuJoCo collision and gravity reports guide revisions. SDK extensions provide parameterized connectors, anchor-based placement, physical attributes, and action manifests identifying interaction regions and target joint travel (§§3.2–3.4; Appendix A).
- **Unified benchmark:** The evaluation combines spatial fidelity, physical attributes, robotic utility, and interactive human judgments. It includes curated ArtVIP and LightWheel subsets, stability prompts, and ABO mass references (§4; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Much lower collisions than the matched Articraft baseline | On ArtVIP, GPT-6-Astra Artisan has single/double/multi-joint collision rates of **1.7/3.7/13.0%**, versus **45.5/60.7/78.8%** for GPT-6-Astra Articraft. Evaluation uses joint sweeps and random multi-joint configurations, excluding corresponding reference contacts. | Table 9; §4.1 |
| Higher gravity stability | On the curated stability set, GPT-6-Astra Artisan achieves **98% rest / 96% open**, versus **82% / 82%** for matched Articraft, under the benchmark’s ten-second MuJoCo tests and tilt/displacement criteria. | Table 9; §4.1 |
| Physical feedback has strong ablation support | Removing validation from GPT-5.6-Sol Artisan raises ArtVIP single-joint collisions from **1.9% to 35.7%** and multi-joint collisions from **16.4% to 73.2%**; open-state stability falls from **96% to 90%**. | §C.2 |
| Spatial fidelity is competitive, according to authors | GPT-6-Astra Artisan leads **8 of 11 ArtVIP metrics**; on LightWheel it leads **4 of 6 geometry metrics**. Underlying numerical table bodies are absent from the supplied text. | §§5.1, C.1; Tables 7–8 captions |
| Mass performance depends on the metric | On the ABO subset, GPT-6-Astra Artisan has **APE 0.260, ADE 5.86 kg, MnRE 0.773**; Agy has **0.296, 5.49 kg, 0.775**, respectively. Artisan improves APE but loses on ADE and MnRE. | Table 9 |
| Physical feasibility does not ensure demonstration compatibility | Of **80 RoboCasa assets per group**, Round 1 / Round 2 pass counts are **76/35 Artisan**, **31/6 Articraft**, and **78/61 native** under the demonstration-collection protocol. | §E.2 |
| Interactive preference favors Artisan | Across ArtVIP/LightWheel objects, Artisan receives **43.3% first-place / 85.1% top-three** selections versus Codex’s **21.1% / 68.7%**; the study has **30 students and 275 valid responses**. | §5.2; §B.4; Figure 6 |

These results support improved physical behavior under the specified simulator tests. They do not establish collision freedom throughout continuous configuration space or real-world validity. The authors report better held-out robotic policy success, but the supplied text omits the numerical success-rate tables, preventing assessment of effect sizes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified:** Plausible mechanisms can fail trajectory transfer; a hinged kettle button does not match MimicGen’s sliding-button demonstration (§E.2; Figure 15). Real-world deployment requires additional validation (Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference:** Sampling and approximate convex collision geometry limit guarantees beyond tested configurations (§A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Independent review’s benefits are narrower than “eliminating” self-evaluation bias: removing review slightly worsens fidelity and mass metrics but leaves collisions similar, including a lower multi-joint rate (§C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Robotic comparisons reflect both asset quality and compatibility with existing collection pipelines; failed assets are excluded from training (Appendix E).
- **Evidence gap:** No uncertainty estimates accompany the reported results. Human preferences come from non-expert students (§B.4). Artisan incurs higher API costs, but actual mean cost and time values are missing (Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **Do gains survive denser or different physical tests?** Validation uses sampled configurations in MuJoCo. Next experiment: evaluate the same assets with denser motion trajectories and another simulator.
- **Which construction aids contribute independently?** Reported ablations isolate agents and physics feedback, but not connectors or anchor placement. Next experiment: remove each SDK extension while holding model and budget fixed.
- **Can physically valid assets support more demonstration transfer?** Many Artisan assets fail Round 2 despite passing Round 1. Next experiment: compare fixed trajectory transfer against interaction annotations or mechanism-adaptive collection (§E.2).
- **What is the quality–cost tradeoff?** Higher cost is acknowledged without accessible mean values. Next experiment: sweep generation budgets and report physical quality and qualification rates against cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the physical results and several ablations, but lacks spatial-fidelity table values, downstream policy success rates, complete component-ablation tables, mean API costs/times, statistical uncertainty, and verified release details.
