# TouchScale: 500 Hours of Human Vision and Touch for Visual-Tactile Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10288) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10288)


## One-sentence takeaway
TouchScale pairs 500 hours of consistently captured human vision and touch with evaluations showing improved cross-sensor tactile prediction, action recognition, and robot manipulation, with generally favorable trends as training data increases (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Video captures hand motion but omits contact and pressure needed to understand physical interaction. The authors identify a shortage of large human visual–tactile datasets collected through one consistent sensing pipeline: smaller datasets restrict scaling studies, while pooling different sensors and collection protocols confounds comparisons (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Dataset and capture pipeline:** Synchronized head RGB-D, two wrist RGB streams, and full-hand bimanual normal-pressure measurements. TouchScale contains approximately 87K episodes, approximately 2K task descriptions, and more than 1.5K objects; each glove has 880 taxels. Collection spans approximately 20 participants, with visual checks and manual review for suspected sensor failures (§3; Fig. 1; §8).
- **Transfer evaluations:** Existing TouchAnything and Hiera-B models assess cross-sensor tactile prediction and tactile-supervised visual representation learning. TouchScale also supplies action-free future-tactile mid-training for an existing N₀-VTLA robot policy before robot-specific training; human motion retargeting is unnecessary (§4).
- **Controlled-pipeline scaling study:** The authors vary TouchScale data quantity while retaining the sensing pipeline, reporting improved tactile prediction and robot success. The principal novelty is the dataset and its empirical evaluation; the predictive architectures are adapted from prior work (§§1, 4).

The authors promise public release of synchronized recordings and reconstructed object models; the supplied text does not establish that release has occurred (Abstract; §11).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better size-matched cross-sensor transfer | Same TouchAnything architecture, approximately 16 h TouchScale versus 16.2 h EgoTouch, zero-shot on EgoTactile: cIoU **0.181 vs. 0.134**, vIoU **0.243 vs. 0.185**, CoP localization error **0.416 vs. 0.444**, pressure magnitude error **0.148 vs. 0.184**; shared anatomical-region evaluation | §4.1; Table 2; §6 |
| Favorable tactile scaling endpoints | TouchScale training increases from 10% to 100%, with fixed model and EgoTactile evaluation: cIoU **0.311→0.383**, vIoU **0.252→0.276**, CoP error **0.332→0.317**, pressure magnitude error **0.108→0.105** | §4.1; Fig. 4 |
| Highest compared action recognition accuracy | Same Hiera-B initialization, tactile objective, schedule, and training budget: average accuracy across MECCANO, SSv2, and Ego-Exo4D is **24.45% linear / 49.62% fine-tuned**, versus OpenTouch’s **22.97% / 48.28%**; TouchScale leads every benchmark in both settings | §4.2; Table 3 |
| Improved real-robot success | Same N₀-VTLA checkpoint and 50 robot demonstrations per task; 20 evaluation trials per task. Without→with mid-training: sorting **10%→60%**, cap removal **40%→70%**, tube transfer **30%→60%**, wiping **10%→40%**; average **22.5%→57.5%** | §4.3; Table 4 |
| Robot success generally scales | Average success across the same four tasks: **30.0%, 32.5%, 50.0%, 50.0%, 57.5%** at **20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, 100%** mid-training data; robot post-training protocol held fixed | §4.3; Fig. 6 |
| Future-tactile retrieval improves modestly with scale | Before robot post-training, Train Top-10 target-retrieval accuracy on 5,469 robot samples is **0.18%** without mid-training, **2.87%** at 20% data, and **3.31%** at full scale | §9; Table 5 |
| Limited tactile–action association | Held-out robot data: Pearson correlation between per-step tactile and action prediction errors is **r=0.119, p=0.020** with mid-training, versus **r=0.003, p=0.953** without it | §4.3 |

These results support transfer benefits in the tested settings. They do not establish universal scaling laws or that better future-tactile prediction causes better control. Future-tactile “accuracy” measures latent-target retrieval, rather than calibrated physical pressure prediction (§9).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated:** Robot evaluation covers one platform and four tasks; TouchScale lacks action labels; broader embodiment transfer and the role of future-tactile prediction remain open (§5).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- Regional pooling and sequence-relative pressure normalization limit conclusions about fine-grained taxel prediction and absolute pressure calibration (§6).
- The robot comparison tests adding TouchScale mid-training; no matched extra-training control or vision-only human-data ablation is reported, leaving the source of gains incompletely isolated (§§4.3, 10.2).
- Robot results use limited trials, and the supplied text reports no repeated-seed variability or confidence intervals (Table 4).
- Quality control excludes little-finger and palm contacts from finger-level missing-signal decisions; noise is recorded without independently determining rejection. Residual error rates are unreported (§8).
- Scaling is not uniformly strict: robot success plateaus at intermediate scales, and pooled future-tactile retrieval is nonmonotonic (Fig. 6; Table 5).

## Open research questions

- **Does transfer generalize across robot embodiments?** Only one platform is tested (§5). Repeat matched mid-training comparisons on different hands and contact-rich tasks.
- **Which supervision produces the control gains?** No vision-only or matched extra-training control is reported. Compare tactile supervision, vision-only training, and shuffled tactile targets under equal training budgets.
- **Does future-tactile prediction causally help action selection?** Error correlation is weak (§4.3). Ablate or perturb predicted tactile tokens during otherwise identical robot evaluations.
- **Do scaling gains survive participant and object shifts?** Such held-out breakdowns are not supplied. Evaluate increasing data scales on explicitly unseen participants, objects, and scenes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main comparisons and robot mid-training details are explicit. Material missing information includes exact perception-training configurations, scaling-subset selection, participant/object split rules, repeated-run uncertainty, quantitative quality-control outcomes, detailed robot success criteria, and confirmed release availability.
