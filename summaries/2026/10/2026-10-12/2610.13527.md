# S-Space: Exploring Spatial Workspace in Multimodal Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13527) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13527)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors identify a linear spatial subspace in object-token activations whose readouts support spatial judgments and causal editing, while explicit coordinate rotations outperform native viewpoint reasoning in the reported evaluations (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Multimodal language models can answer spatial questions yet still fail at viewpoint transformations and reference-frame reasoning. End-to-end answers do not reveal whether failures originate in perception or subsequent computation. The authors seek a unified account of how spatial information is encoded, transformed, and used internally (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Spatial-axis identification:** Average role-antisymmetric gradients of spatial-answer logit contrasts across examples, prompt templates, and swapped object orders. The resulting horizontal, vertical, and distance directions define a model- and layer-specific linear subspace (§2.2).
- **Readout and intervention interface:** Project object-name token activations onto these directions to obtain continuous coordinates; compare coordinate differences to answer relative questions. Hidden-state edits change these coordinates while preserving components orthogonal to the subspace (§2.3; Appendix C.3).
- **Mechanistic characterization and diagnosis:** Test coordinate accuracy, causal effects, prompt persistence, and viewpoint transformations. A readout-plus-rotation pipeline separates available spatial information from native answer generation (§§3–5).

The authors claim novelty in systematically characterizing an internal spatial workspace; related spatial encodings and Jacobian-based interpretation are acknowledged in Appendix A.

Together, these results support readable spatial features and a causal contribution to tested spatial judgments. They do not establish a complete or exclusive spatial mechanism. The rotation pipeline demonstrates benefits from supplying external computation; the label-filled reasoning experiment does not independently establish reliable spontaneous viewpoint transformation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coordinate scope:** Horizontal and vertical axes use image-plane positions without disentangling depth-dependent perspective effects (§2.2, footnote).
- **Conditional theoretical guarantee:** Axis recovery assumes linear decomposition, axis-aligned spatial gradients, no non-spatial gradient contribution, and cancellation of contextual effects in expectation. These are sufficient assumptions, not demonstrated universal properties (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—causal scope:** Interventions cover Molmo2-ER, and non-spatial preservation is tested through color judgments. Broader model coverage and semantic selectivity remain unestablished (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—measurement validity:** Depth targets come from Depth Anything V2-Small estimates rather than measured physical depth (Appendices C.1, C.6).
- **Analyst inference—reasoning and training attribution:** Controlled CoT contains correct benchmark relations; emergence from language training and pretraining-induced direction coupling remain author hypotheses rather than experimentally isolated causes (§§4–5; Appendix C.5).
- **Analyst inference—retrieval limits:** Most result tables contain captions but no values, and referenced prompt templates are omitted. Effect sizes, uncertainty, and exact replication cannot be fully assessed.

## Open research questions
- **Does causal selectivity generalize?** Only Molmo2-ER interventions and color controls are reported. Repeat matched interventions across the other models and several non-spatial tasks.
- **Can models transform S-Space during autonomous reasoning?** The tracked CoT supplies label-derived relations. Probe freely generated reasoning and compare coordinate changes between correct and incorrect answers.
- **What training signals create the axes and coupling?** Language-based emergence is hypothesized, while action-stage comparisons do not isolate training causes. Run matched training ablations varying spatial language, action supervision, and map orientation.
- **How well does S-Space recover physical geometry?** Image-plane coordinates and estimated depth leave metric geometry unresolved. Evaluate readouts and rotations on calibrated scenes with measured depth and varied perspective.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods and qualitative findings are clear, but quantitative strength is difficult to assess.

Material missing information includes most accuracy and intervention table values, correlation coefficients, numerical uncertainty estimates, several prompt templates, and action-study sample counts. Code is linked but was not inspected.
