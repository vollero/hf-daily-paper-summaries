# One Model to Loop Them All: Elastic Latent Recurrence for Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12582) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12582)


## One-sentence takeaway
ELR preserves near-baseline one-loop accuracy in matched dense and MoE language-model experiments while improving average benchmark performance through additional latent loops, with extra training and inference compute (§4; Tables 8–9).

## Problem and motivation
Looped language models reuse layers to adjust inference depth without storing separate models, but their shallowest setting often underperforms a comparable non-recurrent model. The authors call this the “one-loop tax”: latency-sensitive deployment still benefits from a separate baseline. ELR targets preserving that shallow endpoint while making additional loops useful, rather than establishing that recurrence beats parameter scaling at matched compute (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Elastic-depth recipe:** Partition the backbone into input, shared recurrent, and output blocks. A learned per-token, per-channel gate controls state revisions; one-loop rank overrides mix shallow and deeper gradients within optimizer updates. Their combination protects one-loop performance (§§3.1–3.2, 6.1–6.4).
- **Late acquisition of recurrence:** Begin training at one loop, then introduce mixed depths. Zero-initialized modulation conditioned on loop progress and budget initially preserves the checkpoint’s function across depths and subsequently improves deeper-loop loss (§§3.3–3.4, 6.5–6.6). Loop conditioning itself builds on prior methods; its integration and initialization are central here (§7).
- **Cost reductions:** Cross-loop route replay reuses first-loop MoE assignments; last-loop KV caching overwrites a shared attention cache during inference (§3.4).
- **End-to-end demonstration:** Koi-4B carries conditioned elastic depth through pretraining, context extension, supervised fine-tuning, and verifiable-reward reinforcement learning (§5; Appendix A.4). The supplied text does not establish a public checkpoint or code release.

| Similar pattern at MoE scale | Unconditioned 30B-A3B hybrid, trained for 300B tokens: mean 66.08 versus baseline 65.04 at one loop; four loops reach 70.83, gaining 4.75 points. | §4.2; Table 9 |
| Mixing gradients matters beyond sampling frequency | GPT-0.5B, matched one-loop probability 0.32: one-loop validation loss is 1.6871 with synchronized weighted sampling, 1.6830 with overrides, and 1.6774 with independent-rank sampling; baseline 1.6852. | Late training limits estimated overhead | Matched hybrid runs introduce mixed depths in the final quarter; estimated layer-execution cost is 1.16× baseline. This is an estimate, not measured total training cost. | §4.1; Eq. 5 |
| Conditioning improves delayed-training loss | GPT-0.5B, final-quarter mixed depths: conditioning reduces five-loop validation loss to 1.6147 versus 1.6273 for unconditioned mixed-depth training throughout; adds 31.6M parameters. | §6.6 |
| Replay primarily accelerates training | 30B-A3B: replay training reduces mixed-depth update time 11.7%; four-loop validation loss increases 0.0038. Four-loop ten-task means are 70.90 with replay versus 70.83 dynamically routed. Inference forward time falls 1.2% on 8K sequences. | Table 1; §4.3 |
| Post-trained depth scaling | Koi-4B’s authors’ harness estimates AA-Index v4.1 at 17.52, 25.57, 27.93, and 29.23 across one through four loops; CritPt is unrun and assigned zero. | Table 10; Appendix C |
| Attention-cache savings involve tradeoffs | Koi-4B at four loops: last-loop caching reduces attention KV memory from 2.5× to 1× one-loop memory. AA-LCR falls from 68.3 to 65.3; non-hallucination rises from 85.9 to 88.2. | Table 2; §5.3 |

These results support an elastic accuracy–compute tradeoff on the tested models. They do not establish uniform improvements on every task, statistical equivalence at one loop, or superiority to compute-matched larger models. Koi’s comparisons lack a matched training baseline. The introduction’s MoE baseline-relative gain differs from the detailed results; the table above follows §4.2 and Table 9.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** No depth extrapolation evaluation, adaptive per-token serving, or MoE post-training demonstration; fixed-depth specialists still outperform mixed-depth models at their own depths (§§6.2, 7–8).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate parity masks task-level differences and nonmonotonic results. For example, dense-model code scores do not consistently improve with every loop (Table 8).
- **Analyst inference:** Exact parameter equality is approximate: adaptive gates add parameters, and Koi conditioning adds further parameters (Appendices A, A.4).
- **Evaluation limits:** Koi scores are unofficial harness estimates. Cache comparisons use one run per setting; reported uncertainty captures question variation, not run variation (Appendix C.2).
- **Material cache failure mode:** Last-loop caching causes more GDPval timeouts and incomparable judged subsets, preventing a last-loop AA-Index composite (Appendix C.2).
- **Ablation boundary:** The default small-model ablations omit input re-injection; its state-update comparison was not explicitly rerun with re-injection (Appendix F.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can deeper-loop gains be strengthened?** Later loops receive fewer training examples; causality remains untested. Compare loop distributions at matched training compute (§§4.2, 8).
- **Why do endpoint gaps to specialists persist?** Conditioning does not close them fully. Compare conditioning components and depth-specific optimization against fixed-depth specialists (§8).
- **Can depth adapt during serving?** Budget conditioning complicates changing depth mid-request. Test adaptive stopping against fixed-depth policies at matched latency (§8).
- **Can cache savings preserve agentic reliability?** GDPval timeout failures remain unresolved. Repeat paired evaluations with controlled deadlines and analyze failing trajectories (Appendix C.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported experimental patterns; **medium** in broad generalization. Missing information includes public artifact availability, exact corpus subset composition, repeated-training uncertainty, matched Koi baselines, and controlled end-to-end workload latency measurements.
