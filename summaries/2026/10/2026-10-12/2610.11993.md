# DataVista: Diagnosing Multimodal LLMs on Data Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11993) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11993)


## One-sentence takeaway
DataVista evaluates chart reading, temporal reasoning, and narrative understanding in real data videos, finding that the strongest tested model achieves 70.0% exact-match accuracy under sampled-frame evaluation and struggles particularly with causal evidence and narrative structure (§4.2–4.5).

## Problem and motivation
Data videos combine animated charts, narration, and scene transitions, requiring models to locate quantitative evidence, integrate it across charts and time, and interpret how presentation supports an argument. The authors identify a gap between general-video benchmarks and static-chart benchmarks: neither systematically evaluates these abilities together in continuous data videos (§1–2).

This matters for understanding data journalism, business reporting, and science communication, where reading individual values correctly is insufficient to understand the overall claim (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 961 real-world videos and 6,775 single-choice or multiple-select questions across Economy, Society, Science, Politics, and Culture. The authors claim this is the first dedicated data-video understanding benchmark (§1, §3).
- **Capability framework:** Three levels—data perception, temporal reasoning, and narrative understanding—with 10 question types covering chart facts, cross-chart relationships, evidential support, and narrative/design judgments (§3.2).
- **Construction and diagnostics:** Gemini-3-Flash assists screening and evidence extraction; experts verify videos, write questions, filter text-answerable candidates, and independently review retained items. Evaluation covers 19 MLLMs, input ablations, reasoning variants, and response-error analysis (§3.4, §4, Appendix E).

The contribution is an evaluation asset and diagnostic framework; the paper does not introduce a new comprehension model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial model–human gap | Gemini-3.1-Pro: **70.0%**; GPT-5: **67.4%**; Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct: **57.3%** exact match, pooling 50-frame conditions with/without subtitles and no audio. Humans: **91.7%**, using complete videos and original audio. | Table 1, §4.1–4.2 |
| Full audiovisual input leaves a gap | Gemini-3.1-Pro reaches **73.5% overall** and **59.4% narrative accuracy** with complete video and original audio, versus humans’ **91.7%** and **86.8%**. | §4.2, Appendix E.2 |
| Causal and narrative tasks remain difficult | Five-model mean with frames and subtitles: Causal Reasoning **39.2%**, Cross-Temporal Change **73.6%**, Narrative Structure **49.6%** exact match; best causal score **44.2%**. | §4.5, Figure 6 |
| Frames mainly help perception | Adding frames to question-only input raises five-model mean accuracy by **32.0 percentage points** on perception, **14.7** on temporal reasoning, and **5.1** on narrative understanding; no audio. | §4.3, Figure 4 |
| Subtitles mainly help temporal reasoning | Adding subtitles to frame input raises five-model mean temporal accuracy from **52.6% to 59.1%**, versus gains of **2.9 points** for perception and **2.4** for narrative understanding. | §4.3, Figure 4 |
| Denser sampling has uneven benefits | Increasing from **8 to 32 frames**, without subtitles/audio, improves five-model mean accuracy by **11.6 points** on perception, **4.7** on temporal reasoning, and **2.1** on narrative understanding. | §4.4, Figure 5 |
| Partial correctness exceeds complete answers | For multiple-select Argument Synthesis with subtitles, five-model mean option-level **F1 is 88.0%**, but exact match is **53.9%**. | §4.5, Appendix E.5 |
| Errors involve extraction and evidence judgment | Among **120 sampled incorrect Gemini-3.1-Pro responses**, using 50 frames plus subtitles: **47.5%** perceptual, **42.5%** reasoning, and **10.0%** instruction-understanding errors. | §4.6 |

These results establish substantial difficulty under the tested protocols, with limited narrative gains from additional input. They do not isolate the entire human–model gap as a reasoning deficit: humans receive richer input in the main comparison. Native audiovisual results strengthen the gap finding for two models, while error proportions describe sampled failures rather than all responses.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** The benchmark excludes generation and editing, uses multiple-choice questions rather than open-ended subjective items, and primarily covers English videos (Appendix F).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- **Sampling confound:** Uniform frames can miss brief evidence; documented audiovisual cases recover such information (Appendix E.2).
- **Format confound:** Multiple-select items concentrate in several higher-level tasks, and exact match penalizes incomplete option sets. F1 diagnostics partly address this, but cross-task accuracy differences also reflect answer format (§3.3, Appendix E.5).
- **Selection effects:** Filtering removes questions answered correctly by one text-only model. Cross-model checks support reduced textual shortcuts, but do not establish complete visual dependence (§3.4, Appendix B.3.1).
- **Restricted coverage:** Economy dominates the video collection. Equal-domain weighting preserves the five representative models’ ranking, but does not establish generalization beyond the collected sources (Appendices B.5, E.1).
- **Contamination remains uncertain:** Upload-date and view-count analyses find no systematic advantage for older or popular videos; these indirect checks cannot prove absence of training overlap (Appendix E.9).

## Open research questions

- **Would targeted sampling improve higher-level understanding?** Uniform sampling misses brief evidence, while denser sampling mainly helps perception. Compare uniform, chart-aware, and question-directed sampling at equal frame budgets (§4.4, Appendix E.2).
- **Can explicit evidence verification improve causal and narrative answers?** Models sometimes extract facts correctly but misjudge support. Test timestamped evidence selection followed by option-by-option verification against direct answering (§4.6, Appendix E.8).
- **How much difficulty comes from multiple-select completeness?** Precision exceeds recall, and F1 exceeds exact match. Compare matched tasks across single-choice, multiple-select, and expert-scored open-ended formats (Appendix E.5).
- **Do findings transfer across languages?** The corpus is primarily English. Evaluate parallel questions on multilingual videos with verified subtitles and comparable topic distributions (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and main results; **medium** for broader capability interpretations.

Material gaps include omitted bodies of several appendix tables, complete prompt templates, detailed per-type counts, exact model-level confidence intervals, and direct evidence of training contamination. Repository contents and release availability were not independently verified; the paper reports annotation releases and describes the evaluation service and leaderboard as future work.
