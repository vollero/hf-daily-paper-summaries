# SepGen: Multi-Stem Audio-Video Separation and Generation in a Single Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11361) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11361)


## One-sentence takeaway
SepGen adapts a frozen audio-video generator to jointly produce two captioned audio stems alongside its video and soundtrack, with the same weights also separating observed recordings and outperforming tested separators, especially on speech (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Joint audio-video generators output one mixed soundtrack, preventing individual sources from being moved, removed, or spatially rendered. Applying a text-conditioned separator afterward requires additional runs and can return the same source for similar queries, such as two speakers (§1).

The authors address the gap between generating a complete scene and making its sound sources individually accessible. Spatial rendering motivates the work, but an integrated renderer is left for future work (§5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint stem generation and separation:** SepGen appends two stem token sequences to the frozen LTX-2.5 backbone’s audio stream and trains audio-side LoRA adapters. Both stems share the soundtrack context while receiving separate source captions (§3).
- **Protected backbone and source routing:** Attention masks prevent stems from influencing the soundtrack or video; soundtrack tokens bypass the adapter. Aligned temporal positions connect corresponding moments across tracks, and Cross-Stem Attention Guidance uses the sibling caption as a negative branch (§§3.1–3.2).
- **One checkpoint, two modes:** Separation holds the observed soundtrack clean. Generation denoises it alongside the stems, which switch to its estimated clean representation during sampling. Separation training followed by noisy-soundtrack continuation supports both modes (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Evaluation assets:** The authors construct generation and cross-generator benchmarks, evaluate real recordings and reference stems, and report listening studies. They state that code, checkpoints, and datasets are available; availability was not independently checked (Abstract; §4; Appendices B–F).

The “first” multi-stem joint audio-video framework claim is the authors’ characterization, not independently established here (§2).

These results support better caption-addressed isolation within the tested settings. CLAP identity differences are not significant against FlowSep and AudioSep in generation, so superiority is not uniform across metrics (Table S4). Reference-stem tests strengthen the fidelity evidence, but phase-invariant measures do not establish sample-exact reconstruction. The experiments do not establish successful spatial rerendering.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The layout supports only two stems. The decoder regenerates phase, and stems retain reverberation and distance cues that require treatment before spatial rendering (§5).
- **Caption ambiguity:** Same-instrument duets with identical captions yield LSD **11.7 dB**, versus **6.3 dB** for distinct-instrument duets; permutation matching barely helps. Source identity remains difficult when captions cannot distinguish it (Appendix F).
- **Metric dependence:** Main benchmarks lack reference stems. Judge shares an encoder family with SAM Audio, and AudioSep uses the scorer’s CLAP checkpoint. Decoder and bandwidth controls reduce alternative explanations but do not make the metrics definitive (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Scripted speech captions provide unusually strong information. Removing lines substantially worsens SepGen WER and causes some silent outputs, limiting transfer of the headline speech result to transcript-free use (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Ten clips per listening study and broad clip-bootstrap intervals limit population-wide preference claims. Short clips and screened usable Veo scenes also constrain generalization (Appendices B–C).
- **Preservation qualification:** Adapter-on/off outputs are bit-identical under matched inputs, but comparison with the stock pipeline includes rounding and sampler differences (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
1. **Can the layout scale beyond two sources?** The current implementation is fixed at two (§5). Train and evaluate layouts with more stems, measuring leakage, fidelity, and sampling cost.
2. **Can identical-category sources be reliably addressed?** Identical instrument captions remain ambiguous (Appendix F). Compare positional or identity-specific captions on same-instrument mixtures with reference stems.
3. **When does visual conditioning improve separation?** Video removal has no significant effect in the tested setting (§4.5). Evaluate ambiguous audio scenes where visual activity identifies the intended source.
4. **Do the stems enable accurate novel-view sound rendering?** Placement is demonstrated qualitatively, while renderer integration remains future work (§5; Appendix G). Measure localization accuracy and perceptual spatial fidelity after handling retained scene acoustics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark findings; **medium** for broader generalization.

The supplied text does not establish release accessibility, inference latency or peak memory, training wall-clock cost, performance beyond two sources or on long recordings, quantitative placement accuracy, or end-to-end spatial-rendering quality.
