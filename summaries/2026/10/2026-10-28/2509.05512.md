# Quaternion Approximate Networks for Enhanced Image Classification and Oriented Object Detection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.05512) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05512)


## One-sentence takeaway
QUAN uses real-valued approximations of quaternion convolution to reduce model parameters, reporting strong CIFAR-10 and robotic detection results but weaker COCO and DOTA performance, while rotation equivariance remains insufficiently validated.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target compact, orientation-aware vision models for robotic manipulation. They argue that conventional detectors lose orientation information through axis-aligned boxes, while existing quaternion networks and specialized rotation-aware architectures impose implementation or computational overhead (§I–II).

The proposed gap is practical integration of quaternion-inspired operations into standard classification and detection architectures without sacrificing parameter efficiency (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Approximate quaternion convolution:** Map RGB into four quaternion components, then apply four component-wise real convolutions and fixed signed mixing instead of the full Hamilton convolution. Custom CUDA kernels fuse operations; feature representations need not retain unit norm (§III-A–B, Eqs. 4–10).
- **Normalization and architectural components:** Introduce Independent Quaternion Batch Normalization (IQBN) and adapt YOLO blocks into QC3k2, QSPPF, and QC2PSA, including quaternion spatial attention (§III-C–D).
- **Orientation-aware detection objective:** Combine classification and localization losses with quaternion angular distance, norm regularization, and prediction smoothness (§III-E).
- **Cross-task evaluation:** Apply QUAN to classification and axis-aligned/oriented detection. The authors claim quaternion-network state of the art, but the supplied text lacks the underlying comparison-table entries needed to assess that claim (§IV-C–D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Compact CIFAR-10 classifier | QUAN-WRN reports **95.12% accuracy with 717K parameters**, using **74% fewer parameters than WRN-16-4**; baseline accuracy is unavailable. | §IV-C; Table I caption and surrounding prose |
| Smaller ImageNet model | QUAN reports **5.39M parameters versus 21.8M for ResNet34** on the stated ImageNet subset; “without sacrificing accuracy” is asserted without accessible accuracy values. | §IV-A, IV-C |
| Classification training overhead | Training time increased **6.8% on average** across classification experiments; the precise comparator and timing protocol are unspecified. | §IV-C |
| Better robotic axis-aligned detection, slower inference | QUAN versus YOLO11n: **0.9548 versus 0.951 mAP50–95**, **25.3% of the parameters**, and **36% more inference time** on the robotic dataset. | §IV-D1; Table II discussion |
| Lower COCO detection accuracy | QUAN versus YOLO11n: **0.339 versus 0.3908 mAP50–95**, with **12% more inference time**. Authors acknowledge unmatched batch sizes and hyperparameters. | §IV-D1 |
| Better robotic oriented detection | QUAN versus YOLO11n-obb: **76.4 versus 68.5**, with **3.7% lower inference time**. The prose does not identify the accuracy metric. | §IV-D2; Table III discussion |
| Slightly lower DOTA performance | QUAN versus baseline: **60.8 versus 61.4 mAP50–95**, as reported; the baseline name is not repeated in this sentence. | §IV-D2 |
| IQBN improves reported CIFAR-10 accuracy | Traditional quaternion batch normalization reportedly yields **0.61% less accuracy on average** than IQBN; whether this means percentage points is unclear. Poincaré mapping and SiLU are favored qualitatively. | §IV-E; Fig. 5 discussion |

These reports support parameter savings and task-dependent performance tradeoffs. They do not establish universal accuracy or speed improvements. The supplied text contains captions but no data rows for Tables I–III, limiting baseline verification. Detection performance alone also does not establish mathematical rotation equivariance or successful robotic manipulation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** COCO and DOTA results trail the baselines; the authors suggest reduced model capacity as a cause but do not isolate it experimentally (§IV-D).
- **Author-stated:** Computational constraints prevented matching original detection models’ batch sizes and hyperparameters, weakening controlled comparisons (§IV-D1).
- **Author-stated:** Approximation adds arithmetic overhead, only partly mitigated by CUDA kernels; the preferred mapping may not suit every downstream task (§V).
- **Analyst inference:** Equivariance claims exceed the supplied evidence. No explicit transformation law, proof, rotation-sweep evaluation, or numerical equivariance-error measurement is presented (§III–IV).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported ablations address mapping, activation, and normalization, but do not isolate the convolution approximation, attention blocks, CUDA fusion, or individual orientation-loss terms (§IV-E).
- **Analyst inference:** The loss specification leaves implementation questions: loss weights, sequential-prediction construction, and quaternion normalization before angular losses are unspecified; several printed expressions are ambiguous (§III-E).
- **Analyst inference:** Live robotic utility remains untested in the supplied results; live assembly deployment is listed as future work (§V).

## Open research questions
1. **How much rotation equivariance survives the approximation and IQBN?** No direct equivariance measurement is supplied. A next experiment could measure feature equivariance error and detection accuracy across controlled image rotations.
2. **Do detection gaps reflect capacity or training differences?** Both explanations remain unresolved (§IV-D). Compare QUAN and real-valued detectors under matched training settings, then repeat with matched parameter budgets.
3. **Which components drive the robotic detection improvement?** Current ablations do not separate architectural and loss contributions. Ablate quaternion mixing, IQBN, attention, and orientation-loss terms while reporting detection accuracy, orientation error, and latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method description and narrative results support the main tradeoffs, but important evidence is unavailable or ambiguous:

- Data rows for Tables I–III and numerical Fig. 5 contents.
- CIFAR-100 and ImageNet accuracy values, complete baseline results, and repeated-run uncertainty.
- Robotic oriented-detection metric definition and dataset split details.
- Absolute latency, timing protocol, and detailed CUDA comparisons.
- Loss weights, orientation-target construction, and direct equivariance tests.
- Code contents and release completeness; only a link is supplied.
