# RynnWorld-Latent: A Cross-Embodiment World Model Conditioned on Universal Latent Actions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36791) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36791)


## One-sentence takeaway
RynnWorld-Latent uses shared latent actions to pretrain a controllable video world model across human and robot videos, with reported prediction and downstream policy gains after robot-specific adaptation, but without establishing physical consistency.

## Problem and motivation
Native robot actions differ across embodiments, while human interaction videos lack robot control labels. Task-level language also underspecifies movement timing and trajectories. The authors address the resulting gap: a shared, temporally precise conditioning interface for learning visual dynamics from heterogeneous interaction videos (§1).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Shared latent-action conditioning.** Frozen RynnLAM encodes visual transitions into continuous motion, action, and camera descriptors. These condition a text-free Cosmos3-Edge video model pretrained on 23 datasets containing approximately 553.5M frames. RynnLAM itself comes from prior work; its use for world-model conditioning is the contribution here (Abstract; §§3.2–3.3, 4.1).
2. **Global and local action injection.** Global attention tokens combine with an identity-initialized local pathway aligned to future video latent frames. Matching action-extraction stride to video compression enables this correspondence (§§3.2–3.3).
3. **Two synthetic-data pipelines.** Robot-specific action encoders enable digital teleoperation; transferred latent actions plus edited initial frames enable robotized video synthesis. The latter receives pseudo robot actions through retargeting and inverse kinematics, followed by filtering (§3.5).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. The supplied text includes table captions and narrative results, but omits the table bodies.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better public-benchmark prediction | On GR-1 EgoDex Eval, LPIPS is 0.19 versus literature-reported DreamDojo-14B’s 0.21 after robot post-training; authors report improvements across all three benchmark sets and metrics. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Frame-level action conditioning beats task text | On Marvin-WUJI/Astribot-S1, replacing action conditioning with text changes PSNR from 21.86/15.96 to 19.80/13.60 and LPIPS from 0.12/0.34 to 0.30/0.55. | §4.6; Table 3 |

| Digital teleoperation improves physical policy success | For π₀.₅, Real + Digital Teleop versus Real only raises Plate-Placement success from 82.86% to 91.43% and Steak-Serving from 40.00% to 54.29%; 35 trials per task. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Synthetic mid-training improves simulation policies | LIBERO/LIBERO-Plus success changes from 94.4%/53.6% to 96.1%/79.4% for π₀, and from 96.9%/84.4% to 97.8%/84.8% for π₀.₅. | §4.5; Table 6 |

These results support prediction fidelity and synthetic-data utility in the evaluated pipelines. Quantitative prediction uses recorded native robot commands, rather than latent actions extracted from future test frames (§4.1). The evidence does not isolate the latent representation from pretraining-data access, separate synthetic-data benefits from added optimization compute, or establish causal action identification and physical correctness.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Latent actions can encode camera motion and appearance; image metrics and qualitative examples do not establish physical validity (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Generated observations and retargeted pseudo-actions come from separate processes. Synchronization and filtering cannot guarantee consistency (§3.5).
- **Author-stated:** Real-world results use 35 trials per task; small gains are observed differences, without claims of statistical significance (§4.4).
- **Author-stated:** Literature-reported benchmark baselines differ in backbone, data, and training; synthetic mid-training also adds compute (§§4.2, 4.5).
- **Scope:** Real-robot prediction tests held-out demonstrations of training tasks after adaptation, rather than unseen tasks or zero-shot embodiments (§4.2; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is difficult to assess from this retrieval because numerical table bodies and several pipeline details are unavailable.

## Open research questions

1. **How physically consistent are generated videos and pseudo-actions?** Their independent construction leaves alignment unresolved. A next experiment could execute pseudo-actions in matched physical scenes and compare contacts and object trajectories (§3.5).
2. **What causes the gains: representation, data, or compute?** Current comparisons do not separate these factors. A matched-data, matched-compute study could compare latent-action interfaces and synthetic versus real-data continuation training (§§4.5–4.6).
3. **How broadly does transfer generalize?** Evaluated robots receive adaptation, and real-world test tasks occur in training. Leave-one-embodiment-out and held-out-task evaluations could measure adaptation requirements (§4.2).
4. **How does error accumulate over long rollouts?** Chunk chaining is supported, but long-horizon consistency remains future work. Horizon-swept tests could measure drift, contact failures, and action adherence (§§3.5, 5).
