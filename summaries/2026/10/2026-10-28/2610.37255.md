# Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness: The Decision Geometry of Agent Interfaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37255) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under KL-regularized control, harness changes affect both achievable return and policy mismatch, explaining why interfaces that hurt a frozen agent can outperform its original setup after adaptation (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation

Agent harnesses determine prompts, tools, execution, and control flow, yet candidate harnesses are commonly compared using one frozen model. The authors argue that this confounds what a harness offers with how well a checkpoint trained elsewhere uses it: learned action preferences may depend on the training interface (§1).

The missing distinction is between changes to optimal decisions and changes to the decisions that fixed weights express through a new interface.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Identify what behavior encodes.** Optimal policy log odds recover relative action scores combining long-term action values and reference probabilities. They do not uniquely identify rewards, transitions, harness machinery, or absolute return (§3.1; Appendix C).
2. **Separate transfer errors.** Deployed score error decomposes into target shift, residual training error, and interface-induced readout drift. Controlled presentation changes isolate the last component (§4).
3. **Connect errors to return.** Applying an existing regularized performance-gap identity yields \(J=C-M\): optimal regularized return minus occupancy-weighted policy mismatch. The authors derive the mismatch threshold required for adaptation to beat the original pair (§5).
4. **Measure and test the framework.** Exact MiniWoB++ control graphs support post-training and transfer experiments; sampled-action estimates and a teacher-based coding-agent study test practical diagnostics (§6).

The contribution is the harness-specific framework, decomposition, adaptation criterion, and empirical tests; the underlying Gibbs policy and performance-gap identity are credited to prior work.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Presentation alone can severely damage behavior | Reversing observation-field order lowers the H0-trained Qwen3.5-2B checkpoint’s success from **85.0% to 19.2%**, while preserving optimal decisions; evaluation uses the shared **576-task** MiniWoB++ test set. | §1; §4.2; Appendix F.2 |
| Post-training learns harness-specific preferences | Centered-score geometry error falls **69%–90%** across six harnesses on training probes and held-out calibration tasks; each deployment harness’s lowest final error comes from its own trained checkpoint. | §3.2, Fig. 3; Appendix F.1 |
| Batched-click adaptation beats further training alone | H1 initially harms the frozen H0 checkpoint; after matched additional training, H1 adaptation beats both the source pair and H0 continuation in success and regularized return, with paired intervals excluding zero. | §5.3, Fig. 4; Appendix F.3 |
| Pre-adaptation diagnostics predict transfer gains better than frozen loss | Among nine rejected swaps with positive recovery thresholds, all seven ceiling-increasing swaps improve after adaptation. Across eight prospectively recorded swaps, predicted gains have Spearman **ρ=0.95**, versus **0.17** for frozen return change and **0.86** for ceiling gain alone. | §5.3; Appendix F.4 |
| Sampled mismatch predicts deployment changes | Across **72** held-out configuration comparisons, prediction RMSE is **0.00230**; all **70** changes outside a **0.01** tie region have the correct direction, and three-class predictions are correct in **71/72** comparisons. Oracle ceilings and optimal distributions remain available. | Appendices F.5–F.6 |
| Teacher-based estimates reproduce coding-harness ordering | Frozen Qwen3.5-9B accuracy ranges from **47.9% to 64.1%** across five harnesses on **39** Terminal-Bench Lite tasks. Estimates from **three** probe tasks rank all five correctly using ceiling minus mismatch; either term alone misorders them. | §6.3; Appendix F.7 |

The controlled experiments support a causal separation between unchanged optimal decisions and changed model behavior. They also demonstrate that frozen evaluation can reject beneficial adaptation opportunities. The coding study establishes ranking agreement in this setting, rather than an exact decomposition from a known optimum or evidence that adapting those coding harnesses would improve accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Stated theoretical conditions:** Exact results assume finite state/action spaces, bounded rewards, positive reference support, fixed comparison objectives, and predetermined alignments. Log-score results additionally require positive policy probabilities (Appendix B).
- **Stated approximation limits:** The coding teacher need not be optimal; its mismatch estimate measures distance to the teacher. Action categories merge operations with different arguments and effects (Appendix F.7).
- **Analyst inference—limited external validity:** Controlled training uses three restricted MiniWoB++ families, compiled graphs, complete single-token action menus, and exact policy gradients. Transfer to unrestricted agent training remains unestablished (Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference—measurement cost:** The selected sampled estimator requires substantial repeated querying, while retaining oracle access. Its deployment practicality without that access is unresolved (Appendices F.5–F.6).
- **Analyst inference—coding selection risk:** Diagnostics use three probe tasks and exclude trajectories lacking complete probes, with different retained counts across harnesses. Robustness to probe selection and incomplete trajectories is untested (Appendix F.7).
- **Uncertainty scope:** MiniWoB++ bootstrap intervals describe task variation at fixed checkpoints, rather than variability across independent training runs (Appendices F.2, F.6).

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive are coding-harness rankings to the teacher?** Only one teacher is evaluated. Repeat the diagnostics with several teachers and compare ranking stability against benchmark accuracy (§6.3).
2. **Can diagnostics retain value with cheaper measurements?** The successful estimator uses intensive sampling and oracle information. Test reduced query budgets and learned optimal-policy surrogates on held-out harnesses (Appendices F.5–F.6).
3. **Do predicted coding opportunities survive adaptation?** Coding experiments keep the target model frozen. Run matched-budget adaptation and continuation for harnesses estimated to have high ceilings and high mismatch (§6.3).
4. **Can training reduce readout drift across unseen formats?** Presentation perturbations expose failure but no robustness intervention is tested. Compare canonical training with format-randomized training on unseen equivalent presentations (§4.2).
