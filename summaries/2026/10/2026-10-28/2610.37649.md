# Can LLMs Uncover Hidden Physical Laws through Free Exploration?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37649) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37649)


## One-sentence takeaway
LongChang finds that experimentally supported discovery of hidden simulated physical laws is rare without guidance: GPT-6-Astra achieves 16.7% discovery, while the other eleven evaluated LLMs achieve 0% (§4, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Can an LLM notice an unexpected phenomenon, formulate its own research question, and establish a physical law through experiments? The authors distinguish this ability from recovering laws under assigned discovery goals or supplied anomaly descriptions. They argue that autonomous question formation matters for investigating observations that scientists might otherwise overlook (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Free-exploration framework:** LongChang provides MuJoCo worlds with hidden altered physics, structured observations, object manipulation, and optional apparatus construction. Its base set contains an ordinary-physics control and fourteen scenes that each alter one law (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Evidence-based assessment:** A separate LLM judge checks law correctness and experimental support independently; discovery requires both. Reports must preserve interpretations recorded during exploration and cite supporting turns (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Automated scene evolution:** Designer and reviewer agents generate, refine, and compose scenes using unresolved questions and corrected errors in exploration records (§§3.1, 5).
- **Empirical study:** Model comparisons and interventions examine guidance, interaction budgets, and apparatus access, alongside qualitative investigation records (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery is rare | Across twelve LLMs exploring the same fifteen scenes for up to 100 turns, GPT-6-Astra reaches 16.7% discovery on altered scenes; the other eleven reach 0%. | §§3.4, 4; Table 3 |
| Support and correctness diverge | GPT-6-Astra: 97.6% evidence support and 19.0% law correctness. Claude-Opus-5: 16.7% correctness, with no report passing support. | §4; Table 3 |
| Guidance improves discovery | For GPT-6-Astra, adding a discovery task, altered-physics warning, and scene-specific clue raises discovery from 16.7% to 28.6%; mean exploration falls from 39.3 to 30.9 turns, with support unchanged. | §4.1; Table 4 |
| Ordinary physics prompts false conclusions | Across LLMs, 36.1% of normal-control reports are rejected for false anomaly claims or unsupported explanations. | §4.2 |
| Additional available turns do not help | Raising GPT-6-Astra’s free-exploration limit from 100 to 500 turns without context compaction does not improve discovery; it uses about forty turns under either limit. | §4.3; Table 4 |
| Apparatus access helps | Under a 100-turn limit, enabling construction raises GPT-6-Astra’s discovery from 4.8% to 16.7% and correctness from 4.8% to 19.0%, while action failures increase. | §4.3; Table 4 |
| Evolved scenes defeat complete identification | GPT-6-Astra achieves 0% discovery in each of three evolved scenes, versus 33.3% in source Scene 15 and 66.7% in source Scene 14; evolved-scene support remains 100%. | §5; Table 6 |

These reported results support a distinction between collecting defensible observations and identifying complete laws, including their conditions and triggers. They establish performance within this simulator and judging protocol, not general scientific-discovery ability. Apparatus results concern access to construction; they do not isolate the causal contribution of individual tools (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Known altered laws enable evaluation, whereas real scientific questions may lack established answers; extending assessment to those settings remains unresolved (§7).
- **Author-stated:** LLM judges may endorse unsupported findings (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Judge reliability is uncertain because the supplied text reports no human validation, judge agreement, or judge-sensitivity analysis.
- **Analyst inference:** The guidance intervention bundles a task, warning, and clue, so their individual effects cannot be separated (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Scene evolution is demonstrated using one corrected-error source and three derived scenes; this provides limited evidence for broader evolution effectiveness (§5).
- **Reporting gap:** Table bodies and full scene-law specifications are absent from the retrieved text. Run counts, uncertainty estimates, and complete model comparisons therefore cannot be checked.

## Open research questions
- **What makes agents pursue anomalies?** Contrasting trajectories illustrate different responses but do not isolate their cause (§4.1). Next experiment: hold observations fixed and vary an explicit anomaly-tracking procedure.
- **Which guidance component helps?** The bundled intervention leaves task framing, warning, and clue effects unresolved (§4.1). Next experiment: evaluate these components separately and in combination.
- **Can active use of larger budgets improve discovery?** A higher limit fails when explorers stop early (§4.3). Next experiment: compare voluntary stopping with a protocol requiring unresolved hypotheses to receive follow-up tests.
- **How robust are discovery judgments?** Automated assessment is central, and judge error is acknowledged (§3.3; Appendix A.5). Next experiment: compare blinded expert assessments with multiple independent LLM judges.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly describes the framework and headline findings, but omits substantive table contents, complete scene laws, full evaluation prompts, per-setting run counts, uncertainty estimates, model inference settings, and independent judge validation. The linked code’s contents and reproducibility were not assessed.
