# Learning and Reasoning with Geometry: How Generation Helps Spatial Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36022) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36022)


## One-sentence takeaway
GeoUG combines geometric generation with spatial understanding and learns when to check an initial answer using generated depth, preserving nearly all always-generate accuracy with fewer generation calls on three benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation
Spatial question answering supervises selected object relations, while depth and viewpoint generation can supervise richer scene geometry. The authors investigate two gaps: which generation objectives improve spatial understanding during training, and when generated geometry helps reasoning at inference. They also argue that existing evaluations can reward simple image-plane shortcuts rather than geometric discrimination (§§1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified geometric learning:** GeoUG builds on BAGEL, combining text cross-entropy with flow-matching objectives for depth, camera-conditioned views, or another person’s first-person view. Transfer benefits depend on the generation task (§§3, 5.1).
- **Multi-scale Metric Depth Codec (MMDC):** A parameter-free, invertible encoding maps metric depth into three channels with fixed depth scales, allowing a pretrained frozen visual autoencoder to represent depth without architectural changes (§3.1).
- **Adaptive geometric reasoning:** The model drafts an answer, then retains it or generates depth and queries geometric measurements. Reinforcement learning compares generation against draft correctness while charging a generation cost; forced-generation rollouts estimate the alternative branch (§3.2; Appendix C).
- **3DCueBench:** A benchmark balances selected helpful and misleading 2D cues within geometric difficulty intervals, targeting depth and object-distance comparisons (§4.1; Appendix E.2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive generation preserves accuracy while reducing calls | Circular accuracy / generation rate: CV-Bench-3D **97.9% / 50.3%**, 3DCueBench **90.8% / 67.4%**, DA-2K **94.2% / 72.4%**. Always-generate accuracy: **97.4%, 91.3%, 94.9%**, respectively, at 100% generation. | Table 2 |
| Selection beats random generation at equal rates | Adaptive versus random-gate circular accuracy: **97.9 vs 94.4**, **90.8 vs 86.1**, and **94.2 vs 88.5**, in the same benchmark order. | Table 2 |
| Geometry helps more on difficult comparisons | On 3DCueBench, always-generating versus direct answering gains **23.0 percentage points** in each of the two hardest gap intervals, versus **12.5** and **5.0** in easier intervals; circular accuracy, 200 questions per interval. | Table 12 |
| Strong VLM results do not imply superiority to depth readout | GeoUG exceeds all evaluated open-source VLMs across the three benchmarks, but DA3 depth readout scores **90.9%** versus GeoUG’s **90.8%** on 3DCueBench. | Tables 1, 11 |
| Co-training benefits are task-dependent | Against understanding-only training, depth generation improves DA-2K-Visual circular accuracy by **2.08 points**; multi-view and exo-to-ego improve MindCube by **2.21** and **2.31 points**. Depth generation lowers MMSI camera-motion accuracy. | §5.1; Figure 6 |
| MMDC provides a modest encoding gain | Across five depth datasets after affine alignment, mean **AbsRel×100** decreases from **7.47** with single-scale encoding to **7.24** with MMDC, then **6.23** with additional training data. | Table 5 |
| Benchmark controls selected shortcuts | **800 questions**, equally divided between Depth and Distance; selected cues are correct for half and misleading for half within difficulty intervals. | §4.1; Appendix E.2 |

These results support selective depth acquisition in the evaluated settings. Circular accuracy requires correct answers under both option orders. Fewer generation calls establish a usage reduction, but do not establish proportional latency or total compute savings. Affine-aligned depth scores do not directly validate absolute metric calibration.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified comparison:** Before-answer and draft-conditioned pilots differ in model, supervision, training questions, and credit assignment; they do not isolate decision position (Appendix C).
- **Author-qualified estimation:** Forced rollouts approximate generation value; grouping by draft correctness does not ensure unbiased estimates for every reasoning prefix (Appendix C).
- **Observed failure:** Generated geometry can overturn a correct draft into an incorrect answer (Figure 14).
- **Benchmark scope:** Balancing selected cues does not eliminate all shortcuts. Text-only probes retain some predictability, and labels can depend on the depth convention; very small gaps are excluded (Appendix E.2).
- **Comparison confounds:** Sequential versus joint training is not fully matched; external-tool comparisons vary prompts or resolution (Appendices F.1, F.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence for efficiency is narrower than deployment efficiency because runtime, full training cost, and repeated-run uncertainty are not reported.

## Open research questions
- **Does drafting itself improve selection?** The pilots confound decision position with other changes. Train matched policies differing only in whether the decision follows a draft.
- **How robust are geometric checks to depth and camera errors?** Figure 14 shows harmful revisions, and missing intrinsics trigger an assumed camera. Vary depth noise and intrinsics while measuring corrections and regressions.
- **Does selection generalize beyond the benchmark’s cue design?** Only selected shortcuts and gap ranges are controlled. Test unseen cue families, scene sources, and carefully audited smaller-gap comparisons.
- **Do fewer calls deliver practical savings?** Call rates omit complete execution cost. Measure end-to-end latency and compute, including drafting, generation, and geometric queries.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark findings; **medium** in broader causal and efficiency conclusions.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability for main results, end-to-end timing and compute, unaligned metric-depth calibration, complete co-training optimization settings, and verified availability of code, weights, and benchmark artifacts.
