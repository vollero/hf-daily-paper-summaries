# Truncate or Not? A Scaling Study of Looped Transformers with Truncated Backpropagation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26030) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26030)


## One-sentence takeaway
For the studied dense language models, the fitted scaling law and matched-compute experiments favor fewer fully backpropagated loops over more truncated loops, while two fully trained loops offer substantial parameter savings with a small compute premium (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Looped transformers reuse weights to increase computational depth. Truncating backpropagation saves backward compute and activation memory but introduces biased gradients, potentially reducing the value of recurrence. The authors identify a gap in earlier scaling studies: backward-loop counts were fixed or tied to forward-loop counts, preventing joint optimization of loops, model size, and training tokens (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint scaling law:** Independently vary forward loops \(T\) and backward loops \(B\), alongside parameters and tokens. Replace parameter count in a coupled size–data loss law with \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=N_{\mathrm{once}}+g(T,B)N_{\mathrm{rec}}\), where \(g(T,B)=\exp[c(1-T^{-p}B^{-q})]\) models bounded loop gains and truncation discounts (§4, Eq. 4.1).
- **Conditional truncation analysis:** Show that truncation can help when forward depth is prescribed, but joint forward–backward allocation favors full BPTT under the fitted law; test this with matched-compute runs (§5).
- **Compute-allocation rules:** Derive allocations for fixed versus unrestricted model size, and experimentally compare two fully trained loops with compute-optimal non-looped models (§6).

The study reports 592 training runs, including 347 scaling configurations; 230 configurations fit the law and 117 are reserved for separate tests (§4.1). The architecture and baseline size–data law are adopted from prior work; the contribution is their joint treatment of independent loop counts.

These results support the authors’ allocation claims within the measured dense-model setting. They do not establish universal superiority of full BPTT, downstream reasoning gains, or empirical validity at unbounded depth. The claim that unrestricted model size favors no looping follows from the fitted law and leading-FLOP assumptions (§6.2), with experimental support focused on selected comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only dense models and validation loss are studied; MoE transfer and downstream task benefits remain unestablished (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Experiments stop at 16 forward loops. The gain ceiling and later depth switches are extrapolations; stable much-deeper training is outside scope (§7).
- **Author-stated:** The prelude/coda versus recurrent parameter partition is fixed for each model size rather than independently swept (§7).
- **Model validity:** The finite gain ceiling is an empirical modeling assumption. Integer loop restrictions can alter the continuous optimum, and finite-compute overheads affect practical allocations (§§4.1, 5.2, 6.1).
- **Compute accounting:** Analytic ratios omit attention products, output projection, and optimizer updates. Appendix D.4 explicitly warns that fuller accounting or changing recurrent shares breaks the simple factorization.
- **Analyst inference:** Broader transfer is uncertain because the supplied experiments use one corpus and architectural recipe; the untied-model discrepancy and higher out-of-fit errors show that close central fits do not guarantee equally accurate comparison predictions.
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated, omitting substantial middle material, so this analysis cannot provide complete coverage.

## Open research questions
- **Does the full-BPTT preference extend to MoE?** MoE is excluded (§7). A next experiment could independently sweep forward and backward loops in a looped MoE family under matched compute.
- **Do loss improvements translate into downstream gains?** Only validation loss is measured (§7). Evaluate the matched-compute models on downstream language and reasoning tasks.
- **Does bounded loop gain persist beyond the observed depths?** Depths above 16 are untested (§7). Extend depth sweeps with a stable recipe and test saturation predictions on held-out depths.
- **How does the recurrent parameter share change optimal allocation?** That share is fixed per size (§7). Sweep the partition at fixed total parameters and compute, then refit the law.
