# Harness-Model Collaboration for Long-Horizon Continual Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34724) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34724)


## One-sentence takeaway
HaMo combines retrieved strategy memory with continual adapter training and replay to improve text-agent adaptation and retention, although its experiments do not isolate the contributions of these components.

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents repeatedly encounter reusable procedures but often fail to accumulate them across tasks. The authors identify complementary weaknesses: parameter updates can cause forgetting, while external memory provides immediate reuse without necessarily improving the model’s ability to execute retrieved strategies. Their target is continual acquisition of procedural competence across changing task contexts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Coordinated memory and adaptation:** HaMo distills expert trajectories into experience cards containing the task, initial observation, and strategy outline. Cards accumulate externally; one continually updated LoRA adapter learns to execute retrieved guidance through behavioral cloning and historical replay (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Experience-conditioned execution:** Training and inference share an interface combining task, initial observation, retrieved experience, and evolving interaction history. The intended contribution is learning how to instantiate strategies under changed conditions (§3.2).
- **Retrieval-coverage selection:** Leave-one-out nearest-neighbor retrieval identifies cards that support other cards. Greedy complementary coverage selects training trajectories, with a coverage-curve knee determining the budget. Unselected cards remain retrievable (§3.1).

Optional memory release is described but disabled in every reported experiment (§3.2).

## Evidence and main results

Scores below use each environment’s native metric on a 0–100 scale: ScienceWorld task progress, ALFWorld success rate, and WebShop partial-credit purchase reward (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger within-domain averages | Across four ScienceWorld data-incremental stages, HaMo averages **52.22 vs 38.34** for Base+Harness with Qwen3-8B, and **53.09 vs 40.65** with Qwen3-14B. | §5.1.1; Fig. 3 |
| Highest final scores among listed main baselines | Qwen3-8B, ScienceWorld → ALFWorld → WebShop: final HaMo scores are **52.43, 66.79, 59.64**, respectively; reported margins over the strongest non-HaMo baseline per domain are **13.02, 19.71, 5.16 points**. | §5.1.2; Fig. 4 |
| Earlier-domain retention with residual forgetting | In that stream, ScienceWorld changes **52.43 → 54.19 → 52.43**; ALFWorld falls **70.08 → 66.79** after WebShop training. | §5.1.2 |
| Collaboration improves retention but involves tradeoffs | Final ScienceWorld: HaMo **52.43**, model-only **24.57**. Final ALFWorld: HaMo **66.79**, model-only **69.34**. Model-only excludes both memory and replay. | §5.2.1; Fig. 5 |
| Forgetting depends on training order | In WebShop → ScienceWorld → ALFWorld, ScienceWorld falls **55.19 → 47.42** after subsequent adaptation. | §5.2.2; Figs. 6–7 |

These results support the complete system’s adaptation–retention balance in the tested settings. They do not establish which component causes the gains, complete protection against forgetting, or statistical reliability across repeated runs. Within-domain stage averages also do not isolate retention of individual task types (§§5.1.1, 5.2.1; Future Work).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Forgetting persists and depends on domain order. Separate ablations of replay, anchor selection, experience conditioning, and temperature are absent; repeated runs, longer streams, and end-to-end costs remain future work (Limitations; Future Work).
- **Evaluation scope:** Experiments use text-only benchmark environments and Qwen3 backbones. Transfer to multimodal agents or changing websites is untested (§4; Future Work).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism attribution:** Model-only removes both memory and replay, so its comparison cannot isolate external memory’s contribution (§5.2.1).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** The supplied text leaves the distillation model/prompt, embedding model, replay sampling policy and quantity, exact knee detector, and training-time retrieval/self-match handling unspecified (§3; Algorithm 1).
- **Case-study boundary:** Attempted option clicks produce no visible state change; the authors explicitly avoid treating them as a causal explanation of the reward improvement (§5.2.4; Table 1).

## Open research questions
1. **Which components drive retention?** Their effects remain entangled. Run controlled replay × experience-conditioning ablations, comparing coverage selection with budget-matched random selection (§5.2.1; Future Work).
2. **When should retrieved strategies be rejected or refreshed?** Retrieval similarity does not establish applicability. Test misleading cards and changed preconditions with confidence-based rejection and mid-episode retrieval (Future Work).
3. **Does the balance persist over longer streams at acceptable cost?** Long-stream variability and maintenance costs are unreported. Evaluate repeated extended streams while measuring forgetting, training time, retrieval latency, and memory growth (Future Work).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the framework and principal results, but lacks run counts, uncertainty estimates, detailed data counts and expert-trajectory provenance, several retrieval/selection specifications, controlled component ablations, and measured costs. Code, checkpoints, and experience-card releases are not established.
