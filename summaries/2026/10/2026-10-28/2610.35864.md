# VibeVideoBench: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Code-Driven Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35864) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35864)


## One-sentence takeaway
VibeVideoBench evaluates autonomous agents making videos entirely through code, finding that delivery reliability changes rankings and that strong aggregate judge–human agreement masks substantial uncertainty about individual videos.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target videos requiring exact text, correct calculations, consistent state changes, sustained structure, and coordinated sound. They argue that existing evaluations cover generated clips, static code-produced visuals, or specialized video pipelines rather than general agents independently producing, inspecting, and revising complete audiovisual artifacts (§§1–2).

The evaluation gap is both functional and perceptual: producing a playable file does not establish that its content, motion, or sound satisfies the request.

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 239 self-contained English and Chinese tasks across 12 categories, with target runtimes from 8 seconds to roughly 15 minutes. Tasks combine specifications, optional assets, delivery requirements, audio policies, and explicit creative freedom; no reference video is required (§3).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Programmatic delivery checks (L1), 12,985 requirement-linked rubric checks (L2), and five task-anchored quality dimensions (L3). Three independent judge panels evaluate outputs, with modality routing in one panel. Delivered-only scores and missing-video-as-zero scores separate quality from reliability (§§3.4–4).
- **Empirical study:** Nine complete model–harness systems undergo 2,151 scored rollouts, supplemented by failure analysis, blind human preferences, judge stress tests, and resource ablations (§5; Apps. H–I).

Agents receive one hour in identical CPU-only containers; image/video generation services are unavailable, while TTS is provided. This is a benchmark and analysis contribution, rather than a new video-generation model (§3.1; App. C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Two systems lead delivered quality | Across 239 tasks, three-panel delivered-only L3 means were 4.126/5 for GPT-6 Astra and 4.074/5 for Claude Opus 5.5; human Elo point estimates ranked them first and second. | Table 2; Table H.1 |
| Complete adherence remains difficult | Opus achieved the highest L2 strict success, 76.3%, averaged across panels over all tasks. Success requires no failed core check and at least one passed core check; missing videos fail. | §4; Table 2 |
| Reliability materially changes ranking | Gemini 4 Argon delivered 184/239 videos; its L3 rank fell from fourth on delivered videos to seventh when missing videos scored zero, with means of 3.989 and 3.069 respectively. | Table 2 |
| Most delivery failures concern render planning | Authors assigned 169 of 224 non-deliveries to late/slow final renders or renders never started, after reviewing each failed trajectory. | §5.2; Table F.4 |
| Judge validation is stronger in aggregate | Three-panel delivered-quality rankings correlated with human Elo at Spearman ρ=0.98 across nine systems; pair agreement was 73.3% on human-decisive pairs the mean judge did not tie. | Table 3 |
| Fine audiovisual timing escapes judging | In the J1 stress test, neither injected audio delay—0.5 or 1.5 seconds—was mentioned in any of 15 reports per condition. | App. I.1; Fig. I.1 |
| More CPU cores produced no significant pooled improvement | Across six systems and 20 render-heavy tasks, increasing from 8 to 16 cores changed three-panel quality with missing videos scored zero by +0.13; paired-test p=0.44. | App. I.4; Fig. I.4 |
| Cost and score differ substantially | Under logged list-price API accounting, DeepSeek scored 3.911 at $0.47/task versus Astra’s 4.109 at $8.19/task, with missing videos scored zero. | Table G.1 |

These results support comparisons of complete agent systems under the specified environment. They do not isolate model capability from harness effects or establish that high scores certify an individual video’s acceptability. “Strict success” also permits unknown core checks, so it does not mean every requirement was positively verified (§4). Human evidence does not clearly separate the leading pair: their rating intervals overlap, and the reported comparison probability is 0.69 (App. H).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** Evaluation emphasizes adherence over originality; coverage is predominantly English, mostly 1080p-class, and includes few very long videos. Judging depends on proprietary models and misses fine audiovisual synchronization (App. J).

**Additional evidence-backed risks:**

- **Single-run variability:** Each system–task pair has one scored rollout, and ablations run once per cell. Analyst inference: task-bootstrap intervals do not quantify repeat-run variability (§4; App. I).
- **Observational process findings:** Renderer choice is coupled to system identity. Neither renderer comparisons nor self-inspection correlations demonstrate causal improvements (§5.3; App. F).
- **Judge dependence:** Strict success varies substantially by panel; two judges show own-model preference, although exclusion analyses largely preserve rankings (Apps. D–E).
- **Limited educational validation:** The grounded quiz measures evidence-supported coverage using a model grader, rather than learning gains in human students (App. I.2).
- **Budget qualification:** App. J leaves longer budgets to future work, but App. I.3 already tests them on a restricted subset; broad conclusions beyond that subset remain unsupported.

## Open research questions
- **Can delivery-first planning improve reliability?** Failure records implicate render scheduling, but no controlled scheduling intervention is reported. Compare early complete renders plus copy-based refinement against standard execution.
- **Does targeted self-inspection improve quality?** Inspection frequency is observational and varies across systems. Randomize event-focused inspection, uniform inspection, and no-inspection conditions within each system.
- **How can synchronization be evaluated reliably?** Injected delays escaped J1 detection. Test timestamp-grounded audiovisual checks against human-labeled offset examples.
- **Do educational videos improve learner understanding?** The grounded quiz establishes coverage rather than student outcomes. Run randomized pre/post assessments with the intended audiences.
