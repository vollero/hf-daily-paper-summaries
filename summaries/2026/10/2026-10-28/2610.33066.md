# OvisOCR3 Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33066) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33066)


## One-sentence takeaway
OvisOCR3 combines five OCR task families in one 0.8B model, reporting strong public and in-house benchmark results, although missing quantitative ablations leave the contribution of its training stages unresolved (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Practical OCR requires more than transcription: users need page structure, extracted fields, figure descriptions, and tables reconstructed across pages. The authors identify a training imbalance when combining these tasks: long page reconstructions dominate token-averaged supervision even when sample counts are balanced, while different tasks require different correctness criteria (§1).

The goal is specialist-level performance across these requirements using one compact model with shared parameters and no separate teachers at inference.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified model and interfaces.** Built on Qwen3.5-0.8B, OvisOCR3 supports document parsing, general OCR, KIE, document figure captioning, and cross-page table reconstruction. Relative to OvisOCR2, parsing adds structured JSON output and layout detection (§§1–2).
- **Training recipe adapted to multiple domains.** Continued pretraining uses approximately 72 million sampled examples. Mid-training uses approximately 8.8 million examples with more balanced domain sampling, trains separate token-normalized and sample-normalized branches, and merges their weights (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Specialist distillation and joint refinement.** Five domain-specific 4B teachers supervise student-generated responses through capped reverse-KL matching over top-K tokens. Mixed-task GRPO then applies domain-specific rewards (§§3.3–3.4). These components extend an existing staged recipe; the report does not establish that each underlying technique is new.
- **Cross-page workflow and evaluation assets.** The same model parses pages and reconstructs tables from adjacent images plus parsed text; recursive merging handles longer continuations. The authors introduce in-house DocFigCapBench and CrossTabBench (§2.5; §§4.4–4.5). Their public release is not established.

These comparisons support broad task competence in a compact model, with particularly strong robustness, extraction, and continuation detection. They do not establish universal superiority: OvisOCR3 trails listed competitors on overall OmniDocBench parsing, figure Caption Score, and CrossTabBench reconstruction quality. Benchmark performance also does not isolate which training component causes the gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
The report has no dedicated limitations discussion. The following are **analyst assessments grounded in reporting gaps or evaluation design**:

- **Training attribution is unresolved.** Sample normalization and top-K distillation are described as empirically better, but no quantitative ablation tables isolate normalization, fusion, distillation, or MixRL (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Reproduction details are incomplete.** The text omits exact data sources and deduplication procedures, fusion coefficients, top-K size, KL cap, major optimization settings, and complete reward weights (§3). Training–evaluation overlap cannot be assessed.
- **Comparisons use mixed provenance.** Parsing tables combine local evaluations with leaderboard/report scores. KIE interfaces are adapted, while specialized figure/table baselines use native pipelines (§§4.1, 4.3–4.5).
- **In-house evaluation has specific validity limits.** DocFigCapBench uses VLM-drafted references and Qwen3.8-Max judging, without reported human–judge agreement; it contains 182 pages. Benchmark release is unconfirmed (§4.4).
- **Deployment efficiency and long-table reliability remain unmeasured.** Parameter count supports compactness, but latency, memory, throughput, and reconstruction accuracy by continuation length are absent. No uncertainty estimates accompany the result tables (§§2.5, 4).

## Open research questions
1. **Which training stages produce the gains?** The report lacks controlled quantitative ablations. Compare stage checkpoints and normalization/fusion variants using matched data and compute across all five domains.
2. **Does recursive reconstruction accumulate errors with table length?** Pairwise evaluation and aggregate document-level scores do not resolve this. Measure continuation and cell-structure accuracy by page span, comparing predicted and reference page parses.
3. **How stable is the figure-caption ranking under human evaluation?** Caption scores rely on one VLM judge. Conduct blinded human factuality and coverage assessment, alongside multiple judges, on the same predictions.
4. **What is the practical efficiency advantage?** No deployment measurements substantiate the compact-model motivation. Benchmark latency, peak memory, and throughput on matched hardware, including parsing, rendering, and recursive reconstruction.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in summarizing the reported methods and comparisons; **medium** in broader generalization and causal explanations.

Material missing information includes controlled ablations, detailed training and inference configurations, data provenance and overlap checks, in-house benchmark release status, human validation of caption scoring, statistical uncertainty, and deployment cost measurements.
