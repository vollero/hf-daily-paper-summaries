# Hy-Nomas: Towards Free-form Interactive World Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36729) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36729)


## One-sentence takeaway
Hy-Nomas combines temporally scoped action conditioning with continuous camera control for multi-turn video world generation, showing strong human-rated instruction following but weaker spatial memory than Genie 3 (§§3, 7.3).

## Problem and motivation
Interactive video worlds must respond to successive commands while preserving identities, scene changes, and earlier action consequences. The authors identify two conditioning failures: actions occurring outside their intended time windows and past actions repeating because their instructions remain accessible. They also target the difficulty of combining semantic instructions and camera navigation within one continuing world (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Aligned captions and attention:** Structured captions separate persistent descriptions from timestamped actions. Action-aware causal attention retains descriptive context across segments while restricting action text to its originating segment and temporal window (§§2.4, 3.1).
- **Unified interactive generator:** An 8B autoregressive diffusion transformer extends HunyuanVideo 1.5 with cached history and frame-aligned cross-attention for continuous camera trajectories. Mixed-control training supports alternating text and camera inputs (§§3–3.2).
- **Training and deployment workflow:** Progressive causal training, training on model-generated histories, and diffusion distillation support segment-wise generation. A VLM Prompt Enhancer converts requests and interaction history into structured captions; LoRA adapters trained before distillation are merged into the distilled model (§§5–6).
- **Evaluation assets:** The authors introduce Hy-Nomas-Bench and state that they release weights, code, and benchmarks. Its distinctive evaluation settings include interleaved control and viewpoint revisitation (§§1, 7.1).

The supplied evidence supports this integrated design; it does not isolate each component’s contribution.

## Evidence and main results

All comparison percentages below are reported from Hy-Nomas’s perspective, except the cinematic adaptation row.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong text instruction following | Bench-1: 60 scenarios, 2–6 turns. Prompt-following win rates: 65.0% against HappyOyster-Directing and 75.0% against LingBot-World v2; physical-plausibility wins: 45.0% and 68.3%, respectively. | §§7.1, 7.3; Fig. 13 |
| Strong semantic performance during interleaved control | Bench-3: 60 scenarios against adapted LingBot-World v2. Win rates: 75.0% prompt following, 73.3% physical plausibility, 26.7% camera control; prose omits corresponding ties/losses. | §7.3; Fig. 16 |
| Camera-navigation advantage reported | On 100 shared trajectories, wins exceed losses against Genie 3, HappyOyster-Wandering, and LingBot-World v2; exact percentages are unavailable in the supplied prose. | §7.3; Fig. 15 |
| Spatial memory trails Genie 3 | Bench-4: 60 revisitation scenarios. Against Genie 3: 13.3% wins / 56.7% ties / 30.0% losses; Happy Oyster: 30.0% / 46.7% / 23.3%; LingBot-World v2: 58.3% wins / 1.7% losses. | §7.3; Fig. 17 |
| Cinematic adaptation trades control performance for other gains | LoRA-adapted versus distilled model: Bench-1 prompt-following wins/losses 35%/24%, physical plausibility 46%/13%; Bench-2 camera control 19%/37%. | §7.4; Fig. 20 |
| Distillation reduces sampling budget | Base autoregressive model uses 100 NFEs per segment; distilled student uses 4. Matched quality and latency measurements are not supplied. | §5.5 |
| Single-GPU memory feasibility demonstrated | Reported peak: 20.53 GiB on RTX 4090 for a 100-second video over seven segments, at 1280×704 and 24 FPS, using inference optimizations. | §6.2 |

These results support human-rated interaction advantages in the authors’ benchmark, with a clear spatial-memory gap. Win rates alone do not establish the size of an advantage without ties and losses. The evidence does not establish real-time responsiveness, physically accurate simulation, or causal attribution of gains to individual architectural choices.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Spatial memory remains weaker than Genie 3; additional looped-camera training data is proposed as a possible remedy (§7.3).
- **Author-stated:** Cinematic LoRA reduces camera-control performance. Limited trajectory diversity is the authors’ hypothesis, rather than a demonstrated cause (§7.4).
- **Author-stated:** Quantization, sparse attention, and other deployment optimizations may produce differences that accumulate across segments (§6.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons require caution because interfaces and resolutions differ, LingBot-World v2 is adapted, and image-initialized baselines receive Hy-Nomas-generated starting frames (§7.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No controlled component ablations, confidence intervals, or inter-annotator agreement statistics appear in the supplied text. Thus, the claimed benefits of captions, masks, and training stages remain incompletely separated (§§3.1, 7.2–7.3).
- **Scope:** Evaluation excludes conventional visual-quality metrics; robotics adaptation is qualitative and does not demonstrate successful executable robot control (§§7.1, 7.4, 9).

## Open research questions

1. **Which component drives instruction-following gains?** The authors hypothesize benefits from captions and attention without isolating them (§7.3). Next experiment: hold data and compute fixed while varying caption structure and action-aware masking.
2. **Can memory improve under a bounded cache?** Revisitation remains a weakness, and retrieval/state tracking are proposed directions (§§7.3, 9). Next experiment: compare cache-only generation with retrieved visual references and explicit state records on longer revisitation sequences.
3. **Can adaptation preserve navigation skills?** Cinematic LoRA shows a camera-control regression (§7.4). Next experiment: vary trajectory diversity and camera-conditioned replay during adaptation, then repeat Bench-1 and Bench-2.
4. **Do deployment optimizations preserve interaction quality?** Memory usage is reported, but latency and matched quality tradeoffs remain unclear (§6.2). Next experiment: measure response latency and long-rollout benchmark scores with each optimization enabled separately.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main design and reported comparisons, but the source was truncated, so coverage is incomplete.

Material missing information includes training-corpus size and detailed provenance, total training compute, controlled ablations, statistical uncertainty, complete numerical figure data, end-to-end latency including PE and decoding, and verification of the stated artifact releases.
