# Omni-InteractScope: Benchmarking Proactive and Agentic Interaction in Continuous Omnimodal Streams

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35439) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35439)


## One-sentence takeaway

Omni-InteractScope benchmarks timely and sustained audiovisual assistance, finding low intervention coverage across 12 model configurations while showing that request priors and response policies complicate interpretation of the scores.

## Problem and motivation

Streaming assistants must decide what to say or which tool to invoke, when to act, and whether further responses remain necessary. The authors identify a gap in evaluating these obligations together: correct answer content alone does not establish timely, event-grounded, continuing assistance (§1).

The benchmark connects user needs, stream evidence, response content, valid timing, and successive targets, supporting evaluation of both individual interventions and continuing obligations (§§3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 1,479 human-annotated, cross-reviewed interactions containing 1,790 temporal targets across eight task groups. Tasks cover tool invocation, notifications, warnings, state tracking, temporal conditions, nonverbal cues, continuous responses, and impairment assistance (§3; Table 4).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Causal observation schedules combine content correctness with temporal eligibility. Explicit-request tasks score the first assigned response; assistance tasks maximize ordered target matches and additionally measure complete guidance coverage (§4.1; Appendix B).
- **Diagnostic controls:** Scene removal and request-only controls examine tool-invocation grounding; matched reminder experiments vary event instructions, scheduling, response history, and cooldowns (§5; Appendices D.1–D.2).
- **Empirical study:** Evaluation of 12 configurations documents gaps in timely action, query-free reminders, and complete guidance. The contribution is an evaluation resource and analysis, rather than a new assistant architecture (§§4–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Timely tool invocation is weak | Across 12 configurations on 200 T1 targets, on-time first responses reach at most 40%; joint exact-tool-set and timing accuracy reaches at most 32.0%, using ±2-second tolerance. | Abstract; §1; Appendix D.1 |
| Query-free reminders have lower coverage than explicit questions | Gemini-3.1-Pro achieves 23.0% timely recall on 122 reminder targets versus 62.3% on 151 question targets; all configurations show the same direction, on different target sets. | §4.2; Table 3 caption |
| Step coverage exceeds complete guidance coverage | Gemini-3.1-Pro covers 51.7% of 87 guidance steps but completes only 3/26 sequences; nine configurations complete none. | Abstract; §4.2; Table 3 caption |
| Tool scores need grounding controls | Matched Gemini-3.5-Flash scene ablation: 59/200 joint hits with scene input versus 66/200 without; difference −3.50 percentage points, 95% paired CI [−7.00, −0.50]. | §5.1; Appendix D.1.2 |
| Request and timing priors can yield high scores | Request-only Gemini-3.5-Flash identifies 193/200 tool labels; emitting its choices after three seconds yields 68.0% joint success, the maximum among four tested delays. Interface and response opportunities differ from the main evaluation. | Appendix D.1.1 |
| History changes output volume and target identity | On 122 reminder episodes, response history reduces outputs by 58.09%, with both arms matching 22 targets; 12 targets are gained and 12 lost. Adding cooldown leaves 10/122 matches. | §5.2; Appendix D.2.2 |
| Low completion does not establish a separate continuity failure | On 20 three-step episodes, Gemini-3.1-Pro covers 30/60 steps and completes three sequences; the marginal-success permutation reference expects 2.40 completions, with interval [0,5]. | §5.3; Appendix D.3 |
| Audio benefits can disappear in aggregates | Under separate target-local probing, native audio adds seven hits on 78 auditory targets for both Gemini-3.5-Flash and Qwen3-Omni; Gemini’s overall gain is only 0.29 percentage points. | Appendix D.4.4 |

These results establish limited coverage under the specified configurations and scoring rules. They do not establish event grounding from tool scores alone, an isolated planning or memory deficit, reduced interruption cost, or closed-loop user-task success. The fixed-delay result is a diagnostic control, not a matched leaderboard comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope:** Tool arguments and execution are excluded; guidance follows recorded trajectories; timestamps exclude inference latency (§3.2; §4.1; Appendix B.6).
- **Coverage versus usefulness:** Unmatched assistance outputs incur no penalty, and references do not enumerate every useful intervention. Recall therefore measures neither selectivity nor interruption cost (Appendix B.5).
- **Comparison confounds:** Inputs, context retention, computation, and interfaces differ across configurations. Reminder–question comparisons use different events; scheduling changes both temporal information and response opportunities (§4.2; Appendices C.2, D.2.1).
- **Limited controls:** History/cooldown experiments concern one model and omit the no-history-plus-cooldown arm. Longer guidance groups are too small to establish length effects (Appendices D.2.2–D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Text-reference judging without scene access may miss errors beyond reference compatibility. Bootstrap intervals exclude judge error and API variability (Appendices B.3, B.7).
- **Source consistency:** Appendix B.6 says 361 assistance targets, whereas Table 4, Table 3’s caption, and the evaluation-population description specify 360. Several numerical table bodies are absent from the retrieved text.

## Open research questions

1. **Do models track event occurrence and timing independently of requests?** Scene removal does not test counterfactual tracking. Next experiment: hold requests fixed while shifting, replacing, or removing trigger events (§6).
2. **Which response policy balances coverage and interruption burden?** Recall ignores unmatched-output costs, and the cooldown design is incomplete. Next experiment: cross history and cooldown in a full factorial study with human-rated intervention usefulness (Appendix D.2.2).
3. **Can explicit obligation tracking improve complete guidance?** Current completion results do not identify a memory mechanism. Next experiment: compare matched assistants with and without a pending-step state, holding response opportunities fixed (§6).
4. **Does benchmark coverage predict successful assistance?** Recorded trajectories do not measure closed-loop outcomes. Next experiment: evaluate interactive tasks with user actions and task-completion measures (§4.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main findings and many methodological details, but omits numerical bodies for several tables. Material gaps include complete per-model results, evaluation-judge validation against humans, verified asset availability, participant characteristics for the assistance recordings, and resolution of the assistance-target denominator inconsistency.
