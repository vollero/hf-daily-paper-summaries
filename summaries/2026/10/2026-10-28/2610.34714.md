# SearchHorizonBench: A Benchmark for Search Value in AI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34714) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34714)


## One-sentence takeaway
SearchHorizonBench separates predicting answer success from predicting search’s incremental benefit, finding that historical calibration improves the former while tested gain predictors remain near constant baselines (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
An agent deciding whether to search needs the expected improvement over answering with existing evidence, together with acquisition costs. Predicting that a question is difficult does not establish that search will help. The authors identify a need for aligned execution data and evaluation that distinguish overall success prediction, search-gain prediction, and cost-aware decisions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired-execution benchmark:** Saved public histories branch into THINK and SEARCH continuations, with repeated executions, fixed references, and recorded costs. The resource contains 45,632 executions and 8,880 forecasts across 3,168 questions (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Separated prediction targets:** A Brier-error decomposition attributes improvements to overall success level versus action gain. A repeated-outcome U-statistic estimates gain error while removing within-question generation noise, under conditional independence (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Controlled comparisons:** Historical means, task priors, calibration maps, public-feature models, and text models establish references; matched actor/backend learning curves test training scale (§§3.2, 5.1–5.3).
- **Decision evaluation:** Token-priced utility charges prediction and continuation costs. A factorial experiment separates gain information from cost information, while paid consistency probes test acquisition overhead (§§4.2, 5.4–5.5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration primarily captures success level | On equal-stratum Test-1632, using 120 historical development questions, the frozen map reduces Brier from global means’ 0.2145 to 0.1797; paired difference interval [−0.0530, −0.0169]. Task priors account for 88.9% of the improvement; success-level error accounts for 99.9%. | §5.1; Table 1 referenced; Fig. 3 |
| Query-specific gains vary, but historical prediction skill is weak | Under Test-1632 reference-equivalent correctness, estimated gain SD is 21.4 percentage points. Historical-map gain R² is −0.17% [−1.64, 0.87]%; public-prefix ridge is +0.10% [−1.36, 1.17]%. | §5.2 |
| More matched training gives limited gains for tested models | With the same Flash actor and DeepSeek backend, public-feature ridge reaches +0.72% gain R² [−1.78, 3.09]% at 1,100 training questions; raw-score ridge reaches +0.27%, and guarded text prediction selects the mean. | §5.3; Fig. 4 |
| Free gain observations reveal a priced decision opportunity | On Test-1632 at λ=0.005 per thousand tokens, gain information with fixed costs gives +2.48 utility percentage points [1.28, 3.52] over the best constant action; cost information alone gives +0.68 [−0.64, 1.73]; both give +2.49. Selection observations and judgments are free. | §5.4; Fig. 5; Appendix E |
| Paid consistency probes do not realize that opportunity | Against always SEARCH, two-probe consistency saves 1,241 net tokens but loses 1.87 success percentage points; utility difference at λ=0.005 is −1.25 points [−2.38, −0.35]. | §5.5; Appendix E |
| Answer objectives can reverse a gain comparison | In the actor-matched component study, map-minus-mean gain-risk contrast is −0.0173 under reference equivalence and +0.0113 under exact match; both intervals exclude zero, while independent-audit shared-label bounds include both signs. | Appendix D, “Component composition” |

The evidence supports a distinction between predicting difficulty and predicting search benefit. It also identifies a decision opportunity when selection information is free. It does **not** establish a deployable policy achieving that opportunity, or show that richer models cannot learn useful gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Stated scope:** Repeated executions use cached retrieval responses, and gain estimation assumes conditionally independent generation draws. Effective historical sampling configurations are not fully recorded (Appendices A–B).
- **Stated population dependence:** Later checkpoints and Pro/Qwen continuations are conditional on Flash-generated histories. Pending-query studies exclude trajectories that already answered; Test-1632 uses source-support screening (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Stated scoring uncertainty:** Reference equivalence differs from source support and exact match. AI audits leave unresolved cases; both independent panels use the same reviewer family, and an exact reviewer snapshot is unavailable (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Near-zero gain skill applies to the tested model families and training budgets. It cannot establish an intrinsic prediction ceiling (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported evaluation intervals condition on fitted predictors, so they do not generally capture full training uncertainty. Equal weighting also gives the small four-hop stratum substantial influence (§4.2; Appendices A–B).
- **Decision boundary:** Free labeled selection executions are diagnostic information, not an available deployment signal. Utility also depends on the chosen token valuation (§5.4; Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Can richer public-history models predict gains?** Tested regressors remain near constant prediction. Next experiment: compare richer encoders or continuation-aware models using the same support-disjoint folds and acquisition-cost accounting (§5.3).
2. **Can a cheap deployable signal capture the free-observation opportunity?** Paid consistency loses utility despite net token savings. Next experiment: train a low-cost gain predictor and evaluate utility after charging its actual inference cost (§§5.4–5.5).
3. **How robust is gain prediction across answer objectives?** A component comparison reverses sign between reference equivalence and exact match. Next experiment: evaluate identical predictors under separately adjudicated equivalence, source-support, and lexical rewards (Appendices C–D).
4. **Does gain prediction transfer to independently generated histories?** Cross-actor continuations currently condition on Flash histories. Next experiment: collect actor-native checkpoints and compare transfer against matched historical means (§3.1; Appendix A).
