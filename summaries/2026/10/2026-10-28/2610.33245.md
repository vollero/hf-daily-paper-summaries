# AdaPRA: Process Reward Agents with Adaptive Rubrics for Data Science

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33245) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33245)


## One-sentence takeaway
AdaPRA improves data science agents’ Kaggle performance by preserving work across attempts and using an execution-enabled supervisor with task-adapted rubrics, although its largest reported gains use oracle submission selection and substantial extra supervision cost.

## Problem and motivation
Data science agents can produce valid submissions while making silent errors in validation, prediction pipelines, or model selection. They also stop exploration early and repeat work when attempts start from scratch. The authors identify a need for supervision that inspects both trajectories and workspace artifacts, intervenes during execution, and retains knowledge across attempts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent harness:** Files, models, predictions, notes, and attempt summaries survive between attempts; model context resets (§3.2).
- **Execution-grounded process supervision:** A Claude Sonnet 4.6 supervisor reads trajectories on demand, runs diagnostic checks against the agent’s read-only workspace, and injects relevant failing rubrics with suggested fixes. Reviews occur periodically and at completion (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Rubric discovery and adaptation:** Offline discovery contrasts stronger and weaker attempts using trajectories and artifacts. Seventeen discovered rubrics, supplemented by two hand-curated cross-attempt rubrics, initialize a task-local store that the supervisor can expand, revise, or retire (§§3.3, 3.5; Appendix J).

This is inference-time supervision; the reported method does not train the base agent from its feedback (§7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large gains over DSGym | With Qwen3-Coder-Next and oracle best of five attempts: mean leaderboard percentile rises **44.5→69.0** on 38 Easy tasks and **21.3→36.1** on 54 Hard tasks. | §§5.1–5.2; Tables 1–2 captions and accompanying prose |
| Supervision adds beyond the harness | Best-of-five percentile improves over harness-only by **8.2 points** on Easy and **10.6 points** on Hard; both **p<0.001**, using task-paired, one-sided permutation tests. | §§5.1–5.2; Appendix I |
| Frozen rubrics transfer to harder tasks | Easy-derived, fixed rubrics add **6.3 percentile points** over harness-only on Hard, best of five; **p=0.007**. | §5.4; Appendix I |
| Adaptation helps on Hard | Removing adaptation lowers best-of-five Hard percentile by **4.3 points**; **p=0.024**. | §5.3; Appendix I |
| Execution helps, with mixed significance | Removing supervisor execution lowers best-of-five percentile **69.0→63.0** on Easy (**p=0.012**) and **36.1→31.8** on Hard (**p=0.077**). | §5.3; Appendix I |
| Transfer to another base agent | On Easy with one attempt per task, DeepSeek-V3.2 improves **36.9→44.7** mean percentile over harness-only; **p=0.005**. | §5.3; Appendix O |
| Deployment selection remains weaker than oracle selection | AdaPRA’s last scored attempt reaches **62.9** percentile on Easy and **30.2** on Hard, versus oracle best-of-five **69.0** and **36.1**. | Appendix H |
| Supervision materially increases API cost | Recorded Hard runs cost **$2,187** for AdaPRA versus **$465** for harness-only; the PRA contributes **$1,640**. These are run totals, with differing recorded attempt counts. | Appendix Q, Table 22 |
| Some flagged errors are corrected | In the frozen-rubric Hard run, **685/1,277 flags (54%)** resolve within the attempt under the paper’s supervisor-score definition. | Appendix K |

The results support performance gains on the evaluated predictive tasks, including transfer beyond the seed-discovery benchmark. They do not establish equal-cost superiority or a deployable way to identify the oracle-best submission. Flag resolution is principally supervisor-assessed rather than an independent error audit.

The authors also claim superiority over DataPRM, but its numerical performance results are absent from the supplied table bodies. The GPT-5.1 comparison uses external single-attempt runs, and AdaPRA slightly trails its reported Hard per-attempt medal rate (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope limits:** Evaluation covers only two Kaggle predictive benchmarks. Larger attempt counts, open-ended analysis, and tasks without predictive objectives remain untested. The second base model is evaluated only on Easy with a single attempt (Limitations).
- **Cost and reproducibility:** The supervisor is expensive and depends on a closed commercial model whose availability or behavior may change (Limitations; Appendix Q).
- **Adaptation can introduce harmful criteria:** Appendix M documents an added dispersion rubric that conflicted with the prediction metric, prompted worse validation performance, and was never retired. The authors acknowledge weak rubric credit assignment (§7; Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—budget confounding:** Appendix C gives supervised Easy attempts a longer wall-clock limit than unsupervised attempts and excludes supervisor time from the agent budget. Consequently, gains are not established under equal total resources.
- **Analyst inference—harness attribution:** The harness comparison changes persistence and the tool interface together; it does not isolate memory alone (§5.3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §4 describes fixed-denominator metrics, while Appendices H–I describe exclusions for some per-attempt means. Best-attempt results are clearer; per-attempt comparisons require denominator care.
- **Selection risk:** Best-of-five results use unavailable test-score knowledge. Appendix M also shows that better local validation can accompany worse leaderboard performance.

## Open research questions
1. **Can adaptation reject harmful rubrics?** The store scarcely revises or retires criteria, and Appendix M documents a counterproductive addition. Compare current adaptation against validation-based acceptance and retirement rules.
2. **Does supervision remain advantageous at equal cost?** Reported gains include substantial extra supervisor resources. Compare AdaPRA with additional unsupervised attempts under matched total API cost and elapsed time.
3. **Can submissions be selected reliably without leaderboard scores?** Oracle selection exceeds the reported last-attempt policy. Evaluate a selector using only local validation, provenance checks, and PRA evidence.
4. **How broadly do rubrics transfer?** Evidence beyond the main base agent is restricted to single-attempt Easy evaluation. Test fixed and adaptive rubrics across more base agents and non-Kaggle workflows.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative supports the central method and major results, but several table bodies are missing.

Material unavailable information includes complete condition-by-condition results, numerical DataPRM comparisons, full seed-rubric definitions, some confidence intervals, a code repository address, and independent measurements of supervisor error rates. Per-attempt denominator inconsistencies also limit precise interpretation.
