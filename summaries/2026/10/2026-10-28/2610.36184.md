# Lipi-Ghor: A Large-Scale, Diarized, Multi-Domain Bengali Speech Corpus and a Scalable Pipeline for Building It

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36184) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36184)


## One-sentence takeaway
Lipi-Ghor supplies 885 hours of weakly supervised, automatically diarized Bengali audio annotations and shows promising ASR fine-tuning results, although its reported model comparisons use noisy caption references rather than the released human-corrected gold split (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a shortage of Bengali training resources combining diverse speech, long recordings, and speaker-attributed transcripts. They argue that existing read or controlled speech corpora underrepresent dialects, emotive delivery, code-switching, and conversational speaker changes, limiting ASR robustness (§§1–2). Their claim to be the first large-scale Bengali corpus combining diarization and long-form audio is an author-reported novelty claim.

## Approach and contributions
- **Construction pipeline:** Manually curate domain-relevant channels, retain native Bengali caption tracks, fetch audio and captions through distributed processing, deduplicate recordings, and align Precision-2 diarization with captions into Speaker–Speech–Transcription–Timestamp (SSTT) annotations. Auto-translated captions are excluded (§3).
- **Corpus and release:** The pipeline yields approximately 885 hours from 998 unique videos, 596 channels, and 17 domains. Released assets include video identifiers, annotations, pipeline code, and an ASR-ready chunked subset; users reconstruct audio themselves (Abstract; §§3–4, 9).
- **Evaluation resource and analysis:** A domain-stratified, human-corrected gold split accompanies zero-shot and fine-tuned ASR comparisons and analyses of domain difficulty, segment duration, and diarization artifacts (§§5–7; Appendix B).

The contribution is primarily the resource and collection workflow; diarization and ASR models are existing components.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large, diverse corpus with concentrated coverage | Approximately 885 hours, 998 deduplicated videos, 596 channels, 17 domains; three domains contribute 58.6% of audio. | Abstract; §§3.3, 4.1 |
| Gold references are available | 500 clips stratified across all 17 domains; 17 native Bengali volunteers contributed approximately 30 hours of correction effort, including speaker labels. | §5; Appendix B |
| Fine-tuned systems outperform reported zero-shot systems | Using approximately 58 training hours, Faster Whisper Medium obtains 20.70% WER / 11.90% CER and Fast Conformer 24.67% / 15.56% on Lipi-Ghor. | Domain difficulty varies substantially | Caption-referenced zero-shot evaluation finds lowest errors on audiobooks/news and highest on drama, comedy, and dialects; Seamless M4T-v2 exceeds 100% WER on Regional/Dialect. | §6.1; Fig. 5 |

These results support the corpus’s potential training utility under the reported settings. They do not establish ASR accuracy against human references: §6.3 evaluates **caption–human disagreement**, not model performance on gold transcripts. Common caption references make scoring consistent but do not establish unbiased model rankings. The supplied text also does not support the authors’ causal explanation of Whisper’s errors through a controlled experiment.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Caption transcripts are noisy; bulk speaker annotations are unverified; Precision-2 is proprietary; regional dialect coverage is sparse—four videos totaling 1.29 hours. Manual curation and changing YouTube policies limit reproducibility (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Caption availability produces substantial domain imbalance. Cross-language pipeline transfer remains a hypothesis, and external benchmark interpretation is complicated by possible prior model exposure (§§4.1, 6.2, 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Long-recording provenance does not demonstrate intact long-form ASR capability because diarization converts recordings into predominantly short turns (§§4.3, 6.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Fine-tuning utility is suggestive, but comparison against different zero-shot architectures does not isolate the corpus’s effect; matched before/after results are unavailable in the supplied text (§6.2).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Some structural statistics use 1,019 pre-deduplication recordings while corpus hours refer to the 998-video release, complicating interpretation of aggregates (§§3.3, 4.3; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Do model rankings persist with human references?** Reported model scores use captions. Next experiment: rescore every baseline on the held-out gold split, with domain-level uncertainty estimates (§§5–6).
- **How much does automatic segmentation affect recognition?** Over-segmentation and overlap limits are documented, but their ASR impact is not isolated. Next experiment: compare automatic, human-corrected, and longer merged segments on identical audio (§7).
- **Does Lipi-Ghor improve dialect robustness?** Dialect data are sparse and recognition errors high. Next experiment: add verified dialect recordings and compare matched fine-tuning runs with and without that addition (Limitations; §6.1).
- **Can the pipeline transfer efficiently across languages?** Only Bengali is validated. Next experiment: apply the workflow to another caption-supported language while measuring curation effort, yield, and annotation quality (§7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The resource and pipeline are clearly described, but quantitative assessment is limited by missing table bodies in the supplied text.

Material unavailable information includes full baseline identities and scores, caption–gold disagreement values, test-set sizes and partition rules, detailed fine-tuning configurations, gold-annotation agreement/adjudication statistics, quantitative diarization accuracy, and uncertainty estimates. No ASR model results against the gold references are supplied.
