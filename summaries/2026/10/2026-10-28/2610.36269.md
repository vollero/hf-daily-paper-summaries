# Cross-modal Steering Vectors Exist Across Both Image and Text Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36269) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36269)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across three shared-weight autoregressive multimodal models, the authors demonstrate bidirectional transfer of text- and image-derived steering vectors, with effectiveness varying substantially by concept, model, and intervention layer (§3.2).

## Problem and motivation
Sharing parameters for text and image generation does not establish that a model uses shared conceptual mechanisms. The authors ask whether a direction extracted exclusively from one modality can causally control generation in the other. They identify a gap between representational alignment studies and intervention-based evidence, and between earlier reports of predominantly text-to-image transfer and their setting of bidirectional, free-form generation (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **A common intervention recipe:** Average hidden-state differences over 100 synthetic minimal pairs per concept and modality, normalize each layer’s vector, and add it during generation. Text extraction uses the final token; image extraction mean-pools visual tokens. Transferred vectors undergo no adaptation, although intervention strengths are tuned separately by setting (§2.2).
- **Bidirectional empirical evaluation:** Compare text→text, image→image, text→image, and image→text across UniAR, Emu3.5, and Liquid, using eight concepts and early, middle, and late layer ranges (§§2–3).
- **Interpretability findings:** Examine qualitative transfers of modality-specific attributes, and show that some text/image concept directions align despite separation of their underlying activation distributions (§§3.3–3.4).

The vector-extraction method is established; the contribution is its systematic cross-modal application and the resulting empirical findings.

## Evidence and main results

Recovery is transferred-vector concept-rate AUC divided by the corresponding intra-modality AUC. Each setting uses its own tolerated strength range; recovery therefore does **not** compare equal intervention strengths (§3.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong bidirectional transfer in UniAR | At middle layer ranges, mean recovery across concepts is **84% for text→image** and **74% for image→text**, relative to same-target intra-modality steering. | §3.2; Fig. 3 |
| Image→text depends on layer selection | Mean recovery reaches **0.87 in Emu3.5** and **0.91 in Liquid** at late layer ranges; UniAR reaches up to **0.96**. | §3.2 |
| Text-derived vectors can outperform image-derived references | Emu3.5 and Liquid report mean text→image recovery of **1.10** and **1.20**. Their middle-layer image→image AUCs are only **0.44** and **0.43**, versus **0.83** for UniAR; the authors attribute the advantage mainly to weaker image-derived vectors. | §3.2 |
| Transfer varies by concept | Averaged over models and layer ranges, size recovery is **1.12 for text→image** versus **0.32 for image→text**. Left/right largely fails, including many intra-modality settings. | §3.2; Table 1 caption |
| Direction alignment predicts transfer imperfectly | Across **63 model/concept/layer-range points**, excluding spatial, cosine similarity versus recovery averaged over both directions gives **Spearman ρ=0.38, p<0.01; Pearson r=0.22**. | §3.4; Fig. 5; §C |
| Steering has a quality cost | At middle layer ranges, aggregated text fluency is approximately **75% of baseline** at normalized strength **0.4**, near peak concept rate. Image coherence remains approximately **90%** at strength **0.6**, where concept rate exceeds **90% of its maximum**. | §3.2; §A.7; Fig. 8 |
| Automated evaluation has partial human support | Gemini judge versus majority human labels achieves **Cohen’s κ=0.62** on **320 held-out pairs**, each reviewed independently by three people. | §A.8 |
| Modality-specific transfers are interpretable but qualitative | Linguistic formality changes visual clothing/design; image resolution changes textual detail. Averaging text/image vectors yields no straightforward quantitative improvement. | §3.3; §§D–E |

These interventions support the existence of directions that affect both output modalities in the tested models. They do not establish fully shared circuits, universal modality-independent concepts, or transfer of safety behavior and factual knowledge. The geometry correlations remain observational even though the steering interventions themselves are causal.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported scope:** The study covers three shared-weight autoregressive models and a selected concept set; broader model families and novel concepts remain future work (§5).
- **Comparison sensitivity:** Recovery depends on separately selected strength ranges and the quality of its intra-modality denominator. Table 6 omits ratios when reference AUC is below 0.2 (§§3.1, A.4, B).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Twenty synthetic evaluation prompts per concept, a fixed decoding seed, and prompt iteration leave robustness to natural prompts and generation randomness unresolved (§§A.2–A.4).
- **Analyst inference—measurement dependence:** Most results use one model judge. Human validation supports that judge but does not eliminate disagreement; DreamSim provides complementary image evidence, with only small shifts reported for Emu3.5 and Liquid (§§A.6, A.8).
- **Qualitative uncertainty:** Modality-specific findings depend on prompts where an attribute can surface. Camera-height transfer is weak, and §D.1’s forced-choice description is difficult to reconcile with §D.2’s report that the selected answer never changes.
- **Mechanistic ambiguity:** The authors speculate that tokenization explains UniAR’s stronger transfer, but no controlled tokenizer ablation establishes this (§3.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does transfer survive broader prompts and decoding variation?** Evaluation uses synthetic prompts and a fixed seed. Next experiment: repeat on independently authored prompts and multiple seeds, reporting uncertainty.
2. **What causes differences between models?** Tokenization is a hypothesis rather than an isolated variable. Next experiment: compare otherwise matched models with semantic versus reconstruction-oriented visual tokenizers.
3. **Does transfer remain strong at matched output quality?** Recovery integrates separately tuned strength ranges, including degeneration. Next experiment: compare transfer and intra-modality steering under equal fluency/coherence thresholds.
4. **Can shared directions control safety or knowledge?** These are proposed applications, not evaluated outcomes (§5). Next experiment: construct relevant contrastive pairs and assess target behavior plus unintended changes in both modalities.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports bidirectional transfer and its major qualifications. However, many referenced tables contain captions without numerical cells, and several promised prompt blocks, edit instructions, strength grids, and decoding settings are absent. Full per-condition results, uncertainty estimates, and independent verification of released artifacts are therefore unavailable.
