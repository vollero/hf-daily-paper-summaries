# Sub-monolayer tracking of molecular states at buried interfaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31924) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31924)


## One-sentence takeaway
An ML-refined DFT optical model enables polarized resonant X-ray reflectivity to recover model-dependent, sub-monolayer orientation and density profiles in ZnPc films, revealing buried interfacial states distinct from the bulk.

## Problem and motivation
Buried molecular orientation, packing, and chemical states influence material function but are difficult to measure non-destructively. Conventional resonant soft X-ray scattering (RSoXS) fits require many independently fitted optical parameters, making chemical and orientational interpretation unstable or dependent on simplifying assumptions. The authors seek to distinguish genuine interfacial molecular states from contamination and fitting artifacts, and to test whether substrate patterning explains bulk film texture (pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Physically constrained optical analysis:** Apply a previously developed ML-refined DFT framework to RSoXS. Hierarchical clustering of calculated transition dipole moments, refined against angle-resolved NEXAFS, produces an optical tensor linking chemical transitions, energy, and polarization. Density and molecular tilt then become direct fitting parameters (Fig. 1; Methods, pp. 8–9).
- **Joint reflectivity interpretation:** Fit energies and polarizations together and compare against independently fitted optical tensors on the same slab structure, substantially reducing material parameters (Figs. 2–3; SI §5.4).
- **Continuous interface reconstruction:** Replace discrete slabs with coupled exponential orientation and density gradients motivated by a Ginzburg–Landau framework. Applied to ZnPc on native-oxide silicon, this identifies a less edge-on, lower-density buried interface and supports an alternative to substrate-imposed bulk orientation (Fig. 4; Table I; SI §6).

The optical-tensor clustering framework predates this paper; its application to joint RSoXS analysis and the resulting interface reconstruction are the central contributions here.

These results support a compact, physically constrained interpretation of the measured reflectivity and a nonuniform ZnPc interface. They do not independently establish a unique microscopic packing arrangement or directly observe growth dynamics. Good agreement with ZnPc-only optics supports **no measurable contamination**, rather than proving absolute chemical purity.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated assumptions:** The optical model neglects new electronic states arising from intermolecular interactions. It assumes uniaxial symmetry and uniform azimuthal averaging (SI §4).
- **Author-stated theoretical limit:** The gradient is an empirical approximation; boundary conditions and the mechanism of molecular realignment remain unresolved (SI §6).
- **Restricted experimental scope:** Reliable reflectivity was obtained for the oxide-supported films. CuI-supported films produced dominant off-specular scattering, preventing the intended contrasting substrate analysis (SI §1.4).
- **Analyst inference—conditional resolution:** Sub-monolayer recovery depends on the optical and structural model. Fig. S13 treats the best fit as ground truth, holds characteristic widths constant during boundary scans, and modifies the error model; it is not an independent structural measurement.
- **Analyst inference—comparison and uncertainty:** The Free baseline deliberately retains full tensor complexity rather than the reduced parameterization used in some prior work. Experimental corrections can correlate with structure and optics, and reported uncertainties derive from local curvature/covariance estimates (SI §5.4).
- **Reconstruction is illustrative:** Molecular positions, disorder, and interdigitation in the visualization include choices made for visual continuity; the image is not an atomically resolved observation (p. 10).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** The headline “17× fewer” parameters differs from the detailed counts above. The second-film evidence establishes density consistency, despite broader wording in the main text about orientation and density (p. 4; Fig. S10).

## Open research questions
1. **How robust are the recovered boundaries to alternative profiles and error models?** Sensitivity is evaluated around the preferred model. A next experiment could compare alternative gradient families, correction bounds, and uncertainty models using held-out energy/polarization measurements.
2. **Does the inferred gradient arise during deposition as proposed?** Final-state profiles do not directly measure growth dynamics. In-situ polarized reflectivity during continuous deposition could track whether orientation and density evolve as predicted.
3. **Can the approach resolve altered chemistry or intermolecular electronic states?** The demonstrated fit assumes ZnPc-only optics and neglects new collective states. Controlled oxidation or mixed-composition films could test detection limits and model failure.
4. **Does the interface behavior generalize across substrates?** CuI samples could not yield reliable specular data. Preparing smoother films on contrasting substrates could test whether boundary states and decay lengths respond systematically to substrate interactions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods and supporting information substantiate the fitting workflow, but physical uniqueness and broad generalization remain conditional.

Unavailable here are numerical AIC/BIC and gradient-fit improvements, raw reflectivity data and full fitted parameters, independent sub-monolayer structural ground truth, and a quantified contamination detection limit. The paper provides code links and public NEXAFS data; most other supporting measurements are available only upon request.
