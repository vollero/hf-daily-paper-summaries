# AlphaBet: Reinforcement Learning for LLM Event Forecasting Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22684) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22684)


## One-sentence takeaway
AlphaBet trains an LLM to acquire evidence and report event probabilities across multiple dates, with reported Brier-score improvements on two benchmarks, although the supplied text ends before the main results table’s values.

## Problem and motivation
Event forecasts must change as evidence accumulates. The authors identify a gap in outcome-based LLM training: it typically optimizes probability reporting from preassembled evidence at one date, leaving evidence acquisition and updating across dates outside the learning loop (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint agent training:** A single policy searches date-restricted articles, reads sources, runs Python, and reports outcome probabilities. Each dated forecast receives feedback against the eventual outcome, primarily through negative Brier score (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Multi-date supervision:** Resolved events provide training examples under several information states. Variants either start each date afresh or carry a belief notebook across dates (§3.1).
- **Objective analysis:** Under stated assumptions, forecasting regret decomposes into information-acquisition and probability-reporting gaps. This does not guarantee that training improves each gap separately (§3.2, Proposition 1).
- **Normalization analysis:** The authors extend prior binary analysis to multiclass proper scores, showing how GRPO standardization can shift the reporting target. AlphaBet retains mean-centering and removes standard-deviation normalization (§3.3, Proposition 2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Memory-on training uses immediate per-date rewards. Earlier notebook actions receive no explicit credit for their effects on later forecasts (§3.3).
- **Author-stated:** Proposition 2 concerns local, fixed-context, score-only updates, not calibration of the complete trained agent (§3.3).
- **Author-stated:** The implemented probability-floored log score lacks exact properness over the full simplex (§3.2).
- **Conditional theory:** The regret decomposition assumes no outcome leakage. The format-reward argument additionally assumes a forecasting-optimal policy can satisfy every format check (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence from one trained base model and two reported benchmarks leaves transfer to other models and forecasting environments uncertain (§§1, 4.1).
- **Missing evidence:** The supplied text stops at the beginning of Table 1. Referenced appendices, proofs, full implementation details, and empirical ablations are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Would cross-date credit assignment improve notebook use?** The current surrogate omits future rewards for earlier actions (§3.3). Compare it with episode-return training under matched budgets.
- **When does memory help beyond multi-date training?** Both variants are described, but their comparative results are missing. Compare memory-on and memory-free policies on the same dated events and retrieval budgets.
- **How broadly does removing normalization help?** Theory isolates reporting under fixed context, while end-to-end numerical ablations are unavailable. Compare normalization choices across outcome counts and proper scores, measuring calibration separately.
- **Do acquisition gains transfer beyond the training corpus?** Training uses temporally restricted CC-News (§4.1), while evidence–reader swap details are missing. Repeat those swaps on held-out source collections.
