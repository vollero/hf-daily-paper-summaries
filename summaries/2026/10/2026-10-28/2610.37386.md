# Channel Selection for Mixture-of-Experts Decoding Needs No Calibration Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37386) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37386)


## One-sentence takeaway
On the tested frozen MoE models, top-k channel selection usually matches or exceeds calibrated thresholds, while the authors’ weight-derived reader retains 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads (Sections 4.2 and 7).

## Problem and motivation
Interactive MoE decoding at small batches is bottlenecked by loading expert weights. Channel selectors reduce these reads by computing only influential intermediate channels, commonly using thresholds fitted on calibration data.

The authors question whether calibration provides predictable read budgets or better accuracy. They identify a gap in earlier comparisons: most tested dense models on short tasks, sometimes changed channel scores alongside selection rules, and did not compare against top-k at thresholds’ realized reads (Sections 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled keep-rule comparisons:** Compare threshold and top-k versions using the same scores, both at calibrated target budgets and at realized reads. Context interventions and fixed-count controls investigate what threshold-driven channel counts track (Section 4; Appendices E–F).
- **Weight-derived reader:** Apply a blockwise randomized Hadamard rotation and ternary weight sketch to estimate channel contributions. Rank channels across routed experts using router weights, down-projection norms, and estimated activations; compute selected channels exactly; approximate skipped contributions through a rank-32 SVD recovery map (Section 5).
- **Hardware implementation and accounting:** Provide Triton kernels integrated with vLLM, count all auxiliary and selected-weight reads in hardware sectors, and check traffic with a GPU profiler (Section 2; Appendices C and J).

“Calibration-free” means no fitting on target-model activation data: three design settings were selected on OLMoE development data and reused across models (Section 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold budgets drift downward | In 72 runs covering Prox and the reader’s threshold variant across three models, three benchmarks, and four budgets, all undershot target reads. Prox targeting 18% averaged 12.9%, with a minimum of 9.9%. | Section 4.1; Figure 4 |
| Top-k generally matches or exceeds thresholds | At 13% and 18% target budgets, same-score top-k tied in 30 comparisons, led in 10, and trailed in 3; two jointly collapsed TEAL comparisons were excluded. At realized reads, it matched or exceeded thresholds in 37/41 comparisons. | Section 4.2; Figures 5 and 8 |
| Gemma collapse persists at similar reads | On Gemma 4 GPQA-Diamond, four samples per question: Prox’s threshold variant scored 11.7 at 13.3% reads, versus 56.3 for top-k at 13.5%; length-limit hits fell from 674/792 to 145/792. | Section 4.3; Appendix H, Figure 10 |
| Reader preserves most benchmark accuracy | Across nine pairs spanning three post-trained models and GPQA-Diamond, IFEval, and MMLU-Pro, mean accuracy was 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% routed-expert reads; the weakest pair retained 95.0%. | Abstract; Section 1; Section 7 |
| Partial screening improves the read allocation | On held-out OLMoE at equal reads, partial versus full screening reduced next-token KL from 0.488 to 0.310 at 13.5% reads, and from 0.245 to 0.187 at 18.5%. | Section 5.3 |
| Whole-model acceleration is smaller than block acceleration | At 17.8% reads on one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU, batch-one MoE blocks accelerated 1.60–1.90× and whole models 1.15–1.36× over optimized vLLM. Whole-model peaks were 1.75×, 1.74×, and 1.88× for Qwen3-Thinking, Gemma 4, and Qwen3.5 at batch eight or sixteen. | Section 6.2; Appendix J |

These results support predictable channel counts and strong accuracy–read trade-offs in the tested decoding regime. They do not establish universal top-k superiority: some thresholds win, including CATS on two higher-budget Gemma comparisons (Appendix F.3). Statistical “ties” mean no detected difference beyond two paired standard errors, rather than demonstrated equivalence; comparisons receive no multiplicity correction (Appendix B). Routed-expert read percentages also exclude always-active shared experts and dense MLPs.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Only routed experts during decoding are modified; tested models reach 35B parameters. Kernels were tuned for one budget and GPU type, and gains diminish at larger batches and longer contexts (Section 7; Appendix J).
- **Memory and development choices:** Sketch and recovery storage add approximately 7% and 0.5% of a BF16 checkpoint. Partial-screen settings were chosen on OLMoE and checked only on Gemma 4 (Section 7).
- **Comparison qualification:** WINA variants omit its orthogonal weight transform. Gemma’s implementation also overlaps its dense MLP, unlike baseline vLLM; matching that overlap would reduce reported block speedups (Appendices B and J).
- **Analyst inference:** The mechanism behind Gemma’s collapse remains unresolved. Fixed-count controls cannot separate calibrated layer allocations from the low reads those allocations produce (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Near-full average accuracy can conceal task-specific losses: an eight-sample GPQA rerun found deficits of 3.9 ± 1.0 points on Gemma and 4.6 ± 1.4 on Qwen3-Thinking at 17.8% reads (Appendix H).

## Open research questions

- **Can adaptive counts outperform fixed top-k predictably?** Variable counts helped Qwen3.5 in some equal-read tests, while the tested need predictor underperformed fixed counts. Next experiment: evaluate budget-constrained adaptive allocation on held-out generation tasks (Appendix E).
- **What triggers repetition collapse?** Layer allocation and low realized reads remain entangled. Next experiment: independently vary per-layer budgets and total reads while measuring repetition onset (Appendices E and F.2).
- **Do gains transfer to other deployment settings?** Speed evidence uses one GPU type and one reader budget. Next experiment: repeat quality, memory, and latency measurements across hardware, quantized checkpoints, and larger models (Section 7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methods, controls, and evaluation protocols, but many tables contain captions without numerical bodies, preventing complete reconstruction of per-task results and timings. Repository execution was not verified. Broader hardware transfer, larger-model behavior, and a causal explanation of collapse remain unestablished.
