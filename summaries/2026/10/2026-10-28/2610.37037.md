# Zooming without Zooming v2: From Seeing Details to Connecting Evidence for Generalizable Multimodal Perception

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37037) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37037)


## One-sentence takeaway
Training VLMs with RLVR on 20.4K verified, single-image questions requiring evidence from multiple regions improves accuracy across 25 benchmarks, including multi-image and agent evaluations, although the proposed shared-capability explanation remains only partially isolated.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target failures to identify, bind, and combine visual facts dispersed across objects, images, or dialogue turns. They argue that these operations underpin spatial perception and multimodal long-context understanding, but direct supervision is costly: spatial labels require reliable localization and relations, while long-context and interactive data require diverse, answerable sequences (§1).

Their proposed gap is a scalable source of verifiable supervision for this shared capability, which they call **connecting evidence**.

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared training target:** The authors frame evidence identification, entity binding, relation recovery, and composition as a transferable capability underlying several perception tasks (§§1–3).
- **EVIDENCECONNECT synthesis:** From SA-1B images, the pipeline selects coherent groups of 3–7 regions and generates aggregation, comparison/matching, or dependency-chain questions using privileged crops. Training receives the full image, usually with colored region outlines (§3; Appendix A).
- **Answer and dependency checks:** Qwen and Kimi independently anchor answers. Adaptive drop-one tests mask individual crops and reject questions when more than one region is empirically redundant. Verified questions become four-option examples, followed by difficulty filtering (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Transfer evaluation:** GRPO training without supervised fine-tuning produces ZwZ2 models at 4B, 9B, and 27B. Experiments examine downstream transfer, synthesis ablations, additional Qwen3-VL models, context length, and attention alignment (§4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad improvements over base models | Unweighted mean accuracy across 25 benchmarks: 49.41→54.79% at 4B, 51.63→56.59% at 9B, and 55.52→60.51% at 27B; every listed benchmark improves at each scale. | Table 1 |
| Single-image training transfers to other settings | At 4B, MileBench accuracy rises 60.08→70.08%, ViewSpatial-Bench 37.43→45.26%, and AgentVista 12.44→17.22%. | Table 1 |
| Strong synthetic-data comparison | With Qwen3.5-4B and the same GRPO recipe, mean accuracy over 11 benchmarks is 53.86% for EVIDENCECONNECT versus 49.86% for ZwZ and 47.16% for the base model; training sets contain 20.4K and 73K examples, respectively. | Table 2; §4.3 |
| Drop-one verification helps on average | At 7K examples, 11-benchmark mean accuracy is 52.65% with verification versus 51.03% without it; ERQA falls 51.50→47.50%. | Table 3 |
| Privileged views help on average | At 7K examples, mean accuracy is 52.65% with privileged-view synthesis versus 51.38% with original-image synthesis; original-image synthesis performs better on Chartography and ERQA. | Table 3; §4.4 |
| Transfer extends to additional base models | Across the same 11 benchmarks, Qwen3-VL-2B improves 36.82→41.63% and Qwen3-VL-4B improves 41.70→44.61%. | Figure 3; §4.5 |
| Long-context benefits weaken at the longest lengths | MemLens gains persist through 256K tokens, but 4B and 9B merely match their bases at 128K on the MMLongBench visual-core subset. | Figure 5; §4.5 |
| Attention alignment changes are small and mostly positive | On 405 paired TreeBench examples, mean evidence-token IoU increases in 7/8 full-attention layers at 4B and 6/8 at 9B; the largest listed increase is 0.533 percentage points. | Appendix E; Table 5 |

The results support transfer from this particular synthetic-data and RLVR recipe across the reported benchmarks. They do not establish that a single atomic capability causes all gains: the single-region comparison is approximate, and attention IoU measures spatial alignment at the final prompt position rather than reasoning correctness or causal evidence use (Appendix E).

ZwZ2-27B also has the highest overall score among the models in Table 1, but the advanced-model comparison is qualified by platform-dependent input-resolution constraints and low/non-thinking settings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Single-image supervision does not directly model temporal or cross-frame dependencies; video extension remains future work (Appendix G).
- **Author-stated:** Benefits beyond 27B parameters and at earlier training stages remain untested (Appendix G).
- **Author interpretation:** Short-context training may leave very-long-input retrieval as a bottleneck; gains narrow at the longest evaluated contexts (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Multi-region composition is not fully isolated. ZwZ differs in dataset size and synthesis design, and the authors explicitly call it an approximate ablation (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Model agreement and prompted masking tests provide imperfect evidence guarantees. The pipeline tolerates one redundant region, acknowledges crop-overlap leakage and response variability, and reports no human-audited label-error rate (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Dependence on colored outlines remains unclear because no marked-versus-unmarked training ablation or final unmarked proportion is reported.
- **Reporting limits:** The supplied results lack repeated-run uncertainty estimates. Prompt templates are condensed, and synthesis cost and compute requirements are not quantified (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
1. **Does multi-region composition itself drive transfer?** The ZwZ comparison changes several factors. A matched experiment could hold source images, example count, verification, difficulty, and GRPO fixed while varying single-region versus multi-region questions.
2. **How accurate are the evidence-dependence checks?** Model agreement is not a direct correctness audit. Human review of accepted and rejected examples could measure label errors, shortcuts, and false rejection rates.
3. **Would sequence-based synthesis improve the longest contexts?** Current gains narrow there, and temporal dependencies are unmodeled. Compare single-image supervision with matched multi-image or video supervision across context-length bins.
4. **How much does training rely on region outlines?** Most policy views supply explicit anchors. A matched marked/unmarked study could measure transfer to unmarked scenes and reference-resolution tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark outcomes; **medium** in the shared-capability interpretation.

Material missing information includes human-validated data quality, synthesis yield and cost, training hardware/runtime, repeated-run variability, detailed benchmark-overlap checks, the marked/unmarked data split, complete uncondensed prompts, and verified contents of the linked releases.
