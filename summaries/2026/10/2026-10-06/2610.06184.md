# Arm-wise Compositional Generalization in Dual-Arm Vision-Language-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06184) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06184)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under supplied per-arm skill plans, AE-VLA combines skill-conditioned adapters and arm-wise attention to improve unseen dual-arm composition success over matched π₀.₅ baselines, with lower in-domain performance and persistent synchronization difficulty (Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation
The paper studies whether familiar arm-level skills can execute together under unseen ordering, synchronization, or cross-task requirements. This could reduce the need to collect demonstrations for every combination. The authors identify two gaps: conventional task success can conceal incorrect coordination, and differences in backbones and data make architectural comparisons difficult (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **ACG-Bench:** 23 task–condition pairs across eight families, comprising six in-domain conditions and 17 unseen compositions. Success requires the goal plus prescribed physical milestones, order, and timing. A shared state-based scheduler supplies atomic prompts, separating execution from planning (§3.2).
- **Controlled design study:** Methods share pretrained π₀.₅ initialization, source demonstrations, and evaluation rules. Comparisons include joint control, arm-role augmentation through adapted MA-VLA, independent per-arm policies, and grouped architectural variants (§5.1).
- **AE-VLA:** One shared policy uses arm-specific action-token groups, skill-routed LoRA adapters shared across arms and tasks, and attention that restricts local streams while retaining shared global context. The contribution is their tested combination and empirical interaction; the authors distinguish these variants from complete prior systems (§4; Appendix B.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher simulation generalization success | Mean CCSR over 17 unseen conditions, 100 episodes per condition: AE-VLA **21.53%**, Single **2.94%**, adapted MA-VLA **3.06%**, Dual **5.53%**. | §5.2; Table 1 |
| Strong interaction between design choices | Generalization CCSR: Token Group **3.82%**, +SkillLoRA **6.00%**, +AWA **5.82%**, both **21.53%**; reported interaction is **13.53 percentage points** in the grouped comparison. | §5.3; Table 2 |
| Gains carry an in-domain cost | Six-condition simulation mean CCSR: AE-VLA **27.17%**, Single **37.00%**, Token Group **45.00%**. | Table 2 |
| Pure synchronization remains difficult | AE-VLA group-mean CCSR: Reorder **26.50%**, Sync **8.67%**, Sync+Reorder **22.00%**, Cross-task **40.00%**, with respective condition counts **4, 6, 4, 3**. | §§3.2, 5.2 |
| Partial progress differs from full success | Across unseen simulation conditions, AE-VLA and Dual achieve **44.25%** and **40.64%** normalized milestone progress, versus **21.53%** and **5.53%** CCSR. | §5.2 |
| Physical-robot generalization improves | Five unseen SO101 conditions with distractors, 20 trials per condition: mean success **39.00%** for AE-VLA versus **2.00%**, **6.00%**, and **10.00%** for Single, MA-VLA, and Dual. | §5.4; Table 3 |
| Physical gains are uneven | Push Cubes/Sync: AE-VLA **2/20**, Dual **6/20** successes. Three-condition in-domain means: AE-VLA **60.00%**, Dual **70.00%**. | Table 3 |

These results support improved compositional **execution in the tested settings**, with complementary benefits from the combined architectural choices. They do not establish autonomous plan construction, uniform task improvements, or which internal component causes the interaction. Progress can include colliding executions and therefore does not establish safe completion (Appendix C.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated confounds:** SkillLoRA adds parameters and router supervision; AWA changes both cross-arm and temporal attention. Token grouping also doubles the action-token sequence length. The comparisons do not isolate these changes individually (§5.3; Appendix B).
- **Author-stated scope:** Plans are supplied, robot setups remain fixed, and cross-task groups also change targets or context. Matching operation names does not establish that every manipulation detail is familiar (§3; Appendix A.4).
- **Comparison boundaries:** MA-VLA is adapted to π₀.₅; complete TwinVLA and SkillVLA systems are not quantitatively evaluated (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Reliability across training runs is unresolved because training-seed repetitions and uncertainty intervals are not reported. Physical evidence covers a limited task and condition set (§§5.1, 5.4).
- **Scoring caveat:** Earlier Single/MA-VLA logs were rescored under finalized timing windows; two Dual conditions received manual collision overrides. The authors document consistency checks, but progress retains those collisions (Appendix C.4).

## Open research questions
- **What produces the adapter–attention interaction?** The bundled changes prevent causal attribution. Compare parameter-matched shared adapters, shuffled skill labels, and separately controlled cross-arm and temporal masks (Appendices B.2–B.3).
- **Can timing improve without sacrificing familiar routines?** Synchronization and in-domain results remain weak. Sweep synchronization windows and test added waiting demonstrations while measuring both in-domain and unseen CCSR (§5.3; Appendix B.1).
- **Can a planner construct executable compositions?** The scheduler currently supplies the plan. Replace it with a planner that assigns skills and revises them from feedback, then separately measure planning errors and execution failures (§6).
- **How stable are the gains across runs and setups?** Training-run variability and broader embodiment transfer are unreported. Repeat training with multiple seeds and evaluate additional robot setups using matched protocols.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons and task scope; causal interpretation remains limited.

Material missing information includes public code/data/checkpoint availability, training-seed variability and confidence intervals, router accuracy, isolated parameter/supervision/mask controls, inference latency and memory costs, exact real-world synchronization windows, and robustness to incorrect prompts.
