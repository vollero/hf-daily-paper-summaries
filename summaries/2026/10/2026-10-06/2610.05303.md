# ASCENT: Online Test-Time Training of Long-Horizon Agents via Self-Distillation of Verified Experience

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05303) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05303)


## One-sentence takeaway
ASCENT improves long-horizon agents across task streams by distilling their own verifier-accepted trajectories through a frozen, hindsight-conditioned teacher into persistent LoRA weights, with reported gains in success, interaction efficiency, and held-out-scene transfer.

## Problem and motivation
Deployed agents encounter related tasks, but textual memories depend on retrieval and a frozen model’s ability to execute the retrieved guidance. The authors identify a gap in persistent parameter adaptation using just one attempt per task, immediate outcome verification, and no separate adaptation phase or replay (§§1–3.1).

Their controlled experiments show that directly imitating or reinforcing these single trajectories can cause policy drift, invalid actions, and declining success (§3.2, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Online evaluation setting:** OaTTT scores each task before updating from its trajectory; weights persist into subsequent tasks. Each task receives one attempt, and trajectories are discarded after updating (§3.1, Appendix B.3).
- **Verified hindsight self-distillation:** After an accepted episode, the frozen initial model receives the successful trajectory as privileged context. Its full next-token distributions supervise the student at the student’s original prefixes through forward KL; failed episodes trigger no update (§3.3.1).
- **Filtered teacher context:** Invalid-action turns are removed from privileged context, while their original token positions still receive student supervision. Default context includes reasoning and actions but omits observations (§3.3.2).
- **Analysis and validation:** The paper characterizes the population distillation target and what sparse verification cannot identify, then tests success, transfer, efficiency, and design choices. This is a deployment-specific combination of existing self-distillation and LoRA components, rather than a new distillation primitive (Appendix A, §§4.2–4.4).

## Evidence and main results

Results are reported over three independent runs; ± values below denote standard deviations. Online outcomes are scored before their own updates (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher ALFWorld success | On the 140-task seen stream, exact SR: 4B ASCENT **69.5±5.6%**, base 46.4%, online MemP 63.6±5.9%; 9B ASCENT **77.4±1.8%**, base 55.0%, online ACE 69.3±6.3%. | Table 1 |
| Higher WebShop exact success | On 500-task streams, strict SR requires score=1: 4B ASCENT **41.9±0.3%**, base 17.8%, online MemP 36.8±3.4%; 9B ASCENT **41.5±1.8%**, base 17.2%, TT-OPSD 24.2±2.4%. Adaptation accepts score≥0.9. | Table 2; §4.1 |
| Transfer and continued learning | On 134 held-out ALFWorld tasks, frozen-transfer SR is **62.7±7.5% / 88.8±6.6%** for 4B/9B; continued adaptation reaches **78.6±5.1% / 90.0±3.6%**. Bases score 43.3% / 49.3%. | Tables 4–5 |
| Shorter paths on shared successes | On ALFWorld tasks solved by both policies, mean ASCENT-minus-base turns: **−4.8±0.9** at 4B and **−7.0±0.3** at 9B. | Table 8 |
| Runtime depends on benchmark | Mean seconds/episode, including updates: ALFWorld ASCENT/base **115/97** at 4B and **133/115** at 9B; WebShop **76/87** and **164/166**. | Tables 1–2; §4.2 |
| AppWorld gains have a scenario-level boundary | At 4B, Normal TGC rises from 20.2% to **26.2±1.3%**; Challenge TGC from 15.8% to **20.0±2.5%**. Challenge SGC is **5.3±0.9%**, versus base 5.8%. | Table 6 |
| Filtering benefit varies by scale | Reasoning-plus-action context, unfiltered→filtered ALFWorld SR: **60.0→69.5%** at 4B and **75.3→77.4%** at 9B. | Table 3 |

These results support improvement under the tested streaming protocols. Shared-success comparisons strengthen the efficiency evidence beyond merely replacing capped failures with successes. They do not identify causal turn credit, guarantee future-task improvement, or demonstrate performance in real deployments. The population identity characterizes teacher-target averaging under a fixed collection distribution; it does not prove convergence or transfer (Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Full teacher distributions restrict the method to open-weight models. Sparse verification and equal turn weighting provide no fine-grained credit. Teacher context may partly reconstruct student-generated text (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Verification and validity filtering cannot distinguish useful valid actions from unnecessary ones; shorter paths are not theoretically guaranteed (Appendix A.1.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Generality is bounded by the tested Qwen backbones and simulated benchmarks; AppWorld is evaluated only at 4B (§4.1, Appendix B.5).
- **Analyst inference:** TT-OPSD differs in both hindsight scope and retention of invalid-action turns, so its comparison alone does not isolate full-trajectory hindsight (Appendix B.2).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Teacher-evolution prose/Fig. 7 describe a frozen-teacher reference near 65%, whereas Table 7 lists 69.5%; the supplied text does not reconcile the settings (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
- **How much gain comes from hindsight versus text reconstruction?** The authors leave this unresolved. Compare verbatim context with paraphrased reasoning and masked current-response text while holding accepted trajectories fixed (§5).
- **Can finer turn credit improve learning?** Equal weighting retains valid detours. Compare outcome-only distillation with turn-weighted supervision using controlled environment diagnostics (§5; Appendix A.1.3).
- **Does adaptation remain stable over longer, changing streams?** Held-out scenes show transfer, but broader long-term retention is unestablished. Test alternating task domains and periodically measure earlier-domain performance (§4.2).
- **Can teacher refresh improve on a frozen teacher?** Tested evolving teachers provide no overall gain. Evaluate refreshes triggered by independently verified student improvement (Appendix C.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark findings; **medium** for broader deployment implications.

Unavailable or unresolved: verified code/checkpoint release status, task-order sensitivity, longer-stream retention, isolation of teacher text reconstruction, and reconciliation of the teacher-evolution reference results.
