# CANOPY: Adaptive-Granularity Evidence Compression for Multimodal RAG

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00923) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00923)


## One-sentence takeaway
CANOPY’s learned hierarchical compression reduces reader-input evidence tokens by 14.2–27.7% with comparable answer quality in the unrouted Qwen3-VL-8B setting, while additional retrieval drives its main multi-hop QA gains (§4.3, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Retrieving a relevant item does not determine how much of it the reader needs: whole items can introduce distractions, while fine fragments can lose interpretive context. The authors identify a gap in shared, inexpensive procedures that adapt retained granularity within heterogeneous items, beyond modality-specific compressors or predefined retrieval granularities (§§1–2).

Compression also cannot recover missing evidence, motivating a separate mechanism for follow-up retrieval (§3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive evidence selection:** Represent text as sentence hierarchies, tables as row hierarchies with headers, and videos as temporal hierarchies. Explore every child scoring at least as high as its parent; retain the parent when none qualifies. Branches stop independently, preserving multiple original regions at different depths (§3.2).
- **Training aligned with refinement:** Fine-tune a node encoder using gold-derived parent–child and sibling ranking preferences, while freezing the query encoder. Cached node embeddings enable pruning through similarity comparisons without node-level LLM calls (§§3.2, 3.4).
- **End-to-end integration and evaluation:** An LLM critic requests missing facts through targeted follow-up queries. Component ablations distinguish retrieval gains from compression savings (§§3.3, 4.3).

The authors locate the methodological distinction in learned hierarchical compression, rather than in combining compression with iterative retrieval (§2). Images remain whole, and aggregate table questions bypass refinement (§3.2).

These results support compression savings within the tested pipelines and substantial benefits from additional retrieval. They do not establish compression-driven accuracy gains, superior hierarchical operating points over tuned flat selection, or universal gains across readers and modalities. Reader-token counts exclude critic inputs; reported latency excludes vector search and node encoding (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Explicitly acknowledged boundaries:** Critic sufficiency judgments are operational stopping decisions, not completeness guarantees. Gold-derived training targets may omit useful context. Images are never spatially compressed, aggregate tables stay whole, and no explicit retained-token budget is enforced (§§3.2–3.4).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**
- Evaluation covers filtered benchmark subsets, especially NQ and available LVBench videos, limiting conclusions about the original full populations (Appendix A).
- Questions are disjoint between training and testing, but item/source-disjoint splits are not established (Appendix A).
- S2G-RAG’s trained judge is replaced by an untrained reader LLM, so the comparison does not establish performance against its original configuration (Appendix B).
- Cached encoding can make pruning inexpensive, but total serving cost remains uncertain because timing omits node encoding and search (Appendix B).
- Previously retrieved items are skipped, preventing reconsideration of discarded regions under a later query (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Can video compression preserve more evidence?** LVBench has weak refinement decision accuracy and low conditional gold recall (Appendix H). Compare finer temporal leaves and denser frame sampling at matched reader budgets.
- **Does hierarchy help under a fixed budget?** Tuned flat selection reaches comparable operating points (Appendix G). Compare both methods under identical budgets across item lengths and modalities.
- **Should later queries reopen earlier items?** The pipeline keeps their original selections (Appendix B). Test re-refinement against skipping duplicates, measuring recovered evidence, answer quality, and cost.
- **Do token savings translate into total cost savings?** Current latency excludes search and encoding (Appendix B). Measure full pipeline latency and all prompt tokens under cold and warm caches.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and experimental distinctions; broader efficiency and generalization conclusions remain conditional.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes verified code/checkpoint release availability, complete pipeline cost including critic tokens and node encoding, item/source-disjointness, aggregate-classifier error rates, and direct calibration or completeness measurements for critic judgments.
