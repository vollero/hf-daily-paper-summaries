# Foresight: planning future perception in streaming VLMs without retraining

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03123) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03123)


## One-sentence takeaway

Foresight uses a frozen VLM to schedule future perception and reasoning asynchronously, reporting stronger streaming results, although unequal evaluation splits and averaging conventions qualify its headline comparisons (§§3–4; Appendix D.1).

## Problem and motivation

Streaming assistants must decide when evidence is sufficient, what to inspect next, and how much computation to spend while video continues arriving. The authors identify a gap in existing systems: even proactive, asynchronous methods generally trigger computation reactively or follow fixed planning schedules. They propose using anticipated future evidence to control the next observation and reasoning step (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Anticipatory control:** Each plan chooses the next check time, a question to revisit, and future sampling density. Current evidence is recomputed at each check; control settings persist until changed (§§3–4.1).
- **Concurrent architecture:** Two execution roles share frozen model weights and a causal KV cache. Ingest appends observations; Think plans from a read-only snapshot, allowing perception to continue during reasoning (Figure 2; Algorithm 1).
- **Lightweight execution:** Schema-guided decoding supplies fixed structure, probes Boolean confidence through token logits, and generates control differences. The executor applies sampling, class-based token pruning, and cache controls; calibrated temporal gates suppress premature or repeated responses (§4.1; Appendix A).

The proposed contribution is the integrated inference-time controller, rather than a newly trained backbone or benchmark. Weights remain frozen, but response thresholds are fitted using benchmark data (§4.1).

The ablations support scheduling and question retention as useful components in the tested configuration. They do not establish that every adaptive control improves accuracy. ## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** Performance depends on backbone anticipation and temporal grounding. False triggers can lead to repeated unsupported reports despite response history, and control remains sensitive to prompt wording (§6). Short anticipation horizons also require repeated checks during uneventful periods (Appendix F).

**Analyst inferences and comparison risks:**

- Foresight uses a randomly held-out **80%** of each benchmark after **20%** calibration, while baseline results use full evaluation sets; differences may partly reflect evaluation composition (§4.1).
- StreamingBench excludes Proactive Output from Foresight’s overall and Contextual averages, while baseline Contextual averages include it. This weakens direct interpretation of the claimed overall lead (Appendix D.1).
- Baseline provenance and aggregation vary: Dispider uses a different subset, OmniPro sources pool overall scores differently, and OVO includes method-reported values alongside revised benchmark references (Appendices C.1, E).
- Only one backbone is evaluated within Foresight. Claims of transfer to stronger backbones remain prospective (§§4.1, 5).
- The supplied text does not isolate shared-cache concurrency, probe decoding, or diff updates through measured runtime ablations; their efficiency benefits are chiefly modeled (Appendix G).

## Open research questions

- **Do headline gains survive matched evaluation?** Splits and averaging differ. Next experiment: rerun baselines and Foresight on identical held-out examples with identical task aggregation.
- **Which controls improve the accuracy–compute tradeoff?** FPS removal preserves accuracy, and cache-control removal improves it slightly. Next experiment: measure latency, memory, admitted tokens, and joint F1 for every Table 4 variant.
- **How robust is planning across backbones and prompts?** One deployed backbone and acknowledged prompt sensitivity leave this unresolved. Next experiment: repeat controller evaluation across frozen backbones and controlled prompt paraphrases.
- **Can longer anticipation reduce checks without missed events?** Appendix F shows horizon decay. Next experiment: compare scheduling policies across anticipation horizons, measuring checks per event, recall, and false triggers.
