# OmniReasoning: Pushing the Limits of Audio-Visual Joint Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39490) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39490)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors combine evidence-dependent audio-visual questions with modality-factored token supervision and report improvements over Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking, but the supplied text lacks the detailed experimental tables needed to assess those gains fully.

## Problem and motivation
Video reasoning can require combining spoken information with visible evidence across multiple steps. The authors argue that existing benchmarks often permit single-modality shortcuts, training data insufficiently preserves cross-modal dependencies, and outcome-level reinforcement learning does not identify which reasoning tokens benefit from combining modalities (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **OmniReasoningBench:** Questions with annotated evidence chains covering reasoning within a video and applying video-derived knowledge to new scenarios or figures (§3.1).
- **OmniQA:** A data engine producing timestamped modality-specific clues, dependency chains, QA pairs, and synthesized thinking processes. Candidates undergo structural, answer, media-grounding, and modality-shortcut checks (§3.2).
- **Modality-Factored Self-Distillation (MFSD):** Extends RLSD by scoring the same sampled response with no clues, audio clues, visual clues, and joint clues. It combines joint-clue likelihood gains with a centered non-additive interaction signal to weight token-level outcome advantages (§3.3).
- **Training assets and model:** The authors describe released SFT and RL datasets and train OmniReasoning-30B-A3B from Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking (§3.2; §4, Table 1 caption).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Reported model improvement | OmniReasoning-30B-A3B reaches 50.0% accuracy on OmniVideoBench and 42.5% on OmniReasoningBench, improving over Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking by 12.8 and 9.3 percentage points, respectively. | Abstract; §1 |
| Joint inputs outperform an independent-modality oracle | On reasoning-over-video questions, reported accuracy gains are 9.9–26.7 percentage points across three models and both question formats. Model identities and individual results are absent here. | §3.1, referring to Appendix |
| Restricted visual-only performance | Qwen3.5-Plus without audio reportedly achieves 17.2% MCQ and 8.7% open-ended accuracy on the proposed benchmark; detailed protocol and breakdown are unavailable. | §1 |

These are author-reported results. The oracle comparison supports complementary evidence use beyond separately successful modality runs. It does not establish that every question universally requires both modalities. The headline improvements combine training data and MFSD; the supplied text does not isolate their respective contributions. Broader video-benchmark gains are claimed without numerical results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated qualification:** MFSD’s interaction score measures response likelihood under clue conditioning, not reasoning-step correctness. Its conditional-PMI interpretation requires an assumption described in the unavailable Appendix (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Shortcut screening depends on the models used for filtering. Their failure under restricted inputs cannot rule out shortcuts exploitable by other models (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Synthesized thinking is generated from captions and evidence chains without viewing the source video, making its fidelity dependent on upstream annotations and verification (§3.2).
- **Evidence limitation:** The supplied text stops at the Table 1 caption. It provides no experimental rows, uncertainty estimates, method ablations, or training-cost comparison. Consequently, MFSD’s incremental benefit and the robustness of reported gains cannot be assessed.

## Open research questions
- **How much improvement comes from MFSD itself?** The reported gains combine data and learning changes. A matched-data comparison of SFT-only, GRPO, RLSD, and MFSD would isolate the learning contribution.
- **Does modality dependence survive stronger shortcut solvers?** Filtering and oracle evidence involve particular models. Re-evaluating all questions with held-out question-only, audio-only, and visual-only solvers would test that boundary.
- **Does likelihood interaction track correct evidence integration?** The authors explicitly deny a correctness guarantee. Comparing token scores with human-judged reasoning steps and controlled clue removal would test the relationship.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and data pipeline are clearly described, but empirical assessment is limited by missing experiment tables and appendices.

Unavailable information includes detailed benchmark scores, evaluation and answer-grading protocols, model identities for the oracle comparison, ablations, training hyperparameters and compute, data-source and split details, and the full joint-consistency assumption.
