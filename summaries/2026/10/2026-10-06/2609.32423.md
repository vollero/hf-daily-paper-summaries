# PluginRSI: Recursive Improvement of Agent Harnesses with Reusable Plugins

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32423) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32423)


## One-sentence takeaway
PluginRSI improves fixed-model agent harnesses by alternating isolated plugin mutations with workflow recomposition, reporting stronger benchmark performance than whole-harness optimization while leaving long-term and cross-domain reuse unresolved (§§3–4; Appendix D).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that repeatedly rewriting an entire agent harness couples mechanisms, restricts exploration, and can overwrite useful improvements. They target harness optimization without model-weight updates, aiming to preserve discovered mechanisms as reusable components rather than rediscovering them across candidates (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Plugin-parameterized search:** Represent roles, tools, skills, and memory as versioned Python implementations with YAML interfaces, coordinated by a separate executable workflow (§3.1; Appendix J).
- **Two-stage optimization:** A fixed proposer mutates individual plugins while holding the surrounding harness fixed, screens changes on balanced solved/unsolved validation minibatches, and retains improving variants. It then selects accumulated plugins and revises their coordinating workflow; full-validation improvements replace the incumbent (§3.2; Algorithm 1).
- **Accumulated reusable mechanisms:** A shared library preserves successful implementations across iterations. Experiments test component ablations, solver-model transfer, and restarting optimization with the evolved library (§§4.4–4.5).

The claimed contribution is the combination of independent mechanism discovery, retention, and recomposition; modular harness design itself is acknowledged as prior work (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher software-repair performance | SWE-bench Verified: optimized on 400 tasks, tested on 100. GPT-5.6 Terra and Kimi-K3 settings reach **65% and 69% resolve rate**, respectively, **10 and 6 percentage points above Meta-Harness**, identified as the best baseline. | §4.2; Table 1 caption; §1 |
| Transfer across solver models | Fixed GPT-optimized harness: **60% with Kimi-K3**, **52% with GLM-5.2**, exceeding strongest baselines by **7 and 4 points**. | Command-line gains | Kimi-K3 on Terminal-Bench-2.1, with 59 held-in and 30 held-out tasks: PluginRSI **69.5% / 73.3%** resolve rate versus Meta-Harness **64.4% / 66.7%**. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 2 |
| Smaller QA transfer gains | Kimi-K3, optimization on 150 tasks from three domains, evaluation across eight domains: reported baseline-to-PluginRSI accuracy changes are **0.753→0.820 held-in**, **0.568→0.588 held-out**, and **0.638→0.675 overall**. The prose does not identify the corresponding baseline for each aggregate. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 3 |
| Both optimization stages matter | Kimi-optimized SWE harness, Kimi solver: held-out resolve rate falls from **69% to 57% without mutation**, and to **59% without recomposition**. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Library reuse speeds further optimization | Restarting from the initial harness with the evolved library reaches **69% after two steps**, versus **65% without reuse**. However, this subsequent search optimizes directly on the original 100 held-out tasks. | §4.5; Figure 4; Appendix E |

The reported main evaluations support task-level generalization and transfer of fixed harnesses across the tested solvers. They do not establish unseen-repository generalization or sustained improvement under changing distributions. The reuse experiment demonstrates faster adaptation on its optimization tasks, **not fresh held-out generalization**. Full numerical table bodies and plotted curves are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fixed task sets and limited iterations leave long-term evolution and growing-library management unexplored; transfer depends on domain and solver; isolated mutations can miss mechanisms requiring coordinated changes (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated scope:** SWE held-out tasks come from repositories already represented in held-in data; Seaborn and Flask have no held-out coverage (Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference:** Screening mutations on small minibatches against cached original outcomes may admit noisy improvements; no uncertainty estimates or repeated-search results are supplied (§3.2; Appendices E, K).
- **Analyst inference:** The recomposition ablation selects mutation candidates using minibatch scores, changing both the optimization operation and selection procedure; its performance drop does not isolate coordination alone (§4.4).
- **Comparison boundary:** ACE uses only task-local online memory, without cross-task experience, so the comparison applies to that restricted implementation (Appendix E).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** API-model variability is acknowledged, and experiments were rerun in September; complete configurations, raw prompts, code, and result tables are not available here (Appendices A, E, H).

## Open research questions
- **Can improvement persist under distribution shifts?** Fixed-set experiments leave this open (Appendix D). Next experiment: evaluate a changing task stream with separate future-task tests and library pruning policies.
- **Do coordinated mutations recover missed mechanisms?** Isolated screening can reject changes needing complementary plugins (Appendix D). Next experiment: compare single-plugin and joint plugin/workflow mutations under matched rollout and token budgets.
- **Does library reuse generalize beyond its adaptation tasks?** The reuse search consumes the original held-out split (Appendix E). Next experiment: adapt with evolved versus initial libraries, then evaluate on a third untouched split and new domains.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported aggregate results are clear, but independent assessment is limited by missing full table entries, figure data, confidence intervals, repeated runs, exact model configurations, complete proposer prompts, executable assets, and the repository URL.
