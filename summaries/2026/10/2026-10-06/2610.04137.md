# Dynamic Harness Search: Building Multi-Agent Systems Per-Query via Prediction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04137) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04137)


## One-sentence takeaway

SHIFT uses learned policy and utility predictions to search for a query-specific agent harness without executing candidates during search, reporting 79.9% mean accuracy versus 72.7% for its strongest baseline across six equally weighted benchmarks (§§3–4.2).

## Problem and motivation

Agent roles, instructions, tools, and communication structure interact, and their usefulness varies by query: extra verification may help a complex document task while wasting computation on a lookup. The authors identify a gap between reusable offline workflows and query-specific construction that requires costly candidate execution: predicting utility could enable per-query lookahead before running a selected harness (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Joint harness construction:** SHIFT grows executable directed graphs from a single tool-free Coder using a predefined library of structural changes, directives, and tool grants. Feasibility checks preserve executability (§3.1; Appendix A.1).
- **Learned search without candidate execution:** A local architect supplies action priors and utility estimates to Monte Carlo tree search. Training combines search-visit policy targets, measured execution utilities, and same-query ranking supervision; only LoRA adapters and policy–value heads are trained (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Two selection modes:** SHIFT-search follows the most-visited path; SHIFT-value ranks recovered candidates by predicted utility, optionally expanding search. Their reported accuracy–cost trade-offs, action-space ablations, and harder-task transfer provide the empirical contributions (§4; Appendix B).

The distinctive contribution is this combination of query-specific joint construction, learned utility, and predictive lookahead; the paper does not introduce MCTS or LoRA themselves.

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. Main-suite accuracy averages three inference runs and weights benchmarks equally; execution cost aggregates token ratios to Direct geometrically and excludes architect inference and training (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest main-suite mean accuracy | Across 9,193 tasks with Gemini 3.5 Flash, SHIFT-search achieves 79.9% versus Trace’s 72.7%; improvement: 7.2 percentage points, paired task-bootstrap 95% CI approximately [4.4, 9.9]. | §4.2; Table 1 reference; Appendix E.2 |
| Gains concentrate on tool-intensive tasks | SHIFT-search reaches 59.3% on OfficeQA and 68.6% on GAIA, exceeding Trace by approximately 30.0 and 15.0 percentage points respectively. Test sets contain 41 and 51 questions. | §4.2; Appendix E.2; Figure 16 |
| Lower-cost selection retains a mean advantage | SHIFT-value achieves 74.5% mean accuracy, above every baseline, with 32% lower aggregated execution-token cost than Trace and roughly one-third SHIFT-search’s cost. | §4.3 |
| Search improves cost over greedy construction | With the same trained architect, SHIFT-search achieves 79.9% versus greedy’s 79.8% mean accuracy while using about 30% fewer execution tokens. | §4.3; Table 2 reference; Appendix B |
| Full action space beats restricted variants | On 500 HotpotQA questions, three runs, and 64 simulations: full search scores 69.4%, tools-only 67.9%, and directives-only 60.3%; restrictions are imposed without retraining. | §4.2; Appendix E.3 |
| Learned value ranks identical candidate pools effectively | With Gemma 3 27B, value selection improves accuracy over expected uniform selection by 10.5 percentage points on 400 HotpotQA questions and 2.9 on 200 GSM8K questions; selected execution tokens are 9.5k versus 19.5k and 1.8k versus 8.8k respectively. Each candidate is executed twice. | Appendix F.1; Figure 17 |
| Limited harder-task transfer succeeds | GSM8K-trained architect, without retraining, achieves 83.6% versus a fixed Coder’s 81.2% on MATH-500 using Llama 4 Scout, one execution per problem. On 262 level-4–5 problems: 75.6% versus 71.0%, difference CI [0.4, 9.2] percentage points. | §4.5; Appendix G |
| Search overhead is small in the measured hardware setting | Default 64-simulation construction takes benchmark medians of 0.73–0.90 seconds on one H100 NVL; measurement uses ten questions per benchmark, three repeats, excluding model loading. | Appendix B.2; Figure 15 |

These results support predictive harness selection in the evaluated settings, especially OfficeQA and GAIA. They do not establish universal executor or domain generalization, total deployment-cost savings, or causal effects of individual components. The authors explicitly describe training-time structure–success comparisons as observational (Appendix E.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Separate architects require upfront training per benchmark and executor. Broader dataset/executor generalization and value targets averaged over repeated executions remain future work (§5; Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference — comparison scope:** Several text-output baselines cannot produce SpreadsheetBench workbooks and receive zero accuracy; some are not executed there, and their cost averages exclude that benchmark. Aggregate comparisons therefore include capability mismatches, although excluding SpreadsheetBench preserves SHIFT’s lead (Appendices D and E.2).
- **Analyst inference — ablation scope:** Action restrictions are tested only on a HotpotQA subset, without retraining, and also prevent adding agents. They support the full construction space but do not isolate each component’s independent causal contribution (Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference — validity limits:** OfficeQA and GAIA use small study-specific test partitions; transfer evidence covers one mathematical target with single executions. Architect input truncation and the fixed action library further bound the evaluated design space (Appendices A.2, C, G).
- **Source inconsistency:** Figure 2 names Gemma-2-2B, whereas §3.2 and later implementation text name Gemma 4 E2B.

## Open research questions

- **Can one architect generalize across domains and executors?** Training remains benchmark-specific (§5). Train a shared architect and evaluate held-out benchmarks and executor swaps without adaptation.
- **When do total savings repay training cost?** Reported execution cost excludes training and local inference (§4.1). Measure cumulative monetary and latency costs against Trace and greedy construction across deployment volumes.
- **How much do component interactions contribute?** Existing restrictions conflate missing roles with missing action families (Appendix E.3). Retrain matched structural, instruction, tool, and combined variants across several benchmarks.
- **Would repeated-outcome supervision improve value reliability?** The authors propose averaging execution outcomes (§5). Compare single-outcome and repeated-outcome targets on held-out ranking, calibration, and downstream selection.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative provides substantial methods and results, but most referenced tables contain captions without cell contents.
