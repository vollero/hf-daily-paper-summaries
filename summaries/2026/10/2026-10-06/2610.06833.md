# Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06833) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)


## One-sentence takeaway
Learning the training-depth distribution and controlling input injection improve looped language models, while approximately stationary recurrent states enable smaller KV caches, faster prefill, and cheaper RL gradient computation—with measurable quality tradeoffs.

## Problem and motivation
Every recurrence adds training activations, decoding KV storage, prompt-processing compute, and RL replay work. The authors argue that approaching fixed points allows the recurrent endpoint to replace parts of this trajectory, but existing training choices inadequately balance prediction quality and compatibility with terminal KV sharing: fixed-depth training predicts well at its training depth, while a broad fixed depth prior spreads supervision more than necessary (§§1–4).

A second gap concerns input injection: carried-over states can amplify or cancel the injected input. The paper proposes controlling this interaction explicitly (§4.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Learned depth prior:** An unconditional categorical distribution selects one depth per microbatch before execution. Detached prediction feedback updates its probabilities; entropy encourages depth diversity, and a mean-depth penalty controls the recurrence budget. This is a training distribution, not an inference exit policy (§4.1, Algorithm 2).
- **Orthogonal input injection, OrthoInj:** Project the carried-over state away from the injected input before adding that input. The recipe also removes prelude normalization; an ablation separates these changes (§4.2; Table 14).
- **New endpoint shortcuts:** A non-recurrent student predicts the teacher’s endpoint for prefill, followed by one teacher recurrence to construct terminal KV banks. For RL, saved rollout endpoints support an approximate Neumann-series gradient through one recorded recurrence (§3; Algorithm 1).
- **Explanation and verification of existing shortcuts:** The paper connects truncated backpropagation and terminal KV sharing to fixed-point behavior, with conditional theoretical results and controlled experiments. It does not introduce these two shortcuts themselves (§3; Appendices A, C.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Learned prior improves prediction under sharing | At L scale, mean-depth-five training with entropy coefficient 0.01 lowers shared MixVal PPL from 3.10 to 3.07 and raises seven-task average accuracy from 59.30% to 60.00% versus fixed PLN-5; training FLOPs rise from 1253 to 1273 × 10¹⁹. | Table 2 |
| Smaller cache preserves competitive downstream accuracy | At five recurrences, terminal sharing retains four rather than twelve KV banks. L learned-prior shared accuracy averages 60.00%, versus 59.90% for fixed-depth training with full KV. Fixed-depth GSM8K accuracy falls from 50.64% with full KV to 21.23% with sharing. | Tables 2, 8 |
| Truncation alone improves sharing tolerance | Small models trained at fixed depth five with random initialization and diagonal injection: full BPTT has a +21.9% cached-PPL sharing penalty; a two-recurrence backward window reduces it to +2.9%, while training-depth WikiText PPL worsens from 20.62 to 22.29. | Table 18 |
| OrthoInj gives modest, uneven gains | Under PLN-5 training and depth-five terminal sharing, validation PPL versus Parcae-Decay is 5.67 versus 5.75 at S, 3.94 versus 3.96 at M, and 3.08 versus 3.09 at L. Seven-task averages improve slightly, but generation scores do not improve consistently. | Table 3 |
| RL scoring and backward computation become approximately twice as fast | At L, depth six, GSM8K reuse + Neumann-4 takes 1.39 s versus 2.77 s for full BPTT; sampled pass@1 is 61.65% versus 63.20% on 500 test questions, using eight samples each. The difference’s paired 95% interval is −3.13 to +0.05 points. Timing excludes rollout and optimizer work. | Table 4; Appendix C.8 |
| Distilled prefill trades quality for latency | L student trained on one quarter of teacher tokens: eight-prompt, 8K prefill latency is 830.3 ± 3.0 ms versus 1482.1 ± 32.0 ms; downstream average falls from 60.00% to 55.53%. At similar latency, two-recurrence teacher prefill scores 54.96% at 848.6 ± 1.8 ms. | Table 5 |
| Update savings do not guarantee end-to-end RL savings | MBPP+ reuse + Neumann-4 reduces scoring/backward time from 1.03 to 0.51 s, but total training takes 21.8 versus 20.0 minutes for full BPTT because rollout time increases. | Table 26 |

These results support endpoint shortcuts in the tested models and protocols. They do not establish universal contraction, exact finite-depth gradient equivalence, or uniform quality preservation. Main-table PPL tests prefix sharing with parallel continuation scoring; dedicated CacheEval tests incremental decoding. Native and cached PPL values therefore require matched protocols before comparison (Appendices D.3–D.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One seed per configuration, incomplete design-space coverage, and untested larger scales, other configurations, and real-world feasibility (§7). The scale labels approximately describe non-embedding parameters; total looped-model counts are larger (Table 6).
- **Theoretical scope:** Contractiveness of the projected carryover does not establish contraction of the complete transformer recurrence. A small endpoint residual alone is insufficient to guarantee gradient accuracy (Appendices A.2, C.4).
- **Convergence selection:** Exact-convergence corroboration excludes spans failing convergence checks and requires far deeper solves than practical depth-five sharing (Appendix A.1).
- **Comparison limits:** Depth-prior experiments use Parcae without prelude normalization, rather than OrthoInj; their joint benefit is not established. OrthoInj’s downstream ranking also changes with learning rate, and Table 3 does not support the prose claim of strictly lower WikiText PPL at every scale (Appendices C.1–C.2).
- **Data validity:** Document-level training separation and pretraining decontamination remain unverified (Appendices C.8, D.1–D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation bootstrap intervals cannot establish training-seed robustness. In particular, near-identical reuse/recompute gradients do not substitute for a multi-seed equivalence study.

## Open research questions
1. **Do learned priors and OrthoInj combine reliably?** Their main comparisons are separate. Next experiment: cross both changes under matched learning-rate selection and multiple seeds.
2. **When does endpoint reuse remain accurate?** RL tests one recurrence depth, with approximate states and no estimator convergence guarantee. Next experiment: vary depth and Neumann budget while measuring gradient agreement, quality, and total runtime.
3. **How can distillation preserve deeper-teacher gains?** Quality losses grow with scale and teacher depth. Next experiment: compare student capacity and KV-aware objectives at matched distillation compute (Appendix C.7).
4. **Does sharing survive long-form reasoning?** The paper explicitly leaves this unmeasured. Next experiment: paired full/terminal-cache evaluation with longer completion budgets and convergence diagnostics (Appendix E.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported settings and numerical findings; **medium** in generalization and the proposed causal explanation.

Material information unavailable includes multi-seed training variance, verified decontamination, joint learned-prior–OrthoInj results, long-form reasoning performance, broader deployment measurements, and confirmed availability of trained checkpoints.
