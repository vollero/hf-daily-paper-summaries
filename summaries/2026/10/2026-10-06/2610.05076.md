# Self-Generated Feedback Destabilizes Test-Time Training: A Causal Decomposition of Long-Horizon Adaptation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05076) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the tested long language-model streams, persistent updates on self-generated text worsen independent real-text prediction through feedback into future training data, while validating candidate states largely suppresses the observed damage (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Test-time training stores information in weights during inference, potentially extending memory beyond attention. When an adapting model generates its own next training examples, updates also change its future data. Improved fit to generated text may therefore conceal deteriorating prediction elsewhere.

The authors identify a gap in understanding this within-stream feedback: whether harm comes from synthetic text itself, reading degraded content, or retaining updates on it, especially beyond earlier short evaluation horizons (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Long-horizon failure evidence:** Compare persistent self-writing with **Writes Off**, which reads generated text but discards its updates. Both retain initial real-prefix updates. Replications cover three TTT-E2E configurations and ordinary Adam updates to Qwen3-4B (§§3–4).
- **Controlled decomposition:** **Fixed Generation** freezes the generator while a separate learner adapts; **Recorded Replay** holds degraded tokens fixed while toggling updates; paired one-update branches isolate local effects on source fit and independent prediction. These locate causal pathways rather than assign additive shares of total damage (§5).
- **Update admission criterion:** **Settlement** compares the current and candidate states on independent evidence before commitment, evaluating successive candidates against already accepted changes. Agent experiments use task reward or success. The authors explicitly do not claim external validation itself as a new learning principle (§6.2; Appendix E).

These controls support feedback-mediated damage and a persistent cost beyond reading identical degraded tokens. They do not establish additive causal contributions, a scaling law, or universal failure of synthetic-data learning. Settlement intervals containing zero do not establish equivalence to Writes Off; the agent results also do not establish reliable superiority over that baseline.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Independent language-model evaluation can use disjoint passages from the same book; it measures transfer beyond generated training text, not necessarily cross-domain generalization (§3).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Models have different training lineages; contamination screening cannot prove clean training membership. The reported magnitudes do not establish a scaling law (Appendix A.3).
- **Author-stated causal limit:** History comparisons change both model state and generated text, so they do not isolate receiver susceptibility alone (Appendix C.3).
- **Author-stated validation requirements:** Settlement requires eligible external evidence. Source labels are unnecessary for candidate admission, but identifying validation text remains necessary (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated agent risks:** WebShop validation and final goals are drawn from the test partition without enforced disjointness. ALFWorld uses three seeds and incurs additional validation tasks (Appendices G–H).
- **Analyst inference:** Repeated use of retained validation text could favor that evidence over broader transfer; the supplied experiments do not establish resistance to this effect. Some stochastic execution parity is also explicitly unverified (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Does Settlement transfer across domains?** Most language evidence is book-based and may use same-book passages (§3). Next experiment: validate on one domain and evaluate accepted updates on disjoint domains.
- **What independently causes high-cost trajectories?** History changes both receiver state and source text (§5.3). Next experiment: cross early/late receiver states with fixed early/late source passages.
- **How much validation evidence is sufficient?** Scheduling and buffer capacity are implementation choices, and retained text supplies a fallback (Appendix E). Next experiment: vary validation-bank size, freshness, and reuse while preserving a separate evaluation set.
- **Do agent gains survive strictly disjoint validation?** WebShop does not enforce goal disjointness (Appendix G). Next experiment: separate training, validation, and final goals and increase paired seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative provides detailed controls and consistent directional findings, but many tables appear only as captions, without their cells.

Material missing information includes complete numerical tables and intervals, manifest contents and exact training hyperparameters, Settlement runtime and memory costs, and detailed validation-task selection. Repository availability is reported by the authors; its contents were not inspected.
