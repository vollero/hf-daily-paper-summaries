# ProgressCompass: Embodied Progress Reward Models Are Lost Without the Right Context

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36684) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36684)


## One-sentence takeaway
On ContextProgress-Bench, ProgressCompass uses VLM-generated subtask context and verification to reduce a frozen RoboMeter-4B’s progress MAE from 25.4 to 9.3, although the oracle-context diagnosis also changes clip boundaries and inference settings (§5.1; Appendix C).

## Problem and motivation
Long manipulation tasks can contain visually similar moments with different progress: a relevant state has disappeared, earlier ordered steps may be complete, or an action may have repeated. The authors argue that existing progress benchmarks emphasize tasks readable from current observations and insufficiently test these context dependencies (§§1–2).

Raw history may contain the necessary evidence without making the relevant fact explicit. The paper studies whether PRMs need better context extraction rather than better local progress scoring.

## Approach and contributions
- **Problem formulation and benchmark:** ContextProgress-Bench isolates State Recall, Sequence Tracking, and Recurrence Disambiguation using manually selected tasks and annotated subtask contexts from RMBench, RoboDojo, and LIBERO-Mem (§2).
- **Paired diagnosis:** Five frozen PRMs score complete tasks versus annotated subtask clips with contextual instructions. Oracle and self-chained positioning distinguish local scoring ability from accumulated endpoint errors (§3; Appendix C).
- **ProgressCompass:** An Orienter produces the next subtask and expected transition; a frozen PRM scores its clip; a separate Verifier checks completion; a text-only Navigator maintains the plan and verified memory. Experiments use Qwen3.5 models with RoboMeter-4B, without training (§4; Appendix B).
- **Conditional theoretical analysis:** The paper derives an error floor for current-frame estimators and bounds ProgressCompass error through step-position and local-scoring errors. Superiority requires those errors to be sufficiently small; it is not guaranteed (§4; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark targets context-dependent manipulation | 24 tasks, five sampled episodes per task, 120 videos, 71,708 frames, and 552 annotated subtask intervals. | §§1, 2.3; Fig. 2 |
| Annotated context substantially improves five PRMs | On a 0–100 progress scale, MAE ranges fall from 15.6–31.0 without context to 3.4–6.7 with oracle context; reported relative reductions are 77–82%, with improvement on 93.3% of paired intervals. | §1; §3; Table 1 description |
| Autonomous context improves the frozen backbone | RoboMeter-4B MAE falls from 25.4 to 9.3; Spearman correlation with time rises from 0.53 to 0.93. Authors report closing 78% of the oracle-context gap. | §5.1; Fig. 5 |
| Early-stop and extra-step cases improve | Across a constructed set of 15 held-out negatives, Early-stop mean deviation is 7.3 for ProgressCompass versus 32.7 for RoboMeter and 50.3 for TOPReward. Extra-steps deviation is 15.8 versus 24.0–40.2 for comparators. | §5.2; Fig. 7 |
| Unrelated instructions are rejected | ProgressCompass stays at zero throughout Mismatch cases. VLAC also has zero deviation, but the authors report a terminal value of −63.8 with the correct instruction, complicating interpretation. | §5.2 |
| Expected transitions aid completion verification | Subtask-end error rises from 8.6 to 17.2 when transitions are withheld from the Verifier, and to 18.8 when also removed from Orienter output; progress MAE rises from 9.3 to 17.5 and 18.3. | §5.3; Fig. 8 |
| Scheduling reduces reported processing time | Running up to five episodes concurrently reduces time per episode from 114.7 to 39.5 seconds; MAE and correlation remain unchanged within paired bootstrap intervals. | §5.4 |

These results support improved progress annotation on the constructed benchmark and a useful role for explicit transition descriptions. They do not establish better robot control or reinforcement-learning outcomes. Spearman correlation measures ordering against time, rather than agreement with ground-truth progress values (§3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundaries:** The theoretical floor applies to current-frame estimators. Failure with sampled history is empirical; the ProgressCompass superiority condition depends on plan-position and local-scoring errors (§4; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—diagnosis is not strictly context-only:** Although the main text describes paired runs as differing only in context, Appendix C changes whole-episode inputs to subtask clips and changes sampling rates, frame limits, or settings for several models. Ground-truth boundaries and oracle positioning also supply information beyond instruction wording.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** The wrapper is demonstrated with one PRM backbone and one VLM family on manually selected manipulation tasks. Broader model compatibility remains proposed rather than demonstrated (§§2.3, 4–5).
- **Analyst inference—small robustness evaluation:** The negative evaluation contains 15 examples in total; the supplied text does not give counts per negative type (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution remains incomplete:** Expected-transition ablations are provided, but separate removal tests for the Navigator, Orienter, and entire Verifier are not reported in the supplied text (§5.3).

## Open research questions
- **How much of the oracle gain comes from contextual wording versus segmentation and anchoring?** Appendix C changes multiple inputs. A next experiment could hold sampling and inference settings fixed while independently varying instructions, boundaries, and endpoint anchors.
- **Does the wrapper transfer across PRMs and VLMs?** Only one wrapper configuration is evaluated. Test the same loop with the other diagnosed PRMs and alternative Orienter/Verifier models.
- **Does improved annotation improve agent behavior?** Evaluation measures progress curves. Compare policy learning or runtime recovery using baseline versus ProgressCompass rewards or monitoring signals.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main architecture and aggregate results, but omits Table 1’s numerical cells and most figure values. Material missing information includes annotation agreement and detailed labeling procedures, per-task uncertainty, negative-case allocation, R²VLM implementation details, hardware and cost measurements, and confirmation of code/data release.
