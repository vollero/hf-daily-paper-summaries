# DEPICT: Scoring Text-to-Image Alignment by Answer Agreement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03617) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03617)


## One-sentence takeaway
DEPICT combines caption–image answer agreement with holistic VQA scoring, improving negation handling and human-rating correlation in the reported evaluations, although several gains are statistically uncertain (§§5.2–5.4).

## Problem and motivation
Text-to-image evaluators must detect missing objects, incorrect attributes, counts, relations, and negations. The authors identify three gaps: fine-tuned evaluators depend on their training backbone and distribution; holistic scoring can overlook details; and decomposed metrics using a fixed “yes” reference penalize faithful images when a question should be answered “no” (§§1–2).

The paper focuses on training-free evaluation of image–caption alignment. Caption evaluation, hallucination detection, and data curation motivate the work but are not separately evaluated applications (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Two-channel agreement:** Generate yes/no questions directly from the caption, then answer each using the image and, separately, the caption alone. Score agreement as \(p_v(\mathrm{yes})p_t(\mathrm{yes})+p_v(\mathrm{no})p_t(\mathrm{no})\), rewarding either matching polarity. Probabilities retain full-vocabulary mass rather than being renormalized (§3, Eq. 3).
- **Commitment weighting and holistic merge:** Weight questions by \(|p_t(\mathrm{yes})-p_t(\mathrm{no})|\), aggregate their agreement, and average this with a whole-caption VQAScore term. The merge weight is fixed at 0.5 without benchmark tuning (§§3–4, Eqs. 4–7).
- **Diagnosis and comparative study:** Test the fixed-yes failure, pipeline components, and backbone dependence across five benchmarks and eleven backbones, using paired bootstrap confidence intervals (§§4–5). The authors claim novelty for the combined metric and breadth of the study; the supplied text cannot independently establish priority.

These results support agreement scoring for the tested negation task and complementary benefits from merging. They do not establish universal superiority or gains in downstream generator training.

The authors report matching or beating baselines in **54/55** settings, with **43 wins** and **23 significant wins** (§5.4). Tables 7–8 contain several negative point estimates, so “matches” appears to include statistically indistinguishable losses; literal point-estimate dominance is not supported.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Shared backbone priors can cause both channels to agree on the same hallucination. A qualitative failure also shows visual and holistic scoring sharing a perceptual error (§6, Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** Decomposition brings little benefit on short captions distinguished by compositional word order. Answering with reasoning helps Winoground but can create score ties elsewhere (Appendix C.4).
- **Evidence-based qualification:** Commitment weighting has small, uneven effects and slightly hurts some backbones; it is not uniformly beneficial (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** NegBench reference reliability is encouraging but incomplete: the parser covers only part of the negated-caption population and may misattribute questions (Appendix D.2).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Many tables and prompt figures appear only as captions in the supplied retrieval. Exact intervals, several ablation values, and full prompt templates are unavailable. Code is promised rather than demonstrably released.

## Open research questions
1. **Can independent channel backbones reduce shared hallucinations?** The authors identify this risk but do not report testing the remedy. Compare same-backbone and mixed-backbone channels on annotated hallucination cases, including hosting cost (§6).
2. **Does agreement generalize beyond templated negation?** Reference validation centers on parsable NegBench captions. Test human-verified questions from free-form captions with varied negation scope and ambiguous entailment (Appendix D.2).
3. **Can reasoning improve compositional discrimination without losing graded scores?** Existing reasoning ablations show a task-dependent tradeoff. Compare reasoning followed by calibrated probability scoring against the default on full correlation benchmarks and Winoground (Appendix C.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose and Tables 7–8 clearly describe the method and major outcomes, but important evidence is missing: full Tables 1–3 and most appendix tables, numerical confidence intervals, complete prompt figures, some dataset counts and decoding details, and a code release link. Novelty priority and broader application validity remain unverified.
