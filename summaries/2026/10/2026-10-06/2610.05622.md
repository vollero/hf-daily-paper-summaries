# UndoBench: Separating Task Competence from Recovery Capability in Tool-Using AI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05622) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05622)


## One-sentence takeaway
UndoBench’s paired evaluations show that nominal tool-use success can substantially exceed safe fault recovery, with recovery depending on fault timing, endpoint deduplication, and accessible repair mechanisms (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
An external operation can commit while its acknowledgment is lost, leaving an agent unsure whether retrying will duplicate a payment, deployment, or other mutation. The authors argue that nominal task-completion benchmarks obscure this risk by conflating planning competence with recovery capability and overlooking external effect histories (§1).

The evaluation gap is therefore conditional: when an agent can complete a workflow without faults, can it still complete that workflow safely after an execution fault?

## Approach and contributions
- **Recovery benchmark:** UndoBench comprises 36 base workflows and 36 fault scenarios across eight enterprise domains. Its taxonomy distinguishes five mutation-lifecycle boundaries; experiments cover three (§§1–3).
- **Paired competence conditioning:** Identical-seed control and faulted runs define Conditional Recovery Success Rate (CRSR), measuring faulted success among pairs whose nominal control succeeds (§§2.1, 2.4).
- **Dual evaluation oracles:** Final-state checks assess task invariants, while wire-level histories detect duplicate and missing committed effects (§2.4; Appendix K).
- **Empirical mechanism characterization:** A frozen lost-acknowledgment study is supplemented by hosted-model, fault-boundary, and benchmark-wide server-idempotency interventions. These characterize recovery mechanisms rather than introduce a new trained agent (§§3–6).

These reported results support a competence–recovery separation and mechanism dependence within the evaluated environments. They do not establish benchmark-wide statistical superiority for standard B₂, general model-size rankings, or production reliability. CRSR and conditional exactly-once rate coincide here because successful recovery already requires no duplicate or missing effects (§2.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** The independent TEST support is small despite repeated seeds; environments simulate enterprise APIs; each trajectory receives one injected fault; partial-mutation evaluation covers only four workflows. Checkpoint-window, compensation, cascading, and concurrent failures remain untested. Hosted generation is not guaranteed reproducible, retry-budget sensitivity is untested, and B₆ is post-hoc (Limitations).

B₅’s restricted result does not evaluate unrestricted EvoUndo. EOR was reconstructed from frozen logs after a verdict-field omission. CRSR is undefined when nominal competence is zero, requiring inspection alongside unconditional metrics (Limitations; Appendix C).

**Analyst inference:** The strong B₂-K result depends on a modified server contract and an identity assumption that identical arguments within a task denote the same logical mutation. It does not establish safety for intentionally repeated identical operations or deduplication across server restarts (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Can inspection plus permitted repair recover partial mutations broadly?** B₆ recovered only one qualified workflow. Next experiment: compare probes alone against probes with registered repair actions across the same workflows (§5).
- **Does deduplication remain effective under concurrency and restarts?** Evaluation uses episode-local caches and single faults. Next experiment: inject server restarts and concurrent requests while distinguishing retries from intentional repeated operations (Appendix A; Limitations).
- **How sensitive are outcomes to retry policy?** Only a fixed retry budget was tested. Next experiment: sweep retry counts and backoff schedules separately by fault boundary (Limitations).
- **Do findings generalize to additional workflows?** Repeated seeds do not expand independent task support. Next experiment: freeze a larger held-out suite and repeat task-clustered comparisons (Limitations).
