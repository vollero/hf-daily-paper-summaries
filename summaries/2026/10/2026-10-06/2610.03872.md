# Training Numerical Intelligence via Auto-Diagnosis and Skill Discovery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03872) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03872)


## One-sentence takeaway
ADSD uses verified numerical diagnoses to retrieve and implement reusable solver skills, improving agent-generated solvers across four evaluated domains without updating model weights, although the experiments do not isolate diagnosis from literature access.

## Problem and motivation
Scientific code can execute successfully while its underlying numerical algorithm remains inaccurate, slow, or unreliable. The authors identify a gap between observing solver failures, explaining their numerical causes, and selecting methods whose assumptions match those causes (§1).

They propose separating runtime observations, testable diagnostic knowledge, and executable solver knowledge so that improvement follows a structured process rather than repeated code revision.

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnosis-first pipeline:** Freeze a baseline solver, collect public-case traces, construct literature-grounded diagnostic probes, and verify symptom–condition hypotheses on a separate public verification pool (Algorithm 1, §3.1).
- **Diagnosis-guided skill discovery:** Retrieve methods against verified hypotheses, audit their theoretical assumptions and compatibility, implement and empirically check candidates, then package implementations, instructions, and evidence dossiers as frozen skills (§3.2).
- **Solver improvement without weight updates:** A fresh agent receives the original solver and skill packets under the same final-generation model, reasoning budget, runtime constraint, and prompt. Evaluation spans power flow, AC-OPF, stiff ODEs, and heterogeneous diffusion PDEs (§§1, 3.2–4).

The contribution is the coordinated diagnosis-to-skill workflow; the illustrated numerical methods themselves come from existing literature (Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

The evidence supports improved measured solver quality across the tested models and settings. It does not establish downstream engineering benefits experimentally, causal correctness of every diagnosis, or universal efficiency improvement: GPT’s GOC-500 runtime increases under unseen N1 and full-grid topologies despite lower residuals (Table 6). Runtime units are not consistently labeled in the main tables.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** IEEE and GOC use incompatible formulations and schemas, requiring separate solvers; PDE evaluation tests new realizations within existing configuration families (§§4.1, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The main ablation removes both diagnosis and discovered skills. It supports the combined pipeline but does not isolate diagnosis from retrieval, implementation auditing, or extra development effort (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—verification strength:** Diagnostic detectors establish empirical associations. The general “over 90%” verification rule lacks a fully specified statistical interpretation; Appendix B.1 also describes cutoff exploration involving the verification pool (§3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and reproducibility:** Final solver-generation budgets are matched, but total ADSD acquisition cost, repeated independent agent runs, and statistical uncertainty across generated solvers are not reported. AC-OPF objective errors exclude cases outside the common passing subset (§§3.2, 4.5; Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—practical significance:** Smaller power-flow residuals are demonstrated, while improved operational decisions and dynamic initialization remain argued benefits rather than measured outcomes (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **How much does diagnosis contribute beyond literature access?** The combined ablation leaves this unresolved. Compare diagnosis-guided retrieval with unguided retrieval under matched total acquisition budgets.
- **How reliable are diagnostic hypotheses under distribution shifts?** Solver transfer is measured, but diagnostic detector transfer is not. Freeze detectors and test their associations on unseen topologies and operating regimes.
- **Are gains reproducible and cost-effective across agent runs?** Acquisition costs and between-run variability are unavailable. Repeat the full pipeline independently and report solver gains alongside total inference and execution costs.
- **Do tighter residuals improve engineering decisions?** Appendix C supplies motivation rather than downstream experiments. Compare contingency assessments or dynamic initializations using baseline and ADSD solutions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives clear procedures, benchmark settings, quantitative tables, and technical case studies, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain less established.

Material missing information includes release links, complete prompts and skill packets, total pipeline costs, hardware and consistent runtime units, independent generation repetitions, detailed AC-OPF passing criteria, and diagnosis-versus-retrieval ablations.
