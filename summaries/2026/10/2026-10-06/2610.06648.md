# Representation-Space MMD for Diffusion Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06648) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06648)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that post-training diffusion language models with token-level MMD in frozen DLM features improves generation quality at limited sampling budgets and increases decoding parallelism in evaluated math and code settings (§§4.1–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Token-wise denoising objectives do not necessarily produce good text with few sampling steps. Existing acceleration methods often require teacher trajectories or jointly trained auxiliary models; the authors investigate whether generated and reference samples alone can provide a useful distribution-matching signal. Their proposed comparison space addresses the difficulty of measuring meaningful text similarity with simple distances (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Contextual token-feature MMD:** Compare distributions of individual token representations from a frozen pretrained DLM using an RBF kernel. Within-distribution comparisons exclude all same-sequence pairs to account for dependent token features. Conditional training needs only one reference per condition because the reference–reference term does not affect optimization (§3.1).
- **Discrete realizations:** MDLM-MMD and DMax-MMD optimize sampled parallel token predictions with grouped REINFORCE and a leave-one-out baseline. DMax-MMD covers both masked prediction and refinement of the model’s own predictions; original inference procedures remain applicable (§3.2).
- **Continuous realization:** ELF-MMD directly differentiates through generated latents and the frozen extractor. Bootstrap self-conditioning supports iterative refinement; optional iterative refinement distillation (IRD) subsequently compresses teacher refinement trajectories (§3.3; App. B.1).

The contribution is this DLM post-training formulation and its realizations, rather than MMD, feature matching, or REINFORCE themselves (§1; App. A). Continuous training also includes decoder cross-entropy fine-tuning (App. B.2).

## Evidence and main results

All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lower discrete-generation perplexity | On OWT, MDLM-MMD has approximately **17–21% lower generative perplexity than IDLM** at reference entropy ≈5.43, interpolating temperature curves at **8, 16, and 32 steps**. Evaluation uses 1,000 generated samples and GPT-2 Large scoring. | §4.1; Fig. 2; Table 2 referenced, cells absent |
| IRD improves continuous few-step generation | At **4 steps** on OWT, ELF-MMD+IRD reduces generative perplexity versus ELF-MMD by approximately **30 points with T5 latents** and **10 points with GPT-2 latents**, with entropy closer to the reference. | §4.1; Fig. 2; Table 3 referenced, cells absent |
| Better continuous math accuracy | TinyGSM-trained ELF-B on GSM8K: ELF-MMD→ELF-MMD+IRD accuracy rises **14.2%→20.8% at 4 steps** and **27.5%→32.5% at 8 steps**; ELF-PD achieves **15.9% and 23.5%**, respectively. | §4.2; Fig. 3; Table 4 referenced |
| Gains persist at larger sampling budget | On the same GSM8K setting at **64 steps**, ELF-MMD+IRD reaches **36.3%**, versus **35.2% for ELF-MMD** and **31.6% for ELF**. | §4.2; Table 4 referenced |
| Higher large-model decoding parallelism | **16B DMax-Math-MMD**, threshold **0.85**, increases tokens per forward by **10.3–16.5%** across four math benchmarks versus reported DMax-Math operating points, with similar or higher accuracy. | §4.3; Table 1 caption; App. D |
| Better code pass@1 with higher parallelism | **16B DMax-Coder-MMD**, threshold **0.9**, improves pass@1 by **2.4 percentage points on HumanEval-Instruct** and **3.8 points on MBPP-Instruct** versus reported DMax-Coder baselines; TPF also increases. | §4.3; Table 1 caption |
| Short large-model post-training runs | **400 training steps on eight H100 GPUs** take approximately **13 minutes for math** and **19 minutes for code**, excluding initialization and evaluation. | §4.3 |

DLM features also outperform tested input/external representations on GSM8K. | §4.4; Apps. C.1–C.2; Figs. 4–6 |

These results support improvements within the evaluated models and settings. They do not establish universal superiority or wall-clock inference speedups: TPF measures parallelism. ## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Performance depends on representation choice and RBF bandwidth; experiments use one layer on clean inputs (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Matching these token-feature distributions is not proven to uniquely identify the underlying sequence distribution (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Continuous gains cannot be attributed exclusively to MMD because training also includes decoder fine-tuning, and the strongest reported variant adds trajectory-supervised IRD (App. B).
- **Analyst inference:** Large-model comparisons use selected decoding thresholds and originally published baseline values. App. D reports reproduced baseline curves and five-run MMD uncertainty, but their numerical values are unavailable here.
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence covers web text, math, and code; broader language-task generalization remains untested in the supplied account (§4).

## Open research questions

- **Would richer representations improve matching?** Single-layer clean features leave complementary information unexplored (§5). Compare layer ensembles and mixed noise-level features at equal training compute.
- **How much continuous improvement comes from MMD alone?** Decoder fine-tuning accompanies MMD (App. B.2). Run a factorial comparison of MMD, decoder fine-tuning, and IRD.
- **Does higher TPF reduce actual latency?** The paper reports parallelism rather than inference timing (§4.3). Measure latency and throughput at matched accuracy on the same hardware.
- **Does the objective transfer beyond evaluated tasks?** Current evaluation concentrates on OWT, math, and code (§4). Test additional conditional language tasks with matched sampling budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline claims are explicit, but numerical table bodies, figure values/error bars, and hyperparameter table contents are missing from the supplied text. Consequently, absolute DMax scores, detailed uncertainty, exact bandwidths and extraction layers, and full replication settings cannot be assessed.
