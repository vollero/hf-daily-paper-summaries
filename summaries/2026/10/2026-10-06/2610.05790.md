# FairRSFM: A Biome-Aware Benchmark and Debiasing Framework for Remote Sensing Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05790) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05790)


## One-sentence takeaway
FairRSFM exposes biome-dependent performance gaps in frozen remote sensing foundation models and shows that biome-aware mitigation can improve worst-group performance, with benefits that vary by model and task (Tables 4–8).

## Problem and motivation
Aggregate classification and segmentation scores can conceal weak performance in particular ecological regions. The authors identify a gap in existing remote sensing evaluation: task-level benchmarks do not routinely measure robustness across biome-derived groups, despite ecological variation in imagery and uneven regional coverage (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and metadata pipeline:** Assign georeferenced patches to 14 terrestrial biomes using centroid-to-ecoregion lookup, then consolidate them into six fixed ecological macro-groups. Apply this to four classification and segmentation datasets (§§3.1–3.3; Table S1).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Compare Prithvi-EO-2.0, SatMAE, and DOFA with frozen encoders, reporting task performance, worst-group scores, disparity, and calibration across three seeds. Heads and decoders remain model-specific (§§3.4, 5; Table 2).
- **Representation mitigation:** BOLP computes training-group feature centroids, recenters embeddings, and projects away dominant inter-group mean directions using SVD. It adds no trainable parameters beyond the downstream head (§4.2).
- **Complementary mitigation comparison:** DBR adapts inverse-frequency loss weights using validation feedback; the study compares it and BOLP against ERM and established GroupDRO. The contribution is their biome-aware evaluation, rather than a new GroupDRO method (§§4.1–4.3).

## Evidence and main results
All performance values below are percentages, reported as mean ± standard deviation across three seeds on test splits with frozen encoders.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Aggregate classification hides weaker ecological performance | Prithvi ERM, m-EuroSAT: overall macro-F1 **90.98 ± 2.36**, mean per-seed worst-group macro-F1 **83.72 ± 0.87**. | Table 4 |
| Dense prediction also shows biome gaps | Prithvi ERM, m-SA-Crop-Type: overall mIoU **27.30 ± 0.15**; Xeric **18.47 ± 0.16**, versus Transitional **28.55 ± 0.19**. | Tables 4, 7 |
| BOLP improves one classification setting | Prithvi, m-BigEarthNet, ERM → BOLP: overall F1@opt **60.09 ± 0.99 → 62.75 ± 0.16**; worst-group **46.12 ± 1.51 → 50.27 ± 0.63**; NFR **31.14 ± 1.99 → 20.92 ± 0.39**. | Table 4 |
| Stronger worst-group scores can cost aggregate performance | SatMAE, m-EuroSAT, ERM → GroupDRO: worst-group macro-F1 **74.45 ± 0.51 → 89.71 ± 1.51**, while overall **93.89 ± 0.13 → 91.26 ± 1.03**. | Table 5 |
| BOLP harms a segmentation setting; GroupDRO helps its worst group | Prithvi, MMEarth20K: ERM overall/worst-group mIoU **47.99 ± 0.32 / 33.75 ± 0.58**; BOLP **43.31 ± 0.85 / 30.64 ± 1.20**; GroupDRO worst-group **37.16 ± 0.26**. | Table 4; Table S4 |

These results support ecological subgroup auditing and task-dependent mitigation within the tested backbones. They do not establish a universally superior method, causal attribution of disparities to biome alone, or robustness under end-to-end fine-tuning. Worst-group scores are minima computed separately per seed; they should not be reconstructed by taking the minimum of reported group means (§3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Centroid labels can be noisy near ecological boundaries; incomplete group coverage and small test groups increase uncertainty. Segmentation disparity definitions are less standardized, and biome grouping does not capture all geographic, sensor, temporal, or resolution shifts (§7). The supplementary assignment check reports high centroid–majority agreement, but does not resolve every boundary case (§S6.2).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers only three frozen backbones and four datasets. Model-specific heads make absolute cross-model comparisons descriptive (§§5, 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Class composition may contribute to group differences: metrics average over classes present within each group, and MMEarth20K sampling is class-unconditioned. The supplied experiments do not isolate this contribution (§§3.4, S4).
- **Analyst observation:** MMEarth20K group counts in Table 3 do not reconcile with allocation totals in Table S2. Some summary and biome-wise results also differ; reported per-seed aggregation may explain some differences, but the text does not resolve all of them (Tables 4–8).
- **Analyst observation:** Sensitivity studies are concentrated on Prithvi; the supplied ablations do not separately isolate BOLP recentering from projection (§S5).

## Open research questions
- **Which BOLP component drives gains or losses?** Existing ablations vary projection rank without isolating recentering. Compare recentering-only, projection-only, and combined transforms using identical heads (§§4.2, S5.2).
- **How stable are disparities under alternative ecological groupings?** Sparse groups and the fixed macro-group taxonomy constrain interpretation. Repeat evaluation with larger minority test sets, raw-biome groups, and alternative pooling schemes (§7).
- **Do frozen-backbone findings persist with adaptation?** Backbone updates are excluded here. Compare probing, parameter-efficient adaptation, and full fine-tuning under the same group-aware evaluation (§§5, 7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central findings and includes supplementary methods and sensitivity results. Material gaps include reconciliation of inconsistent counts/results, exact GroupDRO update settings, fully specified decoder architectures, independent recentering/projection ablations, and statistical uncertainty beyond seed variability. Code and dataset availability is author-reported; the linked assets were not inspected.
