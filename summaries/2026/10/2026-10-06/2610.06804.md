# Sharpen Without Search: On-Policy Distillation of Sequence-Level Power Distribution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06804) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06804)


## One-sentence takeaway
On-policy power distillation trains language models without reference answers to approximate sequence-level sharpening, recovering 94% of a sixteen-candidate sampler’s MATH500 accuracy gain with one generation in the authors’ matched evaluation (§5.1; Table 9).

## Problem and motivation
A correct answer can be individually likely while incorrect answers collectively dominate sampling. Raising complete-answer probabilities to a power shifts mass toward likely sequences, but approximate power sampling requires multiple candidates for every query. Lowering token temperature is a different operation because it lacks information about future continuations (§§1–2).

The gap is specifically **on-policy training toward the sequence-level power distribution**. The authors acknowledge that moving power sampling into training already exists; their distinction is using student-generated candidates and a Rényi-divergence objective rather than supervised learning on stored sampler outputs (§6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared sampling and training computation:** The student generates candidates inside sequential Monte Carlo (SMC); a frozen teacher scores token blocks. Those scores supply separate resampling weights and weighted maximum-likelihood training weights, without reference answers, verifiers, or reward models (§§3.1–3.5).
- **Sequence-level objective with an optional anchor:** A Rényi-divergence gradient yields importance-weighted likelihood updates. A token-level forward-KL anchor, controlled by λ, limits excessive concentration but shifts the objective away from pure sequence-level sharpening (§3.4).
- **Practical implementation:** Training uses sixteen candidates, temperature \(1/\alpha\), blockwise scoring, corrections for duplicated training weights, and an early-stopping weight correction (§3.5; Appendices P–Q).
- **Mechanism diagnostics:** The authors measure ESS-implied divergence and fit an “absorbed exponent” between initialization and trained-model token probabilities. The latter reveals context-dependent sharpening where the estimator has sufficient resolution (§4; §5.3).

These results support useful single-generation accuracy gains and partial amortization of sampling. They do not establish exact matching of the power distribution, general superiority over verified-reward training, or measured wall-clock savings. ESS and token-slope diagnostics provide indirect evidence of distributional movement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated comparison limits:** GRPO is one implementation at one small budget; its pass@1 margins are individually inconclusive. AIME contains only sixty problems (§5.2; Appendix B.10).
- **Authors’ stated collapse risk:** Removing the anchor harms an already sharp instruction model. The λ-selection heuristic is supported by a limited set of models (§5.3; Appendix B.8).
- **Authors’ stated diagnostic limits:** Absorbed-exponent fits become noisy near deterministic distributions; ESS-implied divergences cannot be compared across particle counts (§4; Appendix M).
- **Authors’ stated missing ablation:** The self-versus-larger-teacher comparison fixes λ=1; other λ values and larger teacher–student gaps remain untested (Appendix B.13).
- **Analyst inference:** The exact Rényi-gradient derivation does not by itself validate the full practical approximation, which includes finite particles, block-reset training weights, top-token truncation, and stopping corrections. Small-model implementation checks offer supporting evidence, but do not establish exactness at benchmark scale (§3.4; Appendices J, O–Q).
- **Analyst inference:** Higher best-of-4 demonstrates improved success within four attempts, but cannot establish that successful answers were absent from the initialization’s full distribution (Appendices B.10–B.11).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is concentrated in mathematics and one coding benchmark; broader language capabilities are unassessed (§5).

## Open research questions
1. **How does OPPD compare with GRPO at larger budgets?** The current comparison covers one small-budget implementation. Run multiple seeds and training-budget sweeps, matching both generated-token counts and measured compute.
2. **Which practical corrections materially change the target distribution?** The theory and implementation differ in several approximation choices. Ablate block length, truncation, training-weight resets, and stopping correction while measuring accuracy and exact distribution error on enumerable models.
3. **Can λ be selected reliably across unfamiliar models?** The temperature-gain heuristic has limited validation. Test its predictions on additional checkpoints against a prespecified λ sweep, measuring pass@1 and diversity.
4. **Does mathematics-only sharpening preserve broader capabilities?** HumanEval provides limited transfer evidence. Evaluate additional coding and non-mathematical language tasks before and after training.
