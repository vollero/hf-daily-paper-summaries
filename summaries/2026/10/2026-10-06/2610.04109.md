# SEER: Self-Evolving Event Reasoning and Retrieval for Time Series Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04109) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04109)


## One-sentence takeaway
SEER improves event-driven forecasting by using past forecast errors to refine retrieval memories and textual domain rules, with positive average gains across four LLM backbones but uneven gains on individual tasks (Tables 4 and 6).

## Problem and motivation
Historical numerical observations can miss external drivers such as supply disruptions, policy changes, and market shocks. The authors identify three weaknesses in static event retrieval: noisy evidence, semantic relevance without causal reasoning, and failure to use forecast errors to improve subsequent context (§1).

SEER addresses these weaknesses by learning the context supplied to a frozen LLM rather than updating its parameters (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Error-driven context learning:** During training, the LLM compares completed forecasts with observed outcomes and produces textual updates for future forecasting (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Decoupled feedback:** Retrieval memory guides additional searches; selection memory filters events; a separate knowledge base stores generalized forecasting rules described by the authors as causal knowledge (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Chronological backtesting:** Training updates require outcomes observable within the training window. Memories and knowledge are frozen during testing, while new events are retrieved subject to the forecast cutoff using LEAF filtering (§3.3).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The framework is tested on five time-series domains and one event-resolution domain, with component ablations across four LLM backbones (§4).

The proposed novelty is the combination of these feedback mechanisms for forecasting; the supplied evidence does not independently establish that the learned rules identify causal mechanisms.

These results support improved average forecasting performance in the reported backtests. They do not establish universal gains across tasks or backbones. Table 5 contains broadly overlapping individual-method intervals; Table 6 more directly supports positive **average** improvements. Judge preferences and case studies support perceived reasoning quality, rather than validated causal identification.

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no dedicated limitations section. They explicitly acknowledge stock predictability as an assumption and impose memory-capacity limits (§4.1; §3.3).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- **Temporal safeguards remain partly unverified:** Retrieval filtering and frozen memories address stated leakage channels, but the supplied text gives no quantitative audit results or assessment of information already encoded in backbone models (§3.3).
- **Retrieval effort is confounded with memory guidance:** SEER adds a guided search round beyond Event RAG’s initial searches. No equal-search-budget control or separate expansion-versus-filtering ablation is reported (§4.1; §4.2).
- **Evaluation scope is bounded:** Targets comprise selected component products, US cities and equities, and a restricted event-question subset; broader geographic or domain transfer is not demonstrated (Table 1; Appendix A).
- **Reasoning assessment has limited validation:** A single LLM judge evaluates plausibility, and Appendix A.1 ambiguously describes its evidence as both forecast-horizon events and events retrieved by Event RAG. Human agreement is not reported.
- **Reproducibility is incomplete in this retrieval:** Prompt bodies, full learned rules, and plotted Figure 3 values are unavailable. Appendix A also gives differing server-memory and retail-kit descriptions, leaving exact product mapping unclear.

## Open research questions
- **How much benefit comes from learned retrieval guidance rather than extra searches?** Search budgets differ (§4.1). Compare SEER with Event RAG given the same search rounds and token budget.
- **When do learned rules help or hurt?** Table 4 contains regressions after adding knowledge. Test rule-level removal and transfer to held-out entities or later regimes.
- **How reliable are historical information boundaries?** Filtering is described without quantitative audit results (§3.3). Repeat backtests using archived, publication-timestamped evidence and audit a sampled set.
- **Does selective prediction improve accuracy at matched coverage?** Selection and abstention contribute to the reported advantage (§4.6). Compare accuracy–coverage curves under identical selection budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied method description and tables support the main average-performance conclusion, but causal and broader-generalization claims are less firmly established.

Material missing information includes exact prompt and figure contents, aggregate Polymarket and selective-stock scores, stock Neutral-label thresholds, search-provider details, cost and latency, repeated-run variability, quantitative leakage audits, and exact memory-product mapping.
