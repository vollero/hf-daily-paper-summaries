# Code2Games: Enabling Coding Agents for Gaming World Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05033) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05033)


## One-sentence takeaway
Code2Games grounds gameplay in a structured Blender scene and uses execution feedback to reconstruct playable Unreal Engine 5 projects, with reported improvements over coding-agent baselines on ten game prompts (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Generating a game requires consistency between scene geometry, object identities, objectives, interactions, and executable rules. The authors argue that independently generated assets and scripts can yield inaccessible objectives, unsuitable placements, or incorrect actor bindings even when individual components appear reasonable. Their identified gap is maintaining this correspondence from natural-language intent through world generation to engine execution (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Coordinated generation:** Four specialized agents perform scene analysis, gameplay planning, constrained world realization, and engine customization, starting from an intent and its paired Code2Worlds Blender scene (§3).
- **Shared representation and persistent identity:** Regions encode semantics, boundaries, accessibility, and affordances; gameplay specifications encode objectives, actors, elements, and interactions. Persistent identifiers connect Blender objects or annotations to Unreal actors or trigger volumes and executable logic (§3.1).
- **Constrained placement and targeted repair:** Geometric checks filter candidate locations, a vision-language model selects semantically suitable placements, and compilation, runtime, and gameplay feedback guide repairs to affected components (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Evaluation asset:** GameCode4D introduces ten frozen prompts and a protocol covering visual quality, interactive fidelity, artifact quality, and playable-game quality. Public release availability is not established (§4.1).

Scores use two approximately 50-second routes per game, averaged within each game and then across ten games. LLM-judged metrics use Qwen3.8-Max at temperature 0; Human aggregates video ratings from 15 raters (Appendix E).

These author-reported results support benefits under the stated benchmark and ablations. They do not establish comprehensive gameplay correctness, statistical significance, or generalization to unseen scenarios. The authors interpret the larger content and presentation gains as evidence for scene-grounded gameplay benefits; the supplied results do not independently isolate that explanation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Blender-to-Unreal translation can change materials, procedural shaders, lighting, transparency, and atmosphere, leaving a visual fidelity gap (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Ten games and short predefined routes provide limited coverage of long sessions, alternative actions, and failure states (§4.1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Human evaluation rates videos rather than direct play, limiting conclusions about usability and player experience (Appendix E).
- **Reporting gaps:** Tables 1–4 lack their actual cells; uncertainty estimates and repeated-generation results are unavailable. Baseline access to paired worlds and generation-resource parity are unclear.
- **Internal ambiguities:** Appendix G includes cinematic route staging explicitly distinguished from playable collision planning. Its relationship to evaluated executable games is unexplained. Appendix C’s Qwen-family implementation description is also not reconciled with all reported backbone configurations.

## Open research questions
- **How sensitive is performance to base-world quality?** Inputs are paired Code2Worlds scenes, without a reported scene-quality sensitivity study. Next experiment: hold intents fixed while varying scene completeness, accessibility, and geometric defects.
- **Do repairs generalize beyond tested routes?** Validation uses objective assertions and scripted traces (§3.3). Next experiment: evaluate repaired projects with held-out action sequences and extended play sessions.
- **What does persistent correspondence contribute independently?** Reported ablations remove broader components rather than identity tracking alone (§4.3). Next experiment: compare persistent identifiers with inferred bindings under identical repair budgets.
- **Can visual translation fidelity improve without harming gameplay?** Appendix A identifies unresolved rendering discrepancies. Next experiment: compare material-translation and calibration variants using paired scene renders and gameplay regression checks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and evaluation protocol are described clearly, but quantitative verification is limited by missing table contents.

Material missing information includes exact quantitative benchmark prompts and their mapping to the 20 qualitative prompts, per-game results, uncertainty estimates, generation costs and budgets, detailed baseline conditioning, the ablation backbone, human-rating agreement, and code/benchmark release availability.
