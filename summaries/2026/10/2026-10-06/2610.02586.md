# Labels Override Definitions in Jev-Style Typed Decision Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02586) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02586)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the tested open typed decision models, including option labels in the encoder input causes label-dependent decisions, while removing them restores label invariance but can reduce accuracy when class names are informative (§5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Typed decision interfaces let callers specify option labels and definitions, implying that definitions determine the decision rule. The authors test whether models instead follow familiar labels, potentially overriding application-specific policies. They identify two evaluation gaps: missing label-invariance checks and ordinary language-model scoring baselines, which obscure both the failure and its mechanism (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Channel ablations:** Independently vary labels, definitions, and input states across public classification tasks (§§4.1–4.2).
- **PolicyBench:** Introduce synthetic support-ticket routing with rules supplied only through definitions and attribute-derived ground truth. The primary XF control gives both branches comparably substantive definitions (§4.3).
- **Mechanism intervention:** Remove labels from three laya checkpoints and insert them into von, keeping weights, heads, and calibration fixed (§5.5).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Test language-model scoring and generative readouts, question negation, and confidence reliability (§§5.3, 5.7–5.8).
- **Practical diagnostics:** Propose a two-call relabeling test and measure arbitrary labels, null-state correction, instructions, and disagreement screening (§5.6; §6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Definitions add little under familiar labels | On SST-2, RTE, and CB pooled over 833 items, laya-td accuracy is 0.8487 with both channels and 0.8559 with labels only; definitions with arbitrary labels achieve 0.7971. Reported intervals overlap for the first comparison. | §5.1; Fig. 3; Table 9 caption |
| Arbitrary labels improve controlled policy following | On PolicyBench-XF, laya-td gains +0.1511 accuracy, bootstrap 95% interval [+0.1377, +0.1646], from arbitrary versus aligned labels; n=3500. | §5.2; Tables 2–3 |
| A typed head is unnecessary for the effect | Qwen2.5-7B label-log-probability scoring gains +0.1203 accuracy [+0.1019, +0.1395] under the same label contrast on XF; n=2100. | §5.3; Tables 2–3 |
| Rendering interventions remove and create sensitivity | Removing labels makes all three laya checkpoints invariant on both suites: contrast +0.0000 [+0.0000, +0.0000]. Adding labels to von reduces fixed-label classification accuracy from 0.8511 aligned to 0.2281 misleading. | §5.5; Table 5 |
| Removing labels has a task-dependent tradeoff | For laya-td, definition-only rendering changes XF accuracy from 0.7420 to 0.8914, but fixed-label classification accuracy from 0.8487 to 0.8067. | §5.5 |
| Negation exposes a separate failure | Across seven yes/no tasks, laya-td accuracy falls from 0.6419 to 0.3576 after question negation; the authors describe returned probabilities as essentially unchanged. | §5.7; Fig. 5 |
| Confidence can rank errors backward | On fixed-label choice tasks, laya-td confidence AUROC falls from 0.8123 under aligned labels to 0.2433 under misleading labels. | §5.8; Table 6 |
| Mitigations differ in effectiveness | On laya-td XF, null-state correction adds 0.0391 accuracy under aligned labels; an ignore-label instruction adds 0.0138 [+0.0077, +0.0200]. Disagreement screening achieves 0.8921 retained accuracy at 0.8100 coverage, versus 0.7420 before screening. | §6; Tables 7–8 |

The bidirectional patches support a causal role for rendering in the tested systems. They do not explain why training produces preference for labels when both channels are visible, or establish the mechanism in closed Jev. Scoring-versus-generation comparisons change direction between controlled and uncontrolled suites, so the evidence does not establish generation as a general remedy (§5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Jev is untested; PolicyBench is synthetic and templated; definition wording may change absolute results; conflicting labels can make requests ambiguous; near-chance laya-multilingual performance cannot demonstrate robustness (§7). Latency and cost were not measured (§5.7).

**Analyst inferences:**
- Identical outputs from one relabeling pair cannot establish universal label invariance or prove that labels never enter the model. The two-call test’s interpretation is stronger than that individual observation warrants (§5.6).
- The negation account needs clarification: §5.7 reports high decision flip rates while describing probabilities as unchanged. Reorientation may explain this, but the missing Table 10 cells prevent verification.
- Exact label invariance after deleting labels follows from identical inputs; it establishes removal of this sensitivity, not reliable definition following in general (§5.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does the rendering result generalize to natural policies?** XF uses templates. Next experiment: evaluate matched renderings on human-written tickets with explicit policy ground truth.
- **How robust is definition following to wording?** Definitions use author-selected phrasing. Next experiment: vary equivalent definitions, length, and branch specificity while holding rules fixed.
- **How reliable is the two-call diagnostic?** Its sensitivity across inputs and label pairs is not established. Next experiment: compare repeated relabeling tests against known rendering implementations.
- **What fixes polarity sensitivity?** Label invariance and explicit polarity instructions do not resolve it. Next experiment: compare equivalent positive and negative formulations under unchanged rendering and polarity-focused training.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main intervention and reported comparisons, but table bodies are absent. Material missing information includes full per-model results and intervals, per-task sizes and prevalence, complete generator attributes and policies, exact prompts and run settings, release locations, and clarification of negation flip-rate orientation.
