# From Knowledge Access to Source Learning: Developing Source-Specific Competence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02150) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02150)


## One-sentence takeaway
SourceLearn progressively revises a source-grounded memory through self-study and task feedback, achieving the highest reported performance in 13 of 15 benchmark–backend settings while retaining imperfect factual grounding (Table 1; Appendix B.3).

## Problem and motivation
Repeated retrieval from the same source can leave an agent reconstructing rules, dependencies, and procedures for every task. The authors identify a gap between methods that organize source content and memories that retain task experience: neither necessarily develops reusable understanding of the source itself. They study persistent, authoritative sources supporting repeated tasks, where accumulated understanding can complement direct retrieval (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Source-learning formulation:** An explicit, persistent, revisable “source model” represents entities, governing conditions, procedures, distinctions, and relationships; the original source remains the factual authority (§§3–4.2).
- **Self-directed learning:** Model-conditioned inspection identifies gaps, adaptive study deepens individual entities or connects pairs, and consolidation reconstructs representations from source evidence (§4.3).
- **Task-guided learning:** Guidance-task failures identify missing source requirements; recurring task demands produce general representation preferences. Local refinement and source-wide recalibration reread the source rather than directly storing answers (§4.4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Comparisons cover source-grounded QA, API selection, and interactive tasks, with additional ablations, context-budget controls, grounding audits, and within-source transfer analyses (§5; Appendix B).

At inference, activated source-model regions supplement the same retrieved evidence used by Hybrid RAG. The default configuration uses one self-study cycle with 20 study actions and a source-model context budget of 24k tokens (Appendix A.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad but nonuniform improvement | Best performance in 13/15 settings across five benchmarks and three backends. Mean gains over Hybrid RAG: +14.3 percentage points for GPT-5.6-Luna, +4.9 for gpt-oss-120b, and +13.4 for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. | §5.2; Table 1 |
| Largest gain and a material failure case | GPT-5.6-Luna APIBench accuracy: 70.4% versus Hybrid RAG’s 47.8% (+22.6 points). gpt-oss-120b AppWorld TGC: 27.4% versus 30.4% (−3.0 points). | Table 1; Appendix A.1 |
| Both stages and task-guided pathways contribute | Removing either learning stage or either task-guided pathway lowers accuracy on all three QA benchmarks in the ablation, using two sources per benchmark and GPT-5.6-Luna. Exact bar values are unavailable in the extracted text. | §5.3; Figure 3; Appendix A.3 |
| Context quantity does not fully explain gains | With matched per-question context budgets and GPT-5.6-Luna, SourceLearn ranks first on DMV, VA, and HuggingFace, but second on Sphinx: 72.5% accuracy versus HippoRAG 2’s 73.5%. | Appendix B.2; Table 2 |
| Representation becomes more task-relevant | Required test-claim coverage rises from 23.2% in the initial model to 40.0% after task-guided learning. Accuracy is 67.5% with no required claims represented versus 86.6% with all represented. | §5.4; Figure 4 |
| Benefits extend to unseen regions of the same source | Aggregated QA accuracy with GPT-5.6-Luna: 77.3% versus Hybrid RAG’s 62.4% on seen regions; 75.0% versus 62.9% on unseen regions. | Appendix B.4; Table 5 |
| Grounding remains imperfect | Auditing 120 units per model/source, final-model fully supported shares are 63.3% for StudentAid, 65.0% for pytest, and 84.2% for HuggingFace; StudentAid has 13.3% unsupported and 0.8% contradicted units. | Appendix B.3; Table 3 |
| More self-study is not consistently better | Across four self-study cycles on SSA and StudentAid with GPT-5.6-Luna, model size grows without consistent accuracy improvement beyond the first cycle. | Appendix B.5; Table 6 |

The evidence supports improved held-out **same-source** performance under the tested configurations. Matched-budget comparisons weaken a pure context-volume explanation. However, coverage–accuracy association does not establish causality, unseen-region results do not demonstrate transfer to new sources, and the grounding audit shows that source reconstruction does not guarantee factual support.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Sources are persistent, authoritative, and relatively stable; evolving, noisy, or conflicting sources remain untested (§6).
- **Observed grounding risk:** Task-guided learning reduces fully supported unit shares on the audited document and code sources, despite grounding and preservation checks (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—limited controls:** Learning ablations cover selected QA sources with one backend; matched-budget controls cover four sources. These checks do not establish the same effects across every main setting (Appendices A.3, B.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation uncertainty:** QA correctness relies on GPT-5.6-Luna judging key-claim agreement. Human-judge validation, confidence intervals, and significance tests are not reported in the supplied text (Appendix A.1; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—practical cost unresolved:** Extra construction, inspection, refinement, and recalibration require LLM operations, but aggregate token costs, latency, and amortization across tasks are not reported.

## Open research questions
- **Can source models track changing or conflicting evidence?** The stable-source assumption leaves this open (§6). A next experiment could introduce versioned source updates and measure stale claims, correction speed, and task accuracy.
- **Can grounding improve without sacrificing utility?** Final models contain unsupported details (Table 3). Compare stricter evidence verification and uncertainty annotations using both unit-level support audits and downstream accuracy.
- **How much benefit comes from representation quality versus additional computation?** Context controls are limited, and learning cost is unreported (Appendix B.2). Run compute- and context-matched comparisons against repeated summarization and additional retrieval.
- **When should self-study stop?** Additional cycles yield inconsistent gains (Table 6). Test stopping policies based on verified novelty or held-out guidance performance across more source types and backends.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported comparisons; **medium** in generalization and causal interpretation.

Material gaps include uncertainty estimates, human validation of QA judging, learning cost and latency, exact numerical values from Figures 3–4, and complete JSON schemas and remaining operator prompts referenced as available in code. Repository contents and release functionality were not independently verified.
