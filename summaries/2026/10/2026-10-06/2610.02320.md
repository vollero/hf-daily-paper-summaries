# DeskForge: Dense Supervision from Desktop Environments for Computer-Use Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02320) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02320)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeskForge generates dense supervision from configurable, running Linux desktops, and the authors report that grounding fine-tuning improves external GUI accuracy and task completion under a fixed planner (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Computer-use agents must distinguish action targets amid overlapping windows, similar controls, and changing application content. The authors identify a gap between static annotated corpora, which cannot systematically vary scenes, and executable evaluation environments, which generally lack dense screen annotations. DeskForge combines controlled desktop variation, structured element labels, and recorded action outcomes (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controllable environment:** Compose real applications with configurable content, initial states, window stacking, appearance, and resolution; explore actionable elements rather than predefined tasks (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Dense annotation pipeline and corpus:** Fuse screenshots, accessibility trees, and window geometry to recover element hierarchy, text, interaction properties, and exposed regions under occlusion. DeskForge-1M retains screenshots, ScreenTag markup, and click-linked successor observations (§§3.2, 4.2).
- **Demonstrated training utility:** Generate single-step instructions using Qwen3.6-27B, then LoRA-fine-tune four VLMs on a fixed 200K-example grounding view. Separately train an element detector on dense annotations (§§4.2, 5, C.1). ScreenTag and LoRA are adopted components; the contribution is their use within the composed-desktop supervision pipeline.

The abstract states that framework code, the dataset, and a fine-tuned model are available; availability was not independently verified.

Matched comparisons support improved grounding after this training recipe and downstream benefit within the tested planner architecture. They do not isolate which data-generation component causes the gains. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Acquisition uses Linux and requires suitable accessibility support; visual presets do not execute native Windows or macOS applications. VLM training uses single targets, leaving structured and transition-based learning unexplored (§§3.1, 6).
- **Audit scope:** One reviewer assessed sampled annotations and instructions. The supplementary missing-element review is explicitly a diagnostic, not an independent recall estimate; the audit does not validate every state field (Appendix D).
- **Comparison scope:** Public checkpoint comparisons use different inference budgets. Detector comparisons use different architectures and confidence thresholds, limiting attribution specifically to training data (§§C.2, E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** No supplied ablation separates composition, occlusion handling, appearance diversity, instruction synthesis, and data volume. Their individual contributions remain uncertain.
- **Analyst inference:** Task evidence covers one planner, a custom browser panel, and one OpenApps seed; repeated-run uncertainty is not reported. Qwen’s browser interaction study also uses a separate training run from its grounding evaluation (§§C.1, C.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Which generation choices matter most?** Component effects are not isolated. Compare equal-sized training sets with and without multi-window composition, appearance variation, and visible-fragment labels.
2. **Do transitions improve action-outcome understanding?** Recorded transitions are retained but unused in the VLM training objective (§6). Compare grounding-only training against joint grounding and forward/inverse dynamics training.
3. **How robust are downstream gains across planners and runs?** Evaluation fixes one planner and specifies one OpenApps seed (Appendix C.4). Repeat matched comparisons across planner models and seeds, reporting uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main method and headline findings, but lacks table bodies, complete per-model scores and split counts, some training/inference settings, detector training details, and repeated-run uncertainty. The asset release statement remains unverified.
