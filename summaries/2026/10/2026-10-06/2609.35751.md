# How to Loop MoE: Flatten the Experts, Untie the Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35751) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35751)


## One-sentence takeaway
Foil improves a looped MoE’s language-modeling loss by replacing its recurrent core with fewer, wider expert layers and more passes with separate attention weights, at matched stored parameters and selected-expert computation (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Sparse MoE routing exposes each token to only a few experts per layer; looping offers additional routing decisions while reusing stored experts. The authors identify a gap in how to distribute experts across layers and passes—and which attention parameters to share—when expert storage and computation are fixed (§1).

The practical motivation is to improve parameter use under memory constraints. The paper studies architecture choices **once looping has been chosen**, rather than establishing that looping beats a non-looped model at equal parameters and compute (Appendix J).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint expert-layout transformation:** Foil maps the recurrent-core shape `(E,D,L)` to `(2E,D/2,2L)`, where these denote experts per layer, physical layers, and passes. This preserves stored experts, selected-expert calls, and effective depth while enlarging each routing pool (§2.2).
- **Pass-specific attention:** Experts and routers remain shared across passes, but each pass receives separate attention weights. Along the flattening sequence, this preserves total parameters and leading attention arithmetic. Base and all Foils have 553.7M parameters; tied-attention controls have fewer (§2.2; Table 3).
- **Controlled evaluation and routing diagnostics:** The authors compare flattened layouts and attention-sharing controls, introduce median margin ratio (MMR), and use traffic-matched expert masking to test whether low-traffic experts are expendable (§§2.3–4; Appendix I). Their claimed novelty is the joint layout comparison under fixed budgets, rather than expert sharing alone (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Flattening lowers training loss at matched budgets | At 20B tokens, Base/Foil-3/Foil-2/Foil-1 losses are 2.666/2.663/2.657/2.659 nat. All have 553.7M parameters, 64 core experts, and 32 core expert calls per token. | Tables 3–4; §3.1 |
| Longer training favors maximum flattening | At 100B tokens, the same sequence reaches 2.511/2.506/2.501/2.499 nat: Foil-1 is 0.012 nat below Base. | Table 4; Figure 3 |
| Untying attention improves the flattest model | Foil-1 beats same-shape proto-Foil-1 by 0.042 nat at 20B and 0.049 nat at 100B; at 100B its mean zero-shot accuracy across LAMBADA, HellaSwag, and XWinograd-English is 3.3 percentage points higher. Untying adds parameters. | §3.2; Figure 4; Table 3 |
| Routing improves under untying at equal shape | All tied/untied shape pairs have higher pooled load balance and MMR with untied attention, measured at 20B on a single-seed probe without error bars. | Figure 4; §3.2 |
| Widening benefits more from additional passes | With tied attention at 20B, widening `(8,4,L)` to `(16,4,L)` lowers final-window loss by 0.038 nat at two passes and 0.049 nat at eight passes; widening doubles stored core experts while preserving expert calls at each fixed pass count. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Flattening does not increase distinct experts reached | At 20B, mean distinct core experts reached per token falls from 24.1 for Base to 9.7 for Foil-1, despite improved loss. | Appendix B; Table 10 discussion |
| Low-traffic experts remain consequential | At 20B, masking the least-used group accounting for approximately 10% of routing traffic increases loss more than the traffic-matched random-group mean in seven of eight models. | §4.3; Tables 12–13 |
| Generalization is not uniform across domains | Held-out FineWeb-Edu reproduces the training-loss ordering; Wikipedia and C4 support the comparisons at 100B, but Foil-3 remains worse than Base on arXiv. Only means are reported. | Appendix F; Table 7 caption |

The evidence supports better layouts within this tested looped-MoE family. It does **not** establish superiority over a non-looped model at equal total resources, causal benefits from routing confidence, or actual expert specialization. “Equal compute” principally matches attention and selected-expert arithmetic: router scoring grows with flattening, and dispatch costs are excluded (Appendices A–B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** No non-looped control matches both parameters and compute; nearly all models use one seed; training-loss standard errors underestimate uncertainty because consecutive steps are correlated; results use one model width; most ablations use tied attention and may transfer imperfectly to Foil (Appendix J).
- **Author-stated diagnostic limits:** MMR depends on logit scale and expert-pool size. Neither MMR nor load balance observes expert outputs, so confident, balanced routing cannot establish functional specialization (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Untied-versus-tied comparisons combine an attention-sharing change with additional parameters, limiting attribution to pass-specific attention alone (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference:** Downstream conclusions are conditional on the task-selection rule: Appendix E retains tasks where all compared models sufficiently exceed baseline. Small held-out slices without uncertainty estimates also limit domain-level conclusions (Appendices E–F).

## Open research questions
- **Does Foil outperform non-looped alternatives under practical resource budgets?** The matched control is absent. Train comparable looped and non-looped architectures and measure loss, memory, throughput, and latency (Appendix J; Appendix A).
- **Do gains persist across scales and random seeds?** Evidence is concentrated at one width with one baseline seed replicate. Repeat the Base/Foil comparison across widths and multiple seeds (Appendices D, J).
- **Can MMR peaks predict useful stopping points?** The authors report only a few correlational cases. Prospectively select pass counts using MMR peaks, then compare against a full pass-count sweep on held-out loss (§4.4).
- **Which attention-sharing strategy gives the best budget trade-off?** Tied controls have fewer parameters. Compare full untying with partial sharing or parameter-matched attention variants across flattened layouts (§3.2; Table 3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main architectural comparison, but broad conclusions face limited replication and understated loss uncertainty.

Material gaps include numerical bodies for several appendix tables, exact per-task downstream scores, per-model runtime and total FLOPs, and detailed masking-sample composition. Checkpoint availability is not established. Appendix E also refers to evaluations across multiple widths, while Appendix J says all results use one width; the supplied text does not resolve this inconsistency.
