# LMBuild: Evaluating LLM Agents for Generating Buildable and Functional Structures

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04292) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04292)


## One-sentence takeaway
LMBuild evaluates structured 3D generation beyond appearance and finds that tested agents struggle most with functional components, articulation, and simulated operability, while explicit functional specifications improve these capabilities.

## Problem and motivation
Visually convincing geometry can still be disconnected, impossible to assemble, or functionally incomplete. The authors identify a gap in evaluations centered on geometry, appearance, and semantic alignment: they do not jointly assess parts, mechanisms, materials, assembly plans, and intended operation (§1, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interactive construction environment:** Agents retrieve or create parts, place and revise them, and declare roles, joints, materials, and assembly sequences. Experiments compare retrieval-only, retrieval-plus-creation, creation-only, revision, and richer specifications (§2.1; Appendix A.1–A.2).
- **Grounded benchmark:** Existing CAD and part-based datasets are filtered for structural diversity and augmented with evidence-backed object knowledge. The annotation pipeline retrieves sources, extracts supported claims, and independently verifies them (§2.2; Appendix A.3).
- **Multidimensional evaluation:** Twelve metrics cover soundness, affordance, design, and realization using geometry checks, grounded annotations, calibrated VLM judgments, and simulation (§2.3; Fig. 2).
- **Capability diagnosis:** Comparisons across general-purpose agents and specialized generators expose functional decomposition, part-creation, and scene-state failures (§3).

These are benchmark and evaluation contributions; the paper does not introduce a newly trained generation model.

| Creation can impair weaker models | On 25 sampled objects, Qwen3-VL-32B’s average across soundness and design falls from 61.7 with retrieval-only to 34.1 with creation-only; GPT-6 Astra scores 81.9 and 84.0 respectively. | Fig. 3; §3.3, p. 8 |
| Functional descriptions substantially improve targeted metrics | Average changes versus baseline: functional-part completeness +10.3 points, kinematics +3.5, operability +12.0; significance markers indicate respectively *p* < 0.001, *p* < 0.01, and *p* < 0.001. Exact model/task counts for this comparison are not established in the supplied passage. | Fig. 4; §3.4, p. 9 |
| Functional gains involve tradeoffs | The same functional-description condition reduces stability by 3.5 points and assembly-sequence score by 9.0 points. | Fig. 4, p. 9 |
| Extra revision produces smaller gains | Two additional revision rounds improve functional parts by 1.6 points and operability by 1.1 points on average versus baseline. | Fig. 4, p. 9 |
| Tool execution and state tracking differ across model groups | Part-creation success: closed-source 95.7%, open-source 51.7%. References to never-placed parts: 0.3 versus 7.2 errors per 100 calls, respectively. Aggregation denominators are not supplied here. | §3.5, p. 9 |
| Rankings are consistent across sampled objects | Across 30 systems, metric-level Pearson correlations between two disjoint stratified 50-object subsets range from 0.937 to 0.988; these use existing evaluation episodes. | Tables 6–7, pp. 59–60 |

The results support a gap between appearance/structural plausibility and integrated functionality under this benchmark. They do not establish successful fabrication or real-world operation. The authors’ explanation that additional creation freedom increases reasoning burden is an interpretation, not an isolated causal result.

Some narrative claims conflict with Table 2: §3.2 describes strongest kinematics scores exceeding 50 and operability reaching the mid-30s, whereas the listed maxima are 42.7 and 22.1. The table values are used here.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated physical limits:** Manufacturing tolerances, fastener strength, deformation, fracture, fatigue, tool access, routing, actuator limits, and sustained loading are not modeled. Simulation uses standardized physical proxies (Appendix A.6, p. 63).
- **Author-stated coverage limits:** Sampling prioritizes construction diversity rather than population representativeness. Results should not be extrapolated to all real-world objects (Appendix A.6).
- **Author-stated evaluator limits:** Human calibration covers a limited pool; grounded capability sheets may miss alternative components or valid realizations (Appendix A.6).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Interfaces, modality support, and native outputs differ. Specialized systems are comparable primarily on shared applicable metrics; generally only one final trajectory is evaluated per model–task pair (Appendices A.2, A.6).
- **Analyst inference—specification sensitivity:** Functional prompts supply additional requirements also relevant to evaluation. Their gains demonstrate translation of explicit requirements, rather than improved autonomous requirement discovery (Appendix A.2, p. 22).
- **Unresolved reporting inconsistencies:** Table 1 and Tables 6–8 identify a 200-object core, while Appendices A.3 and A.6 describe a 50-object core. Full-split counts also differ. Tool budgets are described as per episode in A.1 and per round in A.2.
- **Incomplete source:** A substantial middle portion was truncated, limiting inspection of evaluation definitions and supporting validation.

## Open research questions
- **Do scores predict physical success?** No fabrication validation is established here. Next experiment: fabricate representative designs and compare assembly and operation outcomes with benchmark scores.
- **How robust are rankings to evaluator assumptions?** Object resampling tests sampling consistency, while physical-parameter sensitivity remains open. Next experiment: vary collision geometry, density, friction, and thresholds.
- **Can agents discover functional requirements themselves?** Explicit specifications help, but autonomous elicitation is untested here. Next experiment: compare minimal prompts, agent-generated requirement sheets, and curated specifications under matched budgets.
- **How much of the model gap comes from interfaces?** Provider-specific harnesses differ. Next experiment: compare standardized interfaces with repeated generations, measuring tool errors and functional performance.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main results are directly supported by tables and figures, but source truncation and internal inconsistencies constrain interpretation.

Material missing or unresolved information includes complete omitted metric/calibration details, definitive split membership and sizes, exact Fig. 4 evaluation counts, consistent interaction-budget accounting, repeated-run uncertainty, physical validation, and confirmation that promised artifacts were released.
