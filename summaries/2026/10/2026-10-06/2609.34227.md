# When Does Selection Replace Extraction? A Pre-Registered Test of Agent Memory with a Typed Decision Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34227) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34227)


## One-sentence takeaway
On held-out LoCoMo conversations at approximately matched tight context budgets, Jev-selected raw turns met a pre-registered five-percentage-point non-inferiority margin against extracted-fact memory, while wider-budget comparisons remained confounded by the selector’s read-path ceiling (§§5.1, 5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Conversational agents must decide whether to store raw dialogue or spend resources extracting facts before retrieval. The authors identify conflicting findings about extraction and reranking, and hypothesize that the fraction of retrieved candidates reaching the answer model explains part of the disagreement. They test whether selection can substitute for extraction at a tight context budget, rather than claiming universal equivalence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The raw-turn pipeline embeds dated dialogue turns, retrieves a cosine shortlist of 30, and uses one Jev request to score each turn’s relevance. It prioritizes scores above 0.5, appends a ten-turn cosine floor, and truncates the result. Its extraction comparator, engram v2, uses the same read procedure over LLM-extracted facts containing source quotes and validity windows (§§3.1–3.2).

The contributions are:

- **Controlled, pre-registered evaluation:** a non-inferiority test on previously unused LoCoMo conversations, with approximate mean-token matching, a second answer model, and blind human sensitivity analysis (§4).
- **Budget analysis:** within-study comparisons of reranking gains as more candidates reach the answer model, with explicit qualifications about unequal token use and selection ceilings (§5.3).
- **Typed-selector evaluation:** answer-level comparisons against an LLM reranker and multi-call graph traversal at matched context (§5.2).
- **Diagnostics and artifacts:** evidence-recall decomposition, abstention and grading diagnostics, and reported release of plans, code, answers, labels, and audit grades (§§5.4–5.8; Artifacts).

The authors explicitly identify raw-turn reranking as an existing pattern and engram v2 as an earlier system’s revision; the contribution is primarily the evaluation, not a new memory architecture (§1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Primary non-inferiority test passes | On 778 scored questions from five held-out LoCoMo conversations, Turns + Jev at k=6 achieved 77.0% accuracy using 265 mean context tokens, versus engram v2 at k=3 with 77.5% and 251 tokens. Difference: −0.5 percentage points; one-sided 95% lower bound: −3.0, above the −5 margin. | §5.1; Tables 1, 3 |
| Result survives sensitivity checks | Human replacements for 141 graded judge-discordant questions yield differences of −1.7 to −2.6 points and lower bounds of −3.9 to −4.7. With Llama 3.3 70B answering the same H1 contexts, accuracy is 75.3% versus 75.6%, with lower bound −2.9. | §§5.1, 5.4; Appendix C |
| Reranking gains shrink with larger k | Against cosine ordering, gains at k=3 versus k=20 are +17.4 versus +1.5 points on 778 LoCoMo questions, and +9.1 versus +1.1 on 470 non-abstention LongMemEval questions. Larger-k comparisons are descriptive and not token-matched. | §5.3; Figure 5 |
| Typed selector meets LLM-reranker margin | On LoCoMo at matched context, Jev versus the gpt-4o-mini listwise reranker has difference −0.4 points and non-inferiority lower bound −2.0. Live k=3 reads on a fixed 40-question sample are about 3.0× faster with Jev. | §§5.2, 5.7; Tables 2, 5 |
| Extraction leads the generous-budget comparison | On the same 778 LoCoMo questions, engram v2 at k=20 scores 82.4% using 1,238 tokens; standard Turns + Jev scores 77.6% using 496. This is descriptive and not token-matched. | §5.3; Table 3 |
| Raw-turn writing is substantially cheaper | Held-out list-price write costs per 1,000 turns are $0.00061 for Turns + Jev versus $1.865 for engram v2: a reported 3,061× ratio. This excludes read and answer costs. | §§5.1, 5.7; Appendix H |
| Evidence retrieval and retention constrain accuracy | Across nine held-out LoCoMo conversations and 1,388 questions, 11.5% have no evidence turn in the shortlist; a further 9.2% lose all shortlisted evidence during reranking. | §5.6; Appendix D |
| Reranking reduces correct abstention | On 209 LoCoMo adversarial questions at k=3, correct abstention is 63.6% for cosine retrieval, 54.1% for Jev reranking, and 48.8% for LLM reranking; exploratory evaluation. | §5.8; Table 6 |

The evidence establishes bounded non-inferiority against this extraction comparator at this tight LoCoMo budget, rather than equality or superiority. LongMemEval supports reranking over cosine retrieval, but engram v2 was not evaluated there. The authors’ explanation of disagreement across earlier papers remains an interpretation; their larger-budget results do not isolate context budget from read-path restrictions (§§5.3, 5.5–6).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** the primary evaluation has few conversations and small categories; LoCoMo dialogue is LLM-generated; all development choices used one conversation; Jev is closed and version-specific; and injected conversation content was not tested. The human grader built the evaluated systems, audited only judge-discordant answers, used unspecified partial-grade mappings, and left one question ungraded (Limitations; Appendix C).

The generous-budget comparisons mix budget with threshold and floor restrictions. The wider variant simultaneously changes shortlist size and selection rules, was designed after seeing results, and was evaluated once on the same questions (§5.6; Appendix E). The study amendment also followed knowledge of S1 and S2, although it preceded H1 (§4).

**Analyst inference:** the wide variant cannot identify which individual change improves performance without separate ablations. Mean-token matching controls average context size, not exact per-question equality (Eq. 5). Reported model-serving differences complicate some comparisons; additionally, the text’s claim that only S3 involves mem0 conflicts with its listed S5 mem0 comparison (§§4, 5.2; Appendix B).

## Open research questions

- **Does the budget effect persist after removing read-path restrictions?** Larger-k results mix budget and selector ceiling. Next experiment: independently vary shortlist size, relevance threshold, and cosine floor under matched token budgets on new conversations (§§5.3, 5.6).
- **Can selection substitute for extraction on long histories?** LongMemEval lacks the engram v2 comparator. Next experiment: preregister a matched-token non-inferiority comparison across long-history question types (§5.5).
- **Can selection preserve both answer accuracy and abstention?** Reranking lowers correct abstention. Next experiment: evaluate calibrated relevance thresholds or an abstention gate on answerable and unanswerable questions (§5.8).
- **How much do grading style and evaluator affiliation affect the gap?** The audit is selective and author-conducted. Next experiment: independent graders audit a random sample including judge-agreement cases under prespecified partial-credit rules (§5.4; Appendix C).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed settings and bounded results, but causal and generalization claims remain limited.

Material unavailable information includes full numeric contents of several referenced tables, Jev’s internal training and architecture, independent full-sample human grades, separate wide-variant ablations, adversarial-content results, and long-history engram v2 results. Artifact availability and reproducibility are author-reported and were not independently verified.
