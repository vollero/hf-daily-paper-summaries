# Periscope: Extending Frozen Language Models Beyond Their Context Window

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04047) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04047)


## One-sentence takeaway
Periscope uses bounded local and strided probes of a frozen language model to select evidence beyond its context window, matching or improving some long-context QA baselines with lower peak cache requirements, but with uneven accuracy gains and additional compute.

## Problem and motivation
Long-context inference faces context-window limits, growing attention and cache costs, and accuracy degradation with input length. The authors identify a gap between reading isolated passages, selecting passages with an external retriever, and carrying generated summaries through sequential calls: none provides their desired combination of independent bounded calls, document-wide coverage, and evidence scores produced by the reader itself (§§1–2).

The method targets finite-choice decisions where a supporting passage may suffice; this assumption limits its applicability to tasks requiring distributed evidence.

## Approach and contributions
- **Factorized text reading:** Split text into chunks arranged on a square grid. Probe each row of consecutive chunks and each column of strided chunks using the same frozen model. Scores come from first-answer-token log-odds against abstention (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Evidence map:** For each answer, add its maximum local and maximum strided score. Score each chunk using its intersecting probes, without additional model calls (§3.3).
- **Four readouts:** Use the scores for document ranking, direct multiple-choice answers, chunk localization, or selecting the highest-ranked chunks for one final read (§3.3).

The contribution adapts the grid-probing structure discussed in related work to text; the paper does not originate grid probing generally (§2). Its reach and cost derivations give approximately \(W^2/c\) text tokens for window \(W\) and chunk size \(c\), with attention cost growing as \(s^{3/2}\), subject to prompt overhead and probe fit—not an accuracy guarantee (§3.4).

These results support useful evidence selection and lower peak per-call cache requirements in the tested settings. They do not establish universal accuracy equivalence, lower total compute against truncated window baselines, or reliable evidence localization throughout very long documents. Several important tables appear only as captions plus surrounding prose in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Finite answer sets are required; distant evidence is combined only in the selected read; multi-document QA trails window reading; probing costs more below the compute crossover (§6).
- **Comparison boundary:** Window baselines read prefixes when contexts exceed their configured windows. Such comparisons measure broader coverage as well as differences in reading strategy (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—localization ambiguity:** The highest local and strided scores can come from evidence in different chunks. Their intersection therefore need not itself contain supporting evidence; algebraic agreement between answer and map scores is not a localization guarantee (§3.3).
- **Limited localization validation:** Annotated localization evaluation uses short OBLIQ documents that fit within a call; long-context maps are illustrated with examples rather than a comparable annotated evaluation (Appendix B; Figure 5).
- **Sensitivity and uncertainty:** Chunk-size changes affect accuracy, and the authors describe one difference as close to run-to-run variation. Scores receive no cross-span calibration; strided chunks are joined without explicit delimiters (Appendices A.1, A.4).

## Open research questions
- **Can the map identify evidence reliably in very long texts?** Localization annotations cover short documents only. Next experiment: annotate long-document evidence and measure peak accuracy and selected-chunk recall.
- **Can selection preserve distributed evidence?** Multi-document QA remains weaker (§6). Next experiment: vary selection budgets and compare diversity-aware selection on questions requiring multiple documents.
- **How robust is score composition?** Scores are uncalibrated, and chunk size affects results (Appendices A.1, A.4). Next experiment: vary chunk boundaries, abstention prompts, and calibration while holding total compute fixed.
- **Can the approach support open-ended outputs?** Summarization is proposed future work (§6). Next experiment: evaluate map-guided summaries for coverage and factual consistency against matched-budget reads.
