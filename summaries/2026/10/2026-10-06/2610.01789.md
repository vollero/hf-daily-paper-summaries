# iADD: Improving Alignment and Diversity in Diffusion Policy Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01789) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01789)


## One-sentence takeaway

iADD combines incremental sparse timestep updates with training-time Feynman–Kac branching to improve reported reward–diversity tradeoffs in image and 3D generation, at substantially higher training cost than DDPO (§§4–5; S16).

## Problem and motivation

Reward-based diffusion fine-tuning can increase alignment while reducing diversity and visual quality. The authors target online reinforcement learning without additional preference data, arguing that existing methods insufficiently justify timestep selection and underuse trajectory information before terminal rewards become available (§§1–3).

The central gap is how to move generation toward high-reward outcomes while preserving the pretrained distribution, especially for conditions the pretrained model handles poorly.

## Approach and contributions

- **Timestep analysis:** The authors analyze output sensitivity and generator Jacobian log-volume to motivate updates that include early denoising steps and initially remain sparse. They explicitly frame the theoretical results as comparative predictions under assumptions, rather than exact training bounds (§4.2; S7).
- **Incremental curriculum:** iADD progressively expands the number of updated timesteps, combining initial structural regularization with later refinement (§4.2; §5.1).
- **Training-time FK guidance:** Reward-based particle weighting, pruning, and branching allocate training trajectories toward promising outcomes. The contribution is integrating discrete-time FK guidance into training with the timestep curriculum; FK guidance and branching themselves have prior precedents (§4.3; S9.4).
- **Evaluation and optimizer compatibility:** Experiments cover text-to-image alignment, perspective correction, and indoor layouts; an iADD+GRPO variant tests compatibility with group-relative advantages (§§5.1–5.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** FK sampling increases training cost; rewards inferred from noisy intermediate states can be unreliable, particularly for 3D geometry; CLIP can reward semantically incorrect images. The authors also question IS reliability for strict geometric tasks (S16; S13.2).

**Analyst inferences from the supplied text:**

- **Implementation ambiguity:** §4.3 describes terminal rollout rewards aggregated to intermediate nodes, while S16 describes rewards from denoised noisy-state predictions. Their precise combination is unclear.
- **Configuration inconsistencies:** Main-text and supplementary image settings disagree on resampling checkpoints and curriculum details (§5.1; S9.1).
- **Metric ambiguity:** Collision is described as penetration-based in §5.2 but as the percentage of colliding objects in S10.2. These are different measures.
- **Study accounting ambiguity:** The reported 30 prompts and 40 raters choosing among four outputs do not transparently explain 4,800 judgments; the figure caption instead describes pairwise ratings (§5.3; Fig. 7(c)).
- **Theory has conditional scope:** Higher early-step sensitivity and reward-induced contraction underpin the arguments. The supplied proofs do not establish universal guarantees for nonlinear fine-tuning (§4.2; S7).
- **Evaluation scope:** Recent GRPO/guidance comparisons are limited to Template 1, and 3D experiments use bedroom layouts; broader transfer remains unestablished (§5.1; S9.3).

## Open research questions

- **Can intermediate reward estimation improve 3D performance?** S16 identifies unreliable geometry recovery. Compare denoised-state rewards against completed-rollout estimates under equal compute.
- **Do gains survive stronger semantic evaluation?** CLIP failure cases remain acknowledged. Train with alternative rewards and evaluate with independent, blinded semantic judgments.
- **What is the best tradeoff under fixed training budgets?** Current gains require additional GPU time. Sweep particle counts and resampling frequency while comparing methods at equal GPU hours.
- **How widely does optimizer compatibility generalize?** GRPO comparisons cover only Template 1. Repeat matched-compute comparisons on attribute binding, spatial relations, and 3D layouts.
