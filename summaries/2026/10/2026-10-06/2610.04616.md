# PerturBot: Breaking Shortcut Priors in Vision-Language-Action Models with Perturbative Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04616) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04616)


## One-sentence takeaway
PerturBot combines three training-data interventions that raise reported π₀.₅ real-robot pick-and-place success from 42% to 84%, while its proposed GroundFscore diagnoses responsiveness to task evidence separately from shortcut resistance (§§3–4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Successful demonstrations can make convenient cues sufficient to predict actions: wrist-camera salience substitutes for target grounding, object nouns substitute for requested verbs, and gripper closure triggers lifting regardless of grasp outcome. The authors argue that adding demonstrations with these same correlations can improve task success without resolving these shortcuts (§§1, 3.1).

The identified gap is both methodological and evaluative: training data need examples that distinguish shortcuts from relevant evidence, and success alone cannot reveal which information controls a policy’s decisions.

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified shortcut account:** The authors organize salience capture, noun lock-in, and motor inertia around the decisions they distort. Their information-theoretic formulation motivates data construction; it is neither a new training loss nor a guarantee of learned grounding (§3.1).
- **PerturBot training:** V inserts screened, off-path wrist-view distractors; C adds behavior-supported, decision-relevant captions; R adds accurately relabeled segments from random motion and failed execution. Training retains the backbone’s action loss, and deployment requires no additional model or forward pass (§3.2; Appendices B–E).
- **GroundFscore diagnostic:** Paired null edits should preserve actions, while causal edits demand known action changes. A harmonic mean combines stability and directed responsiveness, preventing complete input neglect from earning a high score. Evaluation requires paired expert reference actions but no policy rollouts (§3.3).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full method improves physical task success | π₀.₅ success: **42% → 84%** on the same 50 unseen General PnP cases using brief instructions; main training uses 500 demonstrations plus auxiliary recordings. | §4.2; Table 3, 1× row; Appendix B |
| Components affect their intended shortcut families | V: GFᵥ **0.28 → 0.51**, SC failure **68% → 36%**; C: GFₗ **0.24 → 0.47**; R: GFₐ **0.19 → 0.44**, MI failure **82% → 44%**. GroundFscore uses 100 held-out observations; SC/MI each use 50 separate trials. | §§3.3, 4.2; Table 4 |
| Improved resistance retains useful conditioning | Full method selects the distractor in **20%** versus fine-tuning’s **68%**, while selecting the instructed object in **90%**. Secured-object lifting is **92% versus 94%**; empty-grasp lifting is **24% versus 82%**. Each probe uses 50 trials per policy. | §5.3; Table 5 caption |
| Gains extend to simulated skills | Across 50 RoboTwin tasks, Clean2Random task-averaged success improves by **12.2 percentage points** on randomized scenes and **2.7 points** on clean scenes versus matched π₀.₅ fine-tuning. Absolute scores are absent from the supplied table body. | §4.3 |
| Scaling improves success and diagnostic scores together | Across 125–1,000 demonstrations at fixed optimizer steps, three seeds per budget: success slope is **16.2 points per doubling** for PerturBot versus **6.0** for fine-tuning; mean GroundFscore slope is **0.100 versus 0.013**. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| GroundFscore tracks shortcut failures | Over 36 checkpoints, Spearman correlation with negative online failure rate is **0.72**; its advantage over task success is **0.41**, with paired 95% cluster-bootstrap interval **[0.12, 0.68]**. Stability-only and responsiveness-only correlations are **0.44** and **0.38**. | §5.4; Appendix F.4 |

The matched data interventions and controls support improved performance and reduced shortcut susceptibility in these settings. They do not establish universal evidence use, recovery competence, or transfer across backbones. GroundFscore’s correlation supports its diagnostic value within the tested checkpoint population; the authors explicitly describe it as a diagnostic rather than a competence certificate (§3.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The method needs accurate captions, screened relabeling, and additional random/failed recordings. Relabeling cannot create missing behaviors, including corrective recovery (§6; Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** All experiments use π₀.₅, and physical evaluation covers only General PnP. Broader skills are tested in simulation, leaving cross-backbone and broader physical transfer unresolved (§4.1; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—metric validity:** GroundFscore depends on valid edits and paired expert action differences. Its causal-sensitivity term measures projection onto the required change; the supplied text does not test whether large action deviations orthogonal to that change can escape penalty (§3.3).
- **Comparison boundary:** Published RoboTwin systems use different architectures and recipes and are contextual comparisons. The paper also acknowledges higher reported scores in its comprehensive comparison table (§4.3; Table 7 caption).
- **Evidence availability:** Many table bodies and the annotation prompt are absent from the supplied text, despite their captions being present. Some prose references also mismatch table captions, limiting verification of uncertainty estimates and detailed comparisons.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the recipe transfer across VLA backbones?** Only π₀.₅ is tested. A next experiment could repeat the V/C/R factorial comparison on another backbone with matched data and training budgets (§4.1).
2. **Can behavioral expansion improve recovery under the original goal?** The auxiliary recordings contain no corrective continuations. Compare current R with recorded recovery continuations and evaluate completion after induced failures under unchanged instructions (Appendix B.3).
3. **How robust is GroundFscore to alternative valid actions?** The score uses expert chunk differences and directed projection, with no supplied analysis of action ambiguity. Test multiple valid expert references and policies producing orthogonal action errors, then compare scores with online failures (§3.3).
4. **How sensitive are gains to annotation and screening quality?** Captions and edits are checked, but error rates and annotation-cost measurements are unavailable. Introduce controlled caption/edit errors and measure success, GroundFscore, and shortcut failures (Appendices D–E).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and central results, but verification is limited by missing table contents.

Material omissions include the complete main and RoboTwin result tables, most numerical confidence intervals, exact optimizer configuration and selected sampling rates, retained auxiliary-segment counts, caption-model identity and annotation prompt, annotation/screening costs and error rates, and confirmation that promised artifacts have been released.
