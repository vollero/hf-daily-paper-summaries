# UnAct: Gradient-Free Unlearning via Targeted Activation Intervention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04426) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04426)


## One-sentence takeaway
UnAct suppresses an image class using forward-only edits from a few forget images while preserving retained-class accuracy in the tested ResNet settings, but its evidence establishes output suppression rather than knowledge erasure (§4.2; Appendices G, M).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address post-hoc class unlearning when a deletion request provides only a few images and no labels or retained training data. SSD and LFSSD avoid retraining but still require gradients and full-training-set importance estimates; the authors identify scarce forget data as a setting where these methods can severely damage retained-class accuracy (§§1–2).

The motivation is cheaper removal of unwanted training influence. However, the paper’s evaluated task is suppressing an entire class, rather than deleting individual records or establishing privacy guarantees (§3.1; Appendix G).

## Approach and contributions
- **Forward-only unit editing:** UnAct scores late units using forget images, selects highly ranked units, and multiplies their incoming and outgoing parameters by an attenuation factor. It repeats scoring after each edit without optimization (§3; Algorithm 1).
- **Bounded selection and iterative coverage:** Percentile selection controls each round’s edit size; re-ranking empirically expands the set of edited units. The exact coverage bound depends on the actual selection count, including percentile rounding and ties (§3.4; Appendices A, N).
- **Architecture-specific scoring:** ResNet-18 uses mean post-ReLU channel activations in its last residual block. ViT-B/16 uses direct class-logit attribution for MLP neurons in its last four blocks, with the target class inferred from mean logits. The transformer version therefore uses weights and a pseudo-label, not activations alone (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix L).
- **Evaluation and metric calibration:** The authors test scarce-data robustness, sequential requests, runtime, feature recoverability, and relearning, explicitly showing that closeness to retraining in output accuracy can overstate forgetting (§4.2; Appendices G–N).

## Evidence and main results
Here, **Δ** is the sum of absolute retain- and forget-test-accuracy gaps from retraining, in percentage points, computed per forget class and then averaged (§4.1). Results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full-class ResNet performance is competitive, without a uniform win. | Across five forget classes per dataset, all methods reach 0% forget accuracy. UnAct Δ is 0.08/0.80/1.15 on CIFAR-10/20/100; LFSSD is 0.11/0.24/0.38. | §4.2 Q1; Table 2 |
| Few images suffice for low output-accuracy gaps. | With five forget images, ResNet UnAct Δ is 0.21/0.77/1.37 on CIFAR-10/20/100. On CIFAR-100, LFSSD is better at 0.44. Across 125 ResNet runs, UnAct’s retain accuracy is never more than 2.45 points below retraining. | §4.2 Q2; Figures 2, 5; Table 10 |
| SSD threshold re-selection does not resolve its five-image failure. | Oracle per-size selection of SSD’s α within its published range gives Δ 87.5/64.6/46.1 on CIFAR-10/20/100; other SSD settings stay fixed. UnAct receives no per-size tuning. | §4.2 Q2; Table 11 |
| Transformer transfer improves on SSD but remains imperfect. | Full-class CIFAR-10 ViT-B/16: mean Δ 11.49 versus SSD 33.74 across five classes; UnAct’s mean retain accuracy remains 6.5 points below retraining. LFSSD was not run. | §4.2 Q1; Table 16 |
| Sequential forgetting trades off with retention. | Five sequential requests over five ResNet class orders: UnAct forget accuracy never exceeds 2.7% on CIFAR-10/20. After the fifth CIFAR-20 request, retain accuracy is 70.1% versus SSD’s 85.7%; SSD is preferable on CIFAR-100. | Appendix I; Figure 6; Table 12 |
| Runtime gains depend on configuration and accounting. | One-round full-class ResNet requests take 0.58/0.38/0.12 s on CIFAR-10/20/100, 3.3/3.0/1.8× faster than precomputed-importance SSD. Selected 20-round CIFAR-10/20 settings are slower than that SSD setting. ViT full-class requests take 73 s versus SSD’s 1,373 s cold, or 1.7× faster with importance precomputed. | §4.2 Q4; Appendix K, Table 15 |
| Low Δ does not establish erasure. | Full-class ResNet logit masking achieves Δ 0.09/0.25/0.33. Forget-class probes achieve AUROC ≥0.98 after every method, but also ≥0.96 after retraining. CIFAR-10 forget-image prediction agreement with retraining is 31.4% for UnAct versus 65.5% for SSD. | Appendix G; Table 9 |
| The transformer edit is quickly reversible. | ViT CIFAR-10, five relearning steps with five forget images per step mixed with retain images, learning rate 5×10⁻⁴: mean forget accuracy returns to 87.3% for UnAct, versus 33.6% for SSD and 0% for retraining. | Appendix M; Figure 11 |

These results support robust class-prediction suppression from scarce images in the tested settings. They do not establish removal of training influence or privacy protection. Feature decodability alone is inconclusive because retrained models also separate the class; rapid relearning and divergent predictions provide additional evidence that low Δ does not imply equivalence to retraining.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** UnAct suppresses rather than erases the class, assumes one class per request, needs architecture-specific scoring, edits more parameters than the baselines, and can accumulate retention damage across requests. Evaluation covers CIFAR-scale data, two architectures, one seed, and test-split operating-point selection (§5; Appendices F, I, M).

**Analyst inference:** Test-split tuning limits claims about performance on untouched evaluation data. The scarcity comparison also leaves some calibration uncertainty: only SSD’s α is re-selected per size, while LFSSD lacks that experiment and SSD’s other settings remain fixed (Appendices D, H). The exact rank-bound interpretation requires care because ResNet percentile ties can enlarge selections (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Can suppression become durable forgetting?** Relearning rapidly restores the class. Compare alternative attenuation or removal rules under identical relearning probes and retrained references (Appendix M).
- **Does scarce-data robustness generalize?** Evidence uses one seed and CIFAR-scale datasets. Repeat with multiple seeds, larger image datasets, and validation-only tuning (§5).
- **Can repeated requests preserve retention?** Sequential damage appears on CIFAR-20/100. Test coverage-aware stopping or request-specific settings against retrained models for every deletion prefix (Appendix I).
- **How much does baseline recalibration change the scarcity gap?** LFSSD lacks per-size oracle tuning. Run complete per-size grids for both baselines using a separate validation split (Appendix H).
