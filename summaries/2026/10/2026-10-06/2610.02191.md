# The Missing Primitive: Diagnosing and Repairing Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02191) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02191)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors’ PRIM benchmark separates identifying a solution’s key mathematical idea from executing it, and their primitive-guided ABSORB training improves reported average solving performance across three Qwen model scales without requiring primitives at inference (§§2–4; Table 4).

## Problem and motivation
Final-answer accuracy can hide whether a model identifies the structure that makes a problem solvable or succeeds through procedural exploration. The authors identify a gap in evaluating this structural understanding separately from execution and in using that diagnosis to guide post-training (§1).

They define a **mathematical primitive** as a concise, problem-specific observation explaining which structural property unlocks a solution, rather than a technique name or a proof (§2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic benchmark:** PRIM contains 182 expert-reviewed, text-only mathematics problems drawn from HLE-Verified. It measures **Discovery** from the problem, **Generation** of a solution, **Digestion** of the primitive from a correct solution, and **Execution** with a supplied primitive. Primitive predictions receive validity, structural-identification, and mechanism scores (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Capability diagnosis:** Across 12 models, the authors compare these dimensions, test different guidance sources, and relate initial failure categories to subsequent repair through post-training (§3).
- **ABSORB:** A self-teacher receives the primitive as privileged training information while the student generates solutions from the problem alone. Distillation uses teacher top-token support and a one-sided cap on reverse-KL contributions to limit suppression of student-preferred choices. The primitive is unnecessary at inference (§§4.1–4.3; Appendix D).

The results establish useful differences between these operational measures and demonstrate gains from supplied primitives and ABSORB in the reported settings. They do **not** establish that the rubric measures all mathematical understanding, or that failed standalone Discovery causally explains every failed solution. The authors’ interpretation of implicit internalization is consistent with improved unassisted solving, but the experiments do not directly identify the learned internal representation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged scoring limitation:** A single gold primitive can under-credit valid alternative routes. Audits found few such cases, but only within the inspected samples (Appendix A.3).
- **Author-acknowledged distribution boundary:** PRIM is a curated, scorable subset with corrected answers; its accuracy should not be equated with full-HLE performance (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited training generality:** Post-training uses three Qwen3.5 scales, one fixed seed, and a qualifying-examination corpus concentrated in algebra and analysis. Cross-family and cross-domain robustness remains unestablished (§3.3; Appendices C–D).
- **Analyst inference—measurement ambiguity:** Extraction prompts request every essential conceptual idea, whereas the correctness prompt emphasizes a single dominant idea. The validity prompt also permits full derivations despite the concise, non-procedural definition (Figures 7–10).
- **Analyst inference—comparison ambiguity:** Table 13 says solution versus primitive privilege is compared under the same ABSORB objective, but its solution-privilege scores exactly match the OPSD row in Table 4. The text does not resolve whether these are separate runs.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and tuning:** No confidence intervals, repeated-seed results, or sensitivity studies for the cap and top-token support are reported. Mechanism ablations are confined to 9B on PRIM (Appendices D–E).
- The authors explicitly identify the dual-channel alternative as an unequal-resource comparison, rather than a strict ablation (§E; Table 16).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the discovery diagnosis survive broader scoring?** Single-gold scoring may miss alternative ideas, and prompt scope varies. Annotate multiple valid primitives per problem and repeat Discovery evaluation with blinded human scoring.
2. **Does ABSORB generalize beyond Qwen?** Diagnostic coverage is broader than post-training coverage. Train matched ABSORB and baseline runs on other model families with multiple seeds.
3. **Which primitive information produces the gains?** Current experiments compare guidance formats but do not isolate the contribution of each structural component. Test matched-length correct, incomplete, and incorrect primitives during training and execution.
4. **Can stronger explicit discovery improve solving reliably?** Self-generated primitive guidance often hurts performance, while ABSORB mainly improves Generation. Test confidence-filtered primitive generation and compare it with direct solving under a matched inference budget.
