# HLA-WM: Hybrid Linear Attention for Long-Horizon Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05739) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05739)


## One-sentence takeaway
HLA-WM improves long-range revisit consistency in pretrained SANA-WM without training by retrieving camera-relevant chunk summaries and recomposing recurrent memory, with substantially less historical storage than full KV caching (Secs. 4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Camera-controlled video world models should recover previously generated scenes after long excursions. Full-history KV caching preserves access to past observations but grows with rollout length; Gated DeltaNet (GDN) instead compresses history into a fixed-size recurrent state.

The authors identify a mismatch between temporal memory updates and spatial relevance: distant scene information passes through every intervening update, including updates from unrelated viewpoints. Their trajectory analysis illustrates attenuation and degraded revisit consistency, motivating independently addressable recurrent memories (Sec. 3; Figs. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Forgetting analysis:** The authors express chunk-level GDN updates as affine transitions and measure attenuation through products of intervening transition matrices (Sec. 3).
- **Selective recurrent memory:** HLA-WM caches each chunk’s affine transition and write summaries, retrieves relevant chunks, and composes them chronologically without replaying historical visual tokens. Selecting all historical chunks recovers the original recurrence (Secs. 4.2–4.4).
- **Geometry-guided integration:** Retrieval uses camera poses, intrinsics, and a median-depth proxy, alongside a persistent first chunk and adaptive recent context. The same selection aligns the softmax KV pathway; camera-control and local convolutional states retain chronological updates. Pretrained parameters remain unchanged (Secs. 4.3–4.4; Appendix A.2).
- **Evaluation:** Benchmark comparisons and component ablations test revisit fidelity, camera control, memory storage, and throughput (Sec. 5; Appendices A.3–A.8).

The evidence supports improved **self-consistency at revisited poses** in the tested generator and benchmarks. It does not establish correctness against an external physical world, downstream planning benefits, or portability to other generators. The storage comparison concerns historical-state memory, rather than total inference memory.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Training-free adaptation limits achievable gains; retrieval does not explicitly model occlusion, visibility, or semantic relevance (Sec. 6).
- **Author-stated comparison caveat:** Historical-selection baselines are not strictly matched for compute because turn-aware context can expand HLA-WM’s state budget (Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Both benchmark evaluations use the same SANA-WM backbone. Architectural generalization and quality beyond minute-scale rollouts remain untested in the supplied results (Sec. 5.1).
- **Analyst inference—heterogeneous effects:** Aggregate gains mask category-level regressions and short-revisit LPIPS degradation. Better memory therefore does not improve every metric in every setting (Fig. 6; Tables 8–10).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** GDN-only and KV-only ablations support complementary pathways, but the authors’ claim that GDN is the primary driver is setting-dependent: Simple Stage 1 shows larger revisit gains from KV-only than GDN-only (Table 4).
- **Reporting gaps:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated-seed variability are reported. The text provides implementation details but does not establish code or checkpoint release.

## Open research questions
- **Can retrieval handle occlusion and scene changes?** The overlap proxy omits visibility and semantics. Compare it with occlusion-aware or learned retrieval on controlled occluded revisits (Sec. 6).
- **Does the advantage persist over much longer rollouts?** Quality evaluation is minute-scale, while summary storage grows with chunk count. Test multi-minute revisits with bounded-memory eviction (Secs. 4.4–5).
- **How much benefit comes from geometry under equal budgets?** The selection comparison permits adaptive budget expansion. Match selected-state counts and recent-context rules across geometry and Uniform policies (Appendix A.6).
- **Can memory selection jointly optimize fidelity and camera control?** Larger retrieval budgets improve some camera errors while worsening revisit metrics. Test adaptive budgets across trajectory types and refinement modes (Tables 4, 6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark direction and method description; **medium** in causal attribution and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates, repeated-seed results, quality evaluation beyond minute-scale rollouts, other-backbone experiments, matched-compute selection comparisons, and confirmed release status. The supplied text also does not spell out exact revisit-pose matching thresholds or detailed memory/throughput measurement procedures.
