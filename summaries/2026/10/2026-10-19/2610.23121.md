# Scene-Designer: Learning Design Intelligence for Agentic 3D Scene Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23121) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23121)


## One-sentence takeaway
Scene-Designer combines consolidated construction trajectories, scene-ranking supervision, and Critic-guided GRPO to improve indoor scene generation and correction efficiency, with evidence strongest within its evaluated room types and constructed preference tests.

## Problem and motivation
Language-driven indoor scene generation requires usable object relationships and coherent layouts alongside geometric validity. The authors argue that general-purpose models make inefficient corrections, while static scene datasets omit the construction and critique experience needed to improve these decisions (§1).

Their proposed gap is therefore process supervision: learning both how to construct and repair scenes and how to distinguish better designs.

## Approach and contributions
- **Construction experience:** Scene-OTA-35K contains 1K scenes and 35K Observation–Thought–Action steps generated using GPT-5.2 and the SceneSmith scaffold. Causal Trajectory Consolidation combines state/error screening, model-based operation assessment, manual review, and replay verification to remove ineffective steps while preserving final scenes (§3.1; Appendix D.2).
- **Critique experience:** Scene-OTC-15K contains approximately 15K ranking examples built by perturbing accepted scenes. Rankings follow degradation strength for two construction strategies; applying the same perturbation to different objects requires manual annotation. GPT-5.2 supplies reasoning traces (§3.1; Appendix D.3).
- **Critic-guided optimization:** Joint full-parameter SFT of Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct produces Scene-Builder. Scene-GRPO then trains a policy using a frozen copy as Critic: sibling rankings reward better alternatives, parent–child comparisons reward improvement, and auxiliary rewards encourage valid calls and efficient, complete construction. Optional inference guidance ranks four candidate branches per editing step (§§3.2–4; Appendix C).

The results support improvements over the SFT model and stronger ranking performance on Scene-OTC. They do not establish universal superiority over baselines, agreement with human design preferences, or lower end-to-end inference cost. Reward ablations reportedly degrade performance when components are removed, but numerical ablation values are unavailable in the supplied text (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no substantive author-stated limitations discussion.

**Analyst inferences grounded in the evaluation:**

- **Limited coverage and uncertainty reporting:** Main quantitative generation tests use generic room prompts and 10 scenes per method per room type. Tables 1–2 provide no uncertainty estimates; detailed instructions appear mainly in qualitative examples (§4; Appendix E).
- **Preference validity:** Perturbation severity supplies many ranking labels. Performance on these constructed comparisons does not establish agreement with independently collected human preferences (§3.1; Appendix D.3).
- **Split independence is unclear:** OTC derives from OTA scenes, but scene-level separation between training and critique tests is not described (§3.1).
- **Efficiency evidence is incomplete:** Fewer corrective moves coexist with evaluating multiple branches. Wall-clock latency, rendering cost, and total inference compute are unreported (§4.1).
- **Mutual enhancement is unevenly demonstrated:** Adding OTA quantitatively improves critique, while critique supervision’s benefit to SFT generation is illustrated qualitatively rather than through a reported aggregate comparison (§4.3).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Trajectory-shortening statistics differ, and Table 5’s tier numbering runs opposite to the surrounding prose’s severity ordering (§3.1; Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **Does critique transfer beyond synthetic degradations?** Current tests use constructed preferences. Next experiment: evaluate on scene-disjoint, unperturbed generated scenes with independent human rankings.
- **Does guidance improve quality at equal compute?** Branching gains lack cost measurements. Next experiment: compare guided selection with unguided sampling under matched tool, rendering, and latency budgets.
- **Does critique supervision reliably improve generation?** Evidence for this direction is qualitative. Next experiment: compare OTA-only and OTA+OTC policies across held-out instructions using geometric metrics and blinded human assessment.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported comparisons, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes scene-disjoint split details, human preference agreement, correction-case counts, generation-score uncertainty, numerical reward ablations, full accessibility results and sample size, end-to-end compute costs, and confirmed release contents. Several figures and tables appear only as captions or partial text.
