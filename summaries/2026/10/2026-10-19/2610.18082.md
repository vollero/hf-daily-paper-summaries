# Failure Recovery via Belief Updates and World Modeling for Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18082) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18082)


## One-sentence takeaway
BeliefWeaver combines persistent evidence across failed attempts with prospective repair reasoning, reporting a 5.1 percentage-point aggregate final-score gain over native continuation on three terminal benchmarks (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents can discover evidence of an early mistake only after many subsequent actions. Local reflection and rollback may preserve experience without determining how that evidence should change priorities across related repair hypotheses. The authors target this gap: making recovery cumulative rather than repeatedly retrying similar failures (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent recovery controller:** A graph retains repair hypotheses (“frontiers”), attempts, observations, and checkpoints across rollback. Relations determine which unary evidence factors affect each frontier’s priority; beliefs are independently normalized ranking scores, not calibrated success probabilities (§4.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Prospective reasoning with separate execution gating:** An implicit world model proposes hypotheses, generates diagnostic probes, judges evidence, predicts repair progress, and infers relations. A consistent diagnostic verdict and an unmet target determine eligibility; belief scores rank eligible candidates. Verified improvements are retained and other attempts rolled back (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Partial distillation:** A Qwen3.5-9B student learns four recurring roles through LoRA SFT and on-policy distillation using teacher labels on student-induced contexts. Prospective evaluation remains teacher-based; an additional variant also transfers hypothesis generation (§4.4; Appendix B).

The claimed methodological contribution is this combined recovery design; the paper does not establish independent benefits for every component.

These reported results support better retained performance under matched executor budgets. They do not establish lower total cost or wall-clock latency: controller overhead is excluded from execution-time metrics, and teacher-cost estimates exclude student serving and training (Appendix C). Student–teacher superiority is reported as a point estimate without an accompanying significance claim. Numerical table bodies and confidence-interval values are absent from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Incorrect IWM predictions or inferred relations can mis-rank candidates; the limited relation set may inadequately represent interacting causes (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Transfer beyond terminal tasks with verifier feedback and reliable restoration remains uncertain (Limitations).
- **Attribution limit:** Belief updates and evidence gating enter the ablation together, so their individual effects are unresolved (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Efficiency claims cover selected resource categories rather than complete deployment expense. Unmatched teacher usage records also contribute no token cost to estimates (Appendix C.2).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Referenced settings and training tables lack their numerical bodies in this retrieval, although substantial procedural detail is available.

## Open research questions
- **Which controller component drives the gain?** Beliefs and gating are jointly introduced (§5.2). A factorial ablation could independently toggle relational updates and diagnostic gating.
- **Can recovery survive imperfect restoration?** Reliable rollback is a stated scope boundary. Inject partial restoration failures and measure retained score, regressions, and recovery cost.
- **Would learned or uncertainty-aware priorities improve robustness?** Current beliefs use fixed factors and are explicitly uncalibrated (Appendix A.2). Compare them with learned updates under controlled probe and relation errors.
- **Do savings persist after full accounting?** Student serving, training, and orchestration are excluded from reported cost estimates (Appendix C). Measure total monetary cost and wall-clock progress for matched teacher and student runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline comparisons are clearly described, but numerical table bodies, confidence-interval widths, complete controller/training settings, exact task identities, repeated-run variability, total deployment costs, and public code/model availability are unavailable in the supplied text.
