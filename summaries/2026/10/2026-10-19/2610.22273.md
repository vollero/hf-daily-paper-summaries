# Librarian Agents: Training LLMs to Curate Long-Context Workspaces that Anticipate Future Queries

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22273) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22273)


## One-sentence takeaway
Training a builder agent with downstream answering rewards improves question answering from reusable, query-unseen document workspaces, with demonstrated accuracy gains but higher answer-time token use than an untrained builder (§2.4; Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Reactive retrieval repeatedly searches documents and reconstructs relationships for each query. Precomputed indexes can reduce this work, but the authors argue that predefined structures constrain what information they expose. The proposed gap is learning how to organize a corpus **before future queries arrive**, so later agents can reuse synthesized information while retaining access to original evidence (§§1–2.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Learned anticipatory workspaces:** A builder creates synthetic summaries, indexes, and hierarchical artifacts with provenance links. Raw documents remain accessible, and evaluation questions are hidden during construction (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Workspace construction as policy learning:** The builder chooses CreateFile, MergeFiles, Delete, or Stop. GRPO optimizes periodic answerability changes plus terminal answer correctness, with a frozen answerer. Although the formulation supports cost penalties, the primary experiment sets them to zero (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Evaluation of reuse and transfer:** Experiments compare retrieval, precomputation, and untrained-builder baselines; test repeated queries over shared corpora; and evaluate zero-shot transfer to BRIGHT-derived question answering (§2.4; Appendices A.2–A.5).

The trained Qwen3.5-4B builder delegates content generation to a fixed Qwen3.5-35B-A3B executor and history compaction to Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite; results therefore concern this composite system (Appendix A.7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher BrowseComp-Plus accuracy | On 50 held-out corpus tasks, Qwen answerer accuracy is 0.75 versus 0.42 embedding and 0.44 reranker; Gemini answerer accuracy is 0.77 versus 0.30 and 0.42, respectively. | §2.4, result (1); Figure 4 |
| Construction requires substantial tokens | Librarian accuracy/construction tokens: 0.77/178,926; untrained builder: 0.28/127,683; GraphRAG: 0.36/11,923. | Table 1 |
| Reused workspaces improve accuracy while increasing navigation | Across 443 synthetic questions over 50 corpora, trained versus base builder accuracy is 0.637 versus 0.242; answer-time tokens average 9,346 versus 1,238 per query. | Table 2; §2.4, result (2) |
| Accuracy-normalized reuse cost improves | At ten questions per corpus, trained/base recorded tokens per expected correct answer ratio is 0.662, paired-bootstrap 95% CI [0.504, 0.836]: 33.8% fewer for the trained builder. | Appendix A.4; Figure 6 |
| Zero-shot transfer improves BRIGHT-derived QA | On 150 questions across AOPS, Biology, and Stack Overflow with Qwen3.5-4B, average pass@1/pass@3 is 52.0%/66.8%, versus 20.8%/28.0% for Dense, the strongest baseline by those averages. | Table 3; Appendix A.2 |
| Organization affects navigation and accuracy differently | In a fresh matched run on 435 questions from 49 completed workspaces, original/flattened/shuffled accuracy is 55.86%/57.70%/51.72%; navigation tokens are 3,635/5,497/3,612. | Appendix A.5; Table 7 |

These results support useful learned curation under the tested settings. They do not establish universally lower inference cost: the trained builder’s workspaces require more answer-time tokens. The structure ablation supports a navigation benefit from hierarchy, while flattening preserves comparable accuracy. Transfer gains are uneven: Stack Overflow contributes particularly strongly to the BRIGHT average (Table 3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers static-corpus QA; document updates, large-context scalability, loss of rare details during synthesis, and deciding when to consult raw sources remain unresolved (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** Improved efficiency is conditional on reuse and accuracy normalization. Recorded token costs do not establish wall-clock or monetary savings (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference:** BRIGHT results evaluate a derived generation task, rather than the original retrieval task; the primary matched comparison covers three domains. Nine additional domains have only base-builder comparisons (Appendices A.2–A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text does not isolate periodic probes, executor choice, or provenance access through dedicated ablations. The full-system gains cannot identify their individual contributions.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix A.7 specifies a 60,000-token trajectory limit, while Table 1 reports 178,926 construction tokens; their accounting relationship is unexplained. Prompt bodies are absent from Appendix A.9.

## Open research questions
- **Can workspaces adapt efficiently to changing documents?** Static corpora leave maintenance untested (§4). Compare incremental edits with complete rebuilding after controlled document changes.
- **What accuracy–cost tradeoff does explicit cost training achieve?** Primary training disables cost penalties (§2.3). Sweep construction and navigation penalties under matched budgets.
- **When should an answerer bypass synthesized memory?** Provenance recovery is illustrated qualitatively, without a systematic fallback evaluation (§4; Figure 7). Test controlled detail omissions and learned fallback decisions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central method and reported comparisons, but leaves material gaps: actual prompt contents, detailed baseline configurations, complete token accounting, training compute and duration, human validation of semantic judging, and confirmed availability of the prospectively announced releases.
