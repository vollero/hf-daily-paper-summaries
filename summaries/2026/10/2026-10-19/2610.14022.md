# OPMD: On-Policy Mechanism Distillation Beyond Output Supervision

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14022) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14022)


## One-sentence takeaway
OPMD trains teacher-informed hidden states to work with a frozen student, then internalizes them on student-generated prefixes, improving reported mathematical reasoning accuracy over output-only distillation across three teacher–student settings (§§3–4.1).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation addresses training–deployment mismatch by supervising student-generated prefixes, but output matching leaves intermediate computation only indirectly constrained. The authors argue that direct hidden-state matching also falls short: geometrically aligned teacher representations may be unusable by the student’s remaining layers. Their activation-patching experiments motivate optimizing transferred states for downstream usefulness before distilling them (§1, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Student-compatible teacher construction.** Freeze teacher and student backbones; train layer-wise projectors that inject teacher hidden states as residual updates into the student. Optimize the resulting output distribution with full-vocabulary reverse KL, or response SFT when tokenizers differ (§3.2, Appendix A.1).
2. **On-policy internalization.** Train a native student’s LoRA parameters to match the constructed teacher’s hidden states on student-generated prefixes. The on-policy experiments use only this latent-state loss during Stage II. Deployment requires neither teacher nor projectors (§3.2).
3. **Extensions and behavioral analysis.** Evaluate domain-routed multi-teacher distillation, cross-family SFT with additional mechanism supervision, and layer-weight patching. The distinctive methodological contribution is constructing functionally useful targets before representation matching; representation supervision itself is acknowledged as prior work (§§2, 4.2–4.3).

These results support useful intermediate supervision in the evaluated settings, including gains without a Stage II output loss and without logit KL in cross-family SFT. They do not establish general recovery of a teacher’s reasoning mechanism. The authors’ broader “mechanism transfer” interpretation is supported by interventions and descriptive route changes, but not uniquely demonstrated by accuracy gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Larger models, broader architectures, and tasks beyond mathematics and code remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated interpretability limits:** The two route-analysis problems were selected for the full model’s advantage. Masked reannotation does not remove selection bias; annotations lack a human gold standard and establish neither general layer specialization nor statistically significant route differences (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst assessment:** Checkpoint curves average repeated generations from fixed checkpoints, rather than independent training runs. They therefore do not establish robustness to training randomness (Appendix B).
- **Analyst assessment:** The direct-MSE ablations support instability in their specific configurations, not universal inferiority of representation matching. Their training configurations differ, and one ablation uses a shorter AIME generation cap than the main evaluations (Appendix D).
- **Evidence availability:** Most numerical table bodies and figure coordinates are absent from the supplied text. Several narrative table references conflict with captions. Code is promised upon acceptance, rather than established as released (§4).

## Open research questions
- **How broadly does compatibility-first distillation generalize?** The evaluation is limited to selected models and math/code tasks. Next experiment: compare OPMD and OPD across additional architectures, larger students, and non-math/code tasks under matched total compute.
- **Which design choices drive the gains?** The supplied experiments do not isolate transfer-layer selection or supervision-layer coverage. Next experiment: vary those choices while holding training budget and projector capacity fixed.
- **Are route changes systematic and causally informative?** Two selected problems and model-generated annotations cannot establish general specialization. Next experiment: preregister a larger problem sample, obtain blinded human route labels, and perform controlled layer interventions.
- **How should construction and internalization budgets be allocated?** Appendix C finds teacher-dependent sensitivity without identifying an optimum. Next experiment: sweep Stage I/II allocations under a fixed total time or FLOP budget across independent training seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several comparisons are explicit, but the supplied text lacks most complete result tables, plotted values, uncertainty estimates, independent-training-seed results, exact projector architectures and transfer-layer mappings, and a released-code location. These gaps limit verification of aggregate rankings, reproducibility, and precise cost advantages.
