# Pack Light, CarryOn: Building Object-Oriented Context for Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18784)


## One-sentence takeaway
CarryOn replaces older agent interactions with maintained records of reusable task progress and remaining obligations, improving reported task scores while reducing token usage on computer-use and browsing evaluations (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents repeatedly process histories describing progress already embodied in documents, application state, or established findings. The authors argue that summaries and reconstructed progress records can duplicate this state and drift from it. Their proposed gap is context management that supports direct reuse of task objects while preserving their conditions of use and unresolved requirements (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Object-oriented context:** CarryOn combines versioned continuation carriers, complementary execution context, and a live interaction tail. Carriers represent external objects, source-supported information products, or state tied to a particular environment (§3.2).
- **Incremental maintenance:** A Tracker identifies candidates; evidence preparation and applicable read-only Grounder checks establish compilation inputs; a Compiler updates affected carriers and execution context together. Unaffected records persist (§3.3).
- **Validated folding:** Successful updates atomically replace covered history in active context while preserving the append-only interaction log. Validation checks consistency and authorization, explicitly **not task correctness** (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Same-host comparisons, summary baselines, and component-masking ablations test performance, resource use, and the complementary roles of carriers and execution context (§§4.2–4.5).

The strongest evidence is the authors’ same-host comparisons: observed task performance improved while recorded model resource use fell. Ablations support complementary information roles, but do not isolate every runtime mechanism. Carrier persistence establishes record continuity, not semantic accuracy. No uncertainty estimates or significance tests are supplied.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Explicit methodological boundary:** Grounding and validation safeguard consistency; they do not establish task correctness. Situated-state reuse also depends on returning to the relevant environment (§3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** All new host-agent experiments use Claude Opus 4.7. Browsing covers a sampled subset with snippets and no full-document retrieval; portability to other models and retrieval interfaces remains untested (Appendix A.3; §4.1).
- **Analyst inference—causal isolation:** Component ablations hide records from the host while retaining internal runtime state. They do not separately measure the benefits of grounding, versioning, evidence binding, or trigger scheduling (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and curve interpretation:** External OSWorld models use differing action interfaces, and Figure 3 averages over each method’s surviving trajectories at each turn. These comparisons offer weaker causal evidence than the same-host results (Appendices B.2, C.1).
- **Reporting limits:** Several table bodies are absent from the supplied rendering, including detailed resource accounting, check types, settings, and prices. Selected runtime prompts contain omissions (Tables 2, 4–8, 11–12, 14).

## Open research questions
1. **Does CarryOn transfer across host models?** Only one host is tested. Repeat matched baseline/CarryOn evaluations with other hosts while holding interfaces and budgets fixed.
2. **Which maintenance mechanisms drive the gains?** Existing masking ablations do not isolate runtime safeguards. Compare matched versions disabling grounding, incremental versioning, or evidence binding individually.
3. **How reliably does reuse survive environment changes?** Reuse conditions are recorded, but no dedicated state-change stress test is reported. Alter files or invalidate sessions after folding and measure stale reuse, recovery, and task success.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported comparisons clearly, but uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variability, several detailed table bodies, complete runtime prompts, and implementation-release availability are unavailable or not established.
