# AVC-Reward: Event-Centric Audio-Video Consistency Evaluation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22321) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22321)


## One-sentence takeaway
AVC-Reward evaluates audio-video consistency through event-level verification, with reported gains in human agreement and generator optimization, although missing result-table values limit assessment of its overall advantage.

## Problem and motivation
Generated clips can combine concurrent events, physical sounds, narration, music, and valid off-screen sources. The authors argue that clip-level similarity and predefined synchronization rules can miss local errors or penalize legitimate asymmetric relations, motivating an evaluator that checks each event according to its role (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Event-centric metric:** Independently analyze audio and video, verify events in both cross-modal directions, then score global narrative harmony, event presence/timing, and fine-grained attributes. The default overall score averages these three dimensions (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Trainable evaluator:** Initialize from Qwen3-Omni-Thinking; apply SFT on approximately 8k structured reasoning examples, followed by GRPO on 10k human-calibrated examples. Supervision combines expert-model annotations, human failure labels, and automatic label-to-score calibration (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Benchmark and optimization validation:** Introduce AVC-Benchmark for human-alignment evaluation across audio-driven video, video-to-audio, and joint generation; replace DeSync with AVC-Reward within OmniNFT to optimize LTX-2 (§§4–5.2). Asset release availability is not established by the supplied text.

The supplied evidence supports benefits on the evaluated clips and LTX-2 optimization setting. External metrics and blinded preferences provide evidence beyond the optimized reward itself. It does not establish universal superiority, the overall evaluator effect size, or statistical reliability: several central tables contain only captions, and uncertainty estimates are unavailable.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged annotation error:** Expert-model annotations can accumulate perceptual errors; automatic score rewriting retains a gap to human agreement (§3.3; Appendix B.3).
- **Author-acknowledged tradeoff:** Stronger audio-video optimization can reduce text alignment (§5.2; Table 3(b)).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confound:** Generator experiments adjust learning rates and reward weights alongside reward replacement, limiting attribution exclusively to AVC-Reward (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—calibration scope:** PLCC uses a logistic mapping fitted on the evaluation subset, so it measures fitted agreement rather than unseen-data calibration; rank metrics avoid this fitting (Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** RL validation uses one generator/framework, and benchmark sources cover a specified model pool. Broader transfer remains untested in the supplied text (§5.2; Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism remains partly unresolved:** Reported ablations compare training stages and score aggregation, but do not isolate event disentanglement, directional verification, or audio-role reasoning (§5.5).
- **Human subjectivity:** Mean pairwise Jaccard agreement is 42.3% for consistency and 44.3% for overall quality in the generator preference study; unanimous-subset results concern a selected subset (Appendix F.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which protocol components drive improvement?** Training-stage ablations do not isolate individual verification mechanisms. Remove audio-role classification and each verification direction separately, holding data and training budget fixed.
- **Does reward utility transfer to other generators?** Only LTX-2 within OmniNFT is tested. Repeat optimization on another generator with matched reward-weight and learning-rate sweeps.
- **Can consistency improve without losing prompt alignment?** Table 3 shows text-alignment declines. Sweep consistency and text-alignment reward weights, then evaluate both metrics and blinded human preferences.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and generation metrics are well described, but overall efficacy is only partly quantifiable.

Material missing information includes numerical contents of Tables 2 and 4, full preference results, several configuration tables, confidence intervals or significance tests, runtime/compute costs, complete prompts, and confirmed availability of code, weights, and benchmark data.
