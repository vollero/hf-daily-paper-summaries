# GlobeBench: Evaluating Text-Based World Model Capabilities for Scalable Environment Simulations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22708) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22708)


## One-sentence takeaway
GlobeBench exposes substantial failures in predicting execution-grounded environment responses, but its hardest long-history audits confound history length, output length, and effect breadth, preventing causal conclusions about those factors.

## Problem and motivation
Text-based world models could supply scalable environments for training agents, but inaccurate simulations may reward agents for exploiting simulator errors. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that attribute failures to specific simulation skills rather than score predicted observations only as a whole (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Skill-focused benchmark:** 1,632 items across five domains, organized into four capabilities and 19 primary skills; each pairs a visible trajectory and final action with an execution-derived reference (§3; Tables 4–7).
- **Construction and diagnostics:** Seeded trajectory operators, pinned backends, 12 quality gates, and annotations for reach-back and impact radius support analysis of difficult item families (§3; Appendices A.3–A.5).
- **Hybrid evaluation:** Deterministic comparisons settle provable matches or contradictions; unresolved responses go to a semantic judge. The authors claim a standalone deterministic error-class check as a specific novelty (Appendix B.3; Table 10).
- **Evaluation assets and findings:** The authors report releasing items, harness, outputs, judge records, and reproduction instructions, alongside comparisons of general-purpose and specialized models (§§2, 4–6; reproducibility statement).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Leading model remains below half correct | Across all 1,632 items, GPT-6 Astra scores 49.57% task success versus Grok 4.6 at 28.13%; refusals count as failures. Astra’s item-bootstrap 95% CI is 47.06–52.02%. | Table 1; Appendix B.1 |
| Specialized models trail the leader | Qwen-AgentWorld scores 14.46%; DWM scores 5.21% on the same benchmark and grader. | Table 1 |
| Wrong values dominate answered failures | Of 22,431 failures with usable answers across 20 models, 53.21% contain a contradicted value, 22.95% are structure-only, and 23.84% have no comparable state fields. | Tables 2, 23 |
| Long-history items are harder in aggregate | Pooled success is 42.28% on 408 items within 40 turns versus 11.44% on 1,192 items beyond 40 turns. Within shared operators, the difference is +1.76 percentage points, 95% CI −1.57 to +5.17. | Tables 14, 17 |
| Low L3 performance depends on label provenance | Pooled success is 5.10% across 977 L3 items. However, 953 have operator-declared labels and score 3.92%; the 24 execution-measured L3 items score 51.88%. | Tables 8, 14 |
| Many items remain unsolved | No evaluated model solves 631 of 1,632 items; 87.6% of those unsolved items are L3 and 93.7% have reach-back beyond 40 turns. | Tables 19–20 |
| Human audit supports gold quality, with limited hard-item coverage | On 80 shared audit items, State Coherence averages 5.00/5 with 100% pair agreement. Only six of the 78 released anchor items are L3. Humans rated golds, not model predictions. | Table 3; Appendix C.1 |

The results establish difficult benchmark families and substantial errors under the reported serving and grading setup. They do **not** establish that longer histories or broader effects independently cause failure: these properties correlate with operators, skills, and answer length. The human gold audit also does not directly validate response-grader accuracy; separate boundary-case adjudications provide narrower evidence for that purpose (Appendices B.4, C.1, D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Each item tests one transition after a fixed prefix, so rollout error accumulation and downstream training reliability remain unmeasured (§7).
- **Authors’ stated scoring limitation:** Whole-state binary scoring fails a long answer for one incorrect value, obscuring partial correctness (§7; Appendix D.10).
- **Construction confounds:** Most L3 labels are declared rather than measured; some cross-file labels explicitly do not describe the action’s actual state change. Measured-radius items show no steady accuracy decline with radius (Appendix A.4).
- **Audit coverage:** Human anchors underrepresent long L3 audits. The full-corpus LLM audit flags items for review, but the supplied text does not report completed manual resolution of that queue (Appendices C.1–C.2).
- **Serving and grading uncertainty:** One primary response per item leaves sampling variability outside the reported confidence intervals. Refusals and recovery settings affect comparisons; judges share a provider with several evaluated models, although judging is blinded (Appendices B.1–B.4).
- **Missing attribution experiment:** Qwen-AgentWorld’s base model is not evaluated, so the results cannot isolate the benefit of its specialized training (§6.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does history length independently reduce fidelity?** Current long audits lack matched short counterparts. Generate paired items varying filler distance while holding operator, state dependencies, radius, and answer length fixed (Appendix D.3).
- **How do errors compound during simulation?** Fixed-prefix evaluation cannot answer this. Compare multi-step model rollouts with backend execution, tracking state divergence and agent outcomes (§7).
- **Does specialized world-model training help under matched conditions?** The base-model comparison is absent. Evaluate a specialized model and its base with identical prompts, budgets, and decoding (§6.2).
- **How reliable is grading on difficult audits?** Human coverage is sparse and judge comparisons used an earlier response prompt. Adjudicate a stratified sample of hard-audit predictions under the frozen final grader (Appendices B.4, C.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported descriptive results; **low** in causal interpretations of reach-back or impact radius.

Material missing evidence includes repeated-response variability, matched long-versus-short audit experiments, rollout and downstream-training evaluations, specialized-model base ablations, completed review of audit-flagged items, and a representative human validation of the final response grader. Release availability was not independently checked.
