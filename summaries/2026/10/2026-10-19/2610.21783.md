# Learning to Think and Explore in 3D World

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21783) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21783)


## One-sentence takeaway
Act3D combines trajectory supervision with reinforcement learning to acquire useful camera views for indoor 3D question answering, reporting 53.2% average accuracy across six benchmarks versus 48.6% for its supervised initialization (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Under partial observability, answering a spatial question requires deciding which visual evidence to acquire as well as interpreting it. The authors identify a gap in methods that receive predetermined scans, images, or videos: these settings largely bypass learning how to obtain missing evidence (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interactive formulation:** A VLM controls a camera in a reconstructed scene, receiving one rendered image after each action and conditioning on its accumulated history. The scene remains external to the model; relative movements and a terminal answer form the action interface (§3.1).
- **Two-stage policy training:** GPT-5.4 demonstrations initialize Qwen3.5-9B’s reasoning and interaction protocol, followed by GRPO over complete rollouts (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Evidence-acquisition reward:** Training combines semantic correctness, exact match, output format, and correctness-gated interaction rewards. Annotated object visibility can grant full interaction credit. Thus, RL supplies no target action sequence, but it is **not answer-only supervision** (§3.3).
- **Empirical analysis:** Comparisons examine cross-domain transfer, exploration demand, observation interfaces, action parameterization, and supervised initialization (§§4.2–4.8). The distinctive contribution is their integration into a learned exploration-and-answering policy; the text does not establish that its individual components are new.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged scope:** Camera movements perform no collision checking and can pass through geometry (§3.1). **Analyst inference:** These results do not establish physically executable robot navigation.
- **Reward attribution:** Ground-truth object locations inform rewards, and interaction credit can accrue even without observing the target (§3.3). **Analyst inference:** Increased interaction frequency alone cannot demonstrate better information acquisition.
- **Confounded initialization comparison:** The authors explicitly state that RL-only and SFT-initialized settings differ in backbone and training budget (§4.7), weakening causal conclusions about SFT’s necessity or efficiency.
- **Comparison differences:** Passive baselines retain their own input and inference protocols (§4.1), so aggregate rankings do not establish matched computational efficiency.
- **Reporting gaps:** Numerical table bodies, uncertainty estimates, and repeated-run results are unavailable in the supplied text. The stated universal Qwen3.5-9B setup also sits uneasily with §4.7’s backbone-difference caveat.

## Open research questions
- **How much benefit requires annotated grounding?** The final reward uses object locations (§3.3). Next experiment: compare answer-only, interaction-only, and grounding-assisted rewards under matched training budgets.
- **Does the policy transfer to physically constrained exploration?** Current actions can traverse geometry (§3.1). Next experiment: evaluate collision-aware movement with reachable viewpoints and movement costs.
- **Does SFT accelerate learning under controlled conditions?** Existing comparisons differ in backbone and budget (§4.7). Next experiment: compare initialization strategies with identical backbones, rollout counts, and compute.
- **Can useful exploration be achieved with fewer calls?** Additional interaction improves some domains but harms others (§4.8). Next experiment: sweep interaction costs and budgets, measuring accuracy against calls and latency.
