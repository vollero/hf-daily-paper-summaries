# DuET: Note-Frame Dual Encoding for Piano Performance-to-Score Transcription

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21467) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21467)


## One-sentence takeaway
DuET combines note-level and frame-level encoding to improve piano MIDI-to-score transcription, reporting a substantial MUSTER error reduction on Gangqinpu and a small improvement on ASAP (§5.1).

## Problem and motivation
Expressive performance timing must be mapped to score timing using tempo and metrical context. Notes carry score attributes, but beats can occur without note onsets, making note-only metrical prediction incomplete. Sequence positions also fail to represent actual temporal and pitch distances. The authors additionally identify limited popular-piano coverage in prior neural PM2S evaluation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dual encoding:** Coupled note and piano-roll encoders exchange information through bidirectional cross-attention. An autoregressive decoder alternates between their memories, jointly learning score attributes and auxiliary frame-level metrical targets (§3.2).
- **Musical positional encoding:** TimeRoPE supplies a shared performance-time coordinate across branches; TPRoPE encodes relative time and pitch within note attention (§3.3).
- **Evaluation and assets:** The authors evaluate classical and popular-focused repertoire, curate Gangqinpu, and report providing inference code, pretrained weights, and a demo. Gangqinpu’s public release is planned rather than established (§§1, 4).

The model preserves input note identities and pitches while predicting timing, staff, voice, meter, key, and grace-note attributes. Transkun v2 and MuseScore are existing pipeline components, not the methodological contributions (§3.1).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest reported aggregate gain is on Gangqinpu | On the common-valid test subset of 79 performances, MUSTER mean error falls from the strongest baseline’s **6.56% to 3.64%**; that baseline is not identified in the supplied prose. | §5.1; Table 2 referenced |
| ASAP aggregate improvement is small | On 59 test performances, MUSTER mean error is **11.22%**, versus **11.30%** for the published Beyer–Dai Transformer result. | §5.1; Table 2 referenced |
| Timing gains are more consistent than note recovery | Authors report lowest onset, offset, duration, and staff errors on both datasets, but higher ASAP missing/extra-note errors than several baselines. Exact component values are unavailable. | §§1, 5.1 |
| Frame branch improves aggregate transcription error | Removing it raises MUSTER mean error from **8.68% to 10.50%** on 39 ASAP performances and **3.07% to 3.64%** on 72 Gangqinpu performances. | §5.2; Table 3 subset |
| Pitch-aware encoding helps with alternating memory | Time-only → Time–Pitch RoPE lowers MUSTER mean error from **13.68% to 8.68%** on the ASAP ablation subset and **3.57% to 3.07%** on Gangqinpu; single-memory results are mixed. | §5.2; Table 3 subset |
| Masking and joint training have qualified benefits | Removing either masking mechanism worsens aggregate PM2S error on both datasets. Dedicated trackers outperform full DuET on Gangqinpu beat/downbeat F1; checkpoint selection differs. | §5.2; Tables 3–4 referenced |

These are author-reported results. They support improved PM2S timing in the tested settings, but do not establish statistical significance, universal architectural superiority, or end-to-end audio-to-score accuracy. Quantitative predictions bypass MuseScore post-processing (§5.1). Main and ablation results use different subsets and should not be compared directly.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Explicit scope restriction:** Fixed correspondence excludes note insertion, deletion, and many-to-one ornament reduction; pitch is copied (§3.1). **Analyst inference:** DuET cannot directly correct these upstream MIDI errors.
- **Author-reported interactions:** Pitch-aware encoding and alternating memory do not consistently improve all settings or duration metrics; joint training does not uniformly improve beat tracking (§5.2).
- **Comparison caveats:** ASAP baselines come from prior published results, while tracker and score-model checkpoints use different selection metrics (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Unexplained common-valid subset exclusions limit assessment of selection effects. Gangqinpu construction and release remain deferred (§§4–5).
- **Coverage:** Evaluation remains piano-specific; broader styles and improved downbeat accuracy are stated future work (§6). The qualitative example establishes neither accuracy nor general readability (§5.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does metrical supervision itself improve transcription beyond frame context?** The frame-removal ablation changes both representation and supervision. Retain both encoders while separately disabling beat, beat-index, and phase losses.
- **Why do pitch encoding and decoder memory interact?** Benefits reverse across some settings (§5.2). Repeat the encoding-by-memory comparison across seeds and stratify by rhythmic and pitch structure.
- **How robust is DuET to imperfect MIDI?** Fixed correspondence prevents note and pitch correction (§3.1). Evaluate controlled onset, pitch, insertion, and deletion perturbations, then compare with an editable-note decoder.
- **How well do results generalize across styles?** Broader evaluation remains future work (§6). Test held-out styles with genre-stratified transcription and downbeat metrics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main findings, but Tables 1–4 contain captions without their data bodies.

Material missing information includes full metric and ablation values, numerical beat/downbeat F1, uncertainty estimates and repeated-seed results, common-valid exclusion criteria, detailed Gangqinpu construction and split counts, and runtime/model-size measurements. Code and weight availability are author claims and were not independently checked.
