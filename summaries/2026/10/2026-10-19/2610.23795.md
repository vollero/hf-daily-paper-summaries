# Persistent Planar Memory for Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23795) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23795)


## One-sentence takeaway
PPMem feeds an accumulating pool of predicted 3D planes back into video diffusion, improving camera-controlled generation and depth consistency on the reported benchmarks while keeping persistent scene geometry compact (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Long video generation needs spatial memory to preserve previously generated scene structure as the camera moves. The authors argue that existing geometric memories lack some combination of compactness, explicit surfaces, cross-view consistency, and refinement over time. They propose planes as a structural abstraction suited particularly to built environments (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent planar memory:** Each generated chunk predicts oriented rectangular planes. Geometric filtering, deduplication, replacement, and pruning maintain a global pool whose rendered depth conditions later chunks (§3.1; Algorithm 2).
- **Geometry integrated with video diffusion:** A Wan 2.2 backbone gains an adapted planar head, a VAE-initialized feature decoder, and Depth Token Fusion (DTF), which combines depth tokens with geometry features (§3.2).
- **Separate video and geometry passes:** Camera-controlled video training precedes geometry training with the backbone frozen. Inference adds a geometry-adapted pass after video denoising, producing depth, normals, and exportable planes (§3.3; Appendix A.3).

The claimed novelty is the generation–plane-memory–conditioning loop; planar reconstruction and several architectural ingredients are adapted from prior work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong short-context video results | On DL3DV at 121 frames, PPMem versus Gen3C: PSNR **13.98 vs 13.59**, LPIPS **0.464 vs 0.565**, CLIP **0.863 vs 0.798**, DINOv2 **0.769 vs 0.585**. Evaluation uses 100 held-out scenes and each method’s native resolution/crop. | §4.1; Table 2 |
| RE10K improvements include a tie | At 49 frames on 100 held-out RE10K clips, PPMem versus Gen3C: PSNR **22.46 vs 21.93**, LPIPS **0.168 vs 0.238**, CLIP **0.965 vs 0.925**; DINOv2 ties at **0.949**. | Table 2 |
| Long-context advantage is reported | On DL3DV sequences of 300–400 frames, authors report the highest CLIP/DINOv2 similarity throughout and approximately **0.27** DINOv2 advantage over the next-best method at frame 300. | §4.3; Figure 3 |
| Memory helps within the same generator | On 56 indoor scenes at 288×512 over three chunks, joint planes versus no persistent memory improve AbsRel **0.272→0.210**, depth-threshold accuracy **64.0%→70.3%**, and PSNR **14.58→14.91**. | §4.4; Table 3 |
| DTF’s ordinary benchmark gains are mixed | In that indoor ablation setting, concatenation plus DTF versus concatenation alone gives AbsRel **0.210 vs 0.212**, but depth-threshold accuracy **70.3% vs 72.3%**. With scale augmentation, DTF improves scale-response natural-log RMSE **0.293→0.131** relative to augmented concatenation. | Table 4; Figure 5(b) |
| Persistent geometry is small, but additional state matters | At a long-rollout snapshot, **106K planes occupy 4.8 MiB**, versus **1.7 GiB** for a matched accumulating per-pixel point cloud. Maximum simultaneous non-model state is **41 MiB**; geometry modules add **1.1 GiB** of fixed BF16 model state. | Appendix A.3 |
| Performance depends on planarity | Across 100 DL3DV scenes at 121 frames, low/high planarity groups have PSNR **12.98/15.27** and AbsRel **0.289/0.195**; planarity–PSNR Spearman correlation is **0.33**. | Appendix B.3; Table S4 |

These results support the reported system advantage and a useful contribution from persistent memory. They do not isolate planes as the sole cause of superiority: complete systems differ in backbones and rendering resolutions, while memory substitutions are inference-only. The headline three-orders-of-magnitude primitive comparison uses a theoretical fully accumulated dense cloud, rather than Gen3C’s actual bounded buffer (Table 1). Cross-view consistency remains an author characterization supported by indirect metrics and qualitative examples, rather than a dedicated consistency measure.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Organic scenes may lose detail; unseen extreme camera trajectories can degrade quality; frozen video weights prevent end-to-end co-adaptation; dynamic planes and open-world scaling remain future work (§5). Fine text becomes garbled during zoom-in (Figure S2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Native resolutions, crops, different backbones, and inference-only memory swaps limit causal attribution to the planar representation (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Analyst inference—geometry validity:** Supervision uses estimated depth/normals aligned to estimated reconstructions; independent sensor-based metric validation is not reported (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—coverage:** Long-context testing is restricted to DL3DV, and controlled architectural ablations use a smaller indoor setting. The supplied text does not establish uncertainty across repeated runs (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—memory accounting:** Small plane storage does not imply similarly small total model memory, as the appendix explicitly distinguishes fixed geometry modules and temporary state (Appendix A.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Would a hybrid plane–point memory help organic scenes?** Planarity predicts performance, and the authors identify organic detail as a weakness. Compare hybrid and plane-only memories within the same generator across planarity strata.
2. **Can memory represent scene changes?** Dynamic planes remain future work (§5). Test moving objects and changing surfaces for stale geometry, deletion errors, and temporal consistency.
3. **Does joint video–geometry training improve long rollouts?** The frozen-backbone design leaves co-adaptation unexplored (§5). Compare staged and joint training under matched compute and rollout settings.
4. **How robust is initialization outside the evaluation protocol?** Benchmarks supply first-frame depth, whereas deployment permits monocular estimates (§4.1; Appendix A.3). Perturb initial depth and scale, then measure error propagation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial methods, comparisons, and ablations, but does not establish code/checkpoint availability, training hardware and total compute, timing hardware, repeated-run uncertainty, independent sensor-depth validation, or performance beyond the reported rollout lengths and datasets.
