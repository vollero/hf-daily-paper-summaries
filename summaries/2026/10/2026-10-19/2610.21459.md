# Can Visual Generation Expand What Unified Multimodal Models Know?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21459) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21459)


## One-sentence takeaway
In controlled fictional-fact experiments, image-generation training enables limited text-only recall, while interleaved image–text alignment improves average recall on entities without answer supervision across all three tested models (§§4.1–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Image-training targets can contain facts omitted from their captions. The authors ask whether unified multimodal models can retrieve those facts through language after learning to generate images. Strong generation and understanding scores alone do not establish this transfer: a model may reproduce an attribute visually yet fail to name it (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled probing protocol:** Knowledge Transfer Probing (KTP) introduces fictional entity–attribute–value associations through images while withholding target values from captions. It contains 50 entities per category across seven categories and 8,552 image-training examples; evaluation asks for attributes without an input image (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Transfer characterization:** Experiments on BAGEL, SenseNova-U1, and an in-house GLM-Omni compare correctness in generated images with correctness in textual answers (§§3.3, 4.1).
- **Alignment interventions:** Half the entities receive additional textual QA supervision, with interleaved alignment additionally connecting generated images, visual interpretation, and answers within responses. Evaluation uses the other entities, which still receive image-generation training. An image-loss ablation and Visual CoT evaluation examine contributing signals and remaining access barriers (§§4.2–4.4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Direct transfer is uneven | SenseNova-U1 achieves 68.0% text-only accuracy on **size**, reportedly above its random-guessing baseline; BAGEL and GLM-Omni generally perform at or below category baselines. Exact baseline values are absent from the supplied text. | §4.1; Table 1 discussion |
| Visual reproduction can greatly exceed verbal recall | Under direct transfer, SenseNova-U1 reaches **88% visual accessibility versus 0% textual accessibility on number**. | §4.1; Fig. 3 discussion |
| Textual alignment does not consistently improve transfer | Mean textual accessibility rises for BAGEL and SenseNova-U1 but falls for GLM-Omni. All three score **0% on number and text** for entities without alignment supervision. | §4.2; Table 2 discussion |
| Interleaved alignment improves mean recall across models | Mean text-only accuracy exceeds textual alignment for every tested model. For GLM-Omni, **shape rises from 12% to 64%** and **spatial from 0% to 40%** on test entities; some other tasks decline. | §4.3; Table 2 discussion |
| Image loss contributes to interleaved alignment gains | With GLM-Omni, masking image loss within interleaved examples reduces seven-task mean test-entity QA accuracy from **26.86% to 13.71%**, retaining the sequences, text supervision, and standalone generation data. | §4.3, Further experiment 1; Fig. 5 |
| Generated images at inference can unlock additional answers | After interleaved training, **number** accuracy increases from **0%** with direct textual answering to **4% BAGEL, 36% GLM-Omni, and 20% SenseNova-U1** with Visual CoT. | §4.3, Further experiment 2; Table 3 discussion |
| Correct generation still does not ensure correct recall | Under interleaved alignment, textual correctness is positively associated with generation correctness for all models, but textual accuracy conditional on correct generation remains **below 50% for each model**. | §4.4; Table 4 discussion |

These results support the authors’ claim that some image-supplied associations become accessible through language, and that alignment on other entities can improve access. They do not establish broad acquisition of natural-world knowledge, causal architectural explanations, or reliable verbal retrieval whenever generation succeeds. The association analysis is observational, with generation and QA evaluated separately (§4.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified scope:** Natural visual data, isolated architectural effects, and alternative alignment and inference strategies remain future work (§5).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Test entities are unseen in alignment examples, but are included in image-generation training. The results concern access to trained associations rather than learning facts about entirely unseen entities (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—architecture comparisons:** Models differ in architecture, visual representation, and data sampling ratios, preventing attribution of performance differences to architecture alone (§3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation validity:** Visual correctness relies on a VLM and permits edit-distance tolerance for some rendered text. The supplied text gives no human-validation or judge-reliability results (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—ablation scope:** The image-loss ablation supports its contribution for GLM-Omni; corresponding ablations for the other models are not reported (§4.3).
- **Reporting gaps:** Table captions are present, but table bodies are missing, limiting inspection of complete comparisons and training settings.

## Open research questions
1. **Does transfer extend to natural visual facts?** The study uses fictional synthetic associations. A next experiment could repeat KTP with independently verified natural-image attributes withheld from captions (§§3.2, 5).
2. **Which architectural choices improve accessibility?** Current cross-model comparisons vary multiple factors. A controlled experiment could vary modality sharing while matching training data, sampling, and optimization (§3.3; Appendix A.2).
3. **What makes interleaved supervision effective across models?** Only GLM-Omni receives the reported image-loss ablation. Repeat it across models and separately remove visual-observation text while matching supervision budgets (§4.3).
4. **When does Visual CoT recover otherwise inaccessible facts?** Gains coexist with persistent failures. Evaluate whether success tracks intermediate-image correctness and measure the additional inference cost (§4.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The experimental design and selected findings are explicit, but the supplied text omits table bodies containing full results, attribute ranges, sampling ratios, and hyperparameters. It also does not provide repeated-run uncertainty, judge-validation results, detailed compute costs, or sufficient information to establish reproducibility of the in-house GLM-Omni model.
