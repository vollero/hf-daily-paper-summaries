# BizBench: Unveiling Hidden Challenges in Deploying Long-Horizon Agents for Real-World Business

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20424) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424)


## One-sentence takeaway

BizBench shows that agents can complete payment-workflow goals while violating intermediate business requirements, and that even its strongest evaluated models remain inconsistent across repeated executions (§§5.2, 5.5).

## Problem and motivation

Business agents must obtain specific approvals, preserve resources, meet deadlines, and select applicable policies from current and historical guidance. Final outcomes can conceal earlier violations, such as temporarily breaching a reserve requirement before restoring the balance. The authors identify a gap between evaluations focused on task completion and the execution validity required for deployment (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Business-grounded benchmark:** 50 tasks reconstructed from one payments company’s workflows, with synthetic records, 789 documents, and 601 discoverable business capabilities. Tasks connect operations through shared funds, deadlines, and commitments (§§3.1, 4.2).
- **History-sensitive evaluation:** Deterministic checks require goals, process requirements, and protection requirements to pass jointly. Checks inspect states and execution histories without requiring a particular reference sequence (§3.3).
- **Construction and validation:** Reference executions establish feasibility; alternative valid executions, targeted violations, condition changes, and expert review test task and evaluator behavior (§§4.4–4.5).
- **Empirical diagnosis:** Model comparisons, information ablations, repeated runs, and trajectory analysis distinguish failures in information acquisition, execution compliance, reliability, and tool efficiency (§5).

The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation framework; the paper does not introduce a new agent-training method.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest reported models still fail many tasks | On the same 50 tasks, Claude Opus 5 achieves 64.0% strict success with one run per task; GPT-6 Sol averages 61.3% across three runs per task. | §§5.1–5.2; Table 1 description |
| Repeated-run reliability is lower | GPT-6 Sol succeeds in all three attempts on 40.0% of tasks; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash does so on 26.0%. | §5.2; Figure 2(a) |
| Customer information materially changes performance | DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash strict success rises from 43.3% in the default condition to 50.0% with applicable documents supplied and 70.0% with full customer disclosure on the 50 tasks. Required approvals remain. | §5.3 |
| Goal attainment can conceal process failure | Across DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash’s three-run evaluation, five runs reach goals but fail full criteria: four temporarily breach reserve floors; one makes a rejected pre-approval execution attempt. | §5.5 |
| Tool use substantially exceeds reference executions | DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash averages 191.3 assistant calls per run versus 111.8 in Golden traces; database queries and document access account for much of the excess. | §5.4 |
| Retrospective call caps remove otherwise successful runs | DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash falls from 65/150 strict successes without a cap to 40/150 at twice each task’s Golden call count and 1/150 at 1.2 times that count. | §5.4; Table 3 |
| Simulator prompt results are suggestive, not decisive | With DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash in both roles on 50 tasks, protocol violations fall from four tasks to none after prompt revision; paired exact McNemar p=0.125. | §5.6; Appendix D.2 |

These results support the value of checking execution history and show substantial difficulty in this reconstructed environment. They do not establish live-deployment failure rates. Rejected premature attempts count as process failures under the benchmark; they should not be interpreted as completed unauthorized transfers. Full disclosure also removes simulator interaction, so its gain does not isolate customer-fact availability alone (§5.3; Appendix D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-described scope:** Confidentiality requires rewriting sensitive rules and reconstructing workflows with synthetic records; some constraints are introduced during task construction (§§3.1, 4.1–4.2).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Tasks from one company’s payments setting do not establish performance across other businesses or human customers.
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Unequal repetition counts limit direct reliability comparisons. The supplied text provides no confidence intervals for model rankings (§5.1).
- **Author-stated reference caveat:** Golden traces establish feasible strategies, not minimum-length solutions. Their call counts therefore do not define optimal budgets (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—budget validity:** Retrospective truncation measures which recorded runs fit a cap, not how agents would adapt when told a budget beforehand (§5.4).
- **Evidence gap:** Table bodies and plotted values are missing from the retrieved text. Section 5.3 also points to Table 3 for information ablations although Table 2’s caption describes them.

## Open research questions

- **Can structured customer elicitation reproduce the disclosure gain?** Full disclosure changes both information availability and interaction. Compare staged fact disclosure, structured questioning, and default dialogue while keeping the simulator fixed (§5.3).
- **Can explicit obligation tracking prevent intermediate violations?** Reserve breaches and premature attempts remain observed failure modes. Test a state-and-approval tracking agent against the default agent using identical checks (§5.5).
- **Can agents adapt efficiently to known budgets?** Existing caps are retrospective. Run prospective budget-aware evaluations and measure strict success alongside information-access calls (§5.4).
- **Does benchmark performance transfer to broader operations?** The environment reconstructs one company’s workflows. Evaluate matched tasks from additional organizations and compare simulated with human customer interaction (§§3.1, 4.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the central findings, but complete result tables, detailed model configurations, simulator prompts, numerical timeout/error thresholds, evaluator code, validation coverage counts, and public asset-access details are unavailable.
