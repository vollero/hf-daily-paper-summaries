# Belief-Trajectory Energy: Measuring the Path to a Prediction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05114) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05114)


## One-sentence takeaway
BTE measures changes between layerwise token predictions, providing a scorer-dependent signal for difficulty and text provenance whose strongest results concern supervised peer-review detection and attribution (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Final-output statistics discard how a Transformer’s predictions change across layers. The authors seek a compact, interpretable measurement of this trajectory that can characterize inputs without requiring a task-specific high-dimensional predictor. Their central distinction is between input properties defined independently of a model and properties measured through a particular model’s learned behavior (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory statistic:** Apply the scorer’s final normalization and vocabulary head to intermediate residual states, then compute Jensen–Shannon divergence between consecutive predictive distributions. Average over response tokens to obtain a depth profile (§2, Eqs. 1–5).
- **Theoretical interpretation:** Show that local BTE approximates belief-weighted logit-update variance under Fisher–Rao geometry, and that cumulative revisions retain information unavailable from initial-to-final displacement alone (§2.1; Appendix A.1).
- **Readouts and applications:** Use a fixed middle-layer scalar for training-free difficulty measurement; use depth profiles and richer token statistics with logistic regression for review detection and source attribution (§3).
- **Training analysis:** Examine how BTE emerges during pretraining and changes after targeted mathematical fine-tuning (§4).

The logit-lens projection is adopted from prior work; the proposed contribution is measuring and using successive predictive changes (§§2, 5).

| Cross-family transfer is strong, but cross-generation transfer is weaker | Qwen shape-only BTE reaches 0.995 original / 0.994 rewrite macro-AUROC with generator families held out. Legacy-only training yields 0.891 original macro-AUROC for Qwen full profiles and 0.742 for Llama. | Table 1, Settings B and A |
| Token statistics improve attribution | With eight possible output classes, Qwen accuracy on **generated reviews** rises from 91.3% using means to 95.6% using richer features; Llama rises from 86.4% to 93.2%. Test papers are held out under full coverage. | Tables 2, 13; Appendix A.5 |
| Pretraining changes BTE without establishing strong difficulty alignment in OLMo | OLMo middle-layer BTE rises from 0.013 at initialization to 0.093 at the final checkpoint; final difficulty correlation is only 0.03, with 95% CI [−0.06, 0.12]. | Table 17 |
| Targeted fine-tuning lowers BTE selectively | Mathematical fine-tuning reduces middle-layer BTE by 22% on MATH-500 versus 17% on GSM8K, reversing their ordering. | §4, Figure 6 |

These results support useful trajectory features in the tested settings. They do not establish universal difficulty measurement, unrestricted provenance detection, or a causal link between BTE and review quality. The headline attribution accuracy measures generated-review predictions across eight classes; human recall is reported separately in Table 14.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Scorer capability, training exposure, and the particular response being scored affect BTE; cross-benchmark difficulty orderings can reverse (§§3.1, 6).
- **Length dependence:** Token averaging does not remove all length effects. Difficulty associations attenuate substantially after length adjustment, although review-detection performance changes little under the tested linear residualization (Appendix A.6).
- **Transfer failures:** Legacy-only detectors can perform poorly on newer generators. Possible LLM assistance in nominally human reviews also makes provenance labels uncertain, particularly for recent conference-years (§3.2; Appendix A.3).
- **Comparison caveats:** Setting C changes training volume and coverage together. Open-Anchor uses a smaller test subset, and several baselines use adapted implementations, limiting direct comparison (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Review results establish transfer within this peer-review corpus, not across arbitrary genres, languages, or adversarial edits.
- **Analyst inference:** Increasing OLMo BTE magnitude is stronger evidence of learned trajectory structure than of learned difficulty sensitivity, given its near-zero final correlation. Comparisons with Llama also change model and training conditions (Table 17).
- **Analyst inference:** The local geometric derivation does not establish that intermediate readouts are calibrated beliefs or that BTE uniquely explains internal reasoning (§2.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Can BTE separate difficulty from solution presentation?** Length controls substantially reduce correlations. Compare length-matched, content-equivalent solutions with controlled reasoning detail and wording (Appendix A.6).
2. **How robust is provenance detection outside peer review?** The reported corpus is domain-specific and legacy-only transfer fails for some generators. Test frozen detectors on new genres, generators, and controlled paraphrases (§3.2).
3. **Can low BTE reliably indicate training exposure?** Fine-tuning supports exposure sensitivity, but scorer training mixtures remain unknown. Randomize exposure to matched held-out distributions and measure dose-dependent BTE alongside task accuracy (§4; Appendix A.3).
4. **How much does the predictive projection matter?** The method uses the standard output head at every depth. Compare alternative intermediate readouts under identical downstream splits to test whether findings persist beyond this projection (§2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and within-setting results; broader interpretations remain less certain.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes confirmed code/data release contents, BTE inference runtime and memory costs, uncertainty estimates for headline detection and attribution results, comprehensive generator prompting details, and scorer training mixtures.
