# Recognition Is Not Grounding: Benchmarking Multilingual Financial Evidence Extraction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19432) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19432)


## One-sentence takeaway
MultiFinGround shows that eight reference models recognize accounting concepts with 97.3–99.4% accuracy yet omit target facts in 26.6–41.9% of correctly recognized groups under independent, matched-context extraction tests (§5.1).

## Problem and motivation
Multilingual financial comparison requires recovering every relevant numerical fact, including comparative-period values, rather than merely identifying the accounting concept. The authors identify a gap in existing evaluations: recognition and evidence completeness are rarely assessed together on matched multilingual contexts, making omissions difficult to detect (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark construction:** MultiFinGround links independently authored FY2025 reports from 90 issuers across English, French, and Greek using IFRS concepts and traceable iXBRL annotations. Experts audit a stratified subset (§§3.1–3.2, 3.8).
- **Paired evaluation:** Concept Recognition (T2a) and Exhaustive Fact Selection (T2b) use the same contexts in separate calls. T2b receives the gold concept, allowing omissions to be measured independently of recognition errors. Fact Identification (T1) and label-free Concept Discrimination (T2c) provide complementary diagnostics (§§3.3–3.6).
- **Empirical analysis:** Fifteen models are evaluated across evidence-language, taxonomy-label, distractor, and financial-specialization conditions; this is a benchmark and evaluation contribution, without a proposed extraction model (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition frequently coexists with omissions | Eight API/open-weight reference models achieve 97.3–99.4% T2a accuracy; among correctly recognized groups, 26.6–41.9% miss at least one T2b target across the three languages. Evaluation uses 521 matched groups and strict scoring. | §§5–5.1; Figure 2; Table 5 description |
| Exact extraction remains harder | Best reported reference-model T2b scores are 90.4 concept-macro F1 and 71.8% exact-set accuracy; the supplied prose does not identify the winning models. | §5 |
| Evidence recovery varies across languages | On T2a-correct groups, Gemini’s T2b recall is 81.7% English, 82.2% French, and 75.1% Greek. Greek has the lowest recall for seven of eight reference models. | Appendix D.1; §5.1 |
| Label-free discrimination exposes additional variation | Reference-model T2c accuracy ranges from 35.6–95.1%; DeepSeek scores 98.3% on T2a but 35.6% on T2c. The tasks also differ in evidence and structure. | §§5–5.2; Figure 4 |
| Recognition withstands harder distractors | Reference-model T2a accuracy remains 94.4–98.7% with four harder options and 89.4–97.5% with twenty; lexical overlap scores 49.1% and 36.3%, respectively, on the same items. | §5.6; Table 11 description |
| Financial specialization offers mixed benefits | All four specialists underperform their bases on T1, T2a, and T2c; Fin-R1 gains 9.9 concept-macro F1 points on T2b. | §5.5 |
| Audit corrections have limited observed score impact | Experts review a stratified 10% per task; original-to-corrected reference-model score changes on audited subsets are at most 1.4 percentage points across the task-specific metrics. | §3.8; Table 12 |

The evidence supports a behavioral gap between recognition and complete selection under this protocol. It does not establish that recognition causes extraction success or failure: calls are independent, and extraction receives the gold concept. Complete coverage permits false positives, so precision and exact-set accuracy remain necessary (§4.1). Language differences and specialist–base differences are observational, rather than isolated causal effects.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated annotation limits:** Most annotations retain issuer-derived tags; only the sampled subset receives expert corrections. Untagged amount-like numbers remain unscored in T1 (§3.8; Limitations).
- **Author-stated coverage limits:** Three languages, one reporting regime, and mostly table-based primary-statement concepts restrict generalization. Language covaries with issuer, sector, and document style (Limitations).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Localized labels also remove concept identifiers; T2a and T2c differ beyond label availability. Compute is unequal, and budget exhaustion or empty outputs materially affect some scores (§§4, 5.2–5.6).
- **Statistical limits:** Shared issuers create dependent T2 items; no T2 confidence intervals are reported, and item-level significance tests are explicitly exploratory (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Because T2b supplies numbered tagged facts within selected contexts, its results do not establish end-to-end extraction performance on whole reports (§3.5; Appendix G).

## Open research questions
1. **What drives omissions as target counts grow?** The observed association does not isolate count (§5.1). Next experiment: vary target count while controlling context length, language, and concept.
2. **How much of the recovery gap is attributable to language?** Issuer and document differences confound current comparisons. Next experiment: evaluate professionally aligned versions of the same disclosures.
3. **Does label-free recognition explain discrimination failures?** T2a and T2c also differ structurally (§5.2). Next experiment: hold excerpts and response structure fixed while varying candidate-label availability.
4. **Can completeness-focused inference reduce omissions?** The paper evaluates stateless calls without tools (§4). Next experiment: compare one-pass selection with verification passes under matched compute, reporting precision, recall, and exact-set accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the central descriptive finding; lower in causal interpretations.
