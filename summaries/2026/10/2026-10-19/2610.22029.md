# Selection Is Not Learning: Choosing Model Futures

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22029) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22029)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors show that apparently learnable tasks can produce harmful updates, and report that independently confirming successors while retaining experience from rejected models improves self-evolving language models (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Self-evolution changes both a model and the tasks generated for later rounds, allowing regressions to accumulate. The authors identify a **selection-to-update gap**: pre-training pass rates, disagreement, or task validity do not establish whether training will improve the resulting model (§1).

Whole-model acceptance also bundles gains with losses. The paper asks whether useful experience can survive even when its donor’s parameters should be rejected (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Diagnose and formalize the gap.** Audits distinguish task-selection optimism from realized learning. A theoretical construction shows that pre-update behavioral observations excluding gradients cannot universally identify an update’s value (§5; Appendix C.1).
2. **HeirCommit.** Freeze a completed successor, compare it with the current model on fresh confirmation questions, and inherit it only for strictly positive measured gain. The reported implementation uses a zero threshold; positive thresholds support the paper’s conditional error guarantees (§§4.3–5).
3. **EvoHeir and Experience Recovery.** Explore branches, screen a parameter recipient, then repair its failures using current and archived references while preserving its successes through distillation. Confirm the completed result; archive qualified experience regardless of acceptance (§4).
4. **Separate donor quality from experience value.** Fixed-recipient interventions test whether weaker or rejected models can nevertheless provide useful references (§6.4).

The claimed methodological distinction is the separation of parameter inheritance, experience inheritance, and final confirmation—not update acceptance or distillation alone (§2).

## Evidence and main results
All results below are author-reported; pp denotes percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Task admission is unstable under fresh sampling | Under independent responses, 52.2% of Agent0 and 42.0% of R-Zero admitted tasks leave their native frontier. | §6.1; Figure 4a; Appendix D |
| Valid tasks can produce non-improving updates | 60/96 Skill-SP optimizer transitions on exact-valid tasks fail to reduce held-out correct-target NLL; this is not an accuracy result. | §6.1; Figure 4b |
| HeirCommit reduces harmful acceptance | Across 64 native updates per host, acceptance is 23–52%; harmful accepted updates fall from 47–77% to 13–16%, a 68–83% relative reduction versus random acceptance at matched coverage. Harm is assessed through NLL gain on a separate pool. | §6.2; Figure 4c–d; Appendix D |
| EvoHeir leads the reported benchmark averages | After ten rounds and three seeds, Full has the highest equally weighted Math and General averages on all four backbones. Math gains over Agent0 are 1.59–2.40 pp on the three base models and 0.55 pp on the instruction-tuned model; Full wins 45/48 benchmark–backbone comparisons. | §6.3; Table 1 discussion; Figure 5 |
| Weaker donors can provide useful experience | In 16 interventions on four fixed recipients, eight of ten donors weaker than the root improve the recipient through absorption; those ten donors average −1.09 pp donor gain and +0.71 pp absorption gain. Evaluation uses 1,024 items before confirmation. | §6.4; Figure 7a |
| Recovery improves the acquisition–forgetting balance | Using the same 64 references, Recovery averages +1.39 pp net correctness gain versus −0.54 pp for Cache SFT across four recipients: a 1.93 pp advantage. | §6.4; Figure 7b |
| Rejected-source history adds value | At 64 references, all qualified history yields +1.39 pp versus +0.73 pp for committed-round history. Matched source replacements add 0.34–0.73 pp at ages one to four, with pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals above zero. | §6.4; Figure 7c–d; Appendix F |
| Benefits depend on budget | On Qwen3-4B-Base, Full has the lowest mean at 0.8× its default accounting budget and the highest from 1.0× onward. Default Full costs 123.7 accounting units versus 100.0 for the native baseline. | §6.6; Figure 9; Appendix G |

These experiments support selective inheritance and experience reuse in the tested settings. They do not establish universally beneficial absorption, deployment-wide non-regression, or a measured hardware-efficiency advantage. The theoretical guarantee requires fresh, conditionally centered confirmation errors and positive margins; the empirical zero-threshold gate has no nontrivial bound from that theorem (§5; Appendix C.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Flawed references passing verification can misdirect Recovery; absorption can lose existing successes; sampling noise can admit harmful proposals under the zero threshold (Appendix B.3).
- **Author-stated:** Construction overhead can erase the advantage under tight budgets, and accounting units are not measured GPU-hours or FLOPs (§6.6; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Root-generated consensus pseudo-labels can favor agreement with root errors. Fresh responses address sampling reuse, but do not independently establish label correctness (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains bounded by four backbones and ten rounds; component ablations and equal-budget studies use only Qwen3-4B-Base (§§3, 6.5–6.6).
- **Evidence availability:** Several tables appear only as captions, and figure images are not transcribed, limiting independent inspection of exact scores and uncertainty.

## Open research questions
1. **How should confirmation thresholds balance progress and harmful acceptance?** Reported runs use zero thresholds, whereas guarantees require positive margins. Next experiment: compare calibrated margins at matched compute, measuring useful-update retention and harmful acceptance (§5).
2. **How robust is Recovery to verification errors?** The authors acknowledge that incorrect references can pass safeguards. Next experiment: introduce controlled reference and pseudo-label errors and measure acquisition, forgetting, and acceptance (Appendix B.3).
3. **Does rejected-source experience remain useful over longer horizons?** Matched history interventions examine ages one to four within a ten-round study. Next experiment: extend evolution and vary archive capacity and retention policy (§6.4; Appendix B.4).
4. **Does the budget advantage transfer across backbones?** Equal-budget evidence covers one backbone. Next experiment: repeat comparisons across the other backbones using measured GPU-hours (§6.6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main mechanism and reported findings, but full numerical contents of Tables 1–4 and plotted figures are unavailable. Code, checkpoint, and archive release availability is not established. Confirmation-label correctness rates, empirical calibration of theoretical error assumptions, and measured equal-hardware-cost comparisons are also unavailable.
