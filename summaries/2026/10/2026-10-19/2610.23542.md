# Execution Before Memory: Diagnosing Memory Failures in Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23542) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23542)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that missing actions can confound video-model memory scores, and that targeted prompt revision improves execution under score-aware selection without establishing that the model remembered hidden states (§§4–8).

## Problem and motivation

A failed video memory test can reflect an incorrect setup, an unexecuted action, an unreadable reveal, or an incorrect final state. Aggregate scores obscure these distinctions: a closed container cannot reveal whether its contents were remembered. The authors identify a need to check execution before attributing failure to memory (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **MIST evaluation:** Three successive shots establish a scene, hide the target state, and request its reveal. Separate setup, leakage, reveal, and inside-match measures distinguish missing execution from incorrect exposed states. Paired reveal comparisons lock the first two shots (§3).
- **Execution diagnosis:** The authors identify missing reveals on MIST and missing departure/return or state changes on evaluated WRBench and MBench-T clips (§§4, 7).
- **M3-Agent:** A VLM diagnoses visible failures, then revises the failed operation and final-frame evidence clause while preserving task identity and order. The generator remains frozen. Candidate selection uses the reporting scorer, making keep-best results oracle coverage estimates rather than a demonstrated deployable selector (§5).

These results support the authors’ diagnosis that execution and observability contribute substantially to the evaluated failures. They establish improved candidate coverage under target-aware prompting and score-aware selection. They do **not** demonstrate latent memory storage, a reliable scorer-free selector, or controlled superiority across generator scales (§8; Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated boundary:** Target states may appear in revised prompts, and target-withheld rewriting performs poorly. Successful output therefore does not establish memory (§8).
- **Oracle selection:** Best-of-K and official keep-best use the reporting scorer. Scorer-free selectors do not separate from baseline; individual revisions can damage correct clips (§§5, 8; Appendix A).
- **Evaluation scope:** Main results concern one generator family. Packed H3 experiments change generator and protocol jointly, preventing a controlled scale or architecture comparison (§8; Appendices A, C).
- **Judge validity:** No human labels were collected for the MIST last frames. Held-out judge agreement is affected by class imbalance and is weaker for action and official gates (Appendices C–D).
- **Missing comparisons:** Equal-budget VideoRepair and Video-T1 comparisons were not run (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The sharp difference between stitched Wan and packed H3 failure classes limits generalization of the closed-lid diagnosis beyond the tested configuration (§4; Appendix C).

## Open research questions

- **Can selection work without target or reporting-score access?** Existing scorer-free selectors fail to improve reliably. A next experiment could evaluate a frozen, target-blind temporal critic on held-out cases, with human scoring (§8; Appendix A).
- **Does persistence fail once execution is verified?** This paper does not establish stored state. A next experiment could condition evaluation on confirmed setup and action, withhold the target from reveal prompts, and vary the hidden interval (§8).
- **How much does stitching cause the observed failure pattern?** Packed H3 and stitched Wan differ jointly in generator and protocol. A factorial experiment using both generation formats within each generator could isolate those effects (Appendix C).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main findings and unusually explicit claim boundaries, but many tables contain captions without their underlying cells.

Material missing information includes complete task cards and rewrite templates, full official candidate budgets and selector results, detailed judge rubrics and agreement values, human validation, and confirmed code/data release availability. These gaps limit independent reproduction and assessment of selection effects.
