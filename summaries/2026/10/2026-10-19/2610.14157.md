# OpenMobile-2: Building Versatile Mobile Agents with Scalable Environments and App-Native Tools

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14157) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14157)


## One-sentence takeaway
OpenMobile-2 combines controllable commercial-style mobile apps, open interaction data, and hybrid GUI–tool training, reporting improved mobile-task success and transfer to physical devices, although missing table contents limit comparison auditing.

## Problem and motivation
Mobile-agent training needs diverse interaction experience, but proprietary trajectories, costly emulator rollouts, and unreliable app resets constrain open research. The authors identify a particular gap in controllable environments supporting rich commercial and cross-app workflows, alongside limited infrastructure for comparing GUI-only and app-native tool use (§1, §3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Expanded environment:** MobileGym++ adds 35 commercial-style replicas to a resettable simulator with persistent business state, raw-pixel observations, and cross-app sharing, payment, clipboard, and media workflows. It complements Android emulator apps (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Open data and training stack:** OpenMobile-Data-v2 contains 11,643 demonstrations, including 1,774 hybrid trajectories, plus 2,475 executable RL task goals. Models undergo supervised fine-tuning; subsequent RL uses 512 sampled tasks with programmatic rewards (§4, §5.1, Appendices A and D).
- **Shared-state hybrid interaction and benchmark:** More than 300 scoped app-native tools expose selected operations over the same state as the GUI. MobileGym++ Bench evaluates GUI-only and hybrid agents on identical tasks, initial states, and terminal checks (§§3.4–3.5).

These contributions extend the existing MobileGym framework; the simulator foundation itself is inherited (§3.1).

The reported results support useful training-data transfer and complementary benefits from app-native tools. The fixed-budget coverage experiment provides more direct evidence for application diversity than the aggregate training comparisons. However, the supplied text omits the actual result-table cells and uncertainty estimates, preventing verification of broad ranking claims or statistical reliability. Transfer to physical devices is demonstrated within SPA-Bench L3, rather than unrestricted deployment.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** App-native tools are an experimental interface with selected functionality; tool access alone provides limited or inconsistent gains (§3.4, §5.3).
- **Author-stated:** RL benefits concentrate in training-aligned environments, with limited improvements on other benchmarks (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference — training confound:** Hybrid SFT adds 1,774 demonstrations to the GUI-only mixture. Consequently, its advantage does not isolate interaction-channel training from additional data volume (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference — simulator validity:** Human refinement and transition checks support replica fidelity, but the text provides no quantitative fidelity assessment against live commercial apps (§3.2, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference — overlap assessment:** Instruction embedding similarity and exact-string checks do not rule out shared workflow structures or state configurations. The reported overlap analysis is therefore narrower than a full contamination audit (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference — reward interpretation:** RL rewards successful trajectories more when they include an app-native call, and trains with a relaxed verifier while reporting strict-verifier accuracy. These choices complicate attribution of tool-use behavior and RL gains (Appendix A.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does hybrid training help at equal data volume?** The hybrid mixture is larger. Train matched-size GUI-only and hybrid mixtures with controlled task coverage to isolate the effect of tool demonstrations.
2. **What makes RL transfer beyond the simulator?** Gains are limited outside aligned environments. Compare simulator-only and mixed-runtime RL at matched rollout budgets on held-out emulator and physical-device tasks.
3. **How robust are hybrid policies to tool-interface changes?** Unseen MCP results suggest transfer, but robustness to catalog changes is untested. Vary schemas, tool availability, and tool errors while holding task goals fixed.
4. **Which replica properties drive real-app transfer?** Fidelity is checked qualitatively and functionally. Evaluate matched replica/live-app workflows while varying visual fidelity and state-transition richness independently.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main design and numerical findings, but actual table bodies are absent.

Material missing information includes complete baseline comparisons, per-result seed counts and uncertainty, several training and evaluation hyperparameters referenced only in tables, detailed app inventories, quantitative replica fidelity, and independent verification of released artifacts.
