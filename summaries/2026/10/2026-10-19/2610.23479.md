# HexaShell: A Thin Harness for Exploring the Limits of VLM Agents as Robot Coding Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23479) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23479)


## One-sentence takeaway
HexaShell’s persistent robot tools and calibrated RGB geometry achieve a reported 39.7% success on RoboDojo’s weighted metric, but evaluation within one seed and weak insertion performance limit claims about generalization and physical competence.

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether a coding agent can improve robot manipulation by retaining executable repairs across episodes. Their identified gap is between agents using fixed robot primitives and agents that write, test, and preserve those primitives themselves. A central obstacle is converting RGB pixels into sufficiently accurate positions under a limited robot action budget (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Thin interface and persistent repair loop:** An acting agent controls a dual-arm robot through a command line. After failures, an optimizer using the same GPT-6-Astra model edits task tools and tests; the environment retries the layout. The harness and model remain fixed (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Geometry-based RGB measurement:** Tools combine calibrated rays, measured planes, and triangulation. Wrist-camera movements provide additional views and consume robot steps. The contribution is this integration and evaluation, rather than a new triangulation algorithm (§3.2).
- **Empirical capability map:** Frozen-tool evaluations compare perception variants, clutter robustness, physical interaction classes, and failure endings. The authors report releasing the evolved tools (§§4–6; Abstract).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported weighted benchmark score | Frozen RGB tools: 39.7% success and 48.0 progress on ten new layouts per task across 42 tasks; VPP2: 32.26% and 39.26 from leaderboard evaluation. Metrics equally weight five task types. | §§4.1–4.2; Fig. 2 |
| Aggregate advantage is uneven | Plain mean across 54 variants: 35.4% success versus VPP2’s 30.6%; HexaShell is higher on 23 variants and lower on 27. | §4.2 |
| Geometry outperforms the selected depth network | On 420 development-layout retests, triangulation improves weighted success by 8.7 percentage points over Depth Anything V2; discordant outcomes favor geometry 57–23, exact McNemar p<0.001. | §4.4; Table 1 discussion |
| Additional views incur measurable motion cost | Viewing consumes at least 3.6% of triangulation’s executed steps versus 0.7% for depth-network tools. On 180 layouts where triangulation moves a camera, successes are 39 versus 22. | §4.5; Table 2 |
| Performance varies strongly by interaction | New-layout mean success across task variants: pushing 92.5%, rigid manipulation 50.0%, tight insertion 8.3%; memory-labeled tasks reach 65.0%. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| Clutter robustness appears strong during development retests | Standard versus cluttered Generalization success is 35.0% versus 33.3%, retaining 95%; VPP2 reports 46.4% versus 12.6% under its leaderboard protocol. | §4.3 |
| Practical latency is substantial | Median retest duration is 739 seconds for 26 seconds of robot time; the benchmark does not charge model latency. | §4.1 |

These results support the usefulness of persistent tools and geometry within the tested setting. They do not establish a fully matched leaderboard comparison, cross-seed transfer, or real-world effectiveness. The camera-movement subset comparison also does not isolate the causal benefit of moving the camera.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Evaluation scope:** Tools are developed and tested on different layouts of seed 0; leaderboard policies use more episodes across three seeds (§4.1). **Analyst inference:** the reported ranking needs confirmation under matched evaluation.
- **Development assistance:** RGB runs inherit earlier depth tools; optimizer notes cover 25 tasks, and nine tasks initially include colleague-written RGB tools. This is not tool discovery from scratch (§3.4; Appendix D).
- **Protocol differences:** New-layout RGB retests receive task descriptions, score tables, development notes, and revised ending rules; development comparisons use bare instructions (§4.1). **Analyst inference:** differences across these evaluations cannot be attributed solely to tool quality.
- **Contact and feedback:** Authors identify poor insertion, pouring, and articulated manipulation, and note that preplanned tools can underperform stepwise correction on cloth (§5.1).
- **Audit inconsistency:** Appendix D says all seven audited tasks score 0/10 after replacing fixed geometry, while Table 5 reports nonzero new-layout RGB results for deposit coin and insert key. The supplied text does not reconcile the settings.
- **Causal attribution:** Authors acknowledge that success after a repair can also reflect different acting-agent choices or scene evolution (§6.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can feedback during contact improve insertion?** Current tools struggle to measure held parts and holes precisely (§5.1). Compare frozen preplanned tools against tools that repeatedly measure and correct during insertion.
- **Do gains transfer across seeds?** Held-out testing remains within seed 0 (§4.1). Freeze the existing tools and evaluate all official seeds under the leaderboard protocol.
- **How much improvement comes from autonomous repair itself?** Inherited tools and checker notes complicate attribution (Appendix D). Compare matched development budgets with and without inherited tools and notes.
- **When is another view worth its cost?** Viewing measurements are lower bounds and observational (§4.5). Randomize additional-view access under identical action budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported results, but broader conclusions face protocol differences and an unresolved audit discrepancy.

Material missing information includes Table 1’s numerical body, Table 7’s numerical body, matched multi-seed HexaShell results, real-robot evaluation, uncertainty estimates for headline scores, and controlled ablations separating repair, initialization, notes, and camera movement.
