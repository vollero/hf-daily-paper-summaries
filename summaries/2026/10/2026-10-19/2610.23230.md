# RAP: A Dataset for Paper-to-Poster Generation via Declarative Markup from Human-Crafted Exemplars

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23230) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23230)


## One-sentence takeaway
RAP pairs 25,435 scientific papers with human posters and reconstructed HTML/CSS, supporting template retrieval and model post-training that improve measured visual diversity and distributional resemblance to human designs (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Paper-to-poster generation combines scientific summarization, visual design, and code generation under tight space constraints. The authors identify a gap between existing poster collections and usable visual-coding supervision: prior resources lack either full-paper coverage, scale, or renderable markup grounded in human designs. RAP aims to make those designs reusable for generation and directly comparable through rendering (§1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset and benchmark:** 25,435 paper–poster pairs from NeurIPS, ICLR, and ICML, enriched with summaries and domain/task/method labels; a separate held-out benchmark contains 150 papers with human reference posters (§§1, 3, 5.1).
- **Automated derendering:** Layout detection, OCR-based font estimation, visual design captioning, and multimodal HTML reconstruction produce styled, addressable content slots. Figures, tables, and formulas can remain cropped image assets (§3).
- **PosterRAG:** A training-free pipeline retrieves templates through categorical, narrative, and aesthetic matching, fills their slots programmatically, and applies constrained visual repairs (§4.1, Appendix B.1.1).
- **Dedicated model training:** Paper–markup supervision supports SFT followed by GRPO reinforcement learning, combining DOM-based checks with VLM judgments of quality and reference fidelity (§4.2).

These results establish improvements in the paper’s visual metrics. Generation diversity measures compare outputs across different papers; FID/FDiD compare generated posters with the RAP collection as a distribution. They do **not** directly establish scientific accuracy, reader comprehension, or per-paper content completeness. Pairwise reference reconstruction is evaluated separately in Table 3 (§5.2, Appendix C).

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text has no dedicated limitations section. Relevant boundaries and analyst inferences are:

- **Domain restriction:** Data come from three ML conferences; only ICLR contributes 2026 posters. Generalization beyond these settings is untested (Appendix A.2).
- **Metric validity — analyst inference:** Distributional resemblance and cross-paper diversity may improve without better scientific communication. No quantitative reader study or factual-content evaluation is reported (§§5.2–5.3).
- **Reference-informed conditioning — analyst inference:** Training inputs select assets used in the target poster and derive layout/design instructions from that reference. Performance with ordinary user requests and independently selected assets remains unclear; baseline conditioning details are insufficient to resolve comparability (§4.2, §5.1).
- **Template rigidity:** Exact categorical filtering and fixed slot geometry constrain adaptation. No fallback for an empty categorical match, or separate ablation of narrative/aesthetic retrieval, is reported (Appendix B.1.1, §5.4).
- **Implementation ambiguity:** Claims of unchanged typography conflict with dynamic font adjustment after filling; reconstruction prompts also prohibit adding scaffold elements while requiring extra background elements. The precise invariants need clarification (Appendices B.2.2, B.3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Training sample counts, major optimization settings, compute costs, uncertainty estimates, reconstruction evaluation size, and detailed pair-verification procedures are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
1. **Do visual metric gains improve communication?** The evaluation lacks direct reader outcomes. Run blinded studies measuring comprehension and factual-error detection alongside the reported metrics.
2. **How dependent is post-training performance on reference-derived inputs?** Reference-based asset selection and layout specifications inform construction. Compare these with paper-only asset selection and independently written user instructions.
3. **Which retrieval stages matter?** The paper compares HTML with image guidance but does not isolate categorical, narrative, and aesthetic selection. Ablate each stage and test rare or unmatched taxonomy combinations.
4. **Can fixed templates handle substantially different content structures?** Current filling preserves geometry. Compare fixed-slot adaptation with controlled layout changes on papers with differing figure counts and section structures.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the dataset design, pipelines, and reported metric results. Confidence in broader quality claims is limited by missing human/content evaluations, uncertainty estimates, training and reconstruction-evaluation details, and clarification of reference-derived test conditioning and template-preservation rules.
