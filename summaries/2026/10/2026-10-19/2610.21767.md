# From Research Goals to Scientific Closure: Evaluating Agents Through Evidence-Supported Physics Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21767) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21767)


## One-sentence takeaway
PhysClosureBench finds that strong local performance does not ensure a complete, evidence-supported physics investigation: 36.2% of attempts scoring at least 8/10 still miss complete Research Closure (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a gap between evaluating correct answers or executable artifacts and evaluating whether an entire investigation supports its scientific conclusion. Agents must choose evidence, interpret results, explain mechanisms, and establish applicability limits; success on individual components can leave the original question unresolved (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** PhysClosureBench contains 50 expert-verified, published-study-derived physics tasks. Tasks specify goals, physical constraints, and resources while leaving investigation design to agents. Reference results were manually reproduced to verify feasibility and calibrate budgets (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Complementary evaluation:** Atomic Score sums 20 half-point criteria across Formulation, Output, Identification, Mechanism, and Validation/Scope. Research Closure independently assigns RC0–RC4 according to indispensable evidence and scope requirements; RC4 means complete closure (§3.3; Tables 2–3).
- **Comparative study:** Seven models complete three runs per task, producing 1,050 scored attempts. A staged GPT-5.6-Sol grading process, supplemented by selected expert checks, evaluates local accomplishment, closure, and repeated-run consistency (§4.1; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Complete closure remains uncommon for leading models | Over 150 attempts per model, Claude-Opus-5 leads with mean Atomic Score 6.29/10 and RC4 rate 32.7%; GPT-5.6-Sol achieves 5.83/10 and 22.7%; Kimi-K3 achieves 5.17/10 and 19.3%. Reasoning settings differ across models. | §4.1; §5.1; Table 12 |
| High scores can conceal unresolved central requirements | Of 229 attempts scoring ≥8/10 across all models, 83 (36.2%) fall below RC4; 55 remain at RC2. | §5.2; Figure 4 |
| Interpretation and mechanism distinguish high-scoring outcomes | Among ≥8/10 attempts, below-RC4 investigations leave 16.0% of applicable Identification and 20.8% of Mechanism criteria unmet, versus 2.1% and 3.0% for RC4. These are complements of mean attempt-level completion. | Figure 4B; §5.2 |
| Achieving closure once is easier than consistently | Across 50 tasks and three runs each, Claude reaches RC4 at least once on 50.0% of tasks versus all three times on 18.0%; GPT: 40.0% versus 10.0%; Kimi: 40.0% versus 4.0%. | §5.4; Figure 6 |
| Selected expert checks show imperfect grading agreement | Human–automated agreement is 81.8% across 192 effective selected criterion judgments; closure-relevant binary agreement is 60/79 (75.9%). These are criterion checks, not direct RC-label validation. | Appendix B.2 |
| Case analysis separates interpretation from applicability failures | Case 30 examples receive Kimi 10.0/RC4, GPT 9.5/RC3, and Gemini 4.5/RC2: respectively justified scope, unresolved high-field applicability, and misread computed trends. | §5.3; Table 11; Appendix E |

The evidence supports a descriptive gap between component completion, complete closure, and repeated-run consistency under this benchmark’s conditions. It does not establish what causes model differences or which intervention would improve closure. Atomic Score and RC also share task-specific scientific requirements, so their association is not independent validation of either measure.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. Relevant boundaries and analyst inferences are:

- **Scope, documented:** Tasks derive from published physics studies and concentrate on strong-field and neighboring domains (Figure 7). **Analyst inference:** Results do not establish performance on novel discovery, experimental research, or other sciences.
- **Grading validity, documented:** Expert checks deliberately select consequential criteria rather than a probability sample, and do not directly validate full RC assignments (Appendix B.2). **Analyst inference:** Aggregate grading error and its effect on rankings remain uncertain.
- **Score interpretation, documented:** Inapplicable criteria retain half-point Atomic credit but are excluded from functional completion; this credit does not represent completed work (Appendix B.1).
- **Comparison limits, analyst inference:** Models use different reasoning-effort settings; no budget or reasoning-effort ablation is reported (§4.1). Rankings therefore describe these configurations.
- **Reliability limits, analyst inference:** Three runs reveal variability but do not establish precise long-run closure probabilities. Confidence intervals are not reported (§5.4; Appendix C).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Detailed protocols and a repository pointer are supplied, but the text includes only one task specification and selected rubric criteria, rather than the complete benchmark assets (Appendix G).

## Open research questions
- **Would explicit interpretation and validation checkpoints improve closure?** High-scoring failures retain these gaps (§5.2). Next experiment: compare the existing agent setup with checkpoint prompts at matched resource budgets.
- **How sensitive are rankings to grading decisions?** Selected expert agreement is imperfect, and full RC-label agreement is unavailable (Appendix B.2). Next experiment: independently human-grade a stratified sample of complete submissions and recompute rankings.
- **Does additional compute improve consistent closure?** Budgets are calibrated for feasibility, but their effect is not isolated (Appendix A.2). Next experiment: vary wall-time budgets within the same tasks and models, with more independent runs.
- **Does the framework transfer beyond this physics distribution?** Current evidence is concentrated in related physics regimes (Figure 7). Next experiment: construct expert-verified tasks in another science and validate closure judgments independently.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported descriptive findings; **medium** in broader generalization and grading validity.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes complete task/rubric assets and submission traces, direct human validation of RC labels, uncertainty intervals, contamination analysis, and controlled budget or reasoning-effort ablations.
