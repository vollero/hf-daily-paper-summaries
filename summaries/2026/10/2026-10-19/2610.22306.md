# ARC-SORT: Adaptive, Robust, Compensated Multi-Object Tracking with Interacting Multiple Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22306) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22306)


## One-sentence takeaway
ARC-SORT uses motion uncertainty to adapt matching tolerance, revisits ambiguous identity assignments, and merges tracklets offline by appearance, reporting 81.82 HOTA on SportsMOT and 62.1 on DanceTrack without training new components (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Sports tracking must preserve identities through abrupt athlete motion, crossings, and camera movement. The authors argue that existing overlap-based trackers underuse two available signals: predictive covariance indicating unreliable motion, and assignment margins indicating ambiguous identity decisions. Long camera exits introduce a separate problem: extrapolated motion can wrongly prevent valid re-identification (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Uncertainty-adaptive matching:** An IMM filter combines constant-velocity, constant-acceleration, and coordinated-turn models. Camera-compensated positional uncertainty continuously controls per-track box expansion before IoU matching (§§3.2–3.4). The claimed novelty is this covariance-to-tolerance coupling, rather than IMM filtering itself.
- **Deferred identity commitment:** Small assignment permutation margins flag ambiguous pairs. Stored appearance prototypes and subsequent observations determine whether to exchange their identity labels, with one corrective swap allowed per episode (§3.6).
- **Appearance-only offline merging:** Tracklet prototypes link fragments across temporal gaps without a motion-extrapolation veto; temporal and appearance thresholds remain (§3.8).
- **Controlled mechanism evidence:** Identical cached inputs compare adaptive expansion against hard gating, fixed or switched expansion, anisotropic expansion, and distributional overlap estimates (§§3.7, 4.4).

“Training-free” means no new training: the pipeline uses pretrained detectors and appearance encoders (§4.1).

These author-reported experiments support useful association improvements and transfer across two benchmarks. They do not establish universal covariance sufficiency or general superiority over learned trackers. The full-system headline uses different inputs from some competitors; its shared-input result remains below GTA’s reported 81.0 HOTA (§4.2). Runtime prose does not clearly establish whether the approximately 50 FPS measurement includes detector inference and offline merging.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Deferral supports only pairwise, single-swap corrections; gains depend on the embedding stack. Long-horizon merging is offline, and results remain below trained specialists (§5).
- **Mixed correction audit:** Appendix A reports SportsMOT swaps as 11 verified-correct, none verified-incorrect, and 14 undecidable; DanceTrack has 11 correct, 24 incorrect, and 53 undecidable. Aggregate gains therefore do not imply reliable individual corrections.
- **Analyst inference—limited generality:** The covariance ladder is evaluated on SportsMOT validation data, with gains concentrated in basketball. Its “sufficient statistic” conclusion is empirical and restricted to the tested alternatives (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—appearance dependence:** Both correction and gate-free merging rely on discriminative embeddings; transfer to another encoder is evidence of some robustness, but does not establish performance under broader appearance degradation (§§3.6, 3.8, 4.5).
- **Reporting limits:** Several referenced tables contain captions without numerical bodies in the supplied text. Code is promised rather than supplied, and competitor runtime measurements use different hardware (§4.1; appendices).

## Open research questions
- **Can deferral handle larger crossing groups?** Pairwise, single-swap correction leaves this unresolved (§5). Compare it with bounded multi-track permutation hypotheses on annotated group crossings, measuring HOTA, correction precision, and latency.
- **When should motion remain useful during tracklet merging?** Gate removal works in the tested settings, but broader conditions are untested (§4.5). Stratify gaps by duration and camera motion, comparing appearance-only linking with uncertainty-conditioned motion constraints.
- **How robust are corrections to weak appearance evidence?** Embedding dependence and the mixed DanceTrack audit leave this open (§4.6; Appendix A). Hold detections fixed while varying encoders and appearance degradation, then measure swap correctness and association gains.
- **Does scalar uncertainty remain sufficient elsewhere?** The main ladder covers one validation benchmark (§4.4). Repeat the same controlled alternatives on additional tracking domains, including covariance-calibration checks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods and controlled comparisons, but independent verification is unavailable. Material missing information includes the promised code, numerical bodies of several referenced tables, complete runtime accounting for detection and offline merging, and evaluations beyond SportsMOT and DanceTrack.
