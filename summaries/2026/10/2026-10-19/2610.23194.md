# OfficeTown: Generative Organizations for Evolving Agent Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23194) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23194)


## One-sentence takeaway
OfficeTown generates executable professional tasks from persistent, permissioned organizational simulations, with reported evaluations showing incomplete frontier-model delivery and improved external-task performance after training on its data (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that isolated task generation misses how professional assignments depend on earlier artifacts, decisions, reviews, and unresolved questions. They propose simulating the organization that produces work, creating a continuing source of contextualized tasks and training feedback (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Organizational simulation:** Model-driven professionals coordinate asynchronously, delegate to assistants, review artifacts, and retain work records; permissions separately govern visibility and authority (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Executable task extraction:** Assignment-time snapshots preserve authorized inputs while excluding later solutions and evaluator-only rubrics, enabling independent task execution without replaying organizational history (§2.4).
- **Benchmark and training resources:** OfficeTown Bench contains 406 tasks and 4,836 criteria from 23 organizations; training resources contain 2,202 SFT demonstrations and 828 OPD environments (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Empirical validation:** The authors evaluate complete versus partial delivery, transfer from OfficeTown training, and a supplementary principal–assistant harness (§4). The distinctive contribution is their combination with continuing organizational work, rather than any individual delegation or distillation technique (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

These reported results support benchmark difficulty and training gains under the specified protocols. They do not isolate organizational persistence as the cause of those gains, establish real-workplace effectiveness, or demonstrate equal-cost harness superiority. Simulation review decisions are also distinct from independent benchmark grading (§§2.3, C.1–C.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Simulation fidelity, efficiency, and validation against real professional practice remain future work. Occupational taxonomies and generative models may encode biases (§6; Ethics Statement).
- **Review coverage:** Historical human feedback is bound to 394 of 406 retained tasks; the remaining 12 have model review only. Later adaptations were not newly human-reviewed (§B.2).
- **Scoring validity:** GPT-5.5 high judges benchmark outputs; expert rubric review does not establish automated-judge agreement with human graders. Designated evaluation failures receive zero, affecting some model comparisons (§C.1).
- **Separation uncertainty:** Separate task identities do not establish project-level or semantic isolation between resources (§B.1).
- **Comparison limits:** Harness budgets and token use differ; framework comparisons retain native prompts and tools. Budget curves summarize completed runs rather than experiments stopped at each budget (§§4.3, C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Without controlled ablations of history, permissions, review, and matched alternative training data, their individual contributions remain unresolved. The authors explicitly identify these mechanisms for future investigation (§6).

## Open research questions
1. **Which organizational mechanisms improve learning?** Their separate effects are unresolved (§6). Generate matched training sets with history, scoped access, or review individually removed, then compare external performance at equal training cost.
2. **Do gains survive stronger resource separation?** Task identities alone do not establish semantic isolation (§B.1). Repeat training and evaluation with organization- and project-disjoint splits plus documented similarity checks.
3. **How well do scores reflect professional usability?** Human agreement with the automated output judge is not established (§C.1). Independently grade a stratified artifact sample with domain experts and compare criterion judgments and rankings.
4. **Does the harness help under equal cost?** Current comparisons vary in budgets and token use (§4.3). Run paired tasks with matched total token or monetary budgets and repeated trials.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose provides clear methods and several numerical findings, but most table bodies are absent despite captions and references.

Material gaps include complete leaderboard and external-result tables, several optimization settings, judge–human agreement, independent-seed uncertainty, project-level separation evidence, simulation generation cost, and confirmed release contents.
