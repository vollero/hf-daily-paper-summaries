# PRISM: PRIvileged Sibling-guided Multi-Agent Topology Design

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20628) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20628)


## One-sentence takeaway
PRISM improves a query-conditioned multi-agent topology generator by distilling a teacher given successful sibling graphs during training, with evidence of benefits beyond matched constant-input distillation but limited evidence for query-specific crew matching.

## Problem and motivation
Execution-verified training corpora contain multiple successful collaboration graphs per query without ranking them. The authors argue that continued supervised fine-tuning (SFT) can favor common graph patterns while producing uneven downstream gains. Teacher forcing also leaves the generator without direct supervision on partial graphs arising from its own mistakes (§§1, 3).

The practical goal is to improve agent selection and communication structure using existing verified graphs, without additional multi-agent executions during post-training.

## Approach and contributions
- **Privileged sibling teacher:** Clone the pretrained topology designer and add a zero-initialized, low-rank FiLM branch encoding another successful graph for the same query. During teacher training, identical target graphs are excluded from sibling sampling; queries without siblings receive a zero vector (§4.1).
- **On-policy distillation with anchoring:** Sample graphs from the student, then match the frozen teacher’s node and edge distributions using exact categorical reverse KL, alongside supervised anchoring to verified graphs. Stage 2 fixes one successful reference per query and permits overlap with anchors or rollouts (§4.2).
- **Unchanged deployment:** Discard the teacher; the student retains ARG-Designer’s deployed architecture and receives only the query (§4.2).
- **Empirical diagnosis and controls:** Study SFT concentration, compare privileged inputs and divergence objectives, and test whether sibling information adds value beyond the distillation procedure (§§5–7; Appendices D, G, H).

| Crew intervention supports a narrow explanation | On 91 AQuA questions selected by SFT’s default behavior, reported majority-vote accuracy is 61.54% for SFT crews versus 77.66% for PRISM crews, with fixed chain wiring and three executions (McNemar p=0.0009). Random crews reach 75.46% and are not distinguishable from PRISM (p=0.61). | Appendix H; Tables 11–12 descriptions |

These results support improved performance within the evaluated execution stack and a contribution from privileged sibling content. They do not establish recovery of a ranking among successful graphs, superiority at matched total training compute, or a benefit from assigning PRISM’s particular crews to particular queries. Greater measured query dependence also does not reliably imply greater accuracy (Conclusion; Appendices G–H).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation uses one agent backbone and one communication round. Cross-backbone transfer and compute-matched post-training comparisons remain future work (§§5.1, 8).
- **Author-stated causal boundary:** Random and permuted crew controls recover much of the intervention gain; query-specific matching benefits are not established. The intervention subset is selected by SFT behavior (Appendix H).
- **Unequal training computation:** SFT matches the student’s epochs and learning rate, but PRISM additionally trains a teacher and performs sampling and rescoring (§5.1).
- **Incomplete sibling coverage:** Some training queries lack distinct successful siblings. Stage 1 uses zero inputs, whereas Stage 2 uses their sole verified topology (§4; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Almost vanishing edge divergence for text-based channels leaves the distinct contribution to communication-edge learning unclear (§7.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation repairs generated graphs, while distillation scores unrepaired trajectories; repair contributions to final gains are not isolated in the supplied evidence (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
1. **Does query-specific crew assignment help on representative questions?** The selected AQuA intervention detects no assignment benefit. Test original versus permuted and random crews on a representative held-out sample with matched wiring, size, and repeated executions.
2. **Does PRISM outperform alternatives at equal total training compute?** Existing SFT comparisons omit teacher and rescoring costs. Compare methods under matched wall-clock or compute budgets.
3. **Does the method transfer across backbones and collaboration rounds?** Evaluation fixes both. Repeat the teacher controls across different agent backbones and multiple-round execution.
4. **What drives gains beyond role selection?** Edge distillation provides little measured signal for text channels. Compare node-only, edge-only, and combined distillation while controlling evaluation repairs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, controls, and seed qualifications, but omits most numerical table bodies and plotted values. Material gaps include complete per-benchmark results, full corpus/split counts, evaluation sample sizes for most benchmarks, total training compute, exact MMLU training/evaluation separation, and repository contents. Figure numbering and cross-references are also inconsistent.
