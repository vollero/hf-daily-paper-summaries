# ReaDy: Recurrent States as Dynamic Context for Speculative Drafting in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20503) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20503)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReaDy adds position-specific reads of committed recurrent states to a DSpark drafter, achieving 3.16× mean decoding throughput over autoregressive decoding and 5.68% over a fine-tuned no-state control in the evaluated Qwen3.8-27B setting (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Feature-based speculative drafters prepare several future tokens from target hidden features, whose contextual emphasis may not suit every draft position. Querying token-wise KV caches offers additional context but incurs access costs that grow with history. The authors propose using hybrid linear attention models’ fixed-size recurrent states as complementary context that each draft position can query independently (§§1–2).

The theoretical motivation is deliberately limited: new queries can reveal information absent from existing readouts of the same state, but this does **not** prove that complete target hidden features are insufficient for drafting (§2; Appendix A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Position-conditioned state retrieval:** Each draft representation generates a learned query into selected committed target states. Retrieved features and state-scale information enter through a residual update before the final draft-layer MLP. DSpark’s parallel backbone, sequential prediction head, and verification rule remain in use (§3.1).
- **Efficient reader implementation:** Rank-eight query and value projections, shared state preparation across draft positions, fused gathering, and fused feature packing reduce the added execution cost (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Adaptation and empirical analysis:** Reader-only training precedes joint reader/LoRA adaptation. Experiments include a no-state fine-tuning control and ablations of rank, source layers, and injection placement (§§4.1, 4.4).

The authors claim priority for using HLA recurrent states as supplementary speculative-drafting context; the supplied text does not independently establish that priority (§2).

The evidence supports improved drafting efficiency in the tested configuration, including an advantage over continued fine-tuning without state access. It does not establish generalization across hardware, target architectures, sampling settings, or serving loads. Figure 1 averages per-benchmark speedups, whereas Table 1 computes speedups from mean TPS; their aggregation differs explicitly.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical limits:** The analysis concerns idealized state-query interfaces. It neither proves complete hidden-feature insufficiency nor recovers continuation-dependent future writes (Appendix A).
- **Author-acknowledged comparison scope:** Gains over pretrained DSpark combine changed adaptation and state access. Table 2 provides a more informative no-state control (§§4.1, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference—narrow deployment evidence:** Evaluation uses one target, one GPU type, greedy decoding, and concurrency one. Broader deployment benefits remain untested (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty in small differences:** No repeated-run variability or confidence intervals are reported, limiting interpretation of close architectural rankings (Tables 3–6).
- **Diagnostic boundary:** Prefix-survival curves pool different drafting rounds between methods and censor incomplete terminal rounds; they are not paired, conditional token-acceptance comparisons (Appendix E).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text omits adaptation-subset source proportions and several execution/training details, despite providing losses and optimizer settings (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Does the gain generalize to other HLA targets?** Only Qwen3.8-27B is evaluated. Repeat the state/no-state comparison across target architectures and sizes.
- **When does reader overhead outweigh better acceptance?** Serving load and hardware are fixed. Sweep concurrency, prompt length, and hardware while measuring decode TPS and total request latency.
- **Can state access be allocated dynamically?** The paper uses fixed source layers and capacity and identifies adaptive allocation as future work (§6). Compare learned position-dependent access against fixed readers at matched execution budgets.
- **How much does initialization constrain the result?** ReaDy adapts a pretrained DSpark checkpoint; training from scratch is left open (§6). Compare jointly trained state/no-state drafters under matched data and compute.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported configuration and numerical comparisons; broader applicability remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes benchmark sample counts and exact subset/version identifiers, training-subset composition and overlap, training cost and reader parameter count, detailed fine-tuning-control matching, software/kernel versions, repeated-run uncertainty, and performance under sampling or higher concurrency. The code release is author-reported and was not verified.
