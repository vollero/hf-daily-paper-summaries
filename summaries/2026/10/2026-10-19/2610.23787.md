# DLite: History-free Speculative Decoding for Agentic AI

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23787) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23787)


## One-sentence takeaway
DLite removes historical draft KV caches by reusing target-model hidden states, with reported inference gains on Qwen3 models, although missing result tables prevent verification of its headline agentic improvements.

## Problem and motivation
Agent workloads involve long, growing contexts and frequent interactions. The authors argue that existing speculative drafters redundantly model history already processed by the target model, hurting both acceptance length and cache-maintenance efficiency (§§1, 3).

They report acceptance lengths falling to roughly 1–2 tokens on some agentic workloads and draft-cache maintenance consuming 10–20% of inference latency in a DeepSeek-V4/SGLang measurement (§3.1). The gap they identify is a drafter that exploits the target’s contextual representations without maintaining its own growing history.

## Approach and contributions
- **History-free backbone:** Target hidden states, source-layer embeddings, an anchor token, and speculative placeholders feed a parallel backbone using local positions. The target retains its KV cache; the drafter retains no historical KV cache (§4.1.1).
- **Hidden-space sequential correction:** A low-rank recurrent head incorporates previously drafted tokens within each block before vocabulary projection, restoring intra-block dependencies while avoiding an intermediate vocabulary projection (§4.1.2).
- **Two-stage training:** A history-aware teacher with matching architecture first supervises DLite through forward KL divergence. DLite then trains directly against the target using token cross-entropy and distribution discrepancy (§4.2).

These constitute the authors’ proposed combination; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority over all related methods.

The visible tables support throughput gains in most listed settings and specific architectural choices. They do not establish the exact headline gain, the full long-context comparison, or downstream agent task success. Eliminating the **draft’s** historical cache also does not eliminate the target model’s historical-context processing (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no explicit limitations section in the supplied text. Relevant boundaries and analyst inferences are:

- **Restricted generalization:** Evaluation uses two Qwen3 target sizes and H800 serving hardware; transfer to other families and hardware remains untested here (§5.1).
- **Selected agent settings:** SWE-Bench and AgentLongBench each use selected long-context prompts. Memory evaluation constructs independent prompts per question; BFCL uses a multi-turn harness but excludes tool-execution time (Appendix A.2). **Analyst inference:** These settings only partially establish benefits for complete agent workflows.
- **Incomplete evidence:** Tables 1–3 contain captions without result cells, and training/cache ablation figures provide no numerical data in the retrieved text.
- **Comparison uncertainty:** DFlash2 and DFlow results come from external sources; equivalent execution conditions are not established (§5.1).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** The “consistently higher throughput” statement conflicts with one Table 4 cell. Memory benchmark naming also varies between the experimental setup and appendix.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Training configuration is detailed, but release availability, sampling settings, uncertainty estimates, and complete serving configuration are not established.

## Open research questions
- **Does DLite accelerate complete agent workflows?** Tool time is excluded from BFCL and several evaluations use selected prompts. Next experiment: measure full-session latency and task success with tool time included.
- **Does the design transfer beyond Qwen3?** Only two sizes from that family are tested. Next experiment: retrain equivalent drafters for another target family under matched serving conditions.
- **Which component causes the serving gains?** The supplied ablations do not isolate the hidden-space head’s throughput contribution. Next experiment: compare matched-acceptance variants with and without intermediate vocabulary projection, profiling drafting and verification separately.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and visible throughput results are well described, but headline agentic evidence is incomplete.

Material missing information includes numerical Tables 1–3, quantitative figure data, the exact setting behind the 70% claim, output-quality checks, run variability, full serving and sampling settings, and code/checkpoint availability.
