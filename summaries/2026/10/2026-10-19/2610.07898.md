# FC-SWE: Failure-Conditioned RL for Long-Horizon Software Engineering Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07898) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07898)


## One-sentence takeaway
FC-SWE improves verifier-assisted repository repair over the paper’s GRPO baseline by training on failure-conditioned recovery attempts, although its benefits under practical deployment verifiers and equal training compute remain untested (§§4.2–4.5; Appendix G).

## Problem and motivation
Repository-level coding agents must inspect code, edit files, and use tools across long interactions. Independent-attempt RL discards a useful opportunity: a rejected patch’s test failures and diagnostics can guide another attempt. Training on recovery complicates reward assignment and comparison groups because earlier failures determine both whether later attempts occur and their conditioning context (§§1, 3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure-conditioned recovery training:** After failed verification, reset the repository and generate another complete tool-use trajectory using the original issue, failed patch, and bounded verifier diagnostics. Training allows one recovery attempt per chain (§3.2).
- **Trajectory-local rewards:** Each attempt retains its own binary verifier outcome. Successful recovery does not relabel the preceding failed patch as successful (§3.3).
- **Active-set advantages:** Pool all executed initial and recovery attempts for the same issue into a leave-one-out comparison group, excluding unused attempt slots. Only generated policy tokens receive optimization weight (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Controlled evidence of learned feedback use:** Compare recovery training against independent-attempt GRPO, fresh-retry training, and inference-time context controls (§§4.2–4.4).

These results support improved recovery under the paper’s shared benchmark-feedback protocol. They do not establish independent-sampling Pass@K, semantic correctness beyond the tests, deployment-verifier effectiveness, or equal-accuracy latency savings. The verifier-only result also prevents attributing the gains specifically to joint patch-and-feedback conditioning.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Recovery accesses benchmark-test diagnostics. Practical verifiers, noisy feedback, false acceptance/rejection, and independent patch correctness remain unresolved (Appendices A, G).
- **Author-stated:** The controlled study uses one model and training configuration. Decoding seeds do not measure variation across independent RL training runs; several ablations use one seed (Appendix G).
- **Author-stated:** FC-SWE executes additional training trajectories, so its GRPO comparison is not matched in total training compute (Appendix G).
- **Author-stated:** Alternative advantage groups and loss normalizations are not separately ablated; penalty removal is tested only under shared rewards. The outcome-dependent estimator lacks the standard action-independent-baseline unbiasedness guarantee (§3.4; Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting the FC-SWE checkpoint partly using a subset of the final benchmark creates a model-selection validity risk, despite no training-repository overlap (Appendix B.1).
- **Accounting limits:** Generated-token costs are estimated, some Base logs are missing, and verifier costs and measured end-to-end latency are incomplete (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Does recovery transfer to deployment verifiers?** Benchmark-test feedback is assumed. Train and evaluate with generated tests or learned critics, reserving independent tests exclusively for scoring (Appendix G).
2. **Does FC-SWE retain its advantage at equal training cost?** Additional recovery trajectories confound compute efficiency. Compare against GRPO with matched training tokens and wall-clock budgets across independent training seeds (Appendix G).
3. **Which comparison-group design contributes most?** Active-set alternatives are not isolated. Compare issue-wide pooling against attempt-index grouping while holding trajectories, rewards, and token normalization fixed (Appendix D.1).
4. **When should recovery include the previous patch?** Verifier-only context has the highest reported recovery point estimate, without established statistical superiority. Run paired context experiments with uncertainty estimates and deliberately corrupted diagnostics (Appendix D.3; Appendix G).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported controlled comparisons and explicitly describes their limits. Material gaps include omitted bodies of several referenced tables, complete hyperparameters and context budgets, full per-seed counts, statistical uncertainty, independent training replications, total training compute, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability. The introduction’s claimed 2.9-point first-attempt improvement cannot be reconstructed exactly from the rounded 41.7% and 38.9% figures.
