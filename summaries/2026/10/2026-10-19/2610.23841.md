# StopWatch: Goal Grounding for Object-goal Navigation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23841) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23841)


## One-sentence takeaway
StopWatch separates goal perception from action prediction, improving simulated object-goal navigation by explicitly telling the policy which objects are visible and how far away the goal is, with remaining errors largely attributable to perception (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Object-goal navigation requires finding an instance of a named category in an unfamiliar scene and stopping near it, including categories unseen as training goals. The authors identify a gap between reaching goals and recognizing when to stop: behavior cloning supplies many exploration actions but only one STOP action per demonstration, providing sparse supervision for goal recognition and distance estimation (§§3.1–3.2).

They also identify evaluation defects: inconsistent object names and incorrect annotations can reject valid stops, obscuring navigation performance (§3.4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure diagnosis:** Compare RGB-only and ground-truth-grounding policy training to test whether explicit semantic and distance information resolves stopping failures (§3.2; Appendix H).
- **Two-model navigation pipeline:** A Qwen3-VL-4B perception model predicts structured grounding text; a separately trained Qwen3-VL-2B policy reads observations, grounding, and action history. Grounding describes visible categories and a goal-distance bin. Scene annotations supply supervision for both models (§3.3; Appendices F–G).
- **Evaluation assets:** Correct naming and annotation defects, and introduce MP3D-OV and HSSD-OV. The authors state that they release the corrected evaluation and both new benchmarks (§§3.4, 5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

Failure after reaching falls from 24% to 5% of episodes. | §3.2; Fig. 2; Appendix H |
| StopWatch leads the reported comparisons | Authors report highest SR across six benchmarks under every reported protocol; its HM3D-OVON val_unseen advantage over LightNav-0 is 8.8 percentage points under clean evaluation versus 6.4 under official evaluation. | Separate perception helps more than joint narration and action | Clean HM3D-OVON val_unseen SR: joint grounding/action model 50.7%; full separate-model design 69.6%; separate semantic-only grounding 68.7%. | §4.3; Table 2 |
| Ground-truth substitutions expose substantial perception headroom | Same final policy on clean HM3D-OVON val_unseen: predicted grounding 69.6% SR; selectively corrected distance 78.6%; fully corrected inventory and distance 91.6%. | Comparable goal reaching masks different stopping behavior | On 2,309 clean HM3D-OVON val_unseen episodes, LightNav-0 and StopWatch reach within 0.25 m in 75.7% and 77.4%; wrong stops after reaching account for 10.3% and 3.6% of all episodes, respectively. | Appendix H; Fig. 11 |

These results support explicit grounding as an effective intervention in the tested simulation settings. The final-policy substitutions isolate the benefit of better grounding without policy retraining. The initial diagnosis, however, compares two separately trained policies rather than adding grounding to unchanged RGB-only weights (Appendix H).

The authors’ state-of-the-art claim applies to their selected comparisons. Missing Table 1 values prevent independent inspection of every benchmark margin. Official and clean results also change both the success criterion and evaluated episode subset, so their difference cannot be attributed solely to naming corrections (§§3.4, 4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Training depends on semantic and geometric annotations, restricting available data; evaluation covers only static indoor simulation. Real-robot transfer is anticipated but untested (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** “Unseen goals” does not imply absence from semantic supervision: inventories can include categories that never serve as training goals (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Ground-truth substitutions establish potential gains from ideal grounding, but do not establish that a practical predictor can achieve them (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The corrected protocol incorporates vocabulary grouping, manual scene/category exclusions, visibility thresholds, and a Stretch navigation mesh. Appendix C notes excluded episodes where baselines succeeded using their own embodiments, making embodiment alignment relevant to comparisons.
- **Evidence gap:** The supplied text reports no uncertainty intervals or repeated-seed results. Several tables lack their numerical contents, and Table 2’s checkmark alignment is ambiguous; its prose supports the ablation comparisons above.

## Open research questions
1. **Can grounding supervision scale beyond annotated scenes?** Annotation dependence is an explicit limitation (§5). A next experiment could train grounding from real videos with weaker labels and evaluate navigation on held-out scenes.
2. **Can one model match the separate pipeline?** The tested joint model performs worse, but this does not rule out other objectives (§4.3). Compare joint and separate models with matched data, capacity, and training compute.
3. **Do grounding gains transfer to real robots and changing environments?** Only static simulation is evaluated (§5). Test the same policy with predicted versus independently verified grounding during physical navigation.
4. **Which practical perception improvements yield the largest gains?** Both visibility and distance errors remain costly, especially for small goals (§4.4; Appendix A). Improve each component separately and measure navigation SR, false stops, and latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central mechanism is supported by ablations and ground-truth substitutions, but complete comparison evidence is unavailable.

Material omissions include full numerical contents of Tables 1 and 3–7, 9–10; complete simulation settings; uncertainty and seed variation; detailed audit agreement; and concrete release links or confirmation of model/checkpoint availability.
