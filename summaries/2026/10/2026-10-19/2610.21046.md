# PremiseCheck: Efficient Fact-Checking of Human and AI Peer Reviews

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21046) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21046)


## One-sentence takeaway
PremiseCheck separates paper-free premise extraction from cheap decision-model verification, matching the authors’ GPT-5.5 reference within their non-inferiority margin on NLP review comments at roughly one-quarter of its per-comment cost.

## Problem and motivation
Human and AI reviews can misread a paper, overlook reported content, or make unsupported assertions. Checking each comment with a frontier LLM repeatedly processes a long paper, while cheap decision models struggle with whole comments containing several implicit claims. The authors also identify inconsistent factual-error labels across existing review datasets (§§1–3).

The task concerns factual support, excluding judgments about importance, novelty, or whether a request is reasonable (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **PremiseCheck:** An LLM sees only the comment and extracts explicit, self-contained premises. A decision model routes premises toward paper-grounded or general-knowledge checks, verifies them, and assigns the comment its lowest premise score. Natural-science reviews use a distinct decomposition and verification variant (§4; Appendix B).
- **ReviewFact:** A benchmark harmonizing existing expert annotations across NLP, machine learning, and natural sciences: 9,267 comments on 217 papers, including human and AI reviews. It introduces no new papers or comments (§3).
- **Efficiency and design evidence:** Experiments compare accuracy, cost, and latency; test alternative decomposers and verifiers; and examine routing, aggregation, question phrasing, batching, and fusion (§§5–8).

Claim decomposition itself is established prior work; the proposed contribution is its division of labor between a paper-free LLM and a question-conditioned decision model (§2.2).

## Evidence and main results
AUROC values below use the paper’s 0–100 scale. Unless stated otherwise, cost is relative to **per-comment holistic GPT-5.5 with medium reasoning**.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| NLP accuracy at roughly quarter cost | On 6,466 comments with 308 errors, no-reasoning PremiseCheck scores **63.1 AUROC**, versus **63.2** for the reference; paired difference **−0.2 [−2.8, +2.6]**, meeting non-inferiority. Cost: **$0.0053 versus $0.0217** per comment; latency: **2.82 versus 6.27 seconds**. | Table 6 |
| Cross-field point estimates are close, but uncertain | Medium-reasoning PremiseCheck: ML **68.7 versus 66.9**, difference **+1.8 [−9.4, +11.7]**, at **46.6%** cost; natural sciences **67.5 versus 67.8**, difference **−0.3 [−8.9, +7.6]**, at **47.4%** cost. ML has **216 comments/46 errors**; natural sciences **2,585/65**. | Table 10 |
| Decomposition helps several decision models | On NLP reviews, Jev, Kev-27B, Microsoft Decision-1, and Perplexity Decider score approximately **50–57 AUROC** holistically but come within **one point** of holistic GPT-5.5 when verifying decomposed premises; some intervals do not meet non-inferiority. | §7.1; Appendices D.2, D.4 |
| Verification phrasing improves NLP ranking | With a no-reasoning decomposer, using **16 phrasings** raises AUROC from **63.1 to 65.7**; the authors report a significant improvement over the original question. | §7.3; Table 19 |
| Completed-review batching changes the cost comparison | NLP batched holistic GPT-5.5 without reasoning reaches **67.8 AUROC at 7.7% cost**, versus batched PremiseCheck’s **69.0 at 20.2%**; difference **+1.2 [−1.2, +4.0]**. Fusion reaches **71.5 at 28.0%**, exceeding batched holistic by **+3.7 [+2.4, +5.1]**. | Table 23 |
| Simulated flags improve paper-level error discovery | Checking the three most suspicious comments per NLP paper finds an error in **40%** of papers containing errors with batched PremiseCheck, versus **29%** with batched holistic GPT-5.5. | §8.2 |

Non-inferiority means the lower bound of a paper-level paired-bootstrap 95% interval exceeds **−3 AUROC points**, a prespecified margin (§5). The evidence supports the per-comment efficiency claim most clearly on NLP. Cross-field nonsignificance does not establish equivalence, and batching makes holistic judgment the cheaper standalone option. Flagging results are simulations, not evidence of improved real-world review decisions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Judgment errors are excluded; PremiseCheck performs worse on broader labels that include such errors (§7.4; Limitations).
- **Author-reported failure modes:** Decomposition causes 58% of sampled NLP false alarms; verification causes 45% of sampled natural-science false alarms. These are conditional shares from selected failures, not population error rates (§7.5; Appendix D.7).
- **Analyst inference—limited cross-field certainty:** Small error counts and wide intervals weaken claims of transferable accuracy. Domain-specific prompts and support-versus-contradiction questions materially change results (Table 10).
- **Analyst inference—input comparability:** Jev receives paper text truncated to 24k tokens, while natural-science holistic baselines receive full papers. Truncation effects are not isolated in the supplied results (Appendix B.3).
- **Label uncertainty:** Natural-science factual labels use GPT-5.5 classification of expert rationales. Independent human checks and alternate-label evaluations reduce, but do not eliminate, uncertainty (§3.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Reproducibility and cost:** Jev is API-served. Open-weight Kev results exclude GPU expenditure from quoted API-cost percentages; those percentages are not total operating costs (§6.2; Limitations).

## Open research questions
1. **Can premise extraction preserve reviewer meaning more reliably?** Decomposition dominates sampled NLP false alarms. Compare fidelity-constrained prompts or decomposers using blinded premise-faithfulness annotation and downstream flag precision (§7.5).
2. **Can one configuration transfer across fields?** Domain-specific prompts and verification questions materially affect results. Freeze a configuration before testing on additional disciplinary review collections (Table 10).
3. **Do flags help people correct reviews?** The paper simulates flagging rather than testing user outcomes. Run a controlled reviewer or area-chair study measuring verified corrections, false accusations, and checking time (§8.2).
4. **How sensitive is verification to missing paper content?** The main verifier truncates papers. Compare identical systems under full-text, truncated, and evidence-selected inputs, stratifying errors by evidence location (Appendix B.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported NLP comparison and pipeline description; **medium** in broader generalization.

The supplied text lacks rendered contents for several referenced tables and figures, full verification-question lists, complete numerical results for some ablations and reviewer subgroups, and total monetized GPU costs. It also does not establish deployment benefits, calibration of probabilities, or robustness beyond the supplied review collections.
