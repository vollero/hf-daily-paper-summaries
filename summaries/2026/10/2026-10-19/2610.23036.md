# Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part I: Topology, Input Injection, Recurrent-State Design

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23036) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23036)


## One-sentence takeaway
Matched pretraining experiments favor a prelude–loop–coda architecture over full-stack looping, with input injection offering task-dependent gains and MoE transfer preserving much of the advantage while reducing stored parameters relative to a compute-matched feedforward reference (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Looped language models reuse Transformer blocks to increase computational depth with fewer stored parameters. Existing Ouro- and Huginn-style designs bundle recurrence placement, input injection, and state initialization, making their individual contributions unclear. The authors address this attribution gap through controlled dense-model experiments, followed by comparisons of complete architectures with sparse experts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Common architectural framework:** Separate recurrence placement, persistent input writes, and recurrent-state initialization/organization (§2).
- **Controlled dense ablations:** Train variants from scratch with matched resident parameter scale, logical depth, data, and token budgets. Compare full-stack looping against a sandwich envelope, injection against no injection, and input-initialized, random-initialized, and shared high/low states (§3).
- **MoE transfer and routing intervention:** Compare complete Huginn and Ouro configurations with matched resident and active parameters, alongside a feedforward reference matched in logical depth and active compute. Freeze expert identities across iterations to test whether changing expert selection contributes to performance (§4; Appendix C).

The main contribution is controlled architectural evidence; the component architectures themselves come from existing model families.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sandwich recurrence improves selected reasoning tasks | At matched 730M resident parameters and 112 logical block executions, the authors report consistent advantages on MATH500, BBH-CoT, and DROP across training budgets. Numerical Table 2 cells are absent from the supplied text. | §3.4.1; Figs. 4–5 |
| Injection helps context-sensitive tasks but hurts some mathematics results | At 730M parameters and 336B training tokens, middle-loop prelude injection raises BBH-CoT score from 48.30 to 54.94 and HumanEval+ pass@1 from 28.66 to 34.15, while lowering MATH500 score from 32.80 to 29.20 and GSM8K score from 62.02 to 59.51. Exact math-score metric definitions are unavailable. | Table 3; Appendix B |
| Random initialization and shared H/L states have mixed effects | Relative to input initialization, random initialization improves ARC-Challenge by 3.34 points and GSM8K by 1.22, but loses over one point on four benchmarks. Shared-module H/L states show no consistent benefit in the matched dense setting. | §3.4.3; Table 4 caption |
| Huginn’s advantage transfers to MoE | After 500B training tokens, resident- and active-matched Huginn beats Ouro on eight of ten benchmarks: GSM8K scores are 83.62 versus 78.92; MATH500 scores are 53.40 versus 49.80. Ouro remains higher on ARC-Challenge and BBH-CoT. | Table 5; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Parameter savings coexist with task-specific feedforward wins | Under the authors’ matched training/inference FLOP comparison after 500B tokens, Huginn stores 8.0B versus 32.0B non-embedding parameters—75% fewer. It scores 59.65 versus 56.81 on DROP, 83.62 versus 80.82 on GSM8K, and ties at 53.40 on MATH500; the feedforward model leads on the remaining benchmarks. | Fig. 1; Tables 5–6 |
| Iteration-specific expert identities matter | At the 500B-token Huginn checkpoint, freezing later iterations to the first iteration’s expert identities lowers accuracy on all six tested multiple-choice tasks, including MMLU subject-mean accuracy by 11.2 points and ARC-Easy accuracy by 9.6 points. Mixture weights remain iteration-specific. | §4.3; Fig. 12; Appendix C |

These results support architectural preferences within the tested training regime. The routing intervention supports a causal contribution from iteration-dependent expert identities under that intervention. It does not establish that better load balance causes Huginn’s advantage, and the proposed explanation for injection’s mathematical trade-off remains an author hypothesis (§§3.4.2, 4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Scaling across model sizes and budgets and multi-seed replication remain necessary; uncertainty across seeds is not quantified (§5).
- **Author-stated:** MoE experiments change complete architectures together, so individual dense-model findings are not separately established for MoE (§5).
- **Author-stated:** The H/L conclusion applies to a shared recurrent module. Separate H/L modules, multi-start path independence, and extrapolation beyond trained recurrence depth are untested (§3.4.3).
- **Author-stated:** Raw-token versus contextualized injection remains coupled to different recurrence envelopes; the study does not isolate write-source quality across envelopes (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Small score differences are difficult to interpret without uncertainty estimates. Evaluation variations also warrant caution: some TriviaQA traces use different stopping behavior, and three Q3 MATH500 rows use a prompt/stopping variant (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Fewer resident parameters establish storage savings, but the supplied text does not quantify measured peak memory, throughput, or latency.

## Open research questions
- **Does the architecture ordering persist across scale and seeds?** The authors identify both gaps (§5). Repeat matched comparisons across resident sizes and token budgets with multiple seeds and uncertainty intervals.
- **Can injection preserve contextual gains without harming mathematics?** The observed trade-off lacks a resolved mechanism (§3.4.2). Sweep write strength, injection location, and iteration-dependent gating within one fixed envelope.
- **Which components explain MoE gains?** Complete-configuration comparisons leave attribution unresolved (§5). Ablate envelope, injection, and initialization separately in matched MoE models.
- **Does routing flexibility help generation throughout training?** The intervention covers multiple-choice tasks at one checkpoint (Appendix C). Repeat route freezing across checkpoints on mathematics and code generation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central comparisons and selected exact results, but omits numerical contents of Tables 1, 2, 4, and 7 and many plotted values. Material missing information includes seed uncertainty, complete task metric/cap specifications, exact FLOP accounting, measured runtime and memory, and verified checkpoint or repository contents.
