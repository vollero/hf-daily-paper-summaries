# AesCode: Aesthetic Code Generation with Decoupled Cross-Modal Rewards

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18860) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18860)


## One-sentence takeaway
AesCode trains code-generating vision-language models with graph-based correctness and aesthetic rewards, improving editable infographic generation while retaining much of its visual capability without reference images at inference.

## Problem and motivation
Generating infographics as HTML/CSS supports editing and verification, but content-correct code can produce weak visual design. Generated images offer stronger aesthetic guidance yet may contain incorrect text, data, or relationships. The authors identify a gap between copying these images faithfully and learning to use their layout and style while obeying the original prompt (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reference-guided training:** A generated image supplies non-authoritative design guidance; prompt requirements determine required content. Unlike the inference-only pipelines discussed by the authors, AesCode explicitly trains this behavior (§§1–2).
- **Graph-based reward construction:** A design graph separates content, appearance, and relationships. Six deterministic channels assess execution, text, boundaries, tables/charts, semantic layout, and whitespace; a Visual Graph Rubric (VGR) supplies sample-specific Boolean visual judgments. The graph scaffolds supervision rather than serving as policy input (§§3.1–3.2; Appendix D).
- **Multi-objective post-training:** Supervised fine-tuning precedes RL using independently normalized reward channels. GDPO is adopted from prior work, rather than introduced here (§3.3).
- **Separate correctness and visual evaluation:** The authors release an infographic benchmark and describe releasing prompts, requirements, generated references, and evaluated outputs. Final Visual checklists use prompt-derived criteria rather than reference similarity (§3.4; Appendices B.1, E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Training improves both backbones | On 300 infographic prompts, averaging three generations per prompt, reference-conditioned Overall rises from **51.93 to 82.94** at 8B and **61.10 to 85.89** at 32B; Overall averages Rule and Visual on a percentage scale. | §4.2, discussion of Table 1 |
| AesCode preserves structured correctness better than reconstruction objectives | Under reference conditioning, AesCode-8B scores **87.79 Chart / 75.80 Visual**, versus **9.78 / 65.69** for reconstruction SFT plus CLIP-similarity GRPO using the same backbone, prompts, and sampler. | Appendix C.3, Table C1 |
| Reward normalization and graph construction help | In reference-conditioned 8B ablations, GDPO adds **5.12 Visual points** over scalar GRPO. Graph-derived VGR scores **75.80 Visual**, versus **72.61** for directly authored questions; Rule remains approximately unchanged (**90.07 versus 90.05**). | §4.3, discussion of Table 4 |
| Reference-trained capability largely survives reference removal | Removing the image only at inference reduces AesCode-8B Visual by **1.00 point**, versus **19.55** for its backbone and **10.04** for GPT-5.5. | §4.3 |
| Human comparison supports bounded aesthetic equivalence | On **267 eligible briefs**, human Content satisfaction is **86.50%** for AesCode-32B versus **83.47%** for GPT-5.5. For aesthetic ratings on **80 briefs**, the mean difference is **−0.15**, with **95% CI [−0.34, 0.03]**, inside the prespecified **±0.5** equivalence margin on a **1–7** scale. | §4.4; Appendix E.3 |
| Automatic judgments have substantial, imperfect human agreement | Human-majority agreement is **89.3%** for training VGR and **93.3%** for evaluation predicates, sampling **150 predicates each**. Visual–preference consistency is **80.66%**, using a scoring rule that gives ties half credit. | §4.4; Appendix E.3 |
| External transfer is reported | AesCode-32B improves over its backbone on all **14 measures** across Design2Code, SlidesBench, and GDB/lica; GPT-5.5 remains stronger in exact screenshot fidelity. Full Table 2 values are absent. | §4.2 |

These results support the authors’ claim that separating verifiable requirements from visual supervision improves this generation setting. Human evidence supports aesthetic equivalence within the specified study and margin, rather than universal parity with frontier models. External transfer is reported, but missing table values prevent assessment of its magnitude across most metrics.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated failures:** Canvas overflow, disconnected global structures, and semantically unrecognizable icons persist. Relation feedback is coarse, while depicted icon content is deliberately excluded from training rewards (§4.4; Appendix F).
- **Restricted output contract:** Charts must use ECharts, tables use HTML structures, and photographs or logos use placeholder slots. Performance therefore does not establish unrestricted visual asset generation (§4.2; Appendix B.5).
- **Analyst inference—distribution dependence:** RL and the internal benchmark use the same reference generator and construction pipeline. Robustness to other generators or user-supplied references is not established (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** GPT-5.5 constructs and judges training rubrics. Human audits reduce concern about model-judge validity but do not establish reliability across all design styles or populations (Appendices D.4, E.3).
- **Incomplete retrieved evidence:** Several tables have captions without bodies, and human-evaluation figure references conflict with the displayed captions. Some comparisons can therefore be assessed only through prose.

## Open research questions
- **Can rendering feedback eliminate overflow?** The authors identify incorrect cumulative pixel allocations as a residual cause. Compare the current generator with a render-and-revise loop under a matched inference budget (Appendix F.1).
- **Would edge-specific rewards improve connected structures?** Relation failures currently share an aggregate rubric reward. Compare per-edge or subgraph reward channels with existing VGR aggregation (Appendix F.2).
- **Does guidance transfer across reference sources?** The internal setting uses GPT-Image-2 references. Test frozen policies on alternate generators and systematically corrupted references, measuring correctness and visual quality separately (Appendix B.3).
- **Can semantic icons improve without copying reference errors?** Their depicted meaning is currently unscored. Add prompt-grounded icon-recognition checks and evaluate recognizable meaning alongside layout quality (Appendix F.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and several detailed results, including human-study procedures and reconstruction comparisons. Material gaps include full Tables 1–4, external-comparison and failure-rate table bodies, compute and monetary costs, model-weight availability, and complete executable construction templates despite references to their reproduction.
