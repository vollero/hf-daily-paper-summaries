# VIEWCRAFT: Evolving What Multimodal Agents See and How They Read It

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15915) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15915)


## One-sentence takeaway
ViewCraft jointly evolves executable visual evidence interfaces and reading procedures for frozen multimodal agents, reporting a 6.1-point mean task-score gain over direct execution across eight benchmarks and a 5.2-point gain at matched input-token budget (§§1, 4.5).

## Problem and motivation
An agent can fail because relevant evidence is missing or illegible, or because its reading procedure does not exploit the evidence’s organization. The authors identify a gap in reusable skill optimization: evidence selection, transformation, and presentation should become persistent, editable state alongside reading instructions (§§1–2).

Their probes motivate this distinction: selector rankings change with frame budget, stronger reading reminders can hurt on unchanged inputs, and lower-resolution crops reduce field-value recognition despite retaining the requested content (§1; Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Coupled skill representation:** A saved skill combines a reading procedure, evidence selection/transformation, and view layout/contracts; executor weights remain frozen (§3.1).
- **Failure-guided evolution:** Training traces guide proposals. Rendering replay removes visually inert edits, execution guards enforce constraints, and paired development evaluation accepts score improvements or evidence-support improvements when scores tie (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Interface projection:** Eligible rejected joint proposals receive another evaluation using their revised interface with the incumbent reader, recovering visual changes that failed alongside a reader revision (§3.4).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Coordinate restorations, matched-budget search baselines, token matching, and executor transfer test the value of the learned pairing (§4).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Joint evolution improves held-out performance | Equal-weight mean gain over Direct: **6.1 task-score points** across eight benchmarks, pooling three repetitions; all task estimates positive, exact sign-test **p=0.008**. Only FUNSD and STI-Bench remain individually significant after Holm correction. | §4.2; Appendix B |
| Advantage survives input-token matching | **+5.2 points**, CI **[+3.6,+6.9]**, over Direct given ViewCraft’s per-item input budget through content-independent input expansion; gains positive on every task. | Appendix G, Table 23 discussion |
| Joint search beats matched search alternatives | **+1.3 points** over alternating search, 95% cluster-bootstrap CI **[+0.6,+2.1]**; **+2.6 points** over strongest same-space baseline, MPO-fullspace, CI **[+1.3,+3.9]**. Arms submit 250 proposals across 24 task–seed searches. | §4.3; Appendix B |
| Learned components exhibit interaction | Terminal reader–interface interaction averages **+1.8 points**; all eight estimates positive, one-sided sign-test **p=0.004**. Seven bootstrap intervals exclude zero; SlideVQA’s includes zero. | §4.2; Figure 5c |
| Separate transplants recover less than the pairing | Relative to Initial skill with observation trajectory fixed: evolved evidence **+2.3 points**, evolved reading **+3.0**, joint pairing **+8.2**; joint-minus-transplants contrast **+2.9**. | Appendix B, Table 18 |
| Skills transfer across evaluated executors | Every evaluated transfer exceeds its target executor’s Direct baseline by **at least 1.5 points**; cross-transfer uses the source run with median gate score. | §4.5; Appendix C |
| Efficiency depends on the comparator | Multi-source input tokens fall **8–15% versus Initial skill**, but rise **15–23% versus Direct**; single-source increases versus Direct are **67–108%**. Deployment latency averages **7.6 seconds more per item** than Direct across eight tasks. | §4.5; Appendix G |

The controls support the authors’ claim that input volume alone does not explain the gain and that the learned reader and interface work together. They do not establish universal superiority: results concern the evaluated visual tasks, adapted baselines, executors, and budgets. The mean also combines different task metrics rather than representing a single pooled accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Missing retrieved detail:** Many tables retain captions but omit their numerical bodies, including the main score and transfer tables. Per-task results and several controls cannot be fully inspected from this text.
- **Author-reported measurement limits:** The evidence verifier agrees with human consensus on 89% of 100 audited judgments (Appendix H). FUNSD’s constructed field associations are 96% correct in a 200-pair audit; corrected-label scores preserve the reported advantage (Appendix A).
- **Author-reported regressions:** Of 204 accepted edits, 153 both repair and regress items. Aggregate acceptance therefore does not guarantee preservation of previously correct answers (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Source-disjoint benchmark evaluation supports generalization to held-out media within these settings; it does not establish transfer to new task families or nonvisual modalities.
- **Analyst inference—mechanism attribution:** Interaction magnitude changes with the reader/interface partition, and retention mechanisms overlap. The experiments support configuration-level benefits more clearly than uniquely additive component effects (§§4.2–4.4).
- **Reproducibility:** The authors identify starting skills, prompts, interpreter, split manifests, and predictions as necessary. Their complete availability cannot be established from the supplied text; parts of the purported complete skill listing are also absent (Appendices A, I).

## Open research questions
- **Does the pairing transfer to new task families?** Executor transfer retains benchmark tasks. Next experiment: apply saved skills to an unseen visual task family without further search, comparing against Direct and target-evolved skills.
- **How sensitive is retention to verifier errors?** The audit establishes imperfect agreement, not the consequences of specific errors. Next experiment: compare frozen-verifier, human-verified, and deliberately perturbed support judgments under matched budgets.
- **Can evolution preserve prior successes better?** Accepted edits frequently introduce regressions. Next experiment: add a regression constraint to the paired gate and measure mean score alongside retention of previously correct items.
- **When does reuse justify search and deployment cost?** Search is described as amortizable, while execution remains slower. Next experiment: measure total cost and task score over increasing reuse counts under explicit latency budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and headline findings, but missing table bodies prevent full numerical inspection. Material gaps include complete per-task scores and uncertainty, split sizes, transfer matrices, absolute deployment costs, full search grammar and prompts, complete executable artifacts, and verified release contents.
