# Diffusion Editing with Soft Mask: Pixel Level Redo of Image and Video with Adjustable Strength

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00359) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00359)


## One-sentence takeaway
SoftPaint adds training-free, spatially varying edit-strength control to diffusion image and video models, improving several quality–preservation comparisons against Differential Diffusion while requiring additional inference compute.

## Problem and motivation
Text instructions provide coarse spatial control, while binary inpainting masks cannot express partial changes. The authors target continuous per-pixel “redo strength,” ranging from reference preservation to regeneration. They argue that supervised soft-mask editing requires costly annotations, while existing training-free approaches offer weak control or expensive gradient computations (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified Redo (UR) score:** Combines the pretrained model score with an analytic Gaussian reference tether. A time-dependent, per-pixel weight maps mask strength to reference influence, using Gaussian smoothing to permit controlled deviations (§3.1, Eqs. 5–9).
- **Diffusion-Damped Langevin (DDL):** Refines each noisy sample through inner Langevin updates before denoising. An exponential integrator handles the stiff damping component; an optional reference-based warm start supports small inner-loop budgets (§3.2; Appendices B, C.3). Appendix B identifies the damping rule as a simplified variant of LanPaint.
- **Cross-backbone evaluation:** Demonstrates soft editing on Qwen-Image, CogVideoX-5B, and Wan2.2, including equal-compute image comparisons and spatially varying masks (§4). The method requires no retraining or backpropagation through the backbone.

The evidence supports improved quality–preservation trade-offs against DD in the tested settings, including equal-NFE image editing. It does not establish superiority on every metric, equal-compute video superiority, or universal backbone compatibility. CLIP-Text matching controls a proxy for edit strength rather than directly measuring user-perceived equivalence (§4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated latency:** Additional network evaluations make inference roughly **2–5× slower** than standard backbone sampling, limiting interactive use (§6).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Main strength sweeps use uniform values inside supplied masks; the direct nonuniform test uses four vertical zones. Arbitrary detailed masks and spatially varying video strengths remain less established (§4; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Tables report no confidence intervals or repeated-seed variability. Anchor tolerances describe CLIP-Text matching, not statistical uncertainty. Video comparisons lack an equal-NFE control (Tables 2–4).
- **Analyst inference—theoretical justification:** Equation 4 multiplies pixel-conditional factors, but the supplied derivation does not account for their cross-pixel dependence when asserting Equation 9 is its log-density gradient. Normalizability with negative model exponents is also not demonstrated (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—boundary and stability claims:** The implemented variance uses a finite denominator offset, whereas the full-redo argument invokes infinite variance. Appendix B’s closed-form stability reasoning freezes the nonlinear remainder; this does not by itself establish unrestricted stability of the complete nonlinear sampler.

## Open research questions
1. **Does control remain reliable for detailed, moving masks?** Evidence is largely uniform-region sweeps plus vertical image zones. Test irregular gradients and moving video masks, measuring local edit strength, boundary artifacts, and temporal consistency.
2. **Can inference cost fall without losing the advantage?** The inner-budget ablation mainly uses Wan2.2, and latency remains substantial. Compare adaptive inner-step allocation against fixed budgets at matched wall-clock time.
3. **Do CLIP-Text anchors reflect equivalent perceived edits?** The paper uses a proxy and reports mixed aesthetics results. Run blinded human comparisons at matched anchors, rating semantic change, preservation, and visual quality.
4. **What distribution does UR sampling actually target?** The joint-density derivation remains incomplete. Test score integrability and sampling agreement on small, explicitly tractable correlated distributions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported tables are clear, but broad compatibility and exact probabilistic claims are less established.

Missing information includes FlowEdit/DAVIS-Edit sample counts, repeated-run uncertainty, exact accelerator identity and absolute peak memory, equal-compute video results, implementation/code availability, and a complete justification of the claimed joint target and nonlinear stability guarantees.
