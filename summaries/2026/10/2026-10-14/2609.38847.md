# Scoring Higher, Answering Worse: Mitigating Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based RL via Protocol-Level Rubrics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38847) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38847)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProRubric’s grouped, jointly judged rubric dimensions improve held-out medical appropriateness over additive Rubric-RL while preserving coverage, but do not eliminate degradation relative to the untrained model (§§5.2–5.4).

## Problem and motivation
Rubric-based RL typically sums independently judged criteria, allowing easy checklist items to compensate for missed critical decisions. The authors show that improved rubric coverage can conceal worse answers on held-out physician criteria. Their central gap is aggregation: prior work largely improves criterion content, while leaving the conversion from verdicts to reward unchanged (§§1–3).

“Appropriateness” here means LLM-judged satisfaction of physician-written criteria, not demonstrated clinical utility (§2.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure diagnosis:** Separate training-aligned rubric coverage from held-out appropriateness, and audit incentives by editing answers before training (§§2–3).
- **Controlled aggregation analysis:** Keep criterion wording fixed while testing conjunctive grouping (“raw-AND”) and holistic checklist scoring. These controls show that aggregation alone mitigates the medical collapse; science requires additional synthesis (§5.3).
- **ProRubric:** Generate two to five protocol dimensions offline. A dimension earns credit only when its requirements jointly hold and its failure clause does not trigger; dimension weights sum constituent absolute weights. Training retains GRPO and rubric-free inference (§4).

Full ProRubric includes synthesized descriptions and failure clauses, so its complete improvement cannot be attributed solely to regrouping unchanged text. Compensation also remains possible across dimensions (§§4.2, 5.4).

These results support aggregation as a material contributor to the tested failure, especially in medicine. They do not establish that hard conjunction is uniquely effective: graded dimensions are competitive, and holistic aggregation matches or exceeds ProRubric on medical appropriateness (§5.3; Appendix D.2). Nor does the benchmark average describe one universally trained policy: RL policies are trained separately by domain (§5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Evaluation is single-turn and judge-based; interactive consultation and human studies remain untested. ProRubric retains unnecessary elaboration and cross-dimension compensation (§§5.4, 7).
- **Domain confounding:** Tasks and rubric structures differ together, preventing separation of domain effects from rubric-shape effects (Appendix D.2).
- **Generation validity:** Structural validation does not verify semantic coverage or factual correctness; repairs can assign omitted criteria without updating descriptions (Appendix A.3).
- **Comparison limits:** OPSD uses a smaller generation budget; some RuscaRL checkpoints have fewer steps; SFT uses mixed-domain training. These are configured comparisons rather than each method’s best result (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Shared generator/evaluator identity and judge disagreement constrain claims about general answer quality, although alternate-generator and cross-judge checks provide some reassurance (Appendices B.1, D.2, D.4).
- **Reporting limits:** One preregistered high-KL threshold fails, and the restriction to settings where learning continues was selected afterward. Training-judge cost was not measured (§4.3; Appendix D.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can cross-dimension compensation be removed without starving training of useful reward differences?** Residual degradation persists, while a single dimension frequently yields identical rollout rewards. Next experiment: compare hierarchical gating against grouped and holistic rewards under matched budgets (§5.4; Appendix D.2).
- **Do gains transfer to interactive, human-assessed consultation?** Current evidence concerns single-turn judge scores. Next experiment: blinded physician evaluation of multi-turn cases requiring clarification (§7).
- **Does rubric structure explain domain differences?** Task and rubric shape are confounded. Next experiment: construct mention-based and substantive rubrics for identical prompts and cross them with aggregation methods (Appendix D.2).
- **Which synthesis components matter beyond grouping?** Science benefits more from full ProRubric, but description validation is only structural. Next experiment: independently vary synthesis and failure clauses using semantically audited rubrics (§5.3; Appendix A.3).
