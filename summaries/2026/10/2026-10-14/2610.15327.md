# CIViC: Evidence Gates for Audio-Conditioned Visual Prediction on Proxy Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15327) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15327)


## One-sentence takeaway
CIViC separates audio sensitivity from substitution specificity, held-out benefit, and endpoint validity, showing that these checks support different conclusions on controlled visual-prediction proxies (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Changing a prediction by removing audio does not establish that the model uses the intended audio information or improves its task performance. Replacement audio may produce similar outputs, auxiliary conditions may increase error, and non-audio inputs may predict a proxy target better (§1).

The authors address the gap between an intervention result and the interpretation attached to it. Their scope is controlled proxy evaluation, rather than ranking audiovisual generators or establishing perceptual video quality (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interpretation-to-control protocol:** CIViC defines four gates—input activation, substitution specificity, held-out benefit, and endpoint validity—and fixes the split, intervention, score, aggregation unit, and decision rule before held-out scoring. Individual intervention actions are established practices; the claimed contribution is their mapping to bounded interpretations (§§1–2).
- **Actor-disjoint raster checks:** CREMA-D and RAVDESS experiments distinguish small detection effects, failed replacement controls, and constant-audio comparisons (§3; Appendix B).
- **Synthetic composition study:** A withheld feature-mixture experiment separates condition activation from prediction benefit and examines training duration, training support, and parameter-matched controls (§4).
- **SpeakerVid proxy study and toolkit:** A source-video-disjoint control matrix exposes opposite rankings under raster RMSE and segment drift. The authors provide lightweight evaluation code and fixtures, but exclude assets required for full replay (§5; Reproducibility Statement).

No new architecture or general identification method is claimed (§1).

## Evidence and main results

All four matched-seed comparisons favor constant audio on the same opened targets. | §3.3; Appendix B |

The evidence supports endpoint-specific findings on the supplied splits. It does not establish general audio-conditioning failure, natural-video quality, speaker attribution, or perceptual benefit. Five-feature-zero retains identity, dialogue, and condition information; it is not an all-audio-information removal control (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** CREMA-D replacement changes both speaker and utterance; its tiny detected effect lacks a practical threshold. Clip-level resampling does not estimate actor-population uncertainty (§3.1).
- **Author-stated:** Study II’s primary comparison changes input width and uses a fixed-run directional rule without held-out-case intervals. Separate scalar controls do not diagnose its feature-target shortcut (§4).
- **Author-stated:** SpeakerVid uses whole-frame grayscale targets, annotation-shared activity information, and frame-index alignment without retained presentation timestamps. Its drift metric uses flattened windows rather than continuous clips (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Repeated seeds and platforms do not expand the four-video test population; real-person disjointness remains unverified. Human perceptual evaluation remains future work (§§5–6).
- **Reproducibility:** Full replay requires excluded approved source material. The retrieved text supplies table captions and surrounding summaries but omits the numerical table bodies.
- **Analyst inference:** Because endpoint rankings reverse, selecting only raster RMSE would provide an incomplete account of performance; the evidence supports reporting the endpoints separately (§5.1; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Does audio improve a mouth-specific, perceptually meaningful endpoint?** Whole-frame proxies do not establish mouth motion or synchronization (§5). A next experiment could use timestamp-aligned mouth targets and human evaluation with matched audio controls.
- **Can substitution isolate speaker identity?** Existing replacements also change utterance, and filename matching does not guarantee phonetic alignment (§3; Appendix B). A next experiment could hold speech content and timing fixed while changing speaker.
- **What explains the condition branch’s early disadvantage?** The gap shrinks with training, while primary input widths differ (§4). A next experiment could compare aligned and dummy conditions under identical capacity and training support across longer matched trajectories.
- **Does the SpeakerVid ordering generalize across sources?** The current finding covers a small, fixed held-out video subset (§5). A next experiment could preregister a larger source-disjoint test with video-level uncertainty and both endpoints.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the bounded conclusions reported in the supplied text; **low** for broader audiovisual generalization.

Material missing information includes numerical table bodies, full replay assets, verified real-person disjointness, presentation timestamps, practical-effect thresholds for key contrasts, and human perceptual results.
