# CineRecon: Benchmarking Frontier Models for Agentic Cinematic Reconstruction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16577) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16577)


## One-sentence takeaway
CineRecon evaluates agents reconstructing videos as editable Blender scenes and finds persistent action, interaction, and camera errors even when detailed appearance is unnecessary (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Editable dynamic 3D scenes could give filmmakers explicit control over layout, action timing, and camera trajectories while providing rendered guidance for video generation. The authors identify a gap in evaluating the joint reconstruction of scene content, subject interactions, camera motion, and shot transitions from complete videos without supplied assets or camera parameters (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 50 reference videos spanning film, captured footage, and games, with 360 annotated evaluation clips. Agents inspect videos, construct and animate Blender scenes, and optionally refine them using rendered feedback (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Controlled representation settings:** Proxy, Simplified, and Detailed progressively increase geometry and appearance requirements while preserving spatial, camera, and dynamic requirements (§2.2).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Entity- and time-specific checklists cover 11 categories across camera, static, and dynamic consistency. GPT-5.6 Sol judges aligned frames; scores average items within clips, then clips within dimensions, with equal dimension weights (§§2.4, 3.2; Appendix C).
- **Empirical analysis:** Comparisons of 12 models through their respective harnesses, including representation effects and an Opus 5.5 refinement/effort ablation (§§3.1, 4).

The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation framework rather than a new reconstruction model.

## Evidence and main results

All reconstruction scores below use a 0–100 scale.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic evaluation tracks relative human rankings | Across 12 models, overall human–automatic win-rate correlations are Spearman **0.951** and Pearson **0.975**. The study has **1,360 valid comparisons** from **1,440 tasks**, with three independent raters per task. | §4.1; Appendices D.2–D.3 |
| Specific actions remain difficult | Highest action-event scores among evaluated configurations are **42.7 Proxy**, **46.6 Simplified**, and **48.6 Detailed**; category scores average items within clips, then clips equally. | §4.2 |
| Simpler appearance usually improves camera and dynamics | Proxy exceeds Detailed for **12/13 configurations** on camera consistency and **10/13** on dynamic consistency. Equal-configuration mean gains are **7.4** and **4.1 points**, respectively. | §4.3; Fig. 8 |
| Proxy does not resolve dynamic reconstruction | Proxy dynamic-consistency scores are **44.6 for GPT-6 Astra** and **60.8 for Opus 5.5**. Qualitative cases retain steering-reversal and landing-transition errors. | §4.3; Fig. 9 |
| Refinement and effort help in the tested ablation | Opus 5.5 overall scores, averaged equally across settings and dimensions: **76.6 Full**, **66.4 No Refine**, **49.5 Low Effort**. Dynamic consistency falls by **11.6** and **34.6 points** relative to Full. | §4.4; Fig. 10 |

These results support the authors’ claim that reducing appearance demands leaves substantial temporal and interaction failures. Human validation supports **model-level relative rankings**, rather than establishing the correctness of every checklist judgment. The effort/refinement evidence concerns one model; comparisons between models also include differences in harnesses and execution behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated future work:** Whether reconstructed proxies improve downstream video generation remains untested (§6).
- **Analyst inference—model/harness confounding:** Models use corresponding harnesses and choose their own refinement rounds. Results therefore compare complete agent configurations rather than isolating model capability (§3.1; Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited coverage:** The benchmark contains 50 videos lasting 4.90–24.04 seconds; generalization to longer productions is unestablished (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Scores assess rendered observations. They do not directly establish hidden geometry accuracy or practical editability beyond submission of a Blender project (§2.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—weighting and failures:** Clips receive equal weight without subsequent video-level averaging. Operational failures receive no visual zero; aggregate failure counts and their effect on comparisons are unavailable in the supplied text (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—judge validity:** A single VLM judge is used. Model-level human alignment does not establish item-level accuracy or robustness across judges (§3.2; Appendix D.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can explicit event planning improve action fidelity?** Actions remain weak under Proxy (§4.3). Compare direct reconstruction against an intermediate event timeline with contact constraints under matched budgets.
- **How much performance comes from the harness?** Native harnesses differ (§3.1). Evaluate compatible models in a common tool environment with fixed refinement and compute budgets.
- **Does refinement help broadly across models?** The ablation covers Opus 5.5 only (§4.4). Repeat refinement/no-refinement comparisons across several models and representation settings.
- **Do higher reconstruction scores improve filmmaking control?** Downstream generation is left open (§6). Test whether higher-scoring proxies produce more faithful generated videos and more predictable edits.
