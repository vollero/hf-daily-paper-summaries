# Open-o3-Video-v2: Seek, Ground, and Reason with Verifiable Spatio-Temporal Evidence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17656) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17656)


## One-sentence takeaway
Open-o3-Video-v2 combines adaptive video retrieval with timestamped object grounding, improving video reasoning and evidence alignment over its Qwen3-VL backbones, with controlled ablations supporting complementary benefits from seeking and grounding (Tables 2, 5).

## Problem and motivation
Sparse video sampling can miss brief actions or small objects, while dense processing adds cost and irrelevant context. The authors identify a gap between methods that retrieve relevant clips and methods that explicitly identify which moments and objects support an answer: neither capability alone ensures evidence-grounded reasoning (§§1, 3.3).

The goal is to acquire missing observations, localize supporting evidence, and reuse that evidence during subsequent reasoning rather than merely generate a plausible explanation.

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated seek–ground–reason trajectories.** A Qwen3-VL policy interleaves reasoning with `crop_video` calls specifying an interval and sampling density. It records timestamp–object–box evidence in the conversation before continuing or answering. The claimed contribution is this integration, building on existing temporal retrieval and v1 grounding (§4.1).
- **STGR-v2 training corpora.** STGR-v2-CoT-135K combines 52K multi-turn grounded trajectories with grounding, captioning, general QA, and auxiliary tool-use samples. Construction uses answerability filtering, automated object annotation, rationale rewriting, consistency checks, and human audits. STGR-v2-RL-13K selects prompts with both successful and unsuccessful SFT rollouts (§4.2).
- **Clue-Seek GRPO.** CS-GRPO retains the GRPO estimator while adding a trajectory reward based on accumulated retrieved-interval overlap with annotated clues. It rewards coverage and subsequent evidence gains, penalizes inefficient retrieval, and combines this with answer, format, and grounded-thinking rewards (§§4.3–4.5).
- **Models and infrastructure.** The authors report releasing code, models, and the multi-turn training stack; dataset release availability is not explicitly established in the supplied text (Abstract; §4.6).

## Evidence and main results

All scores below are percentages; differences are **percentage points**, consistent with the tabulated values.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger grounded QA | On V-STaR, v2-4B achieves 40.4 mAM / 56.3 mLGM versus Qwen3-VL-4B’s 31.9 / 43.7: gains of 8.5 / 12.6 points. | Table 2 |
| Gains beyond grounded QA | v2-4B versus Qwen3-VL-4B: Video-Holmes accuracy 48.0 vs 37.3; LVBench accuracy 53.2 vs 46.0; Charades-TimeLens mIoU 49.8 vs 45.6. | Table 3 |
| Seeking and grounding contribute separately | With matched backbone, source data, and evaluation: single-turn without evidence → multi-turn without evidence → multi-turn grounded evidence gives V-STaR mLGM 47.8 → 52.3 → 56.3 and LVBench accuracy 48.8 → 51.9 → 53.2. | Table 5 |
| RL improves the SFT checkpoint | For v2-4B, SFT → SFT+RL raises V-STaR mLGM 53.4 → 56.3 and VideoMME accuracy 63.7 → 66.6; backbone VideoMME accuracy is 65.3. | Table 4 |
| CS-GRPO improves over matched GRPO | Same initialization, RL data, rollouts, and optimization setup: Video-Holmes / LVBench / VideoMME accuracy rises from 46.7 / 52.0 / 65.1 to 48.0 / 53.2 / 66.6. | Table 6; §5.2 |
| Adaptive retrieval helps under comparable visual budgets | On LVBench, full-model accuracy is 53.2 versus 45.5 with uniform 120-frame sampling and tools disabled, and 46.2 with length-matched random retrieval preserving calls and returned frames. | Table 16 |
| Correct answers more often have correct grounding | V-STaR Chain1 joint QA–spatial–temporal success is 21.7 for v2-4B versus 11.7 for Qwen3-VL-4B, using spatial IoU >0.1 and temporal IoU >0.3. | Table 14 |
| Long-horizon subset improvement | On 149 LVBench questions from videos longer than 45 minutes, accuracy is 48.3 for v2, 37.6 for Qwen3-VL-4B, and 38.3 for Video-o3. | Table 17; §11 |

These results support useful adaptive retrieval and complementary grounding benefits in the tested settings. The intervention controls strengthen the case that retrieved content matters to answers. They do not establish that every cited box causally supports the reasoning, or that gains imply reliable evidence on every example: joint answer-and-grounding success remains limited.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported failures:** Among incorrect LVBench predictions, approximately 43% involve temporal retrieval/event selection, 27% grounding or fine-grained perception, and 6% knowledge. Camera-motion counting, instance association, and distinguishing similar events remain difficult (§12.2).
- **Analyst inference—reward proxy:** CS-GRPO’s evidence state measures temporal interval overlap, not actual semantic information gain. Object-level correctness receives separate supervision; improved interval coverage alone cannot establish faithful reasoning (§§4.3–4.4).
- **Analyst inference—supervision quality:** Automated annotation and rewriting can introduce referent errors, hallucinated descriptions, and evidence mismatches, all observed in the audits. Final post-filtering residual error rates are not quantified (§8).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Overall backbone comparisons combine new data, training, and adaptive observation. Tables 5, 6, and 16 isolate selected factors, but comparable frame counts do not establish equal latency or compute.
- No repeated-run uncertainty or significance estimates accompany the reported results. Evaluation covers two Qwen3-VL scales; broader backbone transfer is unestablished (§5).

## Open research questions
- **Does grounded evidence causally drive each inference?** Retrieval replacement tests content utility but does not isolate individual evidence claims. Next experiment: mask cited object regions against matched uncited regions and measure answer changes.
- **Can temporal retrieval rewards better reflect semantic progress?** Interval IoU can differ from information usefulness. Next experiment: compare CS-GRPO with a semantic evidence-gain reward under matched training and observation budgets.
- **Can the method resolve repeated instances across long videos?** Instance association and confusing event selection remain reported failures. Next experiment: add persistent identity tracking and evaluate counting and repeated-event subsets with matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings and method summary; **medium** in broader reliability and efficiency claims.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, latency and compute costs, quantitative final-corpus residual errors, the LVBench failure-analysis sample size and labeling protocol, explicit corpus release status, and precise inference crop settings. The supplied text describes mAM/mLGM conceptually but does not provide their exact formulas.
