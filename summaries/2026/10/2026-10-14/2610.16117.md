# Beyond Perfect Teammates: Multi-Agent Collaboration with Unreliable Colleagues

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16117) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16117)


## One-sentence takeaway
UnCoBench shows that strong performance with reliable collaborators can conceal major weaknesses in detecting and repairing mis-scoped or incomplete evidence, especially when several collaboration failures occur together.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a “perfect-colleague” assumption in multi-agent evaluation: collaborators supply relevant, complete, timely evidence. Realistic failures instead require a coordinator to check context, request missing details, and follow up on unanswered requests. The paper seeks to separate task-solving ability from the ability to recover a faulty evidence-gathering process (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** UnCoBench contains 1,000 task groups, each pairing Reliable, Misaligned, Incomplete, and Missed variants with the same underlying decision problem and gold answer. Standard unreliable variants change exactly one necessary role’s response policy (§§3–3.4).
- **Verified generation pipeline:** LLMs generate specifications and render records; programs instantiate facts and check answer uniqueness, role necessity, and perturbation validity. A separate LLM must recover the gold answer in three shuffled full-information runs (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Ten main-agent models are evaluated using task accuracy and interaction rounds. Trace analysis, progressively informative recovery interventions, and a Mixed stress test examine where recovery breaks down (§§4–5; Appendix E).

The contribution is primarily an evaluation framework and benchmark, rather than a new trained recovery method.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Similar reliable accuracy masks different robustness | Across 1,000 tasks per setting, GPT-5.6 Sol scores 98.7% Reliable and 45.8% Misaligned; Claude Opus 5 scores 96.7% and 90.7%, respectively. Accuracy requires both decision and numerical result to match. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Mis-scoped evidence is consistently damaging | Across ten models, Misaligned reduces accuracy by 6.0–78.8 percentage points versus the paired Reliable setting. Incomplete losses are 1.0–30.0 points. | Table 1; §5.1 |
| Failure often precedes corrective follow-up | Under Misaligned, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash has no corrective follow-up in 86.7% of traces; only 7.3% show fact recovery followed by a correct answer. GPT-5.6 Sol lacks corrective follow-up in more than half its traces. | §5.2; Figure 4 |
| Successful retry recovery can cost extra interaction | GPT-5.6 Sol scores 97.4% under Missed versus 98.7% Reliable, while mean rounds rise from 2.18 to 4.97. Wait actions contribute 1.46 rounds per task. | Table 1; §5.2; Figure 5 |
| Combined failures substantially reduce accuracy | Across six models, Mixed accuracy ranges from 5.8% to 45.2%. GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash score 19.6% and 45.2%, versus 97.4% and 99.1% under Missed. | §5.3; Figure 6 |
| Explicit assistance helps some recovery stages | Under Incomplete, all six intervention-tested models improve from None to Action and approach their Reliable references. Under Misaligned, Mistral-Small-4 and Qwen3.5-9B remain substantially below their references even with Action assistance. Exact intervention scores are absent from the retrieved text. | Appendix E; Figure 8 discussion |
| Construction includes substantial validity filtering | The pipeline retains 1,000 of 1,296 candidate groups. Human and automated accept/reject decisions agree on 93%–100% of 100 audited candidates per validation stage. | Appendix C.2; Tables 5–6 discussion |

These results establish differences in robustness within the authors’ controlled tasks. The interventions strengthen the diagnosis that monitoring and repair selection matter, but persistent failures under Action do not uniquely distinguish repair execution, belief revision, evidence integration, and underlying task-solving errors. Real-world teamwork performance is not established.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-defined scope:** Most experiments perturb one necessary role; the Mixed test extends this to simultaneous failures but reports only six models (§§3.4, 5.3).
- **Analyst inference—structural generalization:** Domain variety does not establish generalization beyond synthetic English eligibility-and-calculation tasks with constrained candidates, roles, and formulas (Appendices A.1 and C.1).
- **Analyst inference—role-model dependence:** Main evaluations use one default role model. Appendix D compares response fidelity and coverage, but does not report main-agent robustness rankings after substituting role models.
- **Analyst inference—resource comparability:** Provider-default output limits and model-specific default reasoning settings differ from the fixed local output limit. Mean rounds measure actions, not matched token cost or elapsed time (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Missing diagnostic detail:** Several tables and figures appear only as captions or prose summaries. Trace-labeling procedures, detailed intervention scores, and human-reviewer procedures are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Can agents learn autonomous verification?** Action assistance helps several models, but the paper does not demonstrate a trained recovery policy. Next experiment: train targeted scope-checking and clarification behavior, then evaluate on held-out domains and fault combinations.
- **How sensitive are results to colleague implementation?** The benchmark fixes the default role model. Next experiment: rerun paired tasks with alternative role models and deterministic record-serving colleagues.
- **What drives failure under combined faults?** Mixed accuracy falls sharply, but contributions from fault count, type, and interaction are unresolved. Next experiment: factorially vary fault combinations and affected-role counts while holding tasks fixed.
- **Can recovery retain accuracy with lower cost?** Missed traces include waiting and redundant contacts. Next experiment: compare explicit dependency tracking and retry scheduling under matched interaction and token budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the benchmark design and headline results, but omits many table cells, plotted values, and implementation details.
