# Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure? Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration in LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18614) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614)


## One-sentence takeaway

Rewarding an agent’s confidence against eventual episode outcomes improves early success prediction, but the strongest ALFWorld evidence concerns recognizing difficult tasks rather than distinguishing successful from failed attempts at the same task (§§6–7).

## Problem and motivation

Runtime decisions to continue, verify, or escalate an agent require confidence about eventual task success. The authors identify a mismatch: existing calibration training targets current-answer or current-step correctness, whereas long-horizon tasks require predicting an episode’s final outcome from its current history (§§1–3.1).

They also identify an evaluation problem: pooled confidence scores can appear predictive because of task difficulty or episode length, without demonstrating attempt-level foresight (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Outcome-grounded confidence training.** The agent emits an action and an explicit confidence tag. GRPO combines episode-success advantage with a per-step reward scoring confidence against that episode’s binary outcome. The default calibration term is Brier reward, supplemented by format reward and optional confidence regularization (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Offline and online implementations.** Offline training uses recorded prefixes, recorded-outcome labels, and an action-match reward; online training uses the agent’s own live rollouts. Offline generations for a prefix share one recorded label, so they provide no within-group outcome contrast (§4.3).
- **Evaluation decomposition.** Pooled, between-task, within-task, and depth-matched within-task AUROC separate task ranking, attempt discrimination, and episode-depth effects. Early-window and first-step evaluations test whether prediction precedes substantial feedback (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Empirical characterization.** Experiments compare reward forms, optimization estimators, format-only training, depth controls, and offline white-box confidence signals. The principal finding is earlier task recognition, with more limited evidence for attempt discrimination (§§6–7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Offline confidence becomes more predictive | On held-out recordings, 7B pooled step AUROC rises from **0.517→0.681** on ALFWorld, **0.578→0.722** on TextWorld, and **0.462→0.850** on WebShop. Top-decile success lift reaches **4.33×, 4.35×, and 4.35×** each setting’s base rate. | §6.1; Table 1 discussion; Appendix G |
| Depth and output length explain substantial offline discrimination | A depth-plus-length predictor achieves **0.787/0.855/0.799** AUROC on trained ALFWorld/TextWorld/WebShop rows. Residualized trained confidence retains **0.626/0.677/0.811**, versus **0.534/0.479/0.509** untrained. | §6.1; Appendix G |
| Online early prediction improves beyond formatting | On live ALFWorld 7B, pooled AUROC over steps 0–2 improves by **+0.048 to +0.101** across calibration arms versus untrained; format-only gives **+0.019 [−0.010, 0.051]**. Brier+dispersion exceeds format-only by **+0.082 [0.050, 0.114]**. Intervals are 95% task-cluster bootstraps. | §6.2; Table 4 discussion |
| Calibration improves, partly through learning the base rate | ALFWorld all-step ECE falls **0.419→0.053–0.115**, and Brier score **0.250→0.044–0.054**. Mean confidence drops toward a low step-level success rate; these metrics alone do not establish discrimination. | §6.2; Figure 2 |
| High-confidence early steps have better outcomes | ALFWorld success among the top **10%** of confident early steps rises **0.493→0.56–0.66**; three of four paired improvement intervals exclude zero. Episode success remains **0.189–0.203**, versus **0.201** untrained. | §6.2; Figure 3 |
| ALFWorld gains mainly concern task recognition | Step-0 pooled AUROC rises **0.562→0.642–0.700**, while within-task step-0 estimates remain compatible with chance. No ALFWorld experiment meets the prespecified early within-task criterion. | §§7.1–7.2; Table 5 discussion |
| TextWorld provides qualified attempt-level evidence | With online Brier-plus-regularization training, paired within-task AUROC gains are **+0.265 [0.107, 0.428]** at step 0 and **+0.070 [0.010, 0.130]** over steps 0–2. Format-only comparisons cannot isolate calibration from emission effects. | §6.2; Figure 4; Appendix D |
| Larger-model evidence remains uncertain | On ALFWorld 14B, pooled matched-emission AUROC rises **0.697→0.729**, but the paired gain **+0.032 [−0.016, 0.073]** includes zero. | Appendix E |

The evidence supports improved early ranking and calibration in the studied settings. It does not establish broadly reliable attempt-level failure prediction, improved whole-task triage on ALFWorld, or effective deployed human handoff. “Selective execution” results primarily measure outcomes within confidence-selected subsets of evaluated steps (§§3.3, 6.2–7.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope:** Positive live results concentrate on two text environments and one model family. Live WebShop has too few successes for meaningful within-task evaluation; the larger-model study is limited (Limitations; Appendices D–E).
- **Training uncertainty:** Each online arm uses one training seed. Task-bootstrap intervals therefore do not capture variability across training runs (§5; Appendix J).
- **Incomplete reward isolation:** Every online calibration arm includes regularization; there is no pure-Brier online arm. Online comparisons also lack a white-box competitor (§5; §7.3).
- **Statistical weaknesses:** Offline step bootstraps understate uncertainty because trajectory steps share labels; paired within-task analyses sometimes rely on very few shared mixed-outcome tasks (§3.3).
- **TextWorld attribution:** The authors cannot cleanly separate its early improvement from confidence-tag emission effects (Appendix D).
- **Tail reliability:** The highest confidence values do not reliably yield the highest success, and the most-confident all-step bin remains overconfident (§7.4).
- **Analyst assessment:** Statistical reporting is internally inconsistent: Appendix J describes all intervals as task-cluster bootstraps, while §3.3 and Appendix G explicitly identify offline step bootstraps. Several result tables and the prompt template appear only as captions or headings in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can training improve attempt discrimination consistently?** ALFWorld misses the prespecified criterion. A next experiment could train with explicit same-task success/failure contrasts and evaluate depth-matched AUROC across multiple training seeds (§7.1).
2. **Which online reward component causes the gain?** Pure Brier is absent, and TextWorld attribution remains unresolved. Run matched pure-Brier, regularization-only, format-only, and combined arms with emission-matched evaluation (§5; Appendix D).
3. **Does confidence improve actual intervention decisions?** Selected-subset metrics do not establish controller utility. Test a controller that abstains or requests verification, measuring final success and intervention cost (§§3.3, 7.4).
4. **Does the effect generalize across model families and environments?** Current broader evidence is limited or inconclusive. Repeat the protocol with additional backbones and sufficiently many successful and failed held-out attempts (Limitations; Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative supports the central distinction between task recognition and attempt foresight, but statistical and extraction gaps limit verification.

Material unavailable information includes full numerical table contents, rendered figure data, the actual prompt template, a supplementary-code access link, and results quantifying online variation across training seeds. The contradictory bootstrap descriptions also remain unresolved.
