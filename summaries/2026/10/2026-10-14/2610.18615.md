# VideoJev: System One Model for Video Understanding and Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18615) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18615)


## One-sentence takeaway
VideoJev replaces autoregressive video answers with shared candidate scoring, improving temporal grounding and approaching Thinking-mode multiple-choice QA quality while reducing measured model forward time through video-prefix reuse.

## Problem and motivation
Visual agents repeatedly query the same video to locate events, verify progress, and evaluate possible actions. The authors argue that generating timestamps or answer text token by token adds unnecessary cost when the required output is a structured decision; existing discriminative models, meanwhile, often have task-specific designs or fixed label spaces (§1).

The proposed gap is a language-conditioned decision interface that handles temporal structure and reuses video computation across queries.

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared decision architecture:** Adapt pretrained Qwen multimodal models by removing the final MLP and replacing the language-model head with a normalized similarity scorer over candidate and decision readouts. Timestamp candidates and textual answer options use the same head (§2.1).
- **Task-specific learning and decoding:** Grounding combines interval-membership BCE with duration-weighted soft IoU, then selects a contiguous span. QA uses cross-entropy over answer options and training-time option permutation (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Reusable visual computation:** Cached inference branches independent queries from one video prefix; Joint inference scores concatenated queries in one causal forward pass. Training also groups queries by video (§§2.1, 2.4).
- **Controlled empirical assessment:** Dense and MoE variants are evaluated on grounding and QA, including a same-data autoregressive fine-tuning control. This supports a benefit from the decision-learning design beyond additional training data, although it does not isolate every architectural component (§3.4).

These results support direct scoring for the evaluated grounding and closed-set QA tasks. The exceptionally large QA speedups are relative to Thinking generation; they do not establish similarly large gains over every efficient answer-selection baseline. Benchmark quality and latency also use partly different protocols, and timing excludes video decoding, preprocessing, transfers, and postprocessing (Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified boundaries:** Streaming requires additional encoding and memory mechanisms; open-ended agent workflows and broader decision tasks remain future work (§5). Probabilities overestimate correctness (§3.5).
- **Analyst inference—restricted output space:** QA depends on supplied options, while grounding always returns a nonempty contiguous span. The evaluations therefore do not establish open-ended answering, absent-event rejection, or disjoint-event output capability (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Analyst inference—query-context sensitivity:** Later Joint queries can attend to earlier questions and candidates. The authors report no inserted answers, but systematic query-order robustness is not established (§3.4).
- **Comparison limits:** Some baseline scores retain published settings, and QA architectures retain different native visual settings. The matched-data control is more targeted but evaluates the combined design and supervision changes, rather than a single component (Appendices A.1–A.3).
- **Reproducibility limits:** The QA corpus is internally curated. The supplied text does not establish release access or detailed provenance. Tables 3 and 5–8 have captions and narrative findings but no retrieved numerical bodies.

## Open research questions
- **Does Joint inference remain stable under query reordering?** Causal cross-query access leaves this unresolved. A next experiment could permute query groups and insert irrelevant questions while measuring accuracy and latency (§3.4).
- **Can confidence support dependable escalation?** Reported overconfidence limits direct acceptance thresholds. Test held-out temperature scaling or calibration-aware training using ECE and selective accuracy–coverage curves (§§3.5, 5).
- **Can candidate scoring support open-ended workflows?** Candidate generation and agent execution are proposed rather than evaluated. Test an agent that proposes and revises candidates, measuring task completion and total latency (§5).
- **How well does the method transfer to streaming video?** Current reuse operates over a shared video prefix. Evaluate incremental encoding and bounded memory under growing streams (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main benchmark results and inference protocols are explicit, but several ablation tables lack numerical bodies in the supplied text.

Material missing information includes model/code/data release availability, QA corpus provenance and overlap checks, detailed boundary postprocessing, repeated-run uncertainty for main quality results, end-to-end latency and memory measurements, and evaluated streaming or agent-workflow outcomes.
