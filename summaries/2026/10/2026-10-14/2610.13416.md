# Web$hell: A Unified Interface for Agentic Web Access

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13416) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13416)


## One-sentence takeaway
Web$hell unifies webpage content, browser actions, and persistent evidence through addressable objects, achieving the highest reported scores among evaluated baselines on six benchmarks at comparable or lower LLM inference cost (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Web agents must gather information broadly, interact with controls, and revisit evidence within one task. The authors find that clean-text interfaces lose actionable page structure, browser observations burden information gathering with lengthy context, and hybrid interfaces incur repeated interpretation or lose control context during selective reading (§3).

The identified gap is an interface that jointly provides **operability, consistent representations, and persistent selective access** (§3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **WebContract:** A shared schema represents information and actions through addresses, values, and contextual associations. Common operations include retrieval, selective reading, table lookup, and execution (§4.2; Appendix C.1).
- **WebCompiler:** Deterministic HTML/ARIA-based constructors compile captured webpages into linked content and action objects without model inference. Compilation also incorporates embedded structured data and same-origin text responses (§4.3; Appendix C.2).
- **WebCache:** Persistent captures support revisiting evidence, while live browser connections support action execution. Changed pages receive new state addresses; earlier captures remain readable but do not restore past browser states (§4.4; Appendix C.3).
- **Empirical contribution:** Cross-task comparisons motivate the design; benchmark evaluations, an action-reference ablation, and cache stress tests investigate its performance and efficiency (§§3, 5–6).

The proposed contribution is interface-level integration, rather than a newly trained agent model.

## Evidence and main results

Main comparisons use gpt-5.6-luna with high reasoning effort and equal category-specific main-agent turn budgets across methods. Below, each performance comparator is the highest-scoring baseline for that benchmark (Appendix A.3; Table 3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher broad-search performance | WideSearch item F1: **0.782** versus FtE **0.745**; benchmark contains 100 English and 100 Chinese queries. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Higher difficult-search QA accuracy | Seal-Hard, all 254 questions: accuracy **0.720** versus FtE **0.665**. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Higher controlled browser-task success | WebArena-Verified, 789 reproducible tasks: success rate **0.679** versus Hybrid SBF **0.578**. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Higher within-domain browsing QA accuracy | WebWalkerQA, 420 retained questions after accessibility/evidence filtering: accuracy **0.671** versus FtE **0.636**. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Small report-writing score advantage | DeepResearchBench, 100 tasks: RACE Overall **0.534** versus **0.528** for Hybrid SBF and CLI-Anything Browser; this relative report metric uses **0.5** for equal generated/reference scores. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Higher quantitative research score | DeepWeb-Bench, 100 tasks: overall rubric score **31.11** versus Hybrid SBF **28.37**, on a **0–100** scale. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Lower category-average inference cost, with a small margin over FtE | Web search/navigation/deep research cost per task: **$0.062/$0.022/$0.096**, versus FtE **$0.063/$0.027/$0.097**. These category averages also accompany fewer turns. | Table 3 |
| Linking information to actions helps navigation | Removing action references while retaining objects and cache reduced performance and increased inference cost on samples of 100 WebArena-Verified tasks and 100 WebWalkerQA questions; exact plotted values are unavailable in the supplied text. | §6, Figure 6; Appendix B.2 |

| Compilation adds measurable overhead | Mean compilation-and-storage time ranges from **0.46 seconds/page** on WebWalkerQA to **2.79 seconds/page** on DeepWeb-Bench. | Appendix D, Table 7 |

The results establish a favorable performance–inference-cost tradeoff for the evaluated implementations and backbone. They do not establish statistical significance, universal superiority, or lower total operational cost. The action-reference ablation supports that specific design choice; case studies and evidence-coverage observations offer less decisive explanations for the remaining gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported evaluation restrictions:** WebArena-Verified excludes tasks whose required Wikipedia corpus could not be downloaded; WebWalkerQA excludes inaccessible or changed reference pages (Appendix A.1). Results therefore concern the retained subsets.
- **Author-reported state boundary:** Cached captures preserve evidence, but reading them does not restore historical browser state. Actions depend on currently available tabs and confirmed controls (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—model generalization:** All main agents use one backbone, so transfer to other models or reasoning settings remains untested in the supplied text (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The authors explain that the three components share a schema and cannot be removed cleanly. The targeted reference ablation does not isolate every benefit of compilation, organization, caching, and feedback (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—cost scope:** Reported savings concern LLM inference. Compilation time is measured separately; full infrastructure, acquisition, storage, and end-to-end latency comparisons are unavailable. Cost parity with FtE also depends on evaluated prompt-cache pricing (Appendix D, Tables 7–8).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and robustness:** No confidence intervals or repeated-run variability are supplied. Stored irrelevant pages do not test malicious content or all forms of noisy webpage interaction (§6; Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains persist across agent models?** Only one main-agent backbone is evaluated. Repeat the benchmark comparison across models and reasoning settings with matched budgets.
2. **Which interface features drive which gains?** The ablation isolates action references but leaves other mechanisms coupled. Test controlled variants of contextual associations, state-change feedback, and page organization.
3. **Does inference efficiency translate into operational efficiency?** Compilation overhead is reported separately. Measure end-to-end latency and total cost, including page acquisition, compilation, browser resources, and storage.
4. **How can agents recover useful evidence they never inspect?** Appendix D reports that some gold information remains cached but unseen as prefetching grows. Compare cache-aware exploration policies under fixed inference budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, benchmark definitions, settings, and numerical comparisons. Material gaps include statistical uncertainty, repeated-run results, exact values in figure-only analyses, full prompts and executable implementation, confirmed code-release availability, total operational costs, and evidence of generalization beyond the evaluated backbone.
