# WorkRover: Scaling Long-Horizon Cowork Agent Training Via Recursive Environment Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18067) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18067)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorkRover combines dependency-aware environment repair with verifier-grounded turn credit, reporting improvements over its backbone on five cowork benchmarks and over episode-only GRPO under matched rollout budgets.

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon cowork agents must coordinate files, tools, applications, and dependent deliverables. Synthesized training environments can contain missing dependencies, inconsistent instructions, or faulty verifiers, producing misleading reinforcement-learning rewards. The authors identify two gaps: maintaining alignment between environment components during repeated repairs, and assigning useful credit to individual actions when rewards arrive only after an entire trajectory (§1).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Harness-driven environment synthesis:** Jointly constructs work contexts, tasks, and verifiers while tracking dependencies. Fresh reference executions establish feasibility; instruction-grounded evaluation contracts, negative controls, and valid alternative solutions test verifier consistency (§3.1).
2. **Factorial Compatibility Replay (FCR):** Freezes component-level repair proposals, tests compatible combinations with dependency-induced updates, and selects the smallest validated update set among tested candidates. Promotion requires preserved requirements, regression checks, and fresh-seed confirmation (§3.2).
3. **Verifier-to-Turn Credit Assignment (VTCA):** Adds deterministic workspace-progress signals and local trace costs to trajectory-level GRPO advantages. Positive progress can share bounded credit with earlier turns whose exact outputs were directly consumed; negative progress remains local. Intermediate judge calls are unnecessary (§3.3).

The instantiated model, WorkRover-35B-A3B, starts from Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, using 30,115 accepted SFT trajectories and 3,200 RL task–verifier pairs retained from 20,000 candidates (§4.1).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Backbone transfer gains | WorkRover versus Qwen3.5-35B-A3B: JobBench **+26.27 percentage points**, OfficeQA Pro **+29.32**, AutomationBench **+22.67**, WorkBuddy Office **+13.63**; GDPval v2 **804.75 → 1218.00 Elo**. | §4.2; Table 1 referenced |
| VTCA improves over episode-only GRPO | Fixed SFT checkpoint, task/seed schedules, and rollout budget; gains on all five benchmarks, including **+5.7 percentage points** AutomationBench, **+5.4** OfficeQA Pro, and **+43 Elo** GDPval v2. | §4.3.1; Fig. 6; D.2 |
| FCR improves natural-defect repair | Matched proposal/replay budget caps: safe resolution **60.7% → 70.3%** versus full regeneration; task-intent changes **14.3% → 1.3%**; relative model-token cost **1.34 → 1.00**. | §4.3.2; Fig. 7(b) |
| Combination selection matters | With proposals frozen, FCR exceeds apply-all safe resolution by **9.0 percentage points** on natural defects and **10.4** on injected defects; cross-task regression **7.0% → 0.7%**, at relative token cost **1.00 versus 0.59**. | §4.3.2; maintenance table referenced |
| FCR increases construction yield | Same candidate pool and repair caps: synthesis pass rate **49.8%** before repair, **68.8%** with regeneration, **74.8%** with FCR. | §4.3.2 |

The matched comparisons support benefits from VTCA as a combined learning intervention and from FCR repair selection. Backbone gains reflect the complete post-training pipeline, so they do not isolate individual contributions. Reference-model rankings use source-specific subsets, evaluators, and harnesses rather than uniformly controlled conditions (§4.1). ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** FCR finds a minimum only among tested proposal combinations, not among all possible repairs (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Verifier reliability is bounded by the evaluation contract and validation-control coverage. VTCA additionally depends on checker coverage and accurate dependency records (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Recorded use does not establish causal necessity; wrong provenance and write–revert cycles can alter updates. VTCA has no established policy-invariance or unbiased-gradient guarantee (Appendices A, C.4).
- **Author-stated:** Construction-ablation agent success can reflect differences in admitted tasks, although the same-intent rewriting experiment addresses this concern (§4.3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization across model backbones remains unestablished because policy-training experiments use one backbone (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Matched rollout budgets do not establish equal total compute: checker, capture, and CPU costs are accounted for separately, without totals in the supplied text (D.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does prerequisite credit add value beyond local progress and trace costs?** D.2 describes component controls but supplies no numerical outcomes. Report local-only, verified-recipient, random-recipient, and trace-cost ablations under matched budgets.
2. **How robust is VTCA to incomplete provenance and checker coverage?** These are acknowledged signal limits. Systematically remove dependency links and supported checks, then measure transfer and credit-allocation errors.
3. **Does recursive repair remain reliable over longer evolution?** Reported stability covers three generations, with task-success trends over five iterations (§4.3.2). Extend maintenance runs and audit accumulated intent drift and regressions.
4. **Do the gains transfer across backbones?** Only one trained backbone is evaluated. Repeat matched SFT/RL comparisons on additional model families and sizes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the methods and major comparisons, but omits table bodies and numerical figure contents.

Material gaps include complete benchmark scores and uncertainty estimates, numerical component-ablation results, exact training hyperparameters and total compute, many verifier/admission thresholds, and confirmed code, dataset, and checkpoint availability.
