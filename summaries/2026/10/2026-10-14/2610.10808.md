# Controlled Acquisition and Abstention in Three-Channel Score Conflicts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10808) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10808)


## One-sentence takeaway

In a controlled three-score benchmark, threshold acquisition saves requests without changing always-query decisions, but learned selection remains inferior to pair uncertainty and policy rankings depend on the abstention reward (§§4.4, 6.1, 6.6).

## Problem and motivation

When modalities conflict, prediction accuracy alone does not establish whether acquiring a missing source is worthwhile or abstaining is preferable. The authors identify a gap between **full-modality value**—performance after observing every source—and **pre-request value**—whether available observations can identify useful acquisitions (§1).

The study isolates these decisions through synthetic scores, then tests bounded transfer to descriptors and frozen media features.

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled acquisition-and-abstention protocol:** A policy observes two signed scores and may purchase the third. The fixed threshold rule requests on disagreement or weakness, then answers only if two sufficiently strong scores agree. Utility charges separately for requests and non-ambiguity abstentions (§§3–4.3).
- **Matched decision controls:** The authors establish that threshold and always-query-selective produce identical final actions; their utility difference comes entirely from avoided requests (§4.4).
- **Selection-value diagnostic:** A train-only ridge selector ranks requests under a shared terminal rule and exact budgets, compared with pair uncertainty, random selection, and a privileged oracle (§4.5).
- **Representation and transfer checks:** Equal-information temporal controls and media studies examine whether apparent gains survive matched inputs, encoder changes, and held-out actors or teams (§§5–6; Appendix A).

The claimed contribution is a controlled diagnostic, rather than a generally superior acquisition algorithm (§1.1).

These results establish acquisition savings under a specific decision rule and budget-dependent selection behavior in the simulator. They do not establish general acquisition superiority: the main majority comparison differs in information access and action options, while the stronger matched-budget comparison favors pair uncertainty.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Reward-dependent interpretation:** Both mixed and ambiguity mechanisms are label-nonidentifiable, yet only ambiguity rewards rejection. Their differing zero-score variances provide a statistical cue for this preference (§3).
- **Restricted synthetic setting:** Scalar scores, a small mechanism family, duplicated invert/swap transformations, and a fixed threshold limit generality. Latency, privacy, missingness, and alignment are unmodeled (Appendix E).
- **Comparison confounds:** Whole-policy learned acquisition changes both request scheduling and the no-request action rule, preventing attribution to selection alone (§6.3).
- **Temporal validity:** Fixed waveforms favor full traces over time means; matched full-trace controls remove the central neural advantage. Temporal models receive no ambiguity training and never abstain (§§5, 6.2).
- **Media uncertainty:** Encoder-dependent results, overlapping actor allocations, descriptor abstractions, and only two AMI evaluation teams constrain transfer claims (Appendices A, E).

**Analyst inference:** Strong synthetic targeted accuracy primarily demonstrates compatibility with the benchmark’s reward convention; it provides limited evidence of broadly applicable safety behavior.

## Open research questions

- **Can selection outperform pair uncertainty under distribution shift?** The learned selector loses at every tested budget. A next experiment could compare alternative ranking objectives on separately frozen corruption families using the same terminal rule and budgets.
- **Can abstention reflect non-identifiability consistently?** The benchmark treats two nonidentifiable mechanisms differently. A next experiment could train and evaluate under a common rejection target for both, with costs calibrated independently.
- **Can natural-media routing gains survive encoder and population changes?** Current intervals and encoder swaps leave this unresolved. A next experiment could preregister encoders and request budgets, then evaluate on additional disjoint actors and meeting teams.
