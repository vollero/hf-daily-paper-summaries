# PANINI-DR: Controlled Deep Research through Generative Semantic Workspaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15208) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15208)


## One-sentence takeaway
PANINI-DR combines persistent, evidence-linked research plans with subquestion-level verification, improving BrowseComp-Plus accuracy and retained-evidence quality at substantial computational cost (§§4.1–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Deep research requires intermediate findings to guide later searches and computations. The authors argue that keeping research state implicit in growing conversation histories makes dependencies difficult to track and allows early errors to propagate. Existing systems already use persistent memory and verification; the identified gap is an execution protocol connecting explicit subquestions, local evidence checks, and findings reused across assignments (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Research workspace and execution protocol:** An Orchestrator coordinates Researchers using a GSW-based dependency graph with typed unknowns, retrieval hints, verification claims, and evidence-linked findings. Plans undergo structural checks and can be revised. GSW is inherited; the contribution is how assignments, review, admission, and reuse are connected (§§2.1–2.4, 5).
- **Local research and verification:** Researchers attempt focused retrieval before escalating to ReAct search. A separate Verifier checks support and distinction from plausible alternatives; feedback can trigger further investigation. Computations execute over admitted inputs (§§2.2–2.4).
- **Evidence-aware evaluation:** Observed support retains answer credit recoverable from saved evidence. Complementary claim-support audits, common-reader tests, and calculation replay examine different aspects of evidence quality (§3.2; Appendix C).

Admission is not an absolute verification gate: after exhausted retries, the harness can admit warned findings, and fallback synthesis can answer without a resolved workspace target (Appendices B.3–B.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong BCP accuracy improvement | On 150 CF-150 questions, PANINI-DR scores **73.3%**, versus SLEUTH’s **57.3%**; paired difference **+16.0 percentage points**, 95% CI **[+8.0, +24.0]**. Systems share backbone and retrieval service. | §4.1; Tables 17, 21 |
| Average leadership does not imply winning every benchmark | Authors report the best equally weighted three-benchmark average. PANINI-DR ranks fifth on MoNaCo official F1; the FRAMES accuracy advantage over SLEUTH is **+1.9 points**, CI **[−1.0, +4.9]**. | §4.1; Appendix D.4, Table 21 |
| Saved-evidence advantage | Authors report highest observed support on all three benchmarks. On BCP, excluding identifier-only reconstructed evidence leaves **70.7% versus 54.0%** for SLEUTH; difference **+16.7 points**, CI **[+8.7, +24.7]**. | §4.2; Appendix C.1 |
| Planning/state package improves accuracy | Reduced harnesses on the same 150 BCP questions improve from **41.3% to 58.0%**; with common fallback synthesis, **56.7% to 73.3%**. Both retain Researchers and verification. | §4.3; Appendix D.2.1 |
| Verification policy improves results | On 150 BCP questions, full verification adds **16.0 points** to both accuracy and observed support over removing LLM review and citation/evidence checks. Median prompt use rises from **0.453M to 1.042M** tokens. | Appendix D.2.2; Table 21 |
| Gains involve substantially more computation | Separate BCP engine measurements estimate **7.3×** SLEUTH’s model-core computation. Eight sampled SLEUTH runs use **1.12×** PANINI-DR’s estimated computation but achieve **64.7% versus 73.3%** accuracy. | §4.4; Appendices D.3.1–D.3.2, Table 17 |
| Collection completeness remains weak | On 595 MoNaCo collection questions, PANINI-DR has precision **0.690**, recall **0.462**, and mean F1 **0.495**, versus SLEUTH’s F1 **0.528**. | Appendix D.4, Table 20 |
| Fallback recovery materially contributes | Finalizer synthesis and stored-candidate fallback supply **40 of 110** correct BCP answers; **38** pass observed support. These outputs do not imply verified target completion. | Appendix D.1.2 |

The evidence supports benefits from the tested planning/state and verification packages, especially on BCP. It does not isolate every component or establish universal benchmark superiority. Observed support measures recoverability, while semantic support requires separate audits. The computation comparison tests one repeated-sampling policy and excludes attention and runtime overhead.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One research backbone and frozen corpora; other models and open-web research remain untested. Planner omissions and incorrect model judgments can undermine local verification (§6).
- **Incomplete enforcement:** Warned values remain usable, their warnings do not propagate to derived outputs, and list completeness does not independently gate endorsement. Recorded revision behavior can leave affected findings stale (Appendix B.3).
- **Evidence-measure limitations:** Saved evidence varies across systems; headline observed support permits identifier-based reconstruction and cannot establish support for every claim. Claim-support audits also depend on model judgments (Appendix C; §6).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Planning/state is a bundled intervention; verification changes both checks and subsequent research expenditure. These ablations therefore do not establish the isolated benefit of GSW or of the Verifier alone (Appendices D.2.1–D.2.2).
- **Comparison validity:** Default budgets are unequal. Some baselines are reimplemented; Re-TRAC uses the shared backbone rather than its specialized checkpoint and has substantial answer-output failures (Appendices A.1, D.5).
- **Uncertainty and reproducibility:** Most headline settings use one research execution; repeated-run checks focus on BCP. The public implementation is promised rather than supplied (Appendices A.2, D.4; Reproducibility Statement).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the protocol generalize across models and open-web settings?** Both remain untested (§6). Repeat matched comparisons with multiple backbones and live-web retrieval.
2. **Which mechanisms deliver the best benefit per unit of computation?** Existing ablations bundle components and alter expenditure. Run component-level interventions under matched measured compute, including an alternative structured-plan representation.
3. **Can collection recall improve while preserving precision?** MoNaCo results expose missed valid items, and completeness does not gate endorsement. Test explicit coverage tracking and completeness checks on collection questions.
4. **Can stricter state maintenance prevent downstream errors?** Warned-value reuse and incomplete invalidation are documented. Compare current behavior with propagated warning status and complete dependency invalidation, measuring accuracy, completion, and cost.
