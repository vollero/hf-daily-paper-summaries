# EMHO: EMbodied Agent Harness Optimization via Experience Traces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08432) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08432)


## One-sentence takeaway
EMHO improves frozen embodied agents by revising their prompts and runtime logic from execution traces, although its reported held-out maxima do not measure the generalization of a single search-selected harness (§3; Table 1; Appendix B).

## Problem and motivation
Embodied agents depend on a surrounding *harness* that manages planning, memory, observations, tools, validation, and recovery. The authors argue that this machinery remains largely manually engineered, while existing embodied self-improvement methods primarily adapt skills or recovery components. Sparse action feedback and delayed task outcomes make broader harness failures difficult to diagnose (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **EMHO:** A frozen vision-language model executes tasks and records observations, plans, tool interactions, actions, feedback, and outcomes. An optimizer uses these traces and the history of accepted and rejected revisions to edit harness instructions and code. Candidates replace the parent only when search-set success strictly improves (§3.1). The same model serves as agent and optimizer in each run (Appendix B).
- **EMHO-Merge:** For a shared harness across subtasks, compare parent and candidate trajectories on episodes gained and lost. When evidence supports a distinguishing condition, refine the behavior’s trigger or implementation, allowing at most one evaluated repair per original revision (§3.2).
- **Behavioral analysis:** The authors document changes beyond recovery, including progress monitoring, conditional vision-tool use, and visual grounding (§5; Appendix D). These observations support broader behavioral adaptation, without isolating each edit’s causal contribution.

The evidence supports improved benchmark performance among the generated harnesses and behavioral changes consistent with the proposed mechanism. However, held-out scores are retrospective maxima that can come from different iterations than search maxima; the authors explicitly reject interpreting them as generalization estimates for one search-selected harness (Appendix B). Behavioral associations also do not establish that a particular edit caused a success improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated overfitting:** Later search improvements can coincide with held-out plateaus or declines, motivating better harness selection (§6).
- **Analyst inference—selection optimism:** Retrospective held-out maxima can overstate what a deployable search-only selection procedure would achieve (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—limited robustness evidence:** Evaluation covers one benchmark and two underlying models. Context ablations cover only Navigation Base; the supplied text gives no repeated-run uncertainty estimates (§4; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Published model baselines come from external reports, and one-shot rows lack split labels. The broad claim that EMHO beats one-shot revision across domains therefore needs qualification (Table 1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism attribution:** Qualitative measurements support changed behavior, but component-specific interventions do not establish which non-recovery edits drive task-success gains (§5; Appendix D).

## Open research questions
1. **How should a harness be selected without inspecting held-out performance?** Search and held-out optima diverge (§6; Appendix B). Next experiment: compare search-only selection and validation-based early stopping on an untouched final test split.
2. **Does gain–loss repair improve shared-harness generalization?** Merge beats Combined on search, but Combined held-out results are missing (Table 3). Next experiment: evaluate both using the same selection rule and repeated runs.
3. **Which harness changes produce the gains?** Behavioral analyses associate tool routing, memory, and grounding changes with outcomes (§5). Next experiment: selectively revert these components in a fixed evolved harness and measure task success and relevant behavior metrics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the supplied method description and reported table values; **medium** in generalization and causal interpretations.

Material missing information includes episode counts and split construction details, seeds and repeated-run variability, optimization cost and latency, full runnable configurations, a prospective harness-selection evaluation, Combined held-out scores, and precise annotation procedures and denominators for behavioral measurements.
