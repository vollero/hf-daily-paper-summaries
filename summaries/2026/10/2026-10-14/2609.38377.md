# PhyProbe: Rethinking Physical Consistency Evaluation in Generated Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38377) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38377)


## One-sentence takeaway
PhyProbe combines frozen video features with ranking, regression, and anchor supervision to improve physical-plausibility scoring, although its scores do not establish physics-specific measurement or absolute calibration (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Generated videos can look realistic while depicting impossible dynamics. The authors argue that evaluation needs continuous violation-severity scores that remain comparable across settings, rather than binary plausibility judgments or dataset-dependent ratings. They identify two gaps: existing evaluators can rely on semantic shortcuts, and available supervision provides noisy absolute ratings or relative preferences without a reliable shared scale (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Lightweight video evaluator:** A frozen pretrained encoder—primarily Perception Encoder—feeds temporal mean, standard-deviation, and maximum features into a three-layer MLP. Inference requires one video without a prompt or reference (§3).
- **Heterogeneous supervision:** Approximately 371K video pairs from seven sources combine real–generated comparisons, real–real ties, synthetic failures, human ratings, and artifact-derived heuristics (§3; Appendix B.1).
- **Unified scoring objective:** Bradley–Terry ranking learns ordering, Huber regression learns approximate magnitude, and endpoint anchors encourage a common scale. The head is unconstrained; reported outputs are clipped to [0,1] at inference (§3, Eqs. 1–5).
- **Evaluation across comparison settings:** Experiments distinguish correspondence and pair composition, with additional loss, backbone, aggregation, and dataset-removal ablations (§4; Appendix A.2).

The contribution is this evaluator and supervision framework; the underlying encoders and source datasets are existing assets.

These results support useful ranking and alignment with perceived plausibility. The authors report leading three of four physical benchmarks (§4.2), but the complete core comparison table is unavailable in the supplied text. Correlations do not measure absolute calibration (§4.1), and clipping guarantees an output interval rather than validating severity units. Preference transfer also does not demonstrate that the evaluator isolates physics from visual quality.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated temporal limitations:** Long-horizon causal violations and instantaneous anomalies can be missed; unusual but valid real motion can receive elevated scores (§5; Figure 8).
- **Author-stated construct limitation:** Targets approximate human-perceived plausibility, which overlaps with broader quality. A controlled physics-versus-quality evaluation is missing (§5; Appendix A.3).
- **Restricted generalization evidence:** VF2 test supervision is disjoint, but all prompts and generators overlap with training. Its results therefore do not establish prompt- or generator-level distribution shift robustness (Appendix C).
- **Unvalidated low-score interpretation:** Scores below 0.5 lack direct severity annotations; differentiated predictions there have only indirect support (Figure 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparable Likert semantics, heuristic artifact scores, and real-video endpoint labels are operational assumptions rather than independent physical ground truth (Appendix B.1).
- **Preliminary normalization validation:** The blinded study uses 30 pairs and four non-expert annotators, with Fleiss’ κ = 0.148; authors explicitly treat it as preliminary (Table 11 caption).

## Open research questions
1. **Does PhyProbe isolate physics from appearance?** The paper lacks controlled disentanglement evidence (§5). A next experiment could vary physical validity while holding scene, generator, prompt, and rendering quality fixed.
2. **Are score magnitudes stable under new distributions?** Correlations and endpoint ranges do not establish absolute calibration (§4.1). Test fixed severity anchors across unseen generators and prompts without recalibration.
3. **How can brief and long-horizon failures be detected reliably?** Both remain documented weaknesses (§5). Compare temporal sampling rates and order-sensitive aggregation on violations with controlled duration and causal delay.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and several results, but many tables contain captions without cells.
