# UniForge3D: Joint 3D Reconstruction and Generation in a Shared Anchor-Guided Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15357) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15357)


## One-sentence takeaway
UniForge3D jointly predicts observed geometry and complete 3D structure in an anchor-guided coordinate system, then selects image evidence per voxel to refine shape; the authors report benchmark improvements, but numerical result tables are absent from the supplied text (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Sparse object images with unknown camera poses provide incomplete geometric evidence. Reconstruction captures visible surfaces, while generation supplies priors for unseen regions, but combining them can propagate reconstruction errors or favor plausible shapes over input-specific details. The authors identify two gaps: incompatible reconstruction/generation coordinate systems and indiscriminate fusion of views with different visibility and detail quality (§1).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Shared anchor-guided geometry.** Both branches predict geometry in a centered, normalized object space whose azimuth follows the first input image while retaining the canonical upright direction. Bidirectional feature exchange jointly supports point-map reconstruction and sparse-structure generation, without an additional inter-branch alignment (§3.1).
2. **Structure-conditioned reconstruction.** Generated voxel centers condition point-map prediction through cross-attention, producing pixel-to-voxel correspondences in the shared space (§3.2.1; Appendix A.1.2).
3. **Voxel-wise view conditioning.** Each voxel selects the view with the largest connected component of supporting pixels after confidence filtering. Shape generation alternates attention to all views with attention to each voxel’s selected view; unsupported voxels use neighboring support or the anchor (§3.2.2). The texture stage reuses this scheme with fixed shape latents (Appendix A.1.2).

The system adapts pretrained TRELLIS.2 and VGGT with DINOv3 image features; these backbones are dependencies rather than new contributions (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Synthetic training leaves severe occlusion, strong reflections, and extreme lighting challenging (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative generalization is demonstrated only through rendered views of held-out objects; real-world robustness is supported by qualitative examples rather than a reported quantitative real-world benchmark (§§4.1–4.2; Fig. 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Post-hoc Sim(3) alignment limits conclusions about accuracy in the original output coordinate frame (§4.1).
- **Evidence gaps:** Missing numerical tables prevent checking improvement magnitude. The supplied text does not report uncertainty estimates, inference latency, memory use, or confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
- **Scope uncertainty:** Training and quantitative testing use four views; sensitivity to view count, anchor choice, and the pixel-support selection heuristic is not reported (§4.1; Appendix A.1.1–A.1.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How sensitive is performance to anchor choice and view coverage?** The first view defines the anchor, but no sensitivity study is supplied. Next experiment: permute anchors and vary input count and angular coverage on fixed objects.
2. **Can incorrect generated structure reinforce reconstruction errors?** Reconstruction explicitly conditions on generated voxels, but controlled structure-error tests are absent. Next experiment: perturb occupancy and measure point-map error and final mesh quality.
3. **Does pixel support reliably identify the best view under difficult imaging conditions?** Support is presented as a practical proxy, with qualitative examples rather than isolated validation (Appendix A.2.4). Next experiment: compare support-based selection with visibility-aware or confidence-weighted alternatives under controlled occlusion and reflections.
4. **Would simulated adverse conditions improve real-world robustness?** The authors propose this direction without reporting results (§5). Next experiment: train matched models with and without those augmentations and evaluate a controlled real-capture set.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and training protocol are described clearly, but empirical strength remains uncertain. Material missing information includes numerical contents of Tables 1–4, uncertainty estimates, precise appearance-evaluation camera sampling, quantitative real-world results, inference resource requirements, and confirmed implementation/model availability.
