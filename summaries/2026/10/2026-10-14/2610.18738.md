# Enabling Token-wise Importance Sampling in Off-Policy RLVR with Proximal KL Regularization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18738) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18738)


## One-sentence takeaway

Full future KL credit preserves the proximal stationary target of critic-free token-wise off-policy updates under stated assumptions, while a practical bounded-credit variant improves aggregate math accuracy over GRPO in all seven tested off-policy comparisons (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation

Asynchronous rollouts and response reuse make RLVR training data off-policy. Exact response-level importance sampling multiplies likelihood ratios across long responses, potentially producing high variance; token-wise weighting avoids that product but generally biases the gradient. The authors investigate when a critic-free token-wise update can nevertheless retain the correct KL-regularized optimization target (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Stationary-policy guarantee:** Combining current-token importance weights with full future learner-to-reference KL credit uniquely identifies the proximal optimizer under coverage, local expressiveness, and population-update assumptions—even for supported mixtures of stale policies. This does not make the update an exact gradient away from that target (§5, Theorem 1).
- **Convergence analysis:** The authors establish continuous-time tabular population convergence under persistent coverage and summarize a finite-sample reward guarantee for a projected linear-logistic variant with repeated reference refreshes, exact target representation, controlled mismatch, and initial correct-solution coverage (§5; Appendix F.3).
- **Practical Proximal KL:** Separates the frozen proximal reference from the sampling policy, logs behavior probabilities, and subtracts bounded future KL costs from group-relative advantages before applying token-wise weights. It uses Adam without ordinary PPO clipping; a finite horizon optionally truncates future credit (§4, Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical investigation:** Compares stability and held-out math accuracy, then ablates importance weighting, baseline perturbation, cost bounds, and future-credit horizons (§6).

The authors identify their novelty as the stationary-policy guarantee for this combination; future-KL gradients and reference/behavior separation are acknowledged prior ingredients (§§1–2).

The evidence supports higher **aggregate response accuracy in the tested off-policy configurations**, not uniform improvement on every benchmark or demonstrated statistical significance for every pair. The population theorem establishes a stationary target; the practical Transformer update introduces additional approximations. Update-count efficiency also does not establish wall-clock or compute savings.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated theory–implementation gap:** Cost clipping, horizon truncation, group normalization, and token averaging can alter the expected update and its stationary policy. The full-credit guarantee does not automatically cover normalized practical advantages (§4; Appendix A).
- **Strong theoretical conditions:** Tabular convergence assumes persistent history coverage. The finite-sample result assumes exact nearby Gibbs-target representation, controlled token mismatch, fresh response groups, projection, and random iterate selection (§5; Appendix F.3).
- **Representation can hide horizon dependence:** Appendix G explicitly notes that tabular representation dimension—and therefore its representation bound—can grow exponentially with response length.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Experiments concern Qwen3 and verifiable math. They do not establish transfer to other model families, domains, or reward types (§6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison sensitivity:** Proximal KL receives manual configuration adjustments, including different horizon choices across staleness settings. Periodic-refresh GRPO and Proximal KL also use different reference-refresh intervals. Results therefore compare complete configurations rather than isolating one update component (Appendices B.2, C.2).
- **Uncertainty reporting:** Table 1 supplies three-seed benchmark variability for 1.7B, but no aggregate SD or uncertainty for the 4B scores.
- **Source limitation:** The supplied text is truncated, omitting substantial appendix material and leaving several referenced tables and figure values unavailable.

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptive horizons improve the accuracy–variance trade-off?** The authors propose adaptation but evaluate fixed choices (§7). Next experiment: compare a variance-guided horizon against tuned fixed horizons under matched training budgets.
2. **How much does the practical update move the theoretical stationary target?** The paper acknowledges that normalization, clipping, and token averaging can change it (Appendix A). Next experiment: use small enumerable response trees to measure target displacement as each modification is introduced.
3. **Do gains persist across domains and model families?** The reported evaluation is restricted to Qwen3 math training (§6). Next experiment: repeat matched staleness/reuse comparisons on another model family and another verifier-scored task.
4. **How robust are the aggregate gains to tuning and random variation?** Some Table 1 gaps are small, and configurations were manually adjusted. Next experiment: use equal tuning budgets and repeated seeds, reporting paired aggregate uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and Table 1 comparisons, but truncation prevents complete coverage or independent assessment of all proofs.

Material missing information includes omitted theoretical assumptions and derivations, complete contents of some referenced tables, numerical values behind most plotted ablations, aggregate uncertainty, 4B seed variability, wall-clock costs, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
