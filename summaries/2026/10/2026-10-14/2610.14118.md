# ClawLoop: Self-Improving Agents via Harness-Native Training Loop

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14118) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14118)


## One-sentence takeaway
ClawLoop closes task generation, self-training, and failure diagnosis into a teacher-free harness-agent training loop, with reported benchmark gains whose magnitudes cannot be checked because the supplied text omits the main results tables’ values.

## Problem and motivation
Harness agents must combine shell commands, file operations, skills, and multi-step reasoning across a broad task space. The authors argue that existing synthesis pipelines largely target fixed tool environments or depend on stronger external models and a single training pass, leaving evaluation feedback outside the training process (§1).

ClawLoop addresses the missing mechanism for turning an agent’s own failures into targeted, executable practice.

## Approach and contributions
- **Closed training loop:** The current model synthesizes tasks, collects trajectories, trains through successful-trajectory SFT followed by GRPO, and diagnoses failures on a held-out synthesized split to guide the next iteration (§§4.2–4.3; Algorithm 1).
- **Complementary synthesis routes:** Capability-driven synthesis generates seeded workspaces, computed answers, and explicit output contracts. Domain-grounded synthesis converts executable tools into skills and grounds abstract task graphs in domain environments (§4.1).
- **Difficulty control and validation:** Multi-hop retrieval and controlled data noise increase difficulty. Oracle execution, leakage checks, and repeated agent trials filter candidates for validity and intermediate difficulty (§4.1).
- **Empirical curriculum transfer:** The authors evaluate the full loop with Qwen3.6-27B and transfer its generated tasks to Qwen3.5-4B and 9B. Those smaller models consume the curriculum but do not run their own self-improvement loops (§5.1; Appendix C).

The proposed contribution is this integrated synthesis-and-feedback system; SFT and GRPO are established training components used within it.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scaling boundary:** Only one model produces the curriculum. Smaller-model transfer does not establish that smaller models can independently synthesize, diagnose, and improve (§C).
- **Author-stated horizon boundary:** Longer training horizons and adaptive stopping remain future work (§6).
- **Author-stated case-study boundary:** The two illustrative tasks are different instances, so their verification-effort difference cannot be attributed precisely to difficulty (§E.3).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The supplied ablations vary synthesis routes, difficulty, and iteration count, but do not isolate diagnostic feedback against a matched no-feedback curriculum or establish compute-matched superiority over ClawGym (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference—verification risk:** Passing a model-generated oracle through its model-generated verifier establishes consistency, not independent correctness; no independent verifier-quality audit is reported (§4.1).
- **Internal inconsistencies:** §D.1 describes excessive difficulty as exceeding the upper success-count bound, although harder tasks would ordinarily fall below the lower bound. §D.2 describes rejected tasks as unanimous outcomes, although the stated admission band also rejects groups with six or seven successes out of eight (§4.1; Appendix D).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Main score tables, optimization settings, absolute compute costs, and several figure/table contents are missing from the supplied text despite their captions being present.

## Open research questions
1. **Does diagnosis itself improve learning beyond fresh practice?** No matched feedback-removal experiment is supplied. Compare diagnostic feedback, shuffled feedback, and no feedback with equal task and compute budgets.
2. **Can smaller models operate the complete loop?** They only consume the larger model’s curriculum here (§C). Run synthesis, diagnosis, and training independently at each model scale.
3. **How reliable are synthesized verifiers?** Oracle agreement does not independently establish correctness (§4.1). Audit sampled tasks with independently implemented solutions, adversarial outputs, and human review.
4. **Can better difficulty control improve returns per unit compute?** Admission fluctuates and two benchmarks reportedly plateau (§5.2; Appendix D). Compare fixed growth with success-rate-targeted scheduling and adaptive stopping under equal budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method is described clearly, but quantitative external effectiveness cannot be verified.

Material missing information includes benchmark score values and uncertainty, complete ablation results, training hyperparameters, absolute compute costs, held-out split size, baseline budget matching, handling of reused pre-SFT rollouts during GRPO, independent task-quality audits, and release availability for code, tasks, and checkpoints.
