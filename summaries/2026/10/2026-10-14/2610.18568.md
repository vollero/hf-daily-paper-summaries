# WAVE-0: Bridging VLA Policies and WAMs with Lightweight Latent Future Prediction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18568) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18568)


## One-sentence takeaway
WAVE-0 conditions a pretrained π0.5 policy on action-grounded predictions of future visual features, improving reported manipulation success and robustness with modest measured inference overhead.

## Problem and motivation
VLA policies typically map observations and instructions directly to actions without explicitly predicting their consequences. The authors argue that video-based world action models add expensive visual reconstruction, while some latent alternatives use future prediction only for training supervision or learn action representations indirectly from visual transitions (§§1–2).

Their proposed gap is explicit, inference-time future conditioning learned directly from action-labeled robot demonstrations, without additional large-scale video pretraining.

## Approach and contributions
- **Action-grounded latent dynamics:** A world model predicts residual changes in frozen π0.5 visual features from current images, proprioception, and encoded demonstration actions. Future-feature, residual, and counterfactual ranking losses encourage action-dependent predictions (§3.2).
- **Two-stage integration:** After dynamics training, the world model is frozen. The VLM learns latent action plans aligned with the action encoder; predicted futures are compressed into tokens that condition the action expert alongside its original context. Planning tokens cannot directly condition the expert (§3.2; Figure 2).
- **Evaluation and ablations:** The paper tests manipulation success, distribution-shift robustness, training budgets, inference latency, and the effects of future conditioning and training objectives (§§4.2–4.7).

The approximately 257M parameters comprise the world model **plus action and state encoders** in Stage 1; the full policy has approximately 4.3B parameters (§4.1).

The evidence supports improved performance over π0.5 in the tested settings and an additional benefit from explicit future conditioning over auxiliary-only supervision. It does not establish universal superiority, physically accurate predictions for arbitrary alternative actions, or statistical significance. The ranking diagnostic tests separation from mismatched actions against the demonstrated future, rather than observing the actual outcomes of those alternative actions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-noted comparison caveat:** Latency comparisons involve different model sizes and action horizons, particularly for LaWAM (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited uncertainty reporting:** No confidence intervals or variation across training seeds are reported. Real-world evaluation uses only 20 trials per task (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—real-world comparisons have confounds:** WAVE-0’s world model sees demonstrations from all three tasks, while its policies are task-specific. Methods also differ in optimization settings, action horizons, and proprioceptive conditioning (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—restricted architectural evidence:** Integration is demonstrated with π0.5 and its frozen visual representation; transfer to other VLA backbones or embodiments is not established (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference—counterfactual validity remains incomplete:** Ranking rewards mismatched actions for predicting the observed future less well; no executed counterfactual rollout validation is supplied (§4.7.2).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Section 4.1 describes 12 future-conditioning tokens, while Appendix A.1 specifies two camera views for LIBERO; the relationship between this configuration and per-camera pooling is unclear.

## Open research questions
1. **Do predictions capture actual alternative outcomes?** The ranking experiment measures discrimination, not alternative-action accuracy. Execute several action sequences from matched simulator states and compare predicted features with their realized futures.
2. **How much of the benefit generalizes across backbones?** Only π0.5 integration is shown. Repeat the integration and auxiliary-only comparison with another VLA under matched data and training budgets.
3. **How does prediction horizon affect robustness?** Benchmark-specific horizons are supplied without a horizon ablation. Sweep future offsets while holding the action horizon and training budget fixed.
4. **Do real-world gains persist under stricter matching?** Real-world methods differ in training and execution settings. Compare matched settings over more tasks, trials, and training seeds, including a WAVE-0 variant whose world model uses only the target task’s demonstrations.

## Confidence and missing information
