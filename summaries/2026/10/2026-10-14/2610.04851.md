# CURIO: Curiosity-Driven Test-Time Learning for Open-Ended Discovery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04851) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04851)


## One-sentence takeaway
CURIO adds selective hidden-state prediction-error bonuses to test-time RL, improving reported mean mathematical discovery and held-out denoising performance over a matched task-only control without establishing semantic novelty or statistical significance (§4).

## Problem and motivation
Open-ended discovery requires improving candidates through repeated program generation and evaluation. The authors identify a tension: frozen-model search cannot learn through parameter updates, while reward-driven RL may concentrate sampling around current successes and abandon potentially useful alternatives prematurely (§1). They seek an exploration signal that complements completed-candidate rewards during generation.

## Approach and contributions

- **Representation-based curiosity:** An Intrinsic Curiosity World Model (ICWM) jointly trains an encoder and forward predictor on transitions between policy hidden states, conditioned on sampled-token embeddings. It predicts internal representations rather than scientific outcomes (§3.2).
- **Selective integration with RL:** Cached prediction errors are normalized across an epoch, retained only at sampled tokens outside the policy’s top-k choices, and combined with task coefficients under an annealed weight. Experiments use k=1; only policy adapters receive actor updates (§§3.1–3.3; Appendix A).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors compare this addition against a matched task-only update, evaluate mathematical objectives and cross-corpus denoising, and examine predictor architecture, representation coupling, and curiosity strength (§4). The contribution is this integration and evaluation; prediction-error curiosity itself is attributed to prior work (§3.2).

## Evidence and main results

All numerical performance results below are arithmetic means over three discovery runs. Mathematical results select each run’s best feasible candidate; denoising selects an epoch using held-in Pancreas MSE (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad improvement over matched control | At Qwen3 8B, 14B, and 235B, CURIO improves Erdős, AC1–AC3, and Hadamard objectives versus TTT-Discover; Circle Packing ties at the displayed sum-of-radii value of 2.63598. | Table 1 |
| Largest reported mathematical relative gain | On the 235B order-29 maximal-determinant task, normalized determinant score rises from 0.7846 to 0.9284, a reported 18.33% relative improvement. | Table 1; §4.2; Appendix D.1 |
| Held-out denoising improves versus control | Score, MSE, and Poisson NLL improve on PBMC and Tabula at every tested scale. On 8B Tabula, Score rises from 0.6468 to 0.7165—reported as approximately 10.8%—and MSE falls from 0.1963 to 0.1485. | Table 2; §1 |
| Gains do not dominate every baseline | On 8B Tabula, ThetaEvolve has higher Score than CURIO (0.7207 versus 0.7165) and lower MSE (0.1441 versus 0.1485). | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Greater structural code variation | CURIO generally has higher measured diversity across mathematical trajectories, with some crossings; it is higher at every displayed stage for Erdős and Circle Packing. | Figures 1, 3; Appendix F |
| Curiosity strength matters | Qwen3-14B ablations favor initial weights of 0.3 on autocorrelation tasks and 0.5 on Erdős, Hadamard, and aggregated denoising; 0.8 underperforms task-only RL on some tasks. | Figure 4; §4.3 |

The evidence supports better reported mean outcomes from adding CURIO’s curiosity block under the tested protocol, including transfer to corpora excluded from discovery feedback and checkpoint selection. It does not establish statistical significance, semantic exploration, equal computational cost, or new mathematical records. Numerical ablation values and diversity trajectories are not available in the supplied figure text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Uncertainty across runs:** Tables omit variability, and the authors explicitly state that mean differences do not establish statistical significance (§4.1).
- **Curiosity validity and collapse:** Prediction error depends on predictor capacity and representation drift. Joint encoder–predictor training admits constant-feature solutions without an anti-collapse guarantee (§3.2).
- **Exploration proxies:** The diversity metric depends on code length, newline counts, and character sets; formatting can change it without changing behavior. Neither it nor ICWM error establishes semantic novelty (§3.2; Appendix F).
- **Missing component controls:** Ablations do not isolate non-top-k selection, rule out collapse, or establish seed stability (§4.3). Constant-reward groups receive no curiosity updates, limiting exploration when task feedback is uniformly uninformative (§3.3; Appendix B).
- **Budget and implementation ambiguity:** Nominal generation slots do not ensure equal tokens, runtime, or training cost. Realized counts require bootstrap/refill details and logs; temperature-calibration fallback behavior is incompletely specified (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evidence is confined to Qwen3 backbones, program-based tasks, and the specified denoising protocol. Generalization to other model families or discovery interfaces remains untested (§4.1; Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Does selective masking improve exploration?** Existing ablations do not isolate it (§4.3). Compare non-top-k masking with all-token and randomly selected token bonuses at matched bonus scale and compute.
2. **Does prediction error identify useful semantic alternatives?** Current diagnostics measure internal prediction difficulty and superficial code structure (§3.2; Appendix F). Track execution-based strategy diversity and whether high-error branches produce later improvements.
3. **How stable is the ICWM representation?** Collapse is possible and not ruled out (§3.2). Measure feature variance and prediction residuals across seeds, comparing the current loss with an explicit anti-collapse variant.
4. **Do gains persist under verified compute matching?** Realized generation counts and cost equivalence are unresolved (Appendix A). Repeat comparisons with logged token counts, evaluator time, training time, and run-level uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and reported mean comparisons, but leaves material uncertainty about run-level variability, statistical significance, actual compute and generation counts, bootstrap/refill behavior, calibration fallbacks, numerical figure data, and code/model release availability.
