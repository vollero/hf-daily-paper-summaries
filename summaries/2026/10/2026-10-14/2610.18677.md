# Model-Based Online Decision Making via Generative Trajectory Planning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18677) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18677)


## One-sentence takeaway
GTP learns a diffusion prior over action sequences and refines its proposals through learned-model planning, achieving strong benchmark sample efficiency at substantial training-compute cost.

## Problem and motivation
Online control must balance fast action selection with reasoning about delayed rewards. The authors argue that short-horizon value bootstrapping propagates information slowly, while optimizing trajectories from scratch is expensive and vulnerable to learned-model errors. Existing online diffusion policies largely generate individual actions, leaving coherent sequence generation underused for planning (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Generative proposals for planning:** A state-conditioned diffusion model proposes action sequences; learned dynamics, reward, termination, and terminal-value models score their predicted outcomes (§3).
- **Iterative trajectory refinement:** Beam search progressively fixes action prefixes, branches candidates, and refines remaining suffixes through partial noising and denoising (§3.1; Algorithm 1).
- **Planner-driven online learning:** Search-derived, score-weighted trajectories train the prior; Langevin augmentation counters mode collapse. Ensemble disagreement informs multi-horizon value targets and exploration. Aggregation becomes more pessimistic in termination-sensitive environments (§§3.2–3.3; Appendices B–D).
- **Idealized convergence analysis:** Exponentially reweighted prior updates monotonically improve a fixed planning objective and concentrate on optimal trajectories under strong sampling, fitting, and support assumptions (§3.4; Appendix L).

The evidence supports strong performance per environment interaction on these benchmarks, especially early in visual manipulation. It does not establish universal superiority, better total training time, or general failure of reactive policies. The extended PushT results particularly support a sample-efficiency advantage over TD-MPC2 rather than a lasting final-performance gap.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated boundaries:** Model error limits longer horizons; termination-sensitive tasks require different ensemble aggregation. Terminal-value reliance and planning overhead remain concerns (§4.3; §5; Appendix D).
- **Theory scope:** Guarantees exclude finite-budget search, imperfect fitting, and jointly changing dynamics/value models; they concern the learned planning objective rather than guaranteed real-environment return (Appendix L).
- **Analyst inference—visual comparison confound:** GTP’s encoder receives predictive and reconstruction supervision, whereas several baselines receive reconstruction supervision alone. Thus visual gains do not isolate trajectory proposals from representation-learning differences (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference—baseline dependence:** CEM shares GTP-trained models, making it useful for comparing proposal mechanisms but not an independent end-to-end learning comparison. TD-MPC2 is reimplemented; the QSM implementation subsection is empty (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Evaluation covers simulators and one visual task. Additional training-budget experiments cannot by themselves establish the authors’ stronger claim that baseline failures are structural (Appendix F).

## Open research questions
- **Can aggregation adapt automatically?** Fixed optimistic aggregation fails in termination-sensitive tasks. Compare adaptive uncertainty/risk rules against the task-specific schemes across terminating and non-terminating environments (Appendix D).
- **Can longer horizons help without reliable terminal values?** Horizon gains saturate or reverse, and value dependence remains unresolved. Sweep horizons with improved dynamics and reduced terminal bootstrapping, including sparse-reward variants (§4.3; §5).
- **How much visual benefit comes from the trajectory prior?** Encoder supervision differs across methods. Repeat PushT with matched predictive representation objectives and additional visual manipulation tasks (Appendix H).
- **Does sample efficiency translate into practical training efficiency?** Update costs remain high despite reuse. Compare learning curves under matched wall-clock and total-compute budgets (Appendices I–J).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method summary and reported benchmark results; **medium** in causal and generalization claims.

Material gaps include QSM implementation details, the visual latent dimension, complete update-to-interaction scheduling, total end-to-end training compute, explicit evaluation details for planning-free inference, and real-world validation. Code release is reported by the authors but was not independently verified.
