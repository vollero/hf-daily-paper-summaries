# Image Tokenizers Should Be Trained Between Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16759) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16759)


## One-sentence takeaway
InterpTok trains MAETok on mixtures of image codes, improving ImageNet interpolation FID from 14.42 to 4.30 and matched unguided SiT-B generation FID from 11.61 to 8.48 while largely preserving reconstruction quality (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Reconstruction training supervises a tokenizer’s decoder at individual image codes but leaves the space between them unconstrained. The authors argue that this matters because diffusion generators produce previously unseen codes resembling mixtures of training codes; a decoder can therefore reconstruct well yet decode generated codes into blurry overlays (§§1, 3.2).

Existing noise augmentation trains robustness around individual codes. The proposed gap is explicit supervision between codes, where no target image exists. The paper builds on earlier adversarial interpolation methods; its contribution is their adaptation and extension to tokenizers for latent diffusion (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interpolation supervision:** Linearly mix two encoded images and train a source-conditioned discriminator to distinguish decoded mixtures from their sources. Pixel-distance and DINOv3-feature mixing-ratio losses discourage copying either endpoint while preserving the intended composition ratio (§3.3).
- **InterpTok training recipe:** Fine-tune MAETok’s encoder and decoder with reconstruction, interpolation, semantic alignment, and representation Fréchet losses. The alignment losses replace MAETok’s masked-feature auxiliary decoders and aim to preserve generator learnability (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Decoder-only adaptation:** Freeze MAETok’s encoder and train its decoder on mixtures, allowing existing MAETok generators to benefit without retraining (§4.3).

The novelty is the combined source-conditioned, ratio-constrained interpolation recipe for latent diffusion tokenizers, rather than adversarial interpolation or feature alignment individually (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger interpolation | ImageNet iFID falls from **14.42 to 4.30** versus MAETok. Evaluation decodes spherical midpoints between validation codes and selected nearest training codes. Authors report the best value in the cited tokenizer comparison. | §4.2; Table 1; Appendix D.7 |
| Better matched generation | ImageNet gFID falls from **11.61 to 8.48** for class-conditional SiT-B generators trained for **400,000 steps**, sampled without guidance using **250 stochastic sampling steps**; **50,000 samples** per cell. | §§4.2–4.3; Table 2 |
| Fewer sampling steps can help | The abstract reports **8.11 gFID** at approximately one-eighth of the usual sampling steps; the body identifies **32-step** stochastic sampling as improving over the **250-step** InterpTok result. MAETok worsens as steps decrease. | Abstract; §4.3; Table 2 |
| Faster generator convergence | Under the matched SiT-B training recipe, InterpTok reaches MAETok’s final gFID after approximately **half the generator training steps**. | §4.2; Appendix B.1, Figure 18 |
| Decoder training accounts for much of the gain | With MAETok’s encoder and SiT-B generator fixed, **13,000-step** decoder fine-tuning lowers gFID from **11.61 to 8.43**; equally long continuation with MAETok’s losses reaches **10.80**, and noise augmentation reaches **8.83**. | §4.3; Table 3 |
| Codes become more class-discriminative | On **50,000 ImageNet validation codes**, linear-probe accuracy rises from **73.0% to 77.9%**, and the share nearest their own class average rises from **55% to 73%**. | Appendix B.9; Table 21 |
| Reconstruction is broadly retained, with a qualification | ImageNet rFID remains within **3%** of MAETok’s; PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS improve on ImageNet and MS-COCO, but MS-COCO rFID worsens. | §4.2; Table 11 |

The strongest evidence is the matched MAETok comparison and the fixed-generator decoder experiments. Ablations indicate that interpolation and semantic regularization must work together for the full encoder–decoder recipe: either alone produces worse gFID than MAETok (§4.3).

These results do not establish universal superiority across tokenizer architectures or generative tasks. The lowest-iFID checkpoint is also not the best-generation checkpoint, showing that interpolation quality alone is insufficient (Appendix B.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Scope:** Main training uses MAETok and ImageNet; generation is class-conditional and unguided. MS-COCO provides reconstruction and interpolation transfer evidence, rather than a separate generation study (§4.1; Appendix B.5).
- **Author-qualified mechanism:** The explanation that fewer steps help by moving generated codes toward class averages is a hypothesis supported by their measured generators, not a demonstrated general law (Appendix B.9).
- **Analyst inference—metric alignment:** Training includes an Inception-feature Fréchet loss, while headline evaluation uses FID. This creates potential metric-specific optimization. Small loss-removal effects and improvements in unused DINOv2 features partly address this concern (§4.3; Appendices B.1–B.2).
- **Variability and comparison limits:** A second generator seed reportedly reproduces the gap, but repeated draws can differ by up to 0.3 gFID. Some cross-tokenizer values are quoted under source-specific protocols; reproduction differences are explicitly not uncertainty intervals (Appendices B.10, D.7).
- **Smaller-code tradeoff:** The authors’ exploratory smaller-code model gives more semantic compositions at a reconstruction cost, but architecture and other implementation details also differ, preventing clean attribution to bottleneck size alone (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the recipe transfer beyond MAETok?** The main evidence concerns one tokenizer family. Apply matched interpolation training to another continuous tokenizer while controlling decoder capacity and training budget.
2. **Do fewer steps retain image diversity?** The proposed mechanism increases class adherence and within-class similarity, leaving the fidelity–diversity tradeoff unresolved. Compare precision, recall, and within-class diversity across sampling budgets (Appendix B.9).
3. **Does bottleneck size cause more semantic interpolation?** The smaller-code experiment changes several factors simultaneously. Sweep code size under a fixed architecture, dataset, and objective, measuring reconstruction and semantic path quality (Appendix C).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported matched MAETok improvements; **medium** in broader applicability and the proposed sampling mechanism.

The supplied text omits most numerical table bodies, including complete baseline scores, detailed training configuration tables, and second-seed values. Exact compute costs, comprehensive uncertainty estimates, and human judgments of interpolation quality are unavailable. Code and checkpoint availability are author-reported and were not independently verified.
