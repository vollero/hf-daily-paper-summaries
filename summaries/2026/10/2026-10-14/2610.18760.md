# Beyond the Verdict: Agentic Code Judging with Verifiable Feedback

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18760) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18760)


## One-sentence takeaway
Arbiter learns to judge competitive-programming solutions and produce executable counterexamples, improving verification and downstream repair, although selection and refinement comparisons do not match inference compute.

## Problem and motivation
Selecting or revising generated code requires feedback about correctness and failure causes. Official judges provide authoritative test-suite results but typically withhold failing inputs and explanations, and submission limits constrain their use. Existing correctness judges need not provide executable evidence, while faulty-program hacking benchmarks cannot measure recognition of correct code (§§1–2).

The authors address the joint task: judge one candidate of unknown correctness and, when rejecting it, produce a candidate-specific counterexample that can be checked independently.

## Approach and contributions
- **Verification interface:** Given a problem statement and candidate code, the verifier uses Python and shell execution, then returns a verdict, diagnosis, and deterministic counterexample generator. Hidden tests, reference solutions, validators, and checkers remain inaccessible during review; an external judge subsequently checks the evidence (§3).
- **Arbiter training:** A 35B MoE verifier initialized from Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is trained with GRPO on 20,000 candidates across 6,145 problems. Rewards emphasize legal inputs that actually break candidates, rather than unsupported rejection verdicts. Alternating length penalties control rollout growth (§4).
- **USACO-Judge:** The authors report releasing a benchmark containing accepted and unaccepted human- and LLM-written programs, supported by deployed validators and checkers (§5.2).
- **Downstream evaluation:** Experiments test whether verdicts support candidate selection and diagnoses support iterative repair (§§6.2–6.3).

The results support improved verification over the same agentic backbone and useful downstream feedback under the reported protocols. A validated counterexample establishes a particular failure; failure to find one does not establish correctness. The diagnosis audit supports an association, not verification of every explanatory claim. Downstream results do not isolate gains at equal computational cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated boundaries:** Diagnoses receive no direct correctness reward; counterexamples are only a proxy. Acceptance can still be guessed without a witness (§§3–4).
- **Comparison validity:** Selection uses regenerated pools against published baselines; similar aggregate pass@1 does not establish identical selection difficulty. Refinement matches official submission budgets rather than inference compute (Appendices E–F).
- **Audit scope:** Human diagnosis assessment covers one solver’s USACO refinement outputs. For bug claims about officially accepted code, audit correctness itself depends on counterexample validation, partly coupling the proxy and label (Appendix F.4).
- **Analyst inference:** USACO-Judge’s 39 problems, C++17 candidates, and constructed accepted/unaccepted ratio limit conclusions about broader software verification or deployment distributions (§5.2).
- **Resource accounting:** Reported cost estimates exclude input tokens, tool execution, caching, and infrastructure, so they do not establish total verification cost (Appendix D.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Do downstream gains survive equal compute budgets?** Existing comparisons leave compute unmatched. Run selection and refinement with matched total tokens, execution time, or end-to-end cost.
2. **How broadly does diagnosis quality transfer?** The audit covers only GPT-OSS-120B refinement outputs. Repeat blinded audits across candidate generators, languages, and non-contest tasks.
3. **Can acceptance become more reliable without sacrificing repair opportunities?** The false-positive penalty improves precision while reducing recall (§4.2). Evaluate calibrated acceptance thresholds against final selection accuracy and refinement score.
