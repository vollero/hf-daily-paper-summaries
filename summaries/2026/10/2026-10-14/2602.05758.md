# LongR: Unleashing Long-Context Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning with Dense Utility Rewards

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.05758) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05758)


## One-sentence takeaway
LongR combines explicit evidence quotation with a frozen-verifier utility reward, improving LongBench v2 scores over outcome-only RL by 3.39 and 5.03 percentage points for Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-4B, respectively (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Long-context reasoning requires identifying useful evidence and connecting it through deduction. The authors argue that final-answer rewards provide insufficient guidance for learning evidence use, while approaches requiring predefined chunks and chunk identifiers constrain document handling and reasoning format (§1).

LongR targets evidence-grounded reasoning without fine-grained evidence annotations or demonstrations specifically showing interleaved consultation and reasoning. It still requires answer supervision and an SFT cold start (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix B).

## Approach and contributions
- **Think-and-Read generation:** The policy interleaves reasoning with explicit quotations from the supplied context during ordinary autoregressive generation. Quotations are extracted deterministically from square brackets; no separate retrieval action or predefined document chunks are required (§3.3.1; Appendix B).
- **Relative information-gain reward:** A frozen Qwen3-4B verifier scores each quotation as \(1-\mathcal L(s\mid x,c)/\mathcal L(s)\), using token-normalized negative log-likelihood. The average over unique quotations supplements format and answer-correctness rewards; trajectories without quotations receive zero contextual reward (§3.3.1).
- **Progressive training recipe:** Generic reasoning SFT precedes RL at increasing context lengths. The implemented curriculum progresses from 16K to 32K, using synthesized long-context samples and DocQA-RL-1.6K, without dedicated consultation-and-reasoning demonstrations (§3.3.2; Appendix B).

The distinctive contribution is the combination of quotation-based evidence use and verifier-derived relative utility supervision. The paper does not establish that these scores directly measure causal usefulness to answering.

The strongest evidence supports improved benchmark performance from contextual rewards beyond quotation prompting. Appendix B states that LongBench v2 scores average four runs on the same 503 examples and enable optional partial credit for unparseable answers.

The evidence does not establish causal evidence use, broad immunity to reward hacking, or overall computational superiority. Controlled diagnostics support query-sensitive reward rankings, and NIAH analyses report comparable reasoning lengths and distractor usage, but their scope is limited (Appendices D–E). Table 6 shows gains over SFT across RL algorithms; without matched outcome-only CISPO/GSPO controls, it does not isolate the contextual reward’s contribution for those algorithms.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Computational constraints limit RL training to 32K contexts; longer-context training remains unexplored (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—proxy validity:** The reward measures quotation predictability given the document and query. Irrelevant document-specific text can still receive positive rewards; controlled ranking tests do not establish general relevance calibration (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—curriculum attribution:** Stage-wise gains also involve additional training, longer contexts, and added DocQA data. No matched non-curriculum control isolates the curriculum’s benefit (Appendices B–C).
- **Analyst inference—statistical scope:** Decoding variability is reported for one fixed LongR checkpoint, without training-seed variability or matched baseline uncertainty (Appendix H).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Outcome-only scores differ between Tables 1 and 4, and the shared-prompt control differs again. Appendix B also describes the main baseline as using the quotation template, leaving the relationship among controls unclear.
- **Reward stability:** The original reward has no finite lower bound and is undefined at zero prior NLL; clipping addresses only the lower tail (Appendix I).

## Open research questions
- **Does the reward track causal evidence utility?** Positive rewards for irrelevant text leave this unresolved. Remove or replace selected quotations and measure answer accuracy alongside reward changes.
- **Does curriculum ordering independently help?** Stage comparisons conflate ordering, data, and training duration. Compare progressive and mixed-length training with identical data and compute.
- **How reliable is training beyond 32K?** Only shorter-context RL is tested. Run matched training at longer contexts and report accuracy, stability, memory, and step time.
- **How general is the reward across model families?** Policies and verifiers are Qwen3-based. Repeat matched reward ablations with other policy and verifier families.
