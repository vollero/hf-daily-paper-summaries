# Pristine Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14091) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14091)


## One-sentence takeaway

Pristine Models use prompt decomposition and detachable attention branches during pretraining to suppress predefined restricted concepts while recovering generation quality lost by safe-only training, although open-world suppression remains incomplete (§§2, 3.5).

## Problem and motivation

Removing restricted images from training also removes useful information about lighting, materials, geometry, and interactions. Conversely, learning from all images entangles restricted concepts with useful representations, making subsequent removal potentially damaging. The authors investigate whether pretraining can separate transferable information from restricted identities before that entanglement develops (§1).

The scope is concepts designated before training; the authors explicitly retain a role for post-hoc unlearning when restrictions change (§§1, 5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Pristine Attention:** Independently parameterized shared, safe, and unsafe branches replace existing attention layers. The shared branch operates alongside the appropriate mode branch; spatial updates are summed while text streams remain separate. The unsafe branch is removed for deployment (§2.1).
- **Explicit information routing:** LLM-generated prompt decompositions assign restricted identity to the unsafe branch and transferable attributes or context to the shared branch. ImageNet uses three branches; open-world CC12M uses shared and unsafe branches, with safe examples routed entirely through shared attention (§2.2; Appendix A.5.1).
- **Empirical evaluation of suppression and transfer:** Experiments cover cross-attention U-Nets and self-attention MMJiT, comparisons with unlearning methods, material–object composition, and larger open-world training (§§3.1–3.5). These are evaluations of the proposed method, rather than new general-purpose benchmarks.

For CC12M, Appendix A.5.1 explicitly states that inference needs no prompt decomposition and has the same compute as the ordinary models.

The reported evidence supports a useful empirical trade-off and retained attribute transfer. It does not establish complete knowledge isolation, universal safety, or parity with safe-only suppression: CC12M leakage remains higher than Safe. Activating the unsafe branch restores restricted generation, supporting specialization without proving that restricted information exists exclusively there (§3.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Restrictions must be known during pretraining; later restrictions and deployment harms still require other interventions (§5).
- **Analyst inference—measurement risk:** The CC12M judge rejects malformed or visually unconvincing depictions. Consequently, its rate can miss recognizable but poor-quality restricted content; human calibration is not reported (Appendix A.5.3).
- **Analyst inference—ablation ambiguity:** AttentionSwap removes both decomposition and the shared branch, so that comparison does not independently identify each component’s contribution (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—capacity control:** The “compute-equalized” control primarily matches parameter counts by increasing depth. It supports a capacity comparison, but does not establish equal training cost. The two-branch variant separately matches inference compute (Appendix A.3.1).
- **Reporting gaps:** The supplied extraction contains table captions but omits table bodies. Many results survive in prose, but complete comparisons, uncertainty estimates, and per-category outcomes are unavailable.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is suppression to adversarial elicitation?** ImageNet includes implicit prompts, but CC12M reports explicit requests. Next experiment: test paraphrases, indirect descriptions, and multi-concept prompts with human-validated leakage labels.
2. **How sensitive is routing to annotation errors?** The method depends on automated tagging and decomposition without a reported error-sensitivity study. Next experiment: vary controlled tagging errors and identity leakage into shared captions.
3. **Does the CC12M judge underestimate restricted generation?** Its visual-quality requirements create ambiguity. Next experiment: compare its decisions against blinded human judgments that assess recognizability separately from image quality.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and central trade-off clearly. Material gaps include complete table values and category listings, run-to-run uncertainty, human judge validation, full CC12M training configuration, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability. Appendix A.2.4 also broadly says open-world data does not rely on decomposition, whereas Appendix A.5.1 specifies decomposition during training; the latter provides the more detailed protocol.
