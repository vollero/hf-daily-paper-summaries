# ReTerm: Recovery Training for Self-Improving Terminal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18484) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18484)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that training Qwen3.5-9B on teacher demonstrations and online recovery attempts from self-generated failure states raises Terminal-Bench 2.0 Pass@1 to 34.8%, a 14.6 percentage-point gain over the base model (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation

Terminal agents can compound command, implementation, and diagnostic errors during long-running tasks, especially when earlier actions have already changed the environment. The authors identify two gaps: artificially injected training errors may differ from agents’ natural failures, and recovery-focused supervised training leaves online reinforcement learning unexplored (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **ReTerm-10K recovery dataset:** Run the base model on tasks sampled from TMax-15K, preserve failed execution histories and virtual-machine snapshots, and ask DeepSeek-V4-Pro to recover from those states. The teacher knows the attempt failed but receives no detailed hidden-test feedback. Verifier, format, compliance, and efficiency filters retain roughly 10K successful continuations (§3.1).
- **Recovery-targeted SFT:** Condition on the task, failed history, and a minimal recovery prompt; apply loss only to teacher-generated continuation tokens (§3.1, Eq. 1).
- **ReTerm-GRPO:** Branch multiple recovery rollouts from the evolving policy’s failed states, derive group-relative advantages from task-verifier rewards, and jointly optimize original-task and recovery-task groups. A queue prioritizes recent failures and retains older ones when fresh failures are scarce (§3.2).

The distinctive contribution is recovery training on naturally generated, state-changing failures, followed by online adaptation to the current policy’s errors. The authors state that code, data, and model are released; the supplied text does not independently verify availability (Abstract).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Most encountered errors still resist recovery outside the tool-use category (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence spans several tasks and harnesses but only one trained base model, Qwen3.5-9B; generality across model families or scales is untested in the supplied text (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported ablation does not isolate queue recency, individual filters, teacher choice, or natural failures versus injected errors. Their separate contributions remain uncertain (§§3–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Recovery judgments involve an LLM plus manual verification, but annotation counts, agreement, and uncertainty are unavailable (§4.5).
- **Evidence limitation:** Tables appear only as captions and narrative summaries; figures lack plotted values. Appendix prompt and case-study headings lack their substantive contents.

## Open research questions

- **Does recovery-specific RL help at matched compute?** The supplied ablation lacks numerical results and compute accounting. Compare GRPO and ReTerm-GRPO with matched rollout tokens and training cost.
- **Which failure-selection strategy works best?** Queue recency is motivated but not separately evaluated. Compare recent-first, uniform, and error-balanced sampling.
- **Why do non-tool errors remain difficult?** Recovery remains low across those categories (§4.5). Evaluate paired continuations from identical failure states while varying diagnostic support.
- **Does the method generalize across base models?** Only one trained model is reported. Repeat the pipeline across model families and scales using controlled task splits.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline findings are explicit, but quantitative verification is constrained by missing table bodies and figure values.

Material omissions include exact ablation scores, per-category recovery counts and rates, uncertainty estimates, training duration and total compute, queue capacity, substantive prompt templates, and details establishing task-level separation and benchmark decontamination.
