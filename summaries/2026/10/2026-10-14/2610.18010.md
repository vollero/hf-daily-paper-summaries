# Fish Audio Drama 3 Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18010) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18010)


## One-sentence takeaway
Fish Audio Drama 3 combines a hybrid mixture-of-experts backbone, context-aware acoustic decoder, and preference-only reinforcement learning to unify directed speech, revision, and editing, with reported benchmark gains and faster decoding than S2 (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Expressive speech applications require performances that can be directed, revised, staged across characters, and corrected locally. The authors identify four gaps in S2: expensive long-context attention, single-shot control, regeneration of entire takes for local corrections, and rewards that can penalize legitimate expressive changes such as whispers or laughter (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Architecture:** A Qwen3.5-initialized hybrid linear/full-attention MoE backbone has 35B parameters with about 3B active per token. Only the first two audio codebooks feed back into the backbone; a new codebook-MTP decoder predicts remaining acoustic codes through dedicated layers with causal access to the full preceding sequence (§2).
- **Unified task formulation:** Cloning, direction, multi-turn revision, scene dubbing, infilling, and streaming text input share a conversation format. Training progresses through three pretraining stages, instruction fine-tuning, and RL (§3).
- **Preference-only RL:** A speech reward model trained on human comparisons supplies the sole reward. GRPO updates the backbone without a KL penalty while freezing the acoustic decoder (§3.3).
- **Serving and release:** The engine combines FP8 experts, fewer kernel launches, workload-specific kernels, and prefill–decode separation. The authors report releasing model weights and inference support (§§1, 4).

The tokenizer is reused unchanged from S2; the backbone initialization and optimization algorithms are existing components (§§2–3).

These results support useful capabilities within the reported protocols. They do not isolate the contribution of each architectural change or establish broad human preference superiority. The Expresso analysis supports the motivation for replacing similarity rewards, but is not a controlled comparison of alternative RL reward recipes (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-disclosed comparison differences:** Several baseline results are quoted from other papers; multilingual scoring differs for some systems. Revision comparisons also combine separately executed judge runs (§§5.1, 5.3.2, 5.6).
- **Analyst inference—selection effects:** Invalid outputs are regenerated before most reported scoring. Final scores therefore describe synthesis with retry filtering; first-take reliability is separately reported only for the Seed-TTS-Eval comparison (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** No controlled ablations isolate partial codebook fusion, decoder design, backbone choice, or preference-only versus mixed-reward RL. Serving optimizations have individual comparisons, but these do not isolate speech-quality contributions (§§2–5).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Scene coherence and long-form performance lack dedicated quantitative evaluations. The sentence-level streaming test does not establish quality under incrementally arriving text with different lookahead conditions (§5.4; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—editing scope:** Editing uses supplied target text and forced alignment, so the benchmark does not establish interpretation of free-form edit instructions (§5.5).

## Open research questions
- **Which architecture changes drive quality?** Their effects are not isolated. Train matched variants changing decoder context access and codebook fusion separately, then compare cloning and expressive control.
- **Does preference-only RL outperform mixed rewards for listeners?** The paper compares SFT with preference RL, rather than competing RL objectives. Run matched training experiments with blinded human assessment of expressiveness, fidelity, and failures.
- **How robust is incremental-text synthesis?** The format assumes one-chunk lookahead, while quality evaluation streams completed sentences. Vary chunk size and lookahead, measuring latency, boundary artifacts, and intelligibility.
- **Do revisions preserve voice and content over longer sessions?** Evaluation covers a short revision sequence focused on target delivery. Extend sessions and jointly track human preference, speaker consistency, and transcription errors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main findings, but most evaluation tables contain captions without their numerical bodies.

Material missing information includes full per-system results, uncertainty estimates, exact training-data composition and quantities, preference-annotation details, reward-model size, exact RL step count and learning rate, retry limits, and controlled architectural/reward ablations. Release availability is reported by the authors and was not independently verified.
