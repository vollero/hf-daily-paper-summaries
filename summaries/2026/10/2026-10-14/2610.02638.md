# Batched Speech Decisions Without Decoding: Single-Token Supervision Lets a Frozen LLM Hear Beyond the Transcript

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02638) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02638)


## One-sentence takeaway
DuplexJev trains a small speech connector to support single-token decisions from a frozen LLM, improving gender and emotion recognition while reducing decision latency in transcript-based serving comparisons, although content transfer and streaming performance remain unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Full-duplex voice agents repeatedly make closed decisions about turn completion, routing, fillers, and speakers. The authors argue that generating text for these decisions adds avoidable latency, while transcription discards vocal information and dedicated classifiers answer fixed questions (§1).

They identify a second gap: transcript-only distillation provides no supervision for information absent from words, such as speaker attributes or emotion, even when the speech encoder retains those cues (§§1, 2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Speech-to-decision interface:** A trainable connector maps frozen ASR features into a frozen LLM. Questions specify options represented by single tokens; restricted, renormalized logits provide the answer distribution without autoregressive answer generation (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Answer-token supervision:** Decision examples supervise only the answer letter. A mixed objective retains transcript distillation for content examples. Controlled objective comparisons and feature probes investigate why transcript distillation fails on paralinguistic tasks (§§2.1, 4.3).
- **Batched execution:** Questions batch across utterances; shared prefixes use cached representations and isolated question suffixes to reduce repeated context processing (§2.3).
- **Reported releases:** The authors report releasing connector weights, training instructions, an inference pipeline, and the bilingual qa100 spoken-QA set (§1, §3). Release contents are not independently verified here.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Single-token readout reduces latency on a matched serving engine | On one H200 with Qwen3-32B/vLLM, ten decisions per event over 30 real utterances take 92 ms using transcript-based readout versus 1.57 s generating JSON, reported as 17× faster. The JSON figure excludes 411 ms ASR time. | §3, §4.1; Table 1 caption and surrounding text |
| Multi-GPU capacity supports low-latency batching | Eight replicated H200 engines finish eight events—80 decisions—in 94–103 ms; reported capacity is 1,613 decisions/s within a 0.5 s budget without dialogue context. This uses the transcript readout approximation. | §4.1 |
| Shared prefixes reduce long-context cost, with imperfect numerical agreement | For 100 questions sharing a 1.5k-token context, packed execution takes 5.1 s versus 37.9 s sequentially. Shared versus separate answers agree on 195/200 pairs for A and 198/200 for B; one discrepancy has a reported margin of 0.31. | §4.1 |
| Last-layer connector approaches transcript QA performance | Connector B after R2 scores 90/100 on qa100 versus 91/100 using the oracle transcript; A scores 83/100. Both score 77–79% on ZJU-ML. | §4.2; Table 2 caption and surrounding text |
| Changing supervision improves paralinguistic accuracy | After gender-pack training, distillation remains at 53% gender accuracy. Answer-token supervision reaches 89% for A and 88% for B on the 800-utterance gender set; emotion supervision reaches 72% and 86%, respectively, on the 800-utterance four-class set. | §4.3; Table 3 caption, Fig. 2, surrounding text |
| Mixed training preserves qa100 better but harms another content benchmark | MIX-KD reports 90% gender and 90% emotion accuracy, with qa100 down 1 percentage point versus 4 for MIX-CE. Both four-task mixtures lose 16–17 points on ZJU-ML; MIX-KD loses 15/50 real-speech factual items and 1/50 TTS items. | §4.3 |
| Encoder features already contain accessible paralinguistic information | Logistic probes report 97–99% gender and 88–93% emotion accuracy across final encoder features and connector embeddings. | §4.3 |

These results support the authors’ explanation that the training objective is a major bottleneck for the tested paralinguistic tasks. They also demonstrate a latency advantage for closed decisions under the measured conversational budgets. They do not establish equivalent end-to-end audio-serving latency on the optimized engine, reliable streaming turn-taking, or general preservation of content understanding. At saturation, the authors report broadly comparable throughput between readout and JSON generation (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Streaming input is not evaluated; replacing VAD or endpointing is a proposed deployment pattern (§2.4).
- **Author-reported content regression:** Mixed training preserves qa100 comparatively well but substantially reduces ZJU-ML accuracy, especially on real-speech factual questions (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—timing transfer:** The optimized vLLM comparison uses transcripts instead of audio embeddings. Direct PyTorch measurements use earlier connectors with a different stacking factor, limiting conclusions about optimized end-to-end audio latency (§3).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation breadth:** qa100 contains a small, synthesized bilingual question set with text options. Paralinguistic evaluation uses corpus-provided gender labels and four emotion classes; broader populations, recording conditions, and label schemes remain untested (§3).
- **Analyst inference—numerical exactness:** Prefix sharing is mathematically intended to preserve outputs, but observed answer disagreements mean empirical equivalence is imperfect in bf16 (§§2.3, 4.1).
- **Missing reporting:** Full numerical bodies of Tables 1–3 are absent from the supplied text. It also provides no confidence intervals, repeated-training variability, or detailed probe split protocol.

## Open research questions
- **Can streaming decisions retain the measured benefits?** Streaming is explicitly unevaluated (§2.4). Test incremental audio on overlapping dialogue, measuring decision accuracy and end-of-turn latency under concurrent load.
- **What causes the ZJU-ML regression?** Mixed training does not prevent losses concentrated on real-speech factual items (§4.3). Vary emotion-data proportion and content replay while tracking performance separately by recording type and task.
- **How large is the optimized end-to-end audio advantage?** The matched-engine experiment substitutes transcripts (§3). Implement audio-embedding readout on the same engine and compare latency, capacity, and decision accuracy against the full ASR–JSON cascade.
- **How broadly does answer-token supervision transfer?** The strongest controlled evidence concerns gender and four-class emotion (§4.3). Train additional vocal decision tasks and evaluate on held-out corpora, languages, and prompt formulations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and central findings, but complete result tables, uncertainty estimates, detailed probe procedures, optimized audio-serving measurements, streaming evaluation, and independently verified release contents are unavailable.
