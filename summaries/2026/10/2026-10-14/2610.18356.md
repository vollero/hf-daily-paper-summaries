# Urbex: Agentic Spatial Grounding for City-Scale 3D Scenes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18356) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18356)


## One-sentence takeaway
Urbex uses an RL-trained vision-language agent to search city-scale 3D scenes through spatial tools, outperforming reported grounding baselines while retaining substantial limitations in precise localization and landmark-independent transfer.

## Problem and motivation
City-scale language-guided object localization requires recognizing small, repeated objects and interpreting distant geographic references. The authors argue that point-cloud downsampling loses necessary detail, while candidate-ranking pipelines depend on proposal quality and incur increasing scoring costs as candidate pools grow (§1).

The identified gap is an interface and learned policy for sequentially inspecting large urban scenes without requiring candidate boxes at inference.

## Approach and contributions
- **Active-search formulation:** The agent starts from a global bird’s-eye view (BEV), gathers observations, and submits a free-form BEV box that is mapped to world coordinates; ground-truth boxes supply training rewards and evaluation targets only (§3.1).
- **City-aware tool environment:** Landmark Query supplies geographic anchors, Zoom-in retrieves high-resolution local evidence, and Oblique Rendering supports visual verification. Views retain consistent coordinate mappings (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Learned execution policy:** Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct is trained using GRPO without supervised trajectory demonstrations. Rewards combine overlap, center distance, interaction cost, and tool-level shaping (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Transfer evaluation asset:** The authors construct STPLS3D-Refer, a manually filtered, VLM-assisted evaluation set for buildings and vehicles in two geographically registered scenes (Appendix B.2).

These results support improved identification, direct localization, and warm inference efficiency within the reported protocols. They do not establish tight 3D boundaries, end-to-end deployment speedups, or broad geographic robustness. Urbex also does not dominate every metric: CityAnchor retains higher CityRefer-ND Hit@5 (Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated dependencies:** Reliable rendering, coordinate calibration, and geographic information are required. BEV-aligned boxes leave fine-grained 3D segmentation unresolved (Appendix E).
- **Landmark dependence:** Missing or incorrect landmarks substantially degrade performance. The transfer benchmark’s original expressions all use landmark cues; its separate landmark-free diagnostic is small (Appendix D.3).
- **Narrow transfer coverage:** STPLS3D-Refer contains two selected scenes and only building and vehicle targets, with semi-automatic instance extraction and VLM-assisted descriptions. **Analyst inference:** This limits evidence for broader urban environments and naturally occurring queries (Appendix B.2).
- **Uncertainty and causal attribution:** Repeated decoding evaluates one checkpoint, without independent training seeds or paired baseline significance tests. Fixed-workflow comparisons do not isolate ordering from argument generation and prompting effects (Appendices D.1–D.2).
- **Comparison and cost scope:** The proposal experiment tests one generator; full preparation costs are unavailable. CityRefer is cleaned and re-split, so reported results concern this paper’s protocol (Appendices B.1, D.4, D.7).

## Open research questions
- **Can transfer survive independently authored, landmark-independent queries?** Current evidence is construction-dependent. Next experiment: evaluate on additional cities with human-written expressions collected without landmark-guided generation.
- **Can final refinement close the identification–extent gap?** Thresholded Hit@1 exposes loose boxes. Next experiment: add a segmentation or boundary-refinement tool and compare direct IoU/F1 under matched interaction budgets.
- **What enables recovery from incorrect regional search?** Saved failures retain wrong predictions after oblique inspection. Next experiment: test explicit region revisiting and distractor comparison, measuring search recovery separately from local identification.
- **Do accuracy and efficiency gains persist across training seeds and deployment workloads?** Current uncertainty covers decoding, and timing excludes preparation. Next experiment: repeat training and measure total preparation-plus-query cost across query volumes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol-level findings; **medium** in broader robustness claims.

Material missing information includes independent training-seed results, paired per-query statistical comparisons, complete scene-preparation costs, diagnostic-bin sample counts, explicit ablation split labeling, and the precise conversion from submitted BEV boxes to evaluated 3D extents. Release availability is claimed but cannot be verified from the supplied text alone.
