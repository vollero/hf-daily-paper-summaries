# Harness^2: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14224) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224)


## One-sentence takeaway
Harness^2 improves frozen-model agents through execution-guided harness edits and a reusable improvement library, reporting gains across three professional benchmarks without using correctness labels during adaptation.

## Problem and motivation
Agent performance depends on prompts, tools, memory, skills, and orchestration as well as model weights. The authors identify two gaps: unseen deployment tasks often lack reliable correctness feedback, and task-specific harness improvements do not automatically improve adaptation to subsequent tasks (§1). Their goal is to retain useful experience about *how to improve harnesses* without simply carrying forward a previous task’s specialized configuration.

## Approach and contributions
- **Contrastive task-level refinement:** Propose separate invariant and domain-specific edits, probe both under matched model and environment configurations, then selectively compose changes using observed behavioral differences. These contrasts guide editing but do not verify correctness (§2.2.1).
- **Two exploration modes:** Parallel recursion pools candidate harnesses and executions for independent composition sessions; sequential recursion carries revisions and accumulated evidence into later steps. Parallel recursion uses one population update, with budget controlling width rather than depth (§2.2.2).
- **Persistent improvement experience:** After each task, retain adopted executable harnesses and supported lessons in an indexed library. Subsequent tasks restart from the common base harness and selectively reuse this material; model weights and improvement-stage instructions remain fixed (§2.3).

The authors locate novelty in this combination of label-free task-stream refinement and accumulated improvement experience, rather than in memory reuse or improver updates individually (Appendix A.1).

Main-table values are means and standard deviations over three independently shuffled task streams (§3.1). Evaluation uses 100 WorkBuddy tasks across its domains, 34 JobBench tasks, and 100 LAB tasks (Appendix B).

The results support benchmark-level improvement over the corresponding base harnesses. Harness^2 also leads the compared baselines on WorkBuddy macro-average and JobBench in every main configuration; Meta-Harness leads on LAB for Gemini/Codex (§3.2). These findings do not establish correctness guarantees, universal transfer, or unrestricted open-world reliability.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Additional rollouts and editor sessions increase inference cost; the tested budgets do not establish an optimal allocation (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** Behavioral contrasts remain vulnerable to stochastic execution and shared model biases, and cannot guarantee correctness or transfer (Appendix F).
- **Observed failures:** Selection sometimes chooses weaker outputs, and incorrect assumptions can propagate across candidates (Appendix E.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains bounded by the professional benchmark subsets, executor models, and harnesses tested. WorkBuddy and JobBench also use model judges matching an executor or editor model, respectively; evaluator independence is therefore limited (Appendix B).
- **Interpretation boundary:** Leave-one-out library reruns access later-task entries and may benefit from repeated execution; the authors explicitly identify them as retrospective reuse tests rather than online gains (Appendix E.2).
- **Missing isolation:** The library ablation removes executable examples and experience together, so it does not isolate their individual contributions (Table 3; §2.3).

## Open research questions
- **Which library contents drive transfer?** The combined library ablation leaves this unresolved. Compare harness-only, experience-only, and combined libraries under matched budgets.
- **Can probes detect shared false assumptions?** Documented failures show agreement can preserve mistakes. Test contradiction-seeking probes against standard probing using retrospective benchmark scores.
- **How should recursion budgets adapt?** Budgets are fixed beforehand, and optimal allocation remains open. Compare evidence-based stopping with fixed budgets under equal total-token limits (Appendices F–G).
- **Does experience remain useful over longer distribution shifts?** Library consolidation and outdated-entry management are proposed future work. Evaluate longer ordered streams with domain shifts and controlled retrieval or pruning policies (Appendix G).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported benchmark gains. Material gaps include exact compute totals and latency, statistical significance tests, full numerical data for several figures, benchmark-specific prompt blocks referenced as residing in the code release, and independent confirmation of released implementation availability.
