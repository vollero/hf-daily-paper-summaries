# Reading Right, Acting Wrong: Diagnosing Decision Failures of System One Models in Agent Loops

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18579) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18579)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across frozen agent states, strong choice-only models often answer every measured factual probe correctly yet choose a wrong action, while controlled experiments show that fact composition and sub-goal selection require different remedies (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
System One models select among supplied actions without generating reasoning, making failures difficult to diagnose. Action accuracy alone cannot distinguish misreading the agent’s history from failing to act on correctly read information; correct actions can also coexist with incorrect factual answers (§1).

The authors address this diagnostic gap through independent factual and action questions on identical states. They explicitly distinguish **correct measured facts** from complete understanding or evidence about internal computation (§§2.2–2.4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired behavioral diagnosis:** Cross action correctness with whether every applicable factual probe is correct, producing four observable outcomes. Questions are answered independently, without feeding probe answers into the original decision (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Probe-sufficiency criterion:** With goals, rules and remaining context fixed, states sharing correct probe answers must have a nonempty intersection of acceptable actions. Otherwise, correct probes cannot support attributing an error solely to deciding (§2.4).
- **Frozen benchmark and controls:** Construct 65,228 records, including 19,663 paired test states, spanning simulators, policies, tools, web navigation and coding. Evaluate seventeen fast systems and a reasoning reference with coverage, latency, cost and calibration measurements (§§3–4).
- **Targeted diagnostic interventions:** Use generated procedures with sufficient probes, trace rewrites, model-produced fact summaries, code-based composition and ALFWorld sub-goal probes to distinguish different behavioral failure patterns (§6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Correct measured facts frequently coexist with wrong actions. | On 19,663 paired test states, 73–85% of decision errors for the six most accurate systems occur with every measured fact correct; conditional action-error rates remain 10–21%. | §5.1; Table 2/Figure 3 captions and accompanying prose |
| Failures concentrate on shared states. | All fourteen competent fast systems make facts-right/action-wrong errors on the same 141 states; the within-cell permutation null expects none. The reasoning reference chooses correctly on 40% of these states. | §5.3; Figure 6 |
| Explicit procedures expose a composition gap. | On 1,200 generated states with sufficient probes, JEV scores 0.791 from traces versus 1.000 from facts alone; Qwen3.8-27B scores 0.797 versus 0.994. Appending relevant gold facts raises them to 0.974 and 0.967. | §6.1; Figure 8 |
| Models’ own facts can support effective external composition. | On the same generated states, JEV’s own fact summary raises accuracy from 0.791 to 0.968; composing actions in code from its answers reaches 0.996. Corresponding code-composed accuracies are 0.987 for Qwen3.8-27B, 1.000 for Clef and 0.965 for Qwen3.5-9B. | §6.1 |
| Generic factual summaries transfer weakly to natural states. | On 2,783 paired test states, correct probe answers add 2.9–7.1 percentage points for fast systems other than SimpleJev and Torchcast; the reasoning reference gains 1.3 points. | §6.2; Table 3/Figure 9 |
| Sub-goal information helps some systems substantially more than facts. | On 644 ALFWorld states, JEV gains 5.3 points from decision-oriented facts, 16.3 from a correct next sub-goal and 21.3 from both, reaching 0.929 accuracy. Its own sub-goal answers are correct on 66.8%. | §6.3; Table 4/Figure 9 |
| Gold labels overstate some action failures. | Exact simulator lookahead finds that 5–42% of a system’s scored wrong decisions, depending on system and simulator, are also optimal. For the reasoning reference’s facts-right errors in TextWorld, this share is 96%. | §5.2; Figure 5 |
| Confidence quality differs across deployed interfaces. | Native decision distributions have 15-bin expected calibration error of 2.6% for Cygnet, 6.8% for JEV, 19.1% for Gemma’s likelihood readout and 26.6% for Vega. The reasoning reference is excluded because its probabilities are verbalized. | §7; Figure 11 |

These results establish a behavioral dissociation between answering measured facts and selecting actions. The generated procedures provide stronger evidence for composition failures because the relevant facts determine the action. Natural-state results do **not** establish that every facts-right error is purely a decision failure, nor that these interventions improve complete agent trajectories (§§2.4, 10).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Independent answers do not reveal internal computation or prove that the action used the correctly answered facts. Natural-state probes are not sufficient for the decision (§10).
- **Gold-label validity:** Some scored errors are optimal under exact lookahead. The authors also identify conflicting labels, dev/test input overlap and ALFWorld location-label errors, retaining frozen labels while reporting sensitivity analyses (§§5.2, 5.4).
- **Deployment confounds:** Serving stacks, readouts, input limits and runtime truncation differ. Unsupported questions count as wrong, so low scores can reflect coverage limits. Hosted results may also vary across runs (§4; Appendix D).
- **Narrow intervention scope:** The work evaluates frozen single decisions, not closed-loop execution; follow-up studies cover subsets of systems, and decision-oriented probes focus on ALFWorld (§10).
- **Analyst inference:** Sub-goal labels should be treated cautiously as diagnostic targets: the reasoning reference answers that question poorly while selecting actions accurately, showing that the labeled decomposition need not match successful behavior (§6.3).
- **Source limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, and several referenced tables contain captions without their numerical bodies. This analysis cannot claim complete coverage or independently reconstruct all reported comparisons.

## Open research questions
1. **Do local repairs improve complete tasks?** Frozen-state evaluation leaves this unanswered (§10). A next experiment could compare direct selection, fact-to-code composition and planner-assisted selection in live simulator rollouts, measuring completion, regret and total cost.
2. **Can sufficient probes be constructed for less structured workflows?** Natural-state probes leave most errors unresolved (§6.2). A next experiment could search for states with identical probe answers but disjoint gold sets, then expand probes and retest interventions.
3. **Can useful sub-goals be supplied without an oracle?** Correct sub-goals help JEV and Torchcast substantially, but their own answers help less (§6.3). Compare external planners, deliberating models and learned sub-goal selectors under matched resource budgets.
4. **How much shared failure remains after broadening gold sets?** Exact lookahead reclassifies many scored errors as optimal (§5.2). Recompute cross-system error overlap using all lookahead-optimal actions and audited factual labels.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied main text supports the central behavioral findings and their qualifications, but the source is truncated.

Material missing information includes full numerical bodies of several result tables, many per-system uncertainty estimates, complete omitted content, and verified public availability of the benchmark, generators and raw run artifacts.
