# FlashVSR+: Efficient Video Super-Resolution via Hybrid Attention and Reward-Yield Distribution Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17552) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17552)


## One-sentence takeaway

FlashVSR+ combines hybrid local/global attention with black-box reward-guided distillation, reporting spatially untiled 4K video super-resolution on a 24GB RTX 4090 and improved text rendering, although most main-result table values are absent from the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation

One-step diffusion distillation reduces video super-resolution (VSR) sampling costs, but high-resolution attention still consumes substantial compute and memory. Spatial tiling can introduce boundary artifacts and restrict context. Standard distribution matching distillation (DMD) also lacks an explicit mechanism to favor preferred outputs when rewards, such as OCR accuracy, provide no gradients (§1).

The authors target both inference efficiency and reward-guided training of deterministic one-step generators.

## Approach and contributions

- **Hybrid attention:** Replace most attention blocks with local spatiotemporal attention, retaining periodic global blocks. The final configuration uses `(s,t,b)=(3,1,25)`: spatial context `s`, preceding temporal context `t`, and `b` localized DiT blocks, with a global block every five blocks. Adaptation training is necessary for this configuration (§3.2; §4.2).
- **Reward-Yield DMD (Ry-DMD):** Sample stochastic teacher continuations from a re-noised student output, score them with a black-box reward, and compare reward-weighted versus ordinary endpoint means. Their difference corrects the DMD teacher term without differentiating through the scorer (§3.3).
- **Integrated training and implementation:** Use a 14B teacher, initialize and adapt a 1.3B hybrid student, then perform one-step distillation with pixel loss. RTX 4090 inference additionally requires adapted attention kernels and CPU offloading/prefetching of global-layer KV caches (§3.1; Appendices A–B).
- **Empirical findings:** Test Ry-DMD on OCR-guided VSR and image generation, and show that longer DMD training does not consistently improve VSR quality (§§4.3–4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported boundaries:** Excessively local attention still causes artifacts after adaptation; overly strong reward weighting or correction degrades SDXL results (§4.2; Appendix C).
- **Teacher support constraint:** The proposed reward-tilted target preserves teacher support. **Analyst inference:** improvement depends on useful variation being present in teacher continuations (§3.3; Appendix E).
- **Reward validity:** VSR post-training uses procedurally rendered text and PaddleOCR feedback. **Analyst inference:** transfer to naturally occurring text and agreement with independent readers remain uncertain (§4.3; Appendix F).
- **Comparison constraints:** SeedVR2 uses tiled inference because full-frame inference runs out of memory; FlashVSR’s original attention operators are unsupported on RTX 4090. These differences complicate direct efficiency attribution (Fig. 6).
- **Incomplete accessible evidence:** Tables 1–5 and 7 lack their numerical bodies. Participant counts, uncertainty estimates, dataset sizes, total training compute, and exact evaluation splits are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does OCR-guided VSR transfer to natural text?** Training uses synthetic overlays. Evaluate held-out real text videos with independent OCR systems and human transcription (§4.3; Appendix F).
2. **What is Ry-DMD’s training-cost trade-off?** Multiple teacher rollouts are required, but wall-clock overhead is unreported. Compare quality and training time across rollout counts and depths at matched compute budgets (Appendices A and C).
3. **How closely does the practical correction realize the global reward target?** The implementation uses a group-standardized local residual rather than directly matching the global tilt. Compare standardized and unstandardized updates across noise levels and rollout budgets (§3.3; Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The mechanism and selected OCR gains are explicit, but most main-result numbers are unavailable. Material gaps include full quality and efficiency tables, preference margins and participant details, statistical uncertainty, dataset sizes and splits, training hardware and compute, and independently verified release contents.
