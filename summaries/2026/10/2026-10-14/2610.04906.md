# Scaling Verifiable Environments for Long-horizon Work Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04906) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04906)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorkForge grounds synthesized tasks and verifiers in real workspace evidence, producing agent-training trajectories that improve reported benchmark performance, although verifier reliability and causal explanations remain incompletely validated.

## Problem and motivation
Professional work agents must explore interconnected files and produce concrete deliverables over long interactions. The authors identify a gap between expensive expert-built environments and scalable synthetic environments that simplify workspace structure or impose verification requirements unsupported by available evidence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evidence-grounded environment synthesis:** Expert-authored workflow blueprints guide resource retrieval; workspace profiling extracts file roles, dependencies, and factual anchors before concrete tasks are generated (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Aligned tasks and verification:** Instructions, hidden solution plans, programmatic checks, and semantic rubrics share workspace evidence. Solvers see only the workspace and instructions; final deliverables receive separate programmatic and semantic scores (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Training corpus and empirical study:** The authors construct approximately 16.7K environments across 40 domains and 60 file types, then perform supervised fine-tuning using 16,665 GLM 5.3 trajectories generated through Claude Code and OpenClaw. Studies examine data volume, trajectory length, verification filters, and harness transfer (Abstract; §§3.3–3.4; Appendix C.1).

The evidence supports training benefits for two Qwen models under the reported protocols, plus sensitivity to training volume, trajectory composition, and harness. It does not establish that workspace authenticity alone causes downstream gains: the authenticity comparison measures workspace structure. ALE **Score** and **Pass@1** are distinct reported metrics and should not be conflated.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reliance on expert workflows and accessible resources limits coverage of proprietary or sensitive domains. Automated profiling errors can propagate into tasks and verifiers (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Verifiers receive limited expert calibration, emphasize final deliverables, and do not directly assess intermediate behavior (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Length-based subsets do not isolate interaction horizon from task difficulty, content, or training-token exposure; those controls are not reported (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** GDPval findings cover the selected subset, leaving performance on excluded multimodal tasks unestablished (Appendix C.5).
- **Evidence gap:** Many referenced tables contain captions but no numerical bodies in the supplied text. Uncertainty estimates, full comparison results, and several execution settings therefore cannot be assessed.

## Open research questions
- **How accurate are the generated verifiers?** Limited expert calibration leaves agreement with professional judgment unresolved (§5). Next experiment: independently audit sampled facts, rubrics, and graded deliverables with domain experts.
- **Does resource authenticity improve capability independently of workspace size?** The reported comparison establishes structural differences rather than downstream causal effects (§3.3). Next experiment: train on retrieved and synthetic workspaces matched for file count, types, task difficulty, and trajectory budget.
- **Does longer interaction itself explain the gains?** Turn-group sampling leaves content and token exposure potentially confounded (§3.4; Appendix D.2). Next experiment: compare matched tasks under equal training-token budgets with controlled trajectory lengths.
- **Can verification assess execution quality as well as outputs?** Current graders omit direct intermediate-behavior assessment (§5). Next experiment: add expert-audited process criteria and measure their effect on downstream success and error recovery.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative supports the method and headline results, but lacks full table values, uncertainty estimates, detailed verifier calibration, generation costs, complete execution budgets, and confirmation of public artifact availability. These gaps limit assessment of robustness, reproducibility, and scaling efficiency.
