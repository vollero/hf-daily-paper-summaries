# TacitVLA: Reasoning Vision-Language-Action Models Without Reasoning Latency

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17540) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17540)


## One-sentence takeaway
TacitVLA retains reasoning supervision while masking current reasoning tokens from its action expert, achieving similar aggregate performance to explicit reasoning across four benchmarks with up to 3.4× lower measured inference latency (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Reasoning VLAs typically generate text before predicting actions, placing autoregressive decoding on the action path. The authors ask whether reasoning helps mainly through training supervision or through conditioning actions on the generated text.

Prior approaches had explored training with reasoning and deploying without it, but the authors identify insufficiently controlled comparisons and limited coverage of memory tasks, where generated text must remain available for future calls (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled attention-mask comparison.** Explicit Reasoning and TacitVLA share training sequences, losses, prompts, data, and optimizer settings; TacitVLA blocks the action expert’s attention to current reasoning tokens. Both use a vision-language backbone and flow-matching action expert (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Different deployment schedules for memory and non-memory tasks.** TacitVLA skips text generation on non-memory tasks. On memory tasks, it generates the next ledger entry during action execution, retaining earlier entries as action inputs (§4.2).
- **Evidence across manipulation and driving.** Comparisons with Explicit Reasoning test whether current text conditioning is necessary; comparisons with No Reasoning measure reasoning supervision’s benefit, jointly with ledger memory on memory tasks (§§5–6).

The contribution is the controlled comparison and memory-compatible deployment recipe; training with reasoning but omitting generation was already explored in cited work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

Robot success values below are percentages, reported as mean ± standard deviation over three evaluation seeds (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong counting-only performance | RoboMME Counting, trained only on its four tasks, 200 cases: TacitVLA **89.5 ± 1.3**, Explicit **86.7 ± 2.0**, No Reasoning **79.0 ± 1.0** success. | Table 2 |
| Improvement when trained across RoboMME | Across 16 tasks, 800 cases: TacitVLA **48.1 ± 1.0**, Explicit **45.8 ± 1.5**, No Reasoning **46.5 ± 0.9** success; published FrameSamp + Modulator: **44.5**. Counting subset: TacitVLA **82.7 ± 3.8**, published FrameSamp + Expert **66.8**. | Table 3 |
| Largest aggregate gain over No Reasoning | RMBench’s selected four tasks, 100 cases each: TacitVLA **77.5 ± 1.1**, Explicit **77.0 ± 1.9**, No Reasoning **60.4 ± 1.5** success: a **17.1 percentage-point** TacitVLA gain. Published WLA-0: **56.5**. | Table 4 |
| Similar aggregate robustness, different subgroup behavior | LIBERO-Plus, 10,030 cases: TacitVLA **72.9 ± 0.3**, Explicit **73.5 ± 0.3**, No Reasoning **67.6 ± 0.4** success. Camera perturbations: TacitVLA **42.2 ± 0.8** versus Explicit **51.5 ± 0.9**. | Tables 5, 12 |
| Comparable driving scene scores | AlpaSim public split, 441 scenes, three rollouts per scene: TacitVLA **52.5 ± 0.3%**, Explicit **52.3 ± 1.4%**, No Reasoning **46.4 ± 2.2%** scene score. | Table 6 |
| Lower action-ready latency | Mean per-call latency on one H100: RoboMME counting **127 vs. 220 ms**, LIBERO **147 vs. 495 ms**, driving **127 vs. 269 ms**, TacitVLA versus Explicit. TacitVLA equals No Reasoning on these rows. | Table 15 |
| Deferred memory generation fits tested windows | No deferred generation exceeded its execution window; longest entries were **180 ms** on RoboMME and **225 ms** on RMBench. | Appendix C |

The evidence supports comparable **aggregate** performance without current reasoning-text conditioning in the tested architectures. It does not establish formal statistical equivalence, uniform subgroup parity, or that explicit reasoning computation never helps. On memory tasks, gains over No Reasoning combine reasoning supervision and ledger access (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Performance depends on reasoning-data quality. Deferred generation adds delay whenever it exceeds the execution window. Using post-action text for interpretability remains future work (§7).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** RMBench covers a selected subset of tasks, and the supplied evaluation establishes simulator results rather than physical deployment performance (§5; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—timing:** Driving’s measured TacitVLA latency exceeds its simulator control interval, so the reported speedup does not establish real-time operation at that interval (Table 15).
- **Analyst inference—comparison controls:** RoboMME headline results select each policy’s best cross-entropy weight separately. The fixed-weight sweep supports robustness, but headline comparisons are not uniformly matched at one weight (Appendix A; Table 11).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Driving uses proprietary training data. Reported variation concerns evaluation seeds or rollouts; independent training-seed replication is not established (§5; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—memory validity:** Ledger entries describe attempted subtasks rather than verified outcomes, and training uses annotated ledgers while deployment uses generated ones (Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **When does current reasoning conditioning help?** Aggregate parity hides a camera-perturbation gap. A next experiment could compare the masks across increasingly difficult viewpoint shifts while holding training settings fixed (Table 12).
2. **How much memory-task gain comes from supervision versus ledger access?** No Reasoning removes both. A factorial experiment could vary reasoning supervision and ledger availability independently (§5).
3. **Does deferred generation remain effective under tighter deadlines?** Tested entries always fit their windows. Shorten execution windows and lengthen ledger entries, measuring waiting time and task success (§7; Appendix C).
4. **How sensitive is the method to annotation quality?** The authors identify this dependency without a quality ablation. Compare clean, corrupted, and reduced reasoning annotations under matched training (§7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark results; broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes confirmed code/checkpoint availability, access to proprietary driving training data, independent training-seed variability, formal equivalence tests, physical deployment validation, and driving evaluation that explicitly incorporates measured inference delay.
