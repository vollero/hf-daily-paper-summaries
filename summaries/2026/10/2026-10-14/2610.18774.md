# GreenWAM: A World Foundation Model for Robotics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18774) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18774)


## One-sentence takeaway
GreenWAM adapts Cosmos 3 Edge through a shared anatomical action interface and joint video–action training, producing strong action-conditioned predictions and benchmark-adapted robot policies, with controlled evidence that robot specialization and retained prediction modes improve control.

## Problem and motivation
Robot world models predict action consequences, while policies choose actions, but the authors argue that these roles are usually evaluated separately. Cross-robot learning is further complicated by incompatible joint layouts, units, cameras and action conventions. GreenWAM addresses whether one robotics foundation can support both roles through explicit correspondence between robot bodies (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared anatomical interface:** Layout-specific codecs map measured robot states into 79 masked anatomical slots, followed by shared input/output projections. The corpus covers 20 main robot families; missing or unresolved joints receive no loss (§3.2, Appendix A.3).
- **Joint robotics training:** Starting from Cosmos 3 Edge 4B, the model trains on seven visual-generation, forward/inverse-dynamics and world–action modes. The curated pool contains 1,674.7 hours after quality filtering; the reasoner and video VAE remain frozen (§§3.3–4.3, Tables 2 and 5).
- **Policy adaptation:** An action expert copied from the generator separates action parameters while retaining joint attention with video. Target-robot demonstrations support benchmark-specific adaptation and trajectory decoding (§§6.1–6.2).
- **Controlled analysis and deployment:** Comparisons test specialization, the expert split and retention of prediction modes. Consistency distillation produces a single-step humanoid policy. The authors report releasing adapted policy/world-model weights and evaluation code (§§6.4–7, §9).

The contribution is this combined robotics recipe and its evaluation; the foundation architecture, joint diffusion modes and distillation build on cited methods.

These results support a reusable foundation for prediction and adapted control, rather than one unchanged policy across benchmarks. Matched comparisons support the tested adaptation choices, but do not isolate every component of robotics pretraining. Prediction accuracy and success correlate, especially under randomization; that association alone does not establish causation (§7.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Geometry changes remain difficult; autoregressive prediction errors accumulate; every benchmark requires its own post-training; physical evaluation covers one humanoid and one packing task (§8).
- **Comparison limits:** RoboTwin pretraining exposure differs or is undisclosed for some competitors. WorldArena uses a recomputed motion-capped aggregate, with different available aggregation granularity for competitors (Table 3; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The specialization comparison combines additional robotics training, data curation and action-interface changes; it does not independently establish the anatomical slots’ benefit. The parent also learns joint-space projections from scratch (§7.1).
- **Statistical limits:** Bootstrap intervals quantify evaluation uncertainty for the compared checkpoints, explicitly excluding variation across training runs (Appendix C.4).
- **Deployment limits:** Latency measures model calls with fixed observations/state within each episode; onboard student execution remains future work. Distillation also lowers observed humanoid success (§6.4; Appendices C.2–C.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does anatomical alignment itself improve transfer?** No isolated interface ablation is reported. Compare shared anatomical slots against padded joints and embodiment-specific projections with matched data and compute.
- **Can the model transfer to bodies unseen by both training stages?** The authors leave this open (§8). Adapt matched policies to a new body using the shared codec versus a new projector, measuring demonstration efficiency.
- **Can motion augmentation close geometry gaps?** Appearance augmentation adds no changed trajectories (§7.5). Add demonstrations at varied table heights and initial robot configurations, then test held-out geometry.
- **Can imagined rollouts reliably evaluate policies?** Current evidence links prediction fidelity with execution success but does not validate policy ranking (§§7.4, 8). Compare rankings from imagined rollouts against physical executions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in summarizing the reported methods and results; **medium** in broad generalization claims.

Material gaps include independent anatomical-interface ablations, variation across training seeds, detailed real-humanoid demonstration counts and timeout values, complete adaptation schedules for some policies, competitor per-video WorldArena metrics, and onboard latency measurements. The release statement does not establish availability of the full training corpus or robotics foundation checkpoint.
