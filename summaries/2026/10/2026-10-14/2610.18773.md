# Jeva: System One Models from First Principles

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18773) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18773)


## One-sentence takeaway
Jeva separates reusable option encoding from set-conditioned decision computation, achieving strong reported selection quality, explicit answer-absence detection, stable tested permutations, and substantial cache-dependent latency reductions (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Compact language decisions—selecting a tool or document, judging a condition, or assigning a score—need more than answer-token prediction. The authors identify four requirements: distinguish absent answers from uncertain choices, preserve decisions under option reordering, accommodate variable candidate counts, and reuse computation for recurring options (§1).

Their central criticism is that jointly encoding requests and candidates couples semantic representation, candidate interaction, and answer availability, making these properties difficult to guarantee (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reusable representations with collective judgment.** A shared language encoder independently embeds the request and each option. A compact attention module updates only the request state against fixed option memory. A constructive proof shows that one block can reverse preferences between retained options when surrounding candidates change—something independent scoring cannot express (§§3.1–3.2, Proposition 1).
- **Separate coverage and competition.** A sigmoid gate estimates whether any acceptable option exists; a conditional softmax distributes probability among candidates. Their product provides explicit null and candidate probabilities. Choice, Boolean-probability “Noul,” and numerical-expectation Score outputs share this computation (§3.3).
- **Structural symmetry and reusable computation.** Symmetric aggregation and shared scoring provide permutation-equivariant probabilities in exact arithmetic. Option embeddings and layer projections can be cached individually across newly assembled sets; prepared-memory decision cost grows linearly with candidate count at fixed width and depth (§3.4, Proposition 3).
- **Trained implementation and diagnostics.** Jeva-4B combines a Qwen3.5-4B backbone, LoRA training, and one decision block, evaluated for decision quality, null detection, permutation stability, scaling, and caching (§§3.5–4).

The contribution is the combined architecture and its analysis; the paper explicitly situates independent encoding and symmetric aggregation within existing method families (§2).

These results support the authors’ structural and efficiency claims in the reported settings, while showing that Jeva does not lead every quality metric. Theoretical cache equivalence assumes identical entries and exact arithmetic; measured probability differences demonstrate the practical qualification. Large-set accuracy accepts any highest-target-score candidate and uses added weak or unjudged negatives (§§B.5, C.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated qualifications:** Permutation guarantees concern probabilities and unique semantic winners; first-index tie-breaking can change the selected tied member. Floating-point reductions and batching can perturb outputs (§B.5).
- **Author-stated scope:** Coverage is invariant to lower-scoring additions only when final scores remain fixed. Set refinement can alter scores, and joint null-versus-candidate selection still depends on competition (§A.3, §B.4).
- **Author-stated constraints:** Caches require unchanged weights, tokenizer, templates, length policy, and numeric configuration. Individual texts retain length limits; caching adds storage (§§B.5–B.7).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The supplied experiments do not isolate the quality contribution of state refinement, the coverage gate, or curriculum stages through matched trained ablations.
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Baselines use different native runtimes and null adapters, so cross-model results do not isolate architecture alone. Controlled within-model cache and batching comparisons provide cleaner efficiency evidence (§§4, C.7).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Novel-set cache experiments describe 50 requests per reuse level in §4.4/Figure 9, but §C.5 specifies 46 plotted requests per level.
- **Implementation qualification:** Enabled Noul/Score auxiliary losses still operate on all-null examples; the primary loss’s absence of candidate-ranking supervision does not fully describe those training cases (§B.8).

## Open research questions
1. **How much quality comes from set refinement?** The proof establishes expressivity, but no matched empirical removal is reported. Train an otherwise identical independent-scoring variant and compare quality, especially on controlled relational-choice examples.
2. **Does null detection hold across domains and candidate counts?** The detailed ROC evidence is BANKING77 with 16 candidates. Evaluate answer-withheld tool/document sets across candidate counts, measuring AUROC, calibration, and fixed-threshold operating points.
3. **What is the end-to-end value of caching under turnover?** Reported gains depend on reuse and exclude MLDR priming. Measure complete streams with bounded cache capacity, eviction, changing options, and model updates, including preparation time and memory.
4. **How robust is scaling to harder negatives?** ToolRet additions include weak or unjudged negatives. Repeat nested-set tests with adjudicated, semantically similar distractors and report accuracy and null behavior.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed mechanisms, proofs, protocols, and numerical results, but the actual Table 1/Table 2 cells and plotted figure data are absent. Public code/checkpoint availability, complete training-data provenance and overlap checks, full optimization schedules, matched component ablations, and statistical uncertainty are not established. The cache sample-count discrepancy remains unresolved.
