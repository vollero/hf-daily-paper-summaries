# Omni-Modal LLMs Know More Than They Say

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16135) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16135)


## One-sentence takeaway
Self-generated descriptions added alongside original sensory inputs improve multimodal answering, supporting the authors’ claim that some failures involve accessing perceived information during generation rather than perception alone (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Omni-modal LLMs can extract relevant sensory information when explicitly prompted yet fail to use it when answering questions. The authors call this the “knows-but-cannot-say” gap and investigate whether modality imbalance reflects limited access to encoded information.

Existing alignment metrics mostly compare aggregated input representations. The identified gap is a diagnostic that connects individual sensory tokens to the representations used for response generation (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Token-level Modality Alignment (TMA):** For each input modality token, compute its maximum cosine similarity to any generated response token using last-decoder-layer hidden states. Retaining the score distribution exposes variation obscured by aggregate metrics (§3.1).
- **Accessibility diagnosis:** Compare TMA with attention, perceptual-description performance, and downstream answering under token masking. The authors find that high-TMA tokens matter more for answering, while their perceptual-knowledge measure remains comparatively stable (§3.2; Appendix E).
- **OMNI-CATALYST:** Generate modality descriptions with the same model, then answer using both descriptions and original inputs. Single-pass and separate-pass variants are provided, alongside training objectives for generating and using descriptions (§4).
- **Evaluation:** Test reasoning, modality preference, reconstructed single-modality tasks, and description-based training strategies across several model families (§§5–6; Appendices D–F).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Training-free descriptions improve audiovisual benchmark performance | Qwen2.5-Omni-7B accuracy: OmniBench **46.5→51.7%**, WorldSense **39.2→45.7%**; CoM reaches **49.4%** and **41.9%**, respectively. | Table 1 |
| Gains vary substantially across models | OmniBench overall accuracy: Gemma E4B **27.1→40.5%**; Qwen3-Omni-30B **51.6→52.6%**. | Tables 2, S7 |
| Description training helps, but combined training is not uniformly best | Four-model mean reasoning accuracy: untrained baseline **34.7%**, perceive-only **37.5%**, perceive-only plus inference-time OMNI-CATALYST **43.4%**; inference-only OMNI-CATALYST on untrained models reaches **43.6%**. | Table S8 |
| Audio descriptions generally improve preference balance | Across four models, mean normalized preference entropy rises from **0.892** without augmentation to **0.959** with single-pass audio descriptions; Qwen-7B individually declines from **0.807→0.802**. | Table S6 |
| TMA identifies tokens consequential for answering | Masking higher-TMA tokens causes greater downstream degradation, while perceptual-knowledge performance remains comparatively stable; selected representations are zeroed at every decoder layer during prefill. | Figure 4a; Appendix E, pp. 28–29 |
| TMA is reasonably stable across sampled responses | On up to **300 OmniBench samples**, with **five responses** per prompt, Qwen3-Omni has visual/audio ICC values **0.989/0.979** and tier-assignment κ **0.930**. | Table S5; Appendix F |
| Benefits are not universal | Qwen3-Omni sound-event accuracy falls **60.0→58.9%**; MiniCPM music accuracy falls **50.0→48.1%**, despite overall gains. | Table S7 |

These results establish useful benchmark improvements and support a distinction between recoverable sensory content and successful answering. They do not establish representational misalignment as the sole causal bottleneck. TMA measures similarity to the model’s generated response, and the paper explicitly notes that irrelevant text can improve alignment measures without semantic grounding (Appendices E–F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers modalities supported by existing models; the root causes of modality imbalance remain unresolved (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Separate description generation adds inference cost. Failure examples involving intentions, future events, or off-screen evidence also expose limits of interpreting a single maximally aligned token (§4.1; Figures S16–S17).
- **Analyst inference:** The perceptual-knowledge measure is permissive: an example counts as successful when an independent text judge finds any nonzero keyword recall. Stable performance therefore does not demonstrate complete recovery of answer-relevant information (Appendix E, p. 25).
- **Analyst inference:** Single-modality results depend on keyword-derived category labels and sampled distractors, so they establish performance on reconstructed MCQs rather than the original tasks (Appendix D).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** The headline **+7.29%** lacks a clearly reproducible aggregation specification. Table S12 reports **42.0→42.3** as **+2.3**, an arithmetic inconsistency. The abstract says code is available, whereas Appendix C promises release upon publication.

## Open research questions
- **Does alignment causally mediate improvement?** Attention and TMA shifts accompany gains but do not isolate the mechanism. Next experiment: selectively alter response alignment while holding descriptions’ factual content fixed.
- **When should descriptions be generated?** Audio supplementation is often effective, but visual and all-modality configurations sometimes hurt (Tables S7–S9). Next experiment: compare query-conditioned modality selection against fixed description policies.
- **How much sensory knowledge is actually preserved?** Nonzero keyword recall cannot establish complete perceptual recovery. Next experiment: score full content recall and answer-critical evidence separately using blinded human annotations.
- **What is the accuracy–cost tradeoff?** Separate-pass cost is acknowledged without measured latency or compute comparisons. Next experiment: compare single-pass, two-pass, and Self-CoT under matched token and latency budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and selected benchmark gains, but the mechanism warrants cautious interpretation.

Material gaps include the headline gain’s aggregation rule, complete evaluation sample counts and uncertainty estimates, the knowledge judge’s identity and validation, measured inference overhead, and an unambiguous resource-release status.
