# DeltaTTG: Training Multi-Turn Agents with a Token-to-Go Progress Meter

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17666) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17666)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeltaTTG uses a frozen model’s estimates of remaining completion tokens to redistribute outcome-based training credit, achieving the best aggregate scores among compared RL methods on two actor backbones, while training variability and full preparation costs remain unresolved (Table 1; §4.4; Limitations).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-turn agents receive sparse terminal rewards that poorly identify which actions helped or hurt. The authors argue that existing step-comparison methods depend on comparable states and diverse rollout outcomes, while direct process supervision can require expensive evaluators, annotations, or task-specific rules (§1).

Their proposed gap is a reusable progress signal learned from ordinary trajectories across policies, without requiring matched states or additional actor rewards.

## Approach and contributions
1. **Token-to-Go supervision.** Successful trajectory prefixes receive the number of remaining assistant-generated tokens, including reasoning, tool calls, and answers but excluding environment observations. Failed continuations receive failure labels rather than token targets. A meter learns completion forecasts and remaining-work estimates through SFT followed by GRPO (§3.1).
2. **Sign-aware credit redistribution.** The frozen meter estimates costs before and after actions. Cost reductions receive greater positive credit on positive-advantage trajectories; regressions receive stronger negative credit on negative-advantage trajectories. Uniform mixing and bounded normalization preserve the trajectory advantage’s sign and token-average scale without changing environment rewards (§3.2; Appendix D).
3. **Reuse and diagnostic evaluation.** Domain-specific meters serve both actor backbones. Additional experiments examine step-quality judgments, test-time action selection across actor sizes, and one cross-domain meter-transfer setting (§§4–5).

The central hypothesis is that beneficial actions tend to reduce remaining token effort across policies; the paper treats tokens as a proxy, not an intrinsic measure of progress (§1).

The results support improved credit assignment in the evaluated settings, rather than universal superiority across metrics. Authors also report the best performance on all three out-of-domain benchmarks and successful ALFWorld-to-PaperSearchQA meter transfer, but exact scores for several figure-only comparisons are unavailable in the supplied text (§§4.5, 5.3). The sample-efficiency token axis excludes prompts and meter tokens, so it does not establish total compute efficiency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Transfer evidence covers only two actor families and several domains; broader reuse may require adaptation.
- **Author-stated:** Robustness to the dataset-specific failure cost \(C\) is unestablished; detailed sensitivity analysis remains future work.
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation deviations do not quantify independent training-run variability.
- **Analyst inference:** Remaining tokens can reflect policy verbosity and interaction conventions as well as progress, consistent with the authors’ stated proxy assumption (§1).
- **Analyst inference:** AgentProcessBench results establish limited evidence for unseen-task generalization: trajectories from the same task can cross splits, and this diagnostic uses a different training formulation from the main meters (Appendix E.6).
- **Analyst inference:** The compute comparison does not establish end-to-end savings because it excludes separate meter training, while trajectory collection and complete meter-preparation costs are not quantified (§4.4).
- **Scope boundary:** Test-time guidance requires trial execution and environment replay; fewer selected interaction steps do not measure total branch-execution work (Appendix E.5).

## Open research questions
1. **How sensitive is training to failure cost?** The paper explicitly leaves this unresolved. Sweep \(C\) across domains and independent training seeds while tracking failure-prediction switches and final success.
2. **Does TTG remain useful under different verbosity and token-count conventions?** Its proxy depends on policy style and harness. Collect matched-task trajectories under controlled verbosity and evaluate meter rankings and downstream credit assignment.
3. **How far does meter transfer extend?** Cross-domain optimization tests one source–target direction. Run a source–target transfer matrix with task-disjoint evaluation and compare frozen versus adapted meters.
4. **When does meter preparation pay for itself?** Reported actor costs exclude preparation. Measure trajectory collection, SFT, RL, and inference costs across increasing numbers of actor-training runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and tabulated outcomes are well specified, but training variability and broader validity remain uncertain.

Material gaps include independent training-seed results; complete progress-meter data counts and source-policy mixture; full collection/SFT/RL preparation costs; failure-cost sensitivity; exact figure-only generalization and transfer scores; and confirmation of released meter checkpoints or supervision datasets.
