# Privacy-Preserving Intent Fulfilment and Assurance for 6G RAN

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08809) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08809)


## One-sentence takeaway
Category-based provisioning bounds information disclosed through resource decisions, while experiments on four production networks show that assurance from cell-level counters depends strongly on fault extent, correlation, and calibration.

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses intent fulfilment and assurance without inspecting traffic, tracking individual flows, or accessing proprietary node telemetry. The author argues that existing observability requirements conflict with data minimisation, encrypted traffic, and heterogeneous vendor interfaces. The identified gap is an architecture that provisions resources and checks outcomes using declared categories and standard O1 performance measurements alone (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Information-flow restriction:** Provision resources as \(R=\phi(C)\), where \(C\) is a declared category, and verify outcomes using cell-level O1 PM counters. The paper derives \(I(T;R)\leq I(T;C)\leq H(C)\leq\log_2K\) (§§3–4.1).
- **Privacy definitions and detection ceiling:** Define category-limited intent–traffic unlinkability and node-opaque verification. Requiring at least \(q\) cells per category gives \(H(C)\leq\log_2\lfloor M/q\rfloor\), connecting category-channel privacy to verification occupancy (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Deployment mapping:** Locate category assignment, provisioning, and assurance within the 3GPP lifecycle and an O-RAN Non-RT RIC rApp using O1 measurements and A1 policies (§5).
- **Empirical comparison:** Compare per-cell exceedance and category-mean scores, investigate correlation and calibration failures, and test synthetic counter degradations on production PM data (§6).

The underlying MISES mechanism, welfare bounds, feasibility-band results, and aggregate-versus-individual optimal-power theorem are attributed to earlier work; this paper develops their privacy consequences and deployment evaluation (§§1, 3–4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Resource-decision leakage is category-limited | Under \(T\to C\to R\), \(I(T;R)\leq H(C)\leq\log_2K\); this does not bound total inference from PM counters. | §4.1, Theorem 1; §7.2.1 |
| Verification occupancy caps category entropy | If every category requires at least \(q\) contributing cells among \(M\), \(H(C)\leq\log_2\lfloor M/q\rfloor\). | §4.3, Corollary 7 |
| Per-cell detection plateaus with finer clustering | Four networks; cross-category replacement; target FPR 0.20; 10 paired seeds. Recall gains largely plateau beyond approximately \(K=8\), while the leakage ceiling continues rising. | §6.2, Fig. 1 |
| Category means favour category-wide shifts | At \(K=15\), 30% of test category-hour pairs degraded, with every cell replaced from one other category: category-mean AUC exceeds per-cell exceedance on three networks and is comparable on Net-A, after per-category standardisation. | §6.7, Table 6 discussion |
| Sparse faults can reverse the ordering | Same category-hour setup, but 30% of cells replaced independently: per-cell versus category-mean AUC is 0.710 versus 0.504 on Net-A and 0.811 versus 0.651 on Net-D; category means perform better on Net-B and slightly better on Net-C. | §6.7 |
| Nominal category size overstates independent evidence | At \(K=15\), validation-period category-mean variance is 4–26 times the independent-cell expectation; categories of 29–871 cells behave like 6–14 independent cells. | §6.7, Table 7 discussion |
| Sparse outage detection is weak | Synthetic outage perturbations at magnitude \(f=3\): per-cell recall remains within 0.06 of FPR on Net-A, Net-C, and Net-D. Category-wide outages yield category-mean AUC 0.75–0.78. | §6.8, Table 9 discussion |
| Learned category entropy tightens the ceiling | At \(K=15\), empirical \(H(C)\) is 3.56, 3.16, 3.24, and 3.22 bits across Net-A–D, versus the uniform ceiling of 3.91 bits; these are entropy ceilings, not measured traffic leakage. | §7.2.3 |

These results establish a conditional information bound and empirical behaviour of particular scores under synthetic degradations. They do not establish real-event detection recall, total privacy across both observation channels, or the optimal-test dominance premise. Many referenced tables contain captions but no numerical bodies in the supplied text; the results above use explicit prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated privacy scope:** PM-counter leakage is unbounded by the category theorem. Side channels, correlated auxiliary data, malicious coordinators, and infrastructure-provider threats are outside the formal guarantee (§7.2).
- **Author-stated evaluation scope:** No fault-management or alarm labels are available. The lock stream validates part of the category structure, not event detection; structured faults remain synthetic (§§6.8–6.9, 7.2.2).
- **Author-stated detection qualifications:** Aggregate optimal-power dominance requires state-independent observation, which the replacement experiments do not establish. Correlation, sparse faults, and nonstationarity constrain implemented performance (§7.2.4).
- **Operational gap:** The tested tail-exceedance category alarm saturates on three networks; a sequential decision rule remains outside scope. Retraining-schedule benefits are projections assuming restoration of baseline recall (§§6.5–6.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Deployment categories are proposed from declared intent requirements, whereas experiments cluster observed cell PM profiles. Consequently, the empirical study does not directly validate the complete declared-intent provisioning loop or its welfare gains (§§5.1, 6.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the verifier detect naturally occurring service failures?** Detection labels are synthetic. A next experiment could evaluate frozen detectors against timestamped fault-management labels, measuring event recall, false alarms, and detection delay (§7.2.2).
2. **Can category alarms remain calibrated under drift?** Pooled alarms saturate and held-out false-alarm rates drift. Compare rolling per-category calibration and sequential alarms under a fixed operator-facing false-alarm budget (§§6.5–6.7).
3. **How should occupancy account for correlated cells?** Large nominal categories provide limited independent evidence. Test correlation-aware grouping and occupancy requirements against measured power targets (§6.7).
4. **What privacy remains after combining categories and PM counters?** The theorem leaves PM inference unbounded. Evaluate inference attacks on category-only, PM-only, and joint observations within an explicitly defined threat model (§7.2.1).
