# Cepheid: Decoupling Scale from Structure for Cross-Family Motion Completion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18871) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18871)


## One-sentence takeaway
Cepheid combines harmonic extension with a scale-normalized learned residual, reducing pooled motion-completion error on unseen cloth and legged bodies while still failing to beat the analytical base at the sparsest legged setting (§5.2; Appendix B.6).

## Problem and motivation
The task is to recover dense per-vertex motion from sparse displacements on a target mesh, without skeletons, registration, or cross-mesh correspondence. A learned prior should improve on parameter-free harmonic extension, yet seven of twelve evaluated single-family transfers fail to do so (§§1, 5.2).

The authors identify residual scale as a barrier to joint training: its amplitude differs substantially across geometry families and observation densities, while the true residual is unavailable at inference (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Analytical base and control.** Solve a graph-Laplacian Dirichlet problem to obtain harmonic motion \(H\), then learn the residual \(F-H\). The contribution is its integration and systematic use as a transfer control, rather than a new harmonic solver (§3.1).
2. **Target-independent amplitude recovery.** Estimate residual scale from observed-displacement dispersion multiplied by a ridge-regressed correction using dimensionless features. Fit the rule on DT4D, AMASS, and Franka, then freeze it for evaluation (§3.3; Appendix C).
3. **Shared dimensionless residual prior.** Train a graph-network/Transformer backbone with flow matching on the scale-normalized residual. At inference, average sampled residuals and restore physical amplitude. The authors claim novelty for this cross-family sparse-input setting; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority (§§1, 3.4).

## Evidence and main results

All numerical results below are author-reported. The principal metric is pooled mean per-vertex L2 error divided by pooled mean ground-truth displacement, evaluated on unobserved vertices (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong DT4D comparison | On seven held-out identities and five densities spanning 0.5–50%, unified Cepheid has lower error than retrained NSDP and per-scene DOMA; error is 44–81% below harmonic extension across densities. | §5.1; Table 1 caption |
| Positive pooled held-out transfer | Error is 15.6% below harmonic extension on cloth and 10.1% below it on legged bodies. Cloth pools five density rungs; legged pools 0.5%, 10%, and 50%. | §5.2; Appendix B.6; Table 2 |
| Improvement is not universal by density | Unified Cepheid beats harmonic extension at every evaluated cloth density, but exceeds it at 0.5% observation on legged bodies and improves from 1% upward. | Appendix B.6; Tables 6–7 |
| Amplitude recovery approaches a restricted oracle | On held-out cloth, completion error is 1.4–8.1% above the per-condition optimal scalar applied to the same frozen predicted residual; raw observed scale alone loses to harmonic extension at every density. | §5.3; Table 3 |
| Base and scale materially affect transfer | Removing harmonic extension produces roughly 2.4 times the base error on both held-out families; removing amplitude recovery causes negative transfer on legged bodies. | §5.4; Table 4 |
| Deployment masks benefit from adaptation | For three gripper markers, fine-tuning reduces free-arm-link error from 0.86 to 0.37 times harmonic error over twelve held-out episodes and 36 frame pairs. | Appendix G; Table 15 |

These results support cross-family improvement under the tested displacement and masking protocols. Oracle proximity concerns only scalar rescaling of a fixed predicted residual, not proximity to optimal motion completion. Robot–soft-body and video demonstrations establish integration feasibility; the video lacks ground truth and does not establish reconstruction accuracy (§6; Figure 4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Quantitative evaluation assumes exact displacements, without sensor noise, lost tracks, depth distortion, or scanned geometry. Most evaluated motion sources are animated, kinematic, or simulated (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Training uses random masks; fixed sensor placements benefit substantially from fine-tuning. Noise robustness and training across mask families remain future work (§7; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Pooled gains conceal a sparse-observation failure on legged bodies and do not establish improvement on every condition. Unified training also leaves native-source error above that of specialists (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The no-harmonic ablation also disrupts harmonic-derived amplitude features, which are clamped to their fitted boundaries. It therefore does not cleanly isolate the base field’s contribution (Appendix B.8).
- **Evidence limits:** Training uses one reported seed. Full numerical table bodies are absent from the supplied extraction, limiting independent checking of reported comparisons. DOMA’s best budget and prediction reading are selected after scoring (§4; Appendix A.2).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is amplitude recovery to corrupted observations?** Exact-input evaluation leaves this unresolved. Test controlled displacement noise, missing tracks, and depth errors against harmonic extension (§7).
- **Can one prior handle deployment-specific sensor layouts without fine-tuning?** Random-mask training and the gripper adaptation gain leave this open. Compare random-mask training with mixed structured-mask training on unseen placements (Appendix G).
- **What causes the sparsest legged failure?** Pooled improvement does not resolve the failure at the lowest density. Compare amplitude calibration and residual-direction error by embodiment and marker placement (Appendix B.6).
- **Does completion improve real unobserved-surface accuracy?** The video demonstration has no ground truth and uses simplified track lifting. Evaluate tracked inputs against independently measured dense motion, including hidden surfaces (Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information
