# Elephant in the Fridge: Constant-Memory Frame Packing for Long Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16118) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16118)


## One-sentence takeaway
FramePack-U improves long-video question answering by combining query-aware, diverse frame selection with mixed-resolution packing under a fixed visual-token budget, although unbounded end-to-end constant memory and online streaming are not established.

## Problem and motivation
Encoding every video frame at full resolution makes visual-token sequences grow with video length, increasing memory and latency. The authors argue that existing token reduction and keyframe selection methods retain scaling problems and can discard important transient evidence (§1). Their target is broader temporal coverage within a fixed inference budget.

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint relevance and diversity ranking:** Seed selection with the frame most relevant to the query, then greedily balance CLIP query similarity against similarity to already selected frames (§3.1, Eq. 1).
- **Hierarchical resolution packing:** Assign higher-ranked frames finer resolutions, constrain total visual tokens, and restore chronological order before VLM inference. This adapts packing from video generation to understanding without model training or architectural changes (§3.2, Eq. 3, Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical allocation study:** Evaluate resolution schedules, ranking components, model sizes, and runtime. The practical method selects a finite number of frames; the authors acknowledge that packing more frames can hurt when spatial resolution becomes too low (§§3–5).

These results support improved benchmark performance under nominally equal visual-token budgets, especially for longer videos and stronger backbones. They do not establish universal speedups, lossless temporal preservation, or generalization beyond the tested model family.

The introduction describes the MoE Video-MME gain of 9.7 points as a **long-split** result, but Table 10 identifies it as **overall**; the corresponding long-split gain is **12.8 points**, from **54.1 to 66.9** at the 128-frame-equivalent budget. The table-supported distinction is used above.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Smaller models may struggle with additional observations; tightly coupled video encoders are less compatible; CLIP can miss temporal or reasoning-heavy evidence; optimal packing schedules vary (§6). The proposed model-capacity explanation is an attribution, not an isolated causal finding.
- **Analyst inference—constant-memory scope:** Eq. 3 bounds packed visual tokens. Arbitrarily many nonempty frames face minimum token/resolution constraints, which are not resolved in the supplied text. Algorithm 1 also scores all candidates, without documenting storage management sufficient to establish constant end-to-end memory.
- **Analyst inference—streaming validity:** Main benchmarking uses offline frame access (§4). No online update algorithm or streaming evaluation establishes the claimed applicability to unbounded feeds.
- **Comparison limits:** Appendix D evaluates adapted ranking strategies under a shared packing schedule, including a LongVU-style variant rather than the complete trained system. FramePack-U also does not win every metric there: Q-Frame scores higher on MLVU (Table 11).
- **Reporting and reproducibility:** No uncertainty estimates, repeated-run results, or code release are supplied. Scheme selection uses benchmark performance without a clearly described held-out selection protocol. Ablation descriptions also alternate between feature diversity and temporal midpoint ordering (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Can the complete pipeline maintain bounded memory online?** Current evidence concerns offline selection and bounded packed tokens. Test an incremental implementation on progressively longer streams, measuring decoding, feature storage, ranking memory, and answer accuracy.
2. **Can temporally aware scoring recover evidence CLIP misses?** The authors identify single-frame relevance as a limitation (§6). Compare CLIP with a lightweight temporal scorer under matched token and preprocessing budgets.
3. **Can packing schedules generalize without benchmark-specific selection?** Allocations vary across models and tasks (§6). Select an adaptive schedule on held-out data, then evaluate it unchanged on unseen benchmarks and backbones.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark improvements and practical trade-offs, but broader scalability claims remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes code availability, uncertainty estimates, detailed baseline configurations, held-out scheme-selection procedures, online streaming results, minimum-resolution handling, and full preprocessing storage accounting.
