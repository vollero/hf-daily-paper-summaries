# Self-Distillation with Verbal Regularization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16354) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16354)


## One-sentence takeaway
Teacher-only reasoning prompts improve on-policy self-distillation’s reported Pass@1 in 11 of 12 model–task settings, averaging approximately 3.9 percentage points over OPSD, with generally higher student entropy (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a model against its own teacher distributions conditioned on privileged information (PI), such as verified solutions. The authors argue that complete solutions can encourage premature answer commitment and concentrated token distributions, weakening supervision for verification and alternative reasoning. Their proposed gap is how to retain complete solution guidance while regulating its influence without changing the architecture or distillation objective (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Teacher-only verbal regularization:** VRSD adds task-specific system prompts encouraging verification, reconsideration, or alternative reasoning. The student receives neither these prompts nor PI during generation; training retains the original distillation objective (§4.1).
- **Multiple supervision perspectives:** VRSD (single) uses one composite prompt; VRSD (multi) distributes aspect-specific prompts across rollouts (§5.1).
- **Conditional theoretical explanation:** An affine model describes attenuation of PI-induced log-probability shifts. The authors derive an exact teacher-entropy identity and a local sufficient condition for entropy increase, conditional on that model (§4.2).
- **Empirical analysis:** Accuracy, entropy, prompt ablations, response-pattern heuristics, and reasoning-truncation interventions assess the resulting students (§§5.2–5.6).

## Evidence and main results

Unless otherwise specified, task evaluation samples 16 responses per problem at temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95, using checkpoints selected by validation Pass@1 (§5.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-prompt VRSD improves average accuracy | Versus OPSD, average Pass@1 gains across four tasks are **7.2 pp** for Qwen3-4B, **2.8 pp** for OLMo-3-7B-Instruct, and **1.6 pp** for Qwen3-8B; approximately **3.9 pp** overall, with gains in **11/12** settings. | §5.2; Table 1 discussion |
| Gains include answer consistency | VRSD (multi) matches or exceeds OPSD’s Maj@16 in every setting and improves average Worst@16—the fraction of problems with every sampled answer correct—on each model. | §§5.1–5.2 |
| SQL illustrates recovery from OPSD degradation | Qwen3-4B SQL Pass@1: starting model **71.3%**, OPSD **69.8%**, VRSD (multi) **81.0%**. | §5.2 |
| Student entropy generally increases | Higher entropy than OPSD in **19/24** variant–model–task comparisons. Qwen3-4B SQL entropy: OPSD **0.03**, single **0.49**, multi **0.54 nats/token**. | §5.3; Appendix E |
| Prompt distribution matters | On Qwen3-4B, multi-prompt training beats every individual aspect prompt and their concatenation across four tasks; Pass@1 gains over the strongest individual prompt range from **0.5–4.7 pp**. | §5.4; Figure 4 |
| Measured shifts support average attenuation | Before training, all model–task attenuation-coefficient bootstrap CIs lie below one; mean coefficients range **0.656–0.954**. Analysis uses selected problems having both successful and failed rollouts. | Appendix A.2; Table 4 |
| Truncation interventions favor VRSD | On Qwen3-4B, mean reasoning gain—full-reasoning versus no-reasoning Pass@1—is **5.8 pp** for single, **5.0 pp** for multi, and **3.7 pp** for OPSD across four tasks. Misleading-reasoning rate is lower in **7/8** VRSD comparisons. | §5.6; Table 3 discussion |

These results support improved benchmark performance and less concentrated student distributions in the evaluated settings. They do not establish that entropy preservation causes the accuracy gains. The truncation experiment supports dependence on explicit reasoning under that intervention; it does not establish comprehensive reasoning faithfulness. Many cited tables appear only as captions in the supplied text, so their complete numerical comparisons cannot be inspected.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Prompts require manual, task-specific judgment and may become unsuitable across problems or training stages. Wording controls attenuation indirectly (§6).
- **Observed failure mode:** Adversarial reconsideration prompts can sharply reduce teacher continuation accuracy despite access to a correct solution; the authors associate this with student Worst@16 collapse (Appendix J.2).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Average attenuation slopes do not establish an exact affine fit. Appendix A explicitly measures the combined prompt-plus-PI shift relative to an unprompted reference, rather than isolating PI’s influence conditional on prompting.
- **Analyst inference—theory:** The entropy guarantee is conditional and local. Positive covariance is concentrated in selected strong-PMI prefixes; the complementary group has negative mean covariance (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—scope and measurement:** Prompt ablations and faithfulness interventions use only Qwen3-4B. Response-pattern measurements use lexical heuristics, which cannot establish semantic reasoning quality (§§5.4–5.6; Appendix J).
- **Evidence gap:** The supplied prose provides no training-seed uncertainty for the main accuracy gains, and does not permit a complete numerical assessment against AntiSD.

## Open research questions
- **Can prompts adapt during training?** Fixed task prompts may lose usefulness as capabilities change (§6). Compare fixed prompts with validation-guided prompt selection at matched training budgets.
- **Does attenuation explain the gains?** The theory is conditional, and measured shifts combine prompt and PI effects (Appendix A). Add prompt-only distributions and directly controlled attenuation, then compare entropy and accuracy.
- **How broadly does reasoning dependence improve?** Truncation evidence covers one model (§5.6). Repeat across the other models and add controlled reasoning-corruption interventions.
- **Which prompts preserve correctness?** Some challenge-oriented prompts damage teacher accuracy (Appendix J.2). Screen prompt sets using both teacher correctness and student consistency before comparing them with unscreened sets.
