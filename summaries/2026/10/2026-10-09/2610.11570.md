# Scaling to Tens of Thousands of Test-Time Iterations with Loop-Native Attention Residuals

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11570) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11570)


## One-sentence takeaway
InfiLoop learns to aggregate recurrent states with constant persistent aggregation memory, improving puzzle reasoning and retaining solved Sudoku states over tested trajectories extending to 24,960 executed Transformer layers (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Looped Transformers reuse a shared block to increase reasoning depth without adding parameters. The authors identify “carry-last” state replacement as a bottleneck: later updates can overwrite correct deductions and undo completed solutions. Existing damping approaches regulate update magnitude but still pass only the latest state forward (§§1–2).

The motivation is to make additional test-time computation useful by preserving and selecting earlier computations, rather than relying entirely on their survival in the latest representation.

## Approach and contributions
- **Loop-native aggregation:** InfiLoop combines recurrent outputs using a shared content scorer and learned exponential temporal decay. The aggregate becomes the next recurrent input (§3.1).
- **Exact streaming implementation:** A weighted-state numerator and normalization denominator reproduce the history aggregation exactly. Persistent aggregation memory and per-step aggregation work are both \(O(d)\) per token (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Adaptive update interpretation:** The recurrence interpolates between the accumulated state and each new candidate. Separate scorer–decay pairs operate on the inner and outer recurrent axes (§3.3).
- **Empirical diagnosis and validation:** The authors measure solution loss under carry-last, compare benchmark and depth-scaling performance, and ablate content scoring, connection placement, and decay (§§2, 4; Appendix D).

The novelty is the recurrent aggregation and its gate parameterization; the paper explicitly relates these to existing attention residuals and gated recurrent updates (Appendix B).

| Carry-last loses completed solutions | Among puzzles both models solve within 36 outer updates, TRM loses 19.7% of solutions within 36 updates after first solving; InfiLoop retains every solution in that window. | §2; Figure 1 |
| Solved-state retention withstands tested perturbations | Perturbations of relative magnitude \(10^{-4}\) to 0.3 at update 36: InfiLoop retains 100% of its solved puzzles at update 72; TRM loses 15–20% even without perturbation. | §4.5; Figure 5 |
| Content scoring adds value beyond damping | At 1,872 layers on the Sudoku depth-study puzzles, exact accuracy is 90.9% normally, 84.1% when trained without scoring, and 84.8% when scoring is removed at test time. | Appendix D; Table 3 |

These results support improved accuracy and finite-horizon stability in the evaluated compact reasoner. Table 1 compares complete systems with differing inference procedures; the Sudoku depth study provides a closer comparison using matched layer budgets. The evidence does not establish indefinite solution preservation, broad-domain reasoning gains, or measured runtime savings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated qualifications:** The convex-hull property bounds the aggregate by candidate norms; a uniform bound over arbitrary depth additionally requires bounded candidate norms (§3.3). Compute accounting excludes several operations and does not compare measured runtime. Persistent-state accounting excludes weights and temporary workspaces (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited generality:** All evaluations concern grid tasks, and detailed stability and depth analyses concern Sudoku. Transfer to language reasoning, larger models, or other architectures remains untested (§4).
- **Analyst inference—finite evidence:** Claims of preserving solutions “indefinitely” exceed the reported finite retention tests. The largest depth is measured in executed layers, not that many recurrent iterations (§2; §4.4).
- **Comparison uncertainty:** The reported TRM ARC-2 result is 7.8% pass@2, while the released checkpoint evaluated under the authors’ protocol reaches 4.6%. URM’s ARC figures use pass@1, further limiting direct comparison (Table 1; §4.3).
- **Mechanism interpretation:** Score–correctness alignment supports the authors’ “self-verification” interpretation, but does not establish a general correctness verifier. After solving, the step size returns to its equal-weight baseline; low state movement therefore cannot be explained solely by a vanishing gate (§4.5; Figure 4).
- **Reporting gaps:** No multi-seed uncertainty estimates are supplied. Despite the reference to complete hyperparameters in Appendix A, that appendix primarily gives compute and memory accounting.

## Open research questions
- **Does stability persist much longer?** Reported retention tests are finite. Extend fixed-checkpoint runs beyond the largest tested budget and track both solution loss and newly solved puzzles.
- **Does the connection transfer beyond grid reasoning?** The supplied evaluations do not establish this. Compare carry-last and InfiLoop on a non-grid reasoning task with matched architecture, training, and inference budgets.
- **Does scoring detect correctness or familiar solved-state features?** Its evidence comes from Sudoku score correlations and ablations. Test scores and retention on shifted puzzle distributions and deliberately corrupted candidate states.
- **Do fewer layers deliver practical efficiency gains?** Layer counts omit runtime effects. Measure latency, throughput, and peak memory against FPRM at matched accuracy, including solver and aggregation overhead.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported results; **medium** for causal mechanism and generalization claims.

Material missing information includes multi-seed variability, measured runtime and full peak-memory comparisons, detailed ARC augmentation and candidate-selection settings, complete benchmark-specific training schedules, checkpoint-release status, and evidence beyond the tested tasks and trajectory lengths.
