# USDCraft: Geometrically Grounded Programmatic Modeling of Articulated 3D Assets for Simulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11322) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11322)


## One-sentence takeaway
USDCraft grounds pretrained LLM asset programs in measured mesh geometry and iterative feedback, reporting improved articulation reconstruction and limited sim-to-real success loss on three manipulation tasks without task-specific training (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Robot simulation needs assets that reproduce an object’s geometry, movable parts, joints, and physical properties. The authors identify two gaps: mesh segmentation cannot reconstruct missing surfaces or separate fused components reliably, while image-only programmatic generation can produce functional assets with incorrect dimensions and part placement. USDCraft aims to combine flexible rebuilding with instance-specific geometric measurements (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Geometry-grounded programmatic reconstruction:** A pretrained LLM writes, compiles, and revises an executable asset program from a source mesh and reference image. Programs explicitly define geometry, articulation, and physical parameters; generation uses the same representation without a source mesh (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Source geometry analysis:** Metric axial slice maps and targeted measurements expose observed surfaces, openings, and interior fittings as text. Unobserved cells remain unknown, allowing supported completion rather than treating missing scan surfaces as empty space (§3.2).
- **Iterative geometric rechecking:** Candidates are encoded on the source grid. Missing observed surfaces provide correction signals; additional candidate surfaces require interpretation. Depth residuals distinguish positional offsets from shape errors, while rendered feedback checks appearance, missing parts, and articulated clearances (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Evaluation assets and downstream validation:** The paper introduces USDCraft-bench and the USDCraft-10k generated library, evaluates reconstruction across model backbones, and tests simulation-trained robot policies on real objects. Public release availability is not established by the supplied text (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong Lightwheel part recovery and joint localization | On 243 human-modeled objects, unguided USDCraft–Astra achieves **86.983% part F1** and **0.009 joint-location error**, versus **83.400%** and **0.018** for Instruct-Particulate using dataset meshes. Location errors use normalized object coordinates. | Table 2; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Geometry gains have trade-offs | On Lightwheel, USDCraft–Astra has static gIoU **0.590** versus **0.583** for dataset-mesh Instruct-Particulate, but lower mIoU (**0.683 versus 0.724**) and higher articulated whole-object Chamfer distance (**0.011 versus 0.004**). | Table 2 |
| Shared articulation guidance improves recovery | Giving every Lightwheel case the same category-convention text changes Astra F1 from **86.983% to 89.765%** and axis angular error from **10.151° to 4.463°**. Joint errors cover matched parts in this setting. | Table 2; §4.2; Appendix G.4 |
| Benchmark advantage survives matched-case comparison | On the same 72 USDCraft-bench cases where ArtLLM produced outputs, F1 is **86.524%** for USDCraft–Astra, **80.824%** for USDCraft–Sol, **42.041%** for SIMART, **36.556%** for Particulate, and **19.337%** for ArtLLM. | Appendix C.2 |
| Small reported transfer loss on tested tasks | Across drawer opening, toaster-lever pressing, and toaster switching, USDCraft’s simulation-to-real success drop is at most **10 percentage points**, versus **60–80 points** for Articraft. Each supported method–task setting uses **200 simulation demonstrations**, with **20 simulation and 20 real trials**. | §4.4; Appendix E |
| Harness components contribute differently | Authors report that authoring support gives the largest incremental gain; removing source geometry analysis causes the largest deterioration in recovery, overlap, and joint accuracy. Numerical ablation rows are absent. | §4.6; Tables 6–7 captions |
| Generation and asset-library claims | In evaluation of **50 text and 50 image requests per setting**, an anonymous Sol/high judge ranks USDCraft highest across all five dimensions. USDCraft-10k contains **10,000 assets**, spanning **more than 500 categories**, from **70% text / 30% image** requests. Exact generation scores are absent. | §4.5; Appendix C.3 |

The supplied evidence supports reconstruction advantages with explicit geometry trade-offs and practical transfer on the tested tasks. It does not establish universal superiority or dynamic validity for all generated assets. Many tables appear only as captions, so their claimed margins, variability, runtimes, and costs cannot be independently quantified from this retrieval.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fine lattices remain difficult; shopping-cart wire spacing and connections differ from the source. Flexible members are represented through annotations and rest geometry, without validated bending, contact response, or rigid–flexible coupling (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated scope:** Unmeasured physical properties are modeling assumptions. Static checks and posed renders do not prove dynamic simulation behavior; experiments evaluate only PhysX (§3.3; Appendices G.1–G.3).
- **Analyst inference—transfer coverage:** Three tasks, one robot setup, and one trained policy per setting provide limited evidence about broader manipulation transfer. All methods also share USDCraft-generated support assets (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—comparison sensitivity:** Lightwheel joint errors depend on matched-part coverage, and methods receive different input modalities. Main USDCraft-bench normalization removes global scale and translation errors; reference-frame and source-exclusion checks are reported, but their numerical tables are missing (§4.2; Appendices C.1, D.5–D.6).
- **Analyst inference—generation validity:** A single model judge evaluates supplied records and static motion views, leaving human agreement and physical performance unestablished (Appendices C.3, G.3).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is completion to misleading images or missing scan regions?** Unknown geometry is resolved using image and functional interpretation, but controlled corruption results are not supplied. Next experiment: vary scan completeness and image–mesh consistency while measuring recovery and unsupported additions (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Which physical assumptions matter most for transfer?** Masses, friction, and resistance may be assumed, while downstream coverage is narrow. Next experiment: perturb these parameters independently and compare policy transfer against measured settings (§3.3; Appendix E).
- **Can finer geometric representations recover lattices reliably?** Local spacing and connectivity remain failure modes. Next experiment: compare adaptive local sampling and topology diagnostics against the existing axial representation on lattice objects (Appendices A.4, F).
- **Does model-scored generation quality predict simulator performance?** The rubric explicitly excludes dynamic proof. Next experiment: pair judge scores with human review and standardized contact, stability, and articulation tests (Appendix G.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and evaluation protocols are well described, and Lightwheel results are available. Material gaps include numerical bodies of most result tables, exact ablation effects, success rates by method and task, variability and cost figures, complete SDK guidance and prompts, and confirmed code/data release availability.
