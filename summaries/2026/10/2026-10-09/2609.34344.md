# Learning to Steer, Steering to See: Unveiling the Geometry of RLVR in Large Language Models via Trainable Vectors

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34344) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34344)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors find that small activation interventions recover much of the evaluated RLVR gains, predominantly outside leading activation principal components, and use this geometry to build an empirically effective training stabilizer.

## Problem and motivation
High-dimensional parameter updates make it difficult to identify which internal changes support RLVR improvements. The authors investigate whether those improvements admit compact activation-space interventions, how intervention capacity depends on depth and context, and whether the resulting geometry helps explain transfer and training collapse (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Capacity probe:** Freeze the base model and distill its RL-tuned counterpart into one input-invariant additive vector per controlled layer. Compare against full fine-tuning and LoRA under on-policy and off-policy distillation; use parameter-matched static controls and token-dependent gating to investigate depth-related failures (§2).
- **Geometric analysis:** Sequentially extract independently evaluated, layer-wise orthogonal directions; compare their alignment across training configurations and scientific topics, and measure their energy in base-model activation principal subspaces (§3).
- **Alpha-Stabler:** Monitor principal-subspace intrusion (PSI) against fixed base-model principal components, then conditionally remove those components from incoming activation gradients. It adds no trainable parameters and leaves inference to the trained actor alone (§4; Algorithm 1).
- **Conditional theory:** Develop local, sensitivity-weighted recovery bounds and exact gradient-projection properties, explicitly separating these from empirical task-score recovery and long-run stability (Appendix D; Table 5).

The evidence supports compact, useful interventions and targeted stabilization in the evaluated settings. Held-out scores use **mean@4**, averaging four sampled-response scores rather than selecting the best response (Appendix B.2). It does not establish the intrinsic dimension of RL trajectories, causal prediction of transfer from alignment, or a general no-collapse guarantee. The theoretical recovery ratio measures local target energy, not empirical task-score gain (Appendices D.4–D.8).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Results cover RLVR; RLHF, multi-turn agents, and open-ended generation remain unvalidated (§5).
- **Author-stated theoretical limits:** Recovery bounds depend on local approximation, shared-target structure, metric compatibility, and other assumptions not derived from first principles. Low learned-vector PC energy alone does not verify those assumptions (Appendices D.1–D.6).
- **Author-stated control limits:** Warm-up must initially be stable. Threshold calibration provides no detection-error guarantee, and single-site leakage bounds do not establish cumulative PSI reduction or long-run optimizer-independent stability (Appendix D.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer evidence is narrow: six selected ordered pairs within four scientific topics support association, with broader predictive validity unresolved (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No multi-seed uncertainty estimates are reported in the supplied text. Many central results appear only as qualitative descriptions of plots, limiting assessment of effect sizes and variability.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The main text interprets Fig. 12 as wall-clock overhead evidence, while its caption emphasizes pre-collapse score trajectories. Detailed runtime and memory accounting is unavailable (pp. 9, 25; Appendix D.7).

## Open research questions
- **Does the geometry generalize beyond RLVR?** These settings are explicitly untested (§5). Repeat the steering, localization, and stabilization experiments on RLHF and multi-turn agent tasks.
- **Can directional alignment predict unseen transfer?** The current fit covers six scientific-topic pairs (§3.2). Test predictions on held-out topic pairs and unrelated domains, with uncertainty estimates.
- **How reliable is PSI as an early-warning detector?** Lead times and false-alarm rates are not established (Appendix D.7). Evaluate the fixed detector across repeated stable and collapsing runs.
- **Which gradient-to-activation couplings explain successful control?** Projection guarantees concern local incoming gradients, while activation leakage remains conditional (Appendix D.7). Measure coupling terms and resulting activation shifts across optimizers and control sites.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the methods and their theoretical boundaries, but quantitative assessment is limited by missing plot data.

Material unavailable information includes exact per-task scores and recovery ratios, repeated-run variability, detector lead times and error rates, detailed runtime/memory measurements, and complete model/task mappings for stabilization plots. The text claims code availability but does not establish executable reproducibility.
