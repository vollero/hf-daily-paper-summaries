# WorldGuide: Goal-Directed Video World Model for Procedural Task Execution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12459) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12459)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorldGuide learns action planning, video execution, and stopping from shared procedural demonstrations, improving judged task completion over its open-loop variant and evaluated baselines, while substantial execution failures remain (Tables 2–4).

## Problem and motivation
Procedural video generation must complete an ordered task, rather than merely produce plausible motion. The authors identify two gaps: open-loop generation cannot revise actions after observing errors, and feedback-based planners can request actions that their pretrained executors cannot realize. They also argue that existing datasets lack the combined atomic-action clips, goals, and completion supervision needed to learn the full loop (§§1, 3.5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Learned procedural loop:** Starting from an image and goal, a Qwen2.5-VL ContextPlanner predicts one action or a completion token from recent generated clip–action pairs. A HunyuanVideo-1.5 Executor renders the action, and its output guides the next decision (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Shared demonstrations and action interface:** The planner is trained first, then frozen; the executor learns from ground-truth action captions embedded by that planner. Training is sequential, not end-to-end (§3.3; Appendix A).
- **WorldGuide Bench:** The authors construct 58,679 annotated instructional videos covering 245 tasks and 27 categories, with atomic-action clips and explicit stopping targets (§3.5; Appendix B).
- **Memory integration:** The executor adapts an existing spatial compression schedule to retain visual history at bounded token cost. The compression design itself is inherited, rather than newly introduced (§3.4; Appendix C).

The ablations support benefits from feedback and memory within this system. They do not establish reliable completion, real-world physical execution, or accurate stopping independently of overall task success. Text-plan scores and video-execution scores measure different outcomes and cannot be compared directly. Reported percentage gains between success rates are percentage-point differences.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated:** Planning can repeat actions, skip prerequisites, or stop early; execution can distort geometry and appearance. Errors propagate through generated observations, and replanning cannot undo an incorrect transition. Compressed memory can discard needed details and increases repetition. Training lacks a joint task-completion objective (§5; Appendix H).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the protocol:**

- WorldGuide Bench tests unseen demonstrations of known tasks; it does not establish unseen-task generalization. Video-CraftBench covers only block assembly and paper folding (Appendices B.4, E.1).
- Automatic annotation errors can affect both supervision and reference-based evaluation (Table 9).
- Excluding unparseable judge responses produces different evaluation denominators across models. Confidence intervals, repeated-rollout variability, and exclusion sensitivity are not reported (Appendices E.1–E.2).
- The small human study supports a ranking but does not establish statistical significance (Appendix F).
- No matched frozen-executor ablation isolates the benefit of executor fine-tuning from the other changes; memory ablation disables memory in an already memory-trained checkpoint (Table 4; Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions

- **Can explicit recovery repair failed transitions?** Current replanning cannot undo errors. Test a verifier and corrective-action policy on deliberately incomplete or distorted steps (Appendix H).
- **Can joint training improve execution while preserving readable actions?** Sequential freezing protects the language interface. Compare it with joint training that retains action-language supervision (Appendix A).
- **Can selective memory reduce repetition without losing success?** Memory improves completion but raises redundant execution. Compare task-relevant retention against the current compression schedule under matched token budgets (Table 4; Appendix H).
- **Does the method generalize to unseen procedures?** The main split holds out demonstrations, not tasks. Run task-disjoint and category-disjoint evaluations (Appendix B.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text describes the method and protocols extensively, but leaves material uncertainty about statistical significance, repeated-run variation, standalone stopping accuracy, per-task failure rates, inference latency, exact generated clip lengths and active memory lengths, comprehensive judge-exclusion counts, and actual code, checkpoint, and dataset release availability.
