# SpaceCast-Bench: Evaluating Predictive Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12402) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12402)


## One-sentence takeaway
SpaceCast-Bench exposes a large human–model gap in reasoning about unseen spatial transformations, while scene-disjoint supervised training substantially improves one evaluated model and selectively transfers to other benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation
The authors distinguish spatial perception—reading relations in observed images—from predictive spatial reasoning: integrating observations, applying an intervention, and inferring relations in an unseen result. They argue that existing benchmarks inadequately isolate these operations, although they matter for embodied planning and manipulation (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic benchmark:** 3,862 questions from 182 indoor scenes, covering 16 task types across static perception, local interventions, and global layout rotations. Relations include direction, distance, occlusion, and attachment, with three directional reference frames (§3; Table 5).
- **Geometry-grounded generation:** Posed RGB-D scans provide geometry for selecting depth-verified anchor–bridge view chains, applying transformations, and computing labels. Models normally receive only initial-scene RGB views and textual questions; automatic filtering and human review check answerability (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Evaluation and training evidence:** Comparisons across 21 models, paired prompting/evidence diagnostics, and Qwen3-VL-4B post-training assess capability gaps and transfer (§4).

The claim of being the “first” comprehensive diagnostic benchmark is the authors’ positioning; the supplied text alone cannot independently establish priority.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large human–model gap | Under CoT and deterministic decoding, Gemini 3.5 Flash achieves 58.0% versus human 87.2%: a 29.2 percentage-point gap in unweighted accuracy averaged across 16 task types. | Table 1; §4.1–4.2; Appendix C.2 |
| Spatial specialization does not guarantee success | Six specialized models score 25.7–29.1% overall task-macro accuracy, near the sampled random baseline of 25.9%; strongest general-purpose open-weight model scores 39.3%. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Local prediction is difficult, but levels are not monotonically harder | Gemini 3.5 Flash achieves 67.1% Static, 45.3% Local, and 69.4% Global accuracy, each macro-averaged over its task family. | Table 1 |
| Bridge views improve directional accuracy | Across six representative models, mean directional accuracy increases from 32.9% without bridges to 35.9% with bridges; world-centric accuracy rises from 34.6% to 39.9%. | Figure 6; §4.3 |
| Explicit geometry helps more consistently than generated outcomes | Textual 3D metadata changes mean accuracy by +30.7 points for the selected proprietary pair, +10.9 for the open-weight pair, and −3.3 for the specialized pair. Generated-media effects are smaller and mixed. | Figure 7; §4.3 |
| Generation failures explain part of outcome-image errors | In an audit of 600 local-intervention questions across six models, 80.3% of incorrect responses with Qwen-Image-Edit evidence and 38.3% with GPT-image-2 evidence are associated with generation failures. | §4.3 |
| SFT substantially improves the trained model | Qwen3-VL-4B overall task-macro accuracy rises from 34.0% to 64.8% with Mixed SFT and 65.7% with Staged SFT; GRPO reaches 37.7%. Training scenes are disjoint from evaluation scenes. | Tables 4, 9; Appendix B.1 |
| Transfer is selective | Staged SFT raises the six-subset OOD accuracy macro-average from 33.5% to 38.1%, but MindCube falls from 31.2% to 30.8%. | Tables 4, 10 |

These results establish benchmark-specific weaknesses and substantial improvement for one trained backbone. They do not establish physical planning competence or uniform transfer. The authors’ proposed mechanisms—such as inadequate global representations or underrepresented attachment training—are interpretations rather than directly isolated causes.

The §4.2 statement that all model groups decline on Global relative to Static is difficult to reconcile with Table 1, where several proprietary models improve. Appendix B.2 more carefully states that levels describe transformation scope rather than a scalar difficulty scale.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers indoor ScanNet/ScanNet++ scenes only, with most questions drawn from ScanNet++. Reconstruction errors can affect visibility, contact, and recognizability despite filtering and review (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Transformations do not simulate deformation, friction, or collision-driven motion. The benchmark cannot demonstrate readiness for safety-critical deployment (Appendix A; Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference:** SFT–GRPO differences cannot be attributed solely to the learning objective: adapter ranks and multimodal-aligner training differ (Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** OOD gains concern selected question subsets and one trained backbone, limiting claims about full-benchmark or cross-model generalization (Tables 4, 10).
- **Reporting risks:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated-run results are reported. Appendix B.6 gives 3,801 single-answer plus 97 attachment questions, inconsistent with the stated total of 3,862; the human baseline’s allocation among volunteers is unspecified.

## Open research questions
- **Does training generalize beyond indoor scans?** Coverage excludes outdoor environments and other scales. Next experiment: evaluate the trained model on independently constructed outdoor and unfamiliar-layout tasks.
- **Which training ingredients cause the gains?** Objective comparisons also change adapter capacity and trainable components. Next experiment: compare SFT and GRPO with matched adapters, aligner settings, and training budgets.
- **Can faithful outcome evidence close the prediction gap?** Generation errors coexist with apparent evidence-integration failures. Next experiment: compare generated endpoints with geometry-rendered endpoints under matched viewpoints.
- **Does geometric prediction transfer to physical dynamics?** Current updates omit interaction physics. Next experiment: add controlled friction, collision, and deformation interventions with independently validated outcomes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and table results; **medium** for causal explanations.

Material missing information includes verified asset availability, exact textual 3D-metadata formatting and field coverage, training compute and schedule duration, repeated-run variability, human-question allocation, and resolution of the quality-control count discrepancy.
