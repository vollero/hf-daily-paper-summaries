# SpatialOPSD: Self-Distilling Spatial Intelligence from Verified Coding Agent Traces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11366) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11366)


## One-sentence takeaway
SpatialOPSD transfers verified spatial coding-agent traces into a tool-free multimodal model, improving benchmark accuracy while reducing lexical references to privileged traces, although its combined repetition controls do not achieve the best ablation accuracy (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Spatial reasoning requires recovering spatial structure and viewpoint relationships from images, but intermediate geometric reasoning is difficult to annotate or verify. The authors argue that answer-only supervision and sparse-reward RL provide inadequate guidance, while coding agents obtain execution-grounded evidence at the cost of external tools and iterative inference (§1).

The identified gap is how to retain the benefit of agent traces in a standalone model that receives only images and questions at inference (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trace-guided reasoning observation:** Conditioning Qwen3.5-9B on agent execution traces produces spatial-task performance comparable to running the agent itself in the tested prompting settings (Figure 2).
- **SpatialOPSD:** Collect agent traces whose final answers match ground truth, summarize them into privileged teacher context, and train a student on its own sampled responses using token-level reverse-KL supervision from a frozen copy of its initial checkpoint. Only the teacher receives privileged context, including retained visual artifacts (§3.2; Appendix A.1–A.2).
- **Repetition-Aware Distillation:** Detect long spans containing repeated rollout content or privileged-context n-grams absent from the input; mask their distillation loss and apply an unlikelihood penalty. This targets copying and self-repetition while retaining supervision elsewhere (§3.2, Equations 6–10).

The proposed contribution combines spatial-agent privilege with repetition controls; OPSD itself is presented as an existing method (§2).

These results establish improved accuracy under the reported settings and reduced occurrence of the leakage detector’s keywords. They do not establish semantic elimination of privileged-information dependence or validated intermediate geometry. The authors’ interpretation that the model internalizes geometric reasoning is stronger than the direct behavioral evidence. Likewise, removing inference-time tools is demonstrated by the setup, but latency and cost savings are not measured.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** PI leakage means any qualifying occurrence of “agent,” “trace,” or “tool,” including ordinary uses; it does not assess semantic intent (Appendix A.3). Lexical reasoning markers measure articulation style and are explicitly not causal evidence of improved reasoning (§5; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—verification scope:** Ground-truth agreement verifies final answers, not every intermediate measurement or inference. Correct answers can therefore coexist with flawed traces (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—baseline comparability:** SFT uses a different retained subset and a different rationale generator. GRPO shares SpatialOPSD’s questions, but uses different rollout counts, response limits, and learning-rate schedules. The comparisons do not isolate training objective alone (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—privilege ablation confounds:** Variants differ in reasoning content, explicit answers, and extra images, so Table 6 does not isolate summarization as the cause of improvement (Table 9).
- **Analyst inference—scope and uncertainty:** Training comes from MindCube, evaluated backbones are Qwen-family models, and agent portability is tested with two agents. No repeated-seed uncertainty or significance estimates are supplied (Tables 1–8).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** The Summary protocol requires verbatim numeric preservation, but its worked example rounds raw values while the caption calls them verbatim; the compliance of the broader corpus is unclear (Appendix A.1.2, pp. 19–23).

## Open research questions
- **Does lexical suppression reduce semantic trace dependence?** The keyword metric cannot answer this. Next experiment: annotate fabricated tool evidence and unsupported measurements, including paraphrases without detector keywords.
- **How much gain comes from the objective rather than data and compute differences?** Baseline settings differ. Next experiment: compare methods on identical instances with matched generation budgets and independently tuned training schedules.
- **Which privileged channels drive transfer?** Current variants jointly change text, answers, and images. Next experiment: factorially ablate summaries, metric readouts, derived images, and answer exposure.
- **Does transfer extend beyond the tested model family and answer format?** Reported training uses Qwen models and multiple-choice supervision. Next experiment: train another backbone and evaluate free-form metric estimation and unseen scene conditions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported setup and table comparisons; **medium** in claims of internalized geometric reasoning and broad generalization.

Material information unavailable includes released code/weights/traces, repeated-run uncertainty, hardware models and measured compute costs, inference latency, semantic leakage audits, and independent validation of intermediate trace correctness.
