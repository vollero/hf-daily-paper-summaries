# V-CoLA: Vision Token Compression with Linear Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11251) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11251)


## One-sentence takeaway
V-CoLA combines recurrent-state importance scoring, adaptive token merging, and vision-token early exit to retain 99.5% of Qwen3.5-9B’s reported normalized benchmark performance at a 50% average vision-token budget, with substantial prefill acceleration (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Vision tokens dominate VLM inputs, increasing inference computation and memory demands. The authors identify a transfer gap: compression methods developed for softmax-attention models may perform poorly on hybrid models with linear attention. Their experiments show little advantage over random pruning for representative attention- and similarity-based methods; they attribute this to different attention distributions and information loss in bounded recurrent states (§§2.2–2.3, Figures 2–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Architecture-specific analysis:** The authors combine empirical diagnostics with a finite-state information-capacity argument to motivate importance signals derived from linear-attention recurrence (§2).
- **Uniqueness-aware scoring:** A training-free score combines how accurately the final state reconstructs each token’s value with how much that token changes nearby probe readouts. This balances retained information and local novelty (§3.2, Equations 6–8).
- **Adaptive merging:** Contiguous chunks receive approximately equal importance mass, giving important regions smaller chunks; importance-weighted averaging produces one token per chunk (§3.3).
- **Early exit and parallel implementation:** Deep layers discard vision tokens, while extended Gated DeltaRule operators and parallel chunk partitioning reduce compression overhead (§3.4, Appendix A). Early exit builds on prior work; the recurrence-based score and adaptive merging are the central proposed components.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported aggregate retention | Qwen3.5-9B: V-CoLA’s normalized Avg is 99.5%, 94.8%, and 88.0% at average budgets of 440, 220, and 110 tokens versus the 880-token baseline; VisionZip obtains 99.0%, 94.5%, and 86.4%. Eight benchmarks. | Table 1 |
| Compression has uneven task costs | At the 12.5% average budget, TextVQA falls from 83.2 to 46.0, while POPE changes from 89.9 to 88.3. | Table 1 |
| Faster language-model prefill | On the stated A100 experimental hardware, latency falls from 269 to 145 ms for 2K vision tokens at 50% retention (1.86×), and from 1,034 to 168 ms for 8K tokens at 12.5% retention (6.15×). | §4.1, Table 2 |
| Components improve reported scores | At 75% average token reduction, MME is 2268.7 with V-CoLA, 2197.4 without adaptive merging, and 2235.1 without early exit. | Table 6 |
| Transfer beyond the main model | At 75% compression, V-CoLA has the highest reported MME, SQA, and MMStar scores among compared methods on both Qwen3.5-27B and InfiniteVL; InfiniteVL POPE favors VisionZip, 86.8 versus 86.3. | Table 7 |
| Additional task coverage | Qwen3.5-9B MMDU overall score changes from 66.6 to 65.4 at 12.5% average retention. V*-Bench early-exit-only accuracy is 74.87 at layer 24 versus 75.92 without early exit. | Tables 8–9 |
| Smaller but practical E2E gains | INT4 TensorRT Qwen3.5-9B on Jetson Thor, 2K visual input tokens and 32 output tokens: 12.5% retention reduces TTFT from 1476.3 to 522.9 ms and E2E latency from 2254.9 to 1301.5 ms (1.733×). | Table 10 |

The evidence supports a favorable measured quality–efficiency tradeoff for the tested hybrid models. It does not establish uniform benchmark superiority, negligible quality loss at aggressive compression, or equivalent prefill and end-to-end speedups. Table 1 reports **average** token budgets: early exit can permit higher retention before the exit layer, as Table 9 explicitly illustrates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Pure Mamba-based and fully linear-attention VLMs are untested; removing periodic softmax layers may change the scoring criterion’s behavior (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Method design centers on Gated DeltaNet; broader recurrent-update formulations require systematic evaluation (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** The information-capacity argument motivates the method but does not by itself prove that bounded states cause the observed baseline failures or that the proposed scores identify task-critical tokens (§2.3).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty estimates or repeated-run variability are supplied, limiting interpretation of small differences such as V-CoLA’s aggregate advantage over VisionZip (Tables 1 and 7).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** §3.2 describes probes drawn from queries, whereas Appendix A describes shifted keys. The normalized Avg formula and precise layer-averaged budget accounting are not explicit. MMStar also differs between the main result and default hyperparameter ablations (Tables 1, 4–5).

## Open research questions
- **Does scoring transfer to fully recurrent VLMs?** These architectures are excluded (§7). Evaluate compatible models with and without periodic softmax layers at matched budgets.
- **Can compression preserve scene-text performance better?** TextVQA degrades substantially under aggressive compression (Table 1). Test text-region-aware budget allocation against the current importance-based merging.
- **When should vision tokens exit?** Fixed exit depths produce different small-object accuracy, and E2E benefits depend on workload (Tables 9–10). Compare adaptive exit rules with fixed exits across visual difficulty and output lengths.
- **Which probe construction is intended and most effective?** Main-text queries and Appendix A shifted keys are inconsistent. Implement both and compare quality, overhead, and score agreement under identical settings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main method and reported results, but material gaps remain: explicit code availability, exact normalized-average calculation, complete budget accounting, probe-construction clarification, evaluation splits and generation settings, timing repetition/batch details, and statistical uncertainty. Figures 2–6 provide captions and qualitative descriptions without their numerical plot data.
