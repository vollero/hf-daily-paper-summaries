# OneSearch-VL: Unified Multimodal Deep Research Agent for Image and Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12419) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12419)


## One-sentence takeaway
OneSearch-VL uses visually grounded evidence graphs to train a unified image-and-video research agent, with reported accuracy gains from joint multimodal training and rewards for evidence traceability and visual grounding (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Research over images and videos requires identifying visual entities, retrieving external facts, and composing those facts into answers. The authors identify a provenance gap: existing evidence structures do not jointly retain dependencies connecting localized visual anchors, entity relations, supporting sources, and answer-producing operations. They also seek one policy spanning single images, image collections, and videos (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **VGEG and data engine:** A task-level Visually Grounded Evidence Graph records visual anchors, source-supported facts, answer operations, and their dependencies. It supports question verification and trajectory filtering. New multi-image and video tasks come from curated web videos; existing single-image data are converted into VGEGs (§3; Appendix A).
- **Unified agent and EVGR:** Qwen3-VL-8B is trained through supervised fine-tuning and GRPO using OneSearch-VL-SFT-110K and OneSearch-VL-RL-10K. EVGR adds separate model-judged evidence-traceability and visual-grounding rewards to inherited answer and query rewards (§5; Appendix D). VGEG serves as a training reference; the text does not establish that the policy explicitly maintains this graph during inference.
- **Operation-oriented benchmarks:** OneSearch-MI-Bench and OneSearch-Video-Bench classify questions by their final research operation, enabling breakdowns of retrieval and fact composition rather than visual content alone (§4; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

All accuracy results use GPT-4o binary correctness judgments against reference answers (§6.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Single-image improvement | Seven-benchmark mean score **58.3**, exceeding Qwen3-VL-8B Agent by **16.3 points** and OpenSearch-VL-8B by **1.7 points**. | §6.2, narrative accompanying Table 2 |
| Multi-image and video improvement | Versus tool-enabled Qwen3-VL-8B: **+20.2 percentage points** on OneSearch-MI-Bench, **+17.6** on OneSearch-Video-Bench, and **+27.0** on VideoDR. | Abstract; §6.2, narrative accompanying Table 3 |
| Joint SFT improves the aggregate | With equal training iterations, joint image/multi-image/video SFT averages **55.8**, versus **51.7**, **53.1**, and **55.1** for image-only, multi-image-only, and video-only training. | EVGR adds benefit beyond answer/query rewards | On the same six-benchmark average: answer-plus-query RL **57.3**; adding Trace **59.0**; adding Ground **59.3**; full reward **61.1**, a **3.8-point** gain over answer-plus-query RL. | §6.3; Table 4(b) |

The ablations support complementary training signals under the reported setup. They do not isolate the benefit of VGEG construction from other data-engine choices or demonstrate reliable factual support for every correct answer. Main comparison tables are missing their numerical bodies in the supplied text, so their headline results rely on the authors’ narrative; Table 4 is available directly.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Changing webpages and external tools affect evidence availability and reproducibility; automated graph construction and model judges can introduce errors or bias; multi-turn interaction adds inference and tool costs (§9).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- **Scope is selective:** Multi-image collections contain frames from one source video, so generalization to independently sourced images is untested here (§4). Source curation excludes several video categories, including Education and Music (Appendix A.1).
- **Evidence reliability remains unresolved:** Figures 7–8 preserve conflicting retrieved facts; Figure 8 also contains an inaccurate final-response detail. Answer accuracy therefore does not fully measure evidence quality (Appendix E.2).
- **Attribution is incomplete:** Reported ablations vary input mixtures and rewards, but do not compare graph construction against an otherwise matched graph-free pipeline (§6.3).
- **Uncertainty is unavailable:** The supplied results provide no confidence intervals or repeated-run variation. Human review is described, but reviewer counts and agreement statistics are absent (Appendix B.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does VGEG itself improve learning?** Reward and mixture ablations leave graph-construction effects unresolved. Compare matched pipelines with and without explicit anchor-to-fact-to-operation dependencies.
- **Do EVGR scores agree with human process judgments?** The authors acknowledge judge bias and propose calibration (§9). Audit held-out trajectories with independent human ratings of grounding and factual support.
- **How robust is the agent to retrieval failures and changing evidence?** This remains future work (§9). Evaluate frozen retrieval snapshots alongside controlled missing-page, tool-failure, and conflicting-source conditions.
- **Do multi-image gains transfer to independently sourced images?** Current collections derive from one video (§4). Test matched research operations using image sets drawn from unrelated sources.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reward ablations are well described, but several important evaluation details are unavailable.

Missing information includes full Tables 2–3, operation-count Table 6, anchored scoring Table 8, the exact Trace/Ground combination rule, prompt templates, train–benchmark source-overlap controls, statistical uncertainty, human-review agreement, measured inference costs, and confirmed public asset releases.
