# DreamTrue: Action-Faithful Robot World Model with Counterfactual Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12468) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12468)


## One-sentence takeaway
DreamTrue combines geometric calibration with reward-guided counterfactual post-training to improve robot video prediction, reducing human-assessed interaction defects from 48.12% to 6.25% on the supplied AgiBot counterfactual benchmark (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Robot world models must follow specified actions and predict coherent object responses to support reliable action and policy evaluation. The authors identify two gaps: inaccurate camera calibration misaligns rendered action conditions with training videos, while demonstration-heavy data encourage hallucinated success after unsuccessful actions. They seek to address both without collecting additional real-robot training trajectories (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Offline geometric calibration:** Refines camera parameters and applicable arm mounting offsets using robot-rendering, temporal-background, and cross-view correspondences from existing RGB recordings, without dedicated calibration sequences or sensor depth (§3.2).
- **Shared action-conditioned generator:** Represents embodiment-specific trajectories through rendered RGB, depth, masks, gripper cues, and camera-ray maps. A VACE-conditioned video DiT learns multi-view prediction across robot datasets (§3.3).
- **Counterfactual post-training and assets:** Perturbs trajectory endpoints, retains inverse-kinematics-feasible actions, and optimizes generated videos using a human-trained defect reward model; recorded actions additionally receive a ground-truth PSNR reward. The authors report releasing refined calibration for 153,666 episodes and annotations covering 44.9K videos (including 30.4K defect labels) (§3.4; Appendices A.2, B.1).

The distinctive proposal is the combination of calibration correction and interaction-focused counterfactual reward learning; VACE, GDPO, and DiffusionNFT are adopted components.

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; the supplied text contains table captions but omits the numerical table bodies.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported AgiBot action following | Full-model nDTW: **0.8772 recorded**, **0.8831 counterfactual**, highest among evaluated methods; evaluations use 800 recorded episodes and 160 counterfactual conditions across 54 tasks. | §4.2, Table 1; Appendix C.1 |
| Substantial reduction in visible defects | Stage I → full model: object defects **31.88% → 3.12%**; interaction defects **48.12% → 6.25%**, on the same 160 AgiBot counterfactual conditions using majority votes from three blinded annotators. | §4.2, Table 1; Appendix C.1 |
| Improved DROID recorded-action fidelity | Versus CtrlWorld: PSNR **22.00 → 22.95**, LPIPS **0.162 → 0.073**, on 50 held-out episodes; authors also report better SSIM without supplying its value in prose. | §4.3, Table 3; Appendix C.1 |
| More accurate simulated policy evaluation | Versus Ctrl-World, success-rate MAE **12.92 → 7.08 percentage points** across 24 task–policy–setting groups from 240 trajectories; DreamTrue achieves Spearman **ρ = 0.937** and signed bias **+0.42 pp**. | §4.4, Figure 8; Appendix C.2 |
| Fewer hallucinated policy successes | On those 240 RoboTwin episodes, false positives fall **26 → 12** versus Ctrl-World; DreamTrue reports **90.42% accuracy**. | Appendix C.2, Table 11 |
| Better calibration alignment | Versus dataset calibration, mean mask IoU gains are **0.228** over 130,182 AgiBot episodes and **0.212** over 63,061 DROID episodes; improvements occur on **97.1%** and **91.6%**, respectively. Reference masks come from SAM3. | §4.5, Figure 9 |
| Reward model agrees imperfectly with labels | Across three defect dimensions on **4,893** source-episode-held-out clips: average accuracy **86.33%**, macro-F1 **74.21%**. | §4.6, Table 5; Appendix B.1 |

Independent human evaluation supports reduced **observable** defects, and simulator comparisons support improved outcome estimation in the tested tasks. These results do not establish universally correct physics, real-world policy-evaluation accuracy, or quantitative transfer to unseen embodiments. The authors also report first place in the AgiBot World Challenge 2026, but the supplied Table 2 has no score entries (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Image-space generation and reward assessment can accept visually plausible but physically incorrect interactions under occlusion or limited views (§5).
- **Analyst inference—limited attribution:** The supplied experiments compare Stage I with the combined Stage-II procedure but do not isolate counterfactual augmentation, individual defect rewards, and the PSNR preservation reward (§§3.4, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generalization evidence:** Policy evaluation covers three simulated bimanual tasks with complete executed trajectories. Unseen-scene and unseen-robot transfer is qualitative, with six clips per setting (§4.4; Appendices C.2, D).
- **Analyst inference—measurement dependence:** Calibration quality uses SAM3 masks, while robot segmentation also helps construct calibration correspondences; this is an alignment proxy rather than independent measurement of camera geometry (§§3.2, 4.5).
- **Protocol caveats:** Reward-training labels use one annotation per video. Downstream evaluation uses three blinded annotators, but reported pairwise significance tests are descriptive and uncorrected for multiple comparisons (Appendices B.1, C.1).
- **Deployment boundary:** Evaluation episodes can receive additional calibration using frames outside the prediction interval; deployment requires comparable calibration access (Appendix A.2.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which Stage-II component drives the improvement?** The combined comparison leaves attribution unresolved. Run matched ablations of recorded-only post-training, counterfactual augmentation, individual defect rewards, and PSNR preservation.
2. **Can visually convincing errors survive reward optimization?** The authors explicitly identify occlusion risk. Test controlled occlusion and reduced-view settings against simulator contact and object-state ground truth.
3. **Does outcome accuracy transfer to real robots and unseen embodiments?** Policy evaluation is simulated and OOD evidence is qualitative. Execute matched trajectories on unseen real embodiments and compare generated success labels with actual outcomes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and headline findings, with useful evaluation protocols, but lacks numerical table bodies, per-dimension reward results, reported confidence intervals and test values, training compute and duration, inference latency, perturbation magnitudes, and component-level Stage-II ablations. Asset releases are author claims; their availability was not independently checked.
