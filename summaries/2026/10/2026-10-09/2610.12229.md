# VibeEdit: Image Editing with Canvas Instructions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12229) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12229)


## One-sentence takeaway
VibeEdit combines on-image marks and notes with separate source/annotation conditioning and rubric-guided training, achieving better edit quality and source preservation than evaluated baselines on a benchmark emphasizing similar-object targeting (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Localized image editing requires specifying both the desired change and its target. Text descriptions become cumbersome when several objects look alike. The authors identify a gap between spatial controls that locate edits and language that specifies their meaning: canvas instructions place both directly on the image across five editing operations (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Canvas interface:** Circles, removal scribbles, movement arrows, and rasterized notes express addition, removal, attribute modification, replacement, and movement without a separate text prompt. Movement uses no note in the default grammar (§3.1; Table 12).
- **Data and editor:** The authors construct synthetic source–target pairs with masks and structured descriptions, rendering varied annotations during training. VibeEdit adapts Qwen-Image-Edit: a frozen vision–language encoder interprets the annotated image, while the VAE separately encodes the clean source and annotation layer. Rank-128 LoRA adapters train the generative model (§3.1, §4.1).
- **Training and evaluation:** Region-weighted supervised learning emphasizes edits and annotation removal; DiffusionNFT refinement rewards edit success, local quality, and outside preservation. An independently constructed, human-curated benchmark tests targeting among similar objects (§3.2, §4.2–4.3).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Final model leads the evaluated baselines | On 419 benchmark cases, VibeEdit scores **79.9% VLM rubric / 32.8 dB outside PSNR**, versus **67.4% / 24.0 dB** for FireRed, the highest-scoring text-instructed baseline. VibeEdit receives source plus canvas; FireRed receives source plus detailed text. Evaluation uses one output per case at approximately 1024² pixels. | Table 1; §5.1–5.2 |
| Final configuration improves over supervised base | On the same benchmark, VibeEdit-base scores **67.8% / 27.0 dB**, versus **79.9% / 32.8 dB** after RL. Base inference uses 30 steps and CFG 4; final inference uses 16 steps and CFG 1. | Table 1; §5.1 |
| Separate source/annotation encoding helps | With annotated-image VLM input fixed, VAE conditioning on the annotated image scores **58.6% / 24.2 dB**; separate source and canvas encoding scores **67.8% / 27.0 dB**. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| Region weighting needs dilation in this ablation | Base-model rubric scores are **66.2%** without weighting/dilation, **64.2%** with weighting alone, and **67.8%** with weighting plus dilation. | Table 3 |
| Reward groups are complementary | Edit-success-only RL yields **69.2% / 28.6 dB**; combining success, preservation, and local quality yields **79.9% / 32.8 dB** on the benchmark. | Table 5 |
| Judge agrees with humans on sampled VibeEdit outputs | Across **100 outputs and 947 binary questions**, GPT-5.6-sol achieves **93.0% accuracy**, **95.5% F1**, and **κ = 0.805** against majority-vote human labels. Pooled pass rates are **77.0%** for the judge and **76.6%** for humans. | Table 10; Appendix A |

These results support the complete system on the authors’ benchmark, with ablations supporting separate conditioning and combined rewards. The rubric score averages criterion pass rates; it is not a percentage of fully successful edits. Outside PSNR measures preservation, not edit completion. Human validation supports the judge on sampled VibeEdit outputs, but does not establish equivalent reliability across all baselines.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author acknowledgment:** Automated filtering may leave erroneous synthetic training pairs (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** The benchmark emphasizes similar-object targeting, while training selection favors visible, separable objects and constrained movement destinations. Results do not establish performance on heavily entangled objects, occupied destinations, or broad unrestricted editing (§3.2; Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison attribution:** Baselines receive different input representations, and base/final inference settings differ. The overall gains therefore do not isolate interface benefits from specialized training or sampling changes (§5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated-run variability are reported. Small differences, such as high-variance versus random RL selection, have uncertain robustness (Table 4).
- **Reproducibility:** Prompts and substantial settings are supplied, but Appendix E explicitly omits API syntax, exhaustive schemas, and retry logic. Code, weights, and dataset availability are not established.

## Open research questions
- **How much improvement comes from the interface itself?** Input formats and training differ across comparisons. A next experiment could train matched canvas and text-conditioned editors on identical edit pairs and evaluate both targeting accuracy and user effort.
- **How well does the editor handle unrestricted user annotations?** The paper tests handwriting preferences and limited stroke-color changes, but not a broad distribution of ambiguous gestures. A next experiment could collect free-form annotations and stratify errors by legibility, placement, and conflicting cues.
- **Does judge agreement hold across competing editors?** Human validation covers only VibeEdit outputs. A next experiment could obtain blinded human rubric labels for outputs from every baseline and measure agreement separately by model and operation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark results; **medium** for generalization beyond those settings.

Material missing information includes artifact release status, detailed source-image provenance and overlap checks, repeated-run uncertainty, human validation across baselines, matched-inference base/RL comparisons, and deployment latency or user-effort measurements.
