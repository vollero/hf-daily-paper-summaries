# Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12360) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12360)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the evaluated agent configurations, higher task accuracy does not reliably imply better handling of knowledge conflicts, and prompting often increases communication of unresolved uncertainty while reducing accuracy (§§3.2–3.4).

## Problem and motivation
Final-answer accuracy hides whether an agent notices contradictory evidence, investigates it, and communicates unresolved uncertainty. The authors identify a gap between single-turn uncertainty evaluations and multi-step agents, where conflict recognition can disappear before the final response (§1).

This matters because users may see only the final answer and therefore miss uncertainty encountered during execution.

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory-level measurement:** Operationalize epistemic humility as **Identify**, **Solve**, and **Escalate**. Identify measures intermediate conflict recognition; Solve measures subsequent bounded tool-use attempts rather than successful resolution; Escalate measures substantive uncertainty communication among incorrect conflict-split answers (§2.2; Figs. 7–8).
- **Two conflict settings:** Construct same-question controlled conflict/control pairs using ConflictQA and WikiContradict, and select confident-but-wrong prior beliefs on GAIA, MoNaCo, and BrowseComp for naturally occurring conflict evaluation. Natural-conflict controls contain different questions (§2.1; Apps. A.1–A.2).
- **System-level evaluation and assets:** Evaluate five backbone–harness configurations across four harnesses, test a system-prompt intervention, and report releasing trajectories, per-turn judgments, data, and code (§§1, 2.3, 3.4).

The contribution extends existing conflict datasets and belief-elicitation procedures to agent trajectories; it does not introduce those source benchmarks (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled conflict reduces accuracy substantially | Every configuration with reported TMD results loses **more than 30 percentage points** relative to its same-question no-conflict control. Individual cells are unavailable in the supplied text. | §3.1; Table 1 caption |
| Strong recognition can coexist with poor uncertainty communication | Claude Code reaches **90.0% Identify F1 on BrowseComp**; the authors report that its incorrect final answers often omit unresolved uncertainty. | §3.2 |
| Accuracy and follow-up/escalation are negatively associated across cells | Figure 3’s fitted curves decrease from roughly **45% to under 10% Solve**, and **38% to under 10% Escalate**, from low to high accuracy. These are fitted trends, not individual-agent comparisons; Identify/Solve are restricted to incorrect answers in this figure. | Fig. 3; §3.2 |
| Conflict-relevant information appears early | Tracked answer mentions peak in the **first 10% of execution**, pooled across agent–dataset cells. Mentions are a proxy for exposure, not necessarily explicit conflict recognition. | Fig. 4; §3.3 |
| Prompting can increase escalation while reducing performance | On MoNaCo, OpenHands/GPT-5 Escalate increases from **1.6% to 60.7%**, while official aggregate accuracy decreases from **30.1% to 23.2%** under the humility prompt. Escalate is conditional on incorrect conflict answers; MoNaCo accuracy combines scalar similarity and string correctness. | §§2.3, 3.4; Tables 8–9 captions |
| Judges agree strongly with one another, less strongly with humans | Cross-model Gwet’s AC1 is **0.82–0.92** for Identify/Escalate and **0.93–0.97** for correctness. On **50 Identify turns and 50 Escalate answers**, GPT-5 agrees with human-majority labels on **70.0% and 72.0%**, respectively. | §2.3; Apps. D.1–D.2 |

These results support separating correctness from observable uncertainty handling. They do not establish that improving accuracy causes lower humility, identify why uncertainty disappears, or show that the prompt trade-off is statistically significant: reported significance tests compare conflict with control, not prompted with default runs (§3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** Backbone and harness effects are coupled; a full factorial comparison was not run. ISE covers knowledge-conflict behavior rather than all humility, honesty, or calibration. Only a prompt intervention was tested; harness and training interventions remain untested (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- Natural-conflict/control comparisons are confounded by different questions and potentially different difficulty (§2.2). Backbone-specific filtering also changes evaluation populations across configurations (App. A.4).
- Solve records behavioral effort, not whether the tool call resolves the conflict. Escalate treats incorrectness as an unresolved gap, which is an imperfect proxy (§2.2).
- Metric descriptions are inconsistent: App. A.3 suggests broader uncertainty criteria, while Figure 8 rejects generic hedges and abstention. Figure 3 also uses error-conditioned metrics rather than the headline population.
- App. D.1’s reported negative-label counts contradict its interpretation that alternate judges are stricter and would yield lower EH rates. That directional robustness claim is therefore unclear.
- Figure 4’s caption mixes “first mention” with counts of instance–step pairs, leaving the exact aggregation ambiguous.

## Open research questions
- **Which component loses the conflict signal?** Backbone and harness effects remain coupled. Run the same backbone across multiple harnesses on identical conflict instances and trace persistence of recognized conflicts.
- **Can escalation improve without sacrificing accuracy?** Only one prompt intervention was tested. Compare that prompt with an explicit escalation action under fixed tasks and tool budgets, testing intervention differences directly.
- **Does ISE generalize beyond contradictory evidence?** Unanswerable tasks, ambiguous instructions, and competence limits are outside scope. Construct labeled evaluations for these settings and validate the behavioral criteria with humans.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central qualitative findings, but lacks table bodies, per-cell sample sizes, numerical confidence intervals, complete figure data, and detailed run configurations. Internal inconsistencies in metric descriptions and judge-agreement interpretation limit confidence in finer comparisons; the reported asset release is not independently verified.
