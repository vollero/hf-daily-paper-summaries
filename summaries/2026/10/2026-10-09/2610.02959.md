# TerraVis: Towards Evaluation of World-Grounded Visual Consistency in Text-to-Image Generation via MLLM Workflows

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02959) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02959)


## One-sentence takeaway
TerraVis’s structured MLLM checks achieve the strongest pooled agreement with human world-consistency ratings among tested metrics on two benchmarks, while still missing many human-identified violations (Table 2; Appendix D.1).

## Problem and motivation
Generated images can look attractive and match their prompts while depicting impossible anatomy, object structures, interactions, or spatial relationships. The authors argue that existing quality, alignment, and preference metrics do not directly measure these failures, motivating a separate, prompt-independent evaluation of visible real-world plausibility (§1; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evaluation definition and taxonomy:** World consistency targets perceptible real-world constraints through 18 violation types: seven object, eight interaction, and three scene types. Eligible stylized depictions are included; several nonrepresentational or specialized content categories are excluded (§§3.1–3.2; Appendix A).
- **Structured scoring workflow:** An MLLM checks eligibility, independently checks violation types, and classifies detected types as major or minor according to semantic centrality. The deterministic score is \(S=\exp[-\lambda(N_{\mathrm{maj}}+\alpha N_{\mathrm{min}})]\), with defaults \(\lambda=1,\alpha=0.5\); ineligible images receive N/A (§§3.3–3.4; Algorithm 1).
- **Validation and diagnosis:** The authors compare metrics across five generators and two prompt benchmarks, collect 13,500 human annotations, and examine detection, evaluator choice, taxonomy ablations, aggregation, and efficiency. They report publicly available [code](https://github.com/ShyFoo/TerraVis); release of images or annotations is not established in the supplied text (Abstract; §4; Appendix C).

The evidence supports improved pooled agreement under the authors’ criteria, rather than universal superiority: RAHF exceeds TerraVis for several individual generators, and TerraVis-H slightly exceeds it for SD 3.5-Large on COCO-T2I (Table 2). Detection recall remains incomplete. The aggregation ablation supports improved Pearson agreement from exponential scoring, without a corresponding rank-correlation gain. These results do not establish objective physical correctness or improved generation quality.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** Multi-stage evaluation costs more than single-pass metrics; detection produces false positives and misses; the taxonomy may be incomplete; and ambiguous violations and severity judgments introduce annotator uncertainty (§4.6; Figure 4).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- **Shared criteria constrain validation:** Human raters receive the taxonomy and related severity instructions. Correlation therefore measures agreement with this operational definition, rather than an independent definition of plausibility (Appendix C).
- **Prompt composition can influence generator rankings:** Fantasy and anthropomorphic content count as violations, and GenAI-Bench contains prompts requesting such content. Scores can therefore penalize successful compliance with intentionally implausible prompts (Appendix A; Appendix D.6).
- **Some phenomena are weakly exercised:** The authors attribute low shares of several physical-interaction violations partly to rare relevant prompts; absence of detections does not establish mastery (Appendix D.6).
- **Reporting contains unresolved inconsistencies:** Overall evaluator-ranking agreement is reported as **0.62 / 0.64** in §4.2 but **0.64 / 0.68** in Table A4 and Appendix D.4. Appendix B also omits the promised full prompt templates from the retrieved text.
- **Uncertainty is incompletely quantified:** Confidence intervals and significance tests for metric differences are unavailable; stylized-subset sizes are also missing.

## Open research questions

- **Can recall improve without excessive false positives?** Recall remains low against human majority labels (Appendix D.1). A next experiment could compare representative in-context examples and targeted image crops on a held-out, expert-labeled violation set.
- **How much do prompt intent and scene complexity determine rankings?** Fantasy prompts affect violation prevalence, and simpler FLUX.2-dev scenes are offered as a possible explanation for its ranking (§4.3; Appendix D.6). A next experiment could stratify results by intended realism and match scene complexity across generators.
- **How broadly does style tolerance generalize?** The stylized correlation is promising, but sample sizes and style-specific results are absent (§4.2). A next experiment could evaluate balanced cartoon, sketch, and photographic subsets with expert annotations.
- **Can fewer checks preserve accuracy?** Full coverage helps, but incurs substantial computation (Table 4; Appendix D.5). A next experiment could test adaptive check selection and measure correlation, detection recall, and throughput against the full workflow.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and central comparisons, but leaves material gaps: full prompt templates, annotation-score aggregation details, correlation uncertainty, stylized-subset counts, exact generation configurations, API cost and latency, and image/annotation release status. The conflicting evaluator-ranking summaries remain unresolved.
