# OmniCapBench: A Deep-Structured Evaluation Framework for Fine-Grained Audio-Visual Captioning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12458) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12458)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmniCapBench evaluates native audio–visual caption graphs with structural checks and localized semantic judging, exposing identity-tracking and cross-modal association failures that the authors report holistic caption scores obscure.

## Problem and motivation

Whole-caption scores provide broad coverage but hide local errors; sparse question-based probes localize errors but miss content. The authors argue that both approaches also burden LLM judges with extracting facts, resolving identities, and aligning time from dense prose, making evaluation unstable (§1; Figure 1).

The identified gap is joint design of explicit evaluation units and reliable scoring operators for temporal grounding, identity persistence, and audio–visual association.

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured evaluation:** Models directly generate JSON tracks for persistent **References**, timestamped audio **Events**, and visual **Shots**, with explicit cross-links. The representation builds on MTSS, extending it with finer subshots and continuous time ranges (§3.1).
- **Separated scoring:** Structural validation precedes alignment and bounded semantic judging. Matching combines temporal rules, dialogue matching, and local LLM-assisted comparisons; bidirectional precision and recall distinguish omissions from unsupported descriptions (§3.2; Appendix A.2.2).
- **Benchmark and audit:** A densely annotated video benchmark is constructed through multi-model drafting, invariant-structure refinement, programmatic validation, and human review. Experiments diagnose model failures and compare structured evaluation with holistic judging and post-hoc parsing (§§3.1, 4; Appendices B.6–B.7).

These results support the diagnostic usefulness of separating structure from description. They do not establish universal judge independence, unrestricted video coverage, or architectural causes of model failures. The selected apparent-tie subset demonstrates a failure case for global scores, not its prevalence on unselected videos.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:** Evaluation covers annotated facts rather than every true detail; videos are bounded to five minutes; model-generated reference drafts may retain biases favoring related evaluated families; and strict JSON generation disadvantages prose-specialized captioners (Appendix C). Low scores therefore need not imply poor usefulness for conversational summaries (Appendix D).

**Analyst inferences from supplied evidence:**

- Comparisons mix perception with schema following and inference resources: models use different frame caps, duration coverage, generation strategies, and token budgets (Appendices A.1.2–A.2.3).
- Reproducibility is ambiguous: the universal output limit in A.1.2 differs from model-specific limits in Table A.7; model counts and membership also differ across §4.1, A.2.3, and the displayed configuration table.
- The open-world policy says unsupported extras do not directly reduce main precision, complicating the broader claim that precision strictly penalizes hallucinations; the missing Table A.6 body prevents resolving the distinction.
- Figure 4(b) normalizes metric deficits into a failure composition. Those shares should not be interpreted as independently counted error frequencies.
- The supplied evidence does not quantify residual localized-judge sensitivity or independently measured annotation agreement. Claims of objective scoring and flawless references remain stronger than what can be verified here.

## Open research questions

- **How stable are local matching and semantic scores across judges?** Baseline judge sensitivity is reported, but corresponding detailed local-judge comparisons are unavailable. Next experiment: swap matchers and judges on fixed predictions and measure score, ranking, and human-agreement changes.
- **How much of the model gap reflects schema following or inference resources?** Specialized models struggle with the interface, and settings differ. Next experiment: compare native and fixed-parser routes under matched duration, frame, and output budgets.
- **Does reference drafting favor related model families?** Authors explicitly acknowledge this risk. Next experiment: independently annotate a subset without model drafts and compare family-specific score changes.
- **Do identity and association deficits persist beyond the benchmark’s duration range?** Longer videos are outside scope. Next experiment: extend duration buckets while controlling shot and event density.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The framework and reported diagnostic patterns are clear, but the source was truncated, so this analysis does not provide complete paper coverage.

Material missing information includes most numerical table bodies, complete metric formulas and scoring-policy details, full prompts, human-audit sampling and agreement statistics, uncertainty estimates, and the omitted construction/experimental text. Reported asset availability was not verified.
