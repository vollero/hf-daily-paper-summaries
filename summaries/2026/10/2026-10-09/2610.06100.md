# From Traces to Agentic Worlds: Agentic Language World Models for Interactive Environment Simulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06100) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06100)


## One-sentence takeaway
Trace2Env uses trace-derived environment knowledge, an inspecting world-model agent, and persistent episode state to improve judged next-observation fidelity and real-environment replay success over direct prompting in the evaluated settings.

## Problem and motivation
Environment replicas support agent training and evaluation, but original code, infrastructure, or hidden state may be unavailable. Historical interaction traces provide behavioral evidence without enough information to rebuild the executable system. The authors identify a further gap in conventional language-world-model prompting: flattened knowledge and history can fail to preserve state changes whose consequences emerge later (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Trace-derived worldbook:** A learning-free construction pipeline produces action/state schemas, preserved transition evidence, induced rules and response contracts, and provenance. This supplies a reusable behavioral specification rather than reconstructing the original implementation (§4.1; Appendix C.1–C.2).
- **Agent-operated simulation:** A dedicated world-model agent selectively inspects knowledge, state, and memory, then proposes an observation and typed state changes. Applicability checks separate transferable behavior from facts belonging to other episodes; a harness validates and commits effects (§4.3).
- **Persistent episode workspace:** Immutable environment knowledge is separated from mutable episode facts and interaction memory. Missing state entries mean unknown, not absent (§4.2).
- **Complementary evaluation:** The paper tests both single-step prediction and whether actions induced by simulated interaction succeed when replayed in the real environment (§5).

These are the authors’ proposed contributions; the supplied text does not independently establish priority over all related methods.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher average prediction fidelity | Across seven environments, five judge dimensions averaged on a 0–100 scale: GPT Trace2Env **75.94** versus direct prompting **69.51**; DeepSeek **75.75** versus **68.71**. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Gains over direct prompting have statistical support | Trajectory-clustered 95% confidence intervals exclude zero in all four AgentWorldBench environments for both backbones. | Table 7; Appendix B.2 |
| Stronger ALFWorld replay transfer | On 100 tasks, direct prompting versus Trace2Env: real success **93% / 93%**, simulated success **97% / 88%**, replay success **3% / 85%**, CR **0.032 / 0.914**. | Tables 2, 4 |
| Stronger SciWorld replay transfer | On 40 tasks, direct prompting versus Trace2Env: real success **85% / 85%**, simulated success **92.5% / 87.5%**, replay success **45% / 60%**, CR **0.529 / 0.706**. | Tables 2, 4 |
| Evidence contributes more clearly than abstraction alone | Terminal/GPT ablation: adding evidence to schemas gives paired **+2.45 ± 0.89 SE**; adding abstraction alone gives **−1.12 ± 0.90**; abstraction added after evidence gives **+0.69 ± 0.63**. | Tables 3, 9 |
| Agentic operation adds cost | Terminal/GPT ablation run: full Trace2Env scores **64.89**, using **5.0 model calls**, **$0.187**, and **38.1 seconds** per observation; direct prompting scores **55.76**, using **1.0 call**, approximately **$0.04**, and **26.0 seconds**. | Table 10 |

The results support improved prediction relative to direct prompting and substantially better replay transfer in the two text games. They do not establish universal superiority over retrieval or static-worldbook alternatives: Table 1 contains exceptions, and several paired comparisons have confidence intervals spanning zero. Single-step evaluation uses real observed prefixes; it therefore does not measure accumulated simulation error. Training benefits are proposed, not demonstrated.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Fidelity is bounded by trace coverage, and backbones differ in how consistently they use reconstructed evidence (§7).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** Long-horizon evaluation compares only the full system with direct prompting. State and adaptive-loop ablations are confined to single-step Terminal evaluation, leaving their individual replay contributions unresolved (Tables 2, 9).
- **Analyst inference—validation is incomplete:** The reference policy retains the proposed observation when rejecting its state effects. Schema-valid model-inferred effects are also accepted without requiring an executable rule; validation therefore does not guarantee factual correctness (Appendix C.3; Table 13).
- **Analyst inference—measurement and coverage:** Prediction relies on an LLM judge, while multi-turn evaluation covers only two text games and reports no replay confidence intervals. Several construction sets retain only successful trajectories (§5.1; Appendix A).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Terminal full-system scores differ between the main results and ablation runs; Table 7’s DeepSeek Terminal paired comparison with static worldbook prompting differs from the aggregate-score difference in Table 1. These discrepancies are not explained in the supplied text. Some appendix prompt bodies are explicitly abbreviated.

## Open research questions

1. **Which mechanisms drive replay transfer?** Table 2 does not isolate state, inspection, or applicability checks. Next experiment: ablate each mechanism during multi-turn ALFWorld and SciWorld interaction and measure W2R with uncertainty intervals.
2. **How robust is simulation beyond trace coverage?** Coverage affects transfer, including weaker results for uncovered Android apps (Table 8). Next experiment: hold out action families or apps and compare successful-only construction traces with mixed success/failure traces.
3. **Can stricter validation improve fidelity?** Observation-only fallback leaves observation correctness unchecked (Appendix C.3). Next experiment: compare reference fallback, strict rejection, and observation/state consistency checking on replay success and refusal frequency.
4. **Do simulated environments improve agent training?** The paper leaves this to future work (§7). Next experiment: train agents on Trace2Env interactions and evaluate held-out real-environment success against matched training budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial method detail and quantitative evidence, but some reporting inconsistencies remain.

Material missing information includes multi-turn task-agent configuration, replay uncertainty estimates, multi-turn component ablations, numerical one-time construction costs, explanations for differing reported runs and paired scores, and verified repository asset availability.
