# Synthesis Through Simulation: Generating Coherent Enterprise Data via Scalable Agent-System Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10549) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10549)


## One-sentence takeaway

STS generates enterprise database snapshots through policy-enforcing APIs, achieving full validation compliance in ten simulated environments while leaving distributional realism—especially child-record frequencies—as a separate, imperfectly solved problem (§§3.2, 5.2).

## Problem and motivation

Enterprise tool-calling agents need realistic database states for training and evaluation, but production data and schemas can be restricted. The authors identify a gap between statistical synthesizers, which require seed data and can violate business rules, and scripted generators, which require domain-specific authoring and do not necessarily reproduce realistic distributions (§1).

The target is relational coherence under state-dependent business policies, alongside realistic values and scalable population across domains.

## Approach and contributions

- **Problem decomposition:** Separate structural validity, distributional fidelity, and scalability. The validity proof assumes a valid initial state and API preconditions that preserve every checked invariant (§3).
- **STS generation paradigm:** Populate simulated databases through APIs that reject invalid writes, delegating validity enforcement to the environment rather than the generator (§4.1).
- **Generalist Populator:** A schema-inaccessible LLM agent uses Explore, Plan, and Execute phases. A persistent manifest stores inferred dependencies, operation weights, task history, and execution heuristics; entity identifiers are resolved from live state during execution (§4.2).
- **Research assets:** The authors report releasing the framework, ten simulated enterprise environments, and generated datasets (Abstract). Release contents are not independently verified here.

“Zero-authoring” describes GP’s per-environment configuration requirements; constructing complete policy-enforcing environments can still require substantial engineering (§6).

## Evidence and main results

MDF measures marginal fidelity, PDF within-table pairwise fidelity, and CDF child-count fidelity. Higher scores indicate closer agreement with registered reference distributions (§5.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Checked structural validity is preserved | GP achieved VPR = 1.0 in all ten environments, with K = 3 exploration trajectories and N = 100 trajectories per environment. The guarantee assumes valid seeds and invariant-preserving APIs. | §3.2, Proposition 1; §5.2, Table 2 caption |
| Fidelity varies by granularity | Under that configuration, airline MDF/PDF/CDF were 0.973/0.900/0.949; Forum CDF was 0.318 and Banking CDF 0.421. Authors attribute the latter gaps to task-frequency imbalance. | §5.2 |
| Cold-start coverage exceeds tested statistical methods | Seven environments had no seed rows, making the statistical baselines inapplicable. On the three seeded environments, GP had VPR = 1.0 versus a reported offline maximum of 0.923; GP led fidelity on airline but was slightly below on retail and banking. Some banking baseline VPRs were excluded as artefacts. | §5.3, Figure 3; Appendix J |
| EnvScaler comparison favors GP on airline | With N = 100, EnvScaler produced zero writes in 82% of airline trajectories, attributed to stale task identifiers. On cloud, EnvScaler CDF exceeded GP: 0.649 versus 0.610. | §5.4, Table 3; Appendix K |
| Exploration and planning affect fidelity | Airline PDF increased from 0.813 at K = 0 to 0.900 at K = 3, N = 100. Removing Plan at K = 3, N = 50 reduced MDF/PDF/CDF by 0.065/0.062/0.071; round-trip bookings fell from 52.5% to 7.9%, against a 55% reference. | §5.5; Appendices G–H |
| Heuristics reduce execution overhead | At K = 3, N = 100, removing heuristics reduced Banking successful writes per trajectory by 33% and increased airline calls per successful write by 96%. | §5.5; Appendix G |
| Downstream training shows a limited utility signal | Qwen2.5-7B trained on 19 STS-derived samples reportedly exceeded the base model and an 18-sample seed-trained model on all five airline metrics. Pass@k gains were +0.214/+0.125 and PPT avg_max gains +0.161/+0.077, respectively. | §5.6, Table 4 discussion |
| Generation is relatively slow | Across ten environments, K = 3, N = 100, median latency ranged from 41 to 118 seconds per trajectory; effective throughput ranged from 0.82 to 5.10 successful writes/minute. | Appendix I, Table 15 |

These results support feasibility in the supplied mock environments and show that validity and realism differ. VPR does not establish successful task completion or broad distributional realism. The downstream experiment suggests utility, but does not isolate state diversity as the causal mechanism, despite the authors’ stronger interpretation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Fidelity depends on model quality and unanchored LLM priors; validity requires complete API enforcement. Unstructured content and probabilistic enforcement are outside scope (§6).
- **Observed:** Task-frequency miscalibration limits CDF, and complex prerequisites cause persistent zero-write trajectories (§5.2; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation coverage:** Fidelity covers only registered fields with available reference priors. High MDF can coexist with categorical collapse, as Appendix C.1 explicitly describes; it does not establish whole-database diversity.
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Statistical baselines train on very small seed snapshots and use differing generation budgets. Their observed validity maximum is not evidence of a universal ceiling. EnvScaler’s stale-ID failures also do not isolate schema access itself as harmful (§§5.3–5.4; Appendices F, J–K).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Synthesis uses one LLM and mock systems; downstream evaluation uses one domain and small, unequal training sets. Bootstrap intervals describe field/row variation rather than repeated-run variability (Appendices C and F; §5.6).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** §5.2 calls HR and Banking the highest-write environments, while Table 15 reports higher successful writes per trajectory for Retail.

## Open research questions

1. **Can lightweight profiling correct operational-frequency skew?** Forum and Banking have low CDF attributed to task ratios (§5.2). Compare inferred weights with seed-profiled or explicitly supplied frequency priors at matched budgets.
2. **How robust is validity to incomplete enforcement?** The guarantee assumes all relevant invariants are preserved (§6). Deliberately omit selected API checks and evaluate snapshots with an independent validation suite.
3. **Does GP generalize across models and domains?** Only one synthesis model is reported, and heuristic transfer remains future work (§6; Appendix F). Test multiple models on held-out environments, with and without transferred heuristics.
4. **Does snapshot diversity cause downstream gains?** The small airline experiment does not isolate this mechanism (§5.6). Match training-set size and rollout quality, vary snapshot diversity, and repeat training across seeds and domains.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the mechanism and reported findings, but most tables contain captions without their numerical bodies.

Material missing information includes complete fidelity scores and confidence intervals, registered fields and exact prior sources, generated-record counts, absolute downstream scores and pass@k configuration, downstream fine-tuning hyperparameters, repeated-run variability, and full prompts/tool implementations.
