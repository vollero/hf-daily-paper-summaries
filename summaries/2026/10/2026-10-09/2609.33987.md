# Opera: A Verbal Critic Framework for Long-horizon Coding Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33987) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33987)


## One-sentence takeaway
Opera improves long-horizon coding agents by auditing corrective feedback and tracking each issue until resolution, with higher mean resolve rates than adapted critic baselines and promising results from fine-tuning on critic-guided student trajectories (Tables 1, 3, 5).

## Problem and motivation
Coding agents can repeat failed actions, edit the wrong code, or declare completion without adequate verification. Critics must distinguish genuine errors from reasonable unfinished work, and their feedback can disrupt otherwise successful trajectories. The authors identify a gap in prior approaches: they typically generate feedback without tracking whether subsequent actions actually resolve the diagnosed problem (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent corrective notes:** Opera maintains at most one open issue, with a fixed resolution criterion. Guidance can change as evidence accumulates; adherence and actual resolution are tracked separately (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Scheduled, typed, audited intervention:** Periodic and event-triggered reviews select one of nine repair operators or remain silent. Admission audits screen proposed feedback; release audits require visible evidence before closing a note. A proxy integrates this process through execution traces and natural-language messages without changing policy weights (§3; Appendix A).
- **Training recipe:** Successful critic-guided student trajectories become supervised fine-tuning data. Critic messages are removed, and delivered diagnoses are rewritten into the student’s reasoning targets. The authors call these trajectories “approximately on-policy” because the student generated the actions (§4.3; Appendix B.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest mean resolve rate among tested critic baselines | With Qwen3.8-27B policy and default GPT-5.6-Sol critic: Opera achieves **73.8±2.3%, 79.3±3.5%, 41.3±3.1%**, versus no critic at **65.9±1.7%, 75.3±1.2%, 32.4±1.0%**, on Terminal-Bench 2.1, SWE-Pro subset, and DeepSWE respectively. Margins over the strongest competing baseline are **1.9, 0.6, and 1.8 percentage points**. | §4.2, Table 1 |
| Large gains depend on policy capability | With GPT-5.6-Sol critic, reported Qwen3.5-9B gains are **12.4 pp** on Terminal-Bench and **15.0 pp** on SWE-Pro; it solves no DeepSWE task with or without Opera. | §4.2, Figure 4 |
| Audits contribute to performance | Removing admission and release audits together lowers Qwen3.8-27B resolve rates from **73.8 to 68.5%**, **79.3 to 78.3%**, and **41.3 to 36.9%** across the same three benchmarks; means over three runs. | §6, Table 5 |
| Restricted operator sets retain substantial gains | On Terminal-Bench with Qwen3.8-27B, full Opera achieves **73.8±2.3%**, Edit-only **71.9±1.6%**, and Non-edit **70.2±0.8%**, versus **65.9±1.7%** without a critic; three runs. Full Opera has the highest mean on all three benchmarks. | §5.2, Table 4 |
| Critic-guided SFT improves held-out repositories without observed Terminal-Bench degradation | Critic-free Qwen3.5-9B evaluation: Opera-guided SFT reaches **38.9±2.4%** on 108 held-out SWE-Pro tasks versus base **28.7±1.9%** and teacher-rollout SFT **39.5±1.4%**. On Terminal-Bench, corresponding rates are **25.8±2.2%, 23.6±1.9%, and 7.9±1.9%**; three runs. | §4.3, Tables 2–3 |
| Self-critique can help, but adds compute | On DeepSWE, self-critique improves Qwen3.8-27B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash by **6.2 pp each** over their no-critic policies. The authors explicitly state that inference compute is not matched. | §5.3, Figure 7 |

The evidence supports improved mean performance in the evaluated settings and an aggregate benefit from audits. It does not establish statistically significant superiority over every baseline, isolate every framework component, or prove that approximately on-policy data causes the observed transfer advantage. Operator and note-closure analyses describe associations rather than isolated causal effects (Figures 10–11).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Training uses one student model and one out-of-domain benchmark; larger datasets, iterative training, and reinforcement learning remain future work (§7).
- **Author-stated intervention risk:** Opera sometimes regresses on tasks the policy otherwise solves. Rescue and regression rates use different denominators and should not be directly subtracted (§5.1, Figure 6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Baselines are adapted into Opera’s execution setting. SWE-Search lacks its original search tree, and outcome verifiers become in-flight checks; results therefore compare these adaptations rather than complete original systems (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Admission and release audits are removed together. Separate effects of these audits, persistent tracking, and review scheduling are not established by the reported ablations (§§5.2, 6).
- **Analyst inference—training confounds:** Both sources cover the same selected tasks, but differ in sample counts, supervised tokens, effective batch sizes, and optimizer steps. Diagnosis rewriting also changes targets, complicating attribution solely to trajectory provenance (Appendix B; Table 6).
- **Analyst inference—transfer interpretation:** Terminal-Bench changes both task format and harness, so Table 3 does not isolate harness robustness (§4.3).
- **Evidence inconsistency:** Figure 4’s displayed Qwen3.5-9B bar values do not cleanly reconcile with its annotated gains. The stated gains agree with the prose, but exact underlying values remain ambiguous.

## Open research questions
- **Which lifecycle components drive the gains?** Existing ablations combine audits and retain most other machinery. Next experiment: independently remove admission audit, release audit, persistence, and event triggers under matched budgets.
- **Does Opera outperform equivalent extra computation?** Self-critique is not compute-matched (§5.3). Next experiment: compare Opera with additional policy reflection or retries at equal token and time budgets.
- **What causes better transfer after SFT?** Trajectory source, diagnosis rewriting, and training exposure differ (Appendix B). Next experiment: match supervised tokens and optimization settings, then ablate rewritten diagnoses.
- **Can intervention regressions be reduced?** Stronger policies show both rescues and disruptions (§5.1). Next experiment: vary evidence requirements for admitting notes and measure resolve rate, rescue, regression, and compute jointly.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes methods, appendices, and substantial quantitative evidence, but causal attribution and small baseline margins remain uncertain.

Material information unavailable includes statistical significance tests, compute and latency costs, independent lifecycle-component ablations, the precise corrected SWE-Pro task list, and verified contents of the claimed code release. Exact Claude critic identity and the inconsistent Figure 4 values are also unclear.
