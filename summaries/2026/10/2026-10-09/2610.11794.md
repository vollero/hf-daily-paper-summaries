# Memento 3: Model-Based Recursive Self-Improvement through Reflective Rulebooks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11794) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11794)


## One-sentence takeaway

Memento 3 uses a frozen LLM to revise natural-language rulebooks and executable world models, reporting complete public ARC-AGI-3 performance with fewer actions than humans, while evidence for robustness and population benefits remains limited.

## Problem and motivation

Agents in unfamiliar environments must infer action semantics, dynamics, and goals from interaction. Several executable models can reproduce the same observations yet disagree on unseen states; exact replay therefore cannot guarantee correct generalisation. The authors identify a need for explicit, revisable hypotheses that guide both planning and further evidence collection (§1).

Their proposed improvement occurs in external world-model memory while LLM parameters remain fixed, extending the Memento series from policy and procedural memory to environment modelling (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Persistent rulebook–code pairing:** A natural-language rulebook records environment hypotheses and unresolved mechanics; executable code implements those hypotheses for prediction and planning. Reflection distinguishes incorrect rules from implementation errors (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Continual verification loop:** Observation, reflection, rule revision, compilation, and verification update the model throughout interaction. ARC updates require an LLM assessment of code–rulebook fidelity and cell-exact replay of recorded transitions. Git preserves earlier hypotheses and implementations (§§2.2, 3.2–3.4).
- **Population extension:** Multiple rulebook–code pairs share interaction history. Planning samples uniformly among distinct nonempty action sequences, and new evidence checks every member (§2.4).
- **Evaluation:** Public ARC-AGI-3 experiments include a rulebook ablation and a population comparison; Pong tests whether learned executable dynamics support feedback control (§§4–5).

The authors acknowledge that language rules, executable models, reflection, and replay are established components. Their contribution is their organisation around a persistent environment specification with separate semantic revision and implementation repair (§6).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Complete public ARC performance | Single-model agent clears all 183 levels across 25 games; mean RHAE 100.0 versus baseline1’s 99.0, with both clearing every game. Backbone: Claude Opus 5, extra-high reasoning effort. | §4, Table 1 |
| Lower action cost among complete solutions | 7,518 environment actions versus 17,135 human actions, reported as 44%; baseline1 uses 8,347 actions. Failed attempts and resets count. | §4, Table 2 discussion |
| Rulebook improves reported convergence | On ft09, ka59, and cn04, rulebook runs use 617 versus 677 actions and 678 versus 830 agent turns: reported reductions of 9% and 18%. Both variants clear every level with RHAE 100. | §4, ablation; Table 3 discussion |
| Population improves one reported trajectory | On wa30, two models use 597 versus 899 scored actions for one model; both clear all nine levels with RHAE 100 after live-server replay. | §4, population; Table 4 discussion |
| Learned controller succeeds in Pong | Fixed controller wins 21:0 in three episodes with different openings, without evaluation-time LLM calls; learning costs 9,504 emulator frames under ALE frame-skip 4. | §5, result; Figure 8 |
| Direct-action references perform worse in shown Pong episodes | Same-backbone LLM without a world model scores −19; shown random-policy episode scores −20. The LLM baseline also lacks persistent memory. | §5, baselines; Figure 8 |

These are author-reported results. They support successful external model adaptation and action-efficient performance in the evaluated settings. They do not establish held-out generalisation, repeated-run stability, or broad capability improvement. ARC action scoring excludes model construction, simulation, and replay; Pong’s published RL baselines use their respective evaluation protocols (§§3.1, 5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ qualifications:** Replay consistency cannot resolve unseen-state ambiguity, and a model can remain self-confirming. Rulebook fidelity is judged by the LLM; population hypotheses do not constitute a calibrated Bayesian posterior (§§2.2–2.4, 6).
- **Authors’ evaluation caveat:** Public ARC saturation cannot isolate architectural contributions; model choice, reasoning budget, action efficiency, stability, and held-out evaluation matter (§6).
- **Analyst inference—limited component evidence:** The rulebook ablation covers three games, and the population comparison covers one game. No repeated-run distributions are supplied, so the claimed reduction in sensitivity to initial hypotheses remains unmeasured (§4).
- **Analyst inference—cost comparison limits:** Environment-action efficiency omits substantial LLM computation. The authors mention time and monetary expense but do not quantify total costs (§4).
- **Analyst inference—Pong validity:** Learning stops upon achieving a 21:0 checkpoint, followed by three reported evaluation openings. Broader opening robustness remains unknown. Pong also retains full-frame rendering and paddle-motion errors, weakening the exact-replay guarantee used for ARC (§5).
- **Analyst inference—baseline attribution:** The direct Pong LLM baseline removes both the world model and persistent memory, so its comparison cannot isolate the world model’s contribution (§5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the rulebook benefit persist across tasks and stochastic runs?** Three selected games leave effect consistency uncertain. Next experiment: paired repeated runs across public and held-out games, reporting completion, actions, turns, and uncertainty.
2. **Do populations reliably reduce variance?** One two-model wa30 comparison establishes a trajectory improvement, not variance reduction. Next experiment: repeated runs with several population sizes under matched computation budgets.
3. **How robust is replay-consistent modelling beyond observed histories?** The paper explicitly identifies self-confirming hypotheses. Next experiment: test accepted models on withheld transitions and targeted actions that distinguish competing rules.
4. **Does Pong control generalise beyond the evaluated openings?** Three episodes and residual model errors leave coverage unclear. Next experiment: evaluate the fixed controller over a prespecified, larger opening set without further revision.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are clearly described, but causal and robustness conclusions have narrower support.

Material unavailable information includes the bodies of Tables 2–5, repeated-run counts and uncertainty estimates, held-out evaluations, full prompts and implementation details, quantified LLM/runtime costs, detailed Pong opening protocols, and confirmation of publicly released code or learned artefacts.
