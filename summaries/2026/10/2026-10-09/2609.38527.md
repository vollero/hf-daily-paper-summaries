# Behavioral Persistence and Incomplete Functional Transfer of Co-evolved Communication in Evolutionary Robotics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38527) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38527)


## One-sentence takeaway
Transferring one pair of co-evolved controllers from 2D to 3D simulation preserved some behavioral responses but yielded no joint foraging successes, even after input calibration and explicit sender-direction injection (§§4–7).

## Problem and motivation
The paper asks whether an evolved communication protocol remains useful when its controllers move to a simulator with different contact, friction, and motion dynamics without weight retraining. The identified gap is that transferring coordinated signaling requires preserving both signal interpretation and the receiver’s ability to act on it, whereas much prior transfer work addresses individual controllers (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Transfer evaluation:** Move previously co-evolved controllers from Pygame to PyBullet, using two differential-drive e-puck-like robots, RES-GRU networks, and a stuck-escape mechanism. Evaluate baseline and intervention conditions on matched seeds (§3).
- **Translation and weight analysis:** Correct turning and social-input filtering, then recalibrate Agent B’s hunger input using an observed residual-weight asymmetry; network weights remain unchanged (§3.5).
- **Directional intervention:** During qualifying signaling events, replace food bearing with sender bearing to test whether additional directional information restores performance (§3.6).
- **Interpretive contribution:** Propose “implicit spatial coding”—communication meaning partly depends on the environment—as a working hypothesis, not an established mechanism (§§5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| No joint success after calibrated baseline transfer | Across 30 seeds, A reached within 0.08 m of food in 1 run; B in 0. Joint success requires both agents reaching food, implying 0/30 joint successes. | §§3.7, 4.1; Table 1 |
| Different peak hunger-input values | Baseline magnet_max mean ± SD: A 0.820 ± 0.106; B 0.583 ± 0.110. This is maximum `exp(-distance/hunger_decay)`, with B’s decay calibrated to 1.25. | §§3.5, 3.7, 4.1 |
| B showed more oscillation descriptively | Baseline mean saw_flip_rate: A 0.041, B 0.093; SDs 0.017 and 0.116. Mean saw_cycle_rate: A 0.020, B 0.043. | §4.1, p.8 |
| Residual weights were asymmetric | Author reports B’s obstacle-cue-to-hunger weight relationship as approximately 1.78 times A’s, estimated using residual-weight norms. | §3.5; §5 |
| Sender-bearing injection did not restore success | On the same 30 seeds, B still never reached food; therefore no joint successes occurred. Individual magnet_max changes were mixed. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Intervention counts are internally inconsistent | §4.2 reports B changed in 11 seeds, with 7 increases; counting displayed Table 2 entries gives 12 changes, with 8 increases and 4 decreases. | §4.2; Table 2 |

The evidence establishes poor functional transfer for this controller pair and behavioral sensitivity to the directional intervention. It does **not** isolate navigation failure as the cause or prove implicit spatial coding. Also, different hunger-decay settings mean cross-agent magnet_max values are not a common calibrated distance scale (§§3.5, 3.7; analyst inference).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only one controller pair, one foraging task, and 30 fixed seeds were evaluated; analysis is descriptive rather than a comprehensive statistical characterization (§6).
- **Author-stated:** No matched evaluation of the original 2D controllers is supplied, preventing precise measurement of transfer degradation (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Detailed social-activation timing was not recorded for the bearing experiment; the proposed spatial-coding mechanism remains hypothetical (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Hunger calibration used the same evaluation seeds, leaving its generalization to unseen seeds untested (§§3.5, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied results lack separate ablations for each baseline correction and a communication-disabled control, limiting causal attribution (§§3.5, 4).
- **Reporting risk:** The intervention-count discrepancy above needs reconciliation before aggregate effects are reused (§4.2; Table 2).

## Open research questions
- **Does receiver navigation cause the failure?** Directional injection alone did not restore success. Retrain only B while freezing A, then compare success with and without signaling (§5).
- **Does communication retain a measurable causal benefit?** Signal timing and a disabled-channel comparison are missing. Log signaling events and compare normal, disabled, and shuffled signals on matched seeds (§6; proposed experiment).
- **Does the weight asymmetry generalize?** Only one pair was examined. Evaluate multiple independently co-evolved pairs in both simulators and test whether weight ratios predict transfer outcomes (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The observed failure is clear, but the mechanism is uncertain and intervention counts conflict.

Material missing information includes matched 2D performance, original evolution details, the maximum episode-step limit, exact social-filter and escape-layer parameters, detailed signal traces, independent calibration-validation seeds, and verification of the declared archived assets.
