# On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09639) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09639)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the tested settings, reverse-KL on-policy distillation transfers substantial compositional reasoning skill but limited new factual knowledge, while forward KL markedly improves factual transfer (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Higher reasoning scores can reflect newly memorized facts, better composition of existing facts, or both. The authors argue that conventional benchmarks cannot separate these effects, and pretrained models’ unknown training exposure makes capability transfer difficult to establish. This distinction matters when choosing distillation for factual updating versus reasoning improvement (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled capability separation:** Random lookup tables represent memorized associations; chains and branching trees exercise different ways of composing them. An initialized student knows initial tables and chains. Four teacher conditions independently add held-out facts, branching-tree skill, both, or neither (§3; Fig. 1).
- **Distillation recipe ablation:** A factorial comparison crosses student versus teacher prefixes with reverse versus forward KL, separating the effects of rollout source and loss direction (§4.2).
- **Validation and further analysis:** Recent factual QA and competition mathematics test the observed asymmetry outside synthetic tasks. Additional experiments compare hybrid objectives and examine execution traces and merged LoRA updates (§§5–6).

The contribution is an experimental framework and evidence about transfer, rather than a newly proposed distillation objective.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong transfer to unseen reasoning structures | Across four models, reverse-KL OPD from the skill teacher achieves **79.10% mean greedy final-answer accuracy**, versus **0.72%** with the matched baseline teacher, on seven-step unseen trees using initial facts. | §4.1; Fig. 2; App. B.3 |
| Factual transfer is limited, but nonzero | From the factual teacher, mean greedy accuracy is **13.12% versus 0.03%** for the matched control on held-out single-step facts; accuracy is **0.54%** on seven-step chains entirely using held-out facts. | §4.1; Fig. 2 |
| Additional sampling preserves the asymmetry | Mean final-answer **Pass@32** is **88.11%** for unseen trees after skill-teacher distillation, versus **15.74%** on held-out single-step facts and **2.58%** on held-out chains after factual-teacher distillation; sampling temperature is 1.0. | §4.1; Fig. 2; App. B.3 |
| Forward KL substantially improves factual recall | With student prefixes, held-out single-step greedy accuracy increases from **10.95% to 54.10%** in Qwen3-4B and **32.95% to 94.10%** in Gemma-2-2B when replacing reverse KL with forward KL. Teacher-prefix forward KL gives similar results. | §4.2; Fig. 3 |
| Student prefixes improve compositional execution likelihood | On Qwen3-4B unseen trees, reference-completion NLL falls from **2.40 to 1.77 bits per completion** under reverse KL and **2.13 to 1.15** under forward KL when replacing teacher prefixes with student prefixes. Greedy accuracy improves in three of four model/objective comparisons. | §4.2; Fig. 3 |
| Real-world results show the same direction | Qwen3-4B factual-teacher distillation changes Knowledge-2026 greedy accuracy from **11.25% to 9.64%** on 809 prompts also used for teacher training and distillation. Reasoning-teacher distillation raises Math-2026 accuracy from **17.41% to 23.88%**, a **6.47 percentage-point** gain on 201 held-out problems. | §5; Table 2; App. D |
| Objective choice matters beyond the KL endpoints | With Qwen3-4B student prefixes, ToDi achieves **64.3% Fact-Single accuracy** from the factual teacher and **86.4% Comp-Unseen accuracy** from the skill teacher, versus forward KL’s **54.1%** and **76.4%**, respectively. | §6.3; Table 4 |
| Factual and skill updates differ directionally | Cosine similarity between merged LoRA updates from factual and skill teachers ranges from **0.004 to 0.046** across three models and matched prefix/objective settings. | §6.2; Tables 3, 6 |

These results support a substantial transfer asymmetry under the tested reverse-KL recipe. They do **not** establish that OPD universally cannot acquire knowledge: factual gains are nonzero, architecture-dependent, and much larger under other objectives. The authors’ proposed KL mechanism is an interpretation of the ablation results; update geometry likewise does not establish causal localization of facts or skills.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Analyst inference—generality:** Random mappings and fixed seven-step tests provide strong exposure control but narrow coverage of natural knowledge and reasoning. Real-world validation uses one model (§5; App. B.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** All experiments use training seed 1; repeated-seed estimates, confidence intervals, and significance tests are not reported (App. B.5).
- **Disclosed evaluation boundary:** Knowledge-2026 measures recall on teacher-training and distillation prompts, whereas Math-2026 is held out. The two settings test different forms of transfer (§5).
- **Analyst inference—capability boundary:** Greedy accuracy and Pass@32 demonstrate improvement within those decoding budgets; they cannot exclude pre-existing ability at larger sampling budgets. Final-answer metrics also do not require a correct full trace (App. B.3).
- **Reporting caveats:** “Teacher-only” structures include the initialization chain, so that pool is not wholly novel to the student. Table 6 lists skill-update peak layers 3, 11, and 34, while Table 3 and the main discussion emphasize 3 and 11 (App. B.3; Tables 3, 6).
- **Reproducibility:** Hyperparameters are supplied, but public code, checkpoints, benchmark files, and exact real-world answer-scoring procedures are not established in the text.

## Open research questions
- **Does forward KL restore natural-language factual transfer?** The real-world study tests reverse-KL OPD only. Next experiment: repeat Knowledge-2026 with the prefix-by-KL factorial comparison and identical scoring (§5).
- **How robust is the asymmetry to training capacity and budget?** Results use one seed and rank-16 LoRA. Next experiment: vary seeds, adapter ranks, full-parameter training, and training duration (App. B.5).
- **Does acquired composition generalize to longer computations?** Synthetic evaluation fixes seven operations. Next experiment: test deeper trees and longer chains using the same learned tables (App. B.3).
- **Are the observed update directions causally specialized?** Weight similarities and depth profiles are observational. Next experiment: selectively remove or transplant layer-specific factual and skill updates, then reevaluate both capabilities (§6.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported experimental asymmetry; **medium** in broad claims about parametric knowledge or causal mechanisms.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, public artifact availability, exact natural-language scoring rules, detailed real-world teacher-training data and budgets, and natural-language forward-KL comparisons.
