# The Lattice of Transition Laws

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11216) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11216)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors unify autoregressive and diffusion decoding through a corruption lattice and predict many schedule rankings from discarded dependence, while showing that model errors and higher-order dependencies can reverse those predictions (§§2–7).

## Problem and motivation
Decoding order can strongly affect generation quality even with fixed weights and step budgets. Existing hybrids typically prescribe a schedule, leaving alternatives to be evaluated by decoding. The authors seek a principle that compares schedules beforehand across discrete tokens and continuous fields (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified representation:** Each coordinate has its own corruption level; decoding follows a monotone path toward clean data. Models whose architecture and training cover the relevant states can execute different paths with shared weights (§2).
- **Exact dependence cost:** The sum of conditional total correlations discarded by independent parallel updates equals the KL divergence between exact and factorized **path laws**; finished-sample divergence is bounded by this sum. This extends an acknowledged absorbing-token special case to continuous channels and intermediate levels (§3; Appendix A.2).
- **Graph-based step requirement:** Under graph-Markov and persistent path-dependence assumptions, the minimum number of zero-cost steps equals graph treedepth (§4).
- **Practical ranking:** Pairwise dependence kernels estimated from model weights provide a lower-bound-based schedule estimate, tested on text, image, and video generation (§§5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Dependence cost exactly characterizes path divergence | Exact joint transitions versus products of their own coordinate marginals; finished-sample KL is only upper-bounded. | §3, Proposition 1; Appendix C |
| Zero-cost schedules have a geometric step requirement | Under Theorem 1’s assumptions, a chain of length \(d\) needs \(\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil\) steps; grid requirements scale linearly with side length. | §4; Appendix C |
| Text schedule rankings largely follow estimates | At 8, 16, and 32 steps, reported text8 rankings follow pairwise predictions; evaluation scores 64 held-out characters after a 192-character prefix with teacher forcing. | §6.2; Figure 5a; Table 3 |
| Spread selection improves few-step image quality | On released MAR-B, ImageNet-256 FID-50K falls by more than 25% versus its random order at 8 steps, changing only reveal order. | §6.2; Table 3 |

These results support a useful schedule-selection heuristic, especially when dependence costs differ substantially. They do not establish universally correct rankings or an exact mapping from dependence cost to FID, accuracy, or perceptual quality. Several comparisons are described in prose, but their numerical table cells are unavailable.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Conditional-model errors fall outside the cost and can dominate close comparisons or reverse predictions as training changes (§7; Appendix B.2).
- **Author-stated:** The pairwise bound is tight only under its first-order conditional-independence reference. Real text retains dependence across revealed characters (§7; Appendix C.2).
- **Author-stated:** Continuous decoding additionally incurs conditional estimation and one-step channel errors (§3).
- **Analyst inference:** Pre-decoding evidence is uneven: confidence-rule estimates use selected sets after decoding, and spread-then-raster image floors were also computed afterward (Table 15; Table 2).
- **Analyst inference:** Uncertainty is incompletely characterized: most MAR cells use one seed, and video evaluation covers three prompts (Tables 3, 28). Perplexity gains also accompany increased repetition, although the authors report controls that preserve the gain (Appendix C.9).

## Open research questions
- **Can prediction incorporate conditional-model error?** It explains ranking reversals (§7). A next experiment could combine dependence estimates with held-out conditional losses and test rankings across checkpoints.
- **Can richer dependence estimates fix image-ranking failures?** Nested and low-discrepancy contradict both tested kernel floors (Appendix C.4). Test higher-order or state-sensitive estimates on held-out schedules.
- **When do interior token levels improve generation?** The tested character-class channel loses to absorbing decoding at every measured budget (§6.5). Compare alternative intermediate channels at matched steps and training resources.
- **Does the video memory result generalize?** Evidence covers three prompts on one released checkpoint (Appendix D). Repeat across more prompts and checkpoints with paired rollout evaluation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the framework and reported trends, but most numerical table bodies and plotted values are missing. Exact task gains, absolute FIDs/bpc, many uncertainty estimates, complete training configurations, and runnable code contents are unavailable. The author-reported code release has not been verified.
