# Retrieval-Centric Deep Learning in Growing Nonparametric Neural Networks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03858) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03858)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors derive learning rules for neural layers that grow a retrievable training memory, report strong single-pass CIFAR results, and identify memory growth and limited optimizer support as major obstacles.

## Problem and motivation
A gradient-descent-trained linear layer has an exact dual representation as attention over stored training inputs and error signals (§2). The authors ask whether replacing this linear attention with nonlinear kernels can improve learning beyond fixed-size weight matrices.

The gap is a principled training rule: inserting ordinary softmax changes the normalization for every query, so the linear-case update no longer preserves the previous function before applying a correction (§3.2; Appendix B.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Growing kernel layers:** Store layer inputs as keys and scaled negative output gradients as values. For positive-definite kernels, appending these pairs equals SGD on an implicit RKHS operator (§3.1; Appendix A). The authors acknowledge that the underlying RKHS rule has prior precedent; their contribution includes its integration and evaluation in modern deep networks (§6).
- **Weighted softmax learning:** Introduce scalar entry weights and derive a functional gradient framework using a Gaussian background measure. They propose clipping or a gauge transformation to handle negative weights (§3.2–3.2.1). The experimental implementation also drops a normalization factor and uses separate weight/value learning rates, so it differs from the formal update (Appendix F.3).
- **Optimizer connection:** Relate MesaNet-style retrieval to FOOF and approximate second-order optimization, and derive a DeltaNet-based update for conventional weight matrices (§4; Appendix E.4).
- **Capacity control:** Combine Adam updates of stored tuples with novelty-triggered growth and a maximum database size; provide preliminary CIFAR-10 evidence (§3.3; Appendix F.4; Fig. 5).

These results support promising sample efficiency in the tested settings, rather than universal superiority over parametric networks. Faster loss reduction per training exposure does not establish lower wall-clock cost. Most plotted numerical values and uncertainty intervals are absent from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Memory and compute grow linearly with training exposure and can be prohibitive; hybrid growth is preliminary (§§3.3, 5–6).
- **Author-stated:** Principled growing-model versions of advanced optimizers such as Adam and Muon remain unresolved (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Clipping, omitted normalization, and independent learning rates weaken a direct interpretation of experimental weighted softmax as the exact derived functional update (Appendix F.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Appendix F.1 describes selecting configurations to maximize displayed metrics, including test accuracy; an independent validation-selection protocol is not established, raising a model-selection validity concern.
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation is restricted to CIFAR and synthetic regression, with a single-epoch ViT demonstration; language and continual-learning benefits remain untested in the supplied evidence (§§5–6).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Teacher depth, hybrid initialization, and some figure references differ between passages (§5 versus F.1.2; Algorithm 4 versus F.4). Table bodies and figure graphics are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Can growing models gain from advanced optimizers?** Their derivations remain open (§6). A next experiment could compare a formally derived preconditioned growth rule against SGD growth and Muon/Mesa at matched storage and compute.
- **Does hybrid growth remain effective over long streams?** Evidence is preliminary and single-epoch (§5). Test capacity, accuracy, runtime, and memory across repeated passes and changing data distributions.
- **How much do practical update modifications matter?** The implementation differs from the theory (F.3). Ablate normalization removal, clipping versus gauge positivity, and separate learning rates under matched tuning budgets.
- **Can preserved memories improve continual learning?** This is proposed without evaluation (§6). Test sequential tasks and measure forgetting with and without memory replay or retrieval interventions.
