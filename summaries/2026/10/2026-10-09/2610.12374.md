# AgentGarten: Code Worlds for Evolving Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12374) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12374)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgentGarten combines executable simulation with a real-time neural renderer and inherited written playbooks, demonstrating task improvements in pretrained visual agents without establishing a controlled sample-efficiency advantage over reinforcement learning.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a trade-off: simulators provide explicit, editable dynamics but require costly visual assets, while video world models generate varied appearances but keep state and rules implicit. AgentGarten separates these responsibilities to support inspectable environments with diverse visual observations for agent practice (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Code-world framework:** A simulator maintains persistent state and executes actions; a shared renderer receives depth or surface normals, an appearance reference, text, and visual history. Rendered frames influence state through agent actions rather than directly updating simulation state (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Adversarial Forcing:** A pretrained video model is geometry-conditioned, adapted to block-causal generation, and distilled using distribution matching, differentiable history encoding through bitwise-exact replay, and real-video adversarial supervision. An exact R1/R2 construction computes discriminator-head gradients without double backward through the frozen backbone (§§3.1–3.4; Appendix B).
- **Interactive deployment and agent study:** Bounded caching, custom kernels, CUDA graphs, and a tiny decoder support streaming observations. Pretrained agents act visually and pass written playbooks between fresh conversations; the study covers hide-and-seek and four additional worlds (§§3.5, 5).

These contributions combine and extend existing rendering, distillation, and written-experience methods; the paper does not present playbook reflection or explicit-state rendering as wholly new ideas (§6).

The evidence supports the reported replay agreement, hardware-specific throughput, qualitative rendering comparison, and improvements within recorded agent runs. It does not isolate which components cause agent improvement or establish robust generalization. The authors explicitly reject interpreting the historical RL comparison as a direct sample-efficiency ratio (§5.2), qualifying the stronger efficiency language in the abstract.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Geometry omits material, color, identity, and some motion states; downsampling can erase thin structures and contact changes. Appearance may be forgotten after long occlusions or beyond the memory window (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Worlds were built individually, playbooks remain world-specific, and automated world validation and cross-world experience management remain future work (§7).
- **Method boundary:** Exact R1/R2 head gradients require a frozen backbone; the surrogate does not support arbitrary higher-order derivatives (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** One episode per round in the additional worlds leaves variability and reliability unresolved. No controlled playbook-free or conventional-rendering agent comparison is reported in the supplied text (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Qualitative rendering examples do not quantify state-observation fidelity or separate the contributions of replay and adversarial supervision (§4.1).
- **Evidence availability:** Tables 1–5 have captions but no retrieved cells; Appendix D contains task-file placeholders rather than actual instructions.

## Open research questions
- **Do playbooks reliably cause improvement?** Recorded rounds lack a playbook-free control. A next experiment could compare inherited, empty, and shuffled playbooks across matched seeds.
- **Does exact replay improve rendered quality or agent success?** The replay experiment measures numerical agreement and forward cost. A next experiment could train matched models with exact and divergent replay and evaluate both visual fidelity and task outcomes.
- **Can richer conditions preserve hidden attributes?** Geometry and bounded history leave some states unobservable (§7). A next experiment could add object attributes and motion state, then test long-occlusion revisits and visually symmetric objects.
- **Do lessons transfer across worlds?** Current playbooks are world-specific (§7). A next experiment could evaluate inherited lessons on unseen layouts and tasks against fresh-agent controls.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly explains the methods and selected results, but leaves material evidence unavailable: numerical table contents, complete task files, training-mixture sizes and stage schedules, repeated-seed agent statistics, component ablations, quantitative rendering-quality measures, and code/model release status.
