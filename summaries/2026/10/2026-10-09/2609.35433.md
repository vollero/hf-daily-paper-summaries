# ReSPO: Reshaped Sequence Policy Optimization for Gradient Starvation in Off-Policy Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35433) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35433)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReSPO uses advantage-dependent sequence weights to preserve learning from under-generated positive responses and suppress extreme negative responses, with reported mathematical-reasoning gains over GRPO, GSPO, and VESPO under greater rollout reuse (§§3.3, 4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Reusing expensive LLM rollouts increases the mismatch between the current policy and the policy that generated them. The authors identify two failures in clipped optimization: low-ratio positive responses receive vanishing gradient weight, while high-ratio negative responses can dominate updates. Existing sequence-level alternatives either retain these failures or suppress both tails, losing useful positive-response signal (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Sign-dependent tail design:** ReSPO applies different smooth kernels to the raw sequence importance ratio \(W\), preserving positive-response weight as \(W\to0\) while suppressing both negative-response extremes (§3.3.4).
- **Variational construction:** A power-Bregman divergence produces a behavior–target interpolation; a subsequent KL projection adds an exponential tilt for bounded weight moments (§§3.3.2–3.3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Concrete update and diagnostics:** The chosen kernels are \(\phi^+(W)=(1+W)e^{1-W}/2\) and \(\phi^-(W)=\sqrt W e^{2(1-\sqrt W)}\). Detached weights scale sequence contributions; experiments examine training, held-out accuracy, positive-tail allocation, and compatibility with Routing Replay (§3.3.5; Appendices B, E).

## Evidence and main results

All differences below are author-reported; “pp” means percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better late training scores than clipped baselines | Across Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-30B-A3B at \(N=8,16,32\), last-128-update mean normalized score exceeds the strongest GRPO/GSPO baseline by **4.2–8.4 pp**; the MoE score includes a length penalty. | §4.2; Appendix E.1 |
| Gains over VESPO are not universal | ReSPO has the highest late mean in **five of six** settings; at 1.7B, \(N=16\), it trails VESPO by **0.4 pp**, with overlapping temporal ranges. | §4.2; Appendix E.1 |
| Higher held-out macro pass@1 at greater reuse | Against the strongest baseline, six-benchmark final-checkpoint macro gains at \(N=16,32\) are **0.8/1.3 pp** for 1.7B and **3.2/1.9 pp** for 30B. At \(N=8\), macro results are similar relative to bootstrap uncertainty. | §4.2; Appendix E.1; Table 1 referenced |
| Positive-tail coefficient retention | Matched 1.7B diagnostic runs at \(N=32\), response cap 16,384 tokens, batches 9–16: for \(\log W<-1\), ReSPO assigns **38.5%** of positive coefficient mass to **22.8%** of positive responses; VESPO assigns **15.5%** to **16.7%**. | Appendix E.3; Table 5 referenced |
| Advantage persists within length ranges | On stored 1.7B evaluation responses pooled across six benchmarks, ReSPO has the highest verifier accuracy in **24 of 30** shared length bins across \(N=8,16,32\). | §4.2; Appendix E.2 |
| Partial seed robustness | At \(N=32\), three ReSPO runs yield late training means of **27.07% ± 0.54 pp** for 1.7B and **57.92% ± 0.95 pp** for 30B, versus VESPO reference scores of **23.00%** and **52.30%**; ± denotes cross-seed population SD. | §4.3 |
| Routing Replay complements ReSPO | Matched 30B, \(N=16\): adding R3 raises last-128-update penalized training accuracy from **56.32% to 58.16%**, and lowers temporal SD from **2.11 to 0.75 pp**. | §4.3; Appendix E.4 |

The evidence supports improved optimization and observed held-out performance in these mathematical RLVR settings. Positive-tail diagnostics support the proposed allocation mechanism, but coefficient mass is not a measured gradient norm or a causal demonstration of improved reasoning. Temporal SD measures within-run variation; evaluation bootstrap uncertainty is conditional on fixed checkpoints and generations (Appendices E.3, D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Compute constraints prevent longer-context, longer-horizon MoE experiments; multiple ReSPO seeds are restricted to \(N=32\). Kernel hyperparameters were analytically selected without an exhaustive search (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond mathematical reasoning and the two Qwen3 models remains untested in the supplied experiments (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** Seed robustness is incomplete across methods and reuse settings; the reported ReSPO seed results do not establish equivalent baseline variability (§4.3; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** No isolated positive- versus negative-branch ablation is supplied, so their separate contributions remain unresolved. Length-bin comparisons also do not control for identical problem difficulty within bins (Appendices E.2–E.3).
- **Implementation boundary:** Reshaping intentionally introduces bias, and clamping log importance ratios makes the implementation a finite-range approximation to the analytical tails (Appendices A.6, B).

## Open research questions
- **Which branch drives the gains?** The diagnostics emphasize positive responses without isolating branch effects. Next experiment: replace each branch separately while holding training settings fixed.
- **How robust are the comparisons across seeds?** Replication is concentrated on ReSPO at the largest reuse setting. Next experiment: repeat every method across matched seeds and reuse ratios.
- **Do gains persist with longer MoE reasoning and training?** Compute limits leave this setting unexplored. Next experiment: extend the MoE response cap and update horizon, separately varying the length penalty.
- **How sensitive is performance to kernel shape?** Analytical choices do not establish empirical optimality. Next experiment: sweep branch parameters while recording pass@1, weight moments, and tail allocation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose explains the method and reports detailed comparisons, but many table bodies and plotted values are absent despite their captions and references. Missing material includes complete per-benchmark scores and error bars, full shared hyperparameters, baseline seed distributions, isolated branch ablations, and the accompanying code artifact itself.
