# Pumpire: Unified Benchmark for Metric Distance Estimation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12423) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12423)


## One-sentence takeaway
Pumpire directly evaluates physical point-pair distances across image- and video-level 3D models, revealing that conventional depth/intrinsics rankings and completion accuracy on benign scenes can conceal distance-estimation failures.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that separate depth and camera-calibration benchmarks, point-cloud similarity metrics, and object-level spatial question answering do not directly measure physical distances between specific points. They also identify fragmented evaluation across single-image, multi-view, RGB-only, and depth-conditioned models (§1).

Accurate physical measurements motivate applications in manipulation, driving, and spatial reasoning, but Pumpire evaluates geometry estimation rather than downstream task success (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Measured dataset:** pumpire-6k contains 100 scenes—40 indoor and 60 outdoor—with 64 sampled frames each, totaling 6,400 frames. Each scene has one fixed sticker pair with a tape-measured distance; frames include RGB, aligned sensor depth, intrinsics, and verified sticker coordinates (§3; Appendix A).
- **Unified protocol:** back-project annotated pixels using predicted depth and its associated intrinsics, then compare their Euclidean separation against physical measurements. Metrics include absolute, relative, and logarithmic error, threshold accuracy, and cross-view relative standard deviation (RSD), averaged equally across scenes (§4).
- **Comparative evaluation:** 29 configurations cover image/video estimation and completion. Ablations examine intrinsics substitution, depth-prior density, prior-view coverage, and sampling stride (§§5–6; Appendices B–F).

The contribution is a dataset and evaluation framework, rather than a new geometry prediction model. The claim that this is the first unified benchmark is the authors’ claim (§1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Separate geometry benchmarks do not reproduce distance rankings | MoGe-2 has the best combined depth/intrinsics average rank, but Unidepthv2 performs better on normal-subset Pumpire metrics. Ground-truth intrinsics substitution worsens 47 of 56 model–metric pairs on that subset. | Tables 3, 7, 9; Appendix E |
| Sensor reliability changes sharply across subsets | Raw sensor AE is 0.026 m on 76 normal scenes versus 4.047 m on 24 challenging scenes; corresponding δ₁.₀₅ is 79.32% versus 21.03%. Difficulty uses sensor RSD > 0.1. | §5.1; Table 2 |
| Completion provides strong benign-scene accuracy but can suffer large errors | Lingbot-Depth’s image-level completion AE rises from 0.022 m to 1.045 m, while δ₁.₀₅ falls from 84.52% to 47.92%, between normal and challenging subsets with unprocessed sensor priors. | Table 5 |
| Video estimation has a strong evaluated baseline | Depth Anything 3 has the best average rank on both subsets; normal/challenging RE is 0.108/0.432 and δ₁.₂₅ is 92.25%/81.56%. | Table 4 |
| Multi-view context improves consistency in the aligned comparison | On normal scenes, Metric3D v2 versus Depth Anything 3 gives RE 0.106/0.109 and RSD 0.067/0.029; CAPA-VGGT completion gives RE 0.023 and RSD 0.028. All four compared models use aligned frame-occurrence weights. | Figure 5c; Appendices C–D |
| More depth is not uniformly better | Several image completion models peak at intermediate prior densities; increasing the prior-view fraction reduces RE for both evaluated partial-conditioning video models, MapAnything and pi3x. | Figures 5a–b; §6 |

These results support direct distance evaluation and show setting-dependent accuracy and consistency trade-offs. They do not establish universal superiority of a model family or downstream improvements in robotics or driving. The explanation that sensor noise causes density-related degradation is the authors’ interpretation; the density experiment does not independently isolate noise.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-recognized:** difficult-scene failures can dominate aggregate errors, motivating separate reporting. Sampling stride also trades geometric diversity against visual overlap (§5.1; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** one fixed sticker pair per scene limits evidence for arbitrary point pairs throughout reconstructed geometry. Sticker-free measurement and broader pair coverage are not tested (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** difficulty is defined using the same sensor’s cross-view instability. Consequently, superiority over that sensor on the challenging subset is conditional on this selection rule (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** comparisons across settings change models, depth inputs, and intrinsics conventions; they cannot isolate the causal benefit of multi-view context alone (§5.2; Appendix D).
- Measurement uncertainty, annotation agreement, confidence intervals, and repeated-sampling variability are not reported. Fixed sampling improves comparability but does not quantify statistical uncertainty (Appendices A, C).
- Figure 5c’s aligned results differ slightly from Tables 3–6. Its claim that CAPA-VGGT has the lowest RSD applies to the four selected representatives; Table 6 reports lower normal-subset RSD for other CAPA variants.

## Open research questions
- **Does performance transfer to sticker-free, arbitrary point pairs?** The dataset evaluates fixed sticker centers. A next experiment could independently measure multiple unmarked pairs per scene and compare rankings.
- **How much do sparsity and noise each affect completion?** Current subsampling changes observation density without independently controlling noise. Test matched-density clean, noisy, and outlier-filtered priors.
- **Are robustness rankings stable across sensors and difficulty definitions?** Difficulty currently uses D435-derived RSD. Repeat capture with other sensors and report rankings under independent surface-based and measurement-error splits.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and tabulated findings; **medium** for broader explanations of robustness and causal benefits.

Material missing information includes physical measurement uncertainty, distance and capture-range distributions, annotation agreement, full inference/resource settings, statistical uncertainty, and confirmed dataset/code release terms. Some qualitative figure text is corrupted in extraction, so it cannot support precise additional numerical comparisons.
