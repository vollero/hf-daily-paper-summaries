# SanSi: A Looped Typed Decision Model for System 1.5 Thinking

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07730) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07730)


## One-sentence takeaway
SanSi improves typed decisions by repeatedly applying a pretrained looped backbone without generating text, but its accuracy gains require more computation and do not consistently improve confidence calibration (§§3, 5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Typed decision models return probabilities over caller-declared options, avoiding generated answers and parsing. A single forward pass offers fixed computation, which can limit decisions requiring dependent reasoning steps. The authors investigate whether repeated hidden-state computation can improve these decisions while preserving the typed interface, and measure its accuracy, cost, and probability reliability (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Typed adaptation of a looped backbone:** SanSi freezes Ouro and trains shared LoRA adapters plus small loop-specific readouts. The same transformer stack repeatedly updates its hidden state, without reinjecting input tokens or generating text (§3).
- **Predictions at every trained budget:** Cross-entropy plus Brier loss supervises every loop, making one model usable after any of its trained loops (§3). This extends existing looping approaches rather than introducing looping itself (§2).
- **Empirical characterization:** Controlled comparisons, synthetic depth tests, and ablations examine when loops help, when they overturn correct answers, and how probability averaging improves calibration (§§5–7).
- **Verifier demonstration:** Frozen SanSi probabilities provide rewards for training a question-answering generator without using gold answers in the reward (§8).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large gain over the main shape-matched control | On 10,027 test decisions from 59 sources, eight-loop SanSi achieves 72.0% accuracy, with a reported +13.5 percentage-point gain over SmolLM2-1.7B; 95% bootstrap interval [12.5, 14.5], three training seeds. The one-loop Ouro control reaches 58.6%. | §5.1; Appendix D.3 |
| Fewer parameters do not mean lower inference cost | SanSi is 5.3 points above Qwen3.5-2B and 1.8 points below Qwen3.5-4B on the same suite. Eight loops take 7.7× the one-loop Ouro GPU time and 3.3× Qwen3.5-4B time, measured on an RTX A6000. | §5.1; Table 6 |
| Most accuracy gains arrive early | Three loops reach 70.4% accuracy and deliver 88% of the loop-1-to-loop-8 gain on the main suite. | §§5.1–5.2; Figure 4 |
| Missing-evidence detection improves | On 382 unanswerable items, hard-answer rate falls from 27.1% at loop 1 to 17.5% at loop 8. Evidence AUROC rises from 0.835 to 0.935 on the 786-item answerable/unanswerable subset. | §5.3; Appendix C |
| Calibration gains reverse at later loops | ECE on answerable test items falls from 0.105 at loop 1 to 0.082 at loop 3, then rises to 0.093 at loop 8. Averaging all eight probability distributions gives ECE 0.044 and accuracy 71.8%, versus 72.0% at loop 8. | §§5.3, 7; Appendix H.5 |
| Loops extend synthetic reasoning depth | Trained on depths up to eight, SanSi reaches 72.6% accuracy on unseen liar-chain depths 9–16 versus Qwen3.5-4B’s 50.0%; unseen object-swap accuracy is 63.7%. Each task uses 120 test items per depth and three seeds. | §5.4; Appendix G.3 |
| Supervising every loop supports early readouts | Last-loop-only supervision gives 70.9% accuracy at loop 8 and 35.6% at loop 1, versus SanSi’s 72.0% and 58.4%, on the main suite. | §7; Appendix H.1 |
| Verifier rewards improve F1, but not exact match | For SmolLM2-1.7B trained with GRPO on 2WikiMultiHopQA, eight-loop rewards raise token F1 from 39.5 to 47.3; reported gain +7.7 points [6.1, 9.4], on 3,000 test questions. Exact match is 32.2 versus 32.5 before training; one-loop rewards lower F1 to 29.1. | §8; Appendices I.2–I.3 |

These results support useful additional computation within the evaluated Ouro-based models. They do not establish general superiority over larger models, lower latency, broad reward-model effectiveness, or reliable self-assessment. Qwen comparisons involve different backbones and are explicitly presented as references rather than controlled architectural comparisons (§5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Most analyses use one model size from one looped backbone family. Evaluation is English-only, synthetic depth evidence comes from two tasks, and verifier evidence comes from one dataset and generator (Limitations).
- **Compute and reliability:** Later loops can break correct answers and increase confidence after accuracy plateaus. Running beyond trained loops lowers main-suite accuracy; no adaptive stopping rule is proposed (§§5.2–5.3, 7).
- **Contamination and uncertainty:** Public benchmark exposure during backbone pretraining cannot be excluded. Bootstrap intervals reflect test-item selection, not training-run variability; the verifier’s four- versus eight-loop difference is particularly sensitive to seeds (Limitations; Appendices D.2, I.2).
- **Author-stated missing control:** SanSi-2.6B has no separately trained one-loop control, so its loop gain compares readouts of the same model (Appendix H.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Failed attempts to retrofit SmolLM2 support dependence on looped pretraining under this fine-tuning budget, not an impossibility of retrofitting recurrence (Appendix H.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The verifier’s F1 improvement is incomplete evidence of answer-quality improvement because exact match does not improve and answers become longer (Appendix I.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can a model choose its stopping loop reliably?** The authors leave stopping rules open, and some answers are correct early but wrong later. Train a stopping policy on held-out trajectories and compare accuracy, calibration, and GPU time against fixed budgets (§9).
- **Do the gains transfer beyond Ouro and English?** Current backbone and language coverage is narrow. Repeat matched-recipe comparisons on another looped family and multilingual decision suites (Limitations).
- **Can deeper reasoning remain calibrated outside training depths?** Synthetic unseen-depth errors can remain highly confident. Test probability averaging and held-out calibration separately by depth, measuring accuracy, ECE, and confident errors (Appendix G.3).
- **Does verifier training improve answer correctness across generators?** The case study is narrow and answer length confounds its metrics. Replicate with additional generators and datasets, including option construction that distinguishes concise answers from expanded variants (Appendix I.3; Limitations).
