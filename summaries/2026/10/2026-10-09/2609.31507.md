# SatNav: A Scalable Benchmark for Long-Horizon UAV Vision-Language Navigation from Satellite Imagery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31507) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31507)


## One-sentence takeaway
SatNav scales long-horizon aerial vision-language navigation through satellite imagery and map annotations, with controlled memory ablations showing substantial benefits from historical context and small transfer evaluations supporting preliminary real-UAV feasibility (§§3, 5; Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Urban UAV navigation requires agents to retain route progress, ground landmarks, count junctions, and stop correctly over extended trajectories. The authors identify two benchmark gaps: costly reconstructed 3D environments constrain geographic scale, and existing task designs insufficiently test long-range memory and geospatial reasoning (§1).

Their central premise is that satellite crops preserve enough large-scale structure to approximate UAV nadir observations for these tasks, enabling scalable episode construction without 3D rendering (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and construction pipeline:** SatNav aligns satellite imagery with OpenStreetMap annotations, validates geographic cues, generates executable trajectories, and constructs instructions using Qwen3.5 and Gemini 3 Flash. Sampled candidates receive a manual quality audit (§§3.2–3.4; Appendix C).
- **Explicit navigation diagnostics:** Boundary, Landmark, and Route tasks target loop progress and stopping, landmark-relative orientation, and junction counting, respectively; each has detailed, natural, and concise instructions (§3.1).
- **Controlled memory framework:** SwiftVLN adapts StreamVLN’s dual-memory design into a modular framework, comparing window overlap, history sampling, pose augmentation, map memory, and token clustering. Its contribution is the interchangeable evaluation framework rather than an entirely new underlying navigation architecture (§4.1).
- **Transfer mechanism and evaluation:** A Transformer adapter aligns UAV visual features with satellite features using paired imagery; evaluations include physical flights and rendered UAV observations (§4.2; Appendix I.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large, geographically varied benchmark | 118,494 episodes across 59 scenes and 18 cities; mean reference length 379 m, median 340 m. | §3.4 |
| Sampled generation errors remain | Manual audit accepted 2,829/3,000 candidates; 5.7% rejected before release. This measures sampled candidate quality before filtering. | Appendix C.4; Table 8 |
| Geographic generalization remains difficult | Navigation-pretrained StreamVLN* has the highest aggregate SR: 67.3% Test Seen and 58.4% Test Unseen. SwiftVLN scores 65.8% and 53.7%, with the lowest NE: 34.05 m and 62.29 m. | Table 1 |
| Long-term memory materially helps | SwiftVLN SR falls from 65.8%/53.7% to 44.5%/32.4% without long-term memory, a 21.3-point decrease on each split. Non-memory components and training protocols are held fixed. | §5.1.1; Table 2 |
| Window continuity improves success | Four overlapping dialogue turns raise SR to 71.3% Seen and 60.3% Unseen, versus 65.8%/53.7% for the no-overlap reference. | Table 2 |
| Temporal segmentation beats global clustering | STC achieves SR 68.3%/54.5%, versus GTC’s 63.5%/51.4%, using a total history budget of 512 tokens and all pre-window observations. | Table 2; Appendix E.3 |
| Physical transfer is promising but preliminary | On 18 real-flight episodes, six per family, the adapter raises overall SR from 38.9% to 55.6%; Boundary SR remains 33.3%. | §5.4; Table 3 |
| Rendered transfer corroborates adapter gains | On 94 rendered episodes, adapter SR is 43.6% versus 34.0% on clean views, and 39.4% versus 28.7% under exposure and jitter perturbations. | Appendix I.3; Table 14 |

For satellite evaluation, success requires predicting stop within 10 m of the target, or 30 m for Landmark; Boundary additionally requires first moving more than 20 m from the start (§5.1.2).

The controlled ablations support memory benefits within SwiftVLN’s tested configuration. Cross-model rankings do not isolate architecture effects because backbones, navigation pretraining, history interfaces, and training recipes differ (§5.2; Appendix F). Transfer results establish feasibility in the tested settings, not broad deployment reliability.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fixed-altitude discrete actions and trajectory filtering favor smooth routes and exclude winding paths or richer flight maneuvers (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Reference-trajectory training lacks reinforcement fine-tuning and provides limited exposure to states reached after mistakes (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Real-flight evidence is preliminary; communication latency limits trials, and local ambiguity, stopping failures, and execution uncertainty persist (§§5.4, 6; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is geographically narrow relative to benchmark size: Test Unseen covers three new scenes in three cities (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Filtering for visually clear, unambiguous cues limits conclusions about ambiguous or poorly mapped environments (Table 6; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied results lack repeated-run variability or confidence intervals, limiting assessment of small gains. The audit also does not establish the residual error rate across the entire released dataset.

## Open research questions
- **Can interaction training improve recovery and stopping?** Offline supervision leaves mistake states underrepresented (§6). Next experiment: compare trajectory-only training with interaction fine-tuning on wrong-turn and premature-stop diagnostics.
- **Does a dedicated map encoder improve map memory?** The authors attribute its weaker results to a possible shared-encoder compromise (§5.3), without isolating that explanation. Next experiment: compare shared and dedicated map encoders at matched memory budgets.
- **How robust is satellite-to-UAV transfer across operating conditions?** Physical trials are small, and exposure/jitter reduce rendered performance (§6; Table 14). Next experiment: expand flights across sites, illumination, and altitudes with repeated adapter/no-adapter trials.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and numerical comparisons; **medium** for broader transfer and causal explanations.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, repeated-run results, full-corpus residual annotation error, exact prompt templates beyond their descriptions, adapter validation scores, and verified downloadable dataset/code/checkpoint availability.
