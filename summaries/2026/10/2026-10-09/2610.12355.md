# Distilling Routed 3D Privilege for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12355) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12355)


## One-sentence takeaway
GPD uses question-routed geometric text and reference answers as training-only teacher privilege, improving Qwen3-VL spatial benchmark averages over controlled GRPO and answer-privileged OPSD while retaining RGB-only inference.

## Problem and motivation
RGB-only VLMs must infer geometry from appearance, making depth, direction, and layout judgments difficult. The authors identify a gap between explicit 3D augmentation, which adds inference requirements, and outcome-supervised training, which does not directly correct intermediate geometric errors. GPD uses geometry available during training to bridge this gap (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Geometric teacher privilege:** Convert depth, semantic instances, and bird’s-eye-view layouts into fixed textual summaries. Use aligned annotations where available and estimated cues otherwise (§3.2).
- **Question-conditioned routing:** The base VLM selects relevant available cue modalities offline; the teacher receives those summaries plus the reference answer (§3.2).
- **Selective self-distillation:** The same VLM samples RGB-only student responses and scores them under privileged teacher conditioning. Training combines GRPO with a sampled token-level KL estimate, applied only to trajectories below the reward threshold and scalar-clipped (§3.3).

The contribution is this combination of routed geometric privilege and error-gated distillation, rather than a new backbone or benchmark.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher VSI controlled averages at both scales | Reported VSI average accuracy: 4B GPD **57.1**, GRPO **56.4**, OPSD **55.7**; 2B GPD **51.7**, GRPO **51.3**, OPSD **50.8**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Higher average across four additional benchmarks | MindCube/SPAR/MMSI/ViewSpatial mean: 4B GPD **37.6**, GRPO **35.7**, OPSD **31.5**; 2B GPD **33.6**, GRPO **32.7**, OPSD **30.2**. | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Gains are not uniform | On 4B ViewSpatial, GPD scores **39.8**, below OPSD’s **40.4**. On 2B MMSI, GPD scores **21.1**, below the base model’s **24.3**. | Table 2 |
| Combined, routed textual privilege performs best in the reported ablation | 4B five-benchmark mean: answer-only **40.2**, routed-text-only **40.5**, combined GPD **41.5**, full-text-plus-answer **39.8**, routed-images-plus-answer **40.4**. | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Error gating improves the reported aggregate | With privileged KL weight **0.003**, incorrect-only distillation achieves five-benchmark mean **41.5**, versus **40.6** for all-trajectory distillation. | §4.3; Figure 4 |

These results support improved aggregate performance under the reported controlled training settings. They do not establish improvement on every benchmark or subtask. The authors interpret routing, training dynamics, and reduced test-time privilege gains as evidence of geometric internalization; these are suggestive observations, not direct proof of the mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-specified dependency:** BEV requires reliable camera poses and scene-layout information; examples can use only available modalities (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Controlled experiments cover two sizes of one backbone family. Three evaluation benchmarks also supply training-split samples; transfer to other model families or substantially different environments remains untested (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—cue reliability:** MindCube uses estimated depth and semantic instances, but the paper reports no controlled cue-noise or routing-error sensitivity study (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Reporting gaps:** No repeated-run variance, significance estimates, or measured training overhead are supplied. The Figure 7 evaluation setting is unspecified.
- **Interpretation risk:** Figure 3 provides illustrative token scoring, and Figure 8 provides one successful case; neither establishes systematic correction of intermediate geometric errors.
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** The text names Grounded-SAM-2, while its bibliography cites Grounded SAM. Exact extraction and routing prompts, reward definitions, and teacher-gradient handling are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is GPD to inaccurate privilege?** Estimated cues are used without a noise ablation. A next experiment could systematically corrupt depth, labels, and poses while measuring performance by task.
- **Does error gating improve learning beyond this reward threshold?** The paper compares incorrect-only against all-trajectory supervision but does not sweep the correctness threshold. Test alternative thresholds with repeated runs and task-specific results.
- **Does GPD generalize across backbones and scene distributions?** Evidence is limited to Qwen3-VL 2B/4B and the reported benchmark mixture. Train another backbone and evaluate on held-out scene domains.
- **Does the student acquire better geometry or mainly better answer behavior?** Reduced privilege dependence is indirect evidence. Evaluate intermediate spatial relations and counterfactual viewpoint changes using the same RGB-only models.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and aggregate comparisons are clearly described, but mechanism and robustness claims have weaker evidence.

Material missing information includes exact metric aggregation and reward rules, repeated-run uncertainty, extraction/routing prompts, compute costs, detailed split-overlap checks, teacher-gradient handling, Figure 7’s evaluation set, and verified contents of the announced code release.
