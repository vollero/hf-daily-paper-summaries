# Skill Constellations: Tracing the Supply Chain of Agent Skills on GitHub

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11169) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11169)


## One-sentence takeaway
A reconstructed GitHub skill-copy network identifies promising audit targets, with simulated removal at the top 100 model-ranked repositories preventing 14.9% of held-out high-risk adoptions versus 0.5% for the 100 most starred (Abstract; §II; §III-D).

## Problem and motivation
Agent skills contain instructions and scripts executed with user permissions, but copying them between repositories leaves no explicit dependency graph, version pinning, or update channel. Snapshot studies cannot establish adoption order, distribution sources, or whether downstream copies follow source changes. The paper addresses these gaps to support provenance analysis and audit prioritization (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dated distribution network:** Reconstructs skill versions, adoptions, and inferred copy sources from GitSkills repository histories, covering 2,193,119 adoptions. The author claims this is the first dated agent-skill copy network and contributes an interactive viewer (Abstract; §§I–II).
- **Copy-maintenance analysis:** Tracks exact-version groups through time to measure synchronized changes, source-following edits, and capability changes (§III-C).
- **Audit ranking:** Fits a conditional-logit source-choice model using skills held, past copies, stars, and repository age; evaluates rankings through temporal holdouts and a calibrated diffusion simulation (§II; §III-D).

Copy events require a commit adding at least ten SKILL.md files and an earlier adopter holding at least half its existing lineages. Consequently, inferred sources represent distribution hubs rather than verified authors or immediate copying parents (§II).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Dates validate better than exact source attribution | Among 1,534 installer-recorded folders, reconstructed dates fall within seven days in 94.6%; recorded sources held skills earlier in 99.1%, but the rule selects those sources in only 26.7%. | §II |
| Copy history predicts later distribution better than popularity | Rankings before 1 April 2026: current copy out-degree identifies 4.0 times as many later transmissions as stars; PageRank and skills held perform comparably. Ranking budget is unspecified in the prose. | §III-B; Fig. 2e |
| Copies seldom update together | Only 11.1% of clone-group changes move every member to the same next version; only 4.0% of genealogies ever change consistently. | §III-C; Fig. 3a,d |
| Shared ownership associates with coordinated updates | A single owner raises the odds of consistent change 3.32-fold, controlling for group size, platform, and content. | §III-C; Fig. 3c |
| Source-following is limited | Only 20.9% of skill copies follow a later source edit; these edits are not established as security fixes specifically. | §III-C |
| Risk flags are precise but incomplete against the reference labels | Strict flags achieve 98.3% precision and 80.9% recall on 200 skills sampled by flag stratum. | §II |
| Model-ranked audits outperform star-ranked audits in replay | At the 1 April 2026 split, auditing 100 repositories prevents 14.9% of later high-risk adoptions versus 0.5% for the 100 most starred; evaluation assumes removal of flagged skills and prevention of descendant copies. | Abstract; §II; §III-D; Fig. 4a |
| Audit ordering survives alternate settings | Ordering persists at March and May splits, under lenient flags, and in calibrated simulation; a quarter of prevented adoptions are indirect copies. | §III-D; Fig. 4b |

These results support historical distribution-based prioritization and weak downstream synchronization. They do not demonstrate actual attack prevention, deployed audit effectiveness, verified authorship, or propagation rates for confirmed security fixes. “Prevented adoptions” are counterfactual replay outcomes under the paper’s removal assumptions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Coverage is limited to the format’s first ten months on public GitHub. Flags indicate capabilities rather than malice; the author treats incomplete detection as making risk counts lower bounds (§VI).
- **Author-stated:** Source attribution measures distribution rather than authorship, with limited agreement on the exact installer-recorded source (§II).
- **Author-stated:** Label-validation scores favor Opus because the reference retains answers shared by both models; community names lack human validation (§II).
- **Analyst inference:** Threshold-based reconstruction may miss smaller copying events and misidentify immediate parents, affecting network interpretation (§II).
- **Analyst inference:** Real audits may have lower impact than replay assumes because complete flagged-skill removal and blocked descendant copying are not tested operationally (§II).
- **Reproducibility gap:** The supplied Data Availability section is empty, and figure captions lack underlying plots or detailed values.

## Open research questions
- **Does ranking prevent real harmful adoption?** Evaluation uses replay and capability flags. A prospective audit study could measure removals, subsequent copying, and confirmed harmful behavior.
- **How accurately can immediate copy parents be recovered?** Exact source agreement with installer records is limited. Test alternative reconstruction rules against a larger independently recorded provenance set.
- **Do versioned references improve security-fix uptake?** The recommendation follows observed copy-maintenance failures, without a tested replacement mechanism. Compare copied installations with versioned references after controlled fixes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and headline findings, but material details are unavailable: network/viewer/code release locations, full figure values, complete model estimates, exact threshold-sensitivity settings, holdout end dates, detailed risk rules and codebooks, and prospective intervention evidence.
