# Post-Training Frontier Text-to-Image Models by Composing Preference and Rubric Rewards

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02967) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02967)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that combining preference rewards with intent-gated rubric rewards, exploit vetoes, and complementary policy averaging improves text-to-image generation, including FLUX.2-dev’s live Arena Elo from 1132.8 to 1202.1 (§4.5).

## Problem and motivation
A holistic preference reward can favor aesthetics while overlooking requested content, unwanted additions, or style constraints. Optimizing that reward can therefore increase its score while degrading prompt faithfulness (§1; §A.9). The authors address how to combine complementary rewards without creating conflicting incentives or new opportunities for reward hacking.

## Approach and contributions
- **Complementary reward design:** A Bradley–Terry preference model trained on Arena votes is combined with dependency-aware faithfulness questions and constraints on unwanted content. The constraint reward is activated only for prompts classified as requiring strict adherence (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Reward composition:** Each reward axis is independently standardized; applicable exploit detectors veto positive standardized preference rewards rather than rewarding detector compliance. Active axes receive equal weights before conversion into DiffusionNFT training weights (§3.3).
- **Complementary policy averaging:** FLUX.2 policies trained with and without exploit prevention are combined through LoRA merging. The precise merge differs between Arena-RM and PickScore experiments (§3.3.5; §A.6).
- **Empirical recipe and lightweight asset:** The authors evaluate two base models and report results using Arena-T2I-Training, a training subset described as released in some passages and planned in others (§§4.1–4.5).

DiffusionNFT, dependency-aware faithfulness evaluation, reward normalization, and model soups are adopted techniques; the contribution centers on their reward composition and empirical application.

The results support the composed recipe in the tested settings, with both automated and live human evidence. They do not establish universal superiority or seed-level robustness. Most automated results use checkpoint selection on the reported validation set; the external prompt evaluation provides a stronger separation from selection. Complete table cells and numerical confidence intervals are absent from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Only two base models are evaluated; agentic workflows are untested; faithfulness decompositions lack human auditing; computational constraints permit only one training seed (§5).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- Validation checkpoint selection may make reported validation performance optimistic; bootstrap intervals would not establish variability across training seeds (§4.1; §A.8).
- Detectors target observed text and photorealism failures, so protection against other exploits remains unestablished (§4.2; §A.3.5).
- Cross-run versus same-run souping is not fully controlled for merge implementation in the Arena-RM experiments: exact weight-update averaging also increases adapter rank, whereas same-run merging averages factors (§A.6).
- Generalization to non-English, editing, short, or niche-entity prompts is unestablished because these are filtered from RL data (§4.1).
- Reproducibility details conflict: preference-data size is described as approximately 5 million versus 5.6 million pairs, and reward-model batch size as 1024 versus 1032 (§3.2.1; §4.3; §A.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does the recipe generalize across model families and seeds?** The study tests two bases with one seed; repeat matched experiments on additional architectures and multiple seeds (§5).
- **How sensitive are gains to checklist and intent-label errors?** Faithfulness questions are unaudited and intent classification is imperfect; compare automated metadata with human-corrected metadata (§5; §A.3.6).
- **Do vetoes withstand new exploits or longer optimization?** Detectors cover observed failures; extend training and independently audit emerging failure modes (§4.2).
- **How much of the soup gain comes from complementary rewards?** Merge procedures differ; compare same-run and cross-recipe policies using identical exact-update merging and matched adapter capacity (§A.6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main method and reported gains, but omits table bodies, confidence-interval values, full training hyperparameters, leaderboard vote counts and uncertainty, and complete external-evaluation comparisons. Actual release availability, training compute, and several inconsistent implementation details cannot be resolved from this text.
