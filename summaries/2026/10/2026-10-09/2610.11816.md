# Chaos in the Text: Revealing the Modality Preference in Mixed-Modality Retrievers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11816) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11816)


## One-sentence takeaway
Trident trains text, image, and fused document views as equally weighted positives under a shared contrastive objective, improving mixed-modality retrieval while reducing the observed tendency for irrelevant text to outrank relevant images (Sections 3–4; Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Mixed corpora require retrievers to rank text-only, image-only, and fused documents using comparable relevance scores. The authors find that strong single-modality performance does not ensure this: replacing images with corresponding text produces performance valleys, and irrelevant text disrupts image retrieval more than equally numerous irrelevant images (Section 2; Figures 2–3).

The identified gap is evaluation and training that largely emphasize single-modality candidate pools, leaving cross-modality ranking bias insufficiently tested (Sections 1 and 5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure-mode diagnosis:** Controlled modality replacement preserves document identities and corpus size while changing modality proportions. Similarity and hard-negative analyses associate retrieval degradation with a systematic text scoring advantage, termed *modality preference* (Sections 2–3).
- **Trident training:** Generate a detailed description and brief summary for each image; use the description, original image, and image-plus-summary as three positive views for the same query. Multi-Positive View InfoNCE averages their negative log-probabilities using one denominator containing all positives and negatives (Section 4.1).
- **Objective analysis and evaluation:** The loss decomposes into relevance discrimination and a KL penalty balancing positive-view probability shares. Experiments test retrieval, composition sensitivity, distractors, and component ablations across CLIP-based and VLM-based models (Equation 6; Appendices E–H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Modality mixing exposes retrieval instability | ChartQA and SlideVQA composition sweeps show performance valleys when text and images coexist; fused documents generally alleviate them. Equal-count text distractors cause larger losses than image distractors. | Section 2.2; Figures 2–3 |
| Text scoring advantage affects rankings | On SlideVQA, Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B positive-pair mean cosine similarity is 0.664 for text versus 0.484 for images; text-hard-negative interference is 17.8% versus 87.8%, respectively. | Sections 3.1–3.2; Figure 4 |
| Trident improves visual-document averages | Across six benchmarks, Jina-CLIP-v2 → Trident-JinaCLIP average NDCG@10 rises from 42.68 to 55.51 in Mix and 51.42 to 53.04 in Origin. Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B → Trident-Qwen3VL-2B rises from 40.12 to 73.90 in Mix and 77.59 to 80.51 in Origin. | Table 1 |
| Shared objective adds benefit beyond balanced data | Full versus “w/o Multi-Positive View InfoNCE” six-benchmark Mix average NDCG@10: 55.51 versus 55.11 for Jina; 73.90 versus 66.36 for Qwen. | Table 1 |
| Natural-image results support broader applicability | Across Google-WIT, MSCOCO, VisualNews, and OVEN, baseline → Trident Mix average NDCG@10 is 56.43 → 70.50 for Jina and 73.67 → 76.78 for Qwen. Origin averages also increase. | Table 4; Appendix F |
| Positive-view scores become more aligned | Trident-Qwen3VL mean positive cosine similarities are 0.674, 0.672, and 0.670 for text, image, and fused views under the SlideVQA analysis protocol. | Appendix C; Figure 12 |
| Stronger explicit equality does not improve final retrieval | Adding a balance loss reduces the standard deviation across six modality-pair similarity means from 0.004 to 0.003, but final mixed-corpus NDCG@10 is slightly lower; exact final scores are not tabulated. | Appendix G; Figures 14–15 |

These results support improved ranking and reduced modality sensitivity in the tested settings. They do not establish universal modality invariance or downstream answer-quality gains. The authors explicitly caution that natural-image Origin-to-Mix changes also reflect the greater retrievability of substituted text; same-Mix comparisons are therefore more informative (Appendix F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-recognized view mismatch:** Images, descriptions, and summaries can differ in information content. Appendix G acknowledges that descriptions may omit visual evidence, qualifying the earlier assumption of semantic equivalence.
- **Author-observed residual variation:** Trident-JinaCLIP retains mild composition sensitivity, particularly on InfoVQA and DocVQA. The proposed architectural explanation is an author interpretation, not an isolated causal test (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confound:** Trident-Qwen3VL starts from Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, whereas its principal comparison is Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B. The reported baseline gap therefore does not isolate the objective alone; the within-Trident ablation provides more direct evidence (Appendix D; Tables 1–2).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation uses text queries and constructed modality views; naturally heterogeneous corpora and downstream RAG answers are not evaluated.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix C reports Trident image-positive interference as 11.8%, while Figure 12 labels it 10.6%. Uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variation are not supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Does Trident transfer to naturally heterogeneous corpora?** Constructed views dominate this evaluation. Next experiment: test independently authored text and images with manually verified relevance labels.
- **How sensitive is training to description quality?** The authors acknowledge unequal view information but do not vary the description generator. Next experiment: compare human descriptions, multiple generators, and controlled evidence omissions.
- **How much improvement comes from initialization versus the objective?** The main Qwen comparison uses different starting models. Next experiment: hold initialization, data, and compute fixed while comparing shared and separately normalized objectives.
- **Do retrieval gains improve answer quality?** No downstream answering evaluation is reported. Next experiment: fix a RAG generator and compare evidence recall and answer correctness before and after Trident.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methods, results, and ablations, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain qualified.

Material missing information includes view-generation prompts and fidelity checks, exact visual-document Mix proportions and evaluation pool sizes, hard-negative selection details, loss temperature, precise implementation of the objective-removal ablation, repeated-run uncertainty, and confirmation of released checkpoints or generated data.
