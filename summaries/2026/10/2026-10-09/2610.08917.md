# CARE: Certifying Acceleration for Vision-Language-Action Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08917) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08917)


## One-sentence takeaway
CARE selects accelerated policies using finite-sample bounds on paired episode-level regressions, reporting 9.02–10.81× OpenVLA-OFT inference speedups on LIBERO while preserving statistical risk control under explicit sampling assumptions (§4.1; Appendix A.2–A.3).

## Problem and motivation
Average task success can hide acceleration-induced regressions: newly solved episodes can offset episodes that the reference policy solves but the accelerated policy fails. Per-decision measurements also miss effects that accumulate through changing states and observations. The authors therefore target complete, paired rollouts and ask which acceleration configuration can be deployed within a specified regression budget (§1; Appendix A.5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Episode-level risk formulation:** Acceleration-induced failure (AIF) is \(Y_0(1-Y_\lambda)\), measured from the same task, initial scene, and seeds. Risk averages equally across tasks; retention separately measures the fraction of reference successes preserved (§3.1).
- **Certify-then-select:** CARE applies Learn-then-Test with family-wise error correction, selecting the fastest certified candidate or retaining the reference. Under the stated assumptions, the probability of selecting a policy with risk at or above the budget is bounded by the error level (§3.2; Theorem 1).
- **Efficient certification:** Task-balanced rounds support anytime-valid sequential testing despite heterogeneous task risks. Fastest-first search and early abandonment reduce candidate evaluations; reference rollouts occur only after candidate failures and are cached without changing AIF decisions (§3.3–3.4; Lemmas 1–2).
- **Architecture-independent evaluation:** The same certification procedure is tested with existing VLA accelerators, flow-step reduction, and compute-reduced language-model agents. CARE contributes the selection procedure rather than new pruning or caching mechanisms (§4).

## Evidence and main results

The following are author-reported results.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Certified OpenVLA acceleration | Four LIBERO suites, eight H8 candidates, H1 reference; 250 calibration and 250 test episodes per suite; AIF budget 5%, error level 5%. Reported inference speedups: 9.02–10.81×; simultaneous 95% retention lower bounds: 85.8%–95.7%. | §4.1; Table 1 discussion |
| Heuristic selectors violate tight budgets | Across 10,000 resampling trials using stored LIBERO episodes, latency-only selection exceeds a 2% budget in 75% of trials; average-success selection exceeds a 2.5% budget in 25%. CARE has no observed over-budget selections, using fallback when necessary. | §4.2; Table 2 discussion |
| Sequential certification trades selection power for cost | At a 5% budget, fixed-n CARE averages 8.88× speedup with 11.7% fallback; sequential CARE averages 7.32× with 26.9% fallback, reducing mean evaluation from 2,250 to 592 rollouts. | §4.2; Table 3 discussion |
| Broader-family evaluation remains efficient | With 24 OpenVLA-OFT configurations per suite, CARE certifies acceleration on all four suites using 5,263 versus 25,000 exhaustive rollouts: 78.9% fewer. Reference rollouts fall from 1,000 to 113. | §4.3; Table 4 discussion |
| Component ablation shows complementary savings | Eight-candidate evaluation across LIBERO suites: combined sequential search and failure-triggered reference evaluation reduce rollouts from 9,000 to 1,997 (77.8%) and wall time from 38.6 to 7.0 hours (81.9%). | §4.5; Appendix B.1, Table 10 discussion |
| Flow-step reduction transfers, with modest measured speedup | Against ten-step H8 pi-0.5, CARE selects four steps on Goal and two on Spatial/Object, with measured speedups of 1.26× and 1.45× respectively; Long retains the reference. Total evaluation: 1,276 versus 4,000 rollouts, 68.1% fewer. | §4.4; Table 5 |
| Crafter extension has limited task coverage | Qwen3.5-9B and Llama-3.1-8B certify collect_wood schedules on every tested seed with 25.1%–30.6% compute reduction; collect_drink certifies on two of three seeds per model. | §4.4; Table 6 discussion |

These results support useful acceleration and lower certification cost in the evaluated settings. Zero observed violations in resampling do not establish zero deployment risk; the theoretical guarantee is probabilistic and assumption-dependent. Flow-step computation reductions also exceed measured inference speedups, so those quantities should not be conflated.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-established scope:** Guarantees require prespecification, independent sampling, deployment-matched within-task scenes, and execution independence. They control equal-weight average task risk, not a separate risk ceiling for every task (Appendix A.2).
- **Author-established conservatism:** Tight budgets or insufficient evidence cause fallback. Sequential CARE has higher fallback and lower mean speedup than fixed-n CARE in the reported comparison (§4.2; Appendix B.2).
- **Author-established retention distinction:** An AIF budget does not directly guarantee the same conditional retention when reference success is low. Direct retention certification requires reference outcomes on every calibration unit, losing failure-triggered savings (§3.1; Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer to physical robots, shifted scene distributions, or broader agent tasks remains unestablished: evaluation uses simulated LIBERO manipulation and two Crafter achievements (§4; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** CARE identifies the fastest configuration supported by its certification procedure and profiling measurements; it does not establish that this is the fastest truly safe configuration.
- **Evidence availability:** Most table bodies are absent from the supplied retrieval, limiting inspection of suite-specific outcomes and detailed baseline comparisons.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is certification to deployment shift?** The sampling assumption requires deployment-matched scenes. A next experiment could certify on one scene distribution and measure paired AIF under controlled layout or task-frequency shifts.
- **Can per-task regressions be bounded efficiently?** CARE constrains average task risk. A next experiment could compare mixture-level certificates with simultaneous per-task certificates and measure added rollout cost.
- **What is the best cost–fallback trade-off for direct retention control?** The paper derives retention certification but reports deployment selection primarily under AIF budgets. A next experiment could compare direct retention testing with AIF testing plus reference-success bounds at matched retention requirements.
