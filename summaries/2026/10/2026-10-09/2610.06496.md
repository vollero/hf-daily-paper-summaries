# You Changed Your Mind, The Model Didn&#x27;t: Demystifying Intent in Multi-Turn Dialogue

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06496) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06496)


## One-sentence takeaway
On Intent-Eval, rejected and accepted requirement changes reduce task accuracy, while decision-conditioned self-distillation improves average performance but leaves substantial difficulty with rejected changes (§§4.1–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-turn assistants must distinguish requirements that remain active from proposals users reject or requirements they replace. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that compare clarification, acceptance, and rejection on the same tasks, with controls for task difficulty and dialogue length (§1). This matters because merely discussing an inactive requirement can interfere with otherwise unchanged task execution.

## Approach and contributions

- **Intent-Eval:** A benchmark of 414 source tasks and 3,312 instances per model across tool calling, code generation, database querying, and mathematics. Matched conditions compare uninterrupted disclosure, neutral clarification, rejected proposals, and accepted revisions; single-turn controls and interaction extensions isolate different sources of degradation (§2).
- **Error diagnosis:** The authors measure inactive-content inclusion among newly incorrect responses and examine whether degradation accumulates or persists with further discussion (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Intent-OPSD:** A frozen Teacher and Student start from the same model. The Teacher receives the complete original or revised task according to the user’s decision; the Student receives the full dialogue. Training matches their token distributions on Student-generated final-answer prefixes using Jensen–Shannon divergence. Only the Student is needed at inference (§3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Both decisions introduce additional difficulty | Multi Retained scores 47.37% and Revised 49.88%, respectively **8.06 and 5.55 pp below Multi Original**. Single-turn Retained is **7.52 pp below Single Original**; Single Revised is **5.74 pp below Direct Revised**. | Table 1; §4.1 |

| Intent-OPSD improves aggregate accuracy | Across four models, four domains, and four conditions with equal condition/model–domain weighting: **51.12%**, versus **40.31% Base** and **47.39% matched SFT**—gains of **10.81 and 3.73 pp**. | Table 2; §4.4 |
| Rejection remains a weakness | Mean Retained accuracy is **38.57% for Intent-OPSD versus 39.13% for SFT**. At four-block rejection accumulation, pooled accuracy is **35.22% versus 38.58%**, respectively, over 1,488 evaluations per path/depth. | Tables 2 and 9 |

The matched controls support performance differences associated with dialogue presentation and decisions. Inactive-content inclusion is consistent with the authors’ “mentioned-as-in-effect confusion” explanation, but does not establish a causal mechanism: mentioning an inactive value in commentary need not mean using it operationally (Appendix E.2).

The training comparison establishes an aggregate advantage over matched SFT. Both methods receive decision-aligned supervision, so it does **not** isolate the benefit of decision conditioning itself (Appendix D.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The benchmark changes one selected requirement; interacting requirements and more varied user behavior remain untested (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** Rejected changes remain difficult, with mean Retained performance similar to SFT (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** Natural-conversation training would require reconstructing active-task specifications, which benchmark construction currently supplies (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Simulator errors may affect evaluation. Manual review found faithful information delivery in 98.6% of inspected shard-bearing messages, rather than perfect delivery (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Teacher-based admission restricts training to sources whose original and revised tasks the base model already solves; transfer to tasks beyond that capability is not established (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** No decision-conditioning ablation, repeated-seed uncertainty estimates, or significance tests are reported in the supplied text. Aggregate gains also conceal individual condition regressions (Table 2).

## Open research questions

- **Does decision conditioning itself drive improvement?** Both trained methods use it. Compare Intent-OPSD with identical distillation using an unconditioned Teacher prompt (Appendix D.2).
- **Can rejected-change robustness improve without sacrificing other conditions?** Rejection gains lag the aggregate improvement. Test rejection-focused training mixtures and evaluate all four conditions plus repeated-rejection paths (Tables 2 and 9).
- **Does the method generalize to interacting requirements and natural users?** Current events are controlled and specifications are supplied. Evaluate human dialogues containing multiple dependent revisions, with independently annotated active requirements (Appendix F).
- **Does inactive content cause incorrect execution?** Detection counts commentary as well as operative output. Annotate actual use of inactive requirements and test whether removing those uses changes correctness (Appendix E.2).
