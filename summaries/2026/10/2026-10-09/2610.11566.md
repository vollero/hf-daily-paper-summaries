# Incremental Open-Ended Deep Research with Structured Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11566) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11566)


## One-sentence takeaway
Structured Harness updates prior research reports through selective editing and persistent evidence reuse, achieving similar judged quality with greater report continuity and lower token and search costs than fresh generation in the supplied evaluations (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address maintaining research reports as information changes. They argue that repeatedly generating reports from scratch duplicates retrieval and computation while unnecessarily changing valid content and structure. The identified gap is that existing open-ended deep research systems primarily support one-off generation rather than accumulated research maintenance (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Incremental research setting:** Generate the next report from the query, previous report, and currently accessible information; evaluate both single updates (SST) and consecutive update chains (LCT) (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Structured Harness:** Represent reports as outlines, sections, and evidence; retrieve relevant components, persist source records, and selectively edit report sections. Source refresh uses character similarity, followed by LLM claim-level comparison when similarity falls below the threshold of 0.9 (§3.2; §4.1).
- **Temporal evaluation framework:** Compare quality, continuity, and research cost across annual timestamps from 2016–2025 on two existing benchmarks (§4.1).

The contribution is an environment and state-management harness built on ms-agent, rather than a new model or agent architecture (Appendices A.2, B.1).

These results support the authors’ claim of efficient report reuse under the tested settings. They do not establish statistical equivalence in quality or directly demonstrate that preserved claims remain correct: continuity metrics measure textual and structural similarity.

The supplied text contains reporting inconsistencies. In §4.2, **0.13** is described as another MS-Agent content ROUGE-L value, although Table 3 assigns it to BLEU-4. Section 4.3 calls DeepConsult’s token change from **2.79M to 2.81M** a reduction, although it increases. Its stated 2025 MS-Agent(OC) DeepConsult score also conflicts with Table 2. The findings above use the tables.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ limitations:** Publication-date filtering cannot reconstruct historical changes within existing webpages; the benchmarks were designed for OEDR rather than incremental maintenance (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** The temporal restriction does not establish complete historical isolation of source contents or model knowledge (§§4.1, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** No component ablations, threshold sensitivity results, uncertainty estimates, or repeated-run statistics are supplied, limiting attribution of gains and interpretation of small quality differences.
- **Analyst inference:** Some external baseline scores use different judges or release-year timestamps, limiting direct cross-system comparisons (Table 1 caption).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Appendix B.2 announces detailed prompts but contains none in the supplied text. Initial-report cost accounting and the handling of the first timestamp are also unclear.

## Open research questions
- **Does reuse preserve factual correctness when sources change?** Historical webpage evolution is not simulated (§5). Next experiment: use versioned sources with annotated additions, corrections, and retractions, measuring update accuracy and stale-claim retention.
- **Which harness components drive the gains?** No component ablations are supplied. Next experiment: remove structured retrieval, persistent evidence, and selective generation individually under matched models and budgets.
- **Does high continuity conceal missed updates?** Similarity metrics do not directly measure update completeness (§4.1). Next experiment: annotate required edits and jointly score preservation of valid claims and incorporation of changed claims.
- **When should a report be regenerated?** Long intervals can cost more than fresh generation (Table 4). Next experiment: vary change magnitude and compare fixed updating with a policy that chooses updating or regeneration.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and tabulated comparisons are clear, but historical validity and several reporting inconsistencies constrain interpretation.

Material missing information includes complete prompts, verified release availability, component ablations, statistical uncertainty, incremental-update ground truth, historical source snapshots, initialization-cost accounting, and exact aggregation across update transitions.
