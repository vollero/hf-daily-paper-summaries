# MIRA: A Musical Intent Refinement Agent for Aligning Text-to-Music Generation with User Intent

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10355) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10355)


## One-sentence takeaway
MIRA improves rubric-measured intent alignment across three text-to-music backends by grounding implicit requirements and searching over prompt revisions, with complementary expert evidence for ACE-Step improvements (§5; Appendix D.4).

## Problem and motivation
A convincing track can still omit requested instruments, structure, or contextual mood. The authors argue that global text–audio relevance scores obscure these failures, particularly when requests imply musical traits through references or use cases. Their proposed gap is request-specific evaluation that distinguishes explicit constraints from inferred intent and supplies actionable feedback (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Intent-alignment benchmark:** MuRA-Bench contains 100 curated Mureka-derived requests with expert-revised, independently assessable requirements. Gold rubrics distinguish explicit and inferred intent across seven musical dimensions (§3; Appendix A).
- **Requirement-level evaluation:** MusicFlamingo answers requirement-specific yes/no questions; normalized answer probabilities are aggregated within tracks and then across tracks. The authors validate this scoring design against expert ratings (§3.2; Table 1). AQA scoring itself builds on prior work.
- **Backend-independent refinement:** MIRA constructs a separate online rubric using the request and admitted tool evidence, then generates, verifies, and revises prompts under a bounded candidate budget. Tree search retains strong and complementary candidates; branch-specific memory records improvements and regressions. Generator parameters remain unchanged, and gold rubrics are withheld from refinement (§4; Appendix C.2).

The evidence supports improved request-rubric fulfillment, including gains beyond repeated sampling. It does not establish general audio-quality improvement, listener preference, or statistically reliable superiority over commercial systems. Commercial comparisons use direct prompting, whereas MIRA uses multiple candidates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Inferred intent admits multiple interpretations; three experts and model evaluators may reflect particular musical conventions. Rubric alignment is not a universal measure of artistic quality (Appendix F.4).
- **Author-stated scope:** Human studies assess intent fulfillment, excluding general perceptual quality and listener preference (Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Single-platform requests filtered for stable criteria limit evidence for ambiguous or context-dependent requests (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Disjoint expert assignments provide no overlapping ratings for estimating inter-rater reliability; reported confidence intervals measure variation across requests (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** MusicFlamingo guides search and primary evaluation, creating potential evaluator-specific optimization. Other evaluators and ACE-Step listening results reduce, but do not eliminate, this concern (Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Equal-budget and component evidence covers only two backends on a small subset. Adaptive-search seeds are fixed for ACE-Step/YuE2, while independent sampling uses consecutive seeds; thus the comparison changes more than prompt adaptation alone (Appendices D.2–D.3).
- **Resource limitation:** Budget counts generated candidates, excluding planner, retrieval, verifier calls, and elapsed time (Appendix D.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does inferred intent match the requesting user’s interpretation?** Gold completions reflect expert revision rather than reported user confirmation. Next experiment: ask originating users to assess alternative completions and resulting tracks.
2. **How robust are gains to evaluator choice?** Cross-evaluator results are encouraging, but human system evaluation covers only ACE-Step refinement. Next experiment: blinded, overlapping expert ratings across all three backends.
3. **Which search features matter independently?** Sequential component additions do not isolate memory, frontier diversity, and seed handling. Next experiment: factorial ablations with matched rubrics, seeds, and candidate budgets.
4. **Are gains worthwhile under total-resource constraints?** Candidate budgets omit substantial agent overhead. Next experiment: compare MIRA and sampling under matched wall-clock or total-cost budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text explains the method and reports consistent findings, but lacks numerical bodies for Tables 2–8, plotted values for Figures 3–4, and the displayed prompt-template boxes. Material gaps include exact human means and intervals, repeated-run variability, significance tests, total latency/cost, inter-rater reliability, and confirmed code/asset availability.
