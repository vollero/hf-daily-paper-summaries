# One Block, Multiple Depths: Recurrent Vision Transformers with Depth-Programmed Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12448) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448)


## One-sentence takeaway
reViT uses normalized depth to merge shared FFN experts inside one recurrent Transformer block, reaching 83.0% ImageNet-1k top-1 versus DeiT III’s 82.8% at near-matched inference FLOPs with 73% fewer stored parameters (Introduction; §4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Sharing one Transformer block across depth reduces parameter storage but removes independently learned, depth-specific transformations. The authors ask how to recover those transformations in vision while retaining full-block recurrence. They identify a gap in prior recurrent vision models: Raptor retains several block templates and uses intermediate teacher supervision, leaving single-block recurrence with final-output-only distillation unresolved (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Depth-programmed parameter sharing:** Shared attention and normalization accompany an FFN expert bank. A small router maps normalized depth to softmax coefficients that merge expert weights and biases before executing one dense FFN for all tokens. The contribution combines existing weight-merging ideas with explicit, input-independent depth conditioning (§3.1).
- **Common recurrent comparison:** Seven alternative expert mechanisms are adapted to the same recurrent backbone, objective, and base recipe. The experiments compare their suitability for full-depth sharing rather than rank their original architectures (§4.3; Appendix B).
- **Elastic depth and export:** Whole-step dropout with coordinate reindexing trains the depth program across effective depths. Inference resamples the program; fixed-depth export precomputes dense FFNs, trading compact storage for conventional execution (§3.2).
- **Mechanism analysis:** Routing, assignment permutations, and effective-rank measurements examine learned depth organization and expert utilization (§5; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong supervised results at larger scales | Under the shared supervised recipe and near-matched inference FLOPs, E=4 reViT-B/16 scores 83.0% ImageNet-1k top-1 versus DeiT III’s 82.8%; L/16 scores 83.8% versus 84.1%. S/16 trails at 78.2% versus 79.9%. | §4.2; Table 1 discussion |
| Weight merging leads the tested supervised adaptations | At nominal one-FFN budget U=1, recurrent S models with 12 steps score 78.2% for reViT, 77.9% for Lory, and 77.7% for SMEAR; other adaptations score 67.8–70.6% ImageNet top-1. | §4.3; Table 3 discussion |
| Distilled performance has a task-specific exception | At U=1 with the common B backbone, 12 steps, and distillation loss, reViT leads reported classification, segmentation, and linear-depth results; SMEAR has lower NYUv2 MLP-head RMSE: 0.485 versus 0.491. | §4.3; Table 4 discussion |
| One checkpoint benefits from additional tested depth | With elastic-depth training, ADE20k mIoU rises from 40.3 at 8 steps to 44.9 at 16, then 44.7 at 24; NYUv2 RMSE changes from 0.522 to 0.485, then 0.487. | §4.4; Figure 2 discussion |
| Additional experts particularly help the small model | Increasing E from 4 to 8 adds 2.0 ImageNet top-1 percentage points for supervised S/16, versus 0.2 for L/16. | §5.2; Table 5 discussion |
| Learned gate–expert pairing matters for alignment | On the 256-image CKA subset, permutations reduce mean teacher CKA from intact values of 0.793 to 0.208 for E=4 and 0.849 to 0.202 for E=8. | Appendix D; Figure 10 |
| Compact execution incurs measured overhead | Compiled ViT-S inference at 224×224, bfloat16, on one H100: dynamic reViT has a 3.6× smaller deployment graph and 48% lower total memory at batch 1, with 13% latency overhead; batch-64 overhead is 39%, with similar total memory. | Appendix A.5; Table 7 discussion |

The evidence supports parameter-efficient recurrence and useful depth resampling in the tested settings. It does not establish universal superiority: the small supervised model remains below DeiT III, SMEAR wins one distilled probe, and higher-compute alternatives become competitive. The authors claim that the eight-expert student nearly matches its teacher, but the supplied text omits the table cells needed to quantify that gap.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author qualifications:** Matching U matches nominal FFN arithmetic, not total FLOPs, parameter storage, or latency. Higher-U configurations change expert counts, widths, or activation patterns (§4.3; Appendix B).
- **Author qualifications:** Distilled Raptor comparisons use different supervision objectives and mixed result provenance, so they are reference comparisons rather than controlled ablations (§4.2; Appendix A.3).
- **Author qualifications:** Expert-deletion sensitivity mixes expert identity with schedule position; banded self-CKA also appears without experts and therefore does not establish the routing mechanism (§5.1; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Depth-only conditioning is not completely isolated in the SMEAR comparison: its adaptation also omits the auxiliary losses used by reViT (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is restricted to the named image benchmarks, one distilled teacher configuration, and frozen transfer probes. Elastic-depth evidence covers only the tested depths (§4).
- **Reproducibility limitation:** Many tables appear only as captions and prose summaries. Three-run means are reported for Tables 1–2, but uncertainty estimates and a code/checkpoint release are not established in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **How much benefit comes from depth conditioning versus regularization?** SMEAR differs in both respects. A factorial experiment holding expert banks, routers, and auxiliary losses constant would isolate their effects (Appendix B).
- **How far can depth resampling extend?** Evidence covers a finite depth grid. Evaluate the same checkpoints below and above that grid, tracking probe accuracy and latency (§4.4).
- **Can compact deployment achieve competitive latency?** The measured implementation reconstructs weights with generic reductions and lacks cross-input caching. Benchmark cached weights and optimized merging against materialized export, including storage and memory costs (Appendix A.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium overall**—the method and experimental scope are clearly described, but numerical verification is limited by missing table bodies.

Material unavailable information includes full result tables, exact teacher–student probe gaps, detailed FLOP and parameter counts across configurations, many training settings referenced in Table 6, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
