# It&#x27;s Not What the Image Shows: Irrelevant Context Destabilises VLM Judges Without Informing Them

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37863) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37863)


## One-sentence takeaway
On MIST’s English idiom-annotation task, adding images that judges are instructed to ignore changes roughly one-fifth of labels across thirteen VLMs while leaving aggregate human agreement nearly unchanged (§3.2; Table 6).

## Problem and motivation
Human-replacement tests typically assess a judge under one evaluation configuration. The authors ask whether their verdicts remain meaningful when supposedly irrelevant context changes individual annotations without changing overall agreement. Such instability matters because model-generated labels can become evaluation sets or training data (§1).

The identified gap is testing **annotation invariance alongside human substitutability**, rather than measuring only task accuracy or matching an image to text (§1; Appendix A).

## Approach and contributions

- **MIST benchmark:** 200 English sentences containing potentially idiomatic phrases, balanced between figurative and literal source readings. Each sentence is evaluated with an aligned image, an opposite-reading image, and no image; the annotation target remains the sentence (§2).
- **Controlled judge evaluation:** Thirteen VLMs run under four prompting techniques, with explicit versus absent ignore-image instructions. Measures cover label changes, movement toward depicted readings, human-majority agreement, and the alt-test (§3.1).
- **Empirical finding and released asset:** Similar instability under aligned and misleading images challenges a simple image-following explanation. The authors report releasing MIST and all human and VLM labels (§1; §3.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Images destabilize annotations more than the instruction edit | Across thirteen judges, pooled over four prompts: text-only→aligned changes **20.5%**, text-only→misleading **19.4%**; deleting the ignore-image clause with the image fixed changes **11.6%**. Both image rates exceed the control for every judge. | §3.2; Table 6 |
| Instability persists among judges passing somewhere in the alt-test grid | Seven-judge means: **15.9%** aligned and **14.7%** misleading, versus **8.8%** for the fixed-image prompt edit. | Table 1 |
| Changes usually do not follow depicted meaning | Aligned and misleading inputs disagree in **1,776/10,400 cells (17.1%)**; **37%** of these changes move toward the misleading image’s sense, versus the stated **50%** chance reference. | §3.2 |
| Aggregate agreement conceals changed annotations | Human-majority exact match across thirteen judges is **54.4%** text-only, **53.7%** aligned, and **54.7%** misleading. | §3.2 |
| Substitutability depends on panel and slack | No judge passes against the informed trio; against the blind trio, **two** pass at **ε=0.15**, and **seven** at **ε=0.20**. Passing sets agree under exact-match and ordinal-distance scoring. | §3.2; Appendix H, Table 9 |
| Human image robustness remains unresolved | Aligned-minus-misleading Fleiss κ difference: informed **−0.00 [−0.18, +0.18]**; blind **+0.10 [−0.10, +0.31]**, with 95% bootstrap intervals and 50 items per alignment per trio. | Appendix E.1, Table 5 |

These results establish prediction instability under the tested configurations and show why aggregate agreement alone can miss it. The authors interpret similar aligned/misleading effects as image presence dominating content. **Analyst inference:** this does not isolate a causal mechanism or establish that image content has no effect. A changed prediction violates invariance, but it can correct a previously incorrect prediction; it need not reduce accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Both images concern the target phrase; unrelated images are untested. Evaluation covers English expressions from one corpus, with reasoning modes disabled (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** No human labels the same item twice, so human change rates are unavailable. Human alignment contrasts lack sufficient precision to establish robustness (Appendices B, E.1).
- **Author-stated:** The scale combines phrase-type and sentence-instance judgments, limiting ordinal-shift interpretation. Concentration near scale endpoints also restricts detectable image-following movement (Appendices B, E).
- **Author-stated:** Sensitivity to sentence changes that genuinely alter the correct reading is untested (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** The text-only versus explicit image comparison also introduces an image instruction clause. The fixed-image deletion control helps contextualize prompt sensitivity but does not fully separate image addition from that accompanying prompt change (§3.1; Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference:** Images receive no additional curation beyond source labels, leaving depiction quality as an unmeasured validity risk (Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Do unrelated images cause comparable instability?** Only phrase-relevant images were tested. Add unrelated and blank-image controls using otherwise identical prompts (Appendix B).
2. **How does VLM instability compare with human retest variability?** Human repeated-item measurements are absent. Run a counterbalanced human retest with separated sessions (Appendix B).
3. **Can judges remain invariant while responding correctly to meaningful text changes?** MIST tests only label-preserving perturbations. Pair image swaps with sentence edits that change the intended reading (§4).
4. **Does the effect generalize beyond this task and configuration?** Other languages, annotation tasks, and enabled reasoning modes remain outside the evaluation. Repeat the design across those settings (Appendix B; §4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported descriptive pattern; **medium** in the stronger image-presence interpretation.

Material missing information includes human retest rates, uncertainty estimates for the headline VLM change/agreement rates, independent image validation, repeated-run variability, and results outside English idiom annotation or with reasoning modes enabled.
