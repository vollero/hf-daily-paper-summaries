# SpatialCORE: Confidence-Aware Grounded Spatial Reasoning in Large Vision--Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38716) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38716)


## One-sentence takeaway
SpatialCORE improves spatial multiple-choice accuracy on two benchmarks by post-training a vision–language model with bounding-box rewards that combine localization quality, coordinate-token confidence, and answer correctness.

## Problem and motivation
Correct spatial answers can accompany inaccurate or uncertain object grounding, so answer-only rewards may reinforce weak grounding. The authors identify a gap in methods that reward localization: they do not explicitly account for the model’s confidence when generating grounding within its reasoning trace (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Confidence-aware grounding reward:** Coordinate-token entropy determines confidence weights for generated bounding boxes. Matching scores combine IoU, lexical label similarity, and reference-box validity; Hungarian matching and a coverage term aggregate them (§3.2; §A.4).
- **Answer-gated reinforcement learning:** GRPO combines answer, format, and spatial rewards, retaining reduced grounding credit for incorrect answers (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Practical training pipeline and evaluation:** Qwen3-VL-Thinking variants receive supervised format initialization followed by LoRA-based GRPO. Offline references come from GPT-4o-mini entity extraction and Grounding DINO localization. Ablations test confidence weighting and other components (§4.1; §§A.3–A.4).

The central methodological contribution is confidence weighting within the grounding reward; the backbone, GRPO, and grounding tools are existing components.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher held-out spatial accuracy | OmniSpatial test: SpatialCORE-8B **48.46%**, base **43.90%**, standard GRPO **45.92%**; sample-weighted accuracy over 1,533 questions | Table 1; §A.5 |
| Zero-shot transfer gain | SpatiaLab multiple-choice evaluation: **47.91%**, versus base **44.78%** and GRPO **46.21%**, over 1,400 questions; no SpatiaLab post-training data | Table 2; §A.5 |

| Confidence better tracks reference overlap | Mean matched IoU against pseudo-GT falls from **77%** in SpatialCORE’s lowest-entropy quartile to **36%** in its highest; entropy–IoU correlation changes from **+0.38** for base to **−0.42** | Figure 4; §4.3 |

SpatialCORE-8B has the highest overall accuracy among the listed open-source and specialized models on both benchmarks, but does not lead every category or all proprietary references (Tables 1–2). The matched ablation supports confidence weighting within this training setup. Correlations against pseudo-GT do not establish human-verified calibration or prove that grounding causally determines the answer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Single-view bounding boxes cannot resolve all viewpoint-dependent reasoning; 3D and non-boxable spatial concepts may require depth, multiple views, or richer representations (§4.3; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence spans two benchmarks and one backbone family. It does not establish transfer to other architectures, open-ended answering, or deployed navigation/manipulation (§§4.1–4.2; §A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Grounding analysis uses detector-generated references, while the manual audit assesses retained training boxes. Missing-reference samples receive no spatial reward, creating uneven supervision (§A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** No repeated-run uncertainty or accuracy significance tests are reported. Component ablations cover a subset, and its traffic score differs from the main evaluation; the text identifies separate matched runs (§4.3; Tables 1, 3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The IoU margin is unspecified, and the exact entity-extraction prompt and SFT target-generation procedure are absent. Appendix shaping terms extend the main reward equations, while the attempt-bonus description differs between Table 4 and §A.4.

## Open research questions
- **Does confidence weighting generalize across model families?** Only Qwen3-VL-Thinking variants are tested. Next experiment: matched confidence-on/off training on another backbone with identical data and supervision.
- **Does improved entropy alignment hold against human boxes?** Figure 4 measures overlap with pseudo-GT. Next experiment: evaluate entropy–IoU association and calibration on independently annotated test boxes.
- **Do answers depend on generated grounding?** Qualitative examples and accuracy gains do not isolate causal use. Next experiment: perturb generated boxes while holding image and question fixed, then measure answer changes.
- **Can richer geometry address weak categories?** The authors identify single-view and box-representation limits. Next experiment: compare box-only training with depth or multi-view inputs on perspective and 3D tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and benchmark comparisons, but broader validity and reproducibility remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, full baseline training/evaluation settings, grounding-analysis sample counts, the IoU margin, exact extraction and SFT-generation procedures, checkpoint availability, and human-annotated test grounding results.
