# Not Every Token Is Worth Distilling: Selective Supervision for Direct-OPD

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29142) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29142)


## One-sentence takeaway
Selecting the highest teacher–reference JSD states for Direct-OPD supervision improves mean held-out mathematical reasoning accuracy by 0.95 percentage points across eight teacher–student settings, although training-seed robustness remains untested (Sec. 5.2; App. F).

## Problem and motivation
Direct-OPD transfers a small model’s RL-induced policy shift to a larger student using post-RL versus pre-RL token log-ratios on student-generated prefixes. This offers a route to improving larger models without running RL at their scale.

The authors identify a mismatch: log-ratios measure relative probability changes and can remain large even when both checkpoints assign negligible probability mass to the student’s candidate tokens. Dense supervision therefore need not reflect substantial teacher behavior change (Secs. 1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Theoretical diagnosis:** An exact construction holds candidate log-ratios and their local student-logit update fixed while teacher–reference JSD and both KL directions vanish. A separate bound connects small JSD to small changes in event probabilities (Sec. 4.1; App. B).
- **Selective supervision:** S²D-OPD computes JSD over the student’s top-K candidates plus a residual bucket containing all remaining probability mass. It retains the highest-scoring states within each response, defaulting to 10%, and masks both the reward and student-anchor KL at other states. Existing checkpoint probabilities supply the score without extra forward passes (Sec. 4.2).
- **Controlled utility analysis:** Fixed-budget percentile-bin experiments compare high-divergence, low-divergence, and random supervision; additional experiments vary divergence measure, selection scope, and retention rate (Secs. 5.3–5.4).

| Selective supervision improves aggregate held-out accuracy | Top-10% JSD selection versus dense Direct-OPD across four students and two teacher pairs: mean Avg@32 accuracy increases from 46.36% to 47.31%, or +0.95 pp; 95% CI 0.40–1.54; one-sided sign-flip p = 6.4×10⁻⁴. The evidence supports selective masking over dense Direct-OPD in the tested mathematical reasoning settings. The construction establishes a possible failure mode, not its prevalence or a guarantee that masking improves learning. Bin experiments support supervision utility differences within one teacher–student setting; they do not establish universal causality. Problem-level confidence intervals exclude training-seed variation. No-extra-forward-passes does not establish a measured wall-clock speedup.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** JSD measures change magnitude rather than correctness; it can retain shifts toward errors, as the hand-selected example in Fig. 11 demonstrates (Apps. E–G).
- **Author-stated:** Selection does not directly measure student need beyond the candidate set, potentially retaining already-mastered shifts or discarding useful small shifts (App. G).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers mathematical reasoning, two teacher pairs, and students up to 8B; larger students, code generation, and agentic tasks remain untested (App. G).
- **Author-stated:** Every configuration has one training run; reported uncertainty reflects held-out problems rather than seed variation (Apps. F–G).
- **Analyst inference:** Coarsened JSD cannot reveal redistribution among tail tokens. A low score bounds behavior change at candidate-bucket resolution, not necessarily across the full vocabulary (Secs. 4.2; Eqs. 8–9).
- **Analyst inference:** Because both reward and anchor KL are masked, the comparisons do not separately identify their contributions to the gain; an independent masking ablation is not reported (Sec. 4.2; App. A).

## Open research questions
- **Can selection reject harmful teacher shifts?** Retained errors show that divergence alone is insufficient. Compare JSD masking with JSD plus verifier-based correctness filtering (App. G).
- **Does student-aware selection improve transfer?** The current score does not directly identify unmet student needs. Compare it with a selector combining teacher–reference divergence and student alignment (App. G).
- **How robust are the gains across seeds and domains?** Single runs and mathematics-only evaluation leave this unresolved. Repeat paired training runs and test larger students on code or agentic tasks (Apps. F–G).
- **Which masked objective component drives improvement?** Reward and anchor KL are removed together. Compare reward-only, anchor-only, and joint masking at the same retention budget (Sec. 4.2; analyst-proposed experiment).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the method and reported results; **medium** in generalization and the proposed explanation of gains.

Material information unavailable includes training-seed variability, measured runtime or memory savings, independent reward-versus-anchor masking effects, and results beyond the tested scales and mathematical reasoning domain. The authors report public code, but its repository URL is not recoverable from the supplied text.
