# The Endless Exam: Mathematical Constructions from Today&#x27;s Models toward Superintelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24555) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24555)


## One-sentence takeaway
The Endless Exam distinguishes models using automatically verified, uncapped construction-quality scores across 69 mathematical instances, but evaluated models surpass only construction baselines, never the 30 published-frontier references.

## Problem and motivation
Fixed benchmarks can lose discriminative power when models approach their scoring ceilings. The author proposes mathematical construction tasks whose answers can improve beyond existing references and whose parameters can generate larger challenges. The identified gap is a common evaluation framework that measures both incremental construction quality and improvements beyond published results, without requiring known optima (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Uncapped scoring:** Valid maximisation answers receive objective/reference; minimisation answers receive reference/objective. Invalid answers score zero. The overall score is 100 times mean relative quality; a separate logarithmic “gap closed” metric measures progress toward proven bounds (§4).
- **Expandable benchmark:** Fourteen parameterised families cover sets, geometry, graphs, codes and algebraic designs. The main release contains 69 instances, split between published-frontier references and reproducible construction baselines (§3.2; Table 3).
- **Compact verification:** Products and algebraic certificates permit exact validity and size checks without enumerating enormous objects. Separate mathematical checking implementations validate accepted submissions, although parsing and compact-description expansion are shared (§3.4; Appendix D).
- **Evaluation framework and assets:** Model comparisons, size–quality curves, generators, references, responses and analysis scripts are reported as released. The contribution is primarily a benchmark and measurement framework, rather than a new trained model (§5; reproducibility statement, p. 10).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous scores distinguish models without frontier breakthroughs | Fourteen tool-free configurations on 69 instances score 7.55–91.90 overall; mean relative quality on 30 published-frontier instances ranges from 0.07–0.76, with no reference surpassed. | §5.1; Table 2 |
| Tool-assisted configurations score substantially higher | At high effort, Astra rises from 91.90 to 143.16, Luna from 34.85 to 119.35, and Opus 5.5 from 69.81 to 180.06. Tools include code and web access, with a two-hour limit and no cumulative output-token cap. | §3.5; Table 2; Appendix H.1 |
| Published-frontier parity differs from baseline improvement | Tool-assisted Astra, Luna and Opus match 29, 23 and 23 of 30 published references, surpassing none; they exceed 33, 25 and 33 of 39 construction baselines, respectively. | §5.1; Appendix H.2 |
| Progress toward bounds remains limited | Mean logarithmic gap closed across 64 instances with proven bounds is 6.3% for tool-assisted Astra, 4.7% for Luna and 7.5% for Opus. LABS conjectured targets are excluded. | §5.2; Table 13 |
| A large baseline gain reaches a representation ceiling | On length-64 trifference, tool-assisted Opus constructs 531,441 codewords, scoring 27 against the 19,683-word baseline. This is the format-supported maximum, not a known optimum. | Appendix D.5; Appendix H.2 |
| Larger instances can expose declining relative quality | For maximum-degree-three graphs, Astra’s tool-free mean relative quality falls from 0.89 at diameter 4 to 0.30 at diameter 10, with both responses valid at each endpoint and a 128k-token budget. | Figure 4; Table 6 |
| Verification receives multiple checks | Both mathematical checkers agree on all primary-accepted model constructions and all 69 references; reported invalid-case tests and small-code exhaustive checks also agree. | §3.4; Appendix D.1 |

These results establish discrimination among the evaluated configurations and verified improvements over designated baselines. They do not establish new mathematical records or validate the benchmark as a measure of general superintelligence. Tool-assisted gains combine retrieval, computation and different generation budgets, so they do not isolate any one factor.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Main comparisons use one evaluation per instance and configuration. Instance-bootstrap confidence intervals do not measure run-to-run variability (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Accepted representations and verification budgets restrict evaluable constructions; the trifference format already imposes an attained ceiling (Appendices D.2–D.5).
- **Author-stated:** Construction baselines can be weaker than published constructions, and mathematical bounds need not be attainable (§3.2; §5.2; Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate ranking is sensitive to large uncapped gains: Opus’s advantage over Astra is concentrated in Heilbronn and length-64 trifference, although it remains first when any single family is excluded (Appendix H.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Tool comparisons cannot identify discovery separately from retrieval. Harnesses differ, and benchmark materials were online during Opus evaluation; the reported audit found no direct access (Appendix H.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Shared parsing and expansion leave common failure risks despite independent mathematical checks. Format errors can also obscure valid constructions (Appendices C.11 and D.1).

## Open research questions
1. **How stable are model rankings across repeated runs?** Main results lack repeated sampling (§6). Repeat every configuration under fixed budgets and estimate variability across runs.
2. **How much improvement comes from retrieval versus computational search?** Both are enabled together with changed budgets (Appendix H). Compare code-only, web-only and combined access under matched resource limits.
3. **Can broader certificates prevent representation saturation?** Opus reaches the length-64 format ceiling (Appendix D.5). Extend certificate formats and reevaluate that instance with the original reference retained.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this text-based summary; reported experiments and releases were not independently verified.

Material information unavailable includes repeated-run uncertainty for the main comparison, matched-budget tool ablations, complete token usage for some evaluations, repository contents beyond their description, and the unknown mathematical optima.
