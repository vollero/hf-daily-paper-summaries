# TERRA: Terrain-Aware Reconstruction, Retargeting and Control for Musculoskeletal Locomotion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38653) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38653)


## One-sentence takeaway
TERRA reconstructs static support terrain from motion kinematics and retargets it to a muscle-actuated body, achieving the highest aggregate held-out tracking completion among tested pipelines, with terrain-specific exceptions (Tables II–III, VI).

## Problem and motivation
Musculoskeletal motion imitation remains largely restricted to flat ground because motion datasets often lack aligned scene geometry, while retargeting must preserve contacts and satisfy anatomical and musculotendon constraints. Non-flat terrain amplifies penetration, skating, and floating errors that can make reference motions infeasible. The authors address both missing terrain and target-body compatibility within one pipeline (Sec. I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Motion-derived terrain reconstruction:** Stationary foot contacts, calibrated foot orientation, swing clearance, and body free-space evidence select and fit ramps, staircases, independent supports, and seats. The output is task-relevant static collision geometry rather than a complete scene (Sec. III-B).
- **Musculoskeletal retargeting:** TERRA adapts OmniRetarget’s interaction mesh, adding anatomical and terrain-interaction terms for orientation, stance anchoring, leg separation, swing clearance, and seated contact. Sequential quadratic programming enforces joint bounds and environment separation, followed by bounded repairs for collision outliers and tendon discontinuities (Sec. III-C).
- **Integrated control and evaluation:** A single reference-conditioned PPO policy controls MyoFullBody using reconstructed motion–terrain pairs from five sources, forming a 9.4-hour library. Evaluation covers geometry, reference validity, held-out tracking, and qualitative physiological profiles; code and data are promised for public release (Secs. I, III-D, IV).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Physical cues improve terrain-family selection | Across 5,474 known-terrain clips, accuracy is 99.9% versus 73.5% without foot-orientation and swing-clearance cues. Full-method subject-clustered 95% CI: 99.853–99.982%. | Table II(a); Sec. V-A |
| Reconstruction matches measured terrain | TERRA stair-riser MAE: 8.08 ± 6.99 mm on Gait120 and 5.17 ± 4.36 mm on Darmstadt; ramp-grade MAE: 0.223 ± 0.244° on Gait120 and 0.536 ± 0.353° on Vielemeyer. Entries are per-motion mean ± population SD. | Table II(a) |
| PRISM geometry improves over both baselines | On 31 motions with reference meshes, combined-support height MAE is 12.20 ± 6.36 mm, versus 26.29 ± 13.15 mm for Voronoi and 145.10 ± 57.89 mm for contact least squares. Raised-footprint IoU is 47.3 ± 5.5%. | Table II(b) |
| Retargeting residuals improve physical validity | Across 6,498 inputs, full TERRA versus no residuals reduces environment-penetration interval percentage from 33.45 ± 33.63% to 0.16 ± 2.46%, and increases contact F1 from 88.49 ± 9.51% to 93.80 ± 4.06%. Both converge on every input. | Table III; Sec. IV |
| Aggregate completion improves, with an error trade-off | On identity-held-out motions successfully retargeted by every method, TERRA achieves 87.59 ± 0.18% completion versus 80.29 ± 1.39% for OmniRetarget and 82.16 ± 0.41% for TERRA+Voronoi. MPJPE is 74.47 ± 1.51 mm versus 71.65 ± 1.00 mm for TERRA+Voronoi. Evaluation uses three training seeds, five runs per clip, and a 0.25 m termination bound. | Table III; Sec. IV |
| Terrain-specific performance remains uneven | TERRA box completion is 46.45 ± 4.45% on 61 clips. On 422 stair clips, TERRA+Voronoi achieves 85.01 ± 0.24%, exceeding TERRA’s 81.90 ± 1.73%. | Table VI |
| Missing support evidence hurts reconstruction | Removing 30% of support intervals reduces pooled family accuracy from 99.93% to 90.04%; stool accuracy falls from 100.00% to 70.36%. | Table V |
| Physiological agreement is qualitative | Independently min–max-normalized EMG and vertical-GRF profiles show similarities and visible mismatches across selected held-out conditions. | Fig. 5; Secs. IV, V-D |

The evidence supports improved reconstruction, reference validity, and aggregate simulated tracking within the tested setting. It does not establish complete scene recovery, uniformly superior performance across terrains, or physiological fidelity in absolute signal magnitude. AMASS results measure motion consistency without ground-truth geometry (Sec. IV).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Dynamic geometry, online retargeting, and moving surfaces remain open challenges (Sec. VI).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** The Voronoi baseline is adapted from TIP and SceneBot because SceneBot code was unavailable; this is not a direct comparison with SceneBot’s implementation (Sec. II).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation conditioning:** Tracking uses only motions successfully retargeted by every method, and MPJPE covers executed steps. These choices limit conclusions about full-input coverage and error over complete trajectories (Sec. IV).
- **Analyst inference—physiological validity:** Selected, independently normalized profiles and retention of completed gaits from subsequently failed episodes provide limited evidence about population-wide physiology or failure behavior (Sec. IV).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** Sec. V-C claims the lowest flat-ground MPJPE for TERRA, but Table VI reports 70.01 mm for TERRA versus 69.97 mm for TERRA+Voronoi.
- **Reproducibility:** Code/data availability is only promised; motion selection and some details are deferred to a supplementary video absent from the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Which constraints drive the retargeting gains?** The paper ablates residuals collectively. A next experiment could remove individual residual groups under identical training and evaluation settings.
- **Why do boxes remain difficult and Voronoi references perform better on stairs?** Table VI establishes these gaps without diagnosing them. A next experiment could compare reconstructed versus ground-truth terrain and categorize tracking failures.
- **How well does terrain inference transfer beyond the declared priors?** Evaluation covers static supported families. A next experiment could test irregular or moving supports with measured geometry and contact annotations.
- **Does physiological agreement extend beyond normalized shape?** Figure 5 does not quantify absolute agreement. A next experiment could evaluate calibrated amplitudes and timing errors across all held-out subjects and muscles.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported pipeline and tabulated results; physiological conclusions remain limited.

Material missing information includes accessible release artifacts, supplementary motion-selection details, per-residual ablations, quantitative physiological agreement, explicit definitions of tracking-table uncertainty, and reconstruction/retargeting runtime.
