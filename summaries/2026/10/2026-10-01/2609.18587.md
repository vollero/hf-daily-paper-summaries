# Label-free steering: Compressing test-time reinforcement learning into bias-only subspaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18587) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18587)


## One-sentence takeaway
Majority-vote rewards can train a frozen model’s small additive-bias subspace to improve benchmark accuracy and transfer within mathematics, although the reported audio gain is largely concentrated in a questionable counting template (§§6.1–6.3; Appendix C).

## Problem and motivation
Test-time reinforcement learning adapts models using their own generations, avoiding labeled training data but typically updating many parameters. The authors ask whether self-generated rewards remain useful under a severe optimization bottleneck—and whether the choice of trainable subspace matters beyond its size (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Combined adaptation method:** Sample reasoning rollouts, extract their majority answer, reward agreement, and use GRPO to update only additive decoder-MLP biases. The backbone stays frozen; the main configuration trains 100,352 parameters and retains the learned biases as a fixed inference intervention (§§3–4; Table 4).
- **Empirical study of restricted adaptation:** Compare bias-only training with parameter-matched LoRA and bias-free ReFT, larger LoRA adapters, full fine-tuning, and inference-only self-consistency. Evaluate text, vision-language, audio, and held-out mathematics (§6; Appendix B).
- **Explanatory analysis:** Provide a conditional majority-vote reliability bound and a local projected-gradient improvement bound, then examine rollout reliability and equal-sized single-layer subspaces empirically (§4.2; §§6.4–6.5; Appendix F).

The constituent reward, optimizer, and bias intervention come from prior work; the contribution is their combination and investigation under label-free, restricted adaptation (§2).

## Evidence and main results

Trained headline results below report mean ± standard deviation over three seeds, with final-step evaluation and greedy pass@1 unless stated otherwise (§§5–6; Table 4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong mathematical adaptation with few trainable parameters | MATH-500 accuracy: Qwen2.5-7B **45.9% → 76.67 ± 1.57%**; Math-7B **56.0% → 79.50 ± 0.70%**, using 100,352 biases. Pseudo-label full fine-tuning reaches **77.00 ± 0.53%** and **83.80 ± 0.28%**, respectively. | Table 1; §4.3 |
| Bias subspace beats matched LoRA across evaluated settings | Bias-only accuracy exceeds exact-match LoRA on all six model–benchmark settings. It exceeds inference-only majority voting on all six, but full fine-tuning exceeds it on five. | Table 1; §6.1 |
| Labels do not improve matched bias-only means | On MATH-500, labeled bias-only accuracy is **75.47 ± 1.21%** for Qwen2.5-7B and **77.27 ± 0.70%** for Math-7B, versus pseudo-label results above. Recipe is matched within each parameterization. | Table 2 |
| Lower measured training compute | Bias-only: **4.45–4.95 GH200 GPU-hours/run** on one GPU; full fine-tuning: **79.24–82.12 GPU-hours/run** on four GPUs, a reported **16.0–18.5×** reduction for the mathematical comparison. | §6.2 |
| Transfer beyond adaptation inputs | Frozen MATH-500-trained biases evaluated on **4,500 verified-disjoint MATH problems** improve accuracy **46.3% → 70.9%** for Qwen2.5-7B and **52.5% → 75.4%** for Math-7B, without further training. | Table 3; §6.3 |

| Audio gain has a major template caveat | MMAU headline accuracy: **57.40% → 60.87 ± 0.37%**. | Reliability and trainability diagnostics support qualitative predictions | On **64 MATH-500 problems**, pseudo-label accuracy rises **40.6% → 79.7%** as rollout count increases **1 → 64**. The evidence supports useful adaptation in the tested configurations and within-domain mathematical transfer. It does not establish general superiority over full fine-tuning or labeled steering. The reliability experiment supports a qualitative trend, rather than demonstrating the theoretical exponential rate; gradient-energy correlation does not establish causation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated risks:** Self-generated rewards can reinforce existing errors. MathVista exhibits a fixed-zero answer pattern on age-gap questions; the MMAU audit cannot attribute its dominant template gain to genuine phonetic reasoning (Ethics Statement; Appendix C).
- **Evaluation confounds:** AI2D gains partly reflect improved parser compliance. MMAU audio was not directly verified during the qualitative audit; inspection used textual consistency and grading-code checks (§6.6; Table 6).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Main benchmark inputs also serve as adaptation data. Held-out transfer addresses this concern for mathematics, but equivalent multimodal transfer is not reported (§5; Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—theoretical limits:** Majority-vote reliability assumes the correct answer is already the unique mode. The subspace bound concerns a locally smooth objective and projected-gradient step, rather than a guarantee for the complete stochastic AdamW training trajectory (Appendix F).
- **Comparison limits:** The separately reproduced labeled vector uses different data and optimizer and outperforms the label-free vector under its matched evaluation harness. It cannot isolate reward supervision; the authors explicitly reject a general superiority claim (Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **Can adaptation overcome confidently wrong consensus?** The reliability bound excludes nonpositive correct-answer margins. Next experiment: stratify problems by estimated answer margin and track corrections and reinforced errors during training.
- **Can gradient energy guide subspace selection?** Evidence is correlational across a small layer sweep. Next experiment: select layers by initial gradient energy and compare against random and fixed-depth selections under matched budgets.
- **Do multimodal improvements transfer beyond adaptation inputs?** Only mathematics has a reported disjoint transfer evaluation. Next experiment: freeze multimodal biases and evaluate new problems, separating parser compliance from answer accuracy.
- **Can audio gains survive template controls?** The main improvement concentrates on phoneme-counting questions. Next experiment: evaluate new audio examples with balanced counting answers and verify outputs against independently checked phonetic annotations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains substantial comparative evidence and unusually explicit audits, but broad multimodal reasoning claims require qualification.

Material missing or ambiguous information includes transfer-result seed variability; exact per-run rollout counts and context budgets; full LoRA and full-fine-tuning configurations; hyperparameter-selection criteria and whether labels informed them; and uncertainty for audit breakdowns. Appendix A gives configuration summaries, but does not fully supply every detail claimed in the reproducibility statement.
