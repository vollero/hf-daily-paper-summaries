# Systematically Exploring the Capabilities of GPT-6 Astra as Embodied Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38537) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38537)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across six embodied domains, Astra makes useful navigation and policy-correction decisions, but reliable contact coordination, dense locomotion control, and practical inference speed remain unresolved in the evaluated systems.

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether Astra can function as a numerical robot policy beyond high-level planning. Existing demonstrations do not systematically establish which tasks it completes, which capabilities come from supporting controllers, or how much inference successful control requires. The paper evaluates successes, failures, feedback-driven adaptation, and resource costs together (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Cross-domain capability assessment:** Evaluate gripper, dexterous, and mobile manipulation; navigation; locomotion; and humanoid loco-manipulation, preserving each study’s native metrics and protocol (§2, Table 1).
- **Explicit division of control:** Compare **Direct** Astra commands executed through analytic controllers with **Hybrid** systems that review learned-policy proposals or command frozen whole-body controllers. These are system evaluations with fixed Astra weights, rather than a newly trained general-purpose policy (§2.1, Figure 1).
- **Control and adaptation diagnostics:** Analyze contact failures, completion verification, written-guidance adaptation, controller calibration, and inference demands. Separate analytic locomotion tests examine interface feasibility without Astra (§§4, 7–9).

The evidence supports useful capabilities of the **complete evaluated systems**, especially navigation and selective manipulation correction. It does not isolate Astra’s contribution from controller quality, interfaces, guidance, or feedback management. Local subset rankings do not establish full-benchmark superiority, and experience-guided sequences do not estimate independent-trial reliability.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated control failures:** Grasp acquisition, changing finger contacts, maintaining contact between task stages, completion checking, and retaining successful guidance remain unreliable (§§4.4, 5.3, 8.5, 9).
- **Author-stated comparison boundaries:** In-hand controllers differ in observations and action rates; navigation systems differ in cameras, history processing, and adapters; HumanoidBench uses published reference results (§4.3, Table 8, Appendix E).
- **Author-stated sampling boundaries:** RoboDojo emphasizes tasks with low published π0.5 performance; navigation uses balanced subsets; locomotion evaluates one adaptation trajectory on one course (§3.2, Appendix I.1, §7.1).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** RoboCasa jointly changes policy access, context management, and feedback guards, so its results cannot isolate the benefit of proposal review or subgoal rewriting. Several development studies likewise change multiple components together (§5.2, Appendices F, I.4).
- **Analyst inference—ranking and reliability:** RoboLab retries complicate interpretation as uniform first-attempt performance. Small task samples, two-seed humanoid estimates, and cuboid initialization overlap limit broad generalization (Appendices B.2, E, H.1).
- **Analyst inference—deployment validity:** Paused-physics evaluations leave performance under continuous physical change unresolved (§9.4). Averaging differently scaled humanoid returns also requires caution; Figure 12 explicitly uses task-specific scales.

## Open research questions
- **When should Astra intervene?** Assistance sometimes loses standalone-policy successes (§5.3). Next experiment: compare fixed intervention rules and learned confidence gates on identical starts, holding the harness and review budget constant.
- **Can direct control sustain contact changes?** Rotation often fails despite object retention (§4.3). Next experiment: compare joint increments with contact-aware skills under matched observations and decision cadence.
- **Does written adaptation generalize and retain success?** Reflection can turn a successful handover into failure (§8.5). Next experiment: freeze each guidance revision and evaluate repeated runs plus held-out scenes.
- **Can the system operate without paused physics?** Recorded latency exceeds locomotion execution intervals (§9.4). Next experiment: evaluate asynchronous control with measured delays and continuously advancing simulation.
