# The Geometry of Inference in Transformer Residual Streams

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37824) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37824)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across six pretrained language models, residual states favor their eventual endpoint early but become more selective through changing geometric comparisons, while endpoint anisotropy helps predict competition along held-out trajectories.

## Problem and motivation
Intermediate states can contain information about eventual predictions, but this does not establish how selectively they distinguish their own final representation from alternatives. The authors address this gap by comparing each residual trajectory against a fixed population of final states from other contexts, separating average endpoint preference from individual competitors (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Endpoint-based probe:** An alternative is a competitor when it is closer to the current residual state than the trajectory’s own endpoint. Euclidean and cosine distances yield preference margins, competitor fractions, retrieval ranks, and membership turnover (§2).
- **Geometric theory:** Margin-update identities explain simultaneous entries and exits. A proof establishes nested competitor sets under monotone straight-line convergence; high-dimensional constructions separate norm, alignment, competitive mass, and set cohesion (§3; Appendices A–B).
- **Held-out endpoint modeling:** Five endpoint-distribution families are fitted using final states alone, then evaluated for prediction of mean competition along fixed, unseen residual trajectories. This tests population geometry without fitting competitor curves (§3; Appendix C).

Evaluation uses Gemma-2B/7B, Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B, Mistral-7B, and Llama-3-8B, each with 1,024 non-overlapping 256-token FineWeb contexts and 1,023 primary alternatives per query (§2.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Average preference appears before isolation | All six models pass an unadjusted one-sided document-level test at \(p<10^{-3}\) at the first measured post-block layer under both metrics; many alternatives remain closer. | §2.2; Figures 3, 7–8; Appendix E |
| Competition declines with changing membership | Cosine sets exhibit entries and exits while mean counts decline; Euclidean sets are closer to nested over much of depth. Straight-line convergence permits no entries under either metric. | §2.3; Figures 4, 14; Appendix A.1 |
| Directional progress can accompany flat Euclidean distance | Intermediate-layer patterns are reported especially for Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B; a norm–alignment construction establishes mathematical compatibility. | §§2.3–3; Appendix A.2 |
| Cosine endpoint distance tracks output-token rank | Across rank-conditioned mean-distance curves, reported Spearman correlations range from 0.726 to 0.982 across the six models, with positive one-sided trends. Empty token groups are excluded. | Figure 2; Appendix H.3 |
| Token identity contributes to early geometry | With 39 alternatives per matched bank, first-layer cosine competitor fractions are 0.364 versus 0.058 for same- versus different-token Qwen2.5-1.5B contexts, and 0.253 versus 0.059 for Gemma-7B. | Appendix F.2 |
| Structured endpoint models improve cosine prediction | Projected normal has the lowest synthetic point estimate in five models; the mixture wins narrowly for Gemma-7B. For Qwen2.5-7B, errors are 0.139 versus 1.657 dex for projected normal versus sphere. Evaluation uses held-out documents, eight fixed banks of 256 alternatives, preterminal layers, and a \(10^{-3}\) logarithmic offset. | Figure 5; Table 3; Appendix C.7 |
| Superiority is qualified by uncertainty and metric | Paired document bootstraps favor projected normal over the mixture only for Gemma-2B and both Qwen models. Euclidean prediction identifies no consistently preferred family. | Appendix K; Tables 4–5 |

These results support the authors’ claim of increasing **geometric specificity within sampled endpoint banks**. They do not establish calibrated token probabilities, an explicit internal search process, or a causal role for competitor turnover. Fitted distributions predict competition conditional on observed trajectories; they do not predict the residual updates themselves (§4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Competition depends on bank and metric, and vanishes at the own endpoint by construction. Endpoint geometry explains only part of output variation (§4).
- **Author-stated:** Entries exclude monotone straight-line convergence but do not distinguish curvature from backtracking; near-tie entries depend on numerical precision (Appendix A.1).
- **Author-stated:** Bootstrap intervals condition on fitted distributions and sampled banks, omitting refitting uncertainty. Rank-conditioned averages can use different query subsets, and layerwise preference tests lack multiple-testing correction (Appendices C.7, E, H.3).
- **Author-stated:** All fitted families assume independent endpoint norm and direction; some complexity optima reach search boundaries (Appendices C.4, I.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond these base models, FineWeb contexts, and the final input position remains untested by the supplied evaluation (Appendix C.1).
- **Reproducibility:** Extraction code, residual arrays, fitted models, and seeds are absent. Alternative-endpoint projection, projected-normal sampling, bank sampling, and rank-trend testing retain unresolved implementation details (Appendix L).

## Open research questions
1. **Does geometric turnover affect predictions causally?** Observational geometry does not establish function (§4). Intervene near competitor-margin crossings and measure changes in output distributions.
2. **What determines refinement timing?** Endpoint models leave update origins unexplained (§4). Track geometry and trajectories across training checkpoints.
3. **Does specificity transfer across domains and positions?** Evaluation is restricted to one corpus and final input positions (Appendix C.1). Repeat the probe on other corpora, context lengths, and token positions.
4. **Can joint norm–direction models improve Euclidean prediction?** Current families assume independence and yield inconsistent Euclidean rankings (Appendix K.1). Compare joint models with the existing families on matched held-out splits.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive findings and their stated scope; causal interpretation remains unresolved.

Material missing information includes executable analysis code, residual data, fitted assets, random seeds, resolved implementation conventions, and numerical intervals for paired fitted-family error differences (Appendices K–L).
