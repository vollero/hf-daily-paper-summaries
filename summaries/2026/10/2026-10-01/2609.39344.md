# Who Said What, and Will It Be Remembered? Evaluating Persistent Speaker Attribution Across Meetings

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39344) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39344)


## One-sentence takeaway
Corpus-global speaker scoring changes the evaluated commercial ranking: ThyVoice achieves the lowest mean persistent-attribution error, although SE-DiCoW leads on both NOTSOFAR conditions and real multi-day identity stability remains untested (§5.1; §7).

## Problem and motivation
Conversation memory needs to preserve both words and who spoke them across recordings. Per-recording speaker metrics can independently remap anonymous labels, hiding identity fragmentation or incorrect merging across meetings. The authors argue that these errors can attach commitments or preferences to the wrong person, but they do not measure downstream memory harm (§1; §6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent-attribution evaluation:** The authors adopt SI-cpWER, using one corpus-global, one-to-one speaker assignment. Word-edit costs are computed within recordings and summed before Hungarian assignment; recording boundaries remain intact. This evaluates online-discovered identities without a pre-enrolled roster (§3.1).
- **Layered benchmark:** Eight systems are evaluated on clean and noise-augmented NOTSOFAR plus CHiME-6. Supporting metrics distinguish lexical errors, local diarization, per-recording attribution, and persistent identity failures (§§3.2–3.4).
- **ThyVoice reference implementation:** Existing diarization, separation, embedding, and recognition components are combined with overlap repair, stream rematching, pooled speaker audio, and separately gated identity matching and updates. Its contribution is this system design and evaluation; the text does not establish new component models (§4).

These results establish ranking changes under the reported protocol and support separating transcription audio from identity evidence. They do not establish statistically reliable superiority across the whole panel, identify profile drift as the specific cause of errors, or demonstrate better downstream retrieval. SI-cpWER also includes recognition and attribution errors; it is not a pure speaker-identification metric (§§3.1, 5.3, 6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Persistence uses a fixed serial sequence rather than genuine recurrence across days; recording-order robustness and long-term calibration remain untested (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers English single-channel audio, deterministic noise augmentation, and only two CHiME-6 sessions. Multilingual, device, and environmental generalization remain unestablished (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Diagnostic interfaces differ. Reconstructed word turns and native activity timelines are not equivalent, and ordinary WER can penalize alternative overlap serializations (§7; Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Ablations cover a small fixed subset; provider enrollment contrasts are unpaired single runs, and individual ThyVoice gates are not isolated (§3.2; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** ThyVoice differs from comparison cascades in several components and its identity backend, so headline gains cannot be attributed solely to evidence gating (§§3.3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** ThyVoice’s clean score falls inside ElevenLabs’s observed repeat range; the reported mean lead should not be read as an established significant difference (Tables 2, 9).

## Open research questions
- **Does identity remain stable over days and changing recording order?** Fixed-order simulation leaves this open. Collect recurring multi-day recordings and repeat evaluation under several arrival orders (§7).
- **Which gates produce the benefit?** Existing ablations do not isolate matching, update, or stream-rejection gates. Disable each separately while holding components and input order fixed (Appendix F).
- **Do attribution gains improve conversation memory?** Downstream harm is discussed but unmeasured. Evaluate speaker-linked retrieval, commitments, and action-item ownership from each system’s transcripts (§6; §8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol and numerical comparisons; **medium** in generalization and causal interpretation.

Material missing information includes public code or artifact availability, panel-wide repeated runs and uncertainty estimates, recording-order experiments, isolated gate ablations, real multi-day evaluation, and downstream memory-task results.
