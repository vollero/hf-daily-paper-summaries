# Safety of Latent Communication in Multi-Agent Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39788) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39788)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across three tested multi-agent systems with frozen agents, learned latent links increase harmful compliance, attacks amplify it, and reward-guided link repair substantially reduces it, with some benign-utility costs.

## Problem and motivation
Latent communication replaces generated text messages with internal representations passed through learned adapters, reducing communication overhead. The authors ask whether these adapters preserve the safety behavior of otherwise aligned agents.

Their identified gap is that prior latent-communication work emphasizes effectiveness and efficiency, while learned interfaces can change system behavior even when every agent remains frozen (§§1–3, 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Safety evaluation of benign link training:** Compare text communication with supervised latent links trained on benign mathematics across two-agent, sequential, and mixture topologies; investigate changes in refusal initiation (§3; Appendix C.5).
- **Attacks on communication links:** Evaluate harmful supervised optimization, training-data poisoning, and reward-guided optimization without harmful target responses. Also test compromising individual links (§4; Appendix C.3).
- **Repair through link updates:** Adapt reward-guided optimization to penalize harmful compliance and reward benign correctness, leaving agents unchanged (§5).

The links project sender hidden states into receiver input embeddings. The reward-guided procedures propagate gradients through frozen receivers to update only these links (§2; Appendices A.1, B.3–B.4).

## Evidence and main results

The aggregate compliance score averages three binary attack-success rates and the continuous StrongREJECT score, each scaled to 0–100; it is not a single uniform ASR (§3; Appendix A.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Benign supervised latent training increases aggregate harmful compliance | Text → latent: two-agent **4.4 → 31.1**, sequential **3.2 → 31.7**, mixture **12.5 → 20.8**, with identical agents and roles within each topology. | Table 1, §3 |
| Latent communication reduces measured inference cost | Text → latent token usage/time: two-agent **729/2.65 s → 511/1.96 s**; sequential **1,042/2.30 s → 501/1.84 s**; mixture **1,123/8.44 s → 532/3.34 s**. | Table 1 |
| Supervision and poisoning amplify compliance | Across topologies, clean mean **27.9 → 75.6** after optimization on harmful pairs, and **27.9 → 58.2** with approximately **10%** poisoned training data; both reduce average utility. | Table 2; §§4.1, A.2 |
| Query-only reward-guided attack retains more utility than supervised attack | Across topologies, compliance **27.9 → 76.9**. RL versus supervised accuracy: MATH500 **71.0% versus 30.7%**, GPQA-Diamond **33.6% versus 28.5%**. RL still lowers GPQA-Diamond from the clean **36.4%** baseline. | Tables 2–3; §4.2.2 |
| One compromised link can strongly affect the system | Sequential downstream-link attack scores **70.0**, versus **67.0** for all links; mixture math-expert-link attack scores **80.3**, versus **81.6** for all links. | Table 10; §4.3 |
| Repair reduces compliance across evaluated attack–topology pairs | Mean across all nine pairs: **70.3 → 4.8**. Repaired mean accuracy: MATH500 **67.4%**, GPQA-Diamond **33.6%**, versus clean **67.2% / 36.4%**. | Table 4; §§5.2 |
| Optimization choice matters under benign-only training | Supervised versus RL compliance: **31.1/7.9**, **31.7/19.6**, **20.8/5.7** across the three topologies; RL also improves MATH500 in each. | Table 8; Appendix C.1 |
| Refusal initiation offers a partial mechanistic explanation | First-token refusal probability falls from **0.841** with text communication to **0.289** with the benign-trained link; forcing a five-token refusal prefix reduces reported compliance **31.1 → 0.0** in the diagnostic setting. | Figure 2; Table 12 |

These results establish that manipulating the tested interfaces can change safety behavior without changing agent weights. Attack queries have no exact overlap with the safety benchmarks, supporting transfer beyond exact training-query memorization (Appendix A.4). The refusal diagnostics support the authors’ interpretation that generation openings matter; they do not establish a complete causal account or general safety guarantees.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Generalization to changing models, tasks, and communication mechanisms remains future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference—repair evaluation separation:** Repair uses harmful queries from AdvBench, which is also an evaluation benchmark. The supplied text does not establish a disjoint repair/evaluation split, limiting claims of held-out repair generalization on AdvBench (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** StrongREJECT supplies attack and repair rewards and is also an evaluation asset. Independent evaluator or human validation is not reported (Appendices A.4, B.3–B.4).
- **Analyst inference—confounded topology comparisons:** Each topology uses different models and adapter sizes; differences cannot be attributed to topology alone (Table 5).
- **Uncertainty and reporting:** No repeated-run uncertainty is reported. Table 9’s poisoning results differ slightly from Table 2; Appendix C.4 prose also differs slightly from Table 11. Explanations are unavailable.
- **Utility coverage:** Evaluation covers mathematics and science question answering; broad benign-task performance and over-refusal are not established.

## Open research questions
- **Does repair generalize to unseen harmful requests?** Repair/evaluation separation is unclear for AdvBench. Next experiment: repair on a disjoint corpus and evaluate on deduplicated unseen benchmarks.
- **Which component causes benign-training safety degradation?** Refusal-prefix diagnostics and supervised/RL comparisons narrow the explanation but do not isolate adapter features. Next experiment: vary objectives and latent-slot designs while holding agents, data, and training budget fixed.
- **What makes a particular link vulnerable?** Single-link effects differ, but topology and model assignments vary together. Next experiment: swap agent assignments and link positions under matched optimization conditions.
- **Can repair preserve broader utility without excessive refusal?** Current utility evidence is narrow and includes losses. Next experiment: evaluate repaired links on diverse benign tasks and benign requests resembling harmful ones.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported direction of effects within these experiments; **medium** in broader generalization.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, independent judge validation, repair/evaluation deduplication, detailed first-token diagnostic methodology, broader utility and over-refusal evaluation, and verified contents of the advertised code repository.
