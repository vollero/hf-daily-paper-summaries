# AdviSD: Learning to Advise Frontier LLMs via Targeted Multi-Turn Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38142) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38142)


## One-sentence takeaway
AdviSD trains a small advisor for a frozen executor by combining GRPO with selectively applied feedback-conditioned self-distillation, improving reported tool-use performance while using a predictive selection score that does not measure causal influence.

## Problem and motivation
A trainable advisor can adapt an API-served language model through advice before each response. Episode rewards provide limited guidance about which advice decisions need correction, while reflection can propose changes that the executor would not benefit from.

The authors identify a gap between improving agreement with a feedback-conditioned teacher and improving execution: redundant corrections can still change shared advisor parameters and affect useful advice elsewhere (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Targeted advisor self-distillation.** Reflection flags decisions in imperfect episodes. The pre-update advisor scores the same recorded executor response with and without issued advice; decisions exceeding a calibrated absolute score-difference threshold receive supervision. Flagged original abstentions bypass this gate because their two scoring contexts are identical (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Combined training objective.** A feedback-conditioned copy of the pre-update advisor teaches the student along its originally sampled advice prefixes using reverse KL on fixed token supports. GRPO continues using every episode and unchanged advantages. Reflection and selection operate only during training; selection requires neither executor likelihoods nor additional executor rollouts (§5.3; Algorithm 1).
- **Conditional theoretical explanation and empirical controls.** A shared-parameter model shows that preferentially removing corrections with weaker teacher targets can raise eventual performance under explicit stationarity assumptions. No-gate, inverted-gate, matched-count random, and abstention-bypass ablations test the implemented selector (§§4, 6–7).

## Evidence and main results

Reported trained-method uncertainty is sample SD across three run averages, each averaging four test evaluations; it is not a confidence interval (§7; Appendix E).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher BFCL-v3 accuracy than GRPO | On 320 held-out tasks, equal-weight category accuracy: Gemini **67.8±1.6% vs 61.4±1.0%**; Claude **63.1±0.6% vs 58.9±0.7%**—gains of **6.4 and 4.2 percentage points**. | Table 1 |
| Higher EnvScaler score than GRPO | On 200 test tasks, mean native fractional score ×100: Gemini **84.8±1.6 vs 79.7±2.2**; Claude **84.6±1.1 vs 80.7±2.3**—gains of **5.1 and 3.9 score points**. | Table 1 |
| Targeted selection beats random quotas | Across both benchmarks and executors, AdviSD exceeds matched-count random selection by **2.5–4.9 points**, in each benchmark’s reported units. Quotas match a shadow gate on the random arm’s own episodes. | Table 2; Appendix E |
| Abstention supervision contributes | Removing the abstention bypass lowers BFCL accuracy or EnvScaler score by **0.6–1.7 points** across the four settings. | Table 2 |
| OOD aggregate improvements | BFCL-selected advisors, without retraining: four-benchmark macro-average **77.2 vs 74.5** for Gemini and **74.0 vs 70.4** for Claude versus standalone execution. This index averages different native percentage metrics. | Table 3; Appendix E |
| Cross-family transfer | BFCL-v3 accuracy without retraining: Gemini-trained advisor on Claude **61.2% vs transferred GRPO 58.1%**; Claude-trained advisor on Gemini **63.3% vs 60.2%**. | Figure 2 |
| Selection can improve a theoretical learning limit | Preferential retention raises limiting expected success when insensitive corrections have weaker teacher targets, under the specified shared-logit, stationary model. | Theorems 1–2; Appendix C |

The results support targeted supervision over the tested alternatives and demonstrate transfer in the evaluated settings. They do not establish that the score identifies execution-sensitive decisions or estimates counterfactual returns. Improvements are not universal: on Claude ACEBench multi-step tasks, AdviSD scores **85.0%**, versus **86.7%** for both standalone execution and GRPO (Table 3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical scope:** Fixed or stationary conditional teacher laws and simplified shared parameters do not establish convergence for AdviSD’s evolving teacher and GRPO–AdamW updates. The assumed ordering of teacher targets does not follow from execution insensitivity (Appendices C, E).
- **Author-stated selector limitation:** The response was observed with advice; removing advice changes only its scoring context. Predictor error can affect contrast magnitude, and donor calibration controls only pilot-sample exceedances (Appendix D).
- **Comparison boundaries:** SDPO and DistIL omit GRPO and differ in teacher construction and objectives; GEPA is not compute-matched. Selection ablations provide the more controlled comparisons (Appendix E).
- **Evaluation scope:** Two executor families, one reflector, and three training runs limit generality. API updates constrain exact reproducibility; OOD macro-averages have no uncertainty estimate (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Net efficiency remains unresolved because the supplied text reports no aggregate latency or cost comparison. The selected qualitative example adds advisor inference and is not faster (Appendix G).

## Open research questions
- **Does contrast predict behavioral influence?** The paper explicitly distinguishes prediction changes from causal effects. Replay selected states with and without advice, measuring executor behavior and task return.
- **Does calibration remain useful as training evolves?** The threshold stays fixed, and declining retention could reflect score-scale changes. Compare fixed and periodically recalibrated thresholds under matched supervision budgets.
- **How dependent are gains on the reflector?** Both executor settings share one reflector. Repeat the selection ablations with different reflectors and controlled feedback perturbations.
- **When does advising justify its overhead?** Aggregate efficiency is unreported. Measure accuracy, latency, tokens, and API cost while varying advice frequency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and comparisons; **medium** in broader generalization and the proposed mechanistic explanation.
