# DAGent: Evaluate-then-Grow Planning for Deep Research Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39154) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39154)


## One-sentence takeaway
DAGent grows research task graphs from completed-node evidence and improves benchmark accuracy over upfront planning with reactive edits, while its topology-aware RL adds gains over same-budget outcome-only training.

## Problem and motivation
Deep research requires plans to change as evidence emerges. The authors argue that existing DAG agents commit a task-covering graph before execution, then patch it, risking premature assumptions and wasted branches. They also identify a training gap: outcome-only rewards treat all Executor rollouts alike, regardless of whether their evidence reaches final synthesis (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Incremental planning:** An Orchestrator evaluates completed nodes as Success, Uncertain, or Not Found, then appends the next batch. Weak nodes trigger alternative-search refinements within a three-attempt limit. Nodes are never deleted or rewired (§3.1).
- **Hierarchical context:** Downstream Executors receive compact QueryDocs from direct dependencies; full execution transcripts remain available through targeted recall. QueryDocs include answers, explanations, steps, confidence, and uncertainties (§3.1).
- **DAGRPO:** Role-separated GRPO attenuates rewards for Executors outside the final answer’s ancestor closure and penalizes invalid Orchestrator plans. The authors explicitly characterize ancestry as a contribution proxy, not causal attribution (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Empirical contribution:** Controlled planning comparisons, context ablations, cross-backbone evaluations, and same-budget RL comparisons support the framework. The claimed priority of structured-feedback planning is an author claim, not independently established here (§1, §4).

## Evidence and main results

Benchmark triples below follow **BrowseComp-Plus / GAIA / xbench-DeepSearch** order. Main evaluations use 150 / 103 text-only / 100 tasks, respectively (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled baseline advantage | Training-free Qwen3-32B DAGent achieves **47.3 / 55.3 / 65.0% Pass@1**, versus FlowSearch’s **43.3 / 51.5 / 63.0%**, using the same backbone and retrieval stack. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Advantage at larger backbone scale | Training-free Qwen3-235B-A22B achieves **61.3 / 63.1 / 72.0% Pass@1**, exceeding the strongest non-DAGent baseline on each benchmark by **5.3 / 5.8 / 2.0 percentage points**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Incremental planning contributes within the architecture | Qwen3-32B DAGent’s unweighted mean Pass@1 is **55.9%**, versus **51.2%** for its Plan-then-Patch variant and **43.7%** for upfront planning without replanning. | Table 2; Appendix D.2 |
| Context components contribute | Removing selective propagation, QueryDocs, or transcripts lowers Qwen3-32B mean Pass@1 by **10.4 / 6.9 / 3.0 points**, respectively. Transcript removal also disables recall. | Table 2; §4.3 |
| DAGRPO improves same-budget GRPO | Qwen3-8B, three training seeds: DAGRPO obtains **49.6±1.0 / 53.4±1.0 / 65.7±1.5% Pass@1**, versus **46.0±1.2 / 50.8±1.1 / 63.0±1.0%** for outcome-only GRPO-DAGent; reported mean gain is **3.0 points**. | Tables 1 and 3 |
| Higher accuracy with lower measured cost | On BrowseComp-Plus with Qwen3-32B, DAGent versus its Plan-then-Patch variant uses **1.20M vs 1.68M input-plus-output tokens**, **60.8 vs 82.3 external calls**, and **541.6 vs 731.9 seconds** per task, while achieving **47.3 vs 42.0% Pass@1**. | Table 10 |
| Frontier-scale results are favorable but less controlled | GPT-5 at 327K achieves **85.0 / 85.5 / 85.0% Pass@1**; GAIA uses its full 165-task set with attachment inspectors. Comparator results are drawn from prior reports. | Figure 4; Appendix D.1 |
| Judge agreement supports scoring reliability | On 150 sampled answer tuples, the judge agrees with human-majority labels **97.3%**, with **Cohen’s κ=0.95**. | Table 8; Appendix C.5 |

The strongest evidence is the same-architecture planning comparison and same-budget RL comparison. Cross-vendor results also support portability (Table 9). These evaluations establish performance on the tested answer-verification tasks; they do not establish open-ended report quality, causal node contribution, or superiority at matched total task budgets. Flash-Searcher is cheaper than DAGent in Table 10, although less accurate.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The framework is text-native; the GAIA extension converts attachments into text rather than reasoning over multimodal evidence within the planning loop. Retrieval and extraction quality constrain obtainable evidence (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** Answer ancestry is a structural proxy, and the compliance–accuracy association is correlational. Open-ended report generation is unevaluated (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** RL covers one backbone with LoRA and a short training schedule; larger-scale or full-fine-tuning gains remain unestablished (Appendix F).
- **Author-acknowledged uncertainty:** RL component ablations use one seed against a three-seed full-method mean; smaller differences fall within seed noise (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Efficiency comparisons show observed accuracy–cost tradeoffs, not performance under equal total budgets: workflows retain different caps, and no common per-task cost cap is imposed (Appendices C.3 and D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Confidence and uncertainty guide planning, but the paper reports no calibration study or isolated ablation of those fields. Their individual reliability remains unclear (§3.1; Table 2).
- **Scoring boundary:** GAIA uses an equivalence judge instead of its original quasi-exact-match scoring; frontier comparisons include externally reported results (Appendices C.5 and D.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does incremental planning improve open-ended reports?** Current benchmarks require closed-form answers. Compare planning variants on report tasks with blinded assessments of coverage, citation support, and factual accuracy (Appendix F).
- **Can ancestry-based credit approximate causal contribution?** The closure only records evidence flow. Re-execute selected downstream subgraphs after withholding individual node outputs and compare causal effects with assigned credit (§3.2; Appendix F).
- **How sensitive is planning to unreliable confidence signals?** Their calibration is untested. Ablate, shuffle, or calibrate QueryDoc confidence and uncertainty fields while measuring accuracy and refinement behavior (§3.1).
- **Do DAGRPO gains persist at larger training scales?** Train larger backbones over longer schedules, repeat component ablations across seeds, and compare LoRA with full fine-tuning (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark findings; lower for causal explanations and broader generalization.
