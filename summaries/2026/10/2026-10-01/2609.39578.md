# Thinking Outside the Box: Can Language Models Rely on External Guidance Selectively?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39578) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39578)


## One-sentence takeaway

Box2-Bench shows that models can benefit from useful workflows while remaining vulnerable to misleading ones, and training on bad workflows improves robustness but preserves helpful-guidance use unevenly across tasks.

## Problem and motivation

Agent harnesses supply workflows that can help models solve tasks, but those workflows may contain errors or become unreliable during execution. The authors argue that task performance and procedural compliance do not measure whether a model knows when to follow, verify, or override guidance. Their target capability is **selective reliance**: benefiting from useful guidance without becoming bound by misleading guidance (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Matched benchmark:** Box2-Bench holds the task, model, environment, and evaluator fixed across no-workflow, Good, Bad, Partial, and Mixed conditions. Partial and Mixed share a useful prefix, then either stop or continue misleadingly. Paired score differences measure utilization, bad-guidance robustness, and recovery (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Training study:** Counterfactual SFT pairs bad workflows with verified successful solutions or actions. Subsequent outcome-based RL rewards task success under bad guidance. Good workflows are withheld from training to test generalization (§3; Appendix A).
- **Transfer evaluation:** The same trained checkpoints are tested without additional training on peer collaboration and corrupted memory. These experiments provide preliminary evidence of transfer beyond procedural guidance (§4; Appendix D).
- **Assets:** The authors report releasing Box2-Bench and trained checkpoints through a Hugging Face collection; the supplied text does not give its direct address (Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Helpful-guidance use does not imply robustness | Across three frontier models and four tasks, Good improves benchmark scores in 8/12 model–task pairs; Bad reduces scores in all 12, by 2.3–43.3 percentage points relative to no workflow. Each domain has 30 frozen task bundles. | Table 1; §2.3; Appendix C.2 |
| Misleading continuation often hurts | Mixed scores are below matched Partial scores in 10/12 frontier model–task pairs, with a shared four-step Good prefix. | Table 1; Appendix C.2 |
| SFT improves robustness while reducing utilization | Qwen3-4B/AIME: Bad-minus-no-workflow accuracy changes from −20.0 to −6.7 points; Good-minus-no-workflow from +13.3 to −3.3. Qwen3.5-9B/WebShop: corresponding success-rate effects change from −9.8 to −0.6 and +10.4 to +2.8 points. | Table 3 |
| RL restores some AIME utilization but weakens SFT robustness | After SFT+RL, AIME utilization is +6.7 points, versus −3.3 after SFT; the Bad penalty is −16.7 points, versus −6.7 after SFT and −20.0 for Base. WebShop utilization remains +2.8 points. | Table 3; §3.2 |
| Recovery gains depend on the task | AIME Mixed-minus-Partial accuracy changes from −14.4 points for Base to −4.4 after SFT and +1.1 after RL. WebShop’s corresponding gap remains small: −0.1, −0.7, and −0.7 points. | Table 3 |
| Peer transfer is strongest after SFT | Four-agent EoM evaluation over 150 episodes: success is 18.00%/22.67%/18.67% for Base/SFT/SFT+RL. Repair/corruption counts are 2/2, 11/1, and 5/3. | §4; Figure 6 |
| Memory transfer is modest under corruption | On 30 text-only questions with three rollouts each, Bad-memory accuracy is 10.0%/14.4%/13.3%, and archive-use rate is 65.6%/72.2%/74.4% for Base/SFT/SFT+RL. Good-memory accuracy remains 95.6%/92.2%/94.4%. | Table 4; Appendix D.1 |
| Neither RL variant consistently dominates | AIME majority-vote accuracy under Good/Bad is 80.0%/56.7% for outcome RL and 80.0%/60.0% for relative RL; WebShop success is 34.8%/30.8% versus 34.0%/31.4%. | Tables 7–8; Appendix B.2 |

AIME results use majority voting over eight samples for each of 30 problems; WebShop uses one greedy episode for each of 500 test tasks, capped at 14 actions (Appendix A.2).

The matched comparisons support sensitivity to workflow reliability and a training-dependent robustness–utilization tradeoff. They do not establish uniformly improved selectivity: RL relinquishes some SFT robustness, and transfer gains are small or non-monotonic. Under corrupted memory, every correct trajectory retrieves supporting archive evidence, but that association does not establish retrieval’s causal effect (Appendix D.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Synthetic, frozen workflows do not cover diverse human intentions or interactive, ambiguous, partially correct, and model-adaptive guidance (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Workflow review is model-based, without blind human annotation, inter-annotator reliability, or candidate rejection rates. The benchmark does not estimate naturally occurring workflow-error prevalence (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Training conclusions are limited to two model–task combinations; peer transfer uses 30 AIME problems, and memory transfer uses 30 selected text-only questions. Memory scores are explicitly not comparable with the official leaderboard protocol (§3.2; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated training-seed results are reported, limiting confidence in small differences.
- **Analyst inference:** A standard SFT control using the same successful targets without bad workflows is not reported, leaving ordinary task training as a possible contributor to gains.
- **Comparison ambiguity:** Appendix B calls reward the only RL difference, but relative RL also uses the relative reward directly as the advantage without group normalization. The comparison therefore changes advantage construction as well as the reward baseline.
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** §3 describes sampling successful traces from the base model; Appendix A specifies Qwen3-8B traces for mathematical SFT, whose evaluated student is Qwen3-4B.

## Open research questions

- **Does selectivity generalize to human-written errors?** Synthetic guidance limits this claim. Test frozen checkpoints on independently annotated human workflows with varied error types.
- **Can helpful-guidance use be preserved without sacrificing robustness?** SFT and RL expose a tradeoff. Compare bad-only training with matched mixtures of good, bad, and absent guidance.
- **How much improvement comes specifically from conflicting workflows?** The ordinary SFT control is missing. Train on identical successful targets with and without bad workflows.
- **Does verification cause better memory correction?** Retrieval accompanies successful corrections but remains observational. Randomize archive access or retrieved-evidence availability under identical corrupted briefs.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported comparisons and includes substantial protocol detail, but broader generalization and small gains remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates, repeated training seeds, full optimization hyperparameters and compute costs, detailed frontier execution settings, a no-workflow SFT control, and direct collection links. Full reviewer prompts and schemas are described as forthcoming rather than included (Appendix C.3).
