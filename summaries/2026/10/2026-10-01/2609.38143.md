# Learning Meta-Skills for Agent Harness Design in Test-Time AI4AI

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38143) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38143)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across two benchmarks and three Target models, learned support-design principles improve a fixed-weight Builder–Target system’s macro-average score by 8.95 percentage points over harness construction without learned guidance, with Builder computation excluded from Target budgets (Table 3; §3).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask how an agent can learn to build better execution environments for another agent without updating either model’s weights. Their identified gap is reusable knowledge for **support design**: task-solving advice explains how to solve a problem, whereas a Builder needs principles for deciding which tools, state, verification, and control mechanisms to provide (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Meta-skill representation:** Each principle specifies an observable support trigger (`when`), a capability or resource (`provide`), and how the Target should use it while retaining judgment (`use`; §3.1).
- **Learning and deployment protocol:** Starting with an empty bank, the Builder constructs development-task harnesses, reviews execution feedback, and keeps, adds, or revises guidance. After two development passes, the frozen bank guides fresh harness construction for each held-out task; deployment uses either the full bank or BM25 retrieval of at most two skills (§§3.1–3.2, 4.1).
- **Controlled empirical study:** Comparisons separate learned guidance from construction capability and direct delivery of advice. Additional experiments examine refinement, same-model support design, model transfer, and component removal (§§4–5).

The claimed contribution is this combination of reusable support principles and their learning-to-construction protocol, rather than harness construction alone (Appendix A.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Learned guidance improves construction | Full-bank Builder scores **65.31% macro-average**, versus **56.36%** without skills: **+8.95 pp**, across three Targets on two benchmarks. | Table 3 |
| Builder implementation outperforms direct advice | Same full bank delivered directly to the Target scores **53.29% macro-average**; Builder implementation gains **12.02 pp** and wins all six settings. | Table 3; §4.2 |
| Full-bank access usually beats retrieval | Full bank exceeds BM25 top-2 in **five of six settings**; mean NewtonBench advantage is **7.19 pp**. | Table 3; §4.2 |
| Same-model support design improves scores | Across three Gemini configurations, meta-skills gain **18.71 pp** on average over no-skill construction and **14.14 pp** over independent Target skills. | Figure 3(a); §5.2 |
| Transfer effects are uncertain | Transferring the Sol-to-Qwen bank to Gemini-Pro yields **+2.72 pp** on Harness-Bench and **−4.45 pp** on NewtonBench versus concurrent recipient no-skill controls; both paired 95% intervals include zero. | Figure 3(b); §5.3 |
| Controller benefit is clearest for Gemini | Removing the controller from frozen Gemini NewtonBench harnesses lowers symbolic accuracy from **68.84% to 55.48%**, a **−13.36 pp** change, with paired 95% CI **[−18.49, −8.22]**. Other component-removal intervals include zero. | Table 4; §5.4 |
| Scientific completion improves alongside correctness | Across three Targets on NewtonBench, no-valid-submission outcomes fall from **394 to 254**, while correct discoveries rise from **439 to 535**, comparing no-skill and full-bank Builders. | §5.5; Figure 4(a) |

These results support the usefulness of learned support design **within the supplied framework and fixed Target interaction limits**. They do not establish gains under equal total compute, reliable cross-model transfer, or performance on unseen task families. The direct-bank comparison tests knowledge delivery plus implementation and budget allocation; it does not isolate executable code as the sole cause.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One main Builder, two benchmarks, and one adopted execution per task and condition; broader model and task coverage remains future work (§6; Appendix A.3).
- **Cost accounting:** Learning and construction lie outside Target execution budgets. Total compute, latency, and cost-adjusted benefits are not established (§3; Appendix A.3).
- **Generalization:** Splits preserve known categories and physical mechanisms. Cross-dataset and unseen-family reuse are untested (Appendices A.2–A.3, B.1).
- **Stability and mechanism:** Refinement can regress, and bootstrap intervals measure variation across tasks rather than repeated generations. Individual-principle and component-interaction interventions remain future work (§5.1; Appendices A.3, B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is incompletely resolved by the supplied text: p. 1 claims released code, while Appendix A.2 promises full code, prompts, and configurations upon acceptance. Appendix C also compares Qwen’s full-bank Target skills with the retrieved Builder score without clearly labeling that variant change.

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains survive equal total-compute budgets?** Builder cost is excluded. Compare methods under joint learning, construction, and execution budgets, reporting accuracy, latency, and cost.
2. **Which principles transfer to unseen task families?** Current splits retain known categories. Learn banks on selected categories, then test frozen and recipient-adapted banks on held-out families or another dataset.
3. **Can selective retention prevent refinement regressions?** Qwen’s Harness-Bench score drops after the second pass (§5.1). Compare unconditional updates with development-only validation and rollback across independent learning runs.
4. **Which support interactions drive improvement?** Frozen-harness ablations cover only controllers and memory plus context. Run factorial removals and individual-skill interventions with repeated executions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-benchmark comparisons; **medium** in broader mechanism and transfer interpretations.

Material missing information includes total Builder compute and latency, repeated-run variability, results across alternative partitions, unseen-family or cross-dataset evaluation, individual-skill effects, and confirmed availability of the complete reproducibility artifacts.
