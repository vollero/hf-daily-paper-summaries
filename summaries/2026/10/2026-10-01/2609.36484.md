# The Teacher Is a Direction, Not a Destination: Extrapolating RL-Induced Representation Residuals in On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36484) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36484)


## One-sentence takeaway
RIDE extrapolates layerwise hidden-state differences between an RL teacher and its pre-RL base, improving mathematical-reasoning distillation across four model pairs, although most margins over teachers are smaller than training-seed variability (Tables 1, 4).

## Problem and motivation
Standard on-policy distillation matches a teacher’s outputs on student-generated trajectories. The authors seek to transfer—and extend—the teacher’s RL gains when the student starts from the same base checkpoint.

They identify two weaknesses in output-space extrapolation: the language-model head unevenly attenuates representation changes, and extrapolating sampled-token log-ratio rewards can amplify noise. Existing representation matching supplies intermediate supervision but treats the teacher as the endpoint (§§1, 4.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Context-dependent RL residual:** On identical student-generated prefixes, compute the frozen teacher–base hidden-state difference at each layer and token position. This measures checkpoint differences without confounding them with different input trajectories (§4.2).
- **Extrapolated representation targets:** Regress student states toward \(h^\star=h_T+(\lambda-1)(h_T-h_B)\). Teacher matching, OPRD, is recovered at \(\lambda=1\); larger coefficients place targets beyond the teacher. Experiments supervise all layers and the last response positions, with loss scaling to equalize initial loss across coefficients (§4.2; Appendix G).
- **Interpretation and diagnostics:** The authors derive a rollout-conditioned linear directional reward with a quadratic teacher-proximity penalty, and analyze head attenuation, next-token conditional variance, and residual-direction controls (§§4.3–4.4, 5.4–5.5).

## Evidence and main results
Avg@16 averages correctness over sampled responses per problem; the reported macro score equally averages AIME24, AIME25, and AIMO. Main trained-method results average three training seeds, each using 500 steps (§5.1).

The evidence supports improved mathematical-reasoning scores under this training protocol and a useful role for the intended residual direction. It does **not** establish statistically reliable superiority over every teacher, general-domain improvement, or that conditional advantage variance alone causes ExOPD’s performance decline. RIDE remains stochastic across rollouts and prefixes despite its deterministic gradient conditional on a fixed context (Appendix F).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** RIDE requires the pre-RL checkpoint and shared representation space, uses one global coefficient, and is evaluated only on mathematics with one RL recipe. Calibration, safety, and possible amplification of teacher errors remain untested (§6; Ethics Statement).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- Three teacher margins lie within one RIDE training-seed SD; fixed teacher evaluations lack corresponding uncertainty estimates (Table 4).
- Coefficient selection uses the same named benchmarks reported in the sweep; a separate held-out selection set is not described. ExOPD’s stronger teacher-matching settings outperform its extrapolating settings, so the comparison chiefly supports RIDE over the tested extrapolation protocol (§5.3; Table 2).
- Mechanistic diagnostics and direction controls concentrate on R1; Llama’s AIME scores are near the evaluation floor (§§5.2–5.5).
- The exact head-projection identity requires the specified head-input normalization and shared-head assumptions. Measured teacher-head drift complicates equivalence to native output targets (Appendix E).
- Code, evaluation scripts, and additional teacher checkpoints are promised upon publication. Wall-clock cost and peak-memory measurements are absent; RIDE adds a frozen base forward pass (p. 10; Table 6).

## Open research questions
- **Does RIDE generalize beyond mathematics and this RL recipe?** The evaluation is restricted; repeat matched comparisons across other tasks and independently trained RL teachers (§6).
- **Can extrapolation strength adapt across layers or contexts?** A global coefficient helps only over part of the tested range; compare adaptive coefficients with a globally tuned baseline using separate selection and test sets (Table 2).
- **Which component drives the gain: earlier-layer supervision or avoiding sampled-token noise?** Current comparisons change both; test final-layer-only RIDE and full-distribution output extrapolation (§4.3; Table 6).
- **Does extrapolation amplify undesirable teacher behavior?** Safety and calibration are explicitly unresolved; evaluate calibration and error or bias transfer across coefficients (Ethics Statement).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text specifies the method and main protocol clearly, but generalization and teacher-superiority claims remain limited.

Material gaps include per-seed raw results and direction-control uncertainty, independent benchmark-selection procedures, safety/calibration results, measured runtime and memory, and released implementation artifacts. Figure 5(b) also prints an alignment cosine different from the accompanying prose and Appendix E, leaving that diagnostic inconsistent.
