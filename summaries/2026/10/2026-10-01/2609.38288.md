# AREX-2: Advancing Self-Improving Agents through Long-Horizon Reflective Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38288) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38288)


## One-sentence takeaway
AREX-2 learns from sustained, feedback-driven coding and ML trajectories, achieving strong benchmark results and continued test-time improvement, although the evidence does not isolate reflective training as the cause of cross-domain gains.

## Problem and motivation
The authors define self-improvement as refining a solution within a single task at test time. They argue that conventional agent training retains successful solutions but discards intermediate failures, feedback, and revisions, leaving little supervision for sustained improvement. Their proposed gap is learning both useful revisions and the ability to keep iterating productively, rather than relying primarily on an external harness (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Environment construction:** A teacher converts GitHub repositories and online-judge problems into executable tasks with scoring functions. Environments must support a working reference solution and leave room for improvement over a simple baseline (§2.2).
- **Long-horizon trajectory supervision:** Teachers work across many rounds, acquire operational knowledge, and react to feedback. Selection retains successful trajectories as wholes, including setbacks; training masks unproductive steps while supervising decisions that advance recovery or improvement (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Model and transfer evaluation:** Qwen3.8-27B is fine-tuned on these trajectories plus unchanged AREX deep-research data. The authors evaluate source-domain performance, transfer to research tasks, budget scaling, and staged contributions from skills and training (§§2.4–3.3).

These results support effective iteration within the tested budgets and are consistent with cross-domain transfer. They do not establish domain-independent reflection as a separately measured capability or isolate the new data’s effect on research performance against the same base model. Table 2 also mixes full-HLE and text-only results, explicitly marked by the authors. For Frontier-CS, §3.1 says “accuracy,” whereas Table 1 and the scaling analysis describe graded scores; its precise aggregation is not clarified.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Gaps remain to the strongest BrowseComp and HLE systems. Wider training domains, longer horizons, and retraining on the model’s own trajectories are proposed future work (§§3.2, 4).
- **Analyst inference—transfer attribution:** Previous AREX comparators have different model sizes, and no same-base research ablation removing the new trajectories is reported. Unchanged research data strengthens the transfer argument but does not isolate its mechanism (§2.4; Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—component isolation:** M1→M2 adds task-specific skills and rounds together. Separate ablations for failure retention, loss masking, and teacher trajectory length are absent (§3.3.3).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Best-so-far scoring is nondecreasing by construction; its growth measures discovery of better candidates, rather than guaranteeing that each revision improves (§2.1).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Teacher identity, dataset size, training hyperparameters, selection thresholds, exact ablation round counts, and optimized-harness details are unavailable. Uncertainty estimates beyond MLE’s seed averaging are not supplied (§§2–3).

## Open research questions
- **Does the new data independently cause research transfer?** Existing research comparisons change the model as well as the recipe. Train the same Qwen base with and without the new trajectories and evaluate under identical research budgets.
- **Which trajectory features matter most?** Failure retention and selective supervision lack separate ablations. Compare matched datasets that remove setbacks or change loss masking while holding training compute fixed.
- **How much do task-specific skills contribute independently of budget?** The staged ablation changes both together. Run a factorial skills-by-round-budget evaluation on MLE-bench Lite.
- **Where does sustained improvement stop?** Frontier-CS evaluation ends while gains remain positive. Extend matched runs and measure score, execution cost, and plateau behavior.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reports results, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain limited. Missing information includes training-data scale and composition, teacher models, optimization settings, detailed harness configuration, exact round budgets, contamination checks, uncertainty estimates, and verified contents of the linked model/code assets.
