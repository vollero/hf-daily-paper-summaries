# SeLMRoute: Probabilistic Semantic Evidence for Large Language Model Routing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34736) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34736)


## One-sentence takeaway
SeLMRoute uses explicit probabilistic task requirements to improve benchmark LLM selection over a fixed model, but its advantage over hard semantic labels is statistically uncertain and reliable monetary savings are not established.

## Problem and motivation
LLM routing chooses a candidate model for each query to balance capability and deployment cost. The authors argue that embeddings and coarse domain labels capture similarity without explicitly describing requirements such as exactness, constraint density, or reasoning depth; hard labels also discard uncertainty. Their proposed gap is an inspectable, candidate-independent representation that separates query requirements from learned model suitability (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Probabilistic semantic interface:** A decision model answers sixteen task-property probes: eight binary and eight four-level judgments. Their probability masses form forty features; candidate names and the diagnostic task-family label are excluded (§§3.2–3.3, Table 1).
- **Separated routing stages:** CatBoost predicts candidate scores from semantic features, then a configurable objective selects by quality or normalized quality–cost utility. Existing features can be reused when objectives or model pools change, although new candidates require performance evidence (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Controlled empirical assessment:** The paper compares representations, learners, decision backends, direct routing, reduced probe sets, distribution shifts, and cost objectives using grouped evaluation. The claimed contribution is this semantic representation and decomposition, rather than a new regression algorithm (§4).

## Evidence and main results

AvgAcc is accuracy averaged equally across datasets (§4.1); percentage-point differences below are distinguished from relative percentage gains.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest mean accuracy among internally evaluated representations | On fifteen datasets, twenty candidates, and 11,481 queries, five grouped 70/30 splits give ProbabilityMass **72.08 ± 0.45% AvgAcc**, versus GTE-Qwen2 **71.16 ± 0.74%** and DomainOnly **71.08 ± 1.03%**. Best Single reaches **68.81 ± 0.34%**. | Table 3; §4.2 |
| Probability preservation has an uncertain advantage over hard labels | Grouped five-fold OOF: **72.64%** versus Hard **71.81%**; difference **0.83 pp**, clustered 95% CI **[−0.174, 1.837] pp**, **p = 0.1099**. Best fixed candidate reaches **69.23%**. | §4.3; Fig. 4 |
| Learned semantic routing beats the tested direct-routing configuration | On the same original five-fold assignment, SeLMRoute **72.57% AvgAcc** versus JEV-Direct **68.78%**; difference **3.79 pp**, 95% interval **[2.69, 4.95] pp**. Direct routing uses anonymized profiles built from training outcomes. | §4.5 |
| Extractor choice materially affects performance | Shared grouped OOF procedure: JEV **72.64% AvgAcc**, Laya **70.53%**; reported paired difference **2.10 pp**, 95% CI **[0.940, 3.255] pp**, **p = 0.00035**. | §4.6; Fig. 4 |
| Reduced probes lower overhead, but noninferiority is unproven | Nested Lite-12 yields **72.49% AvgAcc** versus full **72.64%**. The one-sided lower bound is **−0.783 pp**, failing the **0.5 pp** noninferiority margin; input tokens fall approximately **34%**, p95 extraction latency **18%**. | §4.7; §4.13 |
| Entirely unseen domains weaken semantic routing | Dataset-OOD: ProbabilityMass **67.02% AvgAcc**, GTE-Qwen2 **67.29%**. Domain-OOD: **65.66%** versus **67.31%**, respectively. No significance test is supplied for these rankings. | §4.9; Fig. 6 |
| Quality gains do not establish monetary savings | Thirteen-model, ten-dataset setting with 12,446 queries: validation-selected routing improves relative PerfGain over GPT-5 in every grouped split, averaging **2.66 ± 1.85%**. No split achieves positive strict CostSave. Candidate costs exclude semantic extraction. | §4.11; Table 6 |
| Extraction dominates added latency | Full JEV extraction averages approximately **350 ms**, p95 **481 ms**, including API/network overhead; downstream CatBoost takes **1.23 ms/query** at batch size one. Estimated extraction charge is **$0.0675/1,000 queries** on the performance-oriented population at evaluation-time pricing. | §§4.12–4.13; Table 7 |

The evidence supports useful benchmark routing through a compact, interpretable input representation. It does not establish a statistically reliable advantage over hard semantics, superiority to published routers under a shared protocol, or positive end-to-end cost savings. Published router scores use a different evaluation protocol and slightly different benchmark support (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Unseen-domain transfer and candidate cold start remain difficult; reusable semantic states do not replace empirical candidate calibration (§§4.9, 5).
- **Author-stated:** Interpretability concerns the input evidence, not a complete causal explanation of nonlinear routing decisions (§3.3).
- **Author-stated:** Semantic entropy ranks errors somewhat better than the router margin in-distribution, but transferred rejection thresholds provide no meaningful accuracy gain (§4.10).
- **Author-stated:** Semantic extraction adds latency. Remote JEV and local Laya timings are not hardware-normalized comparisons, and extraction expense is excluded from the cost-routing results (§§4.11–4.13).
- **Analyst inference:** Frozen benchmark outcomes leave live adaptation to changing capabilities, prices, and traffic unestablished; the authors propose these as future work (§§4.1, 5).
- **Reporting gaps:** Table 5 omits `answer_openness` despite describing removal of each probe. The all-query Domain-OOD accuracy in §4.10 differs from §4.9 without an explicit reconciliation.

## Open research questions
- **Are probe probabilities calibrated, and does calibration improve routing?** Calibration is future work (§5). Next experiment: obtain independent semantic annotations and compare routing before and after probability calibration.
- **Can new candidates be calibrated with few evaluations?** Candidate-specific evidence is still required (§5). Next experiment: hold out a candidate and compare active sampling of semantic regions with random calibration queries.
- **Can adaptive extraction preserve quality while reducing latency?** Lite-12 noninferiority remains unproven (§4.7). Next experiment: evaluate conditional probe execution under nested selection with accuracy and latency constraints.
- **Can routing deliver positive total cost savings?** Strict candidate-cost savings fail, and extraction is measured separately (§§4.11–4.12). Next experiment: select and test objectives incorporating extraction cost and latency on the same query population.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and main benchmark findings; broader deployment conclusions remain uncertain.

The supplied text lacks exact probe prompts and level definitions, full learner hyperparameters, extractor versions and local hardware details, probability-calibration results, reconciled uncertainty-experiment settings, and end-to-end costs on the performance-cost population. The claimed code release was not inspected.
