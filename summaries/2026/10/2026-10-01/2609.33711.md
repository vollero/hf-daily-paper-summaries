# DuoOPD: Learning from Joint Teacher-Student Outcomes for Multi-Task On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33711) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33711)


## One-sentence takeaway
DuoOPD uses verified teacher–student outcomes to direct and allocate distillation feedback, improving mean macro accuracy over OPD across the tested model pairs and task mixtures, with disagreement-specific teacher references and weight sharing providing most of the measured gain.

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation scores student-generated responses using a stronger teacher, but teacher preferences can suppress correct student responses or reinforce failures. Teacher and student success patterns also differ across tasks. The authors identify a gap between task-level supervision choices and student-correctness gating: neither fully determines how to use a teacher that succeeds or fails on a particular response (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified four-outcome rule:** Binary student verification determines whether every response token receives reinforcement or suppression. Joint teacher–student correctness determines feedback magnitude, using positive or negative Softplus mappings when both models agree (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Teacher-guided correction:** When only the teacher succeeds, its cached, verified response becomes additional teacher scoring context. The student’s input remains unchanged (§3.3, Eq. 6).
- **Shared reinforcement:** When only the student succeeds, all tokens receive a positive coefficient shared across such responses within the same task, preserving the task’s mean reinforcement scale (§3.3, Eq. 7).
- **Empirical contribution:** Comparisons and ablations test the combined rule across model families and heterogeneous task mixtures. The distinctive contribution is the joint-outcome routing and allocation scheme; reference-conditioned scoring itself has precedents acknowledged by the authors (§§4–5).

“No task-specific settings” applies to the feedback rule; task-specific verifiers and task identities for sharing remain necessary.

## Evidence and main results

All accuracy results below use final-checkpoint test **avg@8**: mean binary success across eight sampled responses per question. Macro equally weights the three tasks; reported means average three training runs (§4.1).

The evidence supports higher mean accuracy under the tested budgets and the contribution of the disagreement designs. It does not establish universal task-level superiority: DuoOPD loses some individual scientific columns in Table 2. Table 8 reports run variability, but no formal significance tests are supplied. Feedback diagnostics are measured on DuoOPD trajectories and should not be read as direct measurements of all OPD training trajectories (§4.4; Appendix D.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Binary verification limits applicability to open-ended or uncertain judgments; longer reasoning, exploration, held-out-task transfer, and outcome-based scheduling remain untested (§6).
- **Analyst inference—verification granularity:** Reinforcing every token of a response judged correct can reinforce faulty reasoning. Appendix D.2 explicitly shows a correct final option accompanied by incorrect earlier reasoning.
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Additional mixtures and component ablations use only Qwen3; evaluation uses a fixed short training budget, and Qwen3 thinking is disabled (§4.3; Table 6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Baselines retain some distinct auxiliary losses, filtering, microbatch settings, and rollout corrections. The comparison tests these configured methods rather than isolating the feedback rule alone (Appendix B.2).
- Runtime accounting excludes initialization, checkpoint saving, evaluation, and queue time; cache amortization depends on reuse (Appendix B.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can graded or noisy verification replace binary labels?** Open-ended judgments are outside the tested scope (§6). Test graded feedback and controlled verifier errors against binary routing.
- **Does the method improve unseen-task transfer?** Training and evaluation use the same task identities (§6). Hold out task families and compare transfer with OPD and OPDVR.
- **Can reinforcement preserve correct answers without strengthening erroneous reasoning?** The supplied example exposes this mismatch (Appendix D.2). Compare whole-response sharing with separately verified reasoning-segment feedback.
- **How sensitive is routing to the cached teacher sample?** Each prompt receives one fixed teacher response (Appendix B.1). Repeat training with different cache seeds and multiple verified teacher candidates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and within-setting results; broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material information unavailable includes formal significance tests, verifier-noise sensitivity, cache-seed sensitivity, held-out-task results, longer-reasoning evaluations, and independent confirmation of the repository’s stated contents.
