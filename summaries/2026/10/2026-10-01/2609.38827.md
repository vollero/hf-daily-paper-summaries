# More Choices, Fewer Decisions: Ordinal-Scale Bias in JEV-like Direct-Decision Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38827) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38827)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across four direct-decision models, finer ordinal scales produce increasingly concentrated final predictions despite broad average candidate probabilities, while targeted post-training substantially restores utilization on supervised sources but transfers unevenly (§§4.1–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Direct-decision models return probabilities and labels over user-supplied candidates. Accuracy can conceal population-level underuse of an ordinal scale: a model may repeatedly select a few levels while gold labels occupy many. The authors identify systematic scale utilization as a gap in evaluations centered on correctness, label preferences, and candidate-order sensitivity (§1).

The motivating ANLI Neutral preference concerns a nominal task; the authors explicitly treat it as a clue, not proof of ordinal compression (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnosis:** Define gold-relative utilization, \(R=\exp(H(p))/\exp(H(q))\), comparing effective support of predicted and gold-label distributions. Evaluate four models across an ordinal/nominal dataset panel; report TVD separately because equal support does not imply matching distributions (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Controlled interventions:** Randomize candidate order and refine the same continuous-score items into progressively finer scales, controlling gold support and presentation positions. Compare final-decision utilization with average-probability utilization (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Mitigation study:** Apply existing BA-LoRA to KEV-0.8B and KEV-4B on affected sources, separating held-out items from supervised sources and transfer to unseen sources. The contribution is this application and evaluation, rather than invention of BA-LoRA (§3.6; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinal decisions use less effective support than nominal decisions | Dataset-level macro-average R across four models: **67.2–75.8%** on 36 ordinal datasets versus **86.6–101.9%** on four nominal datasets; each dataset contributes approximately 5,000 items. | §4.1; Table 1; Appendix A |
| Randomization reduces compression without eliminating it | On six ordinal datasets, original → randomized R: JEV **65.0→70.4%**, KEV-0.8B **61.1→73.7%**, KEV-4B **55.3→73.9%**, KEV-9B **57.0→67.7%**. Randomized results average two permutations per item. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Accuracy masks order sensitivity | Agreement between two randomized orders is **58.1–74.4%** on ordinal items versus **95.6–98.2%** on nominal items; aggregate accuracy changes by at most **0.55 percentage points** across models and subsets. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Finer balanced scales compress final predictions | Same 1,400 items per source, three ordinal sources, equal-frequency bins, balanced gold positions: K=2→14 R is JEV **95.9→54.5%**, KEV-0.8B **77.3→26.5%**, KEV-4B **97.9→68.4%**, KEV-9B **96.1→74.9%**. | §4.3; Figure 3(a) |
| Broad average probabilities can coexist with compressed decisions | KEV-4B and KEV-9B maintain Usoft **≥96%** at every tested K under both binning rules, while mean top-1 margins fall from **50–54** to **4–6 percentage points** at K=2→14. | §4.3; Figure 3 |
| Adaptation restores utilization on supervised sources | On disjoint held-out items from eight training sources, BA-LoRA raises R **47.4→86.8%** for KEV-0.8B and **46.4→85.6%** for KEV-4B; TVD falls **54.7→19.7%** and **52.9→19.0%**, respectively. | §4.4; Table 3 |
| Transfer differs by model size | Across 28 unseen ordinal sources, KEV-4B R improves **73.2→81.3%**, whereas KEV-0.8B declines **75.0→72.7%**; corresponding TVD changes are **33.1→27.6%** and **32.5→35.1%**. | §4.4; Table 3 |

These results establish scale underutilization in the tested systems and show that order randomization alone does not remove it. They support the authors’ decision-boundary interpretation, but do not establish semantic coupling as the causal mechanism: the nominal refinement reference is much easier. Usoft measures the entropy of the **average** probability vector, so broad Usoft does not establish broad probabilities on every item (§3.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Evidence covers one hosted model and one open backbone family; controlled refinement uses three ordinal sources; ordinal/nominal structure is binary rather than a graded coupling measure. DBpedia is a ceiling reference, not difficulty-matched. Position testing uses two permutations per item, and all results are point estimates. KEV training includes SST-5, DBpedia, and IMDb, without an item-overlap audit (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences:** The reported mitigation experiment lacks a supervised-only LoRA comparison and objective-term ablations, leaving BA-LoRA’s specific contribution beyond targeted supervision unresolved. One training seed and final-checkpoint selection do not establish run-to-run stability (Appendix D). Population-level support matching also cannot guarantee individual correctness or calibrated probabilities.

## Open research questions
- **Does semantic coupling cause compression beyond task difficulty?** The binary taxonomy and easy DBpedia reference leave this unresolved. Next experiment: compare ordinal and nominal candidate sets matched for difficulty while varying adjacent-label similarity.
- **Which adaptation components drive recovery?** No reported supervised-only or regularizer ablation isolates them. Next experiment: compare ordinary LoRA, full BA-LoRA, and individual-term removals on identical training records.
- **What predicts transfer to unseen scales?** Aggregate transfer improves at 4B but worsens at 0.8B. Next experiment: vary training-source coverage across model sizes and evaluate a predefined unseen-source panel.
- **How stable are the effects?** Results lack uncertainty estimates and use limited permutations. Next experiment: repeat adaptation seeds and expand order permutations, reporting confidence intervals.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported descriptive findings; **medium** in the proposed mechanism and broader generalization.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes uncertainty estimates, repeated-training results, difficulty-matched nominal controls, KEV pretraining-overlap audits, BA-LoRA component ablations, and complete mathematical definitions of its auxiliary losses. Code and data availability is author-reported and was not independently verified.
