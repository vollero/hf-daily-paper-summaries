# Agent Error Dataset: Scaling 50,000 Error--Diagnosis Pairs for Failure Analysis and Error-Aware Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40111) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40111)


## One-sentence takeaway
AED links natural agent failures to diagnoses and optional controlled correction tests, showing improved replay success and internal diagnosis-label agreement, but mixed public transfer and actor-training outcomes (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Failed agent rollouts contain observations, decisions, and environment responses that terminal rewards cannot explain. The authors identify a gap between failure attribution resources and actionable training data: existing resources differ in annotation targets and execution evidence, making diagnosis and corrective policy learning difficult to study together (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure collection:** AED contains 50,228 error–diagnosis pairs across 9,961 source tasks, 33 environments, 19 harness families, and 23 policy models. Records retain traces and provenance; repeated diagnoses do not constitute independent failures (§3.3; Table 4).
- **AET construction pipeline:** Collect natural failures, diagnose a consequential decision, check trace support, optionally compare a correction with an original-action retry from the same checkpoint, and build objective-specific training views (§3.2; Appendix D.1). Diagnosis generation builds on AgentDebug/AgentDebugX; AED adds adapters, evidence checks, and paired replay.
- **Distinct supervision interfaces:** Diagnosis uses completed failed traces; repair supervises executed passing continuations; action preferences require comparable executed alternatives. These interfaces have separate admission requirements (§4; Appendix T.2).
- **Empirical study:** The authors evaluate correction utility, diagnosis SFT, and actor-training recipes separately, avoiding treating replay success as evidence of learned autonomous recovery (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| First corrections outperform retries | Across 3,062 matched replay attempts over 1,284 source tasks, verifier pass rate rises from 18.4% for original-action retries to 51.1% for first/only corrections: +32.7 pp, task-clustered 95% interval [28.4, 37.0]. | Tables 7, 47(a) |
| Internal diagnosis agreement improves | Full-diagnosis Qwen3-8B SFT on 1,656 source tasks reaches 63.56% ± 0.82 sample SD over three seeds, versus 47.19% base and 54.72% strongest prompted reference, on the same 943 teacher-labeled cases. | Figure 5; Table 48(a–b) |
| Public transfer is mixed | Under the unified protocol, larger-arm mean exact-step accuracy is 33.51% ± 1.37 on Who&When versus 25.54% base, but 17.28% ± 3.21 on TrajErrBench versus 18.72% base. | Table 48(c) |
| Overlap limits the Who&When gain | Removing flagged shared tasks leaves 149 cases and a mean gain of +4.92 pp with 95% interval [−2.24, 12.08]. | Table 15 |
| Actor repair gains accompany regressions | Single-seed post-error action training reaches 97.00% initial-state success versus 90.33% success-only on 300 WebShop-lite tasks, but 85.33% versus 93.67% on 300 TextQuest tasks. Both contrasts survive the reported post-hoc adjustment. | Figure 6; Table 49 |
| Human localization agreement exceeds diagnosis acceptance | In 80 historical, coverage-selected records reviewed by author volunteers with shared AI assistance, preferred-step agreement is 59/69 localizable records (85.5%); both reviewers accept the diagnosis on 41/80 records (51.3%). | Table 44; Appendix W.1 |

These results support correction utility under recorded replay conditions and learning of internal annotation conventions. They do not establish collection-wide label accuracy, general diagnostic superiority, or reliable autonomous recovery. Public results vary with protocol and overlap; actor comparisons combine supervision changes with different task exposure and update counts.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Source coverage:** The supplied text is truncated, with a substantial middle portion omitted; this analysis cannot claim complete coverage.
- **Historical label defects:** Evaluated diagnosis data omitted policy system prompts and tool lists and preceded stricter checks. Audits identify retrieval outages, grader contradictions, missing contracts, and invalid responsible-unit labels (Appendices A, E.1, E.5).
- **Limited validation:** Citation matching establishes textual presence, not entailment. Human review used a selected historical sample, author raters, and shared AI assistance rather than independent collection-wide validation (Appendices A, W).
- **Replay scope:** Some selected-proposal replay used substituted harnesses or partly restored state. Supplied locations bypass autonomous detection, and successful corrections do not validate unique causes (§5.1; Table 14).
- **Training confounds:** Internal holdouts share environments and many harness–policy combinations with training. Actor recipes use one seed and differ in exposure and updates; preventive repair also regresses on real ALFWorld and ScienceWorld (§5.3; Appendices X.6).
- **Reproducibility:** Public release is planned and subject to privacy/licensing review; the external evaluation checkpoint mapping was not independently verified against loaded weights (p. 10; Appendix I.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The headline collection size provides stronger evidence of coverage than of training utility, because experiments use smaller, separately frozen populations (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Do corrected labels improve independent diagnosis accuracy?** Historical training labels retain documented defects. Retrain matched students on corrected-contract data and evaluate on independently annotated, task-disjoint traces.
- **Does repair supervision itself cause actor gains?** Current recipes vary in exposure and updates. Compare success-only and repair supervision with matched tasks, budgets, updates, and multiple seeds.
- **Can diagnosis enable autonomous recovery?** Correction experiments supply intervention locations. Test end-to-end detection, localization, and repair, including successful traces where the system should abstain.
- **How stable are corrections across execution settings?** Replay fidelity varies and selected successful contrasts can reverse on repetition (Appendix R). Repeat both arms under fully restored original harnesses and report task-clustered effects.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied results clearly support the qualified conclusions above, but the source is truncated and contains substantial documented validity concerns.

Unavailable information includes complete omitted protocols and ablations, independently verified checkpoint provenance, collection-wide semantic accuracy, training utility of the full collection, and confirmation that planned public artifacts have been released.
