# PatchHolmes: Agentic Patch Retrieval via Listwise Selection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38807) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38807)


## One-sentence takeaway
PatchHolmes combines hybrid commit retrieval with a listwise inspection agent, raising Recall@1 from 32.63% to 59.95% on fixed candidates for 809 GitHubAD CVEs with Qwen3-235B (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Patch retrieval identifies the repository commit fixing a known CVE, supporting advisory curation and vulnerability tracking. The authors cite widespread missing patch links and identify three obstacles: large commit histories, long diffs, and weak vocabulary overlap between vulnerability descriptions and fixes (§1).

Their central gap is candidate selection: static rerankers cannot inspect adaptively, while pointwise LLM classifiers judge commits independently and can produce confidence ties without comparative evidence (§1, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Listwise agentic selection:** The main contribution replaces independent candidate judgments with one conversation that surveys a candidate manifest, selectively inspects commits, and submits a fix with reasoning (§3.2).
- **Budgeted commit inspection:** Four tools expose the candidate list, commit details, individual file diffs, and answer submission. Diff rendering prioritizes files and compresses content within character budgets (§3.2, Appendix A.3).
- **Integrated retrieval and evaluation:** BM25, CVE-date signals, and Qwen3 embeddings feed reciprocal-rank fusion and a top-100 shortlist. These components build on prior methods; the paper’s contribution is their integration with the selector and its controlled evaluation, rather than new foundational retrieval algorithms (§3.1, §4).

The system uses frozen models without fine-tuning or external search APIs. The agent has a 15-iteration limit (§3).

The fixed-candidate controls support the value of the **combined selection interface**. They do not isolate listwise comparison from prompting, model reasoning, and content access. Moreover, Favia receives ten candidates while PatchHolmes can inspect one hundred, so that comparison does not equalize inspection scope.

Recall@1 measures the submitted choice; higher-cutoff metrics also incorporate inspected candidates and appended Phase 1 ordering (Appendix A.11). “One conversation” includes multiple model turns and should not be read as one inference call.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ population limitation:** Evaluation requires already known fixes; CVEs lacking patch links are excluded. The independent check retains this constraint (Limitations).
- **Authors’ sampling limitation:** Large-repository preprocessing imputations distort commit-count and diff-length tails. The working subset fails three of nine distribution tests; thirty CVEs with incomplete preprocessing were removed (Appendix A.1).
- **Authors’ label limitation:** Unrecorded legitimate fixes can score as misses, particularly for backports and multi-commit repairs (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Tool ablations combine capability changes. They do not independently establish the benefit of file drill-down, rendering heuristics, or listwise versus pointwise selection under identical content budgets (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—prompt sensitivity:** The numerical rank hint disagrees with final retrieval statistics, and no experiment removes it. Its contribution remains unresolved despite the authors’ assurance (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—generalization and reproducibility:** Full-corpus scale checking evaluates IRCoT, not PatchHolmes. GitHubAD was privately shared, and PatchFinder_top10 lacks an explicit license (Appendices A.5 and A.13).
- **Authors’ deployment boundary:** Predictions require human verification; calibrated ranking confidence remains future work (§4.2, Appendix A.15).

## Open research questions
1. **How much gain comes specifically from joint comparison?** Existing controls change inspection scope and capabilities. Compare listwise and pointwise selectors on identical pools, rendered content, prompts, and token budgets.
2. **Which inspection tools justify their cost?** Diff access is ablated jointly, leaving drill-down and rendering effects unresolved. Independently vary file drill-down, priority heuristics, and character budgets while measuring accuracy and tokens.
3. **Does performance extend to missing-link and very large repository cases?** Known-fix filtering and sampling artifacts leave these populations uncertain. Prospectively evaluate unresolved CVEs with expert adjudication and fully parsed large histories.
4. **Can the selector abstain reliably?** Fixed pools sometimes contain no recorded fix, while confidence calibration is unfinished. Test an explicit abstention option on verified present-fix and absent-fix pools.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark results; **medium** in causal and deployment conclusions.

Material gaps include isolated prompt/tool ablations, PatchHolmes results on the full source corpus, prospective missing-link evaluation, repeated-run variability, and separate indexing cost measurements. Micro-F1 is described but no numerical micro-F1 results appear in the supplied text.
