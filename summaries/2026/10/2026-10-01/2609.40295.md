# How Much Is an AI Token Worth? Scaling Laws for Wild AI-Generated Web Text

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40295) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40295)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the authors’ small-model pretraining experiments, naturally occurring AI-generated web text generally worsens human-text prediction once human data is sufficient, and a scaling law separating benefit from harm predicts this effect better than tested alternatives (§4; Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Pretraining corpora increasingly contain AI-generated text, yet conventional token-count scaling laws treat it like human text. The authors distinguish this “wild AI” text—produced for web readers by many models—from curated synthetic training data and recursively generated model-collapse data. They investigate when adding it helps, when filtering improves performance, and whether mixed validation sets conceal degradation on human text (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled pretraining study:** Train 800 nanochat models with fixed human corpora and varying additions of AI or fresh human text; evaluate human, mixed, and AI-text targets separately. Additional experiments compare filtering and human-data repetition (§4.1; Appendix B).
- **Benefit–harm scaling law:** Combine a saturating effective-data benefit with a logarithmic harm penalty dependent on model size and human budget. The law recovers Chinchilla without AI text and permits marginal AI-token value to become negative (§4.3, Equations 2–4).
- **Corpus and pipeline audit:** Construct WildAI with provenance, topic, and format labels; measure AI prevalence and quality-filter survival. The authors report releasing the corpus, checkpoints, and reproduction code (§2; reproducibility statement, p. 12).
- **Evaluation guidance:** Demonstrate that improvements on AI text can mask worsening human-text loss in mixed validation sets (§5.2; Figure 22).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| AI prevalence rises in filtered crawls | Pangram labels **27.5%** of June 2026 and **31.1%** of August 2026 FineWeb-filtered tokens AI; monthly estimates use sampled documents. | §2; Appendix A.2 |
| Quality filters favor AI-labeled documents | In separate pipeline extractions from the same crawl files, FineWeb retains **29.3% AI vs. 12.8% human** documents; DCLM retains **14.5% vs. 1.5%**. | Appendix A.1; Figure 9 |
| Adding AI differs from adding fresh human text | At figure-labeled human budgets around **5 tokens/parameter**, AI additions lower C4 loss; around **20–40**, larger additions raise it while fresh human additions lower it. Labels are rounded budgets. | Figure 3; Table 6 |
| Proposed law best predicts paired human-loss changes | Fit on **726 models, 19.9M–268M**; reserved cohort comprises **74 models at 477M/973M**. On held-out AI additions, C4 paired log-loss RMSE is **0.83×10⁻³**, versus **1.41×10⁻³** for Shukor joint and **4.32×10⁻³** for Chinchilla. | §4.3; Table 1 |
| Filtering can improve loss despite reducing data | Removing AI from a **22.3% AI mixture** improves C4 loss above approximately **10–15 human tokens/parameter**, with a size-dependent threshold; the proposed law predicts direction correctly in **18/19 pairs**. | §5.1; Figure 5 |
| Human repetition outperforms AI additions in tested small models | At **19.9M/35.8M**, figure-labeled **20 human tokens/parameter**, C4 loss is **2.3% lower** with one added human-data pass than equivalent AI additions; the gap reaches **6.3–6.4%** after eight added passes. | §5.1; Appendix F.3 |
| Mixed validation conceals human-text degradation | Among **243 AI additions** worsening FW26 human-text loss, a validation mixture with **22.3% AI** reports **95.5%** as improvements. | Figures 6, 22 |
| Compute penalty is a model prediction | At **268M and 20 human tokens/parameter**, the fitted law predicts **1.6×** compute to match human-only C4 performance at **31.1% AI**. | Figure 1; Appendices F.1 |
| Downstream scores do not track human loss consistently | At figure-labeled **20 human tokens/parameter**, additions at least as large as the human corpus raise CORE by **1.6 points with AI** and **1.9 with fresh human**; C4 loss respectively rises **2.3%** and falls **3.0%** across **23/6 runs**. | Appendix G |

The evidence supports target-dependent effects within this training setup, rather than universal degradation from AI text. AI additions improve AI-target prediction; the reported optimal AI shares above 90% are fitted-law optima with human data fixed, not fixed-compute optima (§5.2; Appendix F.2). The compute penalties and future web shares are projections, not directly measured large-model outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only English web pretraining and one architecture/training recipe are studied. Fits stop at 268M and tests at 973M; larger models could behave differently (§6).
- **Author-stated capability limitation:** Models are too small for meaningful downstream performance; MMLU remains at chance. Human-text loss degradation therefore does not establish broad capability degradation (§6; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—provenance uncertainty:** Detector labels define the populations; Mixed documents are excluded, and EditLens preselection makes WildAI unlike a natural web sample. Topic and format distributions also differ, leaving provenance versus distribution mismatch incompletely separated (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—validation selection:** Alternative law forms are compared on the reserved sizes during model selection, weakening a strict interpretation of those sizes as untouched final-test data (Appendix D.1; Table 8).
- **Statistical limits:** Most runs use one seed; reserved-size runs have no additional-seed replicates. Bootstrap comparisons hold fits fixed, and the C4/FW22 advantage over Shukor joint is not significant at the reported interval level when restricted to AI/human ratios below one (Appendix B; Table 12).
- **Metric-dependent ranking:** The proposed law does not lead C4 absolute-loss prediction or paired mixed/AI-target prediction (Tables 9–10).

## Open research questions
- **Does the benefit–harm pattern persist at larger scales and human budgets?** No model above 973M is tested. Next experiment: matched multi-billion-parameter runs with multiple seeds and both loss and downstream evaluation.
- **How much harm reflects provenance versus topic/format mismatch?** The corpora differ substantially in composition. Next experiment: match human and AI pools by topic, format, and filtering criteria before repeating the mixture study.
- **Can models learn AI input without adopting its output style?** The paper proposes tagging or loss masking but does not test them. Next experiment: compare these interventions against ordinary mixing on AI-input comprehension, human-text loss, and generated style (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-setting results; **medium** in broader filtering recommendations and compute extrapolations.

Material gaps include large-model validation, multilingual and post-training results, independent ground-truth provenance validation on contemporary training text, an untouched test set after law-form selection, and strong downstream capability evidence. The supplied text reports releases, but their availability and reproducibility were not independently checked.
