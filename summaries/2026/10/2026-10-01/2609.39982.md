# Mid-Harness: Scaling Actions Between Model and Harness for Terminal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39982) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39982)


## One-sentence takeaway
With a fixed terminal-agent generator and harness, selecting among sampled actions improves task success when verification is capable, but weak verification captures little benefit from wider sampling (Sections 4.1–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Terminal agents commit to commands that change the environment, so a poor action can hinder later progress even when the model could generate a better alternative. The authors ask when additional computation **before execution** improves complete trajectories, and identify candidate diversity and verification quality as jointly important factors (Section 1).

The gap is primarily empirical: prior work already verifies actions, but the authors seek a systematic comparison of candidate width, verification mechanism, verifier capability, and composition with trajectory scaling while holding the generator and harness fixed (Sections 1, 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Model-call wrapper:** Mid-Harness samples several actions from the same history, verifies them without execution, and forwards one existing candidate to the unchanged harness. Unchosen candidates are discarded; only one environment instance is required per trajectory (Sections 2–2.1).
- **Controlled verification study:** The authors compare listwise selection, independent pointwise scoring, and pairwise ranking. The default eight-candidate pairwise implementation uses a ring followed by four pivots, rather than exhaustive comparison (Section 2.2; Appendix B.2).
- **Verifier distillation:** LoRA training transfers frontier-model pairwise rationales, scores, and preferences into TMAX verifiers using 117,631 training examples. The generator remains unchanged (Section 4.3; Appendix B.3).
- **Composition and diagnostics:** Experiments combine action selection with Best-of-*T* and Sequential Refine, test transfer across models and harnesses, and analyze teacher agreement and remaining verification failures (Sections 5–6).

The distinctive contribution is this controlled study and integration point; action verification itself is acknowledged as prior work (Section 7).

These results support improved benchmark task completion from capable action selection, including gains with an unchanged generator. They do not directly measure action correctness or perfect-verifier candidate coverage. Offline agreement measures imitation of the teacher, and cost comparisons use accounting assumptions rather than measured deployment prices or latency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** No gold action labels establish verification correctness, candidate coverage, or attainable success under perfect selection. Different online choices require new environment runs (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Generator reasoning effort is fixed; action scaling is not compared against spending equivalent compute on one candidate. Every step receives sampling and verification, leaving adaptive allocation untested (Appendix A).
- **Statistical scope:** Only three runs per task are used. Four of six reported base-versus-Mid-Harness confidence intervals include zero; transfer evaluations include small software subsets, and task groups show regressions (Appendices B.5, C.4–C.5).
- **Diagnostic validity:** Failure categories are model judgments relative to a teacher. Offline selected-candidate agreement excludes invalid states and uses win counts rather than online margin-weighted aggregation (Appendices D.1–D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Decision-only results cannot isolate the value of reasoning text because response format, aggregation, and training targets change together. The authors also acknowledge missing usage records and separate live runs (Appendix A; Appendices B.2–B.3).
- **Deployment scope:** Benchmark completion does not establish command safety or security; evaluated environments are isolated (Ethics statement).

## Open research questions
- **Can verifier training close the frontier gap?** Distillation leaves substantial disagreement. Compare current distillation with training on observed command outcomes, holding generator, candidates, and compute fixed (Appendix A).
- **Which steps warrant verification?** Uniform verification is costly and adaptive allocation remains untested. Compare uncertainty-triggered sampling against every-step verification at matched token budgets (Appendix A).
- **How accurate is action selection independently of the teacher?** Gold labels are absent. Clone intermediate environments, execute alternative candidates, and measure downstream outcomes against verifier rankings (Appendix A).
- **When does decision-only verification help?** Its effects vary by model size and width. Run a matched factorial experiment separating response format, training targets, and aggregation (Tables 6, 8; Appendix B.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported experimental pattern; more limited for general deployment claims.

Material information unavailable includes confirmed public code/data/checkpoint releases, gold action outcomes, measured end-to-end latency and deployment cost, matched reasoning-budget comparisons, and confidence intervals for frontier, mechanism, composition, and transfer comparisons.
