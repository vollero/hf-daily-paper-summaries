# WUSH-KV: KV Cache Quantization with Data-Adaptive Transforms

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38121) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38121)


## One-sentence takeaway
WUSH-KV uses calibrated, product-aware invertible transforms to improve low-bit KV-cache quality, outperforming OSCAR on all tested Qwen3-8B reasoning tasks at 2 bits while leaving inference-speed benefits unmeasured.

## Problem and motivation
KV-cache memory and repeated reads grow with context length and serving batch size, but aggressive quantization damages attention scores and outputs. The authors argue that transforms should account for both cached-vector statistics and downstream sensitivity: queries consume keys, while output-projection weights consume values. Unlike OSCAR’s orthogonal transforms, general invertible transforms permit anisotropic rescaling to balance these factors (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **KV-specific adaptation of WUSH:** Separate per-KV-head transforms use calibration Gram matrices and local sensitivity Hessians. Value transforms fold into projection weights; key transforms and query compensation run after normalization and RoPE (§4.2, Algorithm 1). The underlying WUSH construction is prior work.
- **Clipping-aware theoretical analysis:** Ideal, undamped WUSH is asymptotically near-optimal for QuEST among sensitivity-balanced transforms, under an additive rounding-noise model and explicit tail and clipping-alignment assumptions (§4.3, Appendix B).
- **Practical integration and evaluation:** WUSH-KV combines quantized cache entries with full-precision sink/recent windows and integrates into SGLang using OSCAR-style percentile-clipped affine quantization (§§4.4, 5.3).
- **Attention-aware variant:** WUSH-A substitutes forward-pass-derived attention-output Hessians, including interactions among query heads; its modest reconstruction improvement motivates retaining simpler Hessians for the main method (§5.1, Appendix C).

The evidence supports improved quantization quality under the reported settings, especially for Qwen3-8B. It does not establish universal superiority, preservation of BF16 quality, or measured throughput gains. The Hadamard baseline is a fixed-transform comparison, not the complete published TurboQuant codec (§5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Dense post-RoPE key/query transforms add online computation; throughput evaluation and system optimization remain future work (§6).
- **Theory scope:** The guarantee covers ideal undamped WUSH and modeled rounding noise. Experiments use damping, and the downstream affine quantizer lacks the same proof (§4.3, §5.3, Appendix B.1).
- **Comparison caveat:** Main WUSH and OSCAR downstream runs use different calibration corpora and substantially different calibration budgets. Matched-data OSCAR controls help assess this issue but do not fully isolate transform choice (§5.3, Appendix D.2).
- **Author-stated:** OSCAR’s published scores were not exactly reproduced; WUSH also reuses OSCAR’s clipping settings without retuning (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation across three Qwen3 models leaves transfer to other architectures uncertain. Long-context accuracy losses, particularly on MRCR, limit claims of reliable aggressive compression (Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Three-seed means and standard deviations do not establish statistical significance for small score differences; no significance tests are reported (Table 2).

## Open research questions
- **Does WUSH-KV improve serving throughput after transform overhead?** Runtime benefits remain unmeasured (§6). Benchmark latency, throughput, and peak memory across context lengths and batch sizes.
- **Can cheaper structured transforms retain the quality gains?** Dense online key transforms are the stated computational limitation (§6). Compare structured approximations against dense WUSH at matched cache settings.
- **How should calibration and clipping be jointly selected?** Calibration sensitivity and untuned WUSH clipping leave this unresolved (§5.3, Appendix D.2). Sweep calibration domains, token budgets, and WUSH-specific clipping thresholds.
- **Can attention-aware sensitivity improve autoregressive and long-context quality?** WUSH-A is evaluated primarily through reconstruction (§5.1). Compare WUSH-A and WUSH on downstream reasoning and MRCR with identical calibration and quantization.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and results; practical speed benefits and broader generalization remain unestablished.

Material missing information includes measured latency/throughput and realized total-memory savings, explicit code or transform release availability, complete downstream sampling settings, exact long-context seed counts, empirical verification of the theorem’s assumptions, and end-to-end WUSH-A results.
