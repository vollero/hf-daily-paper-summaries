# Retrieval Capacity of Self-Attention Under Competition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37879) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37879)


## One-sentence takeaway
Loss-preserving attention set size depends on token ranking, context competition, and retained-weight normalization, so it cannot directly measure how much distinct useful information a model retrieves (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask how many context tokens each attention operation must retain to preserve language-model performance. Prior geometric analysis found structured attention-selected sets, but did not establish their predictive sufficiency. This paper addresses that gap while distinguishing an unobserved “useful” token set from the larger observable set potentially needed because of ranking errors and aggregation effects (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Operational measurement:** Without retraining, retain Top-N tokens independently at every query, head, and layer, recomputing selections during the intervened forward pass. Rank by attention weight or weighted-value norm; primarily delete other contributions without renormalizing. Estimate the smallest tested set size satisfying a loss tolerance (§2).
- **Useful-token framework:** Derive bounds connecting ranking inversions to recovery of a hypothesized useful set. Its membership remains unobserved, and the ranking hypothesis is not directly tested (§2; Appendix A.1).
- **Functional and controlled evaluation:** Compare geometry and loss across nine checkpoints; extend natural context while fixing prediction targets; add background around fixed annotated BABILong support (§3).
- **Aggregation controls and conditional theory:** Compare deletion with renormalization and derive mechanisms through which competition or attention-mass preservation can require growing sets without additional distinct information (§3.4; Appendix F).

These results establish an intervention-dependent measure of average loss preservation. They support competition and aggregation as relevant mechanisms, but do not identify the latent useful-token count, demonstrate inference speedups, or establish universal context-length scaling (§4). Thresholds are locally refined tested crossings, rather than certified minima over every integer budget (Appendices B.2–B.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated interpretive limits:** A common budget hides head/layer heterogeneity; annotated support is only a partial relevance reference; average loss can conceal individual failures; preserving a weak baseline does not demonstrate successful task solving (§4; Appendix E.3).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Natural-context extension adds predictive information as well as competitors. Mistral’s context sweep is incomplete, and normalization controls do not include the same matched-target context sweep (Appendices B.3, F.6).
- **Author-stated comparison risks:** Cross-model windows need not match; the 24B checkpoint uses 8-bit weights; language-model normalization comparisons lack verified document pairing (Appendices B.2, B.5, E.1).
- **Author-stated theoretical limits:** Independence, stable weight distributions, coherence, and downstream sensitivity assumptions are not empirically established for the tested models (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** The competition measurements provide stronger evidence of support displacement than of its causal contribution to loss: Qwen’s functional response is nonmonotonic, and retained representations can carry information indirectly (§3.3; Appendix E.5).

## Open research questions
- **How much context-dependent growth survives renormalization?** The matched-target normalization sweep is missing. Repeat the context-length experiment with paired deletion and renormalization, tracking retained mass and retained/discarded value means (Appendix F.6).
- **Does weakest-support rank predict functional requirements?** Mean displacement does not supply the quantity used by the complete-recovery theory. Measure worst-ranked support tokens and compare support-complete budgets with answer-loss thresholds (Appendices D.1, F.1).
- **Can adaptive head/layer budgets preserve loss more efficiently?** Local sensitivity varies, and separate interventions are not additive. Test jointly optimized heterogeneous budgets against the common-budget protocol on held-out documents (Appendix E.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported measurements and their qualified interpretation; lower for mechanism attribution and broader generalization.

Material information unavailable or unverified includes released experiment code and raw per-document measurements, historical pairing of normalization runs, immutable 24B environment/checkpoint revisions, exhaustive integer-budget crossings, the matched-target renormalization sweep, and empirical validation of the theoretical assumptions (Appendices B/E/F; Reproducibility Statement).
