# CoEvoWhen: Policy-Tool Cooevolution for Ultra-Long Video Temporal Grounding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40048) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40048)


## One-sentence takeaway
CoEvoWhen jointly evolves a frozen VLM’s observation policies and executable media tools from labeled grounding trajectories, improving reported long-video localization accuracy while reducing cumulative visual tokens and transferring to video QA.

## Problem and motivation
Temporal grounding must find query-matching intervals across videos lasting tens of minutes or hours, then resolve brief events and precise boundaries. Sparse observations can miss targets; repeated detailed inspection consumes substantial visual context. The authors identify a gap in agentic approaches: observation policies and media-processing tools generally remain predefined, although their capabilities depend on each other (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint policy–tool evolution:** An external coding-capable updater analyzes execution trajectories and ground-truth intervals, revising policy documents and tool descriptions, interfaces, and implementations. VLM weights remain frozen (§3.2).
- **Adaptive evidence acquisition:** Evolution develops compact image-based global search, candidate retention and boundary refinement, plus selective video verification of motion and continuity. Tools can be created, consolidated, or retired (§3.2; Fig. 5).
- **Reusable inference skill:** The final skill stays fixed during evaluation; the same VLM selects and interprets observations without a separate stronger planner. Grounding skills also support multiple-choice QA without further evolution (§3.3; Appendix B.2.3).

The main setup uses 100 labeled queries from distinct VUE-TR-V2 videos, updating after every four queries in one pass. Separate skills are evolved for each tested backbone; this is not a demonstration of transferring one skill unchanged between VLMs (Appendices B.1.1–B.1.3).

These results support improved accuracy and visual-token efficiency under the reported settings. Base-versus-evolved comparisons hold task prompts, decoding, interaction budget, and scoring constant (Appendix B.2.3). Token accounting includes visual history received again in later calls, so it measures cumulative model input rather than unique footage inspected (Appendix B.2.2).

They do not establish lower total monetary cost or wall-clock latency: updater computation, text tokens, and media-processing time are not quantified. Comparisons against other methods use different backbones and observation formats, making the matched base-skill comparisons stronger evidence for the effect of evolution (Appendix B.2.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evolution uses a limited labeled set from one source. Broader sources, larger scales, and unlabeled feedback remain unexplored. Observation delegation and additional modalities are future work (Appendix E).
- **Observed uneven gains:** Qwen3.6-27B worsens on ExtremeWhenBench’s Environment category, CoMET-Bench false-positive rate, and grounding queries containing 8–15 events (Tables 11, 13–14). Aggregate improvement does not imply improvement in every category.
- **Analyst inference:** Testing three separately evolved Qwen backbones supports repeatability within those models, but leaves unchanged-skill transfer to other model families unresolved (Appendix B.1.3).
- **Analyst inference:** No repeated evolution runs, uncertainty estimates, or evolution-order sensitivity analysis are reported. Greedy evaluation alone does not establish stability of the updater’s skill-generation process.
- **Reproducibility gap:** The text supplies prompts and runtime settings, but not complete final policy documents, evolved tool implementations, or verified release contents.

## Open research questions
1. **Can evolution work without labeled intervals?** Labels currently drive updates (Appendix E). Compare supervised evolution with self-consistency or verification-based feedback on the same held-out queries.
2. **How stable are gains across evolution data and order?** Only one selected evolution set and single-pass procedure are described. Repeat evolution with shuffled orders and independently sampled sets, reporting accuracy and cost variation.
3. **Can one evolved skill transfer between model families?** Current backbone experiments evolve separate skills. Apply an unchanged skill to other VLMs and compare it with model-specific evolution.
4. **How can dense multi-event grounding improve?** High-event-count queries retain low F1@0.5 (Table 14). Test explicit candidate coverage and event-count auditing under a matched observation budget.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the method and reported results; **medium** in broader generalization and efficiency conclusions.

Material missing information includes evolution compute and latency, total inference cost, repeated-run uncertainty, QA evaluation counts, complete evolved artifacts, and unchanged-skill transfer across model families.
