# WorldAuditBench: Interactive 3D World Auditing with Multimodal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40325) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40325)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorldAuditBench tests interactive anomaly discovery in simulated 3D worlds, where the best evaluated VLM auditor achieves 42.3% success versus 83.4% for humans, although unequal exploration budgets limit causal comparisons between auditing paradigms (Table 2; §5.1).

## Problem and motivation
Visually convincing environments can contain defects that invalidate agent behavior, such as traversable walls or objects that disappear after revisiting. Detecting these requires agents to gather evidence through movement, interaction, and comparisons across time and viewpoints. The authors identify a gap between benchmarks using fixed visual observations and interactive benchmarks focused on task completion or out-of-place objects (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and taxonomy:** 213 manually constructed tasks across 13 environments and 27 scenes, covering 15 anomaly categories in five families: static physics, interactive physics, spatial consistency, temporal consistency, and semantic consistency. Each main task contains one target anomaly and passes review by at least two independent validators (§3; Appendices A–B).
- **Auditing comparison:** A VLM agent reasons, acts, retrieves observations, and reports during exploration; a two-stage auditor uses Open-P2P 1.2B exploration followed by offline VLM analysis of shared trajectories. Five VLM backbones are evaluated (§§4–5).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Studies examine starting distance, budget, task guidance, coexisting anomalies, and the distinction between exposing an anomaly and identifying it (§6). These are benchmark analyses; the paper does not introduce a new trained model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best agent remains below humans | Across 213 tasks, GPT-6 Astra VLM achieves 42.3% SR; humans achieve 83.4%. VLMs receive 40 environment actions; humans receive up to ten minutes per task. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Interactive auditing generally improves pooled SR | Across five backbones, VLM SR spans 8.5–42.3%; VLA–VLM spans 6.6–17.4%. Qwen reverses the pattern: 8.5% versus 12.7%. VLA exploration uses 60 simulated seconds, sampled every 0.5 seconds. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Offline analysis costs less | GPT-6 Astra costs $2.955/task for VLM auditing versus $0.281/task for VLA–VLM. Given prerecorded trajectories, offline analysis is reported as 2.7–10.3× faster across backbones. | Table 2; Fig. 4 |
| Category guidance substantially affects discovery | Gemini on 126 Unreal tasks: VLM SR is 33.3% by default, 31.0% without demonstrations, and 15.9% without demonstrations or type hints. | Table 3; §6.1 |
| Finding every coexisting anomaly is difficult | Gemini, 40 actions, 42 runs per multi-anomaly condition: all-anomaly SR is 7.1% with two anomalies and 4.8% with three; recall is 29.8% and 29.4%. | Fig. 5; §§6.2 |
| Adaptive auditing exposes more anomalies | GPT-6 Astra on 213 tasks: human-rated exposure is 68.1% for VLM versus 34.7% for VLA exploration; geometric coverage is 91.1% versus 47.4%. | Fig. 6; §6.3 |
| Advantage varies by engine | Gemini VLM versus VLA–VLM SR is 33.3% versus 5.6% on 126 Unreal tasks, but 31.0% versus 34.5% on 87 Three.js tasks. | Table 9 |
| Judge agreement has partial validation | On human-baseline reports, GPT-6 Astra judgments achieve 91.72% agreement with human judgments and Cohen’s κ of 0.645. | Table 7; Appendix C.4 |

These results establish difficulty under the supplied guidance and evaluation protocol. They support the authors’ interpretation that adaptive evidence gathering helps, but do not isolate its causal contribution from differences in exploration budgets, controllers, tools, and harnesses. Success measures identifying the annotated target; it does not establish comprehensive auditing or penalize additional false reports (Appendix C.4).

Table 2 also contradicts the introduction’s stated VLM range of 28.2–42.3%: the full table includes 15.0% and 8.5%.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged comparison constraints:** Paradigms use different exploration budgets, and each backbone is evaluated together with its own harness (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference—restricted scope:** Handcrafted, bounded, predominantly single-anomaly tasks with category hints do not establish performance on naturally occurring defects, anomaly-free scenes, or unrestricted auditing (§3; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—scoring validity:** Extra reports do not cancel a correct target finding, and screenshots are not universally required. Target SR therefore cannot quantify false-positive burden or consistently demonstrate visual verification (Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference—memory attribution:** The authors credit memory with improved discovery, but no dedicated memory-removal ablation is reported. Paradigm comparisons cannot isolate memory’s effect (§6.3).
- **Analyst inference—judge generalization:** Agreement is validated on human reports; equivalent reliability on model-generated reports is not demonstrated (Appendix C.4).
- **Author-stated measurement limit:** Geometric coverage can count occluded targets and does not establish that an anomaly occurred or was recognized (Appendix E).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Artifact release is promised; Unreal packages will exclude original project sources and standalone third-party assets (p. 10; Appendix A.2).

## Open research questions
- **How much improvement comes specifically from adaptive reasoning?** Budgets and controllers differ. Next experiment: match exploration resources and tool access while comparing adaptive and fixed-trajectory auditing (§5.1).
- **Does explicit memory improve temporal discovery independently?** Temporal anomalies remain difficult, and memory lacks an isolated ablation. Next experiment: disable frame retrieval, history, and notes separately under otherwise identical conditions (§§5.2, 6.3).
- **Can auditors discover defects without knowing that one exists or its category?** Removing guidance reduces success; anomaly-free evaluation is absent. Next experiment: mix clean and faulty scenes, withhold hints, and measure target recall alongside false reports (§6.1; Appendix C.4).
- **What improves complete multi-anomaly discovery?** The small Gemini study finds low complete-discovery rates. Next experiment: compare coverage-oriented exploration and revisit policies across backbones and budgets (§6.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark description and tabulated results; causal explanations remain less secure.

Material missing information includes confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, exact human-score aggregation, judge-validation sample size, detailed human-exposure rating procedures, isolated memory ablations, and confirmation that promised artifacts are available.
