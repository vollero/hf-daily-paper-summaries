# Dating the Model: Hidden Dates in System Prompts Affect LLM Evaluation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36931) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36931)


## One-sentence takeaway
Changing only the system-prompt date shifts benchmark scores and model rankings under otherwise fixed configurations, with reported worst-to-best differences reaching 14.42 percentage points on GSM8K (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Evaluation results can become difficult to reproduce when providers or chat templates silently insert a changing date into system prompts. The authors identify this metadata as an overlooked source of variation beyond previously studied hardware, batching, precision, and prompt formatting (§§1–2; Appendix B). Their concern is fair comparison: identical user-visible prompts may conceal different model inputs.

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled date intervention:** Evaluate nine open-weight LLMs across dates in 2024 on MCQA, math reasoning, code generation, and translation, changing only the date. MCQA uses next-token probabilities; generation fixes seeds and enables deterministic CUDA operations (§3; Appendix C).
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Measure score and calibration changes, ranking reversals, date correlations, prompting effects, and comparisons with other configuration changes (§§4–5).
- **Practical API check and mitigation:** Observe GPT-5.1 across one week with an empty user-defined system prompt; recommend removing dates where possible or fixing and reporting them. Date-free MCQA scores remain within the date-sweep ranges (§5.2; §6; Appendix E).

These are empirical findings and evaluation recommendations; the supplied text does not establish a released software package or new benchmark.

## Evidence and main results

Accuracy, pass@1, and ECE differences below are expressed as percentage points; CV values are percentages.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| MCQA scores and rankings change | Across nine models and three datasets, mean worst-to-best date accuracy difference is 2.52 points; maximum is 6.06 points for GPT-OSS (120B) on GPQA. MMLU rankings reverse between illustrated consecutive dates. | Table 1; Figure 1b |
| Larger differences occur in generated answers | GSM8K accuracy differences average 7.75 points and reach 14.42 for GPT-OSS (120B); HumanEval pass@1 differences average 4.81 points and reach 7.32 for Llama 3.1 (8B) and GPT-OSS (20B). | Table 2 |
| Translation metrics also vary | GPT-OSS (120B), English→Czech: worst-to-best differences of 2.84 BLEU and 2.08 chrF across dates. | Table 2 |
| Calibration shifts | Maximum ECE difference is 6.15 points for GPT-OSS (120B) on GPQA across dates. | Table 8 |
| No broadly favorable date pattern emerges | MCQA median date–accuracy Spearman correlation is 0.02; cross-dataset and cross-model median Pearson correlations are −0.03 and 0.01. Both compared accuracies move in the same direction on 51% of dates. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| Prompting does not eliminate sensitivity | Five-shot MCQA mean accuracy difference is 2.27 points versus 2.52 zero-shot. CoT amplifies sensitivity in the single tested Llama 3.1 (8B)/MMLU setting. | Table 4; §5.3; Figure 4 |
| Date variation exceeds selected system effects | Llama 3.1 (8B)/MMLU accuracy CV: date 0.78%, batch size 0.61%, precision 0.52%, GPU 0.48%; option order 0.75% and system wording 0.78%. | Table 5 |
| Proprietary-model scores vary across days | GPT-5.1 on three MCQA datasets, December 3–9, 2025: authors report up to 4% accuracy variation on GPQA; paired runs on each day give identical scores. | §5.2; Figure 3; footnote 3 |
| Removing dates preserves scores within observed ranges | Date-free accuracy and ECE for all nine models on the three MCQA datasets fall within their date-sweep ranges, according to the authors. | Appendix E; Table 9 |

The controlled experiments support sensitivity to changed date text, rather than randomness with identical inputs; Appendix A explicitly makes this distinction. They do not establish that every deployment injects dates or that dates uniquely cause this sensitivity. Comparable system-wording effects support a broader prompt-sensitivity interpretation. The API experiment supports a date explanation but does not directly expose server-side prompts or exclude every other daily backend change.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Models and tasks are selective; dialogue and summarization are untested. Date format, position, and year are fixed, and proprietary evaluation covers only GPT-5.1 over one week (Limitations, p. 6).
- **Analyst inference—restricted ablations:** CoT amplification and comparisons with hardware, precision, batching, and wording are tested only on Llama 3.1 (8B)/MMLU (§§5.3, 5.5–5.6). Broader conclusions require replication.
- **Analyst inference—temporal screening:** GPT-OSS (120B) labels every evaluated item time-independent; independent human validation is not reported (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Lower confidence on date-inconsistent predictions supports an association with uncertainty, but does not establish a causal mechanism (Appendix E; Table 7).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The paper describes a complete 2024 sweep but also states it requires 365 runs; the precise evaluated date list is unavailable. Uncertainty intervals and exact numerical CoT sensitivity differences are not supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Does date representation change sensitivity?** Only one format and position are tested. Next experiment: cross date formats, prompt positions, and years while holding other settings fixed.
- **Does the larger CoT effect generalize?** Evidence comes from one model–dataset pair. Next experiment: repeat matched zero-shot and CoT sweeps across models and tasks, tracking output length and answer changes.
- **Can API date effects be isolated directly?** Daily repetitions are stable, but hidden backend state is unobserved. Next experiment: use provider-controlled date overrides with a fixed serving configuration.
- **How reliably does confidence identify fragile answers?** Inconsistent predictions have lower average confidence, yet some remain highly confident. Next experiment: test whether confidence predicts date instability on held-out items and models.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported controlled date sensitivity; **medium** in the API causal attribution and broad mechanism claims.

Material missing information includes executable code and release links, exact model revisions and generation defaults, the WMT test-set designation, precise date lists, uncertainty intervals, quantitative CoT deltas, and direct server-side prompt/configuration records.
