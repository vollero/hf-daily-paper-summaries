# Evaluating Bounded Autonomy in Regulated Agentic AI: A Diagnostic Harness with Constitutional Rewards, Escalation Labels, and Runtime Governance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37501) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37501)


## One-sentence takeaway
RegLLM makes answer-versus-escalate decisions measurable, but its small FCA-domain pilots show inconsistent adapter effects and runtime governance benefits confined to a weak offline baseline (§5).

## Problem and motivation
Regulated agents must decide when to answer and when to defer to qualified humans, because mistakes can carry regulatory or client consequences. The authors identify a gap between machine-checkable rewards, which cannot fully verify regulatory advice, and external guardrails, which do not themselves teach appropriate escalation. Their proposal treats escalation correctness as verifiable **conditional on reliable task labels** (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Label-based escalation objective:** A ReAct agent retrieves provisions, cites them, answers, or escalates. Its terminal decision is checked against a task’s `should_escalate` label (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Shared constitution for reward and governance:** One domain constitution supplies a soft alignment score and runtime policies. The hybrid reward combines citation validity, lexical grounding, schema compliance, escalation correctness, constitutional scoring, and a violation penalty; reward-ranked trajectories form DPO preference pairs (§§3.1–3.6). GRPO is proposed as a stretch configuration, not demonstrated.
- **Diagnostic evaluation and audit assets:** The harness separates deterministic checks, label-dependent decisions, and judge scores, and records governance interventions. Its principal empirical contribution is exposing inconsistent outcomes across two smoke-scale GPU runs (§§3.3–5). The supplied evidence does not establish improved learned escalation.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Governance improves the offline baseline | On 12 held-out tasks with a deterministic lexical policy and heuristic judge, escalation recall rises from 0 to 0.67, unsafe-action rate falls from 0.33 to 0.08, and citation validity reaches 1.0. | §5, offline reference narrative; Table 2 caption |
| Base-policy outcomes differ across GPU runs | Same eight-task split, seed 0, Qwen2.5-3B, low-temperature sampling, heuristic judge: Run A versus B has task success 0.25 versus 0.12, escalation recall 1.00 versus 0.50, and citation validity 0.50 versus 0.38. | §5, GPU pilots; Table 3 caption |
| Answer-quality DPO has opposite apparent escalation effects | Recall changes from 1.00 to 0.50 in Run A and 0.50 to 1.00 in Run B. Run A trains on 10 pairs; Run B on 20 pairs; both use 30 steps and the eight-task evaluation setting above. | Abstract; §5 |

These results demonstrate that the harness can expose differing outcomes and that its supervisor helps one weak offline policy. They do not establish a beneficial or harmful DPO effect, reliable governance for LLM policies, or the cause of cross-run variation. The authors identify hardware and software nondeterminism as plausible explanations, but do not isolate them. Adapter training budgets also differ between runs. The supplied text contains table captions and narrative results, but omits the actual table cells.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers one model, one domain, one seed, and very small held-out sets. It is a diagnostic demonstration rather than a benchmark.
- **Author-stated:** Labels are generated by the author from templates, without compliance-expert annotation or inter-rater agreement. Escalation “verification” therefore depends on unvalidated labels.
- **Author-stated:** Lexical overlap is only a grounding proxy. Experiments use a heuristic judge; expert calibration, judge consistency, and adversarial judge evaluation are absent.
- **Author-stated:** GPU governance misses relevant failures because citation gates are bypassed by cited answers and missed-escalation trajectories often never reach a terminal answer.
- **Analyst inference:** Two same-seed runs cannot estimate multi-seed variability or causally separate hardware, package versions, sampling, and training effects. Different preference counts further limit interpretation of the adapter comparison (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Software-level domain pluggability does not establish transfer beyond finance; no other domain is evaluated (Limitations).

## Open research questions
1. **Does escalation-aware DPO improve reliable act-versus-defer behaviour?** Its sole reported pilot shows no change. Next experiment: hold preference budgets and environments fixed, then compare base, answer-quality, and escalation-aware policies across multiple seeds and larger evaluation sets.
2. **Are escalation labels stable under expert judgment?** Template labels lack expert validation. Next experiment: obtain independent compliance-professional labels, measure agreement, and evaluate performance on agreed and contested cases.
3. **Can competence-boundary governance catch failures missed by citation gates?** GPU supervision is inactive. Next experiment: evaluate calibrated boundary detectors on both terminal answers and incomplete trajectories, measuring missed escalations and unnecessary referrals.
4. **What causes the observed run differences?** Hardware and software explanations remain hypotheses. Next experiment: cross controlled GPU hosts with pinned environments and deterministic settings while keeping training data identical.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and narrative findings are explicit, but the evidence is too limited for efficacy or causal conclusions.

Missing from the supplied text: Tables 1–3 bodies, ancillary schemas and sample tasks, exact task-success definition, reward weights and grounding thresholds, detailed training configuration, per-task trajectories, uncertainty estimates, and controlled variance attribution. The Limitations section also summarizes training as 10 preferences, whereas §5 specifies 20 pairs per Run B adapter.
