# Synthetic Pre-pretraining Survives Scale, but Not as a Grammatical Prior

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39827) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39827)


## One-sentence takeaway
Synthetic pre-pretraining improves downstream scores through the tested 7B-model and 100B-token settings, while task ablations support—but do not causally establish—a retrieval-based explanation over consistent grammatical transfer (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Synthetic pre-pretraining (PPT) warms up a language model on artificial sequences before natural-language pre-training (PT). The authors identify a gap in earlier evaluations: small models, short training budgets, and predominantly web-text corpora leave unclear whether gains survive greater capacity, prolonged optimization, or mixtures containing code and mathematics. They also reassess the explanation that PPT supplies a transferable grammatical prior (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
Models receive a synthetic warm-up, then initialize ordinary autoregressive PT with optimizer moments and the scheduler reset. Comparisons use random-initialized **PT-Only** and an equally long warm-up on disjoint PT-corpus text (**Control**). All models use the SmolLM3 architecture family and tokenizer (§4).

The contributions are:

- **Scaling evaluation:** test PPT across model sizes, training budgets, and four PT mixtures; this is an uneven experimental grid, with the largest runs restricted to selected settings (§§3–4, 6).
- **Explanation test:** compare downstream performance with BLiMP grammatical acceptability and verbatim-retrieval NLL, then contrast formal-language tasks with non-formal synthetic tasks (§§5.2–6).
- **Mixture ablations:** vary Marin’s mathematics and web content to examine when PPT benefits persist (§6).
- **Reported reusable artifacts:** release instructions, code, processed datasets, and model checkpoints (Reproducibility Statement, p. 10).

## Evidence and main results
Unless indicated otherwise, standard-run results are paired differences from PT-Only averaged over checkpoints at steps 5K–10K.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Downstream gains generally survive model scaling | With k-Shuffle Dyck and 21B PT tokens, 9/12 combinations of four mixtures and 500M/1B/3B models gain at least 0.6 downstream-average points. Across mixtures, mean gains are +0.8, +1.4, and +1.3 points, respectively. | §5.1; Table 9 |
| Synthetic warm-up beats the extra-training control | Dyck exceeds the identical 500-step text Control in 10/12 scale–mixture combinations; mean margins are +1.0, +1.0, and +1.1 points at 500M, 1B, and 3B. | §5.1 |
| Grammar gains are less consistent than retrieval gains | Dyck improves overall BLiMP accuracy in 9/12 combinations, but only 1 meets the authors’ checkpoint-stability criterion; verbatim full-stimulus NLL decreases in 12/12, with 7 stable reductions. “Stable” means mean improvement exceeds checkpoint SD. | §5.2; Tables 2, 9 |
| Formal grammar is unnecessary among the tested effective tasks | At 3B, averaging over four mixtures, downstream-average deltas are +1.3 points for Dyck, +0.9 for MP-Struct Core, +1.2 for NCA, and −7.2 for Set. Each effective task improves 3/4 mixtures; Set improves 0/4. | §6; Table 3b |
| Increasing math preserves gains; removing web weakens them | At 3B on Marin variants, raising math from 1.3% to 17.0% changes Dyck’s downstream gain from +1.9 to +2.0 points. Removing web, leaving 82% code and 18% math, yields +0.2 ± 0.5 points across checkpoints. | §6; Table 3a |
| Gains persist at extended budgets | Dyck improves the downstream average at all 14 reported budget points across 3B C4, 3B Marin, and 7B Marin. Final deltas are +1.6 and +1.2 points at 100B tokens for the 3B runs, and +1.0 at 75.5B tokens for 7B Marin. | §6; Table 4 |
| A sampled score comparison supports token savings | On 3B Marin, PPT reaches a downstream average of 62.3 after 63B PT tokens; PT-Only reaches 62.0 after 84B. The authors report at least 21B PT tokens saved. PPT itself uses approximately 1.05B tokens. | §6; Tables 6, 15 |
| Seed replication supports downstream gains more strongly than retrieval gains | At 3B Marin, three seeds yield downstream gains of +1.9, +1.9, and +1.7 points. Retrieval NLL improves in two seeds and worsens in one; the seed-mean delta is −0.033 with SD 0.044. | Appendix B.2; Tables 11–13 |

The evidence establishes downstream benefits in the tested settings and challenges **consistent grammatical acceptability improvement** as their explanation. The authors interpret the task contrasts and retrieval results as evidence for long-range retrieval transfer. **Analyst inference:** this is a plausible mechanism, but the experiments do not isolate retrieval as the causal mediator or exclude every form of grammatical transfer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated compute limits:** most configurations have one run. Seed replication covers only 3B Marin with Dyck and PT-Only; 7B evaluation covers only Marin and Dyck, while 100B-token evaluation covers only 3B C4 and Marin (§4.2; §6; Appendix B.2).
- **Unresolved mixture effect:** OLMo3’s weak downstream gains are not explained by web or mathematics proportions; the authors defer direct ablation (§6).
- **Analyst inference—retrieval measurement:** NLL is measured over the full stimulus, rather than only the repeated noun span. Consequently, lower NLL is an indirect measure of copying success (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism and generalization:** the task comparison does not independently manipulate positional retrieval while matching other task properties. The authors’ recommendation that any positional-retrieval task is a safe choice extends beyond the tested tasks (§6).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** checkpoint SD measures within-run variation, not uncertainty across independent training runs. Extended-budget results provide no corresponding seed uncertainty (Table 4; Appendix B.2).
- **Comparison boundary:** extended-run scores at 21B tokens use a different learning-rate schedule from the main experiments and are explicitly not directly comparable (Table 4 caption).

## Open research questions
- **Does retrieval causally mediate downstream gains?** Task properties covary in the current ablation. A next experiment could match synthetic tasks for vocabulary, difficulty, and length while varying positional-retrieval demands.
- **What suppresses PPT gains on OLMo3?** Matching its web share does not reproduce the effect. A next experiment could ablate OLMo3’s individual sources while holding training budgets and seeds fixed.
- **How broadly do gains survive larger-scale training?** The largest settings cover selected mixtures and tasks. A next experiment could replicate multiple PPT tasks and mixtures at 7B with matched tokens per parameter and multiple seeds.
- **Is the retrieval benefit specific to repeated spans?** Full-stimulus NLL and mixed seed results leave this uncertain. A next experiment could score repeated-span NLL and exact copying across controlled retrieval distances.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed protocols, appendix results, and a limited seed replication. Confidence is stronger for the tested downstream gains than for the proposed causal explanation.

Material information unavailable includes broad multi-seed uncertainty, controlled mechanism ablations, an explanation of the OLMo3 exception, repeated-span-specific retrieval results, and evaluations beyond the reported architecture and scale limits. Artifact availability is author-reported and was not independently verified.
