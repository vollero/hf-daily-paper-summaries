# False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39102) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102)


## One-sentence takeaway
Source-excluded proposer feedback reduces shared incorrect agreement and improves search QA performance in the authors’ two-model experiments, although correlated errors and substantial compute overhead remain (Table 1; Table 4; Appendix D).

## Problem and motivation
Self-evolving search agents generate questions and pseudo-labels, train a solver on them, and use that solver’s agreement to reward future question generation. The authors identify **co-cheating**: agreement improves because both agents reproduce shared errors, rather than because answers become more correct (§2).

The gap concerns feedback provenance: verifying labels does not prevent a solver trained on a source’s incorrect labels from subsequently rewarding those same errors (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic audit:** A post-hoc, source-grounded LLM auditor measures label correctness, solver correctness, false agreement, and correct responses denied credit. It audits the five responses actually used for proposer reward without influencing training (§2; Appendix B).
- **Multi-sample verification (MSV):** Three source-aware and three source-blind answers must produce compatible majorities; their consensus replaces the draft label, otherwise the task is rejected (§3.1).
- **CrossFit:** Two auxiliary solvers train on complementary source-document folds and score questions from the excluded fold. The main solver continues training on all admitted tasks; the proposer reward formula remains unchanged (§3.2).
- **Mechanism controls:** Fixed-proposal replay compares source exclusion against evaluator duplication, question-level splitting, and reduced auxiliary training (§§5.2–5.3).

The authors claim novelty in diagnosing and intervening on this feedback ancestry, explicitly disclaiming invention of self-play, verification, or cross-fitting itself (§6).

## Evidence and main results
Paired values below follow **4B / 9B** model order.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Coupled feedback accumulates shared errors | Mean false-agreement mass rises from **0.4% / 0.3%** in round 1 to **6.1% / 8.8%** in round 3, while agreement becomes increasingly optimistic relative to correctness. | §2; Figure 3 |
| CrossFit substantially reduces adaptive-loop false agreement | Round-3 means: Dr. Zero **6.1% / 8.8%**, MSV **5.7% / 7.2%**, CrossFit **3.0% / 3.7%**, combined **2.0% / 1.7%**. | Table 4 |
| CrossFit improves downstream QA | After three rounds, seven-benchmark macro Cover-EM is **48.8% / 51.2%**, versus Dr. Zero **40.0% / 42.8%** and Search-R1 **40.1% / 43.4%**. Evaluation uses a fixed **1,325-question** suite, one greedy trajectory per question, and matched tool budgets and extraction. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Verification adds little downstream benefit beyond CrossFit | Combined macro Cover-EM reaches **49.1% / 51.5%**, **0.3 percentage points** above CrossFit at each scale under the same evaluation. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Fixed-bank replay supports the provenance mechanism | On the same **3,000** saved questions and labels, source-ID exclusion reduces false agreement from coupled **5.8% / 7.3%** to **0.4% / 0.1%**; random question-level splitting yields **5.0% / 6.2%**. | §§5.2–5.3; Figure 7 |
| CrossFit incurs substantial overhead | Main CrossFit runs reserve **650 / 854 H200-hours**, versus Dr. Zero **379 / 476**, and take **81 / 107 hours**, versus **47 / 60**. Reserved budgets include waiting; external audit-service GPU usage is excluded. | Table 3; Appendix A |

The replay controls support source exclusion as a mechanism beyond curriculum changes or simply adding an evaluator. They do not establish universal independence or eliminate co-cheating. Adaptive-loop audit results and fixed-bank replay results describe different settings and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Shared pretraining, overlapping evidence, related sources, and adaptive proposals can preserve correlated errors. Source routing and checkpoint ancestry must actually enforce exclusion (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated:** Lower false agreement can reflect avoiding difficult tasks; downstream capability and difficulty-sensitive checks remain necessary. Robustness to connected sources and improved end-to-end efficiency are not established (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Audit conclusions depend on an automated judge and unresolved-case handling. Round-3 coverage is **85.3–86.1%** across treatments; the supplied text reports no quantitative human validation or judge-error estimate (Table 4; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization and uncertainty remain limited: experiments cover two backbones and three rounds; downstream tables lack run-level uncertainty. Figure 7 reports replay accuracy across five seeds, which does not establish downstream training variability (Figure 7; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Cover-EM accepts a reference appearing within the normalized prediction, so these scores do not establish strict exact-answer accuracy (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Does exclusion work across connected documents?** Source-ID folds allow related evidence across folds. Compare document-ID splits with evidence-overlap or connected-source splits (Appendix D).
- **Can the gains survive matched compute budgets?** CrossFit adds auxiliary training. Run complete adaptive loops with reduced auxiliary budgets and compute-matched coupled baselines; current half-budget evidence concerns replay (§5.3; Appendix A).
- **How reliable is the audit on unresolved or difficult cases?** Judge-error rates are unavailable. Conduct blinded human adjudication stratified by treatment, difficulty, and audit resolution status (Appendix B).
- **Does mitigation persist over longer evolution?** Only three rounds are reported. Extend training while tracking false agreement, task difficulty, and held-out QA performance (Table 2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported comparisons and mechanism controls, but leaves material reproducibility and validity details unavailable: training-source corpus specification, retrieval backend configuration, exact audit prompts and answer matcher, quantitative human audit validation, adaptive-run seed variability, and confirmed code/model/data releases.
