# Mitigating the Length-Scaling Tax with Online Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38854) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38854)


## One-sentence takeaway
Length Self-Distillation routes solved rollout groups to an EMA self-teacher and unsolved groups to reinforcement learning, reducing easy-query response length while maintaining or improving aggregate accuracy in the tested math and retrieval-agent settings.

## Problem and motivation
During reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), responses to already-solved queries can grow longer as the shared policy learns harder problems. All-correct groups have zero group-relative advantages, leaving no direct reward signal to preserve their concise behavior (§§1–3).

The authors identify two gaps: aggregate response length obscures this difficulty-dependent cost, and general compression can constrain useful exploration on hard problems. They introduce the **length-scaling tax (LST)** to measure excess tokens on frozen, previously solved query sets against an accuracy-qualified RL reference (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Conditional efficiency metric:** LST compares mean length on a frozen anchor-defined easy set with the shortest qualifying RL checkpoint. All methods share that cohort and reference, avoiding repeated redefinition of “easy” (§2).
- **Routed self-distillation:** Length Self-Distillation (LSD) uses current rollout-group accuracy to route fully solved groups to on-policy distillation and other groups to the original RLVR objective. An exponential moving average (EMA) teacher supplies token-level supervision without an external model (§4).
- **Three implementations and empirical analysis:** Supervised-gradient forward KL, supervised-gradient reverse KL, and policy-gradient reverse KL provide different preservation objectives. Experiments examine training difficulty, rollout budgets, teacher half-life, and routing thresholds (§§3–5; Appendix C).

These results support selective preservation in the tested settings, rather than uniform shortening. Negative LST means shorter than the shared RL reference; it does **not** establish preservation of that reference’s perfect accuracy. Paired accuracy must be checked separately. Longer hard-query responses also do not by themselves demonstrate more useful exploration.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Larger-model evaluation remains future work (§6). Experiments cover two Qwen3 backbones and math plus retrieval-agent tasks.
- **Metric qualification:** Relaxed-threshold LST does not establish waste at identical accuracy (§2). Strict easy cohorts are empirically selected from sampled rollouts; the paired cohorts are small (Table 8).
- **Observed failures:** Some shortened queries lose correctness despite favorable aggregate results; Appendix D explicitly includes such regressions (Figure 14).
- **Author-stated confounding:** Threshold ablations simultaneously change routing exposure and effective distillation strength, so they do not isolate the causal training signal (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The random-routing comparison does not cleanly isolate query selection because it retains GRPO on all groups, whereas LSD removes easy-route RL (Appendix D.1; Table 11).
- **Cost and reproducibility gaps:** Agent LST excludes tool and refinement costs. The supplied text provides no end-to-end latency or training-overhead comparison, repeated-seed uncertainty estimates, or currently released code (§5.1; p. 10).

## Open research questions
1. **Does LSD transfer across model families and larger scales?** Only two Qwen3 backbones are tested; repeat matched RL/LSD experiments on additional families and scales (§6; Table 10).
2. **Can routing preserve newly acquired skills more reliably?** Student accuracy is a proxy for teacher competence; compare it with teacher-checked routing while measuring extra rollout cost and correctness regressions (§4).
3. **How much improvement comes from routing versus loss weighting?** Existing threshold and random-routing comparisons change coupled factors; hold distillation exposure and easy-route RL treatment constant while varying selection (Appendices B.2, D.1).
4. **Does reduced LST lower total deployment cost?** Overall math length can increase and agent cost components are excluded; measure latency, total generated tokens, tool costs, and accuracy together (Table 2; §5.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed objectives, configurations, and paired results, but statistical robustness and broader transfer remain unresolved.

Material missing information includes repeated-seed variability, confidence intervals, main-result checkpoint-selection details, end-to-end compute costs, a paired agent accuracy/reference breakdown comparable to Table 8, and released implementation artifacts.
