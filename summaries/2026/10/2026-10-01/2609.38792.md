# Training LLM Judges from Language Feedback via Position-Selective Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38792) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38792)


## One-sentence takeaway
Retaining positions with the lowest student-minus-teacher entropy shifts improves feedback-based self-distillation of Qwen3 judges on two out-of-distribution benchmarks, although the proposed semantic-understanding mechanism remains interpretive (§§3.4, 4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Subjective judgments depend on which criteria a judge selects and how it weighs them. The authors argue that verdict-only reinforcement learning provides limited direct guidance for these choices and ignores preference rationales that explain decisive criteria. Self-distillation can transfer this feedback into token-level supervision, but uniformly imitating every position may preserve less useful signals (§§1, 3.1–3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Feedback-conditioned judge training:** Apply existing on-policy reverse-KL self-distillation to preference rationales. The teacher receives feedback; the student generates judgments without it. Implementation uses an EMA teacher and a top-token-plus-tail approximation to the KL (§3.2; Appendix D).
- **Analysis of supervision:** Define entropy shift as student entropy minus feedback-conditioned teacher entropy. Criterion-choice positions disproportionately carry distillation loss. Positive shifts concentrate probability on particular criterion expressions; negative shifts spread it across alternatives (§3.3).
- **Position selection:** Rank positions within each generation and exclude the highest entropy shifts. The main configuration masks 70% and supervises the lowest-shift 30%, with loss normalized over retained positions (§3.4).
- **Empirical validation:** Compare against naive SD, Dr. GRPO, alternative selectors, and a downstream response-selection tournament (§§4.2–4.4).

The authors interpret sharpening as encouraging expression memorization and spreading as supporting semantic understanding; the measurements do not directly establish that distinction.

These results support improved benchmark generalization and response selection under the tested settings. Selector controls strengthen evidence that the signed entropy difference matters. They do not demonstrate semantic understanding causally or establish gains from full gradient-based policy optimization. The supplied text contains table captions and narrative results, but omits the numerical table bodies.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Useful supervision depends on feedback identifying decisive criteria. The method does not explicitly address self-distillation hallucination or instability in long-chain-of-thought models (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Mechanistic evidence comes from one model’s validation rollouts, automated criterion annotations, and embedding similarity; broader criterion diversity does not prove better semantic understanding (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation covers two Qwen3 scales trained on one cleaned preference dataset. Multilingual and tie handling remain outside the tested training scope (§4.1).
- **Comparison limits:** Dr. GRPO and SD use different learning rates and rollout counts; compute-matched comparisons are not established. RationaleRM results are quoted from its authors rather than evaluated from a released checkpoint (§4.1; Appendix D).
- **Downstream limit:** The tournament substitutes for full RLHF runs and uses a model evaluator (§4.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does criterion diversity cause the generalization gain?** Current evidence is associative. Next experiment: intervene on criterion diversity while controlling feedback and supervision budget, then measure OOD accuracy (§3.4).
- **How robust is masking to poor feedback?** Feedback quality is a stated dependency without a reported corruption study. Next experiment: progressively replace rationales with generic, incorrect, or incomplete feedback (§6).
- **Do selection gains translate into policy-training gains?** The tournament omits gradient updates. Next experiment: run matched policy optimization with each judge and evaluate resulting policies independently (§4.3).
- **Does the method extend to long-reasoning judges?** These models are explicitly outside the study’s focus. Next experiment: compare masked and naive SD for accuracy, hallucination, and training stability (§6).
