# ThinkV2V: Unleashing the Reasoning Capability of MLLMs for Instruction-Guided Video Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38541) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38541)


## One-sentence takeaway
ThinkV2V combines explicit multimodal reasoning with diffusion-based video editing, achieving the highest overall scores among evaluated methods on two benchmarks under two automated judges, although reasoning correctness and computational efficiency remain unmeasured.

## Problem and motivation
Implicit editing instructions can identify targets through function, relationships, or context rather than naming them directly. The authors argue that existing editors mainly use MLLMs as semantic encoders, leaving a gap between understanding indirect intent and executing the correct edit, particularly when temporal dynamics or causal relations matter (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reasoning-conditioned editor:** Frozen Qwen3-VL-Thinking-8B reasons over the source video and instruction. A learnable-query connector converts final-answer hidden states into conditions for a Wan-2.1-5B DiT initialized from Lucy-Edit. Original instruction embeddings and source-video VAE features provide additional context; reasoning-trace tokens are not directly passed to the connector (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Training and inference recipe:** Training progresses from simple, lower-resolution edits to high-resolution edits and then complex instructions. Inference uses serial prompt refinement followed by MLLM selection among eight candidates (§3.2).
- **Reasoning-oriented assets:** ThinkV2V-150K contains rewritten instructions for 150K existing video pairs; ThinkV2V-Bench contains 308 rewritten benchmark pairs across five editing categories (§3.3). Release availability is not established.

These results support improved aggregate automated-judge performance within the evaluated settings. ThinkV2V does not lead every category. They do not independently establish correct causal reasoning, human preference, or computational efficiency. The reported **5B** size describes the DiT; the system also uses an **8B** MLLM (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Explicit thinking adds training and inference cost and latency; reasoning traces are not independently evaluated; open-domain, longer-horizon, and rare complex edits have limited coverage (Appendix C).

**Analyst inferences:**
- Synthetic indirect rewrites, often constructed using first frames, may primarily test descriptive target identification rather than temporal causal reasoning; real-user representativeness is asserted but not independently validated (Appendix A).
- Automated judges provide the reported quantitative evidence; human evaluation, uncertainty estimates, and score-rubric details are absent.
- Baselines use different feasible resolutions and frame counts under an 80GB GPU constraint, complicating attribution solely to method quality (§4.1).
- Figure 7’s displayed reasoning introduces detailed constraints absent from its displayed instruction, leaving that example’s input provenance unclear (p. 15).

## Open research questions
- **Does improvement extend to genuine temporal causality?** First-frame-based rewriting leaves this unresolved. Test edits whose target can only be identified from events across multiple frames.
- **Does reasoning accuracy predict editing success?** Traces are unevaluated. Annotate target identification and constraint correctness, then compare these judgments with editing outcomes.
- **What is the quality–latency tradeoff?** Runtime and scaling costs are unreported. Measure latency, memory, and judge/human scores across candidate counts and refinement depths.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture and reported score comparisons are clear, but causal interpretation and generalization remain uncertain.

Missing information includes evaluation rubrics, statistical uncertainty, human ratings, exact baseline configurations, refinement-round details, filtering thresholds, runtime and training cost, leakage checks, and confirmed code/model/data release availability.
