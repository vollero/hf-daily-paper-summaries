# Rubric Rewards from Item Response Theory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35646) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35646)


## One-sentence takeaway
RRT infers rubric-specific quality from criterion verdict patterns and selects informative criteria, improving Qwen3.5-4B’s macro criterion score by 1.7 percentage points over Vanilla GRPO while matching its full-judging performance within 0.1 points at half the criterion budget (Tables 2 and 4).

## Problem and motivation
Open-ended language tasks often lack automatically verifiable answers, so reinforcement learning uses rubric criteria judged individually by an LLM. Fixed weighted sums can assign identical rewards to different verdict patterns, and assigned points express importance rather than how informative a criterion is about current rollouts. Judging every criterion also increases training cost as rubrics grow (§1).

The authors identify a gap in aggregation and judge allocation: existing rubric rewards do not jointly infer response quality and select criteria using parameters calibrated to the changing policy (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Quality inference:** RRT models binary criterion verdicts with a two-parameter item response model: difficulty and positive discrimination. A Gaussian response function and Gaussian quality prior yield a posterior-mode quality estimate used as the GRPO reward. This can distinguish responses with equal rubric point totals (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Text prediction and online calibration:** A Response Parameter Network predicts criterion parameters from prompt and criterion text. Online hard EM updates it using current rollout verdicts, with one accumulated optimizer step per policy step (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Information theory and selection:** The authors prove that the likelihood score achieves the local signal-to-noise bound under their model. Adaptive selection ranks unjudged criteria by summed Fisher information across rollout qualities, updating qualities after each selected criterion (§3.3).

The optimality theorem concerns the **local likelihood score under model assumptions**, rather than a general guarantee that posterior-mode rewards optimize policy performance.

These results support improved primary-policy training and reduced judging under the tested settings. They do not establish consistent gains across policy families, universal noise robustness, or general reward optimality. Advantage correlation measures fidelity to full judging, rather than independent response quality.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope:** Criteria must be monotone indicators of one shared quality target. The discussion proposes multidimensional targets for explicit tradeoffs and dependence modeling for redundant criteria (§5).
- **Observed assumption violations:** Fitted quality explains much pairwise dependence, but semantically similar criteria retain excess redundancy. Independent information summation can therefore misrepresent such rubrics (Table 21; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—training uncertainty:** Each condition has one training run. Evaluation averages three sampling seeds, and bootstrap intervals hold trained policies fixed; they do not measure variability across training runs (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—judge validity:** Evaluation uses GPT-5.5 binary verdicts. Repeated-judge agreement tests consistency, but the supplied experiments do not establish verdict accuracy against independent human judgments (§4.1; Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic interpretation:** Configuration ROC-AUC includes the evaluated verdict in quality inference, so it is a fit diagnostic rather than held-out prediction. The separate leave-one-out experiment avoids this inclusion (Appendix C.1; §4.2).
- **Analyst inference—cost scope:** Time savings are measured on Medical and Science relative to frozen-RPN full judging. Warm-start costs and cached embeddings matter for end-to-end cost comparisons (Appendices G.2–G.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can dependence-aware RRT handle redundant criteria better?** Residual redundancy remains for similar criterion text. Compare the current model with a dependence-aware variant on original and deliberately duplicated rubrics (Table 21).
- **When are policy gains reproducible?** Improvements are small or absent on the two additional policies, with one training run per condition. Repeat matched comparisons across training seeds and report uncertainty over runs (Tables 2 and 22).
- **Can partial judging support online RPN adaptation?** Budget experiments freeze the RPN. Compare frozen and online calibration under matched adaptive budgets, measuring calibration, policy scores, and selection bias (§4.4).
- **Does inferred quality agree with human priorities?** The model estimates shared quality rather than directly preserving assigned importance weights. Evaluate response pairs with conflicting criterion outcomes using blinded human judgments and compare RRT with weighted points (§§1 and 3.1).
