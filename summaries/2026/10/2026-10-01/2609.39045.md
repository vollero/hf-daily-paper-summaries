# RSIGame: Autonomous Agentic Game Development with Recursive Self-improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39045) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39045)


## One-sentence takeaway
RSIGame combines evidence-grounded game editing, adaptive exploration, checkpoint retention, and supervised experience transfer to improve benchmark game quality across Godot and Phaser, although its advantage over Play2Code is statistically unresolved for untrained Qwen on Phaser (Tables 1, 14).

## Problem and motivation
Generated games can be playable while retaining bugs, missing mechanics, and weak presentation. The authors argue that repeated testing and editing can overfit a few interaction traces, leaving broader player behavior insufficiently covered. Their identified gap is reliable improvement across long development runs, rather than iteration itself (§§1–2, 6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive local development:** A controller selects an investigation, an explorer gathers gameplay evidence, an editor revises the project, and a verifier checks the outcome. An evolving checklist carries issues, priorities, and verification results between rounds (§3.1.1).
- **Global progress control:** A separate monitor compares candidate and retained builds using replay evidence and a proxy rubric, preserves the preferred checkpoint, and stops after sustained lack of improvement. Optional human or stronger-model guidance can start another stage (§3.1.2; Appendices C.1–C.3).
- **Experience internalization:** Parameter-efficient supervised fine-tuning teaches Qwen3.8-27B from generation, planning, and verified improvement trajectories. The contribution is this combined development-and-training framework; the authors explicitly do not claim to introduce iterative game development (§3.2, §6; Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results
Overall is the build-gated weighted score: Mechanics 0.15, Depth 0.35, Visuals 0.15, and Art 0.35. Main results average three independent replay-and-score runs per artifact across 140 tasks, using Qwen3.8-27B as judge (§4.1).

These results support improvements in the measured quality dimensions, with limited checks against replay-specific gains and judge dependence. They do not establish universal task-level superiority, human enjoyment, or general autonomous software improvement. The smaller-generator comparison also combines SFT and development by a shared GLM editor; it does not isolate Qwen acting alone (Table 6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Games are relatively compact; originality, narrative quality, long-term engagement, and subjective enjoyment are not fully evaluated. Optional guidance is studied on a limited case set (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—interaction coverage:** Development repeatedly reuses visible demonstrations. Independent free play supports transfer on a small Godot subset, but leaves broader and longer player interactions untested (Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference—uneven benefits:** Aggregate gains hide family-level regressions, and the Qwen Phaser advantage over Play2Code is statistically unresolved (Table 14; Tables 23, 25–26).
- **Analyst inference—training attribution:** Nested training corpora grow when stages are added, without a reported size-matched control. Thus, Table 18 does not isolate supervision type from additional training data.
- **Reproducibility ambiguities:** Release status conflicts between Table 3 and Appendix E; corpus counts differ between Tables 15 and 16 without a clear reconciliation. Appendix E.1 also flags incomplete tool-result recordings. The headline token reduction measures billable tokens rather than total tokens, and excludes training cost.

## Open research questions
- **Does improvement transfer to sustained human play?** The paper lacks long-term engagement evaluation. Next experiment: blinded human sessions measuring enjoyment, completion, and newly discovered failures.
- **Does verified improvement supervision outperform equally sized generation supervision?** The growing nested corpora leave this unresolved. Next experiment: match training tokens and updates across supervision mixtures.
- **When does saturation warrant stopping, restarting, or guidance?** Case studies show that restarting without guidance can also help. Next experiment: randomize these interventions at identical saturated checkpoints under equal budgets (Appendix F.2).
- **Can the framework scale to larger projects?** Compact games dominate evaluation. Next experiment: increase project size and dependency complexity while measuring quality, regression rates, and development cost (Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed protocols and robustness checks support the benchmark findings, but training and artifact descriptions contain material ambiguities.

Unavailable or unclear: reconciled training-subset provenance; consistent corpus and adapter release status; uncertainty estimates for every Table 1 setting; training-inclusive efficiency accounting; broad human-play evidence; and rendered Figure 9 values absent from the supplied text.
