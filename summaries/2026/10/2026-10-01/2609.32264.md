# LANTERN: Illuminating Hidden Mathematical Knowledge in Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32264) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32264)


## One-sentence takeaway
LANTERN uses pretrained-model activations to prioritize missing OEIS connections, yielding 13 screened relations—including four not found in targeted searches—but its mathematical derivations lack independent expert checking (§§4–5; Appendix F).

## Problem and motivation
AI mathematics systems often work on problems selected by people. The authors ask whether a model’s internal representations can also help select promising mathematical connections, addressing the gap between solving supplied problems and identifying worthwhile questions (§1).

OEIS provides well-defined objects, recorded connections, and stored sequence terms for inexpensive initial verification. However, many cross-references are routine, so predicting links alone does not establish meaningful discovery (§§3, 4.5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Activation-based candidate ranking:** Embed each sequence definition using mean-pooled residual states across Qwen3-32B layers. Train logistic regression on centered layerwise similarities and PCA-reduced pair features, using substantive existing links as positives (§§3.2, 4.1–4.2).
- **Staged discovery pipeline:** Rank eligible unlinked pairs, filter plausible candidates, and use a stronger tool-equipped model to produce executable transformations and derivations. Check stored terms, inspect code for hard-coding, and assess derivations (§4.3).
- **Audited mathematical outputs:** Combine outputs from three search configurations, then separately assess mathematical content, significance, and documentation. The authors report 62 technically verified statements, 13 retained relations, nine informative or insightful relations, and four connections absent from their searches (§§4.4–5).
- **Controls for the representation signal:** Compare against surface-text scoring, later-added links, newly created entries, and a label-free model question. These support useful predictive information in activations, while leaving the strongest interpretation—previously undocumented knowledge already residing in the model—uncertain (§6; Appendix D).

The evidence supports an efficient candidate-selection procedure and explicitly documented outputs. It does not establish discovery priority, independently certified proofs, or a clear activation advantage in the later S1 window. Held-out link ranking also measures a different outcome from producing useful mathematical discoveries.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** OEIS is unusually structured, the corpus favors frequently referenced sequences, and embedding evaluations cover only one pretrained-model family (§7).
- **Explicit proof-status qualification:** Appendix F states that all derivations were model-produced and have not been checked by a mathematician. Recorded OEIS formulas are assumed, and literature references for classical ingredients remain unfinished. This qualifies the main text’s “verified” terminology.
- **Analyst inference—novelty uncertainty:** N2 means a connection was not found in targeted searches; search coverage is unspecified and priority is explicitly unestablished (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** The 74 substantive future pairs represent about 45 distinct facts, with more than half belonging to two templates. Template recognition may inflate some ranking comparisons (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—selection stability:** Appendix D reports substantial sensitivity of extreme rankings to PCA-layer choice. No comparison of screened discovery yield against text ranking, random selection, or direct questioning is supplied.
- **Internal inconsistency:** Table 3 presents A003278 × A006368 as a retained highlight, while the item-level ledger excludes it as definitional. Appendix F’s final summary also uses different, unexplained classification labels.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Verification code is mentioned, but no repository address, complete prompts, classifier hyperparameters, hardware specification, or monetary cost is supplied.

## Open research questions
1. **Does activation ranking improve useful discovery yield?** Controls evaluate link ranking rather than screened outputs. A next experiment could give activation, text, direct-question, and random rankings equal downstream agent budgets and independently assess their retained relations.
2. **Do the reported derivations survive expert verification?** Appendix F explicitly lacks mathematician checking. Independent review or formalization of the four N2 relations could test correctness and the claimed structural interpretations.
3. **Does the method generalize beyond popular OEIS entries?** The authors identify both corpus and domain restrictions. Repeat the pipeline on less-referenced entries and another structured mathematical collection with independently documented relations.
4. **How robust is the extreme ranking?** Reported top-ranked pairs change substantially with feature-layer choices. Repeat discovery across layer selections and training samples, measuring overlap and independently screened yield.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial methodological detail and item-level evidence, but proof status and appendix inconsistencies weaken the strongest conclusions.

Material missing information includes independent mathematical validation, reproducible code access and prompts, training and hardware details, literature-search protocol, screening agreement, new-entry negative construction, and controlled downstream discovery comparisons.
