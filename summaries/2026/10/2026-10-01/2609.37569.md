# Decompose Radicals, Then Reward: Fine-Grained Inspection for Accurate Chinese Text Rendering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37569) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37569)


## One-sentence takeaway
IDSpect combines component-level Chinese glyph rewards with OCR semantic rewards to improve Qwen-Image’s structural quality and semantic alignment on two benchmarks, although it does not achieve the highest original LongText text score.

## Problem and motivation
Character-level OCR rewards provide coarse feedback: repairing part of a malformed Chinese character may leave its recognized identity unchanged, while contextual inference may recognize the intended character despite incorrect visual structure. The authors propose evaluating reusable components and their spatial relationships to reward partial structural improvements (Introduction, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Visual IDS recognizer:** An SVTRv2 encoder and NRTR-style autoregressive decoder predict Ideographic Description Sequence (IDS) tokens directly from text crops. The authors construct IDSynth-1M, a synthetic dataset with approximately uniform character sampling, to discourage linguistic shortcuts (§3.2, §4.1).
- **Target-conditioned structural reward:** Canonical target IDS tokens are aligned with crop predictions through semi-global edit alignment and global assignment. Each target token receives match credit at most once; token-level F1 penalizes missing and extraneous predictions (§3.3).
- **Generator post-training:** IDSpect equally weights structural and OCR semantic rewards during Flow-GRPO LoRA training of Qwen-Image. Reward models are discarded afterward, adding no generator inference cost (§3.3, §4.1).

The contribution is using compositional recognition for generation rewards and target alignment; the paper acknowledges that IDS-based recognition itself predates this work (§2.2).

These results support improved evaluator-measured structure and target alignment within the tested setup. They do not establish statistical significance, human-perceived superiority, or generalization across generators. Published baseline results are reused under the same stated benchmark protocol rather than documented as jointly rerun (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Broader Chinese character coverage and Japanese/Korean extensions remain future work (§5).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Experiments cover one generator and two Chinese rendering benchmarks (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—recognizer validity:** Balanced sampling is intended to encourage visual grounding, but no balanced-versus-unbalanced training ablation or independent IDS recognition accuracy is reported. The construction ablation holds the recognizer fixed (§3.2, Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement uncertainty:** No confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, or human evaluation are supplied. Structural and semantic conclusions rely on TextPecker metrics (§4.1–4.3).
- **Analyst inference—pipeline constraints:** Detection errors, font coverage, and the decoded sequence limit could constrain reward reliability; their effects are not quantified (§3.3, §4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does balanced synthetic training improve recognition of malformed components?** The paper motivates this effect without isolating it. Train matched balanced and natural-frequency recognizers, then evaluate controlled component corruptions.
- **How much does each reward branch contribute?** All reported IDSpect experiments use equal weighting. Compare semantic-only, IDS-only, and several fusion weights under matched training budgets.
- **Do evaluator gains correspond to human judgments?** No human assessment is reported. Conduct blinded glyph-structure and target-fidelity comparisons on benchmark outputs.
- **How robust is crop alignment to detection failures and long text?** These conditions are not separately tested. Perturb crop order, omissions, and overlaps, and evaluate increasing text lengths.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and benchmark scores are explicit, but causal and generalization claims have limited validation.

Material missing information includes public code/model/data availability, recognizer accuracy and training details, exact OCR detector and semantic recognizer identities, GRPO training prompts and compute budget, alignment thresholds and solver details, benchmark sample counts, run variability, and independent human evaluation.
