# ATLAS: Aligned Transport of Latent Structure for Reliable World Model Planning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36333) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36333)


## One-sentence takeaway
ATLAS combines internal relational distillation with projected Wasserstein regularization to improve LeWM’s goal-reaching success across three simulated tasks, with its largest gain on higher-novelty TwoRoom episodes.

## Problem and motivation
Latent world-model planners compare predicted outcomes with goal embeddings, so state-to-state distances directly affect action selection. The authors identify a gap: anti-collapse regularization can calibrate the latent distribution without preserving useful relationships between states. In LeWM, novelty-based failure prediction is stronger in encoder patch features than in the final planning latent, motivating explicit preservation of that geometry (§1; Tables 4–5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Relational preservation:** OOD-recovery matches normalized pairwise distances between planning latents and mean-pooled encoder patch features. The patch anchor receives no gradient through this loss but evolves through the shared encoder; no OOD labels are required (§3.2).
- **Marginal calibration:** WEMReg matches randomly projected latent distributions to a standard Gaussian using one-dimensional Wasserstein-2 transport. ATLAS combines it with relational preservation and latent prediction loss, retaining LeWM’s encoder–predictor framework and CEM planner (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Analysis and diagnosis:** The authors establish that marginal and relational objectives impose non-redundant constraints, construct distributions invisible to finite-frequency Gaussian matching, and bound finite-candidate anchor-geometry regret using prediction error, relational distortion, and scale mismatch. These are conditional guarantees rather than guarantees of task success (§3; Appendix C).

The evidence supports improved mean success over LeWM under this protocol and complementary representation diagnostics. It does not establish causal mediation by those diagnostics, universal superiority, or performance under an independently held-out distribution shift.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Evaluation coverage:** Goals come from the full valid episode set rather than a held-out test split. “OOD” means the higher half of relative novelty within each evaluation condition, not a separately constructed unseen distribution (Appendix A/A.1).
- **Training variability — analyst inference:** Table 2 specifies one training seed; reported success variability spans evaluation seeds. Robustness to retraining remains unestablished.
- **Comparison differences:** DINO-WM uses external visual pretraining; FAST-WM uses released checkpoints; FAST-WM and PLDM use different episode draws. LeWM and the controlled ablations provide the clearest objective-level comparisons (§4).
- **Theory scope:** Guarantees apply to the evaluated finite pool with fixed normalizers. Small training losses do not guarantee unseen-state behavior; task-cost regret additionally requires anchor alignment. Finite projection estimates need an integration-error bound (Appendix C.1/C.4/C.9).
- **Diagnostic limits:** The authors describe diagnostics as non-causal, selected trajectories as illustrative, and the empirical regret bound as conservative. Cube relational diagnostics use only 16 exploratory queries (§5; Figures 10–13).
- **Unreported sensitivity — analyst inference:** The supplied experiments do not establish sensitivity to objective weights, projection count, or anchor choice.

## Open research questions
- **Does improvement persist on genuinely unseen states?** The evaluation lacks a held-out test distribution. Next experiment: train on restricted state regions and evaluate disjoint layouts or configurations.
- **When is the patch anchor aligned with task cost?** The task-cost guarantee assumes alignment rather than establishing it empirically. Next experiment: measure anchor-distance agreement with simulator task costs and compare alternative anchors.
- **How robust are the gains across training runs and settings?** One training seed and fixed objective settings leave this open. Next experiment: repeat the controlled ablation across training seeds and sweep objective weights and projection counts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol and tabulated comparisons; **medium** in the proposed mechanism and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes training-seed uncertainty, held-out-distribution results, real-world evaluation, hyperparameter sensitivity, compute overhead, exact values for several plotted diagnostics, and verification of the author-reported code release.
