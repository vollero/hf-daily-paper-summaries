# EvoDuet: Bilevel Co-Evolution of Web Searching and Task Solving for Scientific Discovery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40340) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40340)


## One-sentence takeaway
EvoDuet couples evolving web queries with evaluator-guided program evolution, improving reported best-of-run discovery performance for two tested LLMs while degrading it for Qwen3.5-9B (§4, Figure 6).

## Problem and motivation
LLM-driven evolutionary search can stall when progress requires knowledge absent from both model parameters and previous candidates. The authors argue that adding search alone is insufficient: queries must adapt as solutions change, and retrieved knowledge must be implemented correctly. Their Denoising analysis finds that search inside program generation repeatedly returns previously seen pages and stops improving (§§1–2.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Coupled solution and query evolution:** A frozen LLM evolves programs in an outer loop and refines web queries in an inner loop, both targeting the task evaluator’s objective (§3.1).
- **Knowledge-gap retrieval gate:** The model chooses fresh retrieval, stored-document reuse, or no retrieval using the current program, evolutionary history, and recent search outcomes (§3.3, Appendix B.2).
- **Predicted-performance evidence selection:** The inner loop ranks documents by predicted candidate scores and updates its knowledge state without generating or evaluating programs. The outer loop then measures actual outcomes and stores them with the evidence (§3.4, Appendix B.3).
- **Integration and analysis:** Retrieved-evidence iterations support parallel candidate generation; experiments examine backbone dependence, compatibility with other scaffolds, and how documents influence revisions (§§3.2, 4–5).

These results support useful retrieval-guided optimization under the supplied evaluators, with substantial model and task dependence. They do not establish universal gains or causal effects from the iteration-level correlations. The Denoising trajectory comparison is distinct from Table 2’s aggregate joint-level result of 43.2% NDG; the paper reports different aggregation settings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundaries:** Retrieved ideas can produce worse candidates; predicted gains require evaluator validation. Programs require independent validation before downstream use (§5.1; ethics statement, p.10).
- **Unequal comparison:** DeepEvolve uses published scores and was not rerun with the same model or search budget (Appendix B.5).
- **Artifact dependence:** The reported Erdős best program retains a runtime download of a published witness; Rosetta also uses public artifacts. These are retrieval-assisted refinements rather than wholly independent constructions (Appendix E.1.2).
- **Analyst inference—selection and resource effects:** Headline gains average task-best runs. At larger candidate budgets, gate decisions also change generation counts, complicating attribution to retrieval alone (Figure 6; Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—surrogate attribution:** Predictions assume individual documents, while evaluated candidates use documents jointly; the score correlation does not validate each document’s individual contribution (Appendix B.1).
- **Narrow budget evidence:** Inner-loop budget ablations cover Denoising with GPT-5.6-Luna; more rounds or queries can reduce NDG (Appendix D.3).
- **Incomplete coverage:** The supplied source omits a large middle portion, including the dedicated limitations section; complete author limitations cannot be assessed.

## Open research questions
1. **Which component drives gains at equal cost?** Gating, query refinement, document ranking, and candidate counts interact. A plausible next experiment is a component ablation with matched generation, search, and monetary budgets (§3; Appendix B.4).
2. **Why does Qwen fail despite benefiting from oracle documents?** The authors identify unused and incorrectly implemented methods, but do not isolate retrieval versus implementation failures. Swap search and coding backbones while holding evidence fixed (§4).
3. **Can document-set scoring improve selection?** Individual-document predictions are compared with jointly informed candidates. Compare individual, joint-set, and randomized evidence selection at matched budgets (Appendix B.1).
4. **How much performance survives without public artifacts?** Some best programs reuse published solutions. Compare method-only retrieval, artifact-enabled retrieval, and offline replay with artifacts preserved locally (Appendix E.1.2).
