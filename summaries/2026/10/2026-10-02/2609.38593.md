# Prompt2Skill: Unsupervised Skill Optimization From Natural Language Instructions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38593) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38593)


## One-sentence takeaway
Prompt2Skill turns task descriptions into model-specific inference skills through automatically acquired proxy data and reflective optimization, improving average benchmark performance while leaving supervision quality and regression guarantees unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Expert-written skills are costly and may poorly match a particular model’s failure modes. Existing skill optimizers assume labeled, in-distribution training examples that users may lack. The authors address skill construction from a natural-language task description, with optional format examples but no user-supplied training set (§§1–2).

“Unsupervised” here means no supplied target-task data: the system still retrieves or generates labeled proxy examples and uses their references during optimization (Appendix C).

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated prompt-to-skill pipeline.** A Data Agent infers the task specification, proxy evaluator, and seed skill; retrieves datasets or source material; adapts or synthesizes examples; and validates the resulting pool. The authors claim novelty for this end-to-end integration, rather than reflective editing alone (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Model-specific refinement and selection.** A Skill Agent proposes edits from execution failures, compares candidates against the incumbent on fresh paired batches, and retains qualifying improvements. Initial and accepted skills compete in one terminal evaluation on a frozen selection set; the winner becomes `SKILL.md`, without weight updates (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Empirical and conditional theoretical evidence.** The study evaluates four tasks and five solvers, includes a matched-budget SkillOpt comparison, and bounds terminal-selection loss under independent sampling and bounded proxy-distribution/evaluator mismatch (§4; Appendix A). The bound concerns the best retained skill, not guaranteed improvement over direct prompting.

The evidence supports useful specialization on these benchmark settings, especially extractive QA. It does not establish universal improvement, reliable reference correctness, or freedom from all contamination. Appendix E.4 also notes that small baseline scores can disproportionately affect relative-gain averages.

The abstract/introduction’s **10.8% / 10.8 percentage-point** headline uses inconsistent wording and is not explicitly reconciled with the relative aggregates above. Table 2’s displayed SearchQA values differ by **2.92 points**, whereas its accompanying prose reports **2.93**.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ caveat—proxy validity:** Solver agreement does not establish reference correctness or target-distribution representativeness (§3.2). The theorem assumes bounded mismatch without supplying empirical mismatch estimates (Appendix A).
- **Authors’ caveat—acceptance statistics:** The threshold is a per-candidate screen, not a conventional 5% significance test; comparisons across candidates and rounds receive no adjustment (Appendix D). This qualifies the introduction’s “statistically significant” description.
- **Analyst inference—selection bias:** Rejecting sources the seed cannot solve and synthesized items neither seed nor direct prompting solves may exclude precisely the difficult cases that need improvement (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—unsupported non-regression claim:** Several Table 1 means decline, and the supplied text gives no target-test significance analysis establishing the claim that P2S “never significantly regresses.”
- **Evaluation and attribution limits:** Figure 3 has single-run conditions and acquisition failures. The matched-budget experiment tests the combined optimization/selection procedure; it does not isolate fresh-batch screening or terminal selection individually (§4.3).
- **Provenance and comparison limits:** Proxy source records lack verified item-level attribution. Off-the-shelf development provenance is unverified, and fixed LLM-generated baselines use Qwen3-32B while P2S uses GPT-5.5 reflection (Appendices C, E.2–E.3).
- **Exploratory AutoML evidence:** Appendix B uses a modified fine-tuning baseline and differently named base models; numerical Figure 4 scores are unavailable in the extracted text.

## Open research questions
- **How much do answerability screens bias coverage?** Reference validity and representativeness remain unverified. Compare current screening with independently checked references and explicit difficulty strata (§3.2).
- **Which refinement components produce the gains?** The SkillOpt comparison evaluates the combined procedure. Run matched-budget ablations removing paired screening, fresh acceptance batches, or terminal selection separately (§4.3).
- **Can regression protection hold under distribution shift?** The theorem depends on unmeasured proxy mismatch, and benchmark means sometimes decline. Evaluate deliberately mismatched proxy sources with preregistered target-test regression checks (Table 1; Appendix A).
- **How dependent are gains on the frontier editor?** All reported optimization uses GPT-5.5 reflection. Compare weaker and stronger editors under equal solver-evaluation and total-cost budgets (Appendix E.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the pipeline and benchmark outcomes, but leaves material reporting gaps: the meaning of Table 1’s ± values, complete main-run replication details, realized acquisition sizes and item provenance, full stopping rules and round budgets, monetary/runtime costs, component-isolating ablations, target-test significance analyses, and a clear derivation of the 10.8 headline.
