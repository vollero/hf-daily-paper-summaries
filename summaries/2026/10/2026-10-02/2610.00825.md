# Align Then Reason: A Multimodal Lip-Sync Judge for Dubbing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00825) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00825)


## One-sentence takeaway

Align Then Reason combines explicit monotonic visual–phonetic alignment with an LLM judge, reporting mean AUC of 0.920 versus 0.610 for the strongest baseline on a seven-language lip-sync corruption benchmark (§3.2).

## Problem and motivation

Dubbing teams need to assess whether a proposed line fits visible mouth movements before synthesized audio exists. The judge must detect both incompatible phonetic content and incorrect temporal progression using only silent video and candidate text.

The authors identify a gap between this task and existing systems: lip readers optimize transcription, audiovisual synchronization requires audio, and video-language models can recognize content without tracking fine temporal correspondence (§§1, 2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Candidate-conditioned monotonic alignment:** Auto-AVSR visual features and XPhoneBERT phonetic representations form a frame-by-unit similarity matrix. CTC labels represent positions in the candidate line, enforcing ordered traversal without frame-level alignment labels (§2.2; Appendix A).
- **Training for complementary failures:** Contrastive learning uses content substitutions and frame perturbations; auxiliary phoneme CTC supervision encourages phonetic detail in visual features (§2.2).
- **Structured evidence for an LLM judge:** Per-unit continuous soft tokens convey local matches, while a normalized global alignment scalar conveys temporal fit. A supervised reasoner predicts Yes/No and a short explanation; its Yes-minus-No logits provide the final score (§2.3).
- **Evaluation contribution:** The authors construct multilingual corruption tests and downstream dubbing tasks, and test unseen-language transfer and multiple reasoner families (§§3.1–3.5). Public release of these assets is not established.

## Evidence and main results

All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger overall discrimination | On seven languages and seven corruption axes, ATR with Qwen3.5-9B achieves mean AUC 0.920 versus strongest baseline 0.610: +0.310 absolute, +50.8% relative. | §3.2; Table 1 caption |
| Temporal gains dominate | ATR-9B AUC: reverse 0.964, shift 0.931, freeze 0.970, swap 0.952. Qwen3.5 SFT temporal AUCs remain approximately 0.510–0.520 on the same benchmark. | §3.2 |
| Multiple reasoners work | Seven-language mean AUC / paired accuracy: LLaMA-3.1-8B 0.890 / 0.916; Mistral-7B 0.894 / 0.926. | §3.3; Tables 1–2 references |
| Transfer depends on calibration | Across German, Arabic, and Russian MuAViC tests, excluding dub corruption, ATR-9B mean AUC rises from 0.751 to 0.875 after normalization refitting using 300 held-out target-language genuine samples; no model weights change. Recalibrated gain over Auto-AVSR is +0.297 absolute. | §3.3; Table 3 caption |
| Global scalar carries temporal evidence | Removing the scalar lowers mean AUC from 0.920 to 0.672; removing soft tokens yields 0.872; removing auxiliary phonetic supervision yields 0.898 in the core-component ablation. | §3.4; Table 4 caption |
| Better professional-line retrieval | For 2,100 clips, ranking one professional dub against three meaning-preserving, length-matched paraphrases gives Top-1 accuracy 0.479 versus strongest lip-reading baseline 0.316; chance is 0.250. Component attribution is inconsistent. | §3.5; Table 5 caption |
| Better script assignment | In-domain five-way matching: ATR-9B per-clip accuracy 0.746 and Exact Block accuracy 0.522; reported gains over Auto-AVSR are 17.7% relative and 0.139 absolute, respectively. MuAViC two-way accuracy is 0.704 versus 0.598. | §3.5; Table 6 caption |
| Seed variability is reported | Across three reasoner-training seeds, Qwen3.5-9B mean AUC is 0.910 ± 0.015 in-domain, 0.778 ± 0.060 on uncalibrated MuAViC, and 0.852 ± 0.033 after recalibration. | Appendix D; Table 9 reference |

The results support discrimination of the tested corruptions and usefulness for candidate selection. They do not establish agreement with human lip-sync ratings, precise timing-error localization, or deployment reliability. The multi-seed averages differ from the headline results; the supplied text does not explain which runs produced those headlines.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-identified domain sensitivity:** The global scalar shifts out of domain, and recalibration materially improves transfer (§3.3). The recalibrated result therefore requires target-domain genuine pairs.
- **Analyst inference—synthetic timing coverage:** Reverse, circular shift, freeze, and half-swap tests establish sensitivity to those perturbations, but leave realistic local timing drift and acceptable timing tolerances untested in the supplied report (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—downstream ground truth:** Reranking treats the professional line as the correct choice without reported human comparison against paraphrases. Recovering that line does not necessarily establish superior perceived lip-sync (§3.5).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Table 5’s caption attributes reranking to the alignment scorer, while the prose attributes it to ATR-9B. AUC aggregation is also described variously as pooled, mean, and macro (§§3.2, 3.4).
- **Explanation validity:** Explanations are supervised from the weakest local unit rather than reported human error annotations; their accuracy or faithfulness is not evaluated (§2.3).
- **Baseline scope:** The authors acknowledge English-heavy recognizer training and non-Latin tokenizer constraints. English-only Auto-AVSR wins mismatch, narrowing claims of uniform superiority (Appendix C).

## Open research questions

- **Does the judge track human-perceived dubbing quality?** Professional-line retrieval is only a proxy. Next experiment: compare scores with blinded human ratings of professional and generated lines (§3.5).
- **Can it detect subtle, realistic timing errors?** Current temporal tests use coarse frame transformations. Next experiment: sweep local offsets and duration changes while measuring detection and human acceptability (§3.1).
- **How much target-domain calibration is necessary?** Transfer improves with held-out genuine pairs, but sample-efficiency is unreported. Next experiment: vary calibration-set size and test across distinct domains (§3.3).
- **Are explanations faithful to visual evidence?** Weakest-unit templates lack independent validation. Next experiment: compare predicted error regions with annotated regions and intervene on corresponding frames (§2.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and principal reported results are clear, but full numerical table bodies are absent despite their captions and prose summaries.
