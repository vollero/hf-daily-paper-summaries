# Agent Priors-guided Policy Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35690) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35690)


## One-sentence takeaway
APPL uses structural priors both to train robot skills and to document their applicability for a runtime agent, improving simulated generalization and composition while leaving competence guarantees and sustained success unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Robots learning from few demonstrations must both execute familiar skills in unfamiliar states and recombine skills for new goals. The authors identify an information gap: task-level planners often see skill names or effects without knowing the structural assumptions that determine where each learned policy works. This can cause unsuitable policy choices and failed handoffs (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Training priors become runtime interfaces.** Each policy’s structural assumption—such as object-relative actions—is implemented in its representation, action coordinates, architecture, or auxiliary loss, then described alongside handoff conditions, training support, and verification evidence (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Alternative implementations per skill.** A construction agent segments complete demonstrations, proposes several priors, and trains separate diffusion policies. Demonstrated-entry verification informs an additional candidate and usage notes (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Informed composition of frozen policies.** A runtime agent selects policies, typed arguments, durations, and stopping conditions from current state and goal information; deployment involves no weight updates (§4.4).

The authors explicitly locate novelty in combining diverse prior-specific alternatives with construction-derived runtime documentation. They do not claim agent-designed priors alone, overlapping segments, or duration-and-stop-condition calls as new (Appendix D.3).

These results support the authors’ claim that prior design improves skill coverage and that runtime documentation helps selection. They do not isolate every interface field or establish that prior descriptions alone improve motion OOD. Full-task comparisons combine differences in segmentation, scheduling, library size, and priors. Reported long-horizon success measures executor-assisted goal attainment, not sustained stability or unaided agent stopping (Appendix B.6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated competence gap:** a structural prior expresses intended applicability without guaranteeing policy success. Demonstrations lack recovery trajectories, and failures expose unsupported entry states and missing recovery skills (§6; Appendix B.5).
- **Verification mismatch:** demonstrated-entry, fixed-endpoint checks can misrank policies for shifted states and runtime handoffs. The verification ablation also changes policy count, so its effect is not isolated (Appendix B.4).
- **Evaluation adaptation:** motion-OOD displacement was reduced after observing results on a harder split; some cases were reclassified and replaced after task-level results (Appendix B.6). **Analyst inference:** this limits interpretation as a wholly prospectively fixed evaluation.
- **Stopping semantics:** the intended goal-hold condition never governed reported successes because the executor stopped at first attainment. Persistence was not evaluated (Appendix B.6).
- **Limited uncertainty coverage:** MetaWorld bootstrap intervals condition on fixed tasks and trained models, excluding independent agent-design and training-seed variation; ManiSkill uses one episode per case (Appendices A.4, B.3).
- **Scope and reproduction:** evaluation is simulation-only with structured state access. Artifact release is promised, and agent reruns may differ (p. 11). Feedback-only and handoff-overlap effects are not isolated (Appendices A.6, D.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can verification predict deployment competence?** Fixed-endpoint checks miss useful transient handoffs. Compare endpoint scoring with first-attainment and handoff-aware verification while holding the policy library fixed (Appendix B.4).
2. **Which documentation fields drive selection?** Current ablations remove bundles of information. Independently ablate prior, support, handoff, and verification fields using identical policies and evaluation cases (§5.2).
3. **Can composition survive execution errors?** The library lacks recovery coverage. Add targeted dropped-object and failed-grasp demonstrations, then test recovery on matched perturbations (Appendix B.5).
4. **Do gains persist under stricter success and repeated runs?** Stability and independent design variability remain unmeasured. Repeat agent construction and training, and evaluate goal persistence after attainment without automatic task stopping (Appendices A.4, B.6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and within-protocol results; **medium** in broader generalization and causal attribution.

Material information unavailable includes confirmed released artifacts, complete agent tool definitions and referenced implementation contracts, independent design/training replications, sustained-success measurements, real-robot results, and aggregate construction cost or runtime reasoning latency.
