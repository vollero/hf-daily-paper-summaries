# Where-OPD: Spatially Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation of MLLMs with Synthetic Scenes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02117) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02117)


## One-sentence takeaway
Where-OPD uses automatically generated spatial hints for a frozen self-teacher during synthetic counting post-training, with reported improvements transferring to real-world visual benchmarks across three MLLMs (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
MLLMs must locate and combine question-relevant evidence across an image. The authors argue that existing self-distillation methods emphasizing crops or enhanced visual views produce uneven gains and often require annotated grounding or external models. They investigate whether textual guidance identifying relevant objects and their locations provides a broader transferable training signal (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Spatial privilege:** Student and teacher see the same image and question; only the teacher receives a hint listing target objects’ coordinates and count. A frozen copy of the initial model supervises student-generated prefixes through token-level Jensen–Shannon divergence (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Procedural supervision:** Scene metadata automatically supplies questions, answers, and hints for 3,000 synthetic counting examples containing non-overlapping colored shapes, without manual annotations or an external teacher (§3.2.1).
- **Transfer evidence:** Evaluation tests counting, documents, charts, general perception, and hallucinations across three Qwen models. The contribution is this particular guidance-and-generation setup; self-distillation itself and textual visual cues have precedents acknowledged in §2.

Hints are absent at inference. Distillation uses the student’s top 100 tokens plus a tail-probability term (§3.2.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no explicit limitations section. They acknowledge that almost all ablations and baseline post-training results use one run; only two main Where-OPD configurations receive repeated-seed evaluation (§4.1).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the setup:**

- Evaluation covers three Qwen models, with different training question formats for smaller and larger models; architecture generality and size effects remain unresolved (§4.1; Appendix A.1).
- Exact hints and simple, non-overlapping shapes leave robustness to noisy grounding and richer synthetic scenes untested (Appendix A.1).
- Prior-method checkpoints share evaluation protocols, but matched training data and compute are not established (§4.1).
- Dataset-size experiments use one epoch per size, entangling data quantity with training duration (Appendix B.2).
- Per-cell selection of thinking settings in Table 3 measures the best tested configuration, rather than performance under one fixed inference policy.
- Scoring sometimes uses an LLM judge, whose identity and validation are unavailable; attention evidence is qualitative and uses separately selected layers (Appendices A.3, B.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer extend beyond Qwen?** Only Qwen backbones are tested. Repeat the same training and evaluation on another MLLM family with matched question formats.
- **How much grounding noise is tolerable?** Hints are exact by construction. Perturb coordinates or omit target instances and measure transfer against exact-hint training.
- **What drives the evidence-alignment gains?** Attention visualizations are suggestive. Intervene on relevant image regions and compare answer sensitivity across base, answer-only, and spatial-hint models.
- **Would richer scenes or longer training improve transfer?** Current size experiments vary examples and training duration together. Compare scene diversity and dataset size under a fixed rollout budget.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method is clearly specified and several numerical findings appear in prose, but complete quantitative verification is unavailable.

Missing material information includes table bodies, full per-benchmark and per-seed scores, numerical ablation curves, baseline training-resource comparability, optimizer and sampling details, LLM-judge identity and validation, and confirmation of released code, data, or checkpoints.
