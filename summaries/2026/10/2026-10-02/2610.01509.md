# Sharpening Tax in Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01509) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors find that post-training often improves text-agent accuracy and consistency while reducing the value of repeated sampling, and propose a diagnostic metric and adaptive training sampler that partially mitigate this trade-off.

## Problem and motivation
Does post-training expand an LLM’s reachable solutions or concentrate probability on existing behaviors? The authors identify a gap in earlier math and coding evaluations: they do not establish whether the same accuracy–coverage tension holds for agents requiring tool calls and environmental interaction. Evaluating only single-shot accuracy can conceal solutions recoverable through repeated attempts (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Agentic coverage study:** Compare base/post-trained checkpoint pairs across four model families and three benchmarks. Base models receive a lightweight prompt-and-parser harness; post-trained models use native scaffolding. Measure accuracy (`pass@1`), coverage (`pass@k`, at least one success), and consistency (`passᵏ`, all attempts succeed) (§§2–3, A.3).
- **Sharpening Tax:** Define raw scalability as \(A(K)=\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}[\mathrm{pass@}K-\mathrm{pass@}k]\), calibrate it by single-shot failure headroom, and subtract post-trained scalability from base scalability. Positive tax measures reduced reliance on retries, encompassing both lost coverage and faster saturation (§4; Proposition 1; Appendix B.2).
- **Posterior-tempered group sampling (PTGS):** Maintain discounted per-prompt success/failure counts, draw a success-rate estimate from a Beta posterior, and adjust training rollout temperature: heat difficult prompts and cool easier ones. The RL update uses matching-temperature log probabilities; main evaluations omit PTGS at inference (§5; Listings 2; A.5).
- **Conditional theory:** Formalize scalability loss under collapse to extreme success probabilities and show how increased prompt success probability improves informative rollout-group frequency. These results depend on explicit assumptions (§6).

| PTGS improves Sokoban GRPO means | Same model and five-run setting: pass@1 **36.5→39.1%**, pass@128 **55.3→72.5%**, tax **0.081→0.025**, baseline→PTGS. | Table 2 |
| PTGS improves FrozenLake means | PPO: pass@1 **63.7→65.0%**, pass@128 **74.1→80.0%**, tax **0.039→0.020**. GRPO: **63.4→67.3%**, **77.8→81.2%**, tax **0.029→0.028**. | Table 2 |

PTGS results use 64 unseen tasks per environment, 128 ancestral rollouts/task at temperature 0.5, final PPO checkpoints, and best-validation GRPO checkpoints (A.5). They establish improved reported means over the chosen baselines, not universal or uniformly statistically significant gains: PPO confidence intervals overlap substantially (Table 9).

Positive tax is also **not equivalent to lost endpoint coverage**: faster saturation contributes to it (Appendix B.2). PTGS partially preserves coverage; every PTGS pass@128 in Table 2 remains below its starting instruction model.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Undisclosed pre/post-training data and recipes make checkpoint comparisons observational; they cannot isolate RL from SFT, preference optimization, or distillation (§9; A.2).
- **Author-stated scope:** Text agents and binary success signals; transfer to multimodal systems and open-ended generation remains untested (§9).
- **Analyst inference:** Different scaffolding and rollout-based rather than token-based budgets limit causal and compute-efficiency interpretations. Harness and thinking-mode ablations help, but do not fully resolve these differences (A.2–A.3; Figure 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Selected basic benchmarks, restricted categories, and short grid-world episodes limit generalization to broader agents. ACEBench contains predominantly single-turn/general and multi-step tasks, with only a small multi-turn subset (A.1, A.5).
- **Theory boundary:** Theorem 3 assumes tempering raises success probability; it does not prove heating always helps (§6, p. 12).
- **Internal qualification:** Appendix E.3 broadly says fixed temperatures cannot improve both metrics, but Table 9 and F.2 report that hotter PPO improves both Sokoban means over the default. PTGS’s stronger result is superiority among the reported sampler means, rather than exclusivity of joint improvement.
- **Coverage limitation:** The supplied source is truncated; omitted material prevents complete assessment.

## Open research questions

- **Which training stage causes the tax?** Recipes are undisclosed (§9). Next experiment: compare controlled SFT, RL, and distillation runs from one base checkpoint using known data and matched budgets.
- **Does PTGS scale beyond short grid worlds?** Training evidence uses one model and two environments (A.5). Next experiment: train with PTGS on longer tool-use tasks across model sizes, with matched sampler tuning.
- **Do coverage gains survive equal total inference cost?** Budgets count rollouts, and inference-time PTGS adds pilot evaluations (A.2; E.4). Next experiment: compare policies and samplers under equal token or wall-clock budgets, including verification and pilot costs.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the principal descriptive findings and method details, but not a causal claim that RL generally shrinks capability.

Material missing information includes the truncated middle passage, exact checkpoint training data and recipes, raw rollout records, inspected code/release contents, and comprehensive significance evidence for PTGS comparisons.
