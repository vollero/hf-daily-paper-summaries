# Beyond the Current Scene: Event-Referential Grasping with Active View Selection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39375) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39375)


## One-sentence takeaway
BeyondCSe combines pretrained video reasoning with history-conditioned camera search to grasp objects identified by past interactions, improving reported success and search efficiency in real-robot tabletop experiments.

## Problem and motivation
Requests such as “pick up the object moved first” require identifying an instance from past events, rather than its current appearance. That instance may also be hidden when execution begins. The authors identify a gap between remembering the correct target and acquiring the current visual geometry needed to grasp it: their system uses history for both reference resolution and spatial search (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Event-referential grounding:** A pretrained MLLM records events, selects the referent, proposes an image region, and predicts an action point. If current grounding fails, point tracking and historical depth recover earlier target locations. The pipeline requires no task-specific training (§III-B–C).
- **History-conditioned active perception:** Historical locations initialize a volumetric probability distribution centered on the last observation and shaped by the terminal trajectory. Bayesian updates revise this belief; view scoring discounts sight lines passing through unobserved space (§III-D, Eqs. 2–9).
- **Integrated grasp execution and evaluation:** After finding the target, grasp validation can request additional geometry-refinement views. Real-robot comparisons and belief ablations evaluate success and observation cost (§III-D4; §IV).

These results support the authors’ claims within the evaluated scenes. The event-prior ablation specifically supports reduced search cost; it does not demonstrate increased success in that setting. Comparisons involve different sensing strategies: multi-view grasping baselines receive predefined observations, while BeyondCSe chooses views as needed (§IV-B). The egocentric examples establish illustrative behavior, without quantitative transfer or physical grasping evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The target must remain stationary during search; continued object motion is future work (§V). Egocentric-video region estimation can be imprecise (§IV-D).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation breadth is limited by tabletop scenes, one robot-camera setup, and a small heavy-occlusion scene set (§IV-A). Repeated trials provide execution evidence but do not establish broad scene generalization.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied experiments do not quantitatively isolate the Record/Select/Locate/Point stages. Their separate contributions to full-system gains therefore remain uncertain (§IV-B–C).
- **Evidence gap:** Tables I and II appear only as captions and prose references in the retrieved text. Full baseline identities, per-scene entries, and uncertainty estimates cannot be recovered from it.

## Open research questions
- **Can search handle moving targets?** The stationary-target assumption leaves this unresolved (§V). A next experiment could introduce controlled target motion during camera search and compare static versus motion-aware belief updates.
- **How robust is the event prior to erroneous historical locations?** A uniform mixture permits recovery in principle, but robustness is not quantified (§III-D1). Perturb recovered tracks and measure success and view counts.
- **Which video-reasoning stages drive grounding gains?** The paper illustrates crop-based pointing but lacks a quantitative stage ablation (§III-C; Fig. 3). Compare direct video-to-point, full-image pointing, and crop-based pointing on the same episodes.
- **Does video reasoning generalize reliably to moving-camera histories?** External-video evidence is qualitative (§IV-D). Evaluate annotated egocentric clips for referent selection and final-frame pointing accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and aggregate results, but lacks full table contents, uncertainty estimates, exact active-view budget, detailed likelihood definitions and prompts, runtime measurements, quantitative egocentric evaluation, and confirmation that evaluation data are released.
