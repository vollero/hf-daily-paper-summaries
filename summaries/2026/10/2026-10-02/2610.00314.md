# Predictive Credit: Measuring What Scientific Explanations Add to Experimental Forecasts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00314) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00314)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across 336 prospective experimental states, matched scientific explanations did not establish joint predictive credit over descriptions and donor explanations, although a controlled, researcher-authored mechanism clue improved forecasting accuracy (§§4, 9; Table 28).

## Problem and motivation
Research agents produce both experimental proposals and explanations of why they should work. Final experimental performance does not reveal whether those explanations help predict outcomes. The authors address this gap by measuring an explanation’s incremental forecasting value while holding the experiment, forecaster, and observed outcome fixed (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired predictive-credit protocol:** Compare description-only context (D), the current experiment’s explanation (M), and a frozen donor explanation (S). Loss differences D−M and S−M measure predictive value and local alignment; positive values favor matched explanations (§3.1).
- **Separate evaluation checks:** Measure prospective commitment, evidence-backed delivery, predictive value, donor alignment, and sensitivity to known information. Two fresh calls per context distinguish repeat drift from outcome accuracy; deterministic fallbacks retain failed calls in intention-to-treat analysis (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Prospective studies and diagnostic controls:** Test controlled learning, Tox21, and OpenML, then examine numerical versus mechanism content, direct-note delivery, exact-outcome signals, and a known-mechanism positive control (§§4, 7; Appendix G).

## Evidence and main results

Positive D−M or S−M loss differences favor matched context.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective joint credit remained unconfirmed | Across 120 controlled v5, 72 Tox21, and 144 OpenML states: v5 was inconclusive; Tox21’s harm rule and OpenML’s joint rule returned “confirmation no go.” These were different study-specific gates. | §4.3; Table 3 |

Drift increased from .768 to 1.275 points. | Table 28; Appendix G.3 |
| Forecaster could use explicit outcome signals | Post-outcome OpenML calibration: exact signal beat description and wrong-seed signal in 144/144 states; forecast–signal Spearman correlation .99996. Frozen calibration passed. | Table 24; Appendix F |

The evidence supports separating commitment, repeatability, uncertainty, and accuracy. It does not establish that natural explanations are universally unhelpful: credit is relative to the tested forecasters, losses, populations, delivery pipelines, and donor resolution (§3.1). The positive controls establish sensitivity to strong supplied information, not predictive credit for ordinary agent explanations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope boundary:** Seed donors often represented similar outcomes: matching effect signs occurred in 27/36 Tox21 pairs and 54/72 OpenML pairs. Only one OpenML donor pair had quantitative claims on both sides, limiting alignment discrimination (Appendix E.8).
- **Delivery failures:** OpenML elicited extraction integrity was 87/144; exhausted calls remained as fallbacks. Delivered-content subsets were selected post hoc and unstable across comparisons (Appendices E.7, E.9).
- **Pipeline confounding:** Raw-note replay changed both representation and predictor, from extracted Pro cards to raw-text Flash. **Analyst inference:** it cannot isolate the effect of removing extraction alone (§7.1; Appendix G.2).
- **Aggregate sensitivity:** Brazilian houses contributed 37.0% of description ensemble error and 41.1% of matched-full error, making pooled MAE sensitive to task scale (§7.2; Table 22).
- **Timing and multiplicity:** Follow-ups used already-known source outcomes under separately frozen plans; several component contrasts were exploratory and unadjusted (§4.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Model identification:** Requested DeepSeek routes were recorded, but returned wrappers lacked resolved model IDs (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Would more discriminative donors reveal alignment?** Seed donors often shared effect signs and similar claims. Next experiment: prospectively compare seed donors with donors selected for distinct mechanisms while controlling intervention and task.
- **Does extraction suppress useful explanation content?** Delivery was incomplete, and the raw-text comparison also changed predictor. Next experiment: cross raw notes and extracted cards with the same forecaster and frozen states.
- **When do natural mechanism explanations add information beyond numbers?** The fixed-number mechanism crossover was inconclusive, while the authored positive control succeeded. Next experiment: vary mechanism informativeness while keeping numerical targets fixed (Appendix G.1).
- **Can intervals be calibrated for larger effects?** OpenML coverage deteriorated on post hoc larger-movement subsets. Next experiment: fit calibration on separate development tasks and prospectively evaluate held-out coverage and interval score (§6.3; Appendix E.6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol and study-specific results; broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes resolved served-model identities, an explicit accessible artifact-release location, full prompts and raw ledgers, and a comparison that changes explanation representation while keeping the predictor fixed.
