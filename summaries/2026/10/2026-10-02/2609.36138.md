# When Does Correction Become Repair? Mechanistic Auditing of Internal Interventions in Tool-Using LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36138) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36138)


## One-sentence takeaway
SAKIKO shows that improved tool-decision accuracy can conceal wrong destinations and collateral damage, licensing repair in only one sealed configuration while certifying safety on intervention-exposed correct decisions in none.

## Problem and motivation
Before executing tools, an LLM must choose among calling a tool, requesting clarification, answering directly, and declining. In this multiclass setting, steering away from an incorrect action can lead to another incorrect action, while also breaking previously correct decisions.

The authors identify a gap in evaluations based on aggregate accuracy gains or binary action changes: these do not establish destination correctness, preservation, or sufficient statistical evidence for repair (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Directional error formulation:** Errors are ordered pairs of required and predicted actions. Five outcome classes distinguish retained errors, correct arrivals, alternative errors, preserved correct decisions, and broken correct decisions (§3.1; Table 1).
- **SAKIKO auditing pipeline:** Discover supported channels, fit a linear hidden-state router, apply channel-specific activation steering to frozen models, audit destinations and collateral, then adjudicate claims using a prospectively frozen gate (§3; Apps. D–E).
- **Evidence-qualified repair:** The gate combines sample support, matched-random specificity, destination metrics, uncertainty bounds, population collateral, and execution integrity. Its preservation guarantee concerns all baseline-correct decisions, not specifically those exposed to steering (Tables 6–7).
- **Empirical dissociations:** Controls and exploratory analyses separate decodability from steerability, accuracy gains from correct destinations, and favorable estimates from licensable evidence (§§4–5; App. M).

## Evidence and main results
Here, target-hit is correct arrivals divided by source exits; target gain is correct arrivals minus alternative-error exits, divided by all channel errors.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong accuracy gain can coexist with severe collateral | Historical Phi-3.5-mini, locked When2Call test: 107 correct arrivals and 52 breaks give whole-population net **+55**; 46 additional exits reach other wrong actions. Breakage is **52/93 exposed correct decisions (55.9%)**, versus **52/264 overall (19.7%)**. | Fig. 4a; App. J.1 |
| One sealed configuration earns a narrow repair license | Qwen3-8B, required `cannot_answer` but baseline `tool_call`: **38 correct arrivals, 14 other-wrong exits, 87 channel errors**. Target-hit **0.7308**, 95% bootstrap CI **[0.604, 0.846]**; target gain **24/87 = 0.2759**, CI **[0.115, 0.425]**. All frozen conditions pass. | Table 10; App. H |
| Favorable point estimates do not ensure licensing | Same sealed channel: Qwen3-4B target-hit **37/64 = 0.5781**, CI **[0.4559, 0.6970]**; Gemma-2-9B **17/27 = 0.6296**, CI lower bound **0.4444**. Both target-gain intervals cross zero; both receive **DECLINE**. | App. I; §§5.3; Tables 11, 15; App. Q |
| Sealed effects exceed the specified random-direction null | In each of three sealed settings, **0 of 59** isotropic directions with matched perturbation budget and router gate reach calibrated target gain; add-one **p = 1/60 = 0.0167**. | Table 5; Apps. K, O |
| Directional specificity is configuration-dependent | Historical Llama-3.1-8B: **5/20** random directions reach the real effect. Mistral-7B: calibrated whole-population net **+12**, versus random mean **+29** and reversal **+19**. These records do not establish specificity. | Table 5; App. J.4 |
| Similar gains conceal different destinations, without demonstrated superiority | Sealed Qwen3-8B: activation versus score-space intervention yields whole-population net **+40 versus +39**, target-hit **38/52 versus 37/58**, and other-wrong exits **14 versus 21**. Paired comparisons are non-significant after multiplicity correction. | Table 13; App. L |
| The admitted setting does not certify exposed-decision preservation | Qwen3-8B observes **0/6** exposed correct decisions broken, but the one-sided 95% upper bound is **0.3930**. No evaluated setting certifies the specified exposed collateral bound. | Apps. H.3, O, Q |

The evidence supports destination-resolved auditing and direction-specific steering under the tested configurations. It does **not** establish deployment safety, downstream tool-execution validity, activation steering’s superiority over score shifting, or general repair across models and channels. Historical results carry no formal sealed verdict.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** All formal licenses come from When2Call; MetaTool supplies binary historical evidence, and ACEBench stops before intervention. When2Call has synthetic labels without re-annotation, and direct answering appears only as a distractor, not a gold class (Apps. A, F).
- **Author-stated preservation gap:** The frozen gate uses population collateral. Its bootstrap upper bound degenerates to zero when no break is observed; the authors report informative exact bounds separately. No configuration certifies preservation among exposed correct decisions (Apps. E.4, O).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** ASA and CAST were not re-implemented; score-space comparisons establish no superiority. Mechanistic evidence identifies intervention sites and directions, not detailed causal circuits (App. A).
- **Reproducibility limits:** Several historical configurations and row-level records are unavailable. An independent Phi-3.5 execution differs for unexplained reasons; procedural repairs and a mechanical restart are disclosed (Table 9; Apps. J.1, P, R).
- **Analyst inference:** Development selection—including comparator performance—limits the sealed result’s relevance to arbitrary channels or general method rankings (App. P.1).
- **Text inconsistency:** §5.2 gives Qwen3-4B collateral percentages inconsistent with its detailed frozen counts. Apps. I and Q support **1/50 exposed** and **1/214 overall**; those counts are used here.

## Open research questions
- **Can repair preserve exposed correct decisions?** Existing exposure samples cannot certify the bound. A next experiment should preregister an exposure-based exact bound and collect enough exposed correct examples (Apps. E.4, O).
- **Does repair transfer beyond the selected channel and benchmark?** Only one configuration is admitted. Test additional supported channels on an independently labeled multiclass benchmark under a frozen protocol (App. A).
- **Does correct action selection improve executed outcomes?** Tools are never executed here. Extend evaluation to argument validity and task completion while retaining destination and collateral audits (§6).
- **Can intervention-site selection improve independently of probe accuracy?** Exploratory results show decodability and steering efficacy diverge. Compare preregistered site-selection rules on fresh evaluation data (App. M).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The sealed results are supported by detailed counts, intervals, and provenance, but generality is narrow and some narrative values conflict with detailed records.

Unavailable or unestablished: complete historical configurations and destinations; an explanation for Phi-3.5 execution divergence; independently verified released artifacts; downstream execution outcomes; cross-benchmark repair transfer; and certified preservation on exposed correct decisions.
