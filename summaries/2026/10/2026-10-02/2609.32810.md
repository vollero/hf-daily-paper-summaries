# OpenTumorBoard: A Real-World Benchmark of Multidisciplinary Tumor Board Discussion Trajectories

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32810) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32810)


## One-sentence takeaway

OpenTumorBoard benchmarks specialist responses and simulated tumor boards against recorded clinical discussions, revealing low model agreement with observed decisions and modest gains from training on those trajectories.

## Problem and motivation

The authors target evaluation of LLMs supporting multidisciplinary cancer decisions. They argue that existing benchmarks lack the combination of real tumor-board cases, observed specialist exchanges, reference consensus, and multimodal longitudinal evidence needed to test this workflow (§1, Table 1). Recorded discussions provide references for both individual answers and whole-board decisions, rather than answers constructed retrospectively.

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark and trajectories:** 611 cases from 219 public YouTube meetings, containing 19,157 discussion turns across ten specialist roles and 11 input modalities. The authors claim novelty in pairing complete naturally occurring discussions with observed consensus (§§1–2.3).
- **Automated curation:** Transcription, diarization, case segmentation, slide extraction, role inference, temporal alignment, and LLM-based annotation produce structured cases. A deletion-focused procedure removes information unavailable at the decision point (§2.2, Appendices A and F).
- **Two evaluation tasks:** SPECIALISTTURN answers an extracted clinical question in an assigned role; BOARDSIMULATION generates a discussion and final consensus. Frozen LLM-judge rubrics assess clinical equivalence and conclusion alignment (§2.4, Appendices B–C).
- **Training testbed and audits:** Recorded discussions supervise Qwen2.5-VL-3B finetuning, followed by Dr.GRPO reinforcement learning using decision agreement, discussion–conclusion consistency, and format rewards. Clinicians audit annotation quality separately from model performance (§§3.3–3.4, Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial benchmark scale | 611 cases and 19,157 turns from 219 meetings; test split contains 184 cases and 4,844 questions; recordings split 60/10/30 | §§1–2.3 |
| Best specialist response remains partially equivalent | DeepSeek-V4-Pro reasoning, **caption-only** input: clinical equivalence **3.43/5**, critical-error rate **5.6%**, unsupported-claim rate **11.8%**, across 4,844 test questions | Table A2 |
| Evidence-heavy questions expose grounding failures | The same configuration has unsupported-claim rates of **31.5%** on 73 clinical-trial questions and **23.5%** on 677 evidence-discussion questions | Table A5 |
| Whole-board agreement is low | Gemini 3.7 Flash reasoning, image-plus-caption input: highest conclusion alignment, **2.78/5**, across 184 test cases and 14 configurations | Table A3 |
| Longer outputs correlate with agreement | Across 14 configurations, mean output length and conclusion alignment have Spearman **ρ = 0.63** | §3.2, Figure 5B |
| Post-training improves aggregate agreement | Qwen2.5-VL-3B alignment: **1.58 → 1.67 → 1.86** for base, SFT, and SFT+RL on 184 test cases. Malformed outputs: **22 → 3 → 4**, scored at 1 | Table A4 |
| RL gains are uneven across decisions | Clinical-trial matching falls from **1.89** after SFT to **1.84** after RL, while therapy, surgery, and next-action scores improve; dimension means include only reference-relevant cases | Table A4 |
| Clinician audit supports reference fidelity | Among 447 question–answer pairs, **92.6%** receive High correctness and **94.4%** High discussion support. All 15 audited summaries and conclusions receive High ratings on their respective dimensions after adjudication | Table 2 |
| Curation audit finds residual extraction errors | Fifteen recordings: case-boundary accuracy **98.1%** over 106 boundaries, slide recall **93.6%** over 311 slides, role accuracy **96.3%** over 136 speakers | Table A1 |

These results establish reference agreement under the paper’s automated scoring protocol, not clinical effectiveness or patient benefit. The length correlation does not establish that longer discussions cause better decisions. The clinician audit evaluates extracted references, not agreement between clinicians and the model judge.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated:** Public YouTube recordings may not represent institutional tumor boards; clinician calibration of judge scores remains future work. No independent patient-identifier screening was performed beyond protections in the source recordings (pp. 9–10).

**Analyst inferences supported by the protocol:**

- Comparisons mix caption-only and image-plus-caption inputs, limiting attribution of differences to model capability alone (Tables A2–A3).
- BOARDSIMULATION scoring evaluates the final conclusion alone; it does not directly establish fidelity of generated discussion trajectories (Appendix C).
- No direct-answer baseline or trajectory-supervision ablation isolates the benefit of simulating multidisciplinary discussion (Tables A3–A4).
- Fewer malformed outputs contribute to the training improvement; performance conditioned on valid outputs is not reported (Table A4).
- De-leaking edits summaries, captions, and questions, but does not describe redacting slide images. Residual image leakage therefore remains unresolved; all de-leaking judgments are model-based (Appendix F).
- The narrative says RL improves all four decisions, but Table A4 shows a clinical-trial decline relative to SFT.

## Open research questions

- **Do judge scores match clinician assessments?** Calibration remains explicitly unfinished. Next experiment: blinded clinician scoring of sampled model outputs, with agreement and ranking comparisons.
- **Does simulated discussion improve decisions?** Conclusion-only evaluation and absent direct-answer baselines leave this unresolved. Next experiment: compare direct conclusions and board simulation under matched inputs and token budgets.
- **Does training generalize beyond public recordings?** Representativeness is uncertain. Next experiment: evaluate frozen models on independently curated institutional cases.
- **How much leakage remains in images?** The stated procedure does not cover image redaction. Next experiment: manually audit slides and compare scores before and after removing decision-revealing content.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in the descriptive findings; **medium** in broader claims about clinical reasoning or trajectory learning.

Material missing information includes clinician–judge calibration, uncertainty intervals for main comparisons, details of the paired test mentioned in §1, valid-output-only training results, image-level leakage measurements, institutional validation, and verification of the claimed asset releases.
