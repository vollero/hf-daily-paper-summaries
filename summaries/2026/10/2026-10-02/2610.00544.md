# Memorizon: Training World Models Beyond Their Context Window

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00544) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00544)


## One-sentence takeaway
Memorizon improves camera-controlled video revisit consistency by training on long spans through a bounded bank of per-chunk retrieved latents, with diminishing gains beyond the tested 200-second span and a measurable image-quality tradeoff.

## Problem and motivation
A video world model should render a previously visited place consistently when the camera returns. Short training clips often omit the first visit, leaving no supervision connecting the two visits; extending dense attention across the intervening video is expensive.

The authors identify two gaps: a single retrieval for an entire clip may poorly serve chunks viewing different places, and retrieval cannot supply the first visit unless the sampled training span reaches it. In their corpus, no return under the corpus definition occurs within a 10-second window; the median shortest return interval is 77.5 seconds. This definition omits heading and therefore differs from the evaluation definition (App. B.2, Figure 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Long-span sampling with bounded input:** Sample a variable-length span, score only its last \(k\) chunks, and supply selected history latents instead of tokenizing the intervening history. At the shortest span, the recipe reduces to ordinary training (Sec. 3.1).
- **Shared union of per-chunk retrievals:** Each scored chunk requests its own top-\(K\) history latents. Their union forms a bank bounded by \(kK\); a mask restricts each chunk to its own selections. Proposition 1 establishes that this bank preserves the selections available from the full history under the specified ranking rule (Sec. 3.2; App. A.2).
- **Age-independent memory layout:** Bank entries share one temporal RoPE index while retaining camera information. The authors argue this supports retrieval beyond training ages; their short-span model does benefit from older frames at inference (Sec. 3.2; App. C.3).
- **Controlled evidence on span, retrieval, and cost:** Ablations separate retrieval organization from training-span length, test whether bank contents matter, and diagnose image-quality loss. The overlap-minus-pose retrieval score is explicitly presented as an adaptation of existing forms, rather than a new scoring principle (Secs. 3.3, 4.3).

A Self Forcing/distribution-matching distillation procedure is also specified, but its effectiveness remains unmeasured (Secs. 3.4, 5).

Unless noted otherwise, rollout results average five seeds; reported ± values are standard deviations of per-seed means.

The controlled ablations support separate benefits from retrieval and longer training spans, and the bank interventions support actual use of retrieved content. They do not establish unrestricted horizon generalization, dynamic-world correctness, or a universally optimal span. The open-model comparison also varies native sampling, resolution, intrinsics, and camera-control interfaces (Sec. 4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Scenes are mainly static. Matching an earlier appearance can be incorrect when objects move or the environment changes (Sec. 5).
- **Author-stated quality problem:** Training uses clean history, whereas inference retrieves generated history. Ground-truth-context interventions support exposure bias as a major source of quality loss; the proposed distillation remedy is not quantitatively evaluated (App. C.9; Sec. 5).
- **Retrieval limitations:** Geometry-based cues ignore occlusion, have viewing-axis ambiguities, and depend on scene scale. Inference ranking remains an unindexed linear scan of history (Sec. 3.3; Apps. A.1, A.3–A.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Pixel Revisit is pose-sensitive. The yaw-following check and fold-back analysis reduce concern about camera-control confounding but do not fully establish matched realized poses across systems (Apps. C.1, C.7).
- **Analyst inference—scope of uncertainty estimates:** Tables report rollout-seed variability, not variability across independently trained replicas. Retrieval-width studies grade selections against ground-truth similarity rather than comparing trained models at different widths (App. A.5).

## Open research questions
1. **Can rollout-based distillation recover quality without sacrificing memory?** Its benefit remains unmeasured. Compare matched checkpoints before and after distillation on Revisit, Gain, VBench, and runtime.
2. **How should memory handle legitimate scene changes?** Static evaluation cannot answer this. Introduce controlled object motion or state changes and score both retained structure and correct updates.
3. **Can training-span sampling target useful returns more efficiently?** Uniform sampling peaks at 200 seconds in these experiments, then regresses. Compare uniform and return-aware sampling under matched compute.
4. **Can retrieval become scale-robust and efficient over much longer histories?** Current scoring uses fixed geometric constants and linear scans. Test scale-normalized, indexed retrieval against exact selection across scene scales and rollout lengths.
