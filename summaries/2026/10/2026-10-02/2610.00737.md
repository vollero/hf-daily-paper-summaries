# Personalized Image Generation with Reasoning and Reflection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00737) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00737)


## One-sentence takeaway
Pearl combines history-based image planning with reflection on rendered outputs, reporting stronger personalization on a new Amazon/Instagram benchmark, although missing result tables and evaluation caveats limit verification (§§3–4; Appendix D.6).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address image generation that reflects a user’s interests, lifestyle, and aesthetics inferred from accumulated reviews or posts. They argue that reproducing a visual concept from curated reference images misses the richer personalization signals distributed across real user histories (§§1–2.1).

The motivating applications are personalized product scenes and social media content creation. Improved engagement is a motivation, rather than an outcome measured in this paper (Appendix B).

## Approach and contributions

- **PMH-IG benchmark:** Two tasks separate the requested content from the personalization context: preserve a supplied product while generating a suitable scene, or depict a requested topic in the user’s visual style. Histories come from Amazon reviews and Instagram posts, with user-disjoint splits (§§2.1–2.4; Appendix B).
- **Pearl framework:** A multimodal planner produces reasoning and a generation prompt; a frozen renderer generates an initial image; a reflector compares that image with the history and produces a revised prompt for a second rendering (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Training procedure:** Teacher-generated silver trajectories initialize both policies, followed by alternating DPO updates scored through the downstream rendering pipeline. The implementation uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B policies, LoRA, a Gemini2.5-Flash teacher, and frozen SDXL-1.0 (§§3.3, 4.2).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Combines aesthetics, retrieval-based personalization, held-out-post similarity, MLLM judgments, and preliminary human comparisons (§2.5; Appendix D).

The authors claim the benchmark is the first of its kind; the supplied text alone cannot independently establish priority.

These results support the authors’ claim that Pearl can produce images judged more consistent with displayed histories in the evaluated settings. They do not establish actual users’ preferences, engagement gains, or broad statistical reliability. Automatic effect sizes cannot be checked because the supplied text contains captions, but no numerical bodies, for the main and ablation tables.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Scene generation lacks ground-truth personalized scenes, so alignment is assessed indirectly. Only one planner/reflector DPO alternation round is studied (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Human evaluation uses one outside annotator and does not establish inter-annotator reliability, population preference, or statistical significance. Creative pipelines receive different history subsets, and one scene account has unresolved input contamination (Appendix D.6).
- **Analyst inference—ablation attribution:** The described ablation removes DPO while retaining supervised training; it does not isolate reflection itself or explicit reasoning. Its naming also varies between “Pearl-Reflection” and “Pearl-SFT” (§4.6; Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation independence:** Creative training rewards include CLIP/DINO target similarities also used for evaluation. Scene rewards include recommendation ranking and Qwen judgment. Gains on related metrics therefore need independent validation (§§2.5, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Filtering favors informative histories and selected lifestyle categories. Performance on sparse histories, ordinary non-influencer users, or other domains remains unestablished (Appendix B).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Table 3 lists 3,972 Amazon training users, while Appendix D.2 says 4,000. Human scene sampling includes Clothing/Shoes/Jewelry, absent from the six-category construction list. Appendix E.1 claims improvement across judge metrics while also reporting an overall-score decline; missing tables prevent reconciliation.

## Open research questions

- **Does reflection help independently of DPO?** The reported ablation changes training rather than cleanly removing reflection. Compare planner-only, planner-plus-reflector SFT, and planner-plus-reflector DPO under matched rendering budgets (§4.6).
- **Do inferred-history preferences match users’ own preferences?** Existing human judgments come from one observer. Run blinded comparisons with the actual users and multiple independent annotators (Appendix D.6).
- **How robust is personalization to sparse or shifting histories?** Filtering emphasizes informative histories. Vary history length and introduce chronological preference shifts while holding conditions fixed (Appendix B).
- **Do further optimization rounds improve independent alignment?** Only one alternation round is evaluated. Compare additional rounds using independent judges, user ratings, and measured computation cost (Appendix A).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and evaluation design are well described, but the magnitude and attribution of gains are less secure.

Material unavailable information includes numerical bodies of Tables 1–2 and 5–6; the derivation of the 15% claim; complete prompt templates; reward weights; key training hyperparameters and runtime; exact product-conditioning implementation; public code/data/checkpoint availability; statistical uncertainty; and original inputs for the flagged scene account.
