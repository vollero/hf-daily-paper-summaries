# Persona Dosing: Calibrated Activation Steering for Graded Trait Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36388) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36388)


## One-sentence takeaway
PersonaDose combines a shared persona-conditioned activation controller with behavioral calibration to achieve graded mean trait expression on held-out questions, while coverage and coherence remain trait dependent.

## Problem and motivation
An activation-steering coefficient has no intrinsic behavioral meaning: the same strength can produce different trait intensities across models and traits. Researchers studying behaviors such as sycophancy need requests expressed on a measurable scale, alongside intelligible responses. The authors distinguish two requirements: learning a useful behavioral range and accurately selecting intensities within that range (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persona-response specialization:** Adapt the existing FLAS architecture using trait-expressing responses. A description-conditioned velocity field transforms frozen-model residual states through three Euler steps; seven traits share one controller per model. Training varies flow time without labeling responses with requested intensities (§3.2).
- **Calibration in behavioral units:** Fit an increasing isotonic curve from intervention strength to measured mean trait expression, then invert it through interpolation. Requests outside the fitted range are rejected as unreachable; reachable requests reuse the controller and calibration map (§3.3).
- **Evaluation of reach and targeting separately:** Measure coherence-constrained expression, held-out intensity error, reachable-request coverage, and recalibration after controller updates. The contribution builds on FLAS rather than introducing its architecture (§§1–2, 5).

The evidence supports aggregate expression gains and approximate mean targeting within measured ranges. It does not establish superiority on every trait: CAA exceeds PersonaDose on Qwen sycophancy and hallucination, for example (Table 2). It also does not establish accurate individual-response dosing, universal coherence preservation, or a paired targeting-MAE advantage over CAA.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations (§7):**

- Evaluation covers seven trained traits and short responses; unseen traits and long conversations are untested.
- Reachability is uneven. Optimism has only one reachable target per model, and Gemma hallucination has none. Nonnegative strengths restrict access below unsteered expression.
- Targets concern population means; individual answers may deviate substantially. Coherence measures intelligibility, not factual correctness or safety.
- Sweep maxima reuse evaluation questions for selection. Held-out tests address this, but coherence constraints do not consistently transfer.
- Bootstrap intervals exclude training and judge variability; targeting intervals also condition on fixed calibration policies.
- Two non-core calibration questions overlap specialization data; no test questions overlap by exact string.
- Different reachable sets prevent paired MAE conclusions. Secondary-judge refusals can create missing judgments.
- Controller continuation changes multiple training choices together, preventing attribution to one change.

**Analyst inference:** Because direction baselines use contrastively extracted activations while PersonaDose receives persona-response supervision, the comparison measures complete intervention pipelines rather than isolating the benefit of the flow architecture (§4). No component ablation reported here separates calibration, diversity regularization, or training-time sampling effects.

## Open research questions
- **Can control generalize to unseen traits?** Only trained traits are evaluated (§7). Next experiment: withhold entire traits during specialization, then calibrate and test description-conditioned control on them.
- **Can coherence constraints transfer more reliably?** Some calibration-selected traits fail the test floor (§5.2). Next experiment: compare the current selector with a selector incorporating calibration uncertainty across repeated question splits.
- **Does PersonaDose improve targeting accuracy on common requests?** Reachable sets differ, and secondary-judge MAE intervals include zero (§7; Appendix C). Next experiment: evaluate paired MAE on a predefined shared reachable set using multiple judges.
- **How costly is calibration drift after updates?** The paper remeasures updated controllers but never applies the old map to them (§6). Next experiment: compare old-map and recalibrated targeting errors on identical held-out questions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol and numerical findings; broader generalization remains unestablished.

Material unavailable information includes public code/checkpoint/data release status, controller parameter counts and compute costs, repeated-training variability, calibration-policy uncertainty, unseen-trait and long-conversation results, and component ablations.
