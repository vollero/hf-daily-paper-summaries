# Beyond Memory: Harnessing Long-Horizon Agents with Explicit Belief States

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01415) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01415)


## One-sentence takeaway
PoS maintains explicit world states and unresolved task requirements, validates updates, and redirects stalled agents, achieving the best aggregate results across four benchmarks and three backbones at substantially higher token cost (Table 1; Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Long interaction histories mix outdated observations with intermediate judgments, making current facts and unfinished requirements difficult to track. The authors argue that retaining or compressing history does not ensure a coherent decision state. Their identified gap is continual belief maintenance combining evidence-based consistency checks with recovery during an ongoing episode when actions stop advancing the goal (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task-conditioned belief:** PoS represents task-relevant Entities, States, and Relations, with provenance and confidence, alongside epistemic gaps—what remains unknown—and achievement gaps—what remains undone. One active gap focuses action selection (§3.1–3.2).
- **Continual validation:** A Belief Sentinel checks candidate updates for internal contradictions and disagreement with observations; the task agent considers feedback and revises its belief before commitment (§3.2).
- **Factorized recovery:** Gap persistence, progress stagnation, and belief recurrence trigger trapping detection. Recovery combines a constraint for static, cyclic, or drifting behavior with a requirement for discriminative evidence or goal-relevant state change (§3.3).

The claimed novelty is this integrated maintenance-and-recovery framework; explicit belief representation itself has precedents acknowledged in §2.2.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

These results support the usefulness of PoS under the supplied benchmark protocols and action budgets. They do not establish superiority at equal token cost, universally accurate beliefs, or real-world clinical effectiveness. The removal studies support component contributions, but do not separately isolate every detection signal or each recovery dimension.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Belief maintenance adds computation; repeated natural-language updates may omit information or distort meaning (Appendix G).
- **Author-stated:** Internally consistent beliefs can still contain incorrect judgments when backbone knowledge is inadequate. PoS lacks systematic external domain-knowledge retrieval (Appendix G).
- **Evaluation scope:** ClinDiag excludes its access-controlled Emergency subset and uses a balanced Common/Rare sample. Its evidence provider and judge both use Qwen3.7-plus (Appendix C.2; D.1).
- **Comparison qualifications:** ACON omits guideline optimization and compressor distillation; PACE is reimplemented with a replacement relevance encoder. Comparisons therefore concern these adaptations (Appendix D.3; Table 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Equal action budgets do not control inference computation, leaving the benefit of PoS’s structure versus simply spending more compute unresolved (Tables 2 and 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Diagnostic progress counts absolute changes in independent, unnormalized confidence scores. Such changes need not indicate improved correctness; their calibration is not evaluated in the supplied text (Appendix B.2; Table 3).
- **Reproducibility:** No fixed API snapshots are available; reported results provide no uncertainty intervals or repeated-run variation (Appendix D.1; Tables 1 and 9).

## Open research questions
1. **Does PoS retain its advantage at equal compute?** Current comparisons match action budgets, while token costs differ substantially. Next experiment: compare against baselines allocated the same total inference-token budget.
2. **How accurately does the detector recognize harmful stagnation?** Detected incidence is reported, but independent trapping labels and detection precision/recall are absent. Next experiment: annotate trajectories and measure detection errors and subsequent recovery success.
3. **Which recovery dimension matters most?** Generic recovery and complete removal are tested, but pattern-only and gap-only constraints are not isolated. Next experiment: run those ablations with the same detector and compute budget.
4. **Can retrieved domain knowledge improve belief correctness?** Authors identify knowledge dependence as unresolved. Next experiment: add controlled retrieval during belief updates and evaluate diagnosis accuracy alongside evidence-supported belief quality.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported methods, settings, and numerical comparisons; causal interpretation and broader generalization remain less certain.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, fixed model snapshots, compute-matched comparisons, independent belief/detector validation, threshold sensitivity, and latency or monetary cost. Exact per-backbone values for the context-scaling curves are not recoverable from the supplied figure text; linked code availability was not checked.
