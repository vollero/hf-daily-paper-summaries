# GraphForge: Training Working Agents with Graph-Anchored Workspace Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38923) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38923)


## One-sentence takeaway
GraphForge synthesizes real-file tasks and evidence-anchored grading rubrics that improve two Qwen working agents through supervised training, while its judge remains weak at detecting fine-grained content errors (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Working agents must reason across files, coordinate tools, and produce usable deliverables. Training them requires realistic workspaces and reliable checks of artifact quality. The authors identify a gap between pipelines that synthesize files with limited content verification and pipelines that use real files without task-specific verifiers (§1; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Graph-grounded task synthesis:** O*NET occupational seeds control coverage; a search agent collects public files, and an evidence graph represents source facts and cross-file dependencies. Both task requirements and grading criteria are compiled from this graph (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Execution-conditioned validation:** A teacher rollout exposes specification problems. A revision agent repairs the task or rubrics against the original files; structural checks, evidence-anchored judging, and behavior filters govern trajectory admission (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Training and selection evidence:** The authors evaluate supervised fine-tuning (SFT) across two base models and multiple scaffolds, then compare anchored, unanchored, and random trajectory selection for rejection fine-tuning (RFT) (§4).
- **Released assets:** The authors report releasing tasks, rubrics, trajectories, and checkpoints; workspace documents are represented by source links rather than redistributed contents (p. 11).

The results support improvements from the complete GraphForge training recipe across the tested models and scaffolds. The matched RFT experiment provides narrower evidence that selection quality matters, with anchoring favored on workspace and spreadsheet point estimates. It does not establish a statistically resolved GDPVal advantage for anchoring. The overlap audit reduces concern about direct source reuse but does not prove the absence of every form of contamination.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Scaling beyond the current corpus, stronger synthesis/judge models, and transfer outside the Qwen family remain untested (§6).
- **Author-stated:** GLM-5.2 detects structural evidence failures more reliably than small content corruptions; an admission score therefore cannot guarantee factual correctness (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** No reported SFT ablation separately isolates evidence graphs, occupational coverage, revision, or real-file grounding. Their individual contributions remain uncertain (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** The RFT comparison conditions all arms on queries admitted by anchored selection, so it tests within-query selection rather than the full admission pipeline (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Workspace and spreadsheet improvements lack reported uncertainty intervals. The supplied text also omits perturbation sample sizes and detailed scaffold configurations.

## Open research questions
- **Which pipeline components drive SFT gains?** The paper evaluates the combined recipe. A matched training experiment removing graphs, revision, or coverage selection individually could isolate their effects.
- **Can verification catch subtle artifact errors?** Fine-grained corruption produces small score changes. Compare alternative judges and deterministic content checks on a labeled corruption set.
- **Does the recipe scale and transfer across model families?** Both issues are explicitly untested. Train multiple corpus sizes on an additional model family under fixed evaluation scaffolds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and results; **medium** in causal attribution to evidence anchoring.

Material omissions include synthesis cost, detailed prompts and schemas, judge calibration against human labels, perturbation sample sizes, workspace/spreadsheet uncertainty estimates, and exact scaffold settings. Hardware/software details are claimed in the reproducibility statement but are absent from the supplied text; the grouped GDPVal transfer comparison also does not explicitly identify its model/scaffold pair.
