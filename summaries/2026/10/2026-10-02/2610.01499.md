# VTR-Bench: A Systematic Benchmark for Evaluating Visual Text Rendering in Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01499) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01499)


## One-sentence takeaway
VTR-Bench separates scene fulfillment from rendered-text accuracy, finding substantial text errors across 11 video generators and showing that keyframe-guided refinement improves Minimax H3 on both measures.

## Problem and motivation
Generated videos can depict convincing scenes while misspelling, omitting, or changing the information written within them. This matters for advertisements, scientific explanations, and interfaces, where incorrect text can alter the intended message. The authors identify limited coverage of longer passages in existing text-rendering benchmarks and the expense of entirely human evaluation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Scene-grounded benchmark:** Human-reviewed, model-assisted construction produces 300 prompts across advertising, science, user interfaces, culture, and daily life. The suite contains 1,202 annotated text blocks with explicit reference strings and carrier descriptions; individual blocks range from 1–275 evaluation tokens (§§3.1–3.2; Fig. 3).
- **Separate automated evaluations:** A VLM transcribes each carrier at its clearest occurrence, with reference text masked from the transcription prompt. Bounded word error rate measures text fidelity. A separate, human-reviewed checklist of 20 binary questions measures scene and motion fulfillment through Video Score (§3.3).
- **Inference-time generation framework:** A Director agent coordinates first-frame generation and editing, motion planning, video regeneration, and candidate selection using visual feedback. Video-generator weights remain unchanged (§3.4; Fig. 4).
- **Comparative evidence:** The paper evaluates 11 generators, examines failure modes and generation settings, and checks evaluator agreement with humans and an alternative VLM (§4; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

Main evaluation uses Qwen3.8-27B, bounded WER with additional-token allowance α = 5, and videos configured for 10 seconds at 24 FPS; model resolutions vary (§§3.3, 4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Even the strongest evaluated model retains substantial text error | Across the 300-prompt suite, Wan3.0 achieves the lowest overall WER, **0.250**, and Video Score **0.849**; Minimax H3 achieves WER **0.447** and Video Score **0.756**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Similar scene fulfillment can conceal different text fidelity | Kling v3.0 and Seedance2.5 achieve Video Scores **0.793** and **0.791**, but WERs **0.979** and **0.641**, respectively, under the main protocol. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Higher resolution helps one tested model but does not resolve another’s failures | At 10 seconds, Minimax H3 WER falls from **0.5823** at 832×480 to **0.4468** at 1344×768. LTX-2.3 remains near the WER ceiling across the tested resolution-duration combinations. | Fig. 5; §4.3 |
| Agentic refinement improves Minimax H3 | Overall WER decreases from **0.4468** for direct generation to **0.3015**, a reported **32.5% relative reduction**; Video Score rises from **0.7562** to **0.8315**. I2V and Agentic videos exclude their first frame during evaluation. | §4.4; Fig. 7 |
| Full refinement adds gains beyond initial-image conditioning | Agentic reduces aggregate WER by a reported **9.4% relative to I2V** and improves Video Score in every scenario; I2V nevertheless has lower WER in UI scenes. | §4.4; Fig. 7 |
| Automated evaluation aligns closely with the sampled human judgments | On **100 videos**, **2,000 queries**, and **404 blocks**, CoQ agreement is **92.15%**; video-level WER Pearson correlation is **0.9842**, Lin’s CCC **0.9835**, and MAE **0.0407**. Three annotators label each item. | §4.5; Table 2 |
| Rankings withstand two evaluator choices | Qwen3.6-27B and Qwen3.8-27B produce identical overall rankings across all **11 models**, with rank Spearman correlation **1.000** for both metrics. The top-three WER ordering also persists across α = 0–10 under both evaluators. | Appendix C.1–C.3; Tables 3–5 |

These results support separating textual correctness from scene fulfillment and demonstrate an improvement for the tested Minimax H3 pipeline. They do not establish continuous text correctness throughout a video, broad transfer of the framework to other generators, or whether refinement remains advantageous at equal generation cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text has no dedicated limitations section. Relevant qualifications and **analyst inferences** are:

- **Temporal coverage:** Transcription uses each carrier’s clearest occurrence (§3.3). **Inference:** WER can miss deterioration or inconsistent wording at other times; the separate temporal checklist does not establish frame-by-frame textual fidelity.
- **Human-validation sampling:** The authors deliberately favor models with recoverable text (§4.5). **Inference:** Reported alignment is less informative about predominantly unreadable outputs.
- **Refinement attribution and cost:** Refinement is tested only on Minimax H3, with direct and I2V comparisons (§4.4). **Inference:** The study does not isolate editing, motion planning, candidate selection, or additional sampling, and lacks a matched-budget comparison.
- **Cross-model settings:** Resolutions vary and open-source models use their own default settings (§4.1; Table 1). **Inference:** Rankings compare configured systems rather than architectures under identical conditions.
- **Metric scope:** WER is capped at one, truncates excess transcription tokens, and ignores ordinary prose punctuation (§3.3). **Inference:** It compresses severe failures and does not capture every potentially meaningful punctuation error.
- **Construction dependence:** Feasibility screening relies on generated still images plus human review (§3.1; Appendix B.2). **Inference:** This establishes scene accommodation more directly than feasibility under motion.

## Open research questions
- **Does improved clearest-frame accuracy persist throughout motion?** The current transcription protocol leaves this unresolved. Next experiment: score carrier text at fixed timestamps and report temporal error trajectories.
- **Which refinement actions justify their computational cost?** The aggregate Agentic comparison does not isolate components or sampling effort. Next experiment: matched-budget ablations of image editing, motion-plan revision, and candidate selection.
- **Does the framework generalize across video generators?** Refinement results cover only Minimax H3. Next experiment: repeat direct, I2V, and Agentic comparisons on additional generators with recorded call budgets.
- **How reliable is evaluation on difficult-to-transcribe outputs?** Human alignment favors recoverable text. Next experiment: stratify human validation by model and transcription state, including unreadable and missing-carrier cases.
