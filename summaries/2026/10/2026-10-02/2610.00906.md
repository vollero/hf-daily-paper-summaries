# ActiveSaddler: Automated Curriculum Learning for Agent Harness Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00906) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00906)


## One-sentence takeaway
ActiveSaddler adaptively selects training scenarios through discovered failure patterns, improving test Pass@1 over fixed-order AutoSaddler by 4.4 percentage points on GAIA2 and 7.5 on Terminal-Bench 2.0 under matched rollout budgets (Table 1; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
Automated harness optimizers use execution feedback to revise agent prompts, tools, and control logic, but typically fix the training-scenario schedule beforehand. As the harness changes, repaired failures may stop being useful, unresolved weaknesses may need repeated attention, and unseen scenarios may reveal new problems. The authors identify adaptive selection of **which scenarios generate optimization feedback** as a missing dimension, particularly when rollouts are expensive and limited (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Curriculum formulation:** Treat the harness as an evolving learner and scenario selection as a non-stationary bandit with optimization targets discovered during training; keep the underlying harness-update mechanism fixed (§§3–4).
- **Failure-pattern arms:** Independently extract symptoms from failed traces, then match them to existing patterns, compose existing patterns, or create new arms. Each arm retains supporting scenarios and diagnostic evidence (§4).
- **Adaptive allocation:** An LLM scores known arms using severity, fixability, breadth, and side-effect risk; softmax sampling selects an arm. A separate LLM controller chooses between revisiting known failures and exploring unseen scenarios (§4; Appendix K).
- **Implementation and evaluation:** Instantiate the curriculum on AutoSaddler with a shared pattern registry and CLI, and demonstrate an additional GAIA2 application to GEPA (Appendices A, E).

The contribution is the adaptive training curriculum, rather than a new model-weight training procedure or replacement harness optimizer (§3).

## Evidence and main results
All test results below report mean Pass@1 over three test-time executions. Main optimization budgets are 1,400 task rollouts on GAIA2 and 490 on TB2; candidate evaluations also count toward these budgets (Appendices A–B).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive curriculum improves the main optimizer | GAIA2, 300 test tasks: **59.8 ± 1.0%** versus AutoSaddler **55.4 ± 1.2%**; TB2, 40 test tasks: **80.0 ± 2.5%** versus **72.5 ± 0.0%**—gains of 4.4 and 7.5 pp. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Beats fixed difficulty curricula | Category/scenario ordering achieves 55.9/55.7% on GAIA2 and 70.8/73.3% on TB2, versus ActiveSaddler’s 59.8/80.0%. Ordering uses five base-harness training evaluations, charged to the budget. | Table 1; §5.1; Appendix F |
| Each core component contributes | Replacing pattern arms with category/scenario arms gives GAIA2 56.8/56.2% and TB2 69.2/73.3%; uniform arm scores give 55.3/72.5%; exploration every five iterations gives 55.3/71.7%. | Table 1; §5.3 |
| Limited optimization-run replication supports consistency | A second independent GAIA2 run reaches 61.6 ± 2.9%, versus 55.9–56.6% for fixed-order variants on the same 300 held-out tasks. | Appendix C, Table 4 |
| Benefit extends to another optimizer | GEPA with ActiveSaddler reaches 57.2 ± 0.4% versus GEPA’s 54.2 ± 2.2% on GAIA2: +3.0 pp. | Appendix E, Table 6 |
| Tested non-LLM alternatives underperform | GAIA2: EMA scoring reaches 56.7 ± 1.5%; count-based UCB-AIR exploration reaches 55.6 ± 1.4%, versus 59.8 ± 1.0%. Other curriculum components remain unchanged. | Appendix D, Table 5 |
| Additional optimizer overhead can be offset | Including task-agent and optimizer costs, GAIA2 development accuracy of 58.5% costs $298 for ActiveSaddler versus $1,360 for AutoSaddler; TB2 development accuracy of 78.9% costs $128 versus $220. | Appendix F, Figure 5 |

These experiments support adaptive scenario selection in the tested offline settings, with component ablations strengthening the attribution. They do not establish universal superiority, calibrated predictions of learning progress, or production readiness. Cost comparisons concern development-set trajectories, not test accuracy at matched monetary expenditure.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation uses controlled benchmarks and public/synthetic data. Production safety, security, privacy, misuse, and governance readiness are outside scope; there is no independent safety filter or dedicated reward-hacking detection mechanism (Appendix L).
- **Analyst inference—limited generality:** Main experiments use gpt-5.5 and two benchmarks; optimizer transfer and independent-run replication are reported only on GAIA2 (§5.1; Appendices C, E).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty coverage:** Table 1’s standard deviations describe test-time execution variability, not variation across independently optimized harnesses. TB2 has only 40 test tasks and no reported second optimization run (Table 1; Appendix C).
- **Protocol ambiguity:** §5.1 broadly describes disjoint-group, out-of-distribution splits, but Appendix A explicitly identifies TB2’s split as a uniform random partition. Distribution-shift evidence therefore applies more clearly to GAIA2.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Appendix K references supporting skills whose full contents are absent from the supplied text. It also mentions a minimum-probability floor, whereas the formal algorithm specifies softmax without an explicit floor (Appendix B; Figure 15).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism uncertainty:** Component ablations do not isolate the four scoring axes or quantify extraction accuracy; the estimated utility remains an LLM heuristic (§4; Appendix K).

## Open research questions
- **Do gains persist across models and optimization seeds?** Coverage is concentrated on one model configuration, with limited repeated optimization. Run paired multi-seed experiments across task-agent and curriculum-model choices.
- **How reliable are failure-pattern abstractions?** Pattern lists and case studies show useful examples but do not measure diagnostic consistency. Compare repeated extraction against independently reviewed labels and test controlled merging/splitting errors.
- **Which scoring factors predict useful repairs?** The paper combines four axes without isolating their effects. Ablate each axis and compare predicted scores with subsequent held-out gains and regressions.
- **Can task gains coexist with independent safety constraints?** Dedicated safety and specification-gaming evaluations are absent. Add adversarial tests and safety regression gates during optimization, measuring both task performance and violations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly documents the method, comparisons, and main results. Material missing information includes complete supporting skill files, verified code availability, broad optimization-seed uncertainty, extraction/scoring calibration, an explicit probability-floor implementation, and independent production-risk evaluations.
