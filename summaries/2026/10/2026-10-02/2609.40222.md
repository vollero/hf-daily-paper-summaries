# LOCI: Spatial Linear Memory for Streaming World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40222) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40222)


## One-sentence takeaway
LOCI combines camera-conditioned recurrent memory with retained visual KV history, improving full-segment MIND PSNR by 0.89 dB over an identically trained full-softmax baseline under the same bounded observation budget while supporting constant-memory streaming (Table 2; §5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Camera-controlled video models should restore previously observed content when revisiting a place. Historical KV caches preserve detail but grow with trajectory length; fixed-size recurrent states compress history and can lose individual observations. The authors identify an additional gap: retaining relevant evidence does not ensure that current queries retrieve and use it, especially when spatial relevance differs from temporal recency (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Coupled spatial memory:** LOCI interleaves recurrent/local-attention blocks with historical-softmax blocks. Camera-conditioned recurrent readouts enter the feature stream, influencing queries in subsequent historical-attention layers without a separate router. The layer layout builds on ARL2; the contribution is its spatial-memory adaptation and interaction with explicit history (§§2, 4).
- **Geometry-conditioned recurrence:** PRoPE transforms recurrent queries, keys, and values. Token-wise delta updates preserve associative write resolution, while learned retention occurs once per chunk, reducing dependence of explicit forgetting on raster order (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Bounded streaming:** A fixed-size recurrent state accompanies a bounded bank of complementary views, a recent window, and a conditioning-frame sink. Discarded KV observations are not archived, but their information can persist in compressed state (§4.3; Appendix A.4).
- **Ground-truth revisit evaluation:** Controlled comparisons, state interventions, and attention measurements test fidelity and the proposed recurrent-to-retrieval interaction (§§5.3–5.4).

| Recurrent readout contributes to retrieval | Teacher-forced attention probes on 8 clips and 154 revisits: disabling the readout reduces downstream co-visible attention enrichment by 0.56 [0.21, 0.77]. Own-rollout masked LPIPS is 0.597 versus 0.654 for full softmax. | §5.4; Table 5 |

The strongest evidence supports an architectural benefit under matched training and retained observations. Readout interventions support a contribution to historical retrieval, but explain only part of the attention difference. External rankings are less controlled, and yaw decodability alone does not establish learned spatial understanding: an untrained camera-conditioned branch decodes yaw about as well (§5.4). Constant-memory streaming is demonstrated through the tested duration; it does not establish indefinitely stable visual fidelity.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Recorded held-out trajectories are rendered, with no real-world capture evaluation; absolute fidelity remains low. Experiments use one backbone and a short fine-tuning budget (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Camera-attention KV remains in every block, so LOCI halves main-attention history layers rather than all history storage. Training reads noised features, whereas inference commits generated chunks; bounded-mode speed parity depends on optimization (§6).
- **Author-stated:** The extended retention comparison was defined after the short-range analysis and is exploratory (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** External comparisons cannot isolate memory design because model sizes, training, sampling, and memory interfaces differ. Full-history controlled MIND results cover only jointly completed segments (§5.2; Table 2; Table 9).
- **Analyst inference:** The panorama bank ignores depth and occlusion, and within-chunk delta corrections remain order-dependent. These leave unresolved robustness questions despite chunk-level retention (Appendices A.4 and A.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Selected qualitative frames show favorable margins rather than representative averages; aggregate tables provide stronger evidence (Figs. 4–5).

## Open research questions
- **Does the gain transfer to real captures?** Real-world recorded evaluation is absent (§6). Next experiment: matched revisit trajectories with calibrated real cameras and recorded ground truth.
- **How much fidelity survives after relevant KV is evicted?** The recurrent state carries compressed history, but the reported comparisons do not partition returns by whether relevant observations remain retained. Next experiment: score revisits by bank membership and intervene on the recurrent readout.
- **Is chunk retention robust across tokenizations?** Explicit decay is chunk-based, but delta updates remain order-dependent and the longer-gap evidence is exploratory. Next experiment: predefine evaluation while varying resolution, chunk size, and token order.
- **Does bounded fidelity remain stable over substantially longer rollouts?** Resource measurements reach the tested streaming horizon. Next experiment: repeated-return trajectories over longer durations, reporting fidelity and memory together.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported controlled gains and storage design; **medium** in broader generalization and the complete retrieval mechanism.

Material information unavailable includes confirmed code/weight/data releases, real-world recorded revisit results, cross-backbone replication, multiple-training-run variability, and fidelity beyond the tested long-rollout settings.
