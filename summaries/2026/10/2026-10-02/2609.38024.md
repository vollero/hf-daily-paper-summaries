# Retrieval-Augmented Skill Optimization via Cross-Harness Adaptation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38024) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38024)


## One-sentence takeaway
RASO retrieves and adapts external procedural knowledge for agent skill initialization and refinement, achieving the highest reported mean performance across four benchmarks and two models, with adaptation and fixed-initialization ablations supporting its components.

## Problem and motivation
Agent skills guide a frozen language model’s behavior through reusable natural-language instructions. The authors argue that existing optimization methods rely heavily on costly execution rollouts while underusing publicly shared skills; directly retrieved skills can also reference incompatible tools or domain assumptions (§§1–3). The gap is how to reuse external procedural knowledge throughout optimization while respecting the target execution harness.

## Approach and contributions
- **Retrieval throughout optimization:** RASO combines rollout-free Retrieval-Augmented Skill Initialization (RASI) with feedback-driven Retrieval-Augmented Skill Update (RASU). Both retrieve heading-delimited skill sections from GitSkills using BM25 (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Cross-Harness Adaptation:** An adaptation agent converts retrieved sections into targeted lessons, removes incompatible procedures, and retains specific tool claims only when supported by the target harness description (§4.1).
- **Failure-directed refinement:** RASU generates textual gradients and retrieval queries from failed trajectories, incorporates adapted lessons, and accepts candidate skills only when validation performance improves (§4.3).

The claimed novelty is this combination of external retrieval during both stages and shared adaptation across domains and harnesses; BM25 and textual feedback are existing ingredients.

## Evidence and main results
Scores below are reported performance percentages; differences are percentage points. Table 1 reports means and standard errors over three seeds. Evaluators use correctness for OfficeQA and SpreadsheetBench, environment success for ALFWorld, and product-match reward for WebShop (§3; Appendix A.3).

The evidence supports benefits from retrieved knowledge, adaptation, and feedback-directed updating in the tested settings. Main comparisons combine initialization and update advantages; matched-initialization experiments isolate updating on two benchmarks. Three-seed means and standard errors do not establish statistical significance. “Rollout-free” describes skill construction, not the executions needed to measure its performance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged contamination risk:** Public skills may target evaluation benchmarks. Repository-level keyword exclusions mitigate this, but the text provides no audit establishing that all benchmark-specific knowledge was removed (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited mechanism coverage:** Adaptation, corpus-size, retrieval-size, and fixed-initialization analyses focus on OfficeQA and SpreadsheetBench. Their conclusions are less directly tested on ALFWorld and WebShop (§5.2; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—cost scope:** API-cost measurements do not establish total system cost; corpus preparation, indexing, and retrieval infrastructure costs are not separately reported (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference—reproduction gaps:** Exact prompts, corpus snapshot and indexed section counts, blocklist terms, and executable release details are absent. Exact split identifiers and some harness/scoring parameters are also unavailable (Appendices A.1–A.3).
- **Analyst inference—transfer scope:** Fig. 5 illustrates procedural transfer across harnesses, but the study does not systematically hold source and target harness families apart.

## Open research questions
1. **How robust is adaptation to deliberately unseen harness families?** Current evidence includes a qualitative transfer example and two-benchmark ablations. A next experiment could exclude all source skills sharing the target harness family and compare adapted versus direct retrieval.
2. **Does RASU retain its advantage across all interaction settings under matched initialization?** Those comparisons cover only OfficeQA and SpreadsheetBench. Repeat them on ALFWorld and WebShop with identical initial skills and rollout schedules.
3. **How much do corpus quality and contamination affect gains?** Keyword exclusions and corpus-size experiments leave this unresolved. Compare audited clean, deduplicated, and deliberately noisy corpora under the same retrieval budget.
4. **Do benefits survive a full resource accounting?** Reported costs cover API usage and rollout counts. Measure end-to-end latency, tokens, indexing costs, and performance under matched total-cost budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reports useful component controls, but statistical significance and full reproducibility remain unresolved.

Material missing information includes exact prompts and split identifiers, corpus snapshot/counts and exclusion terms, detailed scoring tolerances, significance tests, end-to-end resource costs, and confirmed code or skill releases.
