# RPTune: Learned Context Curation for LLM Catalog Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00964) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00964)


## One-sentence takeaway
RPTune learns to prune and order merchant catalogs using downstream LLM feedback, then post-trains product selection on those contexts, improving accuracy and latency on a synthetic, partially human-verified benchmark of seven merchants.

## Problem and motivation
The paper studies selecting one best-matching product variant from a merchant’s catalog for a single-turn, multi-constraint query. The authors argue that small merchants can use full-catalog LLM prompting without the infrastructure and behavioral data required by large-marketplace retrieval pipelines, but fitting a catalog into context does not ensure effective use of it (§§1–2).

Their identified gap is jointly adapting catalog presentation and downstream selection: relevance ranking alone does not account for how an LLM responds to context length, ordering, and competing products.

## Approach and contributions
- **Learned catalog curator:** A contrastively trained encoder supplies query–product similarities; an MLP adds learned priority adjustments. The curator retains top-scoring variants and orders them with the highest priorities nearest the generation prompt (§3.1).
- **LLM-feedback training:** A frozen LLM samples selections from curated contexts. Group-normalized relevance rewards train the MLP through a differentiable surrogate over catalog priorities. Selections come from the LLM, rather than the surrogate distribution—an explicit methodological distinction (§3.2, Eq. 1).
- **Context-relative post-training:** With the curator frozen, GRPO rewards choosing the highest-relevance variant *available in the curated context*, penalizes products absent from that context, and gives other selections zero reward (§3.2).
- **Catalog-grounded supervision and evaluation:** Synthetic training queries and catalog-wide relevance scores require no merchant-provided labels or logs. Experiments examine backbone transfer, inventory additions, unseen merchants, and component choices (§§3.2–4.4).

## Evidence and main results
EM denotes exact variant-match accuracy; FR denotes weighted similarity to the reference variant. Main results average seven merchants, each with 100 queries run ten times (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full-catalog prompting beats tested search pipelines | With Gemini 3.7 Flash, raw full-catalog prompting achieves **36.8% EM**, versus **27.8%** for the strongest listed retrieval baseline, Jina-based RAG. Latencies are **8.0 s** and **9.2 s**, respectively; Gemini-embedding RAG is faster at **3.4 s** but achieves **26.9% EM**. | Table 2 |
| Learned curation improves the shared backbone | At **25% variant retention**, Flash improves from **36.8% to 51.4% EM**, **69.0% to 77.3% FR**, and **8.0 to 5.1 s**. Alternative selectors at the same retention achieve **33.8–37.8% EM**. | Table 2 |
| Improvements extend across backbones | Authors report average gains of **13.5 EM points** and **10.1 FR points** across nine frozen backbones, with both metrics improving in all **63 backbone–merchant combinations**; average latency reduction is **28%**. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Post-training adds substantial gains | Gemma progresses from **10.7% EM** raw, to **20.7%** with the curator, to **31.0%** after post-training. FR progresses **56.7% → 65.1% → 74.4%**; latency **5.8 → 3.9 → 3.7 s**. | Table 2 |
| LLM feedback beats the supervised reorganizer control | With the same encoder, scoring architecture, supervision, and inference construction, supervised training achieves **16.4% EM / 60.2% FR**, versus **20.7% / 65.1%** for LLM-feedback training, averaged over seven merchants with frozen Gemma. | Appendix A.10, Table 3 |
| Transfer remains positive within tested catalogs | Cross-merchant evaluation yields average out-of-domain gains of **18.1 EM points / 13.6 FR points** over Gemma. In the Beauty Bakerie inventory-doubling test, full RPTune’s relative EM decline is **14.7%**, versus **29.5%** for raw Gemma. | Fig. 3; §4.3; Appendix A.8 |
| Reference labels do not exhaust acceptable answers | On **154 audited queries**, independent LLM judges accept reference labels for **64.9%** of queries; **44.8%** have at least two acceptable variants, and best-variant pairwise agreement is **36.4–46.1%**. | Appendix A.3, pp. 16–17 |

The evidence supports consistent within-backbone improvements on the constructed benchmark, with ablations supporting contributions from both feedback-trained curation and post-training. It does not establish real-customer satisfaction, definitive backbone rankings, or effectiveness at marketplace scale.

Some summary numbers are inconsistent: §4.2 says maximum backbone-average EM gains reach 20 points, while Table 2 lists 20.3; the introductory 31.4-point maximum corresponds to a merchant-specific comparison. Table 2 also contradicts a literal reading that latency improves in every merchant setting—its stronger supported claim is improvement in every backbone’s merchant-average latency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Evaluation covers single-turn queries; multi-turn shopping is left to future work (Appendix A.3).
- **Authors’ stated label ambiguity:** Many queries admit several defensible recommendations. The authors caution against interpreting Gemini’s absolute scores as proof of backbone superiority; human verification covers pilots, tied cases, and a sample of remaining queries, rather than every standard query (Appendix A.3).
- **Authors’ stated inventory-test caveat:** Early new-product groups are small. Reference labels and group membership change as inventory returns, although the total query set stays fixed (Appendix A.8).
- **Analyst inference—external validity:** Synthetic queries, consensus-based filtering, and seven small catalogs limit claims about organic customer traffic or larger catalogs. The high-consensus robustness test addresses only one merchant (Table 1; Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—metric validity:** FR measures similarity to one reference, rather than direct satisfaction of query constraints; extra tags or description text are not penalized (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference—optimization interpretation:** The reorganizer uses a surrogate rather than sampling from its own priority distribution. Its empirical benefit is demonstrated, but a formal guarantee for optimizing discrete context construction is not supplied (§3.2).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Hardware and some training settings are provided, but complete curator/post-training configurations, training costs, and public artifact availability are not established in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Do gains persist on actual customer queries?** Evaluation is synthetic and only partly human-verified. Next experiment: test consented customer queries with blinded, multi-answer human judgments.
- **Does feedback training need to match the downstream backbone?** The reorganizer learns from frozen Gemma but transfers to other models. Next experiment: train otherwise identical curators with different feedback backbones and cross-evaluate them.
- **Can the method handle evolving multi-turn preferences?** The authors propose adaptation but do not evaluate it. Next experiment: use shopping dialogues with constraint revisions and measure selection quality and cumulative latency.
- **How much do gains depend on a single reference answer?** The independent audit finds multiple acceptable variants and substantial disagreement. Next experiment: evaluate all methods using human-approved answer sets and direct constraint-satisfaction scores alongside EM.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed tables and component controls support the benchmark gains, but synthetic evaluation and ambiguous labels constrain broader conclusions.

Material missing information includes public code/model/data availability, complete training configurations and costs, main-result uncertainty estimates, fully human-labeled evaluation, and real-user or multi-turn results.
