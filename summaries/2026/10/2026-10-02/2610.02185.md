# Decoding Looped Transformers Better for (Almost) Free

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02185) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185)


## One-sentence takeaway
LoopCD reuses earlier recurrent states for training-free contrastive decoding, improving several reasoning and code benchmarks and matching full-depth multiple-choice accuracy at half depth with 22.5%–48.2% fewer analytic forward FLOPs across six settings (§4; Table A9).

## Problem and motivation
Looped Transformers repeatedly apply shared layers, increasing computation without increasing parameter count. Standard decoding uses only the final state, discarding intermediate predictions. The authors identify these earlier predictions as potentially useful weak references for contrastive decoding, avoiding auxiliary models or modified prompts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Recurrent contrast:** Extrapolate from an earlier completed iteration toward the final prediction: final + ω(final − earlier). The method requires neither training nor extra recurrent iterations (§3).
- **Two guidance spaces:** LoopCD-Logits contrasts logits and adds one coda/head pass; LoopCD-Hidden combines states before those layers, retaining one output pass with negligible vector-operation overhead (§3.1; Appendix D.1).
- **Strength allocation and empirical analysis:** Logit guidance supports fixed strength or token-level adaptation using the top-two probability margin. Experiments examine accuracy, reduced-depth compute, reference selection, and re-ranking mechanisms (§§3.2–5). Hidden guidance uses fixed strength; Huginn requires references after its initial noise-removal phase (Appendix B.4).

The distinctive contribution is contrast across completed recurrent iterations; contrastive decoding itself is prior work (§H).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large mathematical-reasoning gain | Ouro-2.6B-Thinking, full depth, adaptive logits: AIME 2024 pass@1 **61.88% → 73.33%**; zero-shot, estimated from 16 sampled solutions per problem. | Table 1(a); Appendix B.2–B.3 |
| Hidden guidance improves code generation | Huginn, R=32, greedy zero-shot HumanEval base-test pass@1 **22.56% → 31.71%**, against its dedicated baseline. | Table 3(b); Appendix B |
| Multiple-choice means improve | All seven full-depth logit configurations improve their equally weighted seven-benchmark mean; adaptive changes range **+0.29 to +1.59 percentage points**, with individual benchmark regressions. | Table 2 |
| Half depth recovers accuracy | Six settings across Huginn, Parcae, and Looped-Qwen3 match or exceed full-depth seven-benchmark means; Huginn logits at 16 versus 32 iterations gains **+1.02 points**. | Figure 4; Table A8 |
| Compute savings are analytic | Those six settings save **22.5%–48.2%** of full-depth forward FLOPs, including guidance, under **512-token prefill** accounting. | Tables A7–A9 |
| Gains are not universal | Looped-Qwen3 adaptive guidance lowers mean mathematical pass@1 by **1.67 points** while raising mean pass@10 by **3.18 points** across AIME 2024/2025 and OlympiadBench. | Table A6 |
| Re-ranking explains part of the gain | Ouro-1.4B, HellaSwag, ω=0.5: isolated orthogonal logit guidance gains **+1.72 points**, versus **+0.76** for the full update. | §5.2; Figure 8 |

These results establish improvements over matched unguided decoding in the reported settings. They do not establish universal task improvements, measured serving speedups, or superiority over competing decoding methods. The half-depth result concerns multiple-choice suite means, not reduced-depth mathematical or code generation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described sensitivity:** Generation tolerates a narrower strength range than multiple-choice scoring; excessive guidance causes steep declines (§5.3; Appendix G).
- **Author-described architectural dependence:** Huginn’s early hidden states contain harmful initialization noise; coda layers can attenuate hidden guidance (Appendices D.3 and E.1).
- **Analyst inference—selection uncertainty:** Screening sweeps select strengths and references, but a separate held-out tuning split is not specified (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** Confidence intervals or significance tests are not supplied, limiting interpretation of small gains.
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Hidden and logit runs sometimes use different prompts, references, and dedicated baselines; their absolute scores are not always directly comparable (Appendices B.1–B.4).
- **Textual inconsistency:** Appendix D.3 says matched Ouro means consistently favor hidden guidance, but lists 0.10 versus 0.51 for Ouro-1.4B. That comparison is ambiguous.
- No explicit code release or end-to-end latency measurements are supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Does half-depth recovery extend to generation?** Reduced-depth experiments cover only multiple-choice scoring. Next experiment: matched half/full-depth code and sampled mathematics evaluations.
- **Do arithmetic savings translate into latency savings?** FLOPs are theoretical prefill estimates. Next experiment: measure prefill latency, decoding throughput, and memory across batch sizes and context lengths.
- **Can guidance settings transfer without benchmark tuning?** Selected references and strengths vary by architecture and task. Next experiment: tune on separate development tasks and freeze settings for unseen benchmarks.
- **Does higher pass@10 reflect greater trajectory diversity?** Appendix C.2 explicitly says aggregate pass metrics do not measure diversity. Next experiment: measure solution diversity alongside per-problem success distributions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the method and reported results; **medium** for generalization and mechanism interpretations.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, clearly separated tuning data, measured serving performance, reduced-depth generation results, competitive decoding comparisons, release details, and clarification of the inconsistent Ouro hidden/logit comparison.
