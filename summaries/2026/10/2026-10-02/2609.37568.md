# Devils in Question Relay: Source-Conditioned Relay Steering to Mitigate Hallucinations in Audio-visual Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37568) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37568)


## One-sentence takeaway
SECRET steers question-token representations using source-conditioned attention interventions, improving source-specific audio-visual question answering across three models and reducing distractor leakage in captioning (§§3–4, Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Audio-visual large language models can answer from the wrong modality: a visible piano, for example, can induce an unsupported claim about piano music in the audio. The authors call this **source-confused grounding hallucination**. They identify a gap in understanding the internal pathways behind this failure, despite existing decoding and alignment methods (§1).

Their question-only diagnostic suggests that recognizing the requested source and grounding answers in that source are separate problems: Qwen2.5-Omni identifies the required modality reliably, yet still exhibits source-confused answers (§2.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Mechanistic diagnosis:** Attention-path cuts and representation probes support a “question relay”: question states carry required-source evidence and interfering cues. Cutting interference into question states restores correct-answer support more effectively than targeting the generation position (§§2.3–2.4).
- **SECRET intervention:** The model first identifies the required modality from the question. Three branches process the complete input: original, interference-blocked, and required-source-blocked. SECRET adds the token-wise positive-minus-negative contrast to original question states, scaling each contrast to the original state’s L2 norm. Steering occurs during prefill; the remaining layers generate the answer (§3, Eq. 3).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors test source-specific question answering across three backbones and extend evaluation to audio-target and video-target captioning with mismatched inputs (§4). No new benchmark or released model is established by the supplied text.

The results support improved source grounding in the evaluated settings, without establishing elimination of hallucinations or universal improvements in caption fidelity. The pathway experiments support the authors’ relay interpretation, but do not establish that this is the only mechanism. Figure 3’s caption says question cuts outperform generation cuts over “most” layers, whereas the prose says every tested layer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Analysis operates at pathway level; individual attention-head roles and disentangling modality-specific cues remain future work (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation assumes one required modality. Ambiguous predictions trigger random source selection; joint-source questions are not evaluated (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Caption judges and human validators receive reference text rather than raw media. One reference per modality and first-sentence preprocessing constrain what grounding quality is measured (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—selection and uncertainty:** Depths are model-specific and guided partly by CMM representation analyses; a separate depth-selection split is not specified. No confidence intervals or significance tests are supplied (Appendices C–D).
- **Comparison limits:** Qwen3 results include MAD but omit VCD and AVCD. Captioning comparisons use intervention variants rather than the main decoding baselines (Tables 1–2).
- **Practical validity:** Three branches add prefill computation, but latency and memory overhead are unreported (§3).

## Open research questions
- **Can SECRET handle ambiguous or jointly required sources?** Random fallback leaves this unresolved. Test explicit single-source, ambiguous, and joint-source questions with an abstention or joint-source policy.
- **Does steering preserve useful cross-modal integration?** Captioning excludes aligned inputs, and target-reference gains are uneven. Compare aligned and mismatched inputs on both single-source and complementary-source tasks.
- **Can depth selection transfer without benchmark-specific analysis?** Optimal depths differ by backbone. Select depth on held-out calibration data and evaluate transfer to unseen tasks and models.
- **Which heads carry harmful interference?** The authors leave finer-grained mechanisms open. Test selective head interventions against full-path SECRET, measuring grounding, latency, and memory.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Numerical outcomes and the algorithm are clearly documented; broader mechanistic and deployment claims remain less established.

Missing material information includes code/release availability, diagnostic sample counts, human-validation sample size and annotator details, uncertainty estimates, detailed baseline hyperparameters, a separate depth-selection split, and measured inference overhead.
