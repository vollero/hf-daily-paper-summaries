# Adaptive Reward Routing: Dynamic Multi-Reward Optimization for Joint Audio-Video Diffusion via Forward-Process RL

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37200) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37200)


## One-sentence takeaway
Adaptive Reward Routing combines dynamic token/layer routing with preference-anchored, branch-specific reward balancing, improving joint audio-video generation on two LTX backbones in the reported JavisBench experiments.

## Problem and motivation
Joint audio-video generation must balance visual quality, audio quality, semantic alignment, and synchronization. The authors argue that fixed update routes become stale as cross-modal functions change during training, while fixed reward weights cannot resolve evolving conflicts; gradient-only weighting can also suppress important objectives such as synchronization (Introduction; Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive update localization:** Detached, pre-gate cross-attention response norms determine token loss weights and layer-specific scaling of cross-modal key/value gradients. The proxy uses responses collected during sampling, avoiding repeated intervention-based model evaluations (Sec. 4.2).
- **Preference-preserving reward coordination:** Reward gradients are measured within their responsible modality branches. After warm-up, smoothed MARBLE coefficients adjust predefined reward weights while retaining a prior-derived floor and preserving each branch’s total weight (Sec. 4.3).
- **Combined framework and validation:** Both components operate within DiffusionNFT forward-process RL. Separate ablation chains and direct cross-modal interventions test component benefits and proxy fidelity (Secs. 4.4, 5.3–5.4).

The results support improvements under the reported automated benchmark protocol: 10,140 prompts, four-second outputs, 24 FPS, and 16-kHz audio (Sec. 5.1). Retrained baselines share data, LoRA, and optimization budgets (Sec. 5.2). They do not establish human preference gains or universal architectural generality. Proxy interventions measure prediction sensitivity, rather than directly proving that every routed update improves perceptual quality.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** No unified reward captures quality, semantics, and synchronization; the method depends on multiple imperfect reward signals (Sec. 6).
- **Author-stated:** Validation centers on DiffusionNFT. Extension to other diffusion objectives is proposed, but supporting experiments are not supplied (Sec. 6).
- **Author-stated:** Routing requires identifiable modality tokens and accessible directional interactions; inseparable representations remain future work (Sec. 6).
- **Analyst observation:** The conclusion claims validation on JavisDiT++, but the supplied experimental setup and tables report only LTX-2 and LTX-2.3. Single-stream validation therefore cannot be assessed.
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation uses one benchmark and automated metrics; CLAP and DeSync also provide training rewards. Independent human judgments would help assess transfer beyond optimized evaluators (Sec. 5.1).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Exact training budgets, LoRA configuration, routing window, reward priors, adaptation hyperparameters, compute overhead, and released code/checkpoints are not specified in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does the method improve human-perceived quality and synchrony?** Reported evidence is automated. Next experiment: blinded audiovisual preference comparisons against matched-budget OmniNFT.
- **Does routing transfer to unified architectures?** JavisDiT++ validation is claimed without presented results. Next experiment: report matched-budget comparisons and routing ablations on that backbone.
- **How robust is preference preservation?** The formula retains prior influence, but systematic preference-setting sweeps are absent. Next experiment: vary synchronization versus quality priors and measure objective trade-offs.
- **What is the benefit relative to computational cost?** Gradient probing frequency and overhead are unreported. Next experiment: compare refresh schedules using both benchmark performance and measured training time.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and LTX benchmark results are explicit, but material gaps include JavisDiT++ evidence, human evaluation, full training and hyperparameter details, computational costs, intervention sample counts, and asset availability.
