# Joint and Cross-Modal Video-Audio Generation and Editing: A Unified Formulation and Design Taxonomy

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34381) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34381)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey unifies audio-video generation and editing around semantic and temporal correspondence, introducing a five-axis design taxonomy and an editing taxonomy while identifying gaps in documented methods and evaluation (§§2, 4–5, 13).

## Problem and motivation
High-quality video and audio can still form an incoherent pair when sounds disagree with visible sources or event timing. The authors argue that existing overviews separate the modalities or focus on understanding, leaving coupled generation—and especially edits that must propagate across streams—insufficiently organized (§1).

The central challenge is maintaining cross-modal correspondence while preserving each stream’s quality; editing additionally requires preserving untouched content (§2).

## Approach and contributions
This is a conceptual survey, with no new trained generator or experimental performance comparison reported.

- **Unified formulation:** Joint generation, generation of one modality from another, and coupled editing are expressed over audio-visual pairs and their conditional distributions. Editing adds instruction fidelity and preservation constraints (§2; Figures 1–2; Table 3).
- **Design taxonomy:** Five complementary axes describe generation strategy, audio representation, video representation, alignment enforcement, and pretrained-weight reuse (§4; Table 1).
- **Editing taxonomy:** Nine categories contain 28 edit types, annotated with modality coupling, representative operations, and use cases. The authors claim this is the first systematic taxonomy of coupled audio-visual editing; that priority claim is not independently established here (§1; Table 4).
- **Research synthesis:** Method, dataset, benchmark, metric, and artifact inventories expose underdeveloped tasks and evaluation gaps (§§5–13; Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** The survey excludes purely unimodal generation and nongenerative audio-visual understanding. Fully closed commercial systems are excluded from the design taxonomy, although their capabilities appear separately. Coverage reflects literature through mid-2026; preprint details and empty taxonomy cells may change. Classification sometimes requires judgment, and the axes are complementary rather than independent (Limitations; §4).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Absence from the surveyed taxonomy supports a coverage-bounded research gap, not proof that a capability does not exist.
- Editing membership is ambiguous: Problem 3 specifies an edited pair, but Table 6 lists only audio output for AV-Edit and only video output for EdiDub and EditYourself. Table 4 also includes single-stream operations. This complicates claims specifically about fully coupled editing.
- No explicit literature-search protocol or completeness assessment is supplied, limiting confidence in exhaustive coverage.
- Table 16 documents incomplete and unavailable releases; its July 2026 checks establish historical availability only.

## Open research questions

- **How should coupled editing be evaluated consistently?** §10.2 reports missing shared supervision and benchmarks. A plausible next experiment is a common source–instruction–target-pair test set measuring edit success, untouched-content preservation, and correspondence together.
- **Can alignment persist over minutes?** §§8.5 and 13 identify drift and evaluation horizons shorter than intended applications. Test streaming and extended-window methods on identical progressively longer clips, tracking event offsets and identity/scene consistency.
- **Does discrete-token coupling offer useful trade-offs?** §4 identifies an unoccupied joint-generation category. Compare discrete-token and continuous-latent models under matched data and compute, evaluating fidelity, synchronization, and sampling cost.
- **Can models distinguish synchronized sound from physically appropriate sound?** §13 separates timing from material/force plausibility. Test controlled visual variations in material and impact force, assessing both temporal alignment and acoustic appropriateness.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The survey’s formulations and contributions are explicit, but exhaustive coverage and some classifications remain uncertain.

Material information unavailable includes an explicit search/selection protocol, original experiments or ablations, numerical benchmark results and uncertainty estimates, detailed human-evaluation protocols, and a demonstrated new model, dataset, or benchmark release.
