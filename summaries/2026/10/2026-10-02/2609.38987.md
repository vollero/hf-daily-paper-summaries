# Smaller Models, Better Rejects: Preference Distillation Scaling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38987) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38987)


## One-sentence takeaway
In controlled preference-distillation experiments with Qwen2.5 students, smaller frozen reject generators consistently improve held-out code avg@4 over self-generated rejects at lower estimated generation cost, while structural and likelihood-selection interventions suggest why these negatives help.

## Problem and motivation
Preference distillation commonly pairs a verified teacher response with the student’s own incorrect response. This makes reject generation increasingly expensive as students grow and assumes that the student’s failures provide the most useful negatives. The authors identify reject-source selection as an independent data-design problem: a verified error need not provide useful contrast against the DPO reference (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled scaling study:** Train students through sequence-level knowledge distillation (SeqKD), then DPO, holding prompts, chosen responses, initialization, and training protocol fixed while changing reject construction. Compare smaller frozen generators with vanilla Self and post-SeqKD Self on code and mathematics (§§2, 4.1).
- **Reject-utility theory:** Derive a finite-horizon utility bound in a fixed-feature, locally linearized DPO model. Utility depends on transfer toward the initial task-reward gradient, offset by adverse transfer and curvature/drift terms (§3; Appendix F).
- **Construction interventions:** Test randomized source mixtures, prompt reassignment and lexical permutation, and reference-likelihood reselection. The authors infer a design principle: preserve task structure while reducing coupling to the reference (§§4.2–4.4).

“Base” here means a frozen vanilla source; the listed generators include instruction-tuned models (§4.1; Appendix C.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Smaller rejects consistently beat Self on held-out code avg@4 | All 18 smaller-Qwen configurations across 7B–72B students exceed both Self constructions on 5,000 KoDCode problems; avg@4 averages four stochastic samples | Figure 1; Table 3; Appendix A |
| Example code improvement | For the 14B student, 3B rejects achieve 62.630% versus 59.575% with vanilla Self on KoDCode avg@4 | Table 9 |
| Math benefits have exceptions | For the 72B student, 32B rejects reach 92.7% MATH-500 avg@4 versus 91.8% for either Self; 0.5B rejects reach only 91.6% | Table 5 |
| Generation cost decreases | Selected-code-response parameter-token total-cost proxy is 0.9%–50.1% of same-scale vanilla Self, equivalent to 2.0×–108.3× reductions; this is not measured FLOPs | Table 2; Appendix B |
| Source composition changes performance | For a 14B student, increasing Llama-1B reject share from 0% to 100% raises KoDCode avg@4 from 59.730% to 63.280%, with ordered intermediate endpoints | Table 6 |
| Utility survives disrupted correspondence and syntax | At 14B, KoDCode avg@4 is 60.920% with gibberish, 61.970% with reassigned real rejects, and 61.650% with lexical permutation; reassigned and lexical arms beat gibberish at every tested scale | Tables 7–8 |
| Lower reference likelihood helps within candidate banks | On a shared 20,136-prompt subset for the 14B student, 1.5B Repelled selection reaches 63.135% KoDCode avg@4 versus 61.880% Attracted; Repelled beats Native for all five sources | Appendix D.4; Table 11 |
| External gains are less uniform | For the 32B student, external weighted code pass@1 is 52.8% with 0.5B rejects versus 53.9% with vanilla Self, although 7B rejects reach 55.4% | Table 4 |

The controlled replacements support a reject-construction effect within this pipeline. Reselection strengthens the evidence beyond cross-source likelihood correlations, but does not prove likelihood is the sole mechanism. The strongest universal result concerns held-out code avg@4; mathematics, external benchmarks, and other metrics show exceptions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical boundary:** The analysis is exact for fixed features, approximates the neural network locally, and becomes informative only while the accumulated-step-size correction remains sufficiently small. Its transfer coordinates are not directly observable (§3.3; Appendices F.2–F.3).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Learners are Qwen2.5 models, tasks are automatically verifiable code and mathematics, and the mechanism interventions focus on code. Llama reject sources broaden generator coverage, not learner-family coverage (§4; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—selection scope:** Reselection retains prompts satisfying matched execution, partial correctness, and length criteria across all sources. Its results therefore concern that filtered population (Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and reproducibility:** Training-seed repetitions and endpoint confidence intervals are not reported. Exact reselection strength, complete decoding/filtering procedures, math-specific hyperparameters, and release availability are not established.
- **Textual overstatement:** The conclusion says gibberish adds no residual benefit, but the 7B control improves over Continued-SFT; §4.3 acknowledges this exception (Table 7).

## Open research questions
- **Does the advantage transfer to other learner families?** Only Qwen2.5 students are tested. Repeat the controlled source sweep with a different student family and the same verified pairs.
- **Do the proposed mechanisms explain math gains?** Structural and likelihood interventions are code-focused. Run matched reference-likelihood reselection on verified mathematical rejects.
- **How reliable are small selection gains?** Endpoint variability is unavailable. Repeat Native/Repelled comparisons across training seeds with paired evaluation uncertainty.
- **What is the full cost–utility trade-off?** The cost proxy covers selected responses, while reselection generates candidate banks. Measure end-to-end generation, verification, scoring, and training cost under matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported code scaling pattern; **medium** in the mechanism interpretation and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes training variability, endpoint uncertainty, exact reselection strength, complete generation/filtering details, math training settings, end-to-end construction costs, and availability of released code, models, or preference datasets.
