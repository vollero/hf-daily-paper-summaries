# OneStreamer: Unifying Perception, Memory, and Proactive Response in Streaming Video Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01762) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01762)


## One-sentence takeaway
OneStreamer combines generated factual caption memory with selective response-state supervision, achieving the highest aggregate scores among compared models on eight streaming-video benchmarks while retaining a recent visual window (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Streaming assistants must preserve observations before knowing which future questions will need them, while responding as soon as sufficient evidence arrives. The authors identify two interacting gaps: historical visual tokens can compete with current perception for context capacity, and dense response-state supervision can overemphasize repeated waiting labels. Existing reasoning traces also need not provide reusable, time-grounded factual memory (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Proactive Hierarchical Caption Memory (PHCM):** A shared causal generation process writes time-aligned local observations and completed-event summaries. These records remain alongside recent visual frames after their source frames are discarded, without retrieving historical visual features (§3.1–3.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Proactive State Transition Learning (PSTL):** Transition-group sampling preserves every output-initiating state token and representative examples of state changes and persistence. Unselected states remain in the sequence but contribute no state loss; all output text retains supervision (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Streaming data construction:** Caption targets are released after their supporting intervals, while QA response times are calibrated against available evidence. The resulting OneStreamer-1M collection combines synthesized and curated existing data, listing 1,160,004 records (Table 6).
- **Unified trained model:** The authors fine-tune Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct with these mechanisms and data, freezing the vision encoder while training the projector and LLM (§5; Appendix C.1).

The controlled memory and supervision ablations support specific benefits of caption retention and transition-aware selection. Aggregate benchmark leadership does not establish superiority on every subtask: for example, OneStreamer’s OVO-Timing CRR F1 trails Qwen3-VL (Table 14). Efficiency findings concern individual clips and specified hardware, rather than general deployment guarantees.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed failure:** The model mistakes opening a printer for closing it and does not correct the answer when later frames clarify the state (Appendix F.3; Fig. 12).
- **Author-acknowledged timing limitation:** Calibration does not always identify the earliest answerable moment; manual inspection supports evidence sufficiency but has no reported sample size or agreement statistics (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—long-stream scaling:** Caption history accumulates, but no pruning policy or indefinite-stream evaluation is described. The reported throughput experiment therefore does not establish sustained operation beyond its tested clip (Appendix A.2; Table 19).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Results mix previously published scores with checkpoint re-evaluations. Re-evaluations select the better of two whole-benchmark configurations, so Table 1 is not a single uniformly configured comparison (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—attribution and reliability:** CQTC jointly changes content verification and timing calibration, preventing attribution to either alone. Tables report no confidence intervals or multi-seed variability (Table 20; Appendix C.1).

The authors report source-video-level decontamination against every evaluated test split, including non-overlapping clips from shared videos (Appendix B.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can erroneous records and responses be corrected reliably?** Fig. 12 shows an uncorrected state-change error. A next experiment could introduce correction supervision and measure revision accuracy and downstream QA after misleading early observations.
- **How does memory scale over much longer streams?** Accumulated text has no specified retention policy. Test multi-hour streams with pruning or summary consolidation, measuring historical QA, context growth, and update latency.
- **Which data refinement drives timing gains?** CQTC combines verification and calibration. Independently ablate each, using human-annotated evidence-sufficiency times to assess correctness and response delay.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark findings; deployment generalization remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes confirmed asset releases and licenses, statistical uncertainty, human verification sample sizes and agreement, synthetic-data acceptance rates, separate verification-versus-calibration ablations, and memory-management behavior once accumulated text reaches the context limit.
