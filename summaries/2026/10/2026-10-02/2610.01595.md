# Before It Fades: Reinforcing Temporal Representations at Inference Time in VideoLLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01595) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01595)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that frame-order-sensitive representations peak before the final layers of VideoLLMs, and that training-free Temporal Activation Injection improves temporal reasoning by reinforcing these intermediate signals.

## Problem and motivation
VideoLLMs can give identical answers to a video and its reversal even when the correct temporal answer should change. The authors identify a gap in prior training and output-level correction methods: they address failures without locating where temporal representations emerge and subsequently weaken inside the decoder (§§1–2).

Their central hypothesis is that models already compute useful temporal information but fail to preserve it through later layers.

## Approach and contributions
1. **A representation diagnostic.** Define the temporal divergence vector as the difference between last-token hidden states for forward and reversed videos. Its norm, normalized by the forward-state norm, gives a layer-wise profile. Question controls and attention knockout test its temporal specificity and functional importance (§3, Fig. 2).
2. **Temporal Activation Injection (TAI).** Extract an input-specific divergence vector at a model’s profile peak, then add it to subsequent last-token states. Injection weights follow the precomputed normalized profile; a global coefficient controls strength. The reversed pass exits at the source layer, and model weights remain unchanged (§4, Fig. 3).
3. **Empirical validation.** Evaluate temporal accuracy, general video understanding, steering direction, injection schedules, configuration sensitivity, and compatibility with ArrowRL-trained weights (§5; Appendices B–L). Code availability is stated in the abstract.

The source layer and schedule are determined from a **model-level mean profile**; the steering vector is extracted separately for each input (§4).

| Peak regions matter for predictions | Blocking last-token attention produces the largest ground-truth-probability drops around the divergence peak; implementation uses model-specific sliding layer windows. Exact drops are unavailable. | §3.3, Fig. 2b; Appendix I |
| TAI improves temporal accuracy across the main evaluation | Authors report gains over unmodified models on TempCompass, TVBench, and AoTBench for all three main models, using uniform 16-frame inputs and accuracy in %. Table values are absent. | §§5.1–5.2; Tables 1–2 |

| Reversing steering direction selectively harms temporal categories | On Qwen2.5-VL-7B/TempCompass, Anti-TAI reportedly drops Attribute Change by “36.3%,” Order by “27.4%,” and Direction by “12.6%”; Action and Speed remain within “1%” of baseline. | Profile estimation is stable with few pairs | On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Pearson correlation with the 50-pair reference exceeds 0.95 using 5 pairs; cross-domain estimation retains source layer L20 in all 18 additional runs. | Appendix B, Fig. B, Table A |

These findings support useful, question-conditioned frame-order information at intermediate layers and an effective intervention in the reported settings. They do not establish universal temporal competence or complete preservation of temporal information. Crucially, the supplied retrieval contains table captions but no table bodies, so absolute accuracies, gain sizes, and method rankings cannot be independently assessed.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The work addresses sequential frame order; spatial reasoning, object interactions, and audio-visual correspondence fall outside its scope (§7).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic specificity:** Attention knockout blocks access to both visual and prompt information. Alignment with divergence peaks supports functional relevance but does not isolate a purely temporal causal pathway (§3.3; Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference—reversal blind spots:** Reversal-invariant concatenated AoTBench inputs receive near-zero steering despite involving temporal judgments. Low divergence therefore need not imply absence of temporal reasoning demands (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference—generation coverage:** Free-form evidence is qualitative and limited to a sunrise/sunset example; it does not establish reliable long-response behavior (Appendix F).
- **Comparison limits:** VTD and SEASON were excluded from main comparisons after unsuccessful reproductions under mismatched frame settings and implementation ambiguities; conclusions about superiority should remain confined to the reproduced protocol (Appendix K).
- **Missing reproducibility evidence:** Table bodies, exact significance results, and the selection procedure for model-specific β values are unavailable. Reported prompt and sampling robustness primarily concerns Qwen2.5-VL-7B on TempCompass (Appendices D, I).

## Open research questions
- **Can contrastive interventions help reversal-invariant temporal tasks?** Appendix H exposes inputs with temporal questions but little global reversal divergence. Next experiment: compare segment-level reversal or event permutation against whole-video reversal.
- **Does TAI improve sustained free-form temporal explanations?** Appendix F supplies only qualitative evidence. Next experiment: quantitatively compare first-token and every-token injection on temporally grounded long-answer questions, measuring correctness and fluency.
- **How robust is a fixed profile under broader distribution shifts?** Cross-domain stability is tested on one backbone and the supplied benchmarks. Next experiment: estimate and transfer profiles across longer videos, different prompts, and frame densities for every evaluated model.
- **Does shrinking normalized divergence mean temporal information is lost or re-encoded?** The diagnostic measures hidden-state distance rather than recoverability directly. Next experiment: compare layer-wise temporal decoding with selective perturbations that preserve non-temporal information.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and qualitative findings are clearly described, but quantitative assessment is constrained by missing table bodies and unavailable plotted values.

Material gaps include absolute benchmark scores and gains, exact baseline margins, statistical test outputs, timing and memory measurements, β-selection details, and quantitative free-form generation results.
