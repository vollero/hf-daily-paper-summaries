# Prefill-Free Cross-Family KV Cache Transfer for Heterogeneous Multi-Agent LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32259) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32259)


## One-sentence takeaway
HeteroFold transfers KV caches between frozen LLM families without receiver prefill, outperforming evaluated cache-transfer baselines on long-context QA and reducing transfer latency, while often retaining substantial quality gaps versus native receiver prefill (Tables 1, 4).

## Problem and motivation
Text communication makes each receiving agent recompute a KV cache for context already processed by the sender. Cross-family reuse must reconcile different tokenizers, layer counts, head structures, and feature distributions. The authors identify two gaps: prior prefill-free methods were evaluated with compatible tokenization, and low cache-reconstruction error does not necessarily preserve receiver attention or outputs (§§1–2; Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Token and layer alignment:** Match tokens at shared character-end boundaries, reuse preceding matched states for unmatched receiver boundaries, and concatenate three sender layers around proportionally matched depths. Flattened heads permit cross-head mapping (§3.1).
- **Receiver-compatible affine maps:** “Recolor” uses whitening, paired-feature Procrustes alignment, and receiver mean/covariance restoration to initialize separate key and value maps (§3.2).
- **Receiver-aware calibration:** Train rank-16 corrections against native receiver attention and projected outputs using prompt question positions, without answers or generated continuations. Fold corrections into fixed affine maps for inference; both LLMs remain frozen (§3.3; Appendix B.2).

The authors claim the first receiver-prefill-free cross-family method with explicit tokenizer alignment; that priority claim cannot be independently established from the supplied text (§1). Output-aware calibration adapts an earlier quantization technique rather than originating the general idea (§3.3).

These results support effective approximate cache reuse under the tested conditions. They establish neither lossless communication nor universal equivalence to native prefill. Reported latency excludes sender prefill, offline fitting, model loading, and subsequent decoding; it measures hand-off through the first receiver token (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged measurement boundaries:** Sender processing must be added when context is not already cached. Incoming payload copies mean cache transfer does not always reduce peak receiver memory versus native prefill (Appendices E.3–E.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Cross-family tests cover three language-model configurations. Detailed component ablations and calibration sensitivity each cover one transfer direction; broader architectural and corpus robustness remains unestablished (Table 3; Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—multi-agent scope:** HIDDENBENCH uses 65 tasks, and Qwen-to-Qwen messages remain text-based for every method. It therefore evaluates a mixed communication system, not exclusively cache-based communication (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—decoding validity:** Stability analysis teacher-forces native traces in two directions. Shared preceding tokens prevent this experiment from establishing stability under diverging, freely generated trajectories (Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Baselines are author reimplementations; statistical uncertainty for quality results is not reported. Small numerical differences should not be interpreted as established superiority (Appendix A.3; Tables 1–3).

## Open research questions
- **Can remaining native-prefill quality gaps be closed?** Table 1 and reconstruction results show incomplete preservation. Next experiment: compare richer mappings and calibration objectives at matched storage and latency.
- **Does calibration transfer across languages and domains?** Corpus sensitivity is tested only in one direction with Open-R1 and HotpotQA (Appendix B.3). Next experiment: evaluate held-out multilingual and specialized-domain prompts with fixed maps.
- **Does transfer remain stable during long, freely generated conversations?** Teacher-forced decoding and one multi-agent benchmark leave this open. Next experiment: track task accuracy, message fidelity, and divergence across increasing free-running communication rounds.
- **When do full-system savings outweigh construction and sender costs?** Timing excludes several costs (§5.1; Appendix E). Next experiment: measure end-to-end latency and memory across reuse counts, hardware interconnects, and workloads requiring fresh sender prefill.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and measured results; **medium** in broader deployment conclusions.

Unavailable information includes statistical uncertainty for quality metrics, a usable repository URL or confirmed release, freely generated decoding-divergence measurements, and end-to-end multi-agent latency including sender processing and subsequent decoding.
