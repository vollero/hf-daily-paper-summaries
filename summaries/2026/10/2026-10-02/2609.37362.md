# Pretrain Once, Route Anywhere: Towards a Foundation Model for LLM Routing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37362) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37362)


## One-sentence takeaway
RouteFM uses episodic pretraining to route among anonymous models with frozen parameters, achieving mean selected-model quality of 0.7323 versus 0.7100 for the strongest baseline on unseen MMR-Bench with eight behavioral observations per candidate (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
LLM routing selects a candidate model for each query to balance response quality and inference cost. The authors identify **local fitting** as a reuse bottleneck: routers trained for a particular workload and model pool may require new supervision and optimization when either changes. They propose learning a shared routing capability that adapts through behavioral context instead (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Anonymous behavioral profiling:** RouteFM represents each candidate using historical query embeddings, observed quality, and relative cost, without model identities. Learned latent tokens summarize this evidence into a capability profile (§4.2).
- **Target-conditioned comparison:** Parallel attention branches retrieve evidence from the profile and raw observations; a permutation-equivariant Transformer compares candidates and predicts quality and relative cost (§4.3).
- **Reusable episodic pretraining:** Training varies tasks, candidate pools, ordering, and context budgets, combining regression, pairwise ranking, and routing regret. Deployment uses frozen parameters and context-based adaptation (§§4.4–4.5).
- **Transfer evaluation and assets:** The paper tests multimodal transfer, candidate-pool expansion, and reduced context budgets, and reports released code and checkpoints (§5; Appendix F).

The principal contribution is the combination of heterogeneous routing pretraining and behavioral adaptation; the supplied text also acknowledges prior methods supporting unseen-model or few-shot routing (§2).

## Evidence and main results
“Quality points” below denote absolute differences multiplied by 100 in the mean selected-model quality metric.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported in-domain quality at every tested budget | RouterEval, four candidates, eight observations each: 0.6192 versus 0.6108 for Embedding-Ridge, a 0.84-point advantage | Table 1; Appendix C.2 |
| Strongest transfer advantage with sparse context | Unseen MMR-Bench, nine candidates, eight observations each, dataset-wise five-fold evaluation: 0.7323 versus EmbedLLM’s 0.7100, a 2.23-point advantage | Table 1; Appendix C.3 |
| Transfer advantage narrows with more evidence | MMR-Bench using 40% context and 60% targets: 0.7614 versus UniRoute’s 0.7607, a 0.07-point advantage | Table 1 |
| Joint candidate comparison helps | MMR-Bench at eight observations: removing the candidate-pool Transformer reduces quality from 0.7323 to 0.7190 | Table 2(a) |
| Pretraining helps beyond target-domain training | Separate support-controlled protocol: frozen RouteFM scores 0.7523 versus 0.7415 for training from scratch at eight observations; the latter receives 20% labeled support | Table 2(b); Appendix C.4 |
| Few-shot candidate incorporation works in the tested pool | Authors report surpassing refitted baselines with eight new-candidate observations and exceeding incumbent-only routing with 16; incumbents receive 64 observations each | Figure 3(a); Appendix C.5 |
| Less context can recover baseline quality | RouteFM matches full-context Weighted kNN, MLP, and EmbedLLM using respectively 1%, 3%, and 26% of the available context in a separate dense-budget protocol | Figure 3(c); Appendix C.7 |
| Pretraining composition materially affects transfer | Removing LLMRouterBench reduces MMR-Bench quality at eight observations from 0.7323 to 0.6914 under the same training budget | Table A3 |

These results support frozen adaptation within the evaluated environments, especially with sparse observations. They do not establish universal transfer or reliable zero-observation incorporation. Table 2(b) uses a different protocol and should be compared only within its block. The main tables provide no uncertainty intervals or significance tests.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Broader environment coverage, richer system context, and continuously changing deployments remain future directions (Appendix G). Zero- and one-observation incorporation are explicitly treated as stress tests (Appendix C.5).
- **Cost interpretation:** Costs mix heterogeneous proxies, including parameter count, and missing costs become zero before normalization. Predictions represent episode-relative cost, not calibrated money or latency (Appendix A.8).
- **Analyst inference—transfer coverage:** The unseen multimodal evaluation covers one benchmark containing seven datasets and nine candidates; broader “route anywhere” generalization remains unestablished (Appendix C.3).
- **Comparison caveat:** ICL-Router uses its original representation pipeline and receives text without images on MMR-Bench, unlike the shared representations used by other baselines (Appendix D).
- **Efficiency boundary:** Timing excludes embedding extraction, candidate answer generation, disk I/O, and checkpoint loading; it does not measure total deployment cost (Appendix C.6).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Reported ablations do not isolate the profile branch, regret objective, or curriculum, leaving their individual contributions unresolved (§5.3; Appendix E).

## Open research questions
- **How broadly does pretraining transfer?** Broader coverage remains future work. Next experiment: hold out entire model families and additional modalities while varying pretraining diversity (Appendix G).
- **How robust is behavioral adaptation to noisy evidence?** The observation encoder uses quality and cost without an uncertainty feature. Next experiment: corrupt context labels or introduce systematically missing observations and measure routed quality (Appendix A.2).
- **Can relative-cost routing meet real deployment budgets?** Absolute monetary and latency calibration is not established. Next experiment: evaluate measured serving costs and budget violations across operating conditions (Appendix A.8).
- **Does frozen adaptation survive ongoing drift?** Evaluation uses static splits. Next experiment: sequentially change query distributions and candidate behavior, comparing context-update policies (Appendix G).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the method description and tabulated results; **medium** for broad deployment generalization.

Material missing information includes main-result uncertainty estimates, exact numerical latency results, pretraining hardware and elapsed compute, isolated loss/curriculum ablations, and evidence from continuously changing deployments. Code and checkpoint availability are reported by the paper but were not independently verified.
