# Honeycomb: Constant-Size Scene Memory Representation for Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37690) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37690)


## One-sentence takeaway
Honeycomb combines fixed-size, factorized scene features with recurrent memory updates to achieve the best novel-view and revisit metrics among the evaluated baselines, while leaving total-system memory scaling unresolved (Tables 2–4; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
Camera-controlled video generators can forget scene layout and appearance once earlier observations leave their temporal context. Existing spatial memories retain RGB observations or latent points, but their storage grows during generation. The authors address how to preserve information for revisits within a fixed-size feature representation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **HexMemory:** Three spatial and three spatiotemporal feature planes represent scene content over world coordinates and observation write time. This adapts the existing HexPlane factorization to shared, feed-forward construction and updating across scenes (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Recurrent writer:** New chunk latents are lifted into world space using estimated depth and camera parameters, then splatted onto the planes. Expanded bounds trigger resampling of existing planes; confidence-weighted pooling and learned residual corrections fuse old and new features (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Memory-conditioned generation:** A reader reconstructs target-view latents using projected points, which condition a pretrained video diffusion transformer through a ControlNet-style branch. Evaluations examine generation, revisits, storage–quality trade-offs, and write cost (§§3.5–4.4).

These results support strong performance on the reported benchmarks and a favorable feature-storage/write-cost trade-off. They establish fixed dimensions for HexMemory’s feature planes, but do not establish bounded total-system storage or indefinitely stable memory. Honeycomb also does not lead every individual WorldScore component (Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Explicit method constraint:** Expanding spatial and temporal bounds makes the fixed grids coarser (§3.4). The paper does not quantify the eventual capacity limit.
- **Analyst inference—storage scope:** Readout still requires projected 3D points and source write times, while Appendix A describes joint decoding of accumulated latents. Storage and lifetime of these supporting structures are insufficiently specified to verify constant total memory.
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Baselines use their default settings, and no reported ablations isolate memory conditioning, temporal planes, warping, or residual fusion. Aggregate improvements therefore cannot be attributed solely to HexMemory (Table 2; §4.4).
- **Analyst inference—long-horizon validity:** The retention probe uses one sequence and ground-truth writes; it does not establish resistance to accumulated errors from generated observations (Appendix C).
- **Reporting gaps:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated-run results are reported. Writer timing hardware and timing boundaries are unspecified; H200 GPUs are identified for training (§4.1; Table 4).

## Open research questions
- **When does fixed memory lose useful detail?** Bounds expansion reduces resolution, but the failure horizon is unmeasured. Extend rollouts across increasing spatial coverage and repeated revisits, measuring fidelity against coverage and elapsed writes.
- **Is total inference storage bounded?** Supporting geometry and accumulated latents are not fully accounted for. Profile peak memory by component across progressively longer rollouts.
- **Can memory preserve evolving objects?** Writes include dynamic objects, but reported evaluations do not isolate persistent object-state evolution. Test controlled motion, occlusion, and later revisits with state-specific accuracy measures.
- **Which update components matter most?** Existing ablations compare resolution and writer strategies. Remove temporal planes, residual correction, and confidence weighting separately under matched storage and training.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported benchmark results; **medium** in broader efficiency and long-horizon conclusions.

Material missing information includes total memory accounting, supporting-point retention policy, exact quantitative rollout lengths and trajectory distributions, uncertainty estimates, writer timing protocol, complete memory-model training settings, component ablations, and a usable code URL.
