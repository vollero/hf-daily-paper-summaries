# Scaling and Distilling Text Embeddings for Better Diffusibility

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01016) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01016)


## One-sentence takeaway
Distilling T5Gemma-2’s decoded soft probabilities into a student encoder improves continuous diffusion text generation, reaching OpenWebText generative perplexity of 17.8 ± 0.1 at real-text-level unigram entropy, while reducing discriminative performance (Tables 1, 4).

## Problem and motivation
Continuous diffusion language models generate text embeddings and decode them into tokens, making embedding geometry central to generation quality. The authors identify a gap: previous approaches either changed embeddings and diffusion jointly or used older encoders, obscuring the embedding space’s contribution (§1).

Stronger embeddings improve generation, but plausible alternative tokens can remain widely separated. The authors argue that imperfect sampling can then end far from any candidate embedding, causing uncertain or erroneous decoding (§§3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Embedding comparison with a fixed diffusion recipe:** Replace ELF’s frozen encoder while retaining its diffusion configuration, apart from vocabulary-dependent output heads. Compare T5-family encoders and ModernBERT; “scaling” includes pretraining data, compute, and recipe rather than parameter count alone (§§2.1, 3.1).
- **A diagnostic for diffusibility:** Measure generated embeddings’ distance to re-encoded candidate words and relate invalid embeddings to uncertain decoding (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Decoder-probability distillation:** Initialize a student from selected teacher encoder layers and train it through the frozen teacher decoder to match soft token distributions using KL divergence. Then train a separate ELF model on the frozen student embeddings (§4.1; Appendix B.2).
- **Mechanism and trade-off evidence:** Objective and depth ablations support soft-label distillation; geometry measurements show closer alternatives under corrupted context, alongside weaker downstream probes (Appendices A.3–A.4).

## Evidence and main results
Main generation results use GPT-2-Large scoring after retokenization. Selected settings are reevaluated on three seeds with 1,024 samples each; selection minimizes Gen. PPL subject to reaching real-text unigram entropy, with exceptions explicitly flagged (Appendix B.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger encoder family improves generation | Same ELF-B on a 25% OWT subset with 512-token sequences: T5Gemma-2 has approximately 40% lower Gen. PPL than T5-small at matched entropy. | §3.1; Fig. 2 |
| Student improves medium-scale OWT generation | OWT-1024: student ELF-M **17.8 ± 0.1 PPL, 5.45 ± 0.01 entropy**; teacher **19.3 ± 0.2, 5.44 ± 0.00**; GPT-2-M **20.8 ± 0.3, 5.45 ± 0.00**. Real text: **15.4 ± 0.3, 5.43 ± 0.01**. Teacher uses NFE 256; student NFE 512. | Table 1; Appendix B.3 |
| Base-scale student improves quality and reaches the entropy target | OWT-1024: student ELF-B **31.2 ± 0.2 PPL, 5.44 ± 0.00 entropy** versus teacher **38.5 ± 0.5, 5.41 ± 0.01**, which falls below the target. Selected NFEs are 256 and 64, respectively. | Table 1; Appendix B.3 |
| Improvement transfers to LM1B generation | ELF-B, 128-token training sequences, both sampled at sc=6/NFE=256: student **50.5 ± 0.5 PPL** versus teacher **60.6 ± 0.3**, with entropy **4.31 ± 0.00** versus **4.32 ± 0.01**. MAUVE remains approximately **0.36** for both. | Table 2; Appendix B.3 |
| Fewer uncertain and invalid positions | Over 64 generated 1,024-token sequences per model at NFE 32: teacher averages **142 uncertain positions**, including **109 invalid**; student **42**, including **12 invalid**. Invalidity is assessed among uncertain positions. | Fig. 6; Appendix A.1 |
| Soft-label objective matters | OWT-1024 ELF-B ablation: MSE student reaches entropy only **5.26**, one-hot CE student remains below **4.18**, against real-text **5.43**. Sampling protocols differ partly across objectives. | Appendix A.3; Fig. 10 |
| Distillation sacrifices discriminative performance | Frozen-encoder SST-2 probe score drops from **89.4 to 78.1** on a 0–100 scale; WikiText-103 reconstruction remains **99.3** for both. | Table 4 |

The evidence supports improved generation within the tested ELF settings and is consistent with the proposed geometry explanation. It does not prove that geometry alone causes the gains or that lower Gen. PPL establishes factuality, reasoning ability, or practical usefulness. Table 1 compares selected operating points with different sampling budgets; Fig. 8 additionally reports improvements across sampling configurations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Larger encoders, practical-scale models, QA, and few/one-step generation remain untested (§3.1; §6).
- **Comparison confounds:** Encoder families differ in pretraining exposure; the authors identify teacher-versus-student as the controlled embedding comparison. Cited baseline rows use different evaluation protocols ( §5.1; Appendix B.3).
- **Metric sensitivity:** MAUVE changes substantially with feature length. The authors also show that mixed-language collapse can receive low GPT-2-Large perplexity (Table 5; Appendix C).
- **Diagnostic coverage:** Invalidity statistics inspect only uncertain positions and may miss other invalid embeddings. Candidate-distance thresholds are operational definitions, not a complete validity test (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** OOD reconstruction establishes retained encoding/decoding ability, but does not establish broad OOD generation quality; generation transfer is tested only on LM1B, which the authors characterize as problematic (§5.2; Table 4).
- **Resource qualification:** Reduced encoding cost excludes the additional distillation run, reported as 38.6 hours on eight H100 GPUs (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does distillation help at larger scales and on conditional tasks?** Those settings remain future work. Train larger encoder/ELF combinations and evaluate QA alongside unconditional generation (§6).
- **Can the student support few-step generation?** Main evaluation excludes very low NFEs because of collapse. Compare teacher and student across low sampling budgets using collapse rates and quality–diversity measures (Appendix B.3).
- **Which geometric change drives the improvement?** The authors leave finer geometry analysis open. Vary soft-target concentration and measure candidate spacing, invalidity, and generation quality under matched sampling settings (Appendix A.4).
- **Can diffusibility improve without losing discrimination?** Probe performance declines after distillation. Test a mixed distillation and representation-preservation objective, evaluating both generation and the same probes (Table 4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental findings; **medium** for the causal geometry explanation and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes verified release contents and checkpoint availability, human quality or factuality evaluation, practical conditional-task results, larger-scale experiments, and a complete census of invalid generated embeddings.
