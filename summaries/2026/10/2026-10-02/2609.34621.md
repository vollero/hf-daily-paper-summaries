# Does Native 3D Texture Generation Necessarily Require 3D Assets for Training?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34621) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34621)


## One-sentence takeaway
Tex-Zero trains native 3D texture models entirely on geometrically transformed 2D images and reports transfer to real 3D assets, although the supplied text omits numerical result tables (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Native texture generation predicts surface colors for supplied geometry and reference images. The authors identify costly, scarce textured 3D training assets as a scaling bottleneck and ask whether image appearance plus constructed geometry can provide sufficient supervision (§1). Their scope is texture generation; geometry is supplied rather than generated (Appendix A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Image-derived supervision:** Convert pixels into colored 3D points, independently rotate image patches, aggregate them spatially, voxelize, and render conditioning views. This preserves within-patch appearance while introducing varied orientations and occlusions (§3.2).
- **Shared appearance representation:** Train a sparse 3D VAE on these samples, following an essential single-plane warm-up. The same VAE encodes target textures and conditioning images represented as oriented planes (§3.3).
- **Conditional texture generation:** Train a FLUX-style DiT from scratch with flow matching, geometry features, shared-VAE image conditions, and positional encoding for space and view identity (§3.4; Appendix B). The central contribution is the training-data construction and shared encoding strategy; the backbone architectures are adopted.

These reports support feasibility within the evaluated texture task. The supplied text contains table captions and narrative conclusions, but no numerical entries for Tables 1–5, so improvement magnitudes cannot be assessed. The evidence does not establish that constructed geometry suffices for geometry generation or every texturing setting.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described constraints:** Geometry must be supplied; conditioning uses canonical orthographic views. Aggregation permits disconnected or intersecting surfaces, and the VAE reportedly fails to converge without its single-plane warm-up (§3.3; Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Both 3D evaluations use internal sets, limiting independent verification and assessment of coverage (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—data comparison:** Image training uses approximately 11.1 million images, whereas the matched-architecture 3D baseline uses approximately one million assets. Thus, data source, quantity, and appearance quality are not isolated (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison ambiguity:** §4 names TRELLIS.2, while Appendix C names TRELLIS for generation and describes a single-view checkpoint. Exact checkpoint identity and six-view comparison handling remain unclear.
- **Analyst inference—ablation scope:** Conditioning comparisons change both encoding strategy and training data, so they do not fully isolate the shared-latent mechanism (§4.5).

## Open research questions
- **How much of the benefit comes from data construction versus scale or appearance quality?** These factors are not isolated. Next experiment: compare image-derived and real-asset training under matched compute, sample budgets, and appearance quality.
- **Does transfer hold across broader geometry distributions?** Internal evaluations do not establish category coverage. Next experiment: use a public test set stratified by curvature, thin structures, and occlusion.
- **How robust is conditioning beyond canonical rendered views?** The reported settings do not establish tolerance to perspective, pose errors, or inconsistent references. Next experiment: systematically perturb cameras and image alignment.
- **Can image completion produce globally coherent unseen object appearance?** Appendix A motivates completion through within-patch image statistics, without separately evaluating hidden surfaces. Next experiment: measure visible and hidden-region quality separately as conditioning coverage decreases.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method details are substantial, but empirical strength is difficult to judge without numerical results.

Material missing information includes Tables 1–5 values, uncertainty estimates, internal test-set composition and access, total training duration and compute, single-plane warm-up duration, guidance scale, exact generation baseline configuration, and code/checkpoint release status.
