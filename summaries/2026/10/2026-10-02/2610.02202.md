# ScholarCatalyst: A Benchmark for Retrieving Papers That Inspire New Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02202) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02202)


## One-sentence takeaway
ScholarCatalyst evaluates retrieval of author-judged research inspirations from early-stage questions, finding that tested search agents struggle to improve on dense retrieval, with candidate coverage emerging as a major bottleneck.

## Problem and motivation
Finding prior ideas that advance an open-ended research project requires more than topical matching. The authors argue that existing scientific retrieval benchmarks and citation annotations miss this distinction: bibliographies do not reliably identify decisive inspirations, and useful papers may never be cited. ScholarCatalyst targets this undocumented relevance through researchers’ firsthand judgments (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Author-grounded benchmark:** 184 researchers validate 894 queries from 207 projects, labeling catalyst papers and related hard negatives with rationales. Queries cover a project’s central question (**CoreQ**) or a specific research direction (**SubQ**) (§3; Table 2).
- **Pre-discovery retrieval protocol:** Systems search a 190,896-paper corpus filtered by source-paper publication date, without the completed source paper or web access. Main agent backbones have stated cutoffs preceding all source papers (§3.1; §§C.2–C.3).
- **Refreshable construction pipeline:** An arXiv ID initiates paper parsing, reference resolution, LLM-generated draft annotations, sparse/dense candidate retrieval, and author revision. Dataset and code links are supplied; an additional model-reconstructed set is described separately (§3.2; §§E–F; p. 1).
- **Comparative evaluation and diagnosis:** The paper compares retrieval families and agent designs, then probes candidate coverage, reranking, query rewriting, source context, and fuller reading (§4; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Dense retrieval leads the main comparison | On 207 CoreQ / 687 SubQ queries, Qwen3-Embedding-8B achieves R@20 **0.37 / 0.51**, versus BM25 **0.23 / 0.26** and SPECTER2 **0.20 / 0.23**. | Table 3 |
| Tested pre-cutoff agents do not improve on their search retriever | Gemini-Embedding-2 R@20: **0.38 / 0.46**; GPT-4.1 tool-calling: **0.37 / 0.43**; o3 deep research: **0.33 / 0.38**, for CoreQ / SubQ. | Table 3 |
| Agent recovery outside the retriever’s pool is limited | Across 4,845 positive query–paper pairs, tool-calling and deep research agents rank **4% / 6%** of positives outside Gemini-Embedding-2’s top 100 in their final top 20. | Table 26 |
| Better coverage enables stronger reranking | On 50 queries per type, Claude Fable reranking of a 20-paper pool raises R@5 from **0.31 to 0.60** for CoreQ and **0.22 to 0.40** for SubQ when missing gold papers replace negatives. This is an oracle intervention. | Table 20; §4.4 |
| Citations omit substantial labeled inspiration | **43.6%** of SubQ positives are uncited; **67.8%** of those share no references with the source paper. | §4.3; §B.3 |
| Fuller reading yields little gain under the tested budget | On 50 queries per type from the full-text subset, abstract-only GPT-4.1 agent R@20 is **0.35 / 0.46**; full-paper reading gives **0.34 / 0.49**. Reads are truncated and share the search budget. | Table 25; §D.5 |
| Automated nomination cannot replace author labeling | CoreQ key-inspiration nomination against author labels: **58.2% precision**, **26.0% recall**, and **9.2% exact-set agreement**. | Table 32 |

These results establish difficulty in recovering the benchmark’s author-labeled positives. Coverage interventions support the authors’ bottleneck interpretation, but ranking failures also occur (§D.6). The experiments do not establish improved research outcomes or failure of every possible agent design.

The similarity evidence is mixed: Table 11 shows negatives scoring higher on both measures for CoreQ, but positives scoring slightly higher for SubQ. Thus, the prose claim that negatives are at least as similar on both measures is stronger than the table supports. The lexical measure is also described differently in §4.3 and Table 11.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Coverage is concentrated in recent CS/AI research with uneven area representation; retrospective judgments are vulnerable to hindsight; the task’s performance ceiling remains unknown (§5).
- **Analyst inference—annotation selection:** Uncited candidates originate from shallow BM25/Qwen retrieval followed by LLM reranking. This may omit inspirations those systems cannot surface; exhaustive relevance coverage is not demonstrated (§E.3).
- **Analyst inference—temporal approximation:** Filtering at source-paper publication approximates historical availability but does not enforce the actual project-start date (§§3.1, C.2).
- **Comparison boundaries:** Agent harnesses are adaptations with restricted budgets; citation expansion is omitted. Ranking backfill affects R@100 and sometimes R@20 (§C.3).
- **Reading and leakage boundaries:** “Full-paper” reads are capped at 12,000 characters. Post-cutoff models are comparison references, and the leakage analysis does not settle memorization (§§D.3–D.5).
- **Label reliability:** The coauthor comparison covers only one project, while rationale agreement uses the same model to generate and judge explanations (§5; §D.6).

## Open research questions
1. **Can inspiration-specific training improve coverage?** Evaluated retrievers remain limited. Train on author rationales and labels, then test on held-out projects with separate coverage and ranking metrics.
2. **How complete and stable are the labels?** Candidate pooling and the small coauthor study leave this unresolved. Expand blinded author review using diverse retrieval pools and multiple coauthors.
3. **Does retrieval help research prospectively?** Retrospective relevance does not measure actual progress. Compare retrieval systems on ongoing projects and track which suggestions researchers adopt and find useful.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark description and reported comparisons; **medium** in the general bottleneck interpretation.

Material missing information includes prospective research outcomes, comprehensive label completeness, broad inter-author agreement, actual project-start dates, statistical uncertainty for main comparisons, and verified training-data exposure. Public asset availability is asserted by the paper but was not independently checked.
