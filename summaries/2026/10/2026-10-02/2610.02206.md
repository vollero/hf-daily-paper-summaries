# KaliBench: A Fine-Grained Benchmark for Cybersecurity Tool Use on Kali Linux with Runtime-Free Verifiable Rewards

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02206) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02206)


## One-sentence takeaway
KaliBench isolates cybersecurity command generation and shows that documentation greatly improves argument accuracy, while benchmark-derived SFT and reinforcement learning raise an 8B model’s average component score close to a much larger model’s performance (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Cybersecurity CLIs require precise tools, flags, values, and argument ordering. The authors identify a gap between knowledge benchmarks and end-to-end agent evaluations: neither isolates whether an LLM can translate an analyst’s request into a correct command. Schema-based function-calling evaluations also provide explicit interfaces, whereas unrestricted CLI generation requires models to infer the interface themselves (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and dataset:** 8,504 query–command pairs covering 1,642 tools, 23 capability dimensions, and five security phases; 5,000 evaluation examples and 3,504 training examples (§3).
- **Verification pipeline:** Documentation-grounded generation followed by LLM verification, sandbox execution, author review, and semantic deduplication. Commands receive structured tool, optional-argument, and positional-argument annotations (§3).
- **Fine-grained evaluation:** Alias-aware scoring under unrestricted queries, restricted candidate-tool lists, and hinted candidate-tool documentation separates tool selection from argument construction (§3; Appendix L).
- **Runtime-free training rewards:** Component correctness, exact-command matching, and formatting provide rewards for GRPO without executing model outputs during training. SFT, GRPO, and their combination are demonstrated on RedSage-Ins 8B (§§3–4).

The authors claim novelty for schema-free cybersecurity CLI benchmarking; the supplied text does not independently establish priority (§1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Correct tool selection does not ensure correct commands | Across 24 open-weight configurations on the evaluation set, unrestricted→restricted mean Tool Accuracy rises from 72.0% to 95.2%, while Exact Correct rises from 22.3% to 28.3%. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Documentation supplies the largest gains | Unrestricted→hinted mean Optional F1 rises from 45.1% to 87.8%; Positional F1 from 61.1% to 90.1%; Exact Correct from 22.3% to 73.1%. | Table 1 |
| Unrestricted command generation remains difficult | Best evaluated open-weight unrestricted Exact Correct is GLM-5.2’s 41.3% on the 5,000-example test set. | Table 1 |
| Compact-model post-training helps, with tradeoffs | RedSage-Ins→8B SFT+GRPO average Total Score across modes rises from 71.7% to 79.2%; DeepSeek-V3.2 685/37B scores 80.2%. Unrestricted Exact Correct rises from 19.7% to 32.2%, but hinted Total Score falls by 0.19 percentage points. | Tables 1, 6 |
| Proprietary/scaffolded systems perform better but remain unsaturated | Unrestricted Exact Correct over all 5,000 queries: GPT-5.6-Sol 61.68%, Codex/GPT-5.5 51.68%, Claude Opus 5 44.02%; missing/blocked outputs count as incorrect. | Tables 2, 8 |
| Noisy wording reduces performance | Across eight configurations, messy queries reduce mean Total Score relative to clean queries by 3.20, 1.40, and 1.07 percentage points in unrestricted, restricted, and hinted modes. | Table 3; Appendix N |

These results support argument construction as a major bottleneck in this controlled benchmark. Nearness to DeepSeek applies to average Total Score, rather than uniform equivalence across modes or metrics. The evaluation does not establish operational task success: model outputs are scored symbolically, and ground-truth verification permits some runtime failures caused by placeholder inputs (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Single-turn scope, documentation-grounded data, version drift, inconsistent manuals, imperfect verification, and incomplete alias coverage (§5; Appendix O).
- **Analyst inference—metric coverage:** Optional F1’s published formula matches flag aliases without explicitly checking associated values; positional F1 ignores ordering. Consequently, component scores can overstate complete correctness. Exact Correct is intended to capture parameter errors, but its implementation is less fully specified (Appendix L).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Training and evaluation share tools; no tool-disjoint result is supplied. Deduplication alone does not establish transfer to unseen interfaces (§3).
- **Analyst inference—selection effects:** Final review targets failures of the strongest hinted model, potentially making dataset retention dependent on that model’s behavior (§3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Cross-model box plots describe dispersion, rather than repeated-run uncertainty. Training-seed variability and confidence intervals for gains are not reported (Fig. 5; checklist item 7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison constraints:** Documentation truncation and differing local/API/scaffolded execution settings complicate attribution of differences solely to model capability (Appendices G.1, M).

## Open research questions
1. **Do symbolic rewards predict successful execution?** Placeholder-related failures leave this unresolved. Execute predictions against prepared targets and compare Exact Correct with task outcomes.
2. **Does post-training transfer to unseen tools?** Current splits share tools. Repeat training with a tool-disjoint split and report results separately for unseen interfaces.
3. **Which rewards drive gains and hinted regressions?** SFT/GRPO comparisons do not isolate reward components. Ablate component, exact-match, and formatting rewards with repeated training runs.
4. **Can retrieved documentation approach full-hint performance?** Retrieval is proposed as future work but unevaluated. Compare retrieved excerpts with full documentation under matched input budgets (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and tabulated findings; **medium** in operational generalization.

Material missing information includes the numerical residual annotation-error rate and audit size, explicit reward weights, repeated-run uncertainty, detailed canonicalized exact-match behavior, and execution-based validation of generated predictions. Artifact availability is author-reported and was not independently checked.
