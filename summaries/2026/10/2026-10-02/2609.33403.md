# DataMagic: Authoring Data Videos through Declarative Multi-Agent Orchestration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33403) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33403)


## One-sentence takeaway
DataMagic combines a shared declarative video specification with staged multi-agent generation to improve raw-table-to-video reliability and authoring efficiency in the reported evaluations, while leaving factual accuracy incompletely assessed.

## Problem and motivation
Creating data videos requires coordinating analysis, charts, narration, animation, and scene ordering. The authors identify a gap between tools that automate individual components and systems that produce complete, editable narratives from raw data with traceable visual elements. Their central challenges are representing cross-modal timing and references consistently, and composing locally useful scenes into a coherent overall story (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **DVSPEC representation:** A scene-based JSON specification unifies chart content, narration, and animations. Data-field references identify animation targets; narration-segment indices determine trigger timing after text-to-speech synthesis (§4).
- **Generate-then-Orchestrate pipeline:** Agents decompose queries, prepare data, and design candidate scenes in parallel. Subsequent orchestration orders scenes, writes contextual narration, and binds animations to narrated entities (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Interactive authoring system:** Canvas manipulation, script editing, and natural-language commands modify shared DVSPEC state. Scoped edits re-render individual scenes; provenance supports structured data Q&A and additional scene generation (§5.3).
- **Evaluation contribution:** An adapted real-world evaluation set, component ablations, expert validation of an automated judge, and a within-subjects authoring study assess the complete system (§§6–7; Appendices B–E).

The contribution is the integrated representation and workflow; the paper does not introduce a trained foundation model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher quality and execution success across backends | On 109 samples from 60 datasets, four direct-generation backends scored 1.91–2.22/5 with 48.62%–86.24% execution success; their DataMagic variants scored 3.38–3.89/5 with 95.41%–98.17% success. Retry policies were identical. | §6.1; Table 1 |
| Strong matched-backend improvement | Claude-Sonnet-4 direct generation scored 2.18/5 and executed successfully on 84.40%; DataMagic with the same backend scored 3.89/5 and reached 98.17%. Narrative rose from 1.98 to 3.84; Animation from 1.91 to 4.39. | Table 1 |
| Planner and orchestration each contribute | With Claude-Sonnet-4, full-system quality was 3.89/5; removing the Story Planner reduced it to 3.44, and removing Orchestration to 3.54. | §6.3; Table 2 |
| Generation costs more time | Reported average runtime was approximately 57 seconds for direct generation versus 176 seconds for DataMagic, including configuration generation and rendering with parallelism set to three. | §6.2 |
| Automated scores correlate with expert judgments | Gemini-2.5-Pro evaluation versus averaged ratings from three experts on 60 stratified videos: overall Pearson r=0.91, p<0.001, MAE=0.38 on the 1–5 scale. | §6.1; Appendix E, Table 7 |
| Faster authoring in the user study | Twelve participants used both conditions, sharing Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Remotion. Mean completion time fell from 39.2 to 8.0 minutes; authors report a 79.7% reduction and Wilcoxon z=−3.06, p<.001, r=0.88. | §§7.1–7.2; Fig. 5 |
| Lower perceived workload | In the same study, five of six NASA-TLX dimensions improved at reported p<.001; self-rated Performance did not differ significantly, p=.34. | §7.2; Fig. 5 |

These results support improved execution reliability, judged presentation quality, and authoring efficiency under the tested conditions. They do **not** establish guaranteed factual correctness or improved audience comprehension: the rubric largely evaluates presentation rather than source-data accuracy, and no viewer comprehension experiment is reported (Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Single-table inputs, one renderer, and model/rendering-dependent visual expressiveness limit applicability. Structured animation guidance trades creative diversity for reliability (§8).
- **Observed failures:** DataMagic still exhibits clipping, narration–label numerical disagreement, and missed analytical intent. Even selected high-quality examples acknowledge numerical discrepancies (Appendix A; Figs. 7–10).
- **Analyst inference—accuracy gap:** Data-bound references support traceability but cannot by themselves verify transformations or narrated conclusions. No dedicated source-data correctness metric is reported; the judge receives the video and query rather than the source table (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Automated baselines generate complete code in one call, whereas DataMagic uses multiple stages and more runtime. The experiments therefore establish a system advantage, without isolating representation benefits from additional inference resources (§6).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** There is no standalone DVSPEC or narration-triggering ablation. The user study compares complete workflows, so it cannot separate interface, orchestration, and synchronization effects (§§6.3, 7.1).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** §6.3 calls orchestration removal more damaging although Table 2 shows a smaller aggregate drop. §5.2 describes subset selection, but Appendix C.4 requires every input scene in the output order; implemented selection behavior is unclear.
- **Population boundary:** The user study recruited university students with data-analysis experience, limiting evidence for other author populations (§7.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does traceability improve factual correctness?** Reported numerical conflicts and presentation-focused scoring leave this open. Next experiment: audit transformations, chart values, and narration against source tables, comparing matched-backend systems.
- **Which mechanisms explain the gains under equal resources?** Current ablations do not isolate DVSPEC or inference budget. Next experiment: compare structured and code-only pipelines with matched calls, tokens, retries, and runtime.
- **Do generated videos improve viewer understanding?** The study measures authoring, workload, and satisfaction. Next experiment: test audience comprehension and recall using matched videos and presentation lengths.
- **How well does scoped editing preserve overall coherence?** Local edits deliberately avoid global re-orchestration. Next experiment: evaluate sequences of scene additions and analytical changes for narrative consistency and binding correctness.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive methods and evaluation detail, but several claims exceed what the measurements establish.

Material missing or unclear information includes exact retry rules, inference budgets and API costs, treatment of failed outputs in quality averages, repeated-run variability, source-data correctness audits, statistical uncertainty for Table 1 differences, and resolution of the scene-selection discrepancy. The paper announces code availability; repository contents and release completeness were not verified.
