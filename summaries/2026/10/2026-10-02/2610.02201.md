# SILSA: Sliding-Window Slice Latents for Topology-Preserving High-Resolution 3D Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02201) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02201)


## One-sentence takeaway

SILSA combines fixed overlapping slice latents, slice-level topology supervision, and shared volumetric communication to report better image-to-3D fidelity and lower generation cost, although its evaluations do not establish guaranteed preservation of full 3D topology (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

High-resolution 3D generators can reproduce an object’s appearance while breaking thin supports, filling holes, or merging nearby parts. The authors identify a representation gap: voxel tokens preserve spatial locality but can require expensive, variable-length generation, whereas compact global representations can obscure the structures that must remain connected or separate (§1).

SILSA aims to retain cross-sectional structure within a short, fixed latent sequence, making generation cheaper while explicitly supervising connectivity and holes.

## Approach and contributions

- **Sliding-window SliceVAE:** Encodes oriented surface samples into overlapping depth windows along three canonical axes. The default uses 128 bins per axis and eight-bin windows, producing 384 tokens. A sparse volumetric decoder reconstructs meshes at a final grid resolution of 256³ (§3.1).
- **Complementary topology objectives:** Matches persistence diagrams for components and holes within decoded slices, then matches Betti-number changes between neighboring slices. These objectives supervise both cross-sectional topology and the depths of topological transitions during VAE training (§3.2).
- **Volumetric Anchor Lattice (VAL):** Coordinates directional tokens through gated reads and writes to shared axis-aligned grid planes inside an image-conditioned rectified-flow transformer. The fixed token layout removes a separate active-voxel prediction stage (§3.3).

The proposed contribution is this combination of spatially indexed slice latents, topology supervision, and cross-axis communication; persistent homology, rectified flow, and sparse decoding are established ingredients in the supplied discussion (§2).

## Evidence and main results

The authors train on Trellis-500K and describe main evaluation using 200 randomly sampled Toys4K assets plus 50 in-the-wild images without training overlap; they do not clearly assign every quantitative metric to these subsets (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher image-to-3D visual fidelity and coverage | Against SparseFlex, PSNR rises from 30.12 to 32.74, reported as an 8.7% relative increase; COV rises from 73.12% to 79.08%, a 5.96 percentage-point gain. | §4, Table 1 |
| Strong aggregate generation scores, with tradeoffs | SILSA has the best reported FD (10.16) and MMD (14.02‰), ties SparseFlex on KD (0.08) and LPIPS (0.05), but has lower CLIP similarity: 87.94 versus 88.22. | Table 1 |
| Better VAE reconstruction topology | Against SparseFlex, Betti-Err decreases from 1.743 to 1.582, reported as a 9.2% reduction; CD decreases from 0.61 to 0.59. Betti-Err is described as average component/hole mismatch. | §4, Table 2 |
| Lower measured resource cost | Against Dora, tokens fall from 1,280 to 384, training memory from 14.6 to 8.7 GB, training time from 0.38 to 0.21 s/iteration, and end-to-end inference from 0.82 to 0.34 s/shape. Training measurements use batch size four on one A100. | Table 3 |
| Both topology terms help reconstruction | Removing topology supervision gives CD 0.78 and Betti-Err 4.43; the combined objective gives 0.59 and 1.58 and outperforms either term alone. | Tables 4 and 7 |
| VAL’s advantage over direct attention is small and mixed | VAL versus direct cross-attention: CD 0.59 versus 0.60 and Betti-Err 1.58 versus 1.64, but IoU 93.01 versus 93.18. The precise evaluation protocol for these communication ablations is unclear. | Table 4 |
| Open-surface reconstruction improves modestly | On DeepFashion3D, SILSA versus SparseFlex gives CD 0.04 versus 0.05 and F-Score@0.005 93.21 versus 93.07. Authors say topology metrics saturate on these simple garments. | Appendix D, Table 5 |

These reported comparisons support improved reconstruction, generation scores, and efficiency in the tested settings. They do not establish statistical significance, universal topological correctness, or topology gains for generated shapes directly: the explicit Betti-Err comparison is for VAE reconstruction. Qualitative preservation claims are supported by the authors’ descriptions of Figures 3–4 and 6; the supplied text does not provide inspectable image content.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Unfamiliar structural patterns outside the training distribution may reduce fidelity; ambiguous or low-information input views can produce less faithful geometry (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Sampled planar component/hole supervision is a structural proxy. The paper provides no guarantee that it preserves full-volume topology or enclosed voids (§3.2, Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Small differences against direct cross-attention are difficult to assess without repeated runs or uncertainty estimates; VAL also loses slightly on IoU (Table 4).
- **Protocol ambiguity:** VAL operates in the generator, but its ablations repeat reconstruction-style metrics and default VAE scores without clearly explaining how evaluation is performed (§3.3, Tables 4 and 9).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Baseline training comparability, detailed metric computation, topology-term weights, and gradient implementation details are incompletely specified. Architecture and training settings are provided in Appendix C.

## Open research questions

1. **Does slice supervision preserve full 3D topology?** No full-volume guarantee or enclosed-void evaluation is supplied. Next experiment: measure volumetric Betti errors on controlled shapes with tunnels, cavities, and connections between sampled planes (§3.2).
2. **How robust is canonical slicing to object orientation?** Axis ablations favor three directions, but rotation robustness is unreported. Next experiment: evaluate rotated copies of identical meshes with fixed model weights (Appendix E, Table 8).
3. **Does VAL reliably improve generation over direct cross-attention?** Reported margins are small and the protocol is unclear. Next experiment: compare matched generators across multiple seeds using generation metrics, topology measurements, and communication cost (Table 4).
4. **How do input ambiguity and distribution shift affect connectivity?** Authors identify both as limitations without quantifying them. Next experiment: vary occlusion and image quality on held-out structural categories (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, tables, and substantial ablations, but leaves material evaluation ambiguities.

Missing information includes uncertainty estimates, exact quantitative evaluation subsets, CD scaling and detailed Betti-Err computation, baseline training protocols, communication-ablation procedures, topology-term weights, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
