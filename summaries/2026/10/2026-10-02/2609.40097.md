# AutoDataBench: A Data-centric Testbed for Accelerating Auto Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40097) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40097)


## One-sentence takeaway
AutoDataBench evaluates LLM agents’ training-data interventions under fixed learning pipelines, finding strong target-task gains but inconsistent hidden-evaluation transfer, alongside promising coding gains from reusing research trajectories (Table 1; Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing automated-research benchmarks mix improvements from data, training methods, hyperparameters, and compute, making data-specific capabilities difficult to assess. They target **data intelligence**: diagnosing, organizing, and constructing training data, then revising interventions using experimental feedback. The identified gap is evaluation that connects these decisions to learning outcomes while controlling non-data factors (§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** Three tasks instantiate data repair through corrupted function-calling examples, data organization through retrieval training mixtures and negatives, and data construction through factual knowledge injection. Training uses fixed LoRA SFT, contrastive learning, and privileged-context distillation, respectively (§3.3).
- **Shared agent evaluation:** Agents inspect data, implement bulk interventions through code and auxiliary models, and submit datasets for training. Each trial restarts from the same checkpoint; target-task feedback selects checkpoints, while hidden evaluations assess transfer or knowledge retention (§3.2–3.4).
- **Data-effect prediction probe:** Additional runs record predicted scores, uncertainty intervals, and justifications before training, testing whether agents’ expectations track outcomes (§4.4).
- **Trajectory reuse experiment:** A matched-token mid-training comparison tests whether collected research trajectories improve a coding model after identical SFT (§4.5; Appendix A.5).

## Evidence and main results

Benchmark scores below are reported on a 0–100 scale. Standard agent results average three independent runs, selecting each run’s checkpoint by its best ID score (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Tool-use repair approaches clean-data performance | Agent mean ID macro AST accuracy spans **80.82–81.98**, versus **70.45** for random corrupted-data sampling and **81.65** for the oracle-clean reference. Mean hidden BFCL accuracy spans **57.26–59.44**, versus baseline **55.18** and oracle-clean **60.23**. | Table 1; §3.3.1; §4.2 |
| Retrieval gains fail to transfer consistently | GPT-5.6-Sol achieves mean ID nDCG@10 **40.46**, versus expert **40.62**, but hidden mean nDCG@10 is **26.15**, versus expert **34.76** and baseline **31.13**. Every agent’s mean OOD score is below the baseline. | Table 1; §3.3.2; Appendix A.3 |
| Constructed data improves novel knowledge, with mixed retention | Kimi-3 achieves mean Novel accuracy **60.60**, versus expert **48.40** and unadapted Talkie **39.10**. Claude-4.7 leads mean Retention accuracy at **50.37**; GLM-5.2 scores **47.15**, below unadapted Talkie’s **47.84**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Most runs improve beyond their initial datasets | First-to-best ID improvements occur in **21/21** tool-use, **21/21** retrieval, and **19/21** knowledge runs; mean gains are **8.11**, **6.46**, and **4.91** score points, respectively. | Table 7; Appendix A.4 |
| Forecasting tracks retrieval scores but may burden optimization | One prediction-enabled run per model/task gives retrieval forecast MAE of **0.64** points for GPT-5.6-Sol and **1.55** for Kimi-3. Best scores fall below corresponding standard-run means in **4/6** comparisons. | §4.4; Figure 3 |
| Trajectory reuse improves reported coding scores | Matched **5B-token** mid-training arms, followed by identical SFT, yield CRUXEval Input **72.00→74.12** and SWE-bench Multilingual **27.67→33.33**; all five reported coding scores increase. Trajectories contribute **80M tokens**, or **1.6%**, after upsampling. Table 2 does not specify the coding score definitions. | Table 2; §4.5; Appendix A.5 |

These results support effective data optimization within the supplied pipelines and expose a substantial retrieval transfer gap. They do **not** isolate which intervention caused a gain, prove that feedback alone improves research ability, or establish causal understanding from score forecasting. The authors explicitly distinguish trajectory gains from effects of checkpoint selection and training variability (Appendix A.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified evidence:** Selected best-run curves exclude failed evaluations and do not represent average behavior or equal compute use. Sequential intervention examples do not isolate individual operations’ causal contributions (Figure 2; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited uncertainty:** Standard comparisons use three runs, while references have no repeated-run uncertainty estimates. Forecasting has one run per model/task, and coding transfer has one matched pair with no reported uncertainty (§4.1; §4.4–4.5; Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Three text-based tasks under fixed models, tools, and budgets cannot establish broader data intelligence. Synthetic tool-use corruption and one historically bounded knowledge model further constrain applicability (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—aggregate interpretation:** “Overall” averages different task metrics and excludes hidden performance, so its ranking alone does not establish general research superiority (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—prediction validity:** Tracking absolute scores after feedback may reflect calibration to recent outcomes rather than understanding a particular intervention’s effect; no corresponding counterfactual test is reported (§4.4).

## Open research questions
- **Can agents improve retrieval transfer without sacrificing ID performance?** Every agent’s mean hidden score falls below the baseline. A next experiment could compare current optimization with source-diversity constraints using fresh hidden datasets (Table 1).
- **Does forecasting reliably impair optimization?** Single prediction-enabled runs cannot separate forecasting burden from run variability. Repeat paired runs with identical budgets and matched initialization (§4.4).
- **Which trajectory components drive coding gains?** The treatment adds complete trajectories without component ablations. Compare inspection, failed experiments, feedback, and successful interventions under matched token budgets (§4.5).
- **Do agents predict intervention effects beyond recent-score extrapolation?** Absolute-score agreement does not resolve this. Compare predicted score changes against a previous-score baseline and randomized intervention controls (§4.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark findings; **medium** in causal and broader-generalization interpretations.

Material missing information includes complete agent prompts and task-training hyperparameters, Retention-set size and detailed construction, forecast interval coverage, coding evaluation protocols, repeated-run coding-transfer uncertainty, detailed expert-curation recipes, and verified release contents.
