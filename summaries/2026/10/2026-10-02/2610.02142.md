# Keyword Harnesses Fail Open: A Cheap Diagnostic Ladder for Tool-Use Claims in Small Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02142) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02142)


## One-sentence takeaway
Strict structural and generalization diagnostics expose misleading keyword-based tool-use scores and verify a targeted small-model repair, while showing that reliable call suppression and causal explanations remain unresolved (§§6.2–6.9).

## Problem and motivation
A keyword grader can reward prose mentioning a tool without checking whether the model emits an executable structured call. In the VectraYX Spanish cybersecurity models, nearly identical tool-use scores concealed sharply different behavior (§§5.1, 6.3).

The author addresses a prerequisite to tool-use evaluation: establishing that a model produces the required format, selects appropriate tools, and extracts arguments rather than merely mentioning tools or recalling training answers. The proposed diagnostics also help distinguish model failures from serving and scoring defects (§§1, 5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic ladder:** Training-format greedy reproduction, separate checks for exact copying and argument generalization, novel prompts, first-token probabilities, and embedding-drift measurements (§§5.3–5.4).
- **Evaluation audit and trajectories:** A documented keyword-harness false positive, followed by checkpoint probes distinguishing structured emission, memorized priors, and call suppression (§§6.2–6.3, 6.7).
- **Repair and bounded mechanism evidence:** Targeted supervised fine-tuning restores structured calls and improves novel-entity performance; weight comparisons support repair outside the trigger-token embedding without identifying a circuit (§§6.5–6.6, 6.8).
- **Recipe comparison and artifacts:** A corpus-diversity × learning-rate factorial qualifies ingredient claims; the paper documents the incomplete 600M model and reports public checkpoints and tokenizer assets (§§6.9, 9).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword scores conceal structural failure | Single-seed GGUF/Ollama B4: 600M **0.660**, recorded 1B **0.650**. Training-format greedy checks: 600M **6/6** generalized calls; tested unrepaired 1B checkpoints **0/4–6**. | Tables 3–4; §§6.1–6.3 |
| Targeted repair restores emission | Reproduced 1B repair: **2,202 steps**, approximately **3.3 GPU-hours**, LR **10⁻⁵**; **4/4** exact training-answer reproductions and **8/8** structural successes on the small novel battery. | §6.5, Table 5 |
| Larger battery supports bounded generalization | Among **166 call-expecting prompts** within a **238-prompt** battery, repaired 1B headline pass **0.536** versus 600M **0.428**; paired exact McNemar **p=0.0039**. Unrepaired repair-parent checkpoint: **0.000**. | §6.8, Table 8 |
| Repair does not substantially move the trigger embedding | In the bf16 parent-child comparison, **97.7%** of embedding entries remain bit-identical; trigger-row cosine **0.99999583**, with greater movement in sampled attention projections. | §6.6 |
| Web-heavy training removes an observed memorized prior | Across the 1B phase-2 window, mean whitespace-adjusted trigger probability falls from **0.432 at 36K** to **5.9×10⁻⁷ at 48K**; the initial calls are exact copies, not demonstrated generalization. | §6.7, Table 7 |
| Both models over-trigger on conceptual near-misses | On no-call prompts containing tool-shaped entities, suppression pass is **0.087** for 600M and **0.174** for repaired 1B; reported over-triggering is **0.913** and **0.783**. | §6.8 |
| Repair ingredients lack a stable dominant explanation | All five completed factorial arms install the format. Low-LR suppression is **0.375 narrow** versus **0.583 diverse**, but the matched-step partial second narrow seed does not reproduce that contrast. | §6.9, Tables 10–11 |

The evidence supports a local harness-validity failure and generalizing structured emission after repair. It does not establish successful tool execution, a useful conversational assistant, or a causal effect of pretraining composition. Headline generalization accuracy remains limited, and suppression is weak.

The supplied full-corpus report also contains unresolved denominator inconsistencies: its emission rates and later parseable/exact-copy counts do not align transparently (§6.8). Those counts should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The model pair differs in size, tokens, curriculum, and hardware; composition is a hypothesis rather than an isolated cause. The 600M run is permanently incomplete (§8, L1, L3).
- **Author-stated:** The battery is constructed internally, excludes unseen tools and multi-call sequences, and has duplicated suppression prompts that make reported suppression intervals optimistic. Language comparisons are confounded by templates (§1.1; §6.8; §8, L11).
- **Author-stated:** Most factorial cells have one completed seed; its numerical regime differs from the main repair. Item-level intervals omit run-level uncertainty (§8, L5).
- **Author-stated:** Embedding drift provides localization by elimination, not circuit identification; small distributed embedding changes are not excluded (§8, L6).
- **Author-stated:** Original repair artifacts were lost, part of the corpus regenerated, and primary repair results come from a surviving sibling parent. Harness and scorer defects required correction (§8, L7, L11–L12).
- **Analyst inference:** Structural success on favorable prompts is insufficient evidence for operational security-assistant reliability, given poor suppression and unverified answer correctness.
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated through part of §6.10 and subsequent material; this analysis cannot provide complete paper coverage.

## Open research questions
1. **Does code fraction causally determine default structured emission?** The natural experiment is confounded; train matched models varying only realized code fraction, with fixed tokens and instruction exposure (§8, L3; proposed F4).
2. **Does corpus diversity reliably improve suppression?** The largest factorial contrast lacks completed seed replication; finish both low-LR replicates and evaluate on distinct no-call prompts (§8, L5).
3. **Which network changes cause the repair?** Drift does not identify a circuit; test parent-child component swaps and frozen versus unfrozen embeddings under updates that exceed the precision floor (§8, L6).
4. **Does the repaired policy transfer beyond this battery?** Unseen tools, multi-call sequences, and balanced language templates remain untested; evaluate held-out schemas and translated, matched prompts (§1.1; §6.8).

## Confidence and missing information
