# 4Director: Controlling Video World Models with Rigid 3D Geometry

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02160) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02160)


## One-sentence takeaway
4Director conditions video generation on depth renderings of complete object meshes following rigid 3D trajectories, improving reported camera and object control while leaving articulated motion to the generator (§§3, 5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Professional video production needs independent control of camera movement and object position and orientation. The authors identify two gaps: image-plane controls leave depth and rotation ambiguous, while existing 3D trajectories, boxes, and lifted proxies lack complete object surfaces. Their proposed solution reconstructs each object once, so newly exposed geometry can be rendered consistently across viewpoints (§1; Fig. 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Explicit scene control:** Reconstruct a canonical mesh for each marked object and a static background point cloud from the input image. Author camera and per-object rigid transformations in a shared coordinate frame, then render their depth and occlusion (§3.1).
- **Motion Adapter:** Train a VACE-style conditioning branch on Wan2.1-VACE-14B while leaving the video prior unchanged. The branch conditions generation on depth, the input image, and text; appearance, illumination, missing background, and non-rigid motion remain generative (§3.2).
- **Training annotations:** Construct RealCOD-Rigid with 20,774 clips using automatic camera/depth estimation, mesh alignment, and robust tracking of each object’s rigid “stable core” (§4; Appendix A).
- **Object-control metric:** Introduce Identity-Gated IoU, which credits mask overlap only when a vision–language judge accepts the object’s identity and structural integrity (§5.2; Appendix C.2).

The results support improved control in the reported evaluation, and the representation ablation supports a contribution from both complete surfaces and rotation. The authors attribute the object-control gain mainly to placement rather than recognition. These results do not establish reliable articulated control, long-horizon persistence, or physical accuracy of reconstructed hidden surfaces. The supplied text contains table captions and narrative results but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–4.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Whole-object rigid transformations cannot prescribe limb motion or deformation. A breakdancer remains in the initial handstand pose rather than dancing (§6; Appendix F, Fig. 9).
- **Author-stated metric boundary:** IG-IoU cannot detect rotations that preserve a symmetric silhouette (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Baselines use different native resolutions, clip lengths, and conditioning capacities; PaC receives no text prompt. Comparisons therefore assess complete systems, with potential confounding from these differences (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—measurement dependence:** SAM3 segmentation and Qwen3-VL judgments determine IG-IoU; human validation of that gate is not reported. Camera errors omit failed trajectory estimates, whose counts are unavailable (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Quantitative testing uses the source dataset, and object scoring covers only the first object. Authored trajectories and insertions lack quantitative comparisons; automatic reconstruction gates also select which clips enter training (§5.1; Appendices A–C, H).

## Open research questions
- **Can articulated control preserve the rigid-control gains?** The handstand failure leaves this unresolved. Compare whole-object meshes against articulated parts using prescribed limb trajectories and pose/control measurements (§6; Appendix F).
- **How sensitive is control to reconstruction errors?** Mesh alignment and tracking are automatically estimated, without a reported robustness study. Perturb mesh shape, depth, and rigid tracks separately and measure control degradation (Appendix A).
- **How reliable is IG-IoU across rotations and occlusions?** Its automated gate and symmetric-silhouette blind spot remain concerns. Compare judge decisions with human labels and add an orientation-specific assessment (§5.2; Appendix C.2).
- **Do gains transfer to freely authored, multi-object scenes?** Authored cases are qualitative and quantitative scoring evaluates the first object. Run a controlled benchmark with novel trajectories, multiple interacting objects, and per-object visibility/identity scores (Appendices C.2, H).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and evaluation protocol are well described, but the supplied text lacks complete numerical tables, uncertainty estimates, camera-estimation success counts, human validation of IG-IoU, quantitative authored/insertion results, runtime and memory measurements, and confirmed code/model/dataset release details.
