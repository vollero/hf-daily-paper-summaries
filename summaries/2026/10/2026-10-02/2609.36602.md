# OTRetarget: Joint Robot and Object Motion Retargeting via Optimal Transport

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36602) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36602)


## One-sentence takeaway
OTRetarget jointly adapts humanoid and object trajectories using surface interactions transferred by optimal transport, achieving 87% robot–object contact Jaccard agreement versus OmniRetarget’s 28% on OMOMO, while remaining a kinematic method evaluated on one robot (Table III; Sec. V).

## Problem and motivation
Human-to-robot motion transfer must preserve contacts despite different body proportions. Skeletal tracking alone cannot fully describe surface contact, scaling trajectories can misalign interactions with an unchanged scene, and fixed object paths can become unreachable. The authors identify joint adaptation of robot and object motion with explicit surface interactions as the missing capability in existing retargeting pipelines (Secs. I–II).

## Approach and contributions
The method extracts signed distances, closest surface points, and relative directions from demonstration geometry. Entropic optimal transport establishes surface correspondences; a constrained inverse-kinematics solver then balances transferred interaction targets against motion style while jointly optimizing robot and object poses frame by frame (Sec. III).

The authors identify three contributions (Sec. I, p. 2):

- **Surface interaction residuals:** separate gap, surface-placement, and direction costs capture relationships beyond skeletal targets.
- **Joint robot and multi-object optimization:** movable object trajectories allow embodiment adaptation while preserving robot–object, object–object, and ground interactions at the captured scene scale.
- **Object substitution:** optimal transport maps interaction targets onto substitute geometries, enabling motion variants from one demonstration.

The downstream reinforcement-learning policy is explicitly an evaluation tool, not a claimed contribution (Sec. IV-E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better robot–object interaction | OMOMO dataset medians: Jaccard **87% vs. 28%**, depth error **8.7 vs. 29.3 mm**, placement error **37 vs. 102 mm**, OTRetarget versus OmniRetarget. Evaluation covers 4,421 sequences; baseline failures are excluded from its aggregates. | Table III; Sec. IV-A |
| Better ground fidelity and style | AMASS CMU/SFU, 2,027 sequences: ground Jaccard **99% vs. 93%**, depth error **1.2 vs. 3.3 mm**, orientation error **6.3° vs. 25.5°**, OTRetarget versus OmniRetarget. | Table I |
| Gains persist with scaling and fixed objects | On OMOMO, scaled/fixed-object OTRetarget achieves **82% Jaccard** and **8.8 mm depth error**, versus OmniRetarget’s **28%** and **29.3 mm**. | Table III |
| Freeing objects improves native-scene adaptation | OMOMO medians, fixed versus variable objects: orientation error **12.0° → 10.7°**, object Jaccard **84% → 87%**; variable-object mean-position drift has a dataset median of **2.9 cm**. | Table II |
| Faster than interaction-aware baseline on two clips | Single-core AMD Threadripper PRO 7955WX: median frame times **21.5 vs. 470 ms** for locomotion and **63.8 vs. 749 ms** for carrying, OTRetarget versus OmniRetarget. | Sec. IV-B |
| Substitute-shape contact remains strong | Eight substitute shapes from one largebox demonstration: Jaccard **81–87%**, depth error **9.4–12.2 mm**, drift **11.1–18.6 cm**. | Table IV |
| Improved downstream locomotion tracking | Identically trained policies on ten AMASS clips: root-relative MPJPE over 14 tracked bodies **28.8 vs. 33.7 mm**; success **98.8% vs. 97.2%**, OTRetarget versus OmniRetarget references. | Sec. IV-E; Fig. 6 |
| Limited physical manipulation transfer | Modified manipulation training reaches **48% simulation success** under a **25 cm object termination threshold**; the same policy completes **6/8 physical pick-and-place trials** without retraining or object perception. | Sec. IV-E, pp. 7–8 |

Contact metrics use a **20 mm proximity threshold**, exact mesh distances independent of the solver, and dataset medians over returned sequences (Sec. IV-A). The evidence supports improved geometric retargeting in these settings. It does not establish dynamically feasible references or broad hardware reliability. The scaled/fixed-object comparison supports the authors’ attribution to interaction modeling, but does not isolate every residual or the contribution of optimal transport.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** evaluation uses one robot, flat ground, and one two-object capture. Optimization is kinematic and framewise, with no explicit force model or trajectory horizon. Hardware manipulation is a single-clip demonstration; its policy required additional rewards and therefore provides no controlled reference-quality comparison (Secs. IV-E, V).

**Analyst inferences:** proximity-defined grip with fixed hands does not establish force-stable grasping. Object substitution is tested from one source clip, limiting evidence for broader geometry transfer. Runtime measurements cover only two clips. Baseline comparisons mix released scaling conventions; OmniRetarget’s 424 failed OMOMO sequences are omitted, which the authors acknowledge favors that baseline. No isolated ablation of transport, direction, and witness-placement terms or statistical uncertainty is reported.

## Open research questions
- **Can contact fidelity yield force-feasible motion?** The solver omits forces and a time horizon. Compare framewise retargeting with horizon-based dynamics optimization on the same manipulation clips.
- **Does transfer generalize across embodiments and terrain?** Only G1 and flat ground are evaluated. Repeat matched demonstrations on another morphology and stepped or sloped terrain.
- **Which interaction components drive the gains?** Current ablations do not isolate transport or individual residuals. Compare nearest-neighbor correspondence and systematic residual removals using identical solver settings.
- **How broad is object substitution?** Shape tests reuse one demonstration. Evaluate multiple source objects and grasp styles, measuring contact fidelity, drift, and downstream task success.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported formulation and results; broader generalization remains uncertain.
