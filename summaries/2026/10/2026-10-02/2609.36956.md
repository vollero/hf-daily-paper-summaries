# Controlled Decoding Attacks on Black-Box LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36956) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36956)


## One-sentence takeaway
BLINDBIAS adapts decoding-time jailbreak control to sampled-text interfaces, achieving the highest mean score in 20 of 24 evaluated comparisons while requiring repeated sampling and attacker-supplied assistant-prefix continuation (§4.3, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Decoding-time attacks typically require model weights or numerical token probabilities. The authors investigate whether comparable control is possible when an interface returns only sampled text. Repeated sampling can estimate a distribution, but sparse estimates weaken control and sampling at every position is expensive (§1).

Their motivating observation is that large distribution changes occur at relatively few positions along successful jailbreak trajectories, suggesting that intervention could be selective rather than continuous (Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Sample-only residual control:** BLINDBIAS adapts the existing BiasNet controller rather than introducing a new residual-control formulation. It maps sampled continuations into local actions and combines their counts with a uniform Dirichlet prior, including probability mass for unseen actions (§§2–3.1).
- **Prefix-dependent intervention:** A fixed local risk classifier scores the evolving candidate prefix. The gate activates and scales the controller when the response appears insufficiently unsafe; opening positions receive full-strength control (§3.2).
- **Multi-token execution:** After consecutive bypasses, the target supplies a longer draft. Local gate verification accepts its prefix until intervention becomes necessary, amortizing target calls (§3.3).
- **Empirical analyses:** The paper evaluates reconstruction priors, gating pipelines, and cross-family proxy priors. It also describes an exploratory empirical string action space, but supplies no corresponding performance results (§§3.4–4.4).

These results support sample-only control under the specified interface. They do not establish superiority under equal access or query budgets: BLINDBIAS requires additional continuation capabilities, and Table 1 does not report matched costs. Means without uncertainty estimates also do not establish statistical significance. Table 2 and Table 3 PPL values use different diagnostic protocols and should not be directly ranked.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated access limits:** The attack requires repeated sampling and assistant-prefix continuation. Local actions may differ from provider tokens (§3).
- **Author-stated modeling limits:** Training uses reference prefixes while inference uses generated prefixes. Draft verification guarantees compliance with the gate rule, not distribution preservation or equivalence to single-step requests (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Author-stated exploratory limits:** String actions cannot emit strings absent from calibration; proxy scoring retains only the first proxy token (§3.4).
- **Author-stated diagnostic limits:** Gate-training comparisons change evaluation support and sometimes training duration, preventing clean isolation of training effects (Appendix A.3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** English benchmarks, four endpoints, target-specific training, and a single automated judge constrain generalization. Gemini also serves as both target and judge in some comparisons (§4).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The retained gate checkpoint lacks original optimizer arguments and dataset size. The supplied text gives no confidence intervals, independent speculative-execution ablation, or main-baseline query-cost comparison (Appendix A.3.3; Tables 1–4).

## Open research questions
1. **How competitive is BLINDBIAS under matched access and budgets?** Table 1 does not isolate these factors. Compare attacks with identical continuation privileges and endpoint-call budgets.
2. **What does speculative execution contribute independently?** Reported savings concern complete pipelines. Hold controller and gate fixed, then toggle drafting while measuring calls, latency, and Harm/Info.
3. **Can the gate handle recovery from unsafe prefixes?** Training labels impose persistent unsafety after a detected boundary (Appendix A.3.3). Evaluate prefixes that later refuse or retract, using independent per-prefix labels.
4. **How reliable is tokenizer-free control?** The string extension lacks reported performance. Compare it with tokenizer-based control across targets and calibration coverage levels.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported means, but leaves statistical uncertainty, matched-budget comparisons, full judge templates and score ranges, original gate-training provenance, monetary/latency costs, and empirical string-extension results unavailable.
