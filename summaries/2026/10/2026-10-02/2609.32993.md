# X-Tree: Tokenizing Reusable Experience for Efficient Agent Generalization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32993) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32993)


## One-sentence takeaway
X-Tree deterministically mines recurring, success-associated action hierarchies and uses them in agent training, improving benchmark performance while leaving broader generalization and the hierarchy’s independent contribution only partly established.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that flat action-stream training underuses recurring sub-procedures in expensive agent trajectories. Existing experience libraries often require LLM-written abstractions; the proposed gap is a reproducible way to recover reusable structure directly from trajectories and incorporate it into model weights (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Experience mining:** Environment-specific rules canonicalize actions into verb–role tokens. A BPE-like miner merges adjacent spans using recurrence, length, and episode-success frequency, subject to compression constraints. The resulting X-Tree records hierarchical compositions without LLM calls during mining (§3.1).
- **Three training integrations:** Offline RL trains on individual nodes using gold-observation replay, step matching, and depth-scaled completion rewards; online RLVR adds skill bonuses that decrease with group success; on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) supplies rendered skills to a privileged-context self-teacher (§3.2–3.4).
- **Controlled evaluations:** The paper compares these integrations against SFT, outcome-only GRPO, alternative segmentations, and LLM-written skill banks. An appendix also evaluates node-based SFT and prompted skill use (§5; Appendices C–H).

The evidence supports useful experience restructuring within the tested environments. It does not establish universal compositional generalization or uniformly superior hierarchy learning: the ScienceWorld random-tree control exceeds X-Tree on G2, and OPSD sometimes matches or trails its comparators (Tables 20, 24–25). Three-run spreads are reported, but statistical support is uneven across comparisons (Appendix H).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Mining occurs once from a fixed corpus; policy-driven remining and combinations of the three integrations are untested. X-Tree reorganizes existing experience rather than creating missing experience (§6).
- **Analyst inference—generalization scope:** Main ScienceWorld mining includes held-out task types. Removing them retains a gain, but that check covers only one scale and fold (Appendix F.5).
- **Analyst inference—imperfect controls:** The offline random tree is substantially shallower than X-Tree, confounding structure quality with depth. Online random-tree results do not establish X-Score superiority on held-out G2 (Appendix E.2; Table 20).
- **Analyst inference—teacher-context attribution:** WebShop renderings include handcrafted glosses and a rule-based plan. The scrambled bank retains much of the OPSD improvement, leaving the hierarchy’s contribution partly unresolved (Appendix G; Table 23).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Offline RL rewards exact agreement with recorded actions, providing no training feedback for alternative valid paths. WebArena excludes fuzzy-match tasks and uses a modified normalized metric, although official scores are supplied (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency evidence:** Zero LLM calls applies to mining/rendering, not trajectory collection or agent training. Broad efficiency claims lack an end-to-end runtime or cost comparison.

## Open research questions
- **Does iterative remining improve learning?** The bank remains fixed. Compare fixed and periodically updated banks under equal rollout and training budgets (§6).
- **Which mining signals matter independently?** Controls do not isolate recurrence, length, success weighting, and canonicalization. Ablate each while matching skill count, depth, length, and reward exposure (§3.1; Table 20).
- **Does hierarchy add value beyond teacher vocabulary and explicit rules?** Scrambling preserves much of the OPSD gain. Compare chains, unordered action vocabulary, and rule-only plans with matched context lengths (Appendix G).
- **How broadly does transfer survive strict corpus separation?** The exclusion experiment is narrow. Repeat it across scales and environments with mining data separated from evaluation task families (Appendix F.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive methods, controls, and uncertainty estimates, but material gaps remain: verified release status, end-to-end mining/training costs, component-wise X-Score ablations, and broad evaluation with strictly separated mining corpora. Aggregate headline maxima also differ across sections; the analysis above uses setting-specific tables and figures.
