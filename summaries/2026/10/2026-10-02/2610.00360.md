# DexPolicy: Scheduled Exploration for Trajectory-Guided Dexterous Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00360) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00360)


## One-sentence takeaway
Scheduling training action noise improves mean deterministic relocation success in paired simulation and hardware experiments, but gains depend on the object, optimization setting, training seed, and execution noise (Tables I–III).

## Problem and motivation
Dexterous manipulation needs exploration to discover finger–object contacts, followed by precise control to preserve them. In the ViViDex state-policy setting, RL refines imperfect trajectories derived from human video; exploration noise can help discover a grasp and subsequently disrupt it. The authors observe limited noise reduction in baseline PPO runs and investigate whether explicitly scheduling training noise improves the learned controller under identical deterministic execution (Sec. I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Training-step noise control:** DexPolicy prescribes a scalar Gaussian action standard deviation shared across action dimensions, holding it fixed during each rollout and update. Paired experiments preserve architecture, rewards, loss form, and optimizer settings (Secs. III–IV).
- **Three optimization settings:** PPO replaces learned standard deviation with a stagewise schedule; critic-free GRPO continuations combine noise resets with annealing; the paper’s FPO variant uses a deterministic flow-generated mean, Gaussian likelihood, and PPO update with linear noise decay. This FPO implementation differs from the cited flow-matching-ratio methods (Sec. IV).
- **Controlled empirical findings:** The authors compare deterministic terminal success, screen PPO schedule components and shapes, and show that training return, task completion, and execution-noise tolerance can diverge. Their contribution is this scheduling study and its evidence, rather than a new perception module or transferable object policy (Secs. V–VI).

These results support within-method improvements for the tested protocols, without attributing deterministic gains to reduced deployment sampling noise. They do not establish a cross-method ranking, universal schedule superiority, or broad robustness. Training diagnostics are insufficient substitutes for completion: PPO sugar-box Target success remains zero despite rising return (Secs. V-F, VI).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** Policies are object-specific with one reference each; several objects remain unsolved, and longer training covers only mustard. Three simulation seeds and one hardware model per condition constrain stability estimates. Hardware uses one platform and a different simulator embodiment from the benchmark, preventing a controlled sim-to-real gap calculation (Sec. VI).

The ablations cover one optimizer contraction and one fixed FPO noise scale; there is no tuned fixed low-noise PPO baseline. GRPO resets, freezing, and annealing are not separated, its credit-assignment rule is not ablated, and developmental seed 0 affects aggregate findings. Matched execution-noise tests cover only GRPO at two scales (Sec. VI).

**Analyst inference:** Hardware trial repetition strengthens estimates for the selected models but cannot resolve variation between independently trained policies. Likewise, the unpaired linear-schedule control gives weaker evidence for schedule-shape superiority than the principal paired comparisons (Secs. V-D–E).

## Open research questions
- **Does scheduling beat a well-tuned fixed noise scale?** The authors explicitly lack that PPO comparison; run paired fixed-scale sweeps against scheduled policies using deterministic Target success.
- **Which GRPO intervention produces the gains?** Resetting, freezing, and annealing remain bundled; test them separately from identical checkpoints across additional seeds.
- **Can success gains coexist with execution robustness?** The GRPO advantage reverses at stronger tested noise; evaluate common-noise sweeps for all three settings and compare robustness-oriented training schedules.
- **Does the scheduling rule generalize more broadly?** Current policies use one reference per object and one hardware platform; test held-out trajectories, objects, and robot embodiments with independent training runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocols and descriptive results; generalization and causal interpretation remain limited.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes complete reward definitions, network and flow architecture details, precise reference-trajectory construction, hardware training-seed variability, and inspected code-release contents. GRPO evaluation did not record per-episode initial-pose equality, and statistical significance is not established (Secs. V-B–C).
