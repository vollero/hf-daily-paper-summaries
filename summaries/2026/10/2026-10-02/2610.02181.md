# OmniSeek: Native Tool Integration for Multi-turn Audio-Visual Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02181) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02181)


## One-sentence takeaway
OmniSeek improves audio-visual benchmark accuracy by training an Omni-LLM to retrieve raw audio and video clips across multiple turns, with ablations supporting both tool use and a reward for dependence on both modalities (Tables 2, 4).

## Problem and motivation
Evidence needed for audio-visual reasoning can be scattered across modalities and distant moments. The authors argue that single-pass processing dilutes brief sounds and fine visual details, while textual reasoning over fixed inputs cannot recover missing sensory evidence. Their proposed gap is **active evidence acquisition**: deciding which modality and temporal window to inspect as reasoning progresses (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Independent audio and video retrieval.** Starting from a coarse global input, OmniSeek alternates reasoning, tool calls, and observations. Audio clips and video clips—with selectable sampling rate and resolution—are appended as raw modality inputs to the context (§4.1).
- **OmniTraj-170K and its synthesis pipeline.** Timestamped visual annotations and audio transcripts support cross-modal questions, evidence chains, and interleaved demonstrations. The corpus contains 169,725 trajectories over 39,797 FineVideo videos; tool calls and observations are constructed deterministically from evidence spans (§3, Figure 3).
- **Progressive training.** Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct receives mixed supervised fine-tuning, broad GSPO reinforcement learning, and refinement on mined hard examples (§§4.2, 5).
- **Audio-Visual Necessity reward.** Two teacher-forced passes mask audio or visual attention while keeping the generated trajectory fixed. Correct trajectories receive a reward based on the weaker modality’s positive token-likelihood drop. This measures a proxy for joint dependence without additional generated rollouts (§4.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large gains over the initialization model | Reported accuracy: MMOU **test-mini**, 70.4% versus 54.1%; LVOmniBench, 44.2% versus 35.8%, comparing OmniSeek with Qwen3-Omni-Instruct. Differences are 16.3 and 8.4 percentage points. | Table 1; §5.1; Appendix A.2 |
| Strong complex-video results, but no universal lead | VideoHolmes accuracy: 74.6% versus OmniVideo-R1’s 62.9%; OmniVideoBench: 47.7% versus 44.8%. OmniSeek trails OmniVideo-R1 on Daily-Omni and WorldSense. | Table 1 |
| Necessity reward adds gains | With Phase 3 rollout size fixed at 16, adding the reward raises accuracy from 59.2% to 62.4% on WorldSense and from 67.3% to 69.5% on OmniVideoTest. | Table 2, rows e–f |
| Tool-enabled reasoning beats the textual baseline | Against text-only CoT receiving the same two-stage RL training, OmniSeek achieves 69.5% versus 56.0% on OmniVideoTest and 62.4% versus 54.3% on WorldSense. | Table 4; §5.2 |
| General video performance improves without tools at evaluation | Video-MME, **without subtitles**, accuracy: 78.5% versus the base model’s 76.8%; MLVU **m-avg** score: 77.1 versus 75.2. These evaluations use single-turn direct answers without tool invocation. | Table 3; Appendix A.2 |
| Corpus utility extends beyond tool demonstrations | Under single-turn SFT, a 100K OmniTraj subset using a 7:3 open-ended/multiple-choice mix yields Daily-Omni accuracy of 75.8%, versus 73.7% using OmniVideo-100K and 71.9% zero-shot. | Table 5; §5.2 |
| Longer retrieval traces correlate with task-dependent accuracy | JointAVBench accuracy is 68.1% for two calls and 80.8% for three; VideoHolmes and MMOU peak at 75.9% and 73.3%, respectively, among trajectories executing at least five calls. | Figure 5; §5.1 |

The results support improved benchmark performance from the combined training and retrieval framework. They do **not** establish that attention-masking scores prove faithful reasoning, or that increasing tool calls causally improves accuracy: Figure 5 reports a correlation. The general-video results establish retained or improved direct-answer capabilities, rather than benefits from retrieval during those evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated context failure:** Extremely long continuous audio can exceed the context capacity and cause repetitive reasoning, fabricated observations, and failure to call tools or answer. Appendix A.7 describes such failures on an 88-minute LVOmniBench video.
- **Author-stated mitigation trade-off:** Playback acceleration could reduce context load but distort pitch and environmental sound cues; context expansion and acoustic compression remain future work (Appendix A.7).
- **Analyst inference—grounding validity:** The necessity objective measures likelihood sensitivity on a fixed trajectory. No reported human grounding audit or regenerated modality-ablation evaluation establishes that its gains reflect necessary, correct evidence use (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Table 4 compares the overall tool-enabled paradigm with textual reasoning, but does not separately isolate audio retrieval, video retrieval, resolution changes, or matched inference budgets.
- **Comparison inconsistencies:** The narrative claims superiority over Gemini-2.5-Pro, while Table 1 lists other Gemini variants. MMOU entries marked † use a different split. The claim that larger Phase 3 rollouts improve performance consistently also conflicts with the VideoHolmes decrease in Table 2 (§§5.1–5.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** Table 6 specifies 65,536-token RL sequences, whereas Appendix A.7 describes a native 32K context limit; their relationship is unexplained. The appendix generation prompt specifies multiple-choice QA, while the corpus experiment uses an open-ended/multiple-choice mix without detailing that conversion.

## Open research questions
- **Does the necessity reward improve evidence faithfulness?** Accuracy gains do not validate the dependence proxy. A next experiment could regenerate answers after removing each modality and audit whether retrieved clips support the answer (§4.3; Table 2).
- **Which retrieval components provide the gains?** Independent modality routing and finer visual sampling are bundled together. Compare audio-only, video-only, coupled, and independent retrieval under matched token and call budgets (§4.1; Table 4).
- **Can hours-long audio remain tractable without losing critical cues?** Context overflow remains unresolved. Compare silence removal, acoustic-feature merging, and context expansion on long videos, measuring accuracy and protocol failures (Appendix A.7).
- **What retrieval depth offers the best accuracy–cost trade-off?** Call-count correlations do not establish an optimal policy. Randomize inference call caps and report accuracy, latency, and modality-token consumption (Figure 5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, numerical comparisons, ablations, and explicit failure cases, but leaves important validity questions unresolved.

Material missing information includes public asset availability, statistical uncertainty or repeated-run results, inference latency and memory costs, human validation of synthesized QA and grounding, exact inference budgets across baselines, and a broad training/evaluation overlap audit beyond the explicitly separated VideoHolmes and OmniVideo splits.
