# Video Generation Models: A Survey of Post-Training and Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00812) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00812)


## One-sentence takeaway
The survey organizes video-generation post-training by how supervision enforces alignment, distinguishing indirect behavioral adaptation from explicit evaluative optimization across four method families (§1, Figure 3).

## Problem and motivation
Pretrained video generators can produce visually strong clips while failing instruction adherence, identity preservation, temporal coherence, physical plausibility, or safety. The authors attribute this gap to pretraining objectives and noisy data that underrepresent desired behavioral constraints (§1).

Video alignment adds temporal error accumulation, coupled appearance–motion objectives, and scarce temporal supervision. The authors identify a survey gap: existing reviews emphasize architectures, conditioning, or applications rather than post-training and how alignment is enforced (§1).

## Approach and contributions
This is a literature survey; the visible text does not introduce a new generator or experimentally validated alignment algorithm.

- **Conceptual framework:** Defines post-training broadly to include adaptation and deployment-time control, and distinguishes implicit from explicit alignment according to whether correctness is directly evaluated (§1).
- **Method taxonomy:** Organizes supervised fine-tuning, self-training/distillation, preference/reward optimization, and inference-time methods; inference-time mechanisms can support either alignment type (§1, Figure 3).
- **Comparative synthesis:** Catalogs methods, backbones, stages, and reported hardware, and describes complementary roles in multi-stage pipelines (Tables 1–4, §7 takeaways).
- **Evaluation overview:** Claims a systematic review of datasets, benchmarks, and protocols. Its detailed treatment is unavailable because the source is truncated (§1 contribution list; announced §8).

The authors claim this is the first comprehensive survey dedicated to this scope; the supplied text alone cannot verify that priority claim.

These are conceptual findings and summaries of cited work. The visible text supplies no quantitative benchmark comparison establishing a best method family or the magnitude of alignment gains. Hardware entries in Tables 1–3 are method metadata, not controlled efficiency comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-described field limitations:** Temporal supervision is scarce and costly; proxy evaluators can introduce bias; stability can conflict with expressive motion (§1). Specialization risks catastrophic forgetting (§3.3), while reward optimization faces credit-assignment difficulty and reward exploitation (§5.1, §5.4).

**Analyst inferences:** The visible catalogs cannot establish comparative superiority because they lack matched evaluation settings and performance results. Missing hardware and stage entries further limit resource comparisons (Tables 1–3). The taxonomy’s boundaries are interpretive: the authors explicitly describe alignment as a spectrum and method families as complementary (§1, §7).

The source is truncated during §7.1 and omits the substantive later evaluation, challenges, and conclusion sections. Complete coverage and the survey’s literature-selection procedure cannot be assessed.

## Open research questions
- **Can rewards remain trustworthy under sustained optimization?** Reward hacking remains a stated concern (§5.4). A next experiment could compare scalar, aspect-specific, and dense rewards using independent human judgments and held-out physical tests.
- **Can alignment improve coherence without suppressing motion?** Stability–expressiveness tension and preference motion bias remain unresolved in the supplied evidence (§1, §5.3). A next experiment could compare preference constructions on matched motion content, measuring both coherence and motion expressiveness.
- **Which post-training sequence offers the best quality–cost trade-off?** Complementary pipelines are described without visible controlled comparisons (§7). A next experiment could vary adaptation, reward optimization, and distillation order on one backbone under matched compute budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The taxonomy and motivation are explicit, but empirical and later-section coverage is incomplete.

Material missing information includes most of §§7–10, detailed benchmark protocols and quantitative comparisons, literature inclusion criteria, evaluation uncertainty, and the linked repository’s contents. The supplied source supports a conceptual digest, not a complete assessment of the survey.
