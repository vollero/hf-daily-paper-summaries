# EgoTools: Towards Tool-Centric Reasoning in Real-World Egocentric Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39378) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39378)


## One-sentence takeaway
EgoTools provides tool-focused egocentric video supervision and a diagnostic benchmark, with fine-tuning improving held-out QA accuracy from 50.0% to 60.9% while reducing spatial-reasoning accuracy (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Understanding tool-mediated activities requires connecting tool choice, physical contact, object-state changes, procedural order, and causal effects. The authors argue that existing egocentric datasets largely annotate activities and objects without making tools the central explanatory unit, leaving gaps in both training supervision and diagnostic evaluation (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Tool-centric corpus:** EgoTools-Data contains 646 videos totaling 100.37 hours, with hierarchical captions, participant-provided tool-use narrations, synchronized audio, and selected grounding and geometry annotations (Table 8; §3.1–3.2).
- **Annotation and tracking pipeline:** Hierarchical video captioning complements narrations about tool choice and physical effects. A recursive VLM-guided framework checks SAM2 tracking and reinitializes it when drift is detected; reconstructed depth and camera poses lift masks into 3D trajectories (§3.2; Figure 4).
- **Diagnostic benchmark:** EgoTools-Bench comprises 1,000 eight-choice questions covering Affordance & Causality, Perception & Grounding, Procedural Dynamics, and Spatial Reasoning, with human verification and independent reliability assessment (§3.4–3.5).
- **Training resource and model:** The authors derive 184,679 instruction examples from source videos excluded from the benchmark and train EgoTools-8B by updating Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct’s language component while freezing its visual encoder and multimodal projector (§3.3; Appendix H.1). The authors’ “first comprehensive suite” claim is not independently verified here.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Current models trail human viewers | On the full 1,000-question benchmark, Gemini-3.1-Pro scores 66.9% overall using 1 FPS video and audio; Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct scores 50.0% using 64 frames without audio. Humans score 83.2%, averaged over five evaluators with unrestricted seeking and replay. | Table 2; §4.1–4.2 |
| Precise grounding remains difficult | Gemini-3.1-Pro achieves 51.7% Perception & Grounding accuracy versus 69.7% Affordance & Causality, 72.1% Procedural Dynamics, and 74.9% Spatial Reasoning under the same evaluation configuration. | Table 2 |
| Fine-tuning helps overall but hurts spatial reasoning | With matched 64-frame MP4 evaluation and source-video-disjoint training, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct improves from 50.0% to 60.9% overall; Spatial Reasoning falls from 55.4% to 44.4%. The other three tracks improve. | Table 3; §4.3 |
| Transfer is mixed | After fine-tuning, accuracy changes from 69.0% to 67.6% on full EgoSchema, 42.3% to 35.2% on EgoPlan-Bench validation, and 61.0% to 64.0% macro accuracy across EgoThink’s twelve single-image subtasks. | Table 3; Appendix H.3 |
| Visual coverage and order contribute | In the matched deterministic frame-list ablation using Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, overall accuracy is 51.7% with ordered 64 frames, 49.3% with shuffled frames, 46.9% with the middle frame, and 42.7% text-only. Eight-choice chance accuracy is 12.5%. | Table 4; §4.5 |
| Annotation reliability is substantial but imperfect | On 250 sampled questions receiving 500 independent judgments, agreement with the original key is 89.8%; paired annotator agreement is 88.4%; 94.4% of items are judged uniquely answerable. Flagged items undergo review and revision or replacement. | §3.4 |
| Domain coverage is highly uneven | Kitchen videos supply 837 of the benchmark’s 1,000 questions, despite the corpus spanning seven domains. | Table 9 |

The evidence supports improved QA performance on held-out source videos and complementary sensitivity to visual coverage and temporal order. It does not establish robotic execution competence, broad domain generalization, or which training component causes the gains. Human and model viewing budgets differ, and the frame-list ablation’s absolute scores should not be directly compared with the main MP4 results (§4.1, §4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Collection does not exhaust tools, environments, populations, cultural practices, or professional procedures. The benchmark is strongly kitchen-concentrated, and its smallest domain slices cannot support stable comparisons (Appendix E.2; Table 9).
- **Author-stated shortcut risk:** Text-only accuracy remains well above chance, indicating residual commonsense or answer-option cues despite filtering (§4.5).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Model variants differ in training and input budgets, preventing clean attribution to scale, audio, or deliberation. Transfer results also confound answer-format coverage with other fine-tuning effects (§4.3–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Source-video separation establishes episode-level holdout, but the paper does not establish participant-, site-, or tool-category-disjoint generalization.
- **Analyst inference:** One backbone and one training mixture cannot identify the value of narrations, captions, spatial supervision, or the tracking pipeline separately. No quantitative tracking-quality evaluation is supplied.
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Raw fisheye streams and internal preprocessing utilities are excluded; grounding, narration audio, and trajectory assets have conditional, incremental release (Appendix D.1; E.2).

## Open research questions
- **Why does spatial accuracy decline after training?** Explicit spatial supervision does not prevent degradation (Table 3). A next experiment could vary spatial-data weighting and compare frozen versus trainable visual components.
- **Do gains generalize beyond kitchen-heavy evaluation?** Domain imbalance limits that conclusion (Table 9). Evaluate a balanced test set with held-out sites, participants, and tool categories.
- **Which annotations drive improvement?** The mixed corpus lacks component ablations (§3.3; Table 10). Compare matched-size training sets using captions, narrations, and grounding supervision separately.
- **How robust is reasoning to answer-option cues?** Text-only performance remains substantial (Table 4). Test controlled distractor rewrites and human-scored open-ended answers on the same clips.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported dataset design and numerical results; **medium** for broader generalization and causal explanations.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes uncertainty intervals and repeated-training variability, component-level training ablations, quantitative tracking accuracy, participant/site/tool-disjoint results, GPU type and training cost, exact license terms, and confirmation of which staged assets are currently available.
