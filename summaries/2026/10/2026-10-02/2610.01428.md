# Generalization Is Stability, Not Accuracy: Multi-Axis Evaluation of LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01428) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01428)


## One-sentence takeaway
SAGO reveals differing forms of prompt sensitivity across eleven LLMs, but its measurements establish behavioral change rather than necessarily harmful errors, and reporting inconsistencies weaken some pooled comparisons.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that aggregate accuracy can conceal changes on individual examples when equivalent prompts produce different responses. Evaluations restricted to one domain, perturbation family, or behavioral signal may therefore overstate robustness. Their proposed gap is a joint evaluation of per-instance stability across domains, input variations, and behavioral axes (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation framework:** SAGO compares each baseline prompt with variants covering social register, surface noise, and structural rewriting, across multiple datasets (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Stability score:** The Stability Generalization Score (SGS) averages per-prompt RMS normalized changes across variants. Lower scores indicate greater stability; axes remain separate. One-sided t-tests assess whether mean instability exceeds a chosen tolerance (§3.3).
- **Behavioral profile and empirical study:** Four conceptual axes comprise six measurements: activation cosine similarity, BLEU, BERTScore, sequence log-probability, token entropy, and response mirroring. Eleven instruction-tuned models are evaluated with deterministic decoding on sixteen prompts per dataset across six datasets (§§3.4, 4.1).

The contribution is the combined evaluation design, rather than new underlying similarity or confidence metrics.

## Evidence and main results

All SGS values below are normalized instability scores, not accuracy or error rates.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Generation changes are widespread | Across all six datasets, all eleven models reject mean BLEU SGS ≤ tolerance at **p < .001**, at tolerances **0.01, 0.05, and 0.10**. | Table 5, Appendix B |
| Semantic similarity conclusions depend on tolerance | BERTScore SGS is significant for **11/11 models at p < .05** with tolerance **0.05**, versus **4/11** with tolerance **0.10**. | Table 5 |
| Generation and confidence rankings differ | Reported pooled scores: Gemma-4-E4B has BLEU/BERTScore SGS **0.837/0.178**; Qwen3.5-9B has **0.520/0.080**, but the latter has the largest open-model log-probability SGS, **0.088**. | §4.2; Table 5 |
| Closed models show little detected mirroring | Reported pooled mirroring SGS: GPT-5.4 **0.020**, Gemini-2.5-Flash **0.005**, Claude-Sonnet-4.6 **0.061**; none significantly exceeds **0.05**. All eight open models do, at **p < .001**. | Tables 1, 5 |
| Entropy behavior differs between open models and GPT | At tolerance **0.05**, no open model rejects the entropy null; GPT-5.4 has pooled entropy SGS **0.154 ± 0.10**, significant at **p < .001**. | Tables 5–6 |
| Dataset choice can reverse a comparison | Activation SGS favors Gemma-7B over Gemma-2B on TruthfulQA (**0.100 vs 0.153**), but reverses on Natural Questions (**0.153 vs 0.079**); lower is better. | Table 7 |

These results support evaluating a profile of sensitivities rather than relying on one metric. They do not establish statistical independence between axes, causal mechanisms, or whether response differences reduce correctness or usefulness. The paper does not directly demonstrate its aggregate-accuracy argument with paired accuracy results.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations (§5):** English-only evaluation; limited coverage of rare failures; BLEU sensitivity to harmless paraphrasing; GPT-4o-mini bias in structural variants and social-register judgments; incomplete closed-model observability; and no causal attribution of instability.

**Analyst observations grounded in the text:**

- **Pooled-score inconsistencies:** With equal dataset sample sizes, §3.3 requires pooled SGS to equal the mean of dataset scores. Several Table 7 totals do not: Llama-8B’s listed activation scores range from **0.084 to 0.314**, yet their reported total **0.267** does not match their arithmetic mean. This undermines affected pooled rankings.
- **Contradictory entropy claim:** §4.2 says entropy remains below threshold across all models, with maximum **0.012**; Tables 5–6 report GPT-5.4 at **0.154**.
- **Change is not necessarily failure:** Mirroring may be intended, and lexical variation may be harmless (§§3.4, 5). Treating every nonzero change as generalization failure requires application-specific justification.
- **Semantic preservation is imperfectly established:** Non-noise variants pass a BERTScore filter, while surface-noise equivalence is asserted by construction (§4.1). Independent semantic validation is not reported.
- **Confidence interpretation is limited:** Sequence log-probability depends on response length (§3.4), and confidence drift is not validated against correctness.
- **Sample-size support is narrow:** Appendix D studies TruthfulQA and reports five of six axes plateauing; this does not establish adequate coverage for every dataset or rare failure.
- **Normalization transfer is uncertain:** Empirical scales for unbounded log-probability do not guarantee the claimed bounded normalized range in future evaluations (§§3.3–3.4).

## Open research questions
- **Which changes harm task performance?** Similarity and mirroring scores do not establish correctness loss. Next experiment: annotate paired responses for factual correctness, usefulness, and acceptable stylistic adaptation.
- **Do axis dissociations share causal mechanisms?** The study reports differing profiles without interventions (§5). Next experiment: activation patching on matched variants while measuring all observable axes.
- **Do profiles transfer across languages and larger samples?** Evaluation is English-only, and sample-size validation uses TruthfulQA. Next experiment: repeat the protocol on multilingual datasets with larger prompt samples and tail-risk estimates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The framework and individual tabulated findings are clear, but contradictions and pooled-score inconsistencies limit confidence in broader comparisons.

Material missing information includes a usable implementation link, exact sampled prompt identities, full generation and judge prompts, judge agreement results, structural-variant generation settings, handling of normalized values outside the stated range, and corrected pooled results.
