# MMEnterprise-Bench: Evaluating Enterprise Agents over Evolving Multimodal Records

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27911) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27911)


## One-sentence takeaway

MMEnterprise-Bench exposes substantial weaknesses in multimodal workplace QA, particularly conflict resolution and evidence integration, while showing that higher retrieval recall does not always improve answer accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Workplace records preserve outdated values alongside corrections distributed across chats, documents, tables, and figures. Answering reliably requires identifying the applicable source and historical state, integrating evidence, and recognizing unsupported questions. The authors identify a gap in benchmarks that jointly evaluate multimodal evidence, cross-source conflicts, and questions whose correct answer is an earlier record state (§§1–2; Table 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evolving workspace benchmark:** 1,613 questions over 7,140 textual and visual retrieval records from 30 simulated projects grounded in public papers and reports. Earlier versions remain accessible after corrections (§3; Table 3).
- **Capability taxonomy and annotations:** Eight tasks grouped into Evidence Reasoning, Conflict Resolution, and Unanswerability, with reference answers and supporting evidence for answerable questions (§3.2; Table 1).
- **Construction and validation pipeline:** Script collaborative events, generate records and questions, apply programmatic and LLM checks, and audit a subset through blind human review (§3.3; Appendices B–C).
- **Evaluation findings:** Compare ten multimodal models under a shared RAG setup and analyze retrieval size, evidence count, and modality (§4). The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation rather than a new answering model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest overall model still makes substantial errors | With the 2B retriever, GPT-5.6 Sol achieves 52.6% accuracy at k=10 and 68.0% at k=50 across all 1,613 questions; strongest open-source model Qwen3.5-27B reaches 50.3% at k=50. | Table 5; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Conflict resolution is harder than extraction | GPT-5.6 Sol at k=10: extraction 67.5%, cross-source conflict 36.3%, temporal conflict 32.5%; at k=50, conflict accuracies rise to 55.2% and 56.1%. | Table 5 |
| Multiple records and visual evidence correlate with lower accuracy | GPT-5.6 Sol at k=10: single/multiple text records 83.3%/51.5%; single/multiple image records 52.9%/27.5%; mixed text–image evidence 25.6%. These are separate question subsets. | Table 6; §4.3 |
| Higher recall can accompany lower accuracy | With the 2B retriever, k=50→100 raises Evidence Recall@k from 57.0% to 64.8%, while Qwen3.5-2B accuracy falls from 25.0% to 22.2%. GPT-5.6 Sol improves from 68.0% to 75.9% under the same change. | Table 7; §4.4 |
| Overall ranking does not determine unanswerability performance | At k=10 on 87 unanswerable questions, GPT-5.6 Sol scores 65.5%, versus Gemini 3.8 Flash’s 87.4%, despite Sol’s higher overall accuracy. | Table 5 |
| Human audit supports accepted annotation quality within the sample | Three reviewers assess 100 items: none rejects any of 50 LLM-accepted items; at least one rejects 29 of 50 LLM-rejected items. | Table 4; §3.3 |

These results establish difficulty under the reported retrieval-and-answering protocol. They do not isolate reasoning failures from missing evidence: no gold-evidence-only answering experiment is reported. Likewise, comparisons across modality subsets do not establish a causal modality effect because the questions differ.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Simulated records grounded in a limited set of public documents may not represent real enterprises fully; LLM generation and validation may leave residual annotation errors (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference — retrieval confounding:** Low evidence recall and the absence of an oracle-evidence baseline limit attribution of errors specifically to conflict reasoning (§4.4; Table 7).
- **Analyst inference — evaluation dependence:** GPT-5.6 Luna judges all responses and is also a competing model. Human validation concerns dataset annotations; human agreement with answer scoring is not reported (§3.3; Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference — unequal subgroup difficulty:** Conflict tasks generally require more evidence records, and modality composition varies by task. Unmatched subgroup comparisons therefore combine several difficulty factors (Appendix A.3).
- **Scope:** Evaluation uses fixed retrieval followed by answering, with all project records visible to the question’s reader. It does not establish performance for interactive agents or permission-restricted QA (Appendices B.2, D).

## Open research questions

- **How much error remains with complete evidence?** Retrieval and reasoning are not separated here. Compare retrieved context against gold-only evidence and gold evidence plus controlled distractors.
- **Can explicit revision tracking improve conflict resolution?** The paper evaluates a common answering prompt rather than a dedicated version-aware method. Compare that baseline with a structured revision graph under identical retrieved records.
- **Does visual evidence itself cause the observed gap?** Existing modality subsets contain different questions. Create matched text and image renderings of the same facts and revision histories.
- **Do results transfer to real workplace records?** The corpus is simulated. Evaluate the same tasks on consented, de-identified workplace histories using independent human answer grading.

## Confidence and missing information
