# PAR: Progress-Aligned Recurrence for Length Generalization in State Tracking

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27628) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27628)


## One-sentence takeaway
PAR combines iteration-specific intermediate targets with recurrent-state norm anchoring, achieving 95.5% mean full-state accuracy on swap-S5 sequences 16× longer than training, with demonstrated benefits concentrated in sequential state tracking (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Running a looped language model for more iterations does not necessarily let it solve longer problems. The authors identify two failure modes: final-answer supervision leaves each iteration’s computational role unspecified, and recurrent-state magnitudes can drift during repeated updates. Sequential state tracking tests this directly because longer inputs require more applications of unchanged update rules (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Progress-aligned recurrence:** Retrofit a pretrained transformer with a shared recurrent core; train iteration \(k\) to predict the answer after the first \(k\) operations. Rescale each token’s recurrent state to its first-iteration RMS norm. The contribution is the combination and its controlled evaluation; recurrence and intermediate supervision have prior precedents (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Learned completion readout:** Train the existing output head to predict completion, then confirm the signal across iterations before returning the answer from its onset. This adds no parameters, although it adds supervision and confirmation computation (§3.4).
- **Prefix self-distillation:** When intermediate labels are unavailable, use a teacher’s answers to shorter prefixes as intermediate targets. Positional-stretch training supports longer-context extrapolation in this variant (§§3.5, 6.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

The controlled ablations support the combination within the tested S5 setting. They do not establish universal necessity across reasoning tasks. RLT results are imported from prior work rather than reproduced under the same PAR training setup (§4). The extrapolation factor measures a threshold crossing and is capped by tested depths; it should not be read as uniformly high accuracy throughout an unlimited horizon.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers algorithmic and templated tasks with well-defined intermediate states. Open-ended natural-language reasoning remains untested; graph reachability performs poorly (§5.5; Limitations).
- **Author-stated instability:** Larger models and prefix self-distillation show substantial seed variation. Results do not establish monotonic improvement with model size (Limitations).
- **Author-stated coverage gap:** Swap-S5, learned halting, and prefix self-distillation are evaluated only on Qwen2.5-0.5B (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Halting avoids a supplied stopping depth but still requires extra iterations to confirm completion; no wall-clock comparison establishes practical inference efficiency (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported stopping-rule comparison does not establish robustness on a separately held-out rule-selection setting (§6.2).
- The supplied text provides training and hardware details but no explicit code, checkpoint, or dataset release location (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Can alignment work without a canonical operation sequence?** Graph reachability exposes this boundary (§5.5). Test frontier-based targets against operation-indexed targets under matched training budgets.
- **What causes seed failures at larger scales?** Large cross-seed variation remains unexplained (Limitations). Run matched seeds while measuring intermediate prediction accuracy and recurrent-state dynamics.
- **Can final-answer-only training become reliable?** Positional stretch produces uneven outcomes (Table 12). Compare teacher choice, curriculum, and stretch schedules across a fixed multi-seed protocol.
- **Does halting generalize beyond the evaluated horizon?** Confirmation rules are tested at specified lengths (§6.2). Freeze the rule and evaluate longer sequences while measuring premature stops and confirmation cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported task-specific findings; **medium** for broader interpretations.

Material missing information includes released artifacts, wall-clock inference costs, independent stopping-rule validation, and evidence on open-ended reasoning or broader backbone coverage for halting and self-distillation.
