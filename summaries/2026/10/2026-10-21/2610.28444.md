# EvaOmni: An Evidence-Grounded Benchmark for Audio-Visual Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28444) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28444)


## One-sentence takeaway
EvaOmni pairs audio-visual multiple-choice answering with timestamped evidence evaluation, revealing a large gap between answer accuracy and precise grounding among the tested models (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Answer-only evaluation cannot distinguish evidence-supported answers from correct guesses or answers obtained through textual and unimodal shortcuts. The authors argue that reliable audio-visual reasoning evaluation should assess whether models locate supporting evidence in both modalities, alongside selecting the correct answer (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evidence-annotated benchmark:** 392 videos and 1,027 human-annotated questions covering 15 capabilities across retrieval and grounding, basic understanding, and complex reasoning. Each question includes separate audio and visual evidence intervals (§3.2; Appendix A.5).
- **Quality-control pipeline:** Expert question construction, video-derived distractors, independent human review, and model-assisted text-only and unimodal screening aim to reduce shortcuts (§3.4; Appendices A.2–A.4).
- **Joint answering and grounding metrics:** Coverage Grounded Score (C.G.) multiplies answer correctness by average evidence-segment hit rate; any positive-duration overlap counts. Bottleneck Grounded Score (B.G.) multiplies correctness by the lower audio/visual temporal IoU, computed over interval unions (§3.5).
- **Comparative evaluation and assets:** The authors evaluate open-weight and proprietary models and report releasing the dataset, annotations, and evaluation framework (§§4.1, 5). No new model or training method is introduced.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best evaluated model still trails human grounding substantially | Across 1,027 questions, Gemini3.7-Flash leads 24 models with **79.4% Acc., 70.8% C.G., 35.1% B.G.**; human reference: **96.8%, 96.0%, 94.3%**, respectively. Human viewing conditions differ. | Table 2; §§4.1–4.2; Appendix B.2 |
| Strongest evaluated open-weight model trails the proprietary leader | MiMo-V2.5-310B-A15B achieves **56.8% Acc., 48.4% C.G., 20.5% B.G.** overall under the common preprocessing protocol. | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Complex reasoning separates answering from localization | Gemini3.7-Flash moves from Level 2 to Level 3 at **73.6% → 79.1% Acc.**, while C.G. falls **65.3% → 49.1%** and B.G. **29.2% → 12.3%**. These are different question sets. | Table 2; §4.3 |
| Native audio adds value beyond transcripts in the tested model | MiMo-V2.5 Video+Audio scores **56.8%/48.4%/20.5%** Acc./C.G./B.G., versus **48.7%/38.1%/11.6%** with Video+ASR. | Table 4; §4.4 |
| EvaOmni has lower restricted-input diagnostic rates | Intersection of correct answers across two models, each run twice: EvaOmni **1.8% text-only, 1.0% audio-only, 3.6% video-only**, versus WorldSense **14.4%, 14.2%, 15.7%**, respectively. | Table 6; Appendix D.1 |
| Correct answers frequently lack threshold-passing grounding | For Gemini3.7-Flash, **41.8% of all questions** have both a correct answer and audio/visual IoUs each ≥0.5; **38.2%** have a correct answer but fail that grounding criterion. | Table 5; Appendix D.3 |

The results establish that answer correctness and temporal evidence localization measure different aspects of performance. They do **not** establish that correctly reported intervals were causally used to produce an answer, or that failed grounding means the answer was guessed. Restricted-input results are diagnostic indicators, not measured proportions of flawed questions (Appendices D.1, D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Selected short-to-medium videos exclude hard-coded subtitles and favor varied events. Generalization to longer videos, sparse scenes, subtitle-rich material, and broader linguistic or cultural contexts is unestablished (Appendix E.1).
- **Author-stated metric limits:** C.G. does not directly penalize overly broad intervals. Both principal grounding metrics incorporate answer correctness, so neither independently isolates localization quality (§3.5).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Humans can pause and replay; their scores are a reference under different viewing conditions. Contamination screening is explicitly heuristic (Appendices B.2, A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed frame sampling may affect localization, but no sampling-rate ablation is reported (§4.2). Screening against selected models also leaves shortcut robustness to other models unresolved (Appendix A.4).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** §4.4 gives a conditional grounding-failure range of 48.1%–79.9%, whereas Appendix D.3 reports 47.8%–79.8%. Table 7 also contains an apparent aggregation inconsistency: Qwen2.5-Omni has nonzero Level 1 Thr@0.5 but zero overall Thr@0.5.

## Open research questions
- **Does reported evidence causally support answers?** Temporal overlap alone cannot establish this (Appendix D.3). Next experiment: remove or replace reported evidence and measure answer changes against matched control intervals.
- **How much does input resolution constrain grounding?** Only one frame-sampling configuration is evaluated (§4.2). Next experiment: compare uniform sampling rates and adaptive sampling under controlled input budgets.
- **Do findings persist without answer options or on broader video distributions?** These settings remain future work (Appendix E.2). Next experiment: annotate matched open-ended questions and longer, subtitle-rich, or sparse-event videos, then reassess answer–grounding disagreement.
