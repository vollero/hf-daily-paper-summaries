# Self-Credit: Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon Agentic RL via Intra-group Rescaling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28388) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28388)


## One-sentence takeaway
Self-Credit uses policy-generated turn citations and comparisons across successful and failed rollouts to reweight RL advantages, improving judged answer accuracy over evaluated baselines on long-horizon search tasks (Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Outcome-only RL assigns the same advantage to every turn, providing weak guidance about which actions helped a long interaction succeed. Context growth further complicates training. The authors identify a gap between costly external credit supervision and existing approaches that use compaction or rollout comparisons without combining both successful and failed trajectories with self-generated citations (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Self-generated credit:** The policy compresses its history into summaries citing important turns; an auxiliary final summary covers the last segment (§3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Intra-group rescaling:** Retrieved evidence matches actions across rollouts for the same query. Repeated success strengthens positive credit, repeated failure strengthens negative credit, and mixed outcomes remove extra positive credit (§3.2; Appendix B.2).
- **Advantage reweighting:** Rescaled weights multiply the trajectory advantage for tokens in cited turns. Failure penalties weaken during training (§§3.3–3.4).

The central contribution is this combined credit-assignment procedure, rather than compaction or group-based RL alone (Table 1).

These results support better performance in the evaluated search settings and show that citations and failure rescaling contribute to the gains. They do not establish causal correctness of individual credit assignments or general effectiveness beyond retrieval-based agents. The meaning of the reported “±” values is not specified.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated approximation:** Turns are treated as contributing independently, although opening a document depends on earlier search and accumulated evidence (Appendix B.2).
- **Scope boundary:** Matching requires task-specific observation rules. Extension to other environments is proposed but not evaluated (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Gold-document overlap and LLM relevance judgments are imperfect proxies for action importance; the authors acknowledge that exploratory turns can help without retrieving gold documents (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Different matching procedures for GiGPO and Self-Credit leave the contribution of matching choice unresolved (§4.2; Appendices B.2, C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No isolated ablation tests failure-weight annealing or sensitivity to matching thresholds and group size. Accuracy and turn counts also do not establish wall-clock or compute efficiency (§3.4; Tables 2, 4; Appendix C).

## Open research questions
1. **Does credit remain reliable when actions depend strongly on earlier steps?** Independence is explicitly approximate. Compare turn-level matching with dependency-aware credit on tasks with controlled action chains.
2. **How sensitive are gains to matching rules and rollout diversity?** The paper uses fixed matching thresholds and group size. Sweep both while measuring accuracy, rule firing, and citation quality.
3. **Does failure-penalty annealing improve exploration?** Its motivation is described without an isolated test. Compare constant, annealed, and reversed schedules under equal training budgets.
4. **Can the method transfer beyond retrieval?** Other environments are discussed without experiments. Evaluate a non-search task with a defined observation-matching function.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and benchmark results; **medium** for broader credit-assignment interpretations.

Material missing information includes code/checkpoint release status, the definition of “±,” deep-research evaluation sample counts and judge identity, statistical significance tests, and measured training overhead or wall-clock comparisons.
