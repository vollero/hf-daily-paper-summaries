# PhysLDM: Latent Diffusion for High-Fidelity Deformable Simulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07609) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07609)


## One-sentence takeaway
PhysLDM combines holistic trajectory compression with latent diffusion to improve deformable-body prediction over matched regression and autoregressive baselines, although its demonstrated generalization remains strongly dependent on the training setting (§§3–4, Appendix M).

## Problem and motivation
High-resolution volumetric meshes make long-horizon neural simulation expensive: autoregressive prediction accumulates errors, while predicting every vertex across an entire trajectory requires substantial memory. The authors identify two gaps: suitable spatiotemporal compression for deformable meshes, and controlled comparisons between deterministic regression and diffusion (§1).

Their motivation for diffusion is that microscopic solver differences can lead to distinct post-contact outcomes; averaging these futures can produce implausibly damped motion (§4, Appendix D).

## Approach and contributions
- **Holistic ST-VAE:** Separates rigid motion from canonical deformation, compresses time using learned queries over the full trajectory, and gives rigid motion a dedicated pathway. This targets temporal “hold-and-snap” artifacts and loss of fine deformation detail (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Conditional latent diffusion:** A rectified-flow diffusion transformer generates the complete trajectory latent from initial geometry, pose, velocity, and stiffness; frozen decoders recover mesh trajectories. Training uses geometric supervision without an explicit constitutive law (§§3.2–3.3, Appendix A).
- **Controlled predictive comparison:** Diffusion and capacity-matched one-shot regression share the same ST-VAE and conditioning, helping isolate the predictive paradigm; an MGN-style autoregressive model provides another baseline (§4, Appendix A.3).
- **Differentiable applications:** Demonstrates stiffness estimation and second-order optimization of release velocity, including replay validation in the source simulator (§4.2, Appendix K).

The authors claim priority for this high-fidelity volumetric ST-VAE and latent-diffusion formulation; the supplied text alone cannot independently establish priority.

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate compressed reconstruction | Held-out mean rigid-free deformation error **2.48 mm**, approximately half the standard-compression baseline; token compression reaches **78×** at **5,000 vertices**. Reconstruction comparisons use 300 scenes per dataset and resolution group. | §4 reconstruction results; Table 1 caption; Appendix B.2–B.3 |
| Better temporal reconstruction | Holistic ST-VAE achieves **DCC >0.93** across evaluation sets; DCC measures correlation of deformation-change timing and is amplitude-invariant. | §4 reconstruction results; Appendix B.3 |
| Diffusion improves prediction checks | For held-out 64-frame predictions, volume violations are **7.9%** for diffusion versus **22.7%** for matched regression. Evaluations use 300 scenes per set, one diffusion sample, and 50 Euler steps; reported prediction metrics are medians across scenes. | §4 prediction results; Table 2 caption; Appendix B.2 |
| Lower error than the AR baseline | Held-out world-coordinate error is **28.2 mm** for diffusion versus **80.6 mm** for MGN-style AR, averaged over vertices and frames per scene, then median across scenes. | §4 prediction results; Appendix B.4 |
| Identical solver inputs can diverge | Across 300 held-out scenes rerun twice, **23.7%** exceed final-frame divergence of **1% of object size**, and **7.7%** exceed **5%**. | §4 chaos analysis, referring to Table 5 |
| Latents improve memory scaling | At 64 frames, bf16, batch 1 on a **140 GiB H200**, raw-token diffusion runs out of memory at **2,600 vertices**; ST-VAE Base supports approximately **14,000**. Latent LDM training uses approximately **4.4 GiB per trajectory**, excluding the separate ST-VAE stage. | §4.1; Appendix F.1 |
| Fast trajectory generation | Approximately **40–80 fps** in bf16 on one H200 for batch-1, 64-frame trajectories, tested on five scenes with **980–9,851 vertices**, using 20 or 50 Euler steps. | Appendix G |
| Differentiable estimation and design work in the tested task | Seven stiffness targets spanning **3×10⁴–3×10⁶ Pa** are recovered within **0.06 dex** in **40 Adam steps**, approximately **40 seconds** each. Throw optimization takes **30 steps / 7 minutes** and yields approximately **13×** rigid-motion sensitivity in simulator replay. | §4.2; Appendix K |
| Representation transfer exceeds prediction transfer | On 40 simulated scenes, median ST-VAE reconstruction error is **4.5 mm** for training-family material versus **4.6 mm** for ARAP; prediction error increases from **23.9 to 37.5 mm**. On 22 real captured sequences, reconstruction is **6.0 mm**, but zero-shot prediction error is **875 mm**. | Appendix M.1–M.2 |
| Fine-tuning fits new trajectory sources | Real-point-cloud error falls from **921 to 6.2 mm**, measured on the fine-tuning subset rather than an established independent test set. | Appendix M.3; Table 17 caption |

These results support compression benefits and improved prediction relative to the tested baselines. They do not establish exact physical correctness or calibrated probabilities over possible futures. The reported multisample analysis supports greater diffusion diversity in chaotic scenes, but its detailed evidence is partly truncated. Most table bodies are absent, limiting verification of complete rankings and dataset-specific results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated horizon limit:** The model currently predicts 64-frame trajectories; extending it through autoregressive operation remains future work (§5).
- **Author-stated capacity failure:** Thin, independently deforming features can overwhelm the fixed latent. A birdcage reconstruction has **9.9 mm** mean error and distorted, tangled bars (Appendix I, Fig. 12).
- **Observed domain dependence:** Geometry OOD tests retain the same simulation setting. ARAP changes degrade prediction, and real manipulation produces severe zero-shot failure (Appendices B.1 and M).
- **Analyst inference—adaptation validity:** Fine-tuning-subset results demonstrate fitting capability but do not establish generalization to unseen manipulated trajectories, especially with unobserved hand forces (Appendix M).
- **Analyst inference—limited downstream breadth:** Appendix K describes both differentiable tasks on the same scene, despite §4.2 referring to seven bodies; the breadth of geometry evaluation is therefore ambiguous.
- **Coverage and reproducibility:** The source is explicitly truncated. Missing table values, portions of baseline descriptions, and unspecified release availability prevent complete assessment. Full training also requires approximately seven days on eight H200 GPUs (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
1. **Can trajectory blocks extend the horizon without drift?** The authors identify this as future work (§5). Test chained predictions against longer solver trajectories, measuring contact failures and accumulated error.
2. **Does diffusion capture outcome probabilities accurately?** Reported sample spread follows solver divergence, but calibration is not established in the supplied evidence (§4). Compare many model samples with repeated solver outcomes under controlled perturbations.
3. **Can adaptive latent capacity preserve thin structures?** Fixed capacity loses individual bar detail (Appendix I). Vary token allocation by geometric complexity and compare reconstruction, intersection rates, and memory.
4. **Does adaptation generalize to new real interactions?** Current results are measured on fine-tuning subsets with unobserved hand forces (Appendix M). Hold out objects and manipulation sequences, and compare prediction with and without observed interaction conditioning.
