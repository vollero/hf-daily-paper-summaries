# HRoPE: Homography Relative Positional Encoding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28284) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28284)


## One-sentence takeaway
HRoPE uses learned per-patch, plane-structured transformations to condition multi-view attention, improving reported novel-view synthesis results while leaving physical cross-view consistency unguaranteed (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-view transformers need geometric information to associate image patches across viewpoints. The authors argue that camera- and ray-based positional encodings omit local surface orientation, which determines how a patch’s neighborhood deforms under perspective changes. They propose local surface planes as a stronger prior for comparing these neighborhoods (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Surface-aware attention:** Each observed patch receives a structured transform \(H=K(R+\kappa tn^\top)\). Queries and keys are transformed so their comparison contains the relative factor \(H_iH_j^{-1}\); values and outputs are also transformed. Remaining channels retain image-position rotary encoding (§3.2; Appendices A.3–A.4).
- **Learned geometric parameterization:** A regression head predicts patch-specific rotation, translation, normal, and inverse-distance parameters from scratch or pretrained features. These are latent parameters optimized for synthesis, rather than guaranteed physical geometry. The structured inverse uses the Sherman–Morrison identity (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Calibrated alternative and evaluation:** A separate variant combines known camera projections with predicted local plane frames. Experiments test encoding structure, feature sources, geometry quality, viewpoint shifts, diffusion synthesis, and spatial video reasoning (§§3.3–4.2; Appendix A.6).

## Evidence and main results

All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better synthesis than PRoPE | VGGT-based HRoPE gains **0.53 / 1.27 / 1.03 dB PSNR** on **CO3Dv2 / RealEstate10K / Objaverse**, respectively, using two-reference-view LVSM; SSIM and LPIPS also improve. | §4.1, narrative referring to Table 1 |
| Gains extend to more input views | On DL3DV with the larger LVSM, HRoPE beats PRoPE with **2, 4, and 8 reference views**; PSNR and SSIM gains are largest with two views. Exact scores are unavailable. | §4.1, Table 2 discussion |
| Local frames help with fixed calibrated projections | On RealEstate10K, PRoPE → calibrated \(P\cdot F\): **PSNR 26.142 → 27.115**, **SSIM 0.826 → 0.840**, **LPIPS 0.111 → 0.104**. | §4.2.2, Table 3 discussion |
| Oracle geometry offers further improvement | On ASE, replacing VGGT-predicted parameters with ground-truth geometry raises HRoPE PSNR **28.555 → 28.751**; SSIM and LPIPS also improve. | §4.2.2, Table 3 discussion |
| Structure matters beyond feature access | Homography encoding has the highest PSNR among tested transformation structures, with scratch and frozen-VGGT predictors; it also beats additive encoding from the same VGGT features. Exact values are absent. | §4.2.1, Fig. 4 discussion |
| Reported robustness under viewpoint shifts | RealEstate10K tests cover target focal lengths **1×–5×**, target sampling extended **50% at both ends**, and reference-frame gaps shifted from **[25,100] to [75,150]**. HRoPE retains reported advantages; exact curves are unavailable. | §4.2.3, Fig. 6 |
| Pretrained features add cost | Under the same RealEstate10K training schedule, DA3-based HRoPE reaches **27.137 PSNR in 48 GPU-hours**, versus VGGT’s **27.410 in 76 GPU-hours**. | Appendix A.5, Table 4 discussion |
| Spatial reasoning improves overall but regresses temporally | On VSI-Bench, the same fine-tuned LLaVA-Video backbone and **32-frame** budget yield average **57.7 → 59.5** with HRoPE; appearance-order score falls **38.5 → 31.6**. | Appendix A.6, Table 6 |

The evidence supports benefits within the evaluated architectures and protocols, including a calibrated comparison that helps isolate local geometry. It does not establish universally correct correspondence or gains on every task. EscherNet improvements are reported across evaluated view counts, but Table 5’s numerical cells are missing from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Independently predicted planes need not encode the same physical surface identically across views; correspondence has no formal guarantee (§5).
- **Geometric scope:** Relative transforms recover a physical homography for a shared plane with consistent geometry. For patches on different planes, they need not represent a physical image warp (§3.2). Appendix A.2 also restricts world-frame invariance claims for geometry-derived transforms.
- **Observed tradeoff:** Appearance-order performance declines. The authors attribute this to allocating visual attention dimensions to geometry, but no allocation ablation establishes that explanation (Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Feature-source ablations support a structural benefit, but headline VGGT-based gains should be considered alongside additional feature extraction cost (§4.2.1; Appendix A.5).
- **Evidence availability:** Most quantitative tables and plotted values are absent. The supplied text provides no repeated-run uncertainty, preventing assessment of statistical reliability.

## Open research questions

1. **Can cross-view consistency be enforced without reducing synthesis quality?** The authors leave this guarantee unresolved (§5). Compare independent predictions against shared-plane constraints or cross-view consistency losses.
2. **Where does the local-plane approximation fail?** Physical interpretation depends on planarity; ASE already uses a fallback for poorly fitted patches (Appendix A.5). Stratify synthesis and correspondence errors by plane-fit residual and depth-boundary proximity.
3. **Can geometry preserve temporal reasoning?** Appearance order regresses, while the proposed explanation remains untested (Appendix A.6). Vary geometric channel allocation and layer placement under identical training and frame budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several results are explicit, but material gaps include most table cells, numerical ablation and robustness curves, run-to-run uncertainty, inference latency and memory, quantitative cross-view plane consistency, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
