# Painting Change Map as guidance for generating painting processes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25369) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25369)


## One-sentence takeaway
Painting Change Maps combine local change timing with lineart to guide painting-video synthesis, delivering strong oracle-control results while generated controls remain broadly competitive with Loomis Painter (§§4.2–4.6).

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses reconstructing and controlling how a finished painting develops over time. The authors argue that object masks, global progress scalars, and fixed frame schedules inadequately specify both **where** changes occur and **when** they happen, limiting control over regional ordering and pacing (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Painting Change Map (PCM):** Threshold adjacent-frame RGB differences, filter frames with excessive global change, and record each pixel’s most recent modification time. This captures spatial and temporal activity without explicitly modeling paint physics (§§3.1–3.3).
- **RGB control encodings:** Compare grayscale interpolation, 24-bit packing, recent-change history, and **Interpolation & Lineart**, which stores temporal interpolation in red and structural outlines in green and blue (§3.3; §7.1).
- **Two-stage synthesis:** Predict control maps from a finished painting using a 5B image-to-video model, then condition a 1.3B video-to-video model on those maps and the painting to generate an unpainting sequence (§4.6).
- **Control evaluation:** Compare structural controls and painting-generation methods, including localized unpainting without localized-task training (§§4.1–4.6).

## Evidence and main results

Evaluation uses 132 training, 16 test, and 13 validation acrylic-painting videos; reported results use the test split. Models generate 81-frame sequences, with training sequences sampled from 1,000 frames per source video (§4).

These results support the hybrid representation’s utility when accurate controls are supplied. The generated-control pipeline is competitive, but its weaker perceptual and semantic scores than Loomis Painter qualify broad superiority claims. **Analyst inference:** the oracle/generated-control gap identifies control prediction as a promising improvement target; it does not isolate the cause experimentally.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Canvas motion causes accumulated changes to drift from current geometry despite filtering; alignment or keypoint rectification remains future work (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Limited dataset diversity constrains investigation of whether maps encode medium, style, or painter identity (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative evidence from a small acrylic test split does not establish generalization across media; unseen-artwork demonstrations are qualitative (§§4, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Localized evaluation masks out background regions, so its metrics do not directly establish background preservation (§4.3).
- **Evidence boundary:** The representation accommodates forward and reverse processes, but reported training and evaluation use reverse chronology (§§3.1, 4).
- **Evidence boundary:** Claimed benefits from randomized temporal sampling lack an isolated sampling ablation in the supplied text (§4.6).

## Open research questions

1. **How can predicted controls close the oracle gap?** Table 3 shows a substantial difference. Compare map predictors while holding the rendering model fixed.
2. **Does canvas alignment improve PCM reliability?** Residual drift remains an explicit limitation (§5). Compare aligned and unaligned preprocessing under identical training.
3. **How reliably does localized control preserve untouched regions?** Current quantitative metrics cover target regions (§4.3). Measure background changes alongside target reconstruction.
4. **Does PCM transfer across media and generation direction?** Quantitative evaluation covers acrylic unpainting (§4). Test held-out media and separately trained forward-generation models.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and Table 3 comparisons are explicit, but Tables 1 and 2 lack numerical bodies. Other material gaps include uncertainty estimates, detailed baseline inference settings, video resolution, localized-mask construction, map-predictor training details, and independent verification of announced code or released weights.
