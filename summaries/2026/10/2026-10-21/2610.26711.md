# PVBench: Quantifying Physical Plausibility in Video Generation with Object-Level Physical Measurements

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26711) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26711)


## One-sentence takeaway
PVBench evaluates generated videos using object-level measurements and physical-law checks, reporting better human agreement than direct MLLM scoring while exposing substantial weaknesses in mechanics and conservation.

## Problem and motivation
Visually convincing videos can still contain impossible geometry, disappearing objects, or inconsistent motion. The authors argue that existing evaluations emphasize appearance and semantic alignment, or use coarse physical judgments that cannot identify the violated quantity or quantify its severity. PVBench targets this gap with measurements relevant to physically usable video, including scale, pose, trajectories, and conservation residuals (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation taxonomy:** 46 dimensions across semantic, perceptual, and physical layers; the physical layer contains 25 dimensions covering geometry, materials/fields, kinematics/persistence, and mechanics/conservation. T2V evaluates 45 dimensions because reference-image fidelity applies only to I2V (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Paired evaluation scenes:** 600 prompt groups with corresponding T2V prompts and synthesized I2V reference images; 180 groups involve embodied agents. Prompts specify target dimensions and observability requirements (§3.2; Table A16).
- **Measurement-based protocol:** Specialized models estimate depth, normals, object geometry, pose, and trajectories. These support consistency checks against prompt constraints and physical laws; hard-to-measure dimensions retain MLLM judgment. The authors report 17 numerical, four hybrid, and four pure-MLLM physical dimensions. Measurements map to five-point scores, aggregated equally across dimensions and reported on a percentile scale (§3.3; Appendix D).

The core contribution is the evaluation framework and curated benchmark, rather than new perception models or a new video generator.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Aggregate human alignment is substantial, but uneven | Across three T2V models and 375 dimension–video pairs, overall Spearman ρ = **0.772**, Kendall τ = **0.643**; ten annotators score each sample, with ICC(2,k) = **0.89**. | §4.3; Tables 4, A4 |
| Some individual metrics align poorly with humans | Object permanence has Spearman ρ = **0.125** and motion continuity **0.240**, each over 15 paired samples across three T2V models. | Table A4 |

| Difficult scenes usually reduce physical scores | Five of six models decline in each modality. For example, LingBot T2V falls **39.1 → 33.6**, while Seedance T2V improves **47.7 → 50.0** on the combined embodied/confounder subset. | Table 3; §4.3 |
| I2V does not uniformly improve physical performance | MiniMax H3 improves perceptual quality **55.4 → 62.5**, while mechanics/conservation falls **25.3 → 20.1** under paired T2V/I2V evaluation. | Table 5 |

These results support the authors’ claim that physical measurements provide useful evaluation signals and reveal weaknesses concealed by appearance-oriented scores. They do not establish accurate recovery of true physical states, uniform reliability across dimensions, or downstream simulation/robotics utility. Cross-layer score differences also depend on separately defined scoring scales.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ limitations:** Dependence on specialized perception models; skipped unobservable samples; continued MLLM scoring for some phenomena; limited ground-truth references; no full ablation of specialized evaluators (Conclusion).
- **Analyst inference—measurement validity:** Tracking, reconstruction, and scale errors can resemble generator failures. Weak correlations for persistence metrics show that aggregate human agreement cannot validate every dimension (Table A4; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—selection bias:** Excluding skipped/error cases and moderation failures may alter model comparisons; missingness is not uniform across configurations (Appendix C; Table A5).
- **Analyst inference—calibration/generalization:** Thresholds are manually chosen, but separation between calibration and human-validation samples is unclear. Human validation covers only three T2V models, without reported uncertainty intervals (§§3.3–3.4, 4.3–4.4).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Table 8 shows identical trajectory-area coverage across tested thresholds, despite prose claiming coverage differences. Appendix A13 also shows perceptual and behavioral declines for several I2V models, limiting the broad claim that first-frame conditioning mainly improves those layers.
- **Metric ambiguity:** R15 describes displacement magnitude but maps smaller values to higher scores without fully specifying the expected-motion residual; free-rotation and deformation checks likewise require careful scene-dependent interpretation (Appendix D).

## Open research questions
1. **How much error comes from the evaluator?** Most dimensions lack ground truth. A next experiment could render simulated scenes with known states and controlled violations, then measure state-estimation and scoring error separately.
2. **Can persistence metrics become reliable?** Their human correlations are weak. Replace tracking/segmentation components and test controlled occlusions and reappearances against annotated object identities (Table A4).
3. **Do thresholds generalize beyond calibration?** Independence is unclear. Freeze thresholds and evaluate unseen generators, I2V samples, and held-out scene categories with uncertainty intervals.
4. **Which scene factors cause degradation?** The difficult subset combines embodiment, clutter, occlusion, and camera motion. Matched scenes varying one factor at a time could separate generator sensitivity from evaluator sensitivity (§4.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed scoring descriptions and results, but leaves material uncertainty about:

- Exact samples and human-rating protocol used for Table 7.
- Calibration/validation separation and statistical uncertainty.
- Full evaluator ablations and ground-truth measurement accuracy.
- Exact released artifact status, model revisions, runtime, and GPU hours.
- Effects of missing evaluations and repeated generation seeds on rankings.
