# Training the Whole Agent, Not Just the Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28069) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28069)


## One-sentence takeaway
HarnessAdapt-RL improves software-repair agents by adapting training guidance and runtime tools alongside reinforcement learning, with reported gains requiring retention of the evolved harness at deployment.

## Problem and motivation
A coding agent’s harness shapes its actions, feedback, and training trajectories, yet policy training commonly keeps that harness fixed. The authors identify two changing needs: task-specific repair mistakes and recurring interaction problems such as failed edits or insufficient verification. Their gap is how to adapt support during reinforcement learning and determine which components must survive deployment (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Repair guidance:** A frozen teacher compares task trajectories and generates mechanism-level skills explaining a repair strategy without paths, symbols, or code. These skills are training-only; comparisons test them against near-answer hints and a general skill bank (§3.1).
- **Training-aware harness evolution:** A separate teacher proposes and implements tools and hooks from current-policy trajectories. Candidates are retained only when observed pass rate or trainable rate improves without either declining. Fresh rollouts then drive GRPO updates (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Deployment and reuse analysis:** Experiments separate persistent learning from temporary guidance, test whether evolved tools remain necessary, and examine reuse of an already discovered harness in another training run (§§4.5–4.6).

The distinctive contribution is this combination and its controlled comparisons; the paper does not establish invention of skills, harness optimization, or GRPO individually (§5).

## Evidence and main results

All benchmark results below use evaluation without skills or teachers, averaging three evaluations of a fixed checkpoint (§§4.1, 8.4). Benchmark order is **Verified / Multilingual / Pro**; “pp” means percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full method improves over fixed-harness GRPO | Mean benchmark pass@1: **44.9% vs 35.3%**; respective benchmark gains **9.8 / 12.6 / 6.4 pp**, using the same 9B base model and each agent’s deployment harness | §4.2, Table 1 discussion |
| Advantage extends to a human-designed harness baseline | Pass@1 gains over OpenHands-GRPO: **6.2 / 4.0 / 7.4 pp** | §4.2 |
| Mechanism-level guidance transfers best after removal | Under the same base harness, average pass@1 is **38.3%** for mechanism skills, **32.7%** for near-answer hints, and **34.1%** for skill-bank guidance | §4.2 |
| Updating the harness during learning beats the tested fixed alternatives | Gains over Pre-Evolved: **2.4 / 4.3 / 2.7 pp**; over keeping first-epoch H₁ fixed: **2.2 / 4.6 / 2.0 pp** | §4.2 |
| Evidence for second-epoch evolution is stronger pooled than per benchmark | Versus H₁+Mechanism, task-weighted pooled gain is **2.6 pp**, **95% CI [0.9, 4.3]**; individual intervals include zero on Verified and Pro | §9.4 |
| Deployment tools contribute independently of policy weights | Replacing H₂ with H₀ lowers HarnessAdapt-RL pass@1 by **4.8 / 7.7 / 4.7 pp**, holding weights fixed | §4.5, Table 2 discussion |
| Discovered harness can be reused | Frozen-H₂+Mechanism reaches **56.8% / 38.3% / 38.9%** pass@1, within **1.0 pp** of the full method on each benchmark; discovery cost is inherited | §4.6, Figure 4 |
| Training comparisons are not compute-matched | Full method collects **18,056 total rollouts**, including **8,464 policy-training rollouts**; GRPO collects **16,928 policy-training rollouts**. Teacher costs are additional | §9.7 |

These results support the authors’ claim that both learned weights and runtime support contribute to the evaluated agent. They also show that an already discovered harness can largely reproduce the full method’s scores without repeated evolution. They do **not** establish robustness across training seeds, equal-compute superiority, or the individual contribution of each tool and hook.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only software repair and one 9B base model are tested; transfer to other domains, scales, and model families remains unknown (Limitations).
- **Author-stated variability:** Each configuration is trained once. Evaluation repetitions and task-bootstrap intervals do not measure training-seed or harness-search variability (§8.4; Limitations).
- **Author-stated search uncertainty:** Candidate selection uses finite observed rates without significance testing; noise and repeated search can influence acceptance (§3.3; Limitations).
- **Author-stated attribution limits:** Candidate bundles combine components, preventing individual-effect attribution. Guidance-gating comparisons also change both coverage and trajectory evidence (§§9.5, 9.8).
- **Reproducibility:** Teacher identifiers do not pin immutable snapshots, and historical CLI versions were not preserved (§8.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Unequal rollout allocation, additional teacher calls, and missing comparable compute totals prevent a firm efficiency conclusion (§9.7). Shorter trajectories and mixed reward groups are descriptive evidence, not isolated causal explanations (§§9.1–9.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How reproducible is harness discovery?** One search and training run per configuration leaves variability unresolved. Repeat complete searches and training across seeds, reporting both scores and accepted components.
2. **Does trainable-rate selection improve final learning?** The reported search uses the joint rule without isolating its contribution. Compare pass-only, joint-rate, and uncertainty-aware selection under matched search budgets.
3. **Which harness components drive gains?** Bundle comparisons cannot identify individual effects. Ablate automatic testing, recovery tools, and feedback hooks separately during training and deployment.
4. **How broadly do the findings transfer?** Evidence covers one model and repair setting. Repeat the guidance and harness comparisons across model sizes and a distinct agent domain, measuring performance and total cost.
