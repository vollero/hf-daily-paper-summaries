# Not All Features Are Created Equal: A Mechanistic Study of Vision-Language-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.19233) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19233)


## One-sentence takeaway

Cross-task activation replay provides strong evidence that tested VLA action pathways can reproduce spatially anchored trajectories despite conflicting observations and instructions, although the precise coordinate frame and generality beyond the tested checkpoints remain unresolved (§§4.3, 4.7; Appendix B).

## Problem and motivation

The authors ask whether fine-tuned vision-language-action models identify an instructed object or execute a trajectory associated with its familiar location. This distinction matters for diagnosing failures under scene changes: high benchmark success alone does not reveal which computation supports the behavior.

The identified gap is that prior single-feature interventions, usually holding the task fixed, cannot separate object identity from its correlated training location. The proposed test instead transfers recorded computation between different tasks and scenes (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Cross-task causal intervention:** Record activations during a successful source rollout, then replace selected residual-stream activations throughout a destination rollout with a different scene and instruction. Counterfactual prompts, pathway interventions, random-direction controls, and trajectory comparisons help interpret the resulting behavior (§§3.2–3.3, 4.3).
- **Comparative mechanistic analysis:** Apply a common intervention framework to five language-capable VLAs and ACT as a language-free control. The authors investigate language dependence and the separation between goal selection and motor generation (§§3.1, 4.4–4.5).
- **SAE methodology comparison:** Compare per-token, mean-pooled, and temporal-contrastive sparse autoencoders using downstream rollout behavior, revealing architecture- and write-back-dependent reconstruction fidelity (§§3.4, 4.6; Appendix M).
- **Physical replication and assets:** Test replay and object displacement on a UR10e and report releasing Action Atlas, SAE checkpoints, rollout artifacts, and a validation harness (§4.7; Appendices O–P). Asset availability was not independently verified.

The strongest evidence establishes that transferred activation content can causally bias behavior toward source trajectories, including recorded locations on hardware. It does not establish that ordinary, uninjected execution always ignores current observations: the uninjected hardware policy follows displaced objects (Appendix O). Nor does it uniquely identify absolute workspace coordinates, which the authors explicitly leave unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** All cross-architecture evidence is simulated; hardware covers π₀.₅ and one task pair. Visual dominance and pathway specialization were not replicated physically (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated coordinate ambiguity:** Scene-relative coordinates coinciding with source locations remain possible. A rigid transformation of the entire world was not tested (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated intervention confounds:** Replay can create off-manifold or temporally misaligned states. Random-direction and displacement controls counter a simple disruption explanation but do not remove every intervention artifact (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated representation limits:** SAE features need not be disentangled; some identify general motor primitives. Independently trained dictionaries complicate cross-model concept comparisons (Appendices A.3, B).
- **Implementation dependence:** Residual write-back restores mean-pool fidelity in individual OFT, GR00T, and X-VLA cells; no corresponding π₀.₅ result is reported. Hardware replay matches source sampling noise, whereas simulation leaves it uncontrolled (Appendices M, O).
- **Analyst inference:** Broad claims about language use need caution because prompt tests are simple and some large evaluation cells repeat one fixed initialization per task. Episode counts therefore do not equal layout diversity (§4.4; Table 4; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Conditional versus tie-inclusive override, action-space versus Cartesian metrics, and inconsistent OFT metric descriptions (§3.6 versus Appendix K.1) limit clean cross-model ranking.

## Open research questions

1. **Which coordinate frame is replay bound to?** Object displacement does not distinguish absolute from coincident scene-relative coordinates. Apply rigid translations and rotations to the whole simulated scene and compare replayed trajectories in both frames (Appendix B).
2. **Does broader scene diversity weaken binding?** The demonstration-count sweep preserves override but does not isolate diversity. Hold demonstration count fixed while varying object positions and layouts, then repeat injection and uninjected displacement tests (Appendices B, N).
3. **Does pathway specialization generalize physically?** Hardware currently tests one model and task pair. Repeat separate VLM/expert interventions on additional architectures and task pairs, controlling sampling noise (Appendices B, O).
4. **How much of pooling preference comes from write-back rather than architecture?** Residual-write-back evidence covers isolated cells and excludes π₀.₅ mean-pooling. Run a matched pooling-by-write-back comparison across layers and suites (Appendix M).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the principal intervention findings and important qualifications, but the source is explicitly truncated; this analysis does not provide complete paper coverage.

Missing material includes much of the intervention-hook protocol, detailed controls, full numerical table bodies, pooling evaluation aggregation, probe splits, and several appendix analyses. Reported code, checkpoints, supplementary instructions, and videos were not inspected.
