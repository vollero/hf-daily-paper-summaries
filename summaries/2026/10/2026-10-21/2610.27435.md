# Harness Self-Evolution for Long-Horizon Terminal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27435) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27435)


## One-sentence takeaway
RiseHarness improves fixed-model terminal-agent success and repeated-run reliability by diagnosing inconsistent executions with paired agent/harness traces and adapting harness mechanisms, with reported gains on both development and transfer benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation
Long terminal workflows can succeed once yet fail on repeated attempts because local mistakes alter subsequent execution and context. The authors target this reliability gap through the harness rather than model training. Their identified gap is connecting trajectory differences to actual internal harness behavior and determining whether designs borrowed from other frameworks fit the current model, interfaces, and available signals (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evidence-driven diagnosis:** Identify “flipping tasks” with both successful and failed runs under the same configuration. Compare paired runs, then check proposed behavioral needs against internal execution traces and a source-aligned Harness Handbook (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Adapted design reuse:** Search external implementations for relevant mechanisms, assess their requirements, and translate suitable ideas into local harness changes (§3.4).
- **Iterative validation:** Implement candidates, perform configured checks, screen relevant tasks, and retain changes only after complete repeated evaluation exceeds the declared aggregate-success baseline. Model weights remain fixed (§3.5).
- **Model-specific adaptation:** GPT-6-sol changes address session recovery, persisted workspace feedback, and completion context; DeepSeek-V4-Flash changes strengthen completion evidence (Appendix B). The contribution is their integrated evolution procedure; the Handbook and several underlying ideas are credited to prior work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

All changes below are **absolute percentage-point differences**. Pass⁴ measures tasks succeeding in every one of four runs; Pass@1 averages success across trials (§4.1).

|---|---|---|

| DeepSeek development performance improves | Final four-mechanism harness versus Terminus-2 on TBH: Pass@1 **+7.25 pp**, Pass⁴ **+14 pp**, four runs per task. | §4.2; Table 1 caption |
| Frozen final-version transfer improves | DeepSeek’s TBH-selected final +IR harness versus Terminus-2 on TB2.1: Pass@1 **+3.09 pp**, Pass⁴ **+6.74 pp**, four runs per task. | §4.2; Appendix D.2 |
| Harness evidence contributes jointly | Full RiseHarness versus removal of both harness traces and Handbook, DeepSeek on TBH: Pass@1 **+7 pp**, Pass⁴ **+8 pp**, four runs per task. | §4.3 |
| External research contributes | Full RiseHarness versus local-information-only proposals, DeepSeek on TBH: Pass@1 **+5.50 pp**, Pass⁴ **+6 pp**, four runs per task. | §4.3 |
| Adaptation depends on execution model | Both tested models reportedly perform better with their own final harness than with the other model’s harness on both benchmarks; numerical comparison cells are unavailable. | §4.4; Appendix D.4 |

The reported evidence supports improved average success and repeated-run reliability in the tested settings. It does not isolate individual mechanisms’ causal effects or establish broad generalization across frameworks. Most table bodies and plotted values are absent from the supplied text, preventing reconstruction of full baseline comparisons. Frozen final-version transfer provides clearer evidence of generalization than selecting the best version using transfer-benchmark scores.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated coverage limits:** Flipping-task discovery excludes consistently failing tasks and failures before recording. Monitoring covers selected boundaries; the Handbook can be incomplete or stale. Neither establishes causality (Appendix E).
- **Authors’ stated selection risks:** Repeated development-set selection can exploit noise; historical baselines can be affected by service or environment drift. Aggregate-success adoption can conceal regressions on previously stable tasks (Appendices C.2, E).
- **Authors’ stated mechanism limits:** Cumulative evaluations do not isolate individual changes. DeepSeek checks overlap and may execute verification twice; its conflicting-readings branch is largely unreachable. Behavioral checks cover finite cases, and both worked examples report that independent behavioral checks were not run (Appendices A.4, B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The harness-evidence ablation establishes a joint contribution of traces and Handbook, not their separate value. Ablations are reported only for DeepSeek; GPT-6-sol ablations remain unreported (§4.3; Appendix D.1).
- **Authors’ stated scope and cost limits:** Other frameworks need compatible monitoring and adapters. Repeated execution, research, and trace storage incur costs; measured speedup and emissions are unavailable (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
- **Which evidence source matters most?** Traces and Handbook are removed together. A factorial ablation separating them, with matched search budgets, could measure their individual and combined value.
- **Which mechanisms improve reliability without harming stable tasks?** Cumulative scores cannot resolve individual effects or task regressions. Independently toggle mechanisms and report paired per-task transitions under fresh controls.
- **How robust is transfer beyond these settings?** Evaluation centers on one seed harness and two main models. Freeze development-selected versions and test additional frameworks, models, and task distributions without target-score selection.
- **Can evolution cost be reduced safely?** Evaluation cost is acknowledged but not quantified. Compare screening strategies under measured inference, runtime, and storage budgets, followed by independent full evaluation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and major reported deltas are explicit, but numerical verification is limited by missing table bodies and figure data.

Material unavailable information includes complete baseline scores, most trial counts and task-level outcomes, uncertainty estimates, concrete search/evaluation budgets, proposer-model identity, full prompts and implementation records, measured resource costs, and confirmed public artifact availability.
