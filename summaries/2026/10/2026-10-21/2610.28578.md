# Sage-A1.5: From Long-Horizon Execution to Scientific Exploration with Capability Composition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28578) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28578)


## One-sentence takeaway
Sage-A1.5 trains a 35B MoE agent to delegate parallel work and reassess evidence during execution, reporting improved search and scientific-task performance while demonstrating computational research workflows rather than experimentally validated discovery.

## Problem and motivation
Long-running agents can accumulate context, repeat ineffective searches, and propagate unsupported assumptions. The authors argue that domain expertise alone is insufficient: decomposition, coordination, and evidence checking should be learned execution capabilities rather than depend entirely on fixed external scaffolds (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Learned orchestration:** The agent delegates alternative investigations or complementary subtasks to subagents with isolated contexts, then integrates structured answers, evidence, status assessments, and unresolved issues. Main-agent and subagent trajectories receive separate supervision (§2.1.1).
- **Learned verification:** Training trajectories incorporate supervisor-triggered evidence reviews and subsequent corrective actions; supervisory control messages are then removed. At inference, a conditioning marker enables autonomous verification within ordinary reasoning and tool use (§2.1.2).
- **Unified post-training:** Multi-domain, multi-harness SFT precedes student-rollout training combining outcome RL, teacher token-distribution feedback, or both. Supporting pipelines cover engineering, science, search, professional workflows, and recursively evolved long-context tasks (§§2.2–2.6).
- **Scientific workflow demonstrations:** Case studies provide computational analyses, executable workflows, and inspectable artifacts. These demonstrate application breadth; they do not independently establish general autonomous discovery (§4).

The evidence supports broad task competence and configuration-specific benefits from orchestration and verification. It does not isolate every training component’s contribution, establish statistical reliability for most gains, or demonstrate discovery validated through new physical experiments. Baseline comparisons mix original-report results with author-run evaluations when results were unavailable (Table 1 caption).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Composition training remains concentrated on search and research synthesis; very long executions retain heavy context overhead; joint training loses some domain-teacher performance; scientific showcases use computation and historical replay (§5).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The supplied experiments do not provide a factorial ablation separating orchestration training, verification training, supervision mixture, and additional inference computation. Table 5 tests verification on a teacher, leaving its isolated final-model benefit unclear.
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Most benchmark results lack uncertainty estimates. Search evaluation permits restarting from scratch after context exhaustion or excessive tool calls, so reported performance should be read with that execution policy (§3.1).
- **Case-study boundaries:** Electrochemistry uses historical conditions and explicitly does not establish proposed mechanisms causally. The computational-chemistry human-time comparison is an estimate, not a measured controlled study (§4.4).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** ALE-CLI is reported as **19.1%** in Table 1 and **19.0%** in prose. BrowseComp’s **82.8%** headline result uses orchestration, whereas Table 1 reports **81.7%** without it (Tables 1, 4; pp. 21–22).

## Open research questions
1. **Do composition skills transfer to engineering?** Training remains search-centered (§5). A next experiment could compare learned orchestration against single-agent and fixed-delegation systems on held-out engineering workflows under matched compute budgets.
2. **Which components drive the gains?** Existing comparisons do not separate the full training package. A factorial study could independently vary orchestration, verification, and teacher feedback while fixing task data and inference budgets.
3. **Can unified training preserve specialist performance?** Teacher-to-final-model gaps remain (Tables 1–2; §5). Varying domain sampling and outcome/process weights could measure retention alongside cross-domain interference.
4. **Does hypothesis revision improve prospective experiments?** The electrochemistry showcase is retrospective (§4.4). A prospective study could compare agent-selected conditions with predefined selection baselines using newly measured yields.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the mechanisms and reported results, but leaves material gaps: complete training-mixture sizes, numerical optimization settings and compute costs, most result uncertainties, compute-matched ablations, final-model verification isolation, and release/access details for supporting datasets, code, and case-study artifacts.
