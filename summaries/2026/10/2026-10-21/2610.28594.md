# Learning the 4D World through Point Trajectory Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28594) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28594)


## One-sentence takeaway
VPM jointly forecasts multi-view video and persistent 3D point motion, with controlled comparisons reporting better visual fidelity, geometric accuracy, and robot control from point supervision, although most numerical result tables are absent from the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation
Plausible videos from different cameras can depict incompatible futures. The authors investigate whether forecasting persistent physical points provides a shared motion representation that makes multi-view predictions more coherent. Unlike tracking within observed footage, this requires predicting correspondences beyond the available frames (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint video–trajectory forecasting:** VPM extends Cosmos3-Nano with shared multi-view token processing, Plücker-ray camera embeddings, and a view axis in rotary positional encoding. An iterative trajectory head predicts point positions, depths, and confidence from intermediate generator features. Trajectory and correspondence losses supervise the representation used for video generation (§§3.2–3.5).
- **Aligned data and evaluation:** VPM-Data supplies 10,000 simulated clips with synchronized videos and persistent trajectories; VP-WorldBench pairs visual and geometric evaluation across simulated and real scenes. VPM-Data is only part of the broader training mixture (§4; Appendix B).
- **Robot action extension:** Additional action tokens are denoised jointly with video, allowing action learning to share the point-supervised backbone. The authors also demonstrate qualitative control by converting forecast trajectories into joint commands (§5; §A.4).

The authors identify persistent metric 3D identity across time, forecasting beyond observed frames, and extension to action learning as distinctions from the closest prior tracking-supervised generation work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Point supervision improves multi-view forecasts | Authors report VPM (V+T) beats video-only VPM under the same training budget on every VP-WorldBench split and metric; numerical table entries are unavailable. Main evaluation withholds future camera parameters. | §§6.1–6.2; Table 2 caption |
| Gains transfer to unseen non-robot scenes | In the zero-shot modality ablation, adding trajectories to video reduces ReCamMaster FVD from **103.12 to 79.63** and raises CMU Panoptic \(\delta^{3D}_{avg}\) from **34.72 to 42.03**. Adding actions alone yields **101.35** and **36.51**, respectively. | §E.6; Table 26 discussion |
| Temporal identity adds value beyond per-frame geometry | With matched backbone, data, feature layer, and schedule, depth and pointmap supervision recover only **19%** and **28%**, respectively, of the FVD improvement from trajectory supervision. Trajectories without correspondence loss still outperform both per-frame variants on every split. | §E.3; Table 22 discussion |
| The trajectory head improves direct point forecasting | On VP-WorldBench Real-world Robot, VPM (V+T)’s directly predicted tracks score **67.22** \(\delta^{3D}_{avg}\), versus **64.75** for tracks extracted from generated videos using the external evaluation pipeline. | §E.1; Table 19 discussion |

| Point supervision benefits action learning | Authors report higher success for VPM (V+T+A) than VPM (V+A) throughout simulated and real evaluations. Real evaluation covers three pick-and-place tasks, **24 trials each**, with partial credit for placement accompanied by obstacle contact; numerical scores are absent. | §6.4; §A.3; Table 5 caption |

The supplied prose supports consistent gains and includes useful controlled comparisons, especially against per-frame geometry. It does not allow independent verification of most rankings, effect sizes, or statistical uncertainty because the result tables largely contain captions without cells. Generated-video motion accuracy also depends on external tracking and depth estimation; it is distinct from the trajectory head’s own accuracy (§A.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Supervision covers annotated robots and objects rather than complete scenes; skeletal annotations omit independent cloth and hair motion. Calibration is required, and computation grows as camera streams share one sequence (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Simulation lacks realistic depth noise, uses fixed rendering profiles, and has a pedestrian-heavy hard urban subset. Seeds alone cannot reproduce all physics and traffic behavior exactly (§D.6).
- **Author-stated metric limits:** Tracking/depth errors prevent perfect generated videos from reaching ideal point accuracy; cross-view MEt3R can penalize a coherent alternative future (§A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Human generalization evidence is narrow: the new real-world human benchmark contains three participants in one furnished space. Hard subsets preserve collective target visibility, so they do not test complete simultaneous occlusion (§D.7; §D.5).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Training is described as 600K samples, but §F.6 lists components totaling 605K. RoboCasa evaluation counts in §A.2 do not reconcile, and its robot-control results cannot be checked numerically from the supplied tables.

## Open research questions
1. **How robust is VPM to calibration error?** Known calibration is assumed, and robot episodes are filtered for alignment. A next experiment could perturb intrinsics and extrinsics while measuring forecasting and control degradation (§7; §B.2).
2. **Does persistent supervision help with dense non-rigid motion?** Cloth and hair motion are omitted. A matched experiment could add corresponding deformable-surface tracks and evaluate those regions separately (§7; §D.6).
3. **Can predicted trajectories support reliable control without action tokens?** Current trajectory-to-control evidence is qualitative. A next experiment could compare repeated trajectory-only and action-token rollouts on identical tasks, reporting placement, collision, and execution failures (§6.5; §A.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several controlled findings are explicit, but most numerical tables and detailed hyperparameters are missing. Material gaps include full baseline scores, uncertainty estimates, stage-specific training settings, complete runtime results, code/weight availability, and quantitative trajectory-only control performance.
