# Epi: Towards a Persistent, Self-Improving Agent for Autonomous Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24944) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24944)


## One-sentence takeaway
Épi combines persistent memory, versioned research branches and fixed verification to produce checkable mathematical advances, while its lightweight coding configuration improves a speed-sensitive benchmark score without establishing better overall solution quality.

## Problem and motivation
Research investigations can outlast a model’s context window and require revisiting failed approaches, running long experiments and retaining the reasons behind decisions. The authors identify three connected gaps: continuity across sessions, reuse of experimental lessons, and exploration with backtracking (§1).

Their target is research with explicit references, budgets and checkable outputs. This makes progress measurable while allowing the agent’s methods to change (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent research workflow:** Épi stores code, artifacts, handover notes, lessons and dead ends outside model context. Versioned branches preserve alternatives, while a different model reviews research direction. Breadboard supports persistent execution and isolated verification (§3).
- **Fixed verification protocol:** Each campaign fixes submission requirements, feasibility checks, objective, normalization, comparison value and minimum meaningful gain—the “ruler” and gain floor. Subsequent campaigns can adopt new references or verification requirements (§§2–3). Model weights remain fixed; “self-improvement” means adapting methods through experience.
- **Checkable research outputs:** The authors report improved constructions, certified numerical bounds and a computer-assisted extension of Tuza’s conjecture. They state that constructions and supporting certificates are released (§§4–5; Appendices A–B).
- **Épi-turbo:** A coding configuration emphasizes economical model calls, continuous sessions, budget reservation and stopping when further sessions cease improving the verified score. Independent review and extra validation were disabled in its reported evaluation (§6.1).

The proposed contribution is this integration and its resulting artifacts; the paper acknowledges prior systems with memory, branching and executable evaluation (§7).

The mathematical evidence supports specific constructions, bounds and the stated restricted theorem, conditional on the described checks and reductions. Record comparisons establish improvements over archived references rather than superiority over other research agents under matched budgets.

The coding experiment supports a higher aggregate quality–speed score in this setting. It does **not** establish generally better objective values, nor does it isolate which architectural components cause the gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated statistical limit:** There is one run per task and system, so the authors do not report statistical significance (§6.2).
- **Author-stated benchmark caveat:** Some commercial-solver reference values are weak. The speed term applies only within 1% of the reference, making the aggregate score sensitive to both reference quality and runtime (§6.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No controlled ablations quantify the contributions of memory, branching, persistence or independent review. The successful outputs therefore do not establish each component’s benefit.
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Coding uses a selected subset of FrontierOR, excluding unreliable references. Mathematical campaigns concentrate heavily on packing constructions, limiting evidence for broader autonomous research (§§4.2, 6.2).
- **Analyst inference—resource comparability:** Mathematical budgets are described as problem-specific, with runs conducted over “a few days,” but comprehensive per-campaign costs, unsuccessful attempts and matched agent baselines are unavailable (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—reproduction:** The text describes retained artifacts and a results repository, but does not provide complete prompts, memory-retrieval settings, reviewer assignments or executable campaign configurations.

## Open research questions
1. **Which persistence mechanisms improve discovery?** Component effects are unmeasured. Run matched-budget campaigns with memory, branching and review individually removed, measuring verified gains and recovery after session boundaries.
2. **How robust is the coding advantage?** Single runs leave variability unresolved, and objective comparisons often favor Codex. Repeat the selected tasks with multiple seeds and report score, feasibility, runtime and objective quality separately.
3. **Does accumulated experience transfer across problems?** Cross-campaign lessons are part of the design, but their transfer benefit is not quantified. Compare fresh-memory and accumulated-memory agents on previously unseen problem families.
4. **Can the Tuza proof strategy extend further?** The theorem restricts clique size and neighborhood-type count. Attempt certificate generation for an additional active type, measuring enumeration growth and whether the same truncation-and-box strategy remains tractable.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives precise claims, comparison conventions and substantial certificate arguments, but cannot independently establish artifact correctness or architectural causality.

Material missing information includes full campaign configurations and costs, unsuccessful-campaign coverage, component ablations, quantified memory transfer, repeated coding trials and complete reproducibility instructions.
