# Axiomatic World Modeling for Physics Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24519) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24519)


## One-sentence takeaway
RLAWM combines premise-fidelity and executable physics-consistency rewards during training, reporting better physics reasoning and zero-shot transfer, although incomplete result tables and imperfect checkers limit assessment.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify **modeling drift**: a model changes the problem’s premises—geometry, applicable laws, or assumptions—and then reasons within that altered setup. They argue that answer-only and local step rewards can miss this failure, motivating supervision of both the physical setup and its subsequent use (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Explicit modeling supervision:** The policy emits objects, constraints, representation, mapping, and plan. Physics Modeling Fidelity (PMF) compares extracted law–quantity, equation-coupling, and assumption motifs against a reference using averaged F1 (§3.2).
- **Executable solving supervision:** A frozen translator converts pseudocode into Python; problem-specific checkers test physical invariants, trace consistency, and responses to input perturbations using a library of 147 law templates (§3.3; Appendix M).
- **Joint training:** GRPO combines modeling, solving, and answer rewards with weights 0.4, 0.4, and 0.2. External scoring and execution operate during training; no separate trainable world-model network is introduced (§§3.4, 4.3; Appendix B).

The reference is an operational proxy constructed from the problem and solution, rather than a fully formalized axiom system (Table 7).

## Evidence and main results

SubPass@1 is greedy-decoding accuracy averaged over each problem’s sub-questions; answer equivalence is judged by an LLM (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated physics improvement | Qwen3-VL-8B on PhysReason: RLAWM **48.1 ± 0.9%** across three seeds; strongest listed baseline, Direct-Likert, **37.1%**. | Appendix H, Table 12 |
| Larger headline gain | Authors report up to **+12.2 percentage points** in PhysReason SubPass@1 over the strongest baselines; the corresponding backbone and numerical table cells are unavailable. | §5.1; Table 1 caption |
| Cross-difficulty transfer | PhysReason-trained policies on OlympiadBench-Phys: **15.2% versus RLVR’s 6.2%** SubPass@1; Qwen3-VL-4B separately reaches **11.3% versus 4.5%**. The first comparison’s backbone is unspecified in prose. | §5.3 |
| Cross-source/domain transfer | Zero-shot PHYBench: **10.5% versus 8.7%** for the strongest baseline; AIME 2026: **+10.0 points versus RLVR** under shared physics-only Zero-RL training. Backbones and absolute AIME scores are absent from prose. | §§4.1, 5.3 |
| Reward benefit without SFT | Qwen3-VL-8B Zero-RL on PhysReason: RLAWM **37.3% versus Reference-Likert’s 31.4%**, a **5.9-point** SubPass@1 difference. | §6.4 |
| Denser training signal | Qwen3-VL-8B PhysReason training: zero-variance GRPO groups decrease from **29.9%** with outcome reward alone to **9.3%** after adding checker feedback. | §6.2; Appendix N |
| Imperfect checker agreement | Among correct, successfully executed training rollouts, **4.9%** are fully rejected; authors also report **15.8% false positives**, without clearly specifying that rate’s denominator. | Appendix N |
| Best-of-k advantage | Authors report RLAWM leads across sampled SubPass@k settings on both Qwen backbones; numerical curve values are missing. | §5.2, Figure 3 |

These results support improved performance under the reported protocols and a denser reward signal. They do **not directly establish faithful access to internal beliefs**: the method scores externalized text and translated programs. Likewise, best-of-k results support broader sampled success, but the authors’ stronger interpretation of an expanded reasoning boundary is not independently demonstrated by a direct diversity measure.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Extra scoring, translation, and checker-synthesis models increase training cost; static checkers cannot adapt to rollouts (Appendix A).
- **Author-documented:** Sign conventions, variable binding, unsupported valid derivations, and strict assertions can penalize correct work. Errors after the final emitted variable can escape checking (Appendix N).
- **Analyst inference:** PMF remains dependent on extraction and semantic matching, despite deterministic F1 aggregation; restricting the scorer’s role does not establish freedom from scorer bias (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Strong causal claims about internal modeling exceed the observable evidence because the reference is a solution-derived proxy and the policy’s internal AWM is inaccessible (§3.1; Table 7).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Most main tables contain captions but no numerical cells. Section 5.3 points to Table 4 for OOD results, whereas the captions identify Table 3 as OOD and Table 4 as reward sparsity.
- **Implementation inconsistency:** Section 3.3 says translation failures omit the solving reward and renormalize weights; Table 14 includes them among zero solving scores.

## Open research questions
- **Can verification tolerate equivalent conventions?** Appendix N documents sign-convention penalties. Compare convention-normalized checkers with current checkers on deliberately equivalent derivations.
- **How much does translation distort supervision?** Execution and variable-contract failures are documented, but translation faithfulness is not directly audited. Manually compare pseudocode, translated code, and rewards on a stratified rollout sample.
- **Do gains reflect more faithful premises or improved output structure?** SFT ablations help separate training components but do not measure hidden beliefs. Evaluate held-out premise contradictions and controlled assumption changes with independent annotations.
- **Does transfer extend beyond the tested mathematics setting?** AIME provides one cross-domain evaluation. Train domain-specific modeling/checking rewards for another verifiable domain and compare matched outcome-only training.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several results are explicit, but most main-table cells, numerical SubPass@k curves, complete per-setting comparisons, and detailed seed results for baselines are unavailable. Also unclear are the false-positive denominator, theorem-library construction’s separation from held-out PhysReason data, translation-failure handling, and the actual contents of the linked release.
