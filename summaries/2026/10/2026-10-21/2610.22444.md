# Event-STVG: Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding for Long-Form Event Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22444) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22444)


## One-sentence takeaway

Event-STVG introduces structured event-and-role grounding across eight video domains, with evaluated models reaching at best 5.40% m-vIoU on its 10-minute soccer setting (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Existing spatio-temporal video grounding typically locates entities from descriptions of their appearance, actions, or relationships. The authors argue that this places limited demands on event reasoning: identifying a particular repeated occurrence, inferring its participants’ semantic roles, and tracking those participants together (§§1–2).

Event-STVG instead asks models to locate a specified occurrence of an event and ground its role fillers without instance-specific participant descriptions. This tests whether long-video understanding produces precise temporal and spatial evidence (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Task formulation.** Inputs specify an event type, ordinal occurrence, required semantic roles, and event-boundary definitions. Outputs comprise an event interval and bounding-box trajectories for each queried role (§3.1; Appendix B).
2. **Benchmark construction.** The benchmark contains 4,016 annotated occurrences across eight domains, 26 event types, and 30 roles. Human annotators verify event boundaries and role trajectories, assisted by tracking tools where available. Soccer is the primary domain; seven additional domains test broader applicability (§§3.2–3.3).
3. **Evaluation and diagnostics.** The authors evaluate nine general-purpose vision-language models and the PTD grounding specialist, then analyze occurrence confusion, spatial grounding errors, and incomplete multi-role coverage (§4; Appendix D).

The contribution is a task, benchmark, and diagnostic evaluation; the paper does not introduce a new grounding model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short-context grounding is difficult | On 3,516 one-minute soccer queries, six of nine general-purpose models score below 1% m-vIoU and achieve no vIoU@0.5 successes; PTD scores 1.72% m-vIoU. | §4.2, discussion of Table 2 |
| Long-context performance remains low | Best m-vIoU among Gemini, GPT, and Qwen is 5.40% on the fixed 300-event, 10-minute soccer subset. GPT leads temporal localization; Gemini leads joint grounding. | §4.2, discussion of Table 3 |
| Repeated occurrences interfere with selection | Across 209 soccer queries with same-type distractors in at least one setting, target hit rates fall by 43.54 / 21.05 / 44.98 percentage points from one to ten minutes for Gemini / GPT / Qwen; all changes survive multiple-comparison correction. | Appendix D.1.1 |
| Spatial errors persist after temporal overlap | On the same 206 multidomain queries temporally hit by all three models, spatial grounding gaps are 67.59% / 69.12% / 73.54% for Gemini / GPT / Qwen. | Appendix D.2 |
| Complete role coverage is rare | Among 274 multidomain multi-role queries, all roles exceed per-role vIoU >0.3 in only 4 / 2 / 0 cases for Gemini / GPT / Qwen. | Appendix D.3 |
| Baseline interface adaptation materially affects results | On 100 matched queries, switching Qwen from the full prompt to PTD-style requests reduces one-minute m-vIoU from 4.214% to 2.184%; paired difference −2.031 percentage points, 95% CI [−4.007, −0.278]. | Appendix C.6 |

These results support substantial difficulty under the tested protocols and distinguish temporal selection from spatial role grounding. They do not isolate internal reasoning mechanisms: the authors explicitly state that spatial gaps combine localization, correspondence, visibility, missing-output, and validity errors (Appendix D.5). Main Tables 2–4 appear only as captions and narrative summaries in the supplied text, preventing a complete numerical comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-qualified comparison:** PTD omits explicit ordinal and complete boundary information, handles roles independently, and uses different visual sampling and decoding. Its comparison with general-purpose models therefore mixes capability and interface differences (Appendices C.5–C.6).
- **Author-qualified annotation evidence:** Soccer re-annotation exposes original intervals or shared tracking candidates, so agreement measures workflow consistency rather than independent double-blind annotation. Sampled audits do not establish correctness of every instance (Appendices E.5–E.6).
- **Author-qualified interpretation:** Later-occurrence multidomain comparisons also vary in video position, difficulty, and available evidence; they do not isolate ordinal interference (Appendix D.1.2).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Long-context and multidomain evaluation covers only three selected models. Soccer dominates the benchmark, and some additional-domain videos concatenate related clips, limiting conclusions about naturally continuous long-form video (§§3.2, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Provider defaults and service failures affect results. Repeatability tests cover selected models and subsets, while the abstract says assets are available and the reproducibility statement says they will be released (Appendix C.4; release statements).

## Open research questions

1. **Can occurrence memory reduce long-context confusion?** Target selection deteriorates while boundaries remain relatively stable on consistently hit queries. Compare direct inference with an explicit event timeline under matched visual inputs (Appendix D.1.1).
2. **Which spatial component causes the largest deficit?** The reported gap aggregates several error sources. Evaluate role assignment using verified tracks, then separately evaluate box localization with role identities supplied (Appendix D.5).
3. **Can joint role modeling improve complete coverage?** Partial success substantially exceeds complete success. Compare joint and independent role prediction while preserving identical prompts, frames, and output formats (Appendices C.6, D.3).
4. **Do results transfer to continuous nonsports footage?** Some multidomain contexts are concatenated. Compare continuous and concatenated contexts matched for event count, duration, and role complexity (§3.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the task, construction process, and reported diagnostic findings, but lacks the numerical bodies of main Tables 1–4 and configuration Table C.1. Complete model rankings, full domain scores, and all input configurations are therefore unavailable. Actual asset availability is unresolved by the conflicting release statements.
