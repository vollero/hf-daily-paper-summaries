# Ask to Distill: Learning to Synthesize Data for On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27450) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27450)


## One-sentence takeaway

A2D trains a question synthesizer using support-aware teacher–student disagreement, producing answer-free prompt banks that outperform real-bank OPD on average across the evaluated mathematics, coding, and agentic settings (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation

On-policy distillation (OPD) teaches a student using teacher predictions along student-generated responses. Training questions determine which states receive this supervision, but their value cannot be judged reliably from surface difficulty or disagreement alone: teacher preferences may fall outside the student’s update candidates (§1).

The authors address how to optimize questions for a particular student–teacher pair without reference answers, correctness rewards, or training a separate student for every candidate. Their proposed gap is question generation guided by *usable distributional supervision*, rather than unoptimized generation or selection from an existing bank (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Optimize questions as distribution elicitors.** A student-initialized synthesizer learns through GRPO while the scoring student and teacher remain frozen. Each candidate is scored using one student response; downstream OPD subsequently starts from the original student (§§2.1–2.4).
2. **Construct a question-level learning surrogate.** Token scores multiply capped teacher-to-student KL over the union of both models’ top candidates by teacher probability mass on the student’s candidates. Sublinear length normalization, a response-length penalty, and a batch repetition penalty control aggregation (§2.3).
3. **Build and validate prompt-only banks.** Format filtering and lexical/semantic deduplication precede a fixed, candidate-restricted OPD procedure. The contribution adapts prior support-aware token scoring to learned question generation; it does not introduce the underlying teachability principle (§§2.5–2.6, 4.1).

Synthesizer optimization requires no external training questions or auxiliary models. Post-generation semantic deduplication does use Qwen3-Embedding-4B, and agentic evaluation uses GPT-4o-mini as simulator and judge (§3.1).

These comparisons support learned prompt construction under the implemented OPD procedure. They do not establish universal superiority, uniform benchmark gains, or lower total compute cost. The signal analyses support predictive usefulness of the surrogate; they do not prove that maximizing it guarantees downstream improvement (§§2.3, 3.9, Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated surrogate limits:** Reward uses one sampled response per question. Its theoretical properties certify compatible disagreement, not parameter-gradient magnitude or downstream learning gains; certificates may cease to hold as the student changes (§5; Appendix B).
- **Checkpoint-selection bias:** AMC23 and LiveCodeBench select downstream checkpoints and also enter reported results. The authors explicitly state that those scores are not held-out estimates. Iterative results use the highest observed checkpoint averages (§3.10; Appendix A.2).
- **Unestablished total efficiency:** Training-token comparisons exclude upstream synthesis and scoring costs (§3.9).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Agentic testing uses one student–teacher pair; detailed learning-signal validation uses one mathematics pair and natural DAPO questions. Neither establishes equivalent behavior across other pairs or optimized synthetic questions (§§3.7, 3.12).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain precision and portability:** Main Tables 1–3 provide seed means without uncertainty intervals. The reward is designed around the implemented candidate support; effectiveness with other OPD objectives remains untested here (§3.3; Appendix A.4).

## Open research questions

- **Does A2D improve end-to-end cost efficiency?** Upstream costs are excluded (§3.9). Compare methods under equal total accelerator time, including calibration, synthesizer training, scoring, curation, and OPD.
- **Can adaptive synthesis avoid diminishing returns?** Repeated rounds yield marginal gains in one setting (§3.10). Test refresh schedules while tracking disagreement, compatibility, and held-out accuracy under matched total budgets.
- **Does the reward transfer to other distillation objectives?** Compatibility targets the implemented candidate-restricted update (Appendix A.4). Compare unchanged and objective-adapted rewards across alternative supervision rules.
- **How much sampling is needed to score synthetic questions reliably?** Training uses a single response, while detailed validation uses natural questions (§§2.3, 3.12). Compare single- and multiple-rollout scoring on synthetic candidates with equal scoring budgets.
