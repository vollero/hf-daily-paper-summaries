# On the Limits of Scale: Inverse Scaling in Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27360) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27360)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across four open-weight VLM families, larger released variants sometimes lose accuracy on specific subtasks, although scaling usually helps and these observational comparisons cannot isolate parameter count as the cause (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Aggregate benchmark scores can conceal regressions in particular visual or reasoning capabilities. The authors investigate whether such regressions occur on standard multimodal benchmarks without deliberately adding distractors—a gap they identify in prior inverse-scaling work (§§1–3). This matters for choosing models whose strengths match deployment tasks.

## Approach and contributions
- **Systematic evaluation:** Evaluate 19 checkpoints from Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, Gemma 3, and Molmo 2 on eight benchmarks comprising nine splits and 29,386 questions per model (§4; Table 1).
- **Paired subtask analysis:** Within each family, compare every size pair using question-level correctness flips. Define `Net` as smaller-only correct answers minus larger-only correct answers; report cases with at least 20 shared questions and `Net ≥5` (§§3–4.1).
- **Robustness and characterization:** Examine cross-family recurrence, dense-only comparisons, parameter-gap restrictions, encoder tiers, option ordering, chain-of-thought prompting, and human/LLM error annotations (§§5–6; Appendices D–L).

The contribution is an empirical evaluation framework and findings about released checkpoints, rather than a new VLM or a causal scaling law.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Regressions form a minority of comparisons | 375 cases among 10,434 eligible within-family subtask comparisons (3.6%), using `n ≥20`, `Net ≥5`; larger models have higher accuracy in 74.8% of comparisons. | Table 2; Appendix D, Table 35 |
| Some dense-model regressions are large | On BLINK Jigsaw, Qwen 4B exceeds 32B by 22.0 percentage points across 150 questions: 44 smaller-only correct versus 11 larger-only correct. | Table 3 |
| Patterns recur across architectures | 13 subtask–dataset pairs have qualifying cases in at least three families; BLINK Multi-view Reasoning appears in all four. | §5.4; Table 36 |
| MoE comparisons do not explain everything | Excluding MoE checkpoints leaves 193 cases and eight subtasks replicated across at least three families. | Table 4; Table 9 |
| Strong statistical evidence is concentrated | Of 375 selected cases, 133 have uncorrected one-sided McNemar `p <0.05`; 16 remain significant after BH correction over all 10,434 eligible comparisons at FDR 0.05. | Appendix D, Table 35 |
| CoT reduces the selected regressions | Re-evaluating both models leaves 96/375 cases (25.6%) at `Net ≥5`; median Net falls from +8 to 0. Larger models recover 43.5% of previously lost question instances, while smaller-model accuracy declines in 251 cases. | Appendix L, Table 45 |
| Human review supports real errors in its sample | Human majority labels 107/127 sampled flips (84.3%) genuine errors and 20/127 ambiguous or incorrectly annotated. The sample deliberately overrepresents LLM-flagged annotation issues. | Appendix H, Table 39 |

These results establish subtask regressions among evaluated released variants, including dense checkpoints. They do not establish that increasing parameters causes regressions or that performance generally decreases with scale. Most affected family–subtask curves are non-monotonic rather than consistently declining (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated confounding:** Within-family variants can differ in training data, alignment, vision encoders, active parameters, and visual processing. Benchmark contamination cannot be ruled out (§6; Appendix K).
- **Exploratory selection and testing:** Cases are selected and tested on the same observations, with overlapping comparisons. Only a small subset survives correction across the full comparison space (Appendix D).
- **Threshold dependence:** A fixed Net threshold implies different accuracy gaps at different subtask sizes. Counts therefore do not directly measure intrinsic benchmark susceptibility (§5.2).
- **Presentation and execution sensitivity:** Option reordering preserves positive Net in the tested BLINK subset, but changes its magnitude; identity replays on different hardware also vary. Paraphrase robustness is untested (§6; Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference:** The authors’ “at least 84%” genuine-error interpretation is not a demonstrated population lower bound: human validation uses a deliberately enriched sample and reports no population uncertainty estimate (Appendix H).
- **CoT interpretation:** CoT uses a larger output budget than direct prompting, and InternVL largely ignores the CoT request on AI2D. The control therefore does not uniformly isolate reasoning effects (Appendices A, L).

## Open research questions
1. **Does parameter count itself cause these regressions?** Released checkpoints differ along several dimensions. Train multiple sizes with fixed data, encoder, alignment, and preprocessing, then repeat the paired analysis (§6).
2. **Which regressions generalize beyond benchmark wording?** Option-order testing covers only selected strong cases, and paraphrases remain untested. Evaluate held-out paraphrases and image-preserving presentation variants (§6; Appendix J).
3. **Why does CoT help some subtasks more than others?** The proposed perception-versus-reasoning explanation remains untested. Compare equal-budget prompts with verified perception outputs and measure both models’ gains and losses (Appendix L).
4. **How much survives independent annotation and replication?** Some labels are ambiguous and testing is exploratory. Pre-register subtasks, use fresh questions, and obtain blinded human adjudication before testing (§5.6; Appendices D, H).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported descriptive regressions; **low** in causal explanations.

Material gaps include controlled training comparisons, contamination measurements, broad paraphrase robustness, independent confirmatory evaluation, and population uncertainty for the human error estimate. The supplied text includes checkpoint identifiers, prompts, generation settings, and case tables, but not executable code or complete raw outputs; the linked repository was not inspected.
