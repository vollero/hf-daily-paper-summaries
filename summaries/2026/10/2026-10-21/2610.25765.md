# TRACE: Tracking Multimodal Clinical Trajectories for Inpatient Risk Prediction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25765) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25765)


## One-sentence takeaway
TRACE combines daily global–local clinical trajectories with phenotype-guided contrastive learning, achieving the highest reported AUROC across nine retrospective dataset–task settings, without establishing prospective clinical utility (§§3–4, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Inpatient records accumulate irregularly across structured EHR, imaging, and clinical text. The authors argue that existing fusion, relational, and missing-modality methods give insufficient attention to how these observations evolve together over an admission. TRACE addresses this gap while supporting different observation windows for early prolonged-stay prediction and completed-admission mortality or readmission assessment (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Daily trajectory representation:** Separate category-specific global context from day-specific clinical events. Frozen vision and language encoders produce tokens augmented with modality and day embeddings; unavailable modalities contribute no tokens (§3.2).
- **Temporal global–local attention:** Global tokens remain accessible, while local tokens directly attend to same-day and earlier events. Separate task and phenotype tokens summarize the trajectory (§3.3).
- **Phenotype-guided supervision:** Combine binary prediction loss with supervised contrastive learning whose positive pairs share both outcome and primary-diagnosis phenotype (§3.4).
- **Evaluation and interpretation:** Compare across three datasets and three tasks, ablate modalities, attention, and contrastive supervision, and illustrate day/modality attribution through repeated prefix inference (§§4.3–4.5). Attribution is qualitative, not causal evidence.

These results support improved retrospective ranking discrimination under the reported protocols. They do not establish uniform gains in precision–recall or threshold-dependent performance, statistically significant superiority, calibration, or clinical benefit. The retrieved text includes table captions and narrative results but omits the numerical table bodies, including confidence intervals and most baseline scores.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Daily aggregation may miss rapid physiological changes; architectural differences complicate baseline comparisons; evaluation is retrospective and limited to public datasets (§6).
- **Analyst inference—information timing:** Diagnosis-derived context is treated as global, but its availability at prediction time is unclear. This matters particularly for early Long LOS prediction. Mortality uses the completed admission, with terminal documentation permitted, so its results do not demonstrate early warning (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference—attention semantics:** Global tokens can absorb all local events and subsequently inform earlier local tokens. Consequently, the mask does not guarantee strictly causal local representations across multiple layers, despite restricting direct local attention (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited isolation:** Attention ablation covers MIMIC-IV only. The reported contrastive ablation compares joint versus prediction-only training, without an outcome-only contrastive control to isolate phenotype conditioning (§§4.4.2–4.4.3).
- Attribution is illustrated with two cases and explicitly lacks causal validation; prospective performance of intermediate-day predictions is not established (§4.5).

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains survive strict prediction-time availability?** Global diagnosis timing is unclear, and mortality uses completed admissions. Rebuild inputs using availability timestamps and evaluate fixed early prediction horizons.
2. **Does phenotype conditioning outperform generic contrastive supervision?** The reported control removes the contrastive objective entirely. Compare outcome-only, phenotype-only, and joint pairing under matched sampling and tuning.
3. **Would finer timing improve acute-risk prediction?** Authors identify daily resolution as a limitation. Compare daily and subdaily representations with matched inputs and computational budgets.
4. **Will performance transfer to deployment?** Prospective and external validation remain future work. Test a locked model on a held-out institution or later cohort, including calibration and changing modality availability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are clearly described, but independent assessment is constrained by missing numerical table bodies.

Material unavailable details include full confidence intervals and baseline scores, outcome prevalence, exact diagnosis availability rules, detailed baseline harmonization, several architecture and training hyperparameters, maximum trajectory length and truncation handling, code/weight availability, calibration, and prospective or external validation results.
