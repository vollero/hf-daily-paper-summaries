# EMBER: Predicting TCR--pMHC Binding Without a Docked Complex

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27334) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27334)


## One-sentence takeaway
EMBER scores independently folded TCR and pMHC structures without a relative pose, achieving the highest mean binding-prediction metrics across four folding tools in the authors’ epitope-disjoint benchmark, with stronger cross-tool ranking agreement and lower folding costs (Tables 1–4).

## Problem and motivation
TCR–pMHC binding prediction helps prioritize candidates for experimental screening. Existing structure-based predictors often select interface residues and contacts from a predicted docked complex, so placement errors can change the model’s input graph.

The authors identify this failure mode as **assembly-induced interface corruption (AIC)** and argue that its downstream consequences have not been systematically characterized. Their proposed solution retains structural information from both partners while removing relative placement entirely (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Structural-error analysis:** Compare predicted interfaces against crystal structures and examine repeated co-folding to distinguish monomer disagreement from systematic assembly errors (§3; Appendix E).
- **Pose-independent predictor:** Independently fold the receptor and pMHC, combine invariant geometric descriptors with frozen ESM-C embeddings, and encode each through a shared equivariant graph network with chemistry gates and dynamically rebuilt neighborhoods. The reported model uses only the TCR β chain (§4; Appendix D).
- **Sequence-defined interaction head:** Bidirectional attention connects CDR3β and peptide representations without coordinates. Peptide pooling uses an anchor-aligned canonical frame; training combines binary cross-entropy and a pairwise ranking loss (§4).
- **Guarantee and reuse:** Proposition 1 proves score invariance to independent rigid motions of the two inputs. Folding each distinct partner once enables reuse across candidate pairs, requiring O(N+M) folds versus O(NM) for a complete pairwise screen (§4.1; Appendix B; §5.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly accuracy is unreliable on the structural benchmark | Across 49 crystal complexes, excluding Boltz-2, median contact Jaccard at 5 Å is 0.19–0.46 and median TCR docking RMSD is 5.87–8.09 Å after MHC alignment. | §3; Table 8 |
| Repeated sampling does not reliably remove displacement | Five stochastic tools generate 20 co-folds per target: directional coherence is 0.839–0.992; equal-weight averaging changes displacement by −2.5% to +6.7%, with no reported Wilcoxon p below 0.05. | Table 10 |
| EMBER leads mean benchmark metrics | Five epitope-disjoint folds, three seeds, 45,909 pairs: EMBER micro-AUROC is 0.661–0.670 and micro-AUPRC 0.366–0.372 across four tools. STAG-LLM achieves 0.582–0.659 and 0.292–0.314; sequence-only NetTCR-2.2 achieves 0.604 and 0.319. | Table 1; Appendix F.1 |
| Cross-tool rankings agree more strongly | On 142 epitopes with at least ten candidates, Kendall’s W is 0.840±0.008 for EMBER versus 0.666±0.013 for STAG-LLM; Overlap@10 is 0.729±0.013 versus 0.675±0.008. | Table 2 |
| Monomer geometry provides a modest gain | Under ESMFold2, micro-AUPRC is 0.372±0.013 for EMBER, 0.349±0.001 without structure, and 0.346±0.033 with a joint docked-complex encoder. | Tables 3, 21 |
| Fixed-threshold superiority does not imply universal calibrated superiority | With thresholds fitted on other folds, TCRmodel2-based EMBER has MCC@t 0.209 versus NetTCR-2.2’s 0.241; EMBER’s MCC@0.5 is 0.267 versus 0.207. | Table 13 |
| Reusing folds reduces preprocessing | For 21,830 distinct partners and 45,909 pairs, inference-only folding savings are 2.6×–19.4× across tools on one A100-SXM4-80GB. | Table 4; Appendix F.6 |

The evidence supports better **mean performance and cross-tool stability on this benchmark**, not superiority on every individual fold: EMBER wins all folds in only 16 of the 24 metric/tool comparisons against the selected strongest baseline (Table 15). Pose independence is an architectural guarantee; improved predictive accuracy is an empirical result. Neither establishes prospective experimental screening success.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Label uncertainty, acknowledged:** Negatives mix panel non-binders and swapped TCR pairs without provenance identifying their source. Evaluation cannot separate these groups (Appendix F.1).
- **Restricted scope, acknowledged:** The encoder excludes TCRα, and peptide pooling assumes class-I anchor organization. Restoring α reduces performance in this dataset; this does not establish that α information is generally unhelpful (Appendices D, F.5).
- **Generalization risk—analyst inference:** Epitope-disjoint splits establish unseen peptide identities, but receptor-disjoint, MHC-disjoint, and sequence-similarity-controlled generalization are not established. The epitope distribution is also strongly concentrated (Appendix F.1).
- **Causal attribution—analyst inference:** The docked-complex ablation changes coordinates, node descriptors, and cross-molecule connectivity together. It therefore does not isolate placement error as the sole cause of reduced accuracy (Appendix F.5).
- **Claims require qualification:** The authors attribute Boltz-2’s structural advantage to memorization using its deterioration on post-cutoff targets; this is evidence consistent with that explanation, not direct verification of training-set membership (§3; Table 9).
- **Cost and implementation caveats:** Timing excludes alignment and other overheads and uses single measurements. Near-tied nearest neighbors can also violate exact implementation-level invariance, which the authors handle through test retries (Appendices C, F.6).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the advantage survive experimentally verified negatives?** Negative provenance is unavailable. Evaluate a separate panel with measured binding and non-binding outcomes.
2. **How much of the docked-arm loss comes specifically from pose error?** Its ablation changes several inputs simultaneously. Hold monomer conformations fixed and perturb only relative pose and cross-molecule edges.
3. **Can reliable paired αβ data improve performance?** Paired-chain provenance is incomplete and differs by label. Compare β-only and αβ models on a provenance-confirmed subset.
4. **Does performance extend to unfamiliar receptors and MHC alleles?** Current splits isolate epitopes. Add receptor-cluster-disjoint and MHC-disjoint tests with calibration selected solely from training/validation data.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the architectural summary and reported benchmark results; **medium** in broader biological generalization and causal explanations.

The supplied text does not establish downloadable code, weights, exact split files, prospective validation, source-resolved negative labels, or training-set overlap for all folding tools. It also lacks isolated ablations of chemistry gating, dynamic graph rebuilding, and the ranking loss.
