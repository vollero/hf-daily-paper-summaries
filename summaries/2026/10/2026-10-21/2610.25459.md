# BiDex-100: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation Across 100+ Simulation Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25459) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25459)


## One-sentence takeaway
BiDex-100 introduces 106 simulated bimanual dexterous tasks with demonstrations and capability-specific scoring, revealing substantial failures under combined out-of-domain shifts across seven evaluated VLA models (Tables III–IV).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing evaluations inadequately measure coordinated multi-finger manipulation, especially memory, instruction fidelity, long-horizon execution, and robustness. They identify a gap in benchmarks combining dexterous bimanual embodiments, broad capability coverage, partial-progress scoring, matched ID/OOD evaluation, and teleoperated demonstrations (§I–II).

## Approach and contributions

- **Capability-organized benchmark:** 106 MuJoCo tasks span generalization, instruction following, limited demonstrations, long horizon, and memory, using two embodiments with a shared 58-dimensional control interface (§III-B–D).
- **Automated compositional scoring:** Predicate-based subtasks provide strict success and weighted progress scores, with ordering, terminal constraints, and permanent violation checks (§III-C).
- **Reproducible robustness evaluation:** Each task has an OOD variant combining appearance, camera, and layout changes, with compatible tasks additionally receiving distractors, rescaling, or mirroring (§III-E; §IX).
- **Dataset and reference evaluation:** The authors report releasing 8,080 success-validated demonstrations containing 6.25 million frames, alongside code and tooling, and evaluate seven pretrained VLAs fine-tuned on the shared task mixture (§IV; §V; §XIV).

These results establish performance differences and severe aggregate OOD degradation under the specified protocol. They do not identify which individual shift causes failure, establish real-world competence, or isolate architecture effects from training differences. Progress scores show credited subtask completion; their averages alone do not establish that individual failed attempts were nearly complete.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Rigid-body simulation omits fluids, deformable objects, and hardware-level contact fidelity; sim-to-real transfer remains unvalidated. Assets emphasize tabletop food and kitchen scenes, and OOD shifts are finite and scripted (§XIV).
- **Analyst inference—OOD attribution:** Domains differ in robot reset procedures as well as scripted scene changes. The ID/OOD gap therefore cannot be attributed solely to visual or geometric robustness (§IX; §XII).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Models share data and evaluation conditions, but differ in training steps, freezing, action representations, and adaptation procedures. Rankings characterize these configurations rather than controlled architecture comparisons (§XI; Table VIII).
- **Analyst inference—data-efficiency claims:** A fixed low-data task group, co-trained with other tasks, does not isolate demonstration-count effects; no matched per-task demonstration-budget sweep is reported (§V-A; §XIV).
- **Analyst inference—metric interpretation:** Some instruction tasks award progress for avoiding forbidden actions, and zero-weight constraints still affect strict success. Progress is therefore not uniformly a measure of active task execution (§III-C; §XIII-B).
- **Reporting uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated fine-tuning runs are reported. The stated Long-Horizon range of 4–9 subtasks also conflicts with the three-subtask bowl-nesting catalog entry (§V; §XIII-D).

## Open research questions

1. **Which OOD components drive the collapse?** Combined shifts and different reset procedures prevent attribution. Evaluate each transformation separately while holding robot resets fixed (§IX; §XII).
2. **Does explicit temporal memory improve hidden-state tasks?** Axis-level results do not isolate memory from manipulation failures. Compare matched policies with and without history on reveal-and-hide tasks, recording subtask failures (§XIII-E).
3. **How does performance scale with demonstrations?** The fixed low-data group lacks within-task learning curves. Vary demonstration budgets on identical tasks, both with and without cross-task co-training (§V-A).
4. **Do simulation rankings transfer to hardware?** Physical validation remains open. Evaluate matched task subsets on the corresponding real platforms using comparable success criteria (§XIV).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark description and reported results; **medium** in capability-specific causal interpretations.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, repeated training runs, compute costs, isolated shift and reset ablations, matched data-budget experiments, hardware results, and per-task outcome tables. Release availability is asserted by the authors but was not independently verified.
