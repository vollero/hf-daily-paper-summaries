# World Models with Configurable Perception

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27914) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27914)


## One-sentence takeaway
ConfigWM uses language queries and paired noisy–clean supervision to construct task-selective visual states, improving planning under synthetic dynamic distraction while leaving real-world control generalization untested.

## Problem and motivation
Task-agnostic world models can retain irrelevant object motion, making dynamics prediction and planning harder. The authors argue that relevance must depend on the current task: the same object can be a target under one instruction and a distractor under another. Their identified gap is conditioning **state construction itself**, rather than only conditioning subsequent prediction or action generation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Query-configured state extraction:** A DINOv2-based visual encoder and language-conditioned Transformer predict frozen clean-target visual embeddings from distracted observations. Training uses spatially aligned, multi-positive InfoNCE, including identical observations paired with different queries and targets (§4.1; Appendix B.1).
- **World modeling and planning:** The configurable encoder is frozen; a projector and action-conditioned causal Transformer learn latent dynamics with prediction loss and SIGReg. CEM-based model predictive control minimizes terminal distance to the query-conditioned goal state (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Controlled distraction evaluation:** The authors construct matched noisy variants of Two-Room, Push-T, and OGBench-Cube, preserving underlying task dynamics while adding independently moving, target-like visual distractors (§3; Appendix A).
- **Real-world representation extension:** VLM-guided image editing creates noisy–clean training pairs for several large visual backbones. Evaluation measures distractor and task-region decodability, rather than downstream control (§6; Appendix D).

The distinctive contribution is combining configurable perception with action-conditioned world modeling; the visual backbones, contrastive objective, dynamics architecture, and planner build on existing components.

These results support task-selective states and robustness within the constructed settings. Query interventions establish that planning depends on the query, while representation–planning correlations establish association rather than causation. The clean-trained experiment does **not** establish transfer without prior distraction exposure, because encoder pretraining includes noisy–clean pairs.

The abstract’s “22.3%” planning improvement and retrieval improvement lack an explicit aggregation/comparison definition in the supplied text; the table above therefore uses directly specified comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied paper has no explicit limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences** grounded in its protocols:

- **Supervision confound:** DINOv2-WM controls downstream architecture, but ConfigWM additionally receives query-conditioned noisy–clean encoder training. No reported ablation separates language conditioning from this extra supervision or same-source multi-query training (§5.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Restricted queries:** Controlled experiments use distinct object colors and short templates. They do not establish generalization to ambiguous, relational, or unseen instructions (Appendix A).
- **Synthetic distraction scope:** Distractors change pixels without changing simulator dynamics. OGBench-Cube also changes tiled layout as distraction increases, so its difficulty axis includes spatial-layout changes (Appendix A.2).
- **Limited real-world validation:** The extension tests representations under image-edited distractors, not action-conditioned prediction or physical control (Appendix D).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Evaluation seeds and substantial training details are supplied, but exact CEM settings, evaluation episode counts, checkpoint-selection criteria, and release availability are not established. Several probing/retrieval results appear only as figure descriptions without numerical series.

## Open research questions
- **Does language conditioning add value beyond clean-target supervision?** The encoder comparison changes both. Train otherwise matched encoders with correct, constant, and shuffled queries using identical noisy–clean pairs.
- **Does configurability generalize beyond color selection?** Current controlled queries identify objects by color. Test held-out attributes, relational descriptions, and ambiguous instructions with matched planning tasks.
- **Does representation robustness improve real-world control?** Appendix D stops at patch probing. Train action-conditioned dynamics on real robot trajectories and evaluate planning with naturally occurring moving distractors.
- **Can paired clean supervision be reduced?** Current training requires corresponding clean targets. Vary the amount of paired supervision and compare against unpaired or weakly supervised alternatives.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and principal controlled results, but broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes artifact availability, exact planner configuration and success criteria, evaluation episode counts, encoder/world-model split overlap, real-world encoder-training dataset size, component ablations, and numerical values underlying several representation figures.
