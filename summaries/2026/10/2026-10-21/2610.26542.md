# Breaking Diffusion: Parallel Reasoning with Draft Refinement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26542) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26542)


## One-sentence takeaway
DRLM uses one denoiser to revise complete answers in parallel and retain candidates with better contextual scores, outperforming evaluated autoregressive and diffusion baselines on three structured reasoning benchmarks (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Parallel token revisions can each look plausible while producing an inconsistent answer together. The authors argue that autoregressive commitment and diffusion reverse transitions constrain revision, while existing complete-draft refinement lacks an explicit decision about whether a revision improves the answer (§§1–2).

The central gap is whole-draft evaluation: can the proposing denoiser also assess a completed candidate efficiently, without mistaking confidence caused by observing a token for support from its surrounding context?

## Approach and contributions
- **Complete-draft prediction:** Train with all-position cross-entropy on uniformly corrupted answers, including unchanged positions, to learn preservation and repair. At inference, positionwise argmax predictions directly revise the entire draft without a diffusion reverse transition (§3.1).
- **One-pass contextual scoring:** Divide predicted token probabilities by the known corruption-channel likelihood and renormalize, removing the observed token’s self-evidence. Summed negative log corrected probabilities define *cavity energy*. Recovery of the contextual conditional is exact for a Bayes-optimal denoiser; a trained model provides an estimate (§3.2, Proposition 1).
- **Energy-controlled refinement:** Re-noise the retained draft, propose parallel revisions, then score the completed candidate in a fresh forward pass. Retain it only if energy decreases. Proposal and scoring passes both count toward the inference budget (§3.3).

The distinctive contribution is the combination of channel-corrected scoring and selective draft retention; complete-sequence denoising itself has predecessors acknowledged in §6.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scoring limits:** Cavity energy is neither a normalized sequence likelihood nor a correctness certificate. Exact contextual recovery assumes the exact posterior; correction does not remove arbitrary model error. Near the clean endpoint, it can amplify prediction errors (Appendices E.2 and E.6).
- **Observed ranking failures:** An invalid arithmetic draft can outrank a valid alternative, and a maze solution can be replaced by a lower-energy draft containing an extra cell (Appendix I, Figure 6).
- **Author-stated initialization caveat:** Training does not sample the zero-keep endpoint used for initial decoding. The learned model remains sensitive to the random canvas there; the authors do not claim initialization guarantees diversity (Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited demonstrated scope:** Evaluation covers structured arithmetic and grid puzzles with explicit validators. Transfer to open-ended language, larger models, or different sequence lengths remains unestablished (§4).
- **Comparison caveats:** Countdown autoregressive results are imported from prior work, while the paper describes stricter evaluation for its diffusion runs; the supplied text does not resolve whether imported results satisfy identical checks. Recurrent-model results also differ in Sudoku training regime and Maze correctness metric (Appendix G.3).
- **Reproducibility gap in the retrieval:** Missing table bodies hide much of the architecture, training configuration, decoding settings, and numerical uncertainty.

## Open research questions
1. **Does refinement transfer beyond structured puzzles?** The evaluation establishes only puzzle performance (§4). A next experiment could compare matched models and budgets on language reasoning tasks with independently checked answers.
2. **How reliable is cavity energy under distribution shift?** Its exact interpretation assumes the correct posterior, and ranking failures occur (Appendices E.2 and I). Test energy–correctness ranking on longer answers and shifted puzzle distributions.
3. **Can better proposals close the remaining accuracy gap?** Coverage nearly matches selected accuracy, suggesting proposal generation is the larger observed bottleneck (Appendix H.3). Compare alternative re-noising or proposal strategies at the same total evaluation budget.
4. **Do evaluation-count gains translate into latency gains?** Compute curves count model calls rather than elapsed time (§5). Measure end-to-end latency and memory across matched hardware, batch sizes, and decoders.
