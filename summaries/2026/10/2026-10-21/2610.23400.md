# On the Tip of the Tongue: First-Token Cueing as a Behavioral Probe of Recall Failures in LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23400) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23400)


## One-sentence takeaway
Supplying the gold answer’s first token corrects 21.6–48.6% of baseline errors on multi-token English factual answers across 16 models, providing behavioral evidence for recoverable recall failures without establishing a deployable correction method (§4; Table 6).

## Problem and motivation
Closed-book factual errors can reflect either missing knowledge or failure to produce knowledge a model can otherwise access. The authors investigate where these recall failures arise during generation: earlier work identified first-token error signals, but had not tested whether supplying the correct first token resolves the error (§§1–2).

The distinction matters because improving access to existing knowledge could require different interventions from adding knowledge through training. Human tip-of-the-tongue states motivate the probe; the authors explicitly avoid claiming a shared mechanism (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **First-Token Cueing:** Prefill the assistant response with the primary gold answer’s first tokenizer token, then continue greedily. Baseline and cueing otherwise share prompts and decoding. Headline results exclude single-token answers, where the cue would reveal the entire answer (§§4–5.1).
- **Localization and breadth:** Measure uncertainty by answer position, sweep gold-prefix lengths, and evaluate instruction-tuned Gemma, Llama, and Qwen models on English and multilingual factual QA (§§3–5).
- **Recognition control:** Cross correct and incorrect cues between questions, matching answer type and first-token prior probability. Compare continuation confidence to test recognition beyond cue informativeness; use first-token candidate oracles and sampled reasoning as complementary behavioral checks (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Gold first tokens recover many errors, but can also break correct answers | Across 16 models, pooled TriviaQA/NQ-Open/WebQuestions, gold length ≥2: judge-based recovery is **21.6–48.6% of baseline errors**; **4.2–37.2% of baseline-correct answers** become incorrect. Corrected answers outnumber broken answers in every model. | Table 6 |
| Improvements span all evaluated tasks and languages | Judge accuracy improves in **160/160 model–task cells**: three English datasets plus seven MKQA languages, using model-specific multi-token subsets. MKQA EM and F1 also improve in **112/112 model–language cells**. | Tables 7, 10 |
| First-token uncertainty predicts correctness | Negative first-token entropy yields **0.633–0.832 AUROC** across 16 models on pooled English QA. Position-wise analysis for Llama-3.1 8B on TriviaQA finds first-token entropy nearly as predictive as whole-answer average entropy. | Figure 2; Table 23 |
| The first token dominates minimum-prefix recovery in most models | The shortest successful prefix is one token more often than any other prefix length in every answer-length stratum for **13/16 models**; exceptions are the three lowest-accuracy models. | Appendix B.2; Figure 10 |
| Cue recovery overlaps with independent sampled-reasoning recovery | For Llama-3.1 8B/70B, **77%/62% of cue-recoverable errors** are also recovered by CoT pass@16, versus **59%/47% of all baseline errors**, on pooled English QA with multi-token answers. | §6.1; Appendix E.5; Table 17 |
| Correct starts receive greater continuation confidence than matched incorrect starts | On questions whose gold start is outside top-p candidates, with prior gaps ≤1 nat, correct-cue continuation has lower mean entropy on **60.0–66.7%** of questions across four models; chance is 50%. | Table 2 |
| Gold-free selection falls well short of the oracle | Llama-3.1 8B, pooled English QA, all questions, strict EM: greedy **50.0%**, 36-character search with total-log-probability selection **52.2%**, beam search **53.0%**, gold-token cueing **64.9%**. | Appendix H; Table 22 |

These results establish substantial **cue recoverability** and support the authors’ interpretation that some errors involve failure to access an answer at its start. Recovery alone does not establish stored knowledge: the cue supplies answer information. Reasoning overlap and crossed-cue confidence strengthen the recall interpretation, but remain behavioral evidence rather than direct identification of an internal mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers short-answer factual QA. Extension to long-form claims is proposed but untested; the probe requires gold answers and is diagnostic (§4; Limitations).
- **Cue information varies:** Tokens can reveal whole words or informative fragments. Cue length and script affect gains, so one token is not a uniform information budget across models or languages (§5.3; Appendix F).
- **Grading coverage is limited:** One judge supplies the primary labels. Author checking covers 200 Llama-3.1 8B MKQA responses in four languages, with 93% sample agreement; lexical metrics independently reproduce gains (Appendices A.6, D).
- **Recognition-control imperfections:** Answer types are model-labeled without human verification, one category is broad, and continuation entropy uses an approximation. Fine-type and alternative-confidence robustness checks are reported (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-family comparisons partly mix model capability with tokenizer-dependent question selection, since headline subsets differ by tokenizer (Table 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Reasoning comparisons do not isolate decoding alone: direct answers use demonstrations and a shorter output budget, while CoT uses a different prompt and longer budget (Appendices A.3–A.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Can external evidence select useful starts without gold answers?** Model-only selection remains far below oracle cueing. Next experiment: retrieve candidate starts and measure net corrections and newly broken answers against retrieval and search baselines (§7; Appendix H).
2. **Does the effect extend to long-form factual generation?** The current target is a short answer’s onset. Next experiment: annotate claim boundaries and intervene on the first token of individual factual claims (Limitations).
3. **How much recovery survives equal-information cues across tokenizers?** Current cue strength varies with script and token length. Next experiment: compare cues matched by character coverage or answer ambiguity across languages (§5.3; Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported behavioral effects; **medium** in interpreting them as encoded-knowledge recall failures.

The supplied text does not establish a code/data release, provide the contents of the fixed few-shot demonstrations, or supply internal-state evidence for the proposed failure mechanism. Gold-free recovery of the oracle gains and generalization beyond short-answer factual QA remain unestablished.
