# More Memory, Worse Agents: Error Reproduction and Anti-Persistence in LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27406) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27406)


## One-sentence takeaway
Anti-Persistence improves average held-out success by retaining execution records while discarding task-specific synthesized guidance, although the paper does not establish that causal error reproduction explains the gains (§§4.2, E.3).

## Problem and motivation
Agents can adapt without parameter updates by retrieving past trajectories and inferred rules. The authors identify a possible feedback mechanism, **error reproduction**: faulty rules shape executions, and those executions later produce new rules containing similar errors. Deleting the original rule may therefore leave its influence in stored records (§1).

The central question is whether agents can benefit from abstraction without making inferred guidance persistent.

## Approach and contributions

- **Temporary abstraction:** Anti-Persistence stores tasks, trajectories, outcomes, and mode-selection feedback; synthesized guidance is discarded after execution. This removes direct abstraction reuse while allowing indirect feedback through trajectories (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Adaptive guidance selection:** A contextual-bandit controller chooses direct trajectory reuse, imitation, strategic synthesis, or corrective synthesis. It combines task similarity, recency, exploration, and a synthesis-call penalty (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Evaluation and mechanism analysis:** The authors compare memory designs across multiple update rounds, test persistence separately, and distinguish historical exposure, flagged generation, and persistent admission. The formal analysis provides a probability decomposition, not a guarantee of error amplification (Appendix A; §4).

## Evidence and main results

Main results use three seeds, ten memory-update iterations, and frozen-state held-out evaluation across ALFWorld, WebShop, and ScienceWorld with three backbones (§4.1).

Anti-Persistence ranked highest in seven settings. | §4.2; Table 1 referenced, cells unavailable |
| Lowest average call cost | Lowest mean synthesis-plus-execution LLM-call count among the four memory configurations across nine settings; exact counts are absent from the supplied text. | §4.2; Figure 3; §E.2 |

The comparisons support temporary guidance in the tested settings, including a targeted persistence comparison. They do not establish universal superiority or show that inherited error content causes the performance differences. The provenance audit is observational, and its aggregates were neither recomputed for the revision nor verified against event-level logs (§E.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Temporary guidance can still influence later tasks through retained trajectories; retrieval quality and experience coverage remain important (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Main experiments cover three text-based benchmarks with fixed backbones and procedures. Longer-term gains and broader generalization remain untested; discarding guidance also sacrifices reuse of reliable knowledge (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Operational flags measure unadjusted success associations, with uncontrolled task difficulty, retrieval selection, and dependence among observations. They do not establish causal harm or reproduction (§E.3).
- **Author-stated:** AutoRefine changes retrieval and synthesis as well as maintenance, so that comparison cannot isolate maintenance effectiveness (§D.3). Auxiliary studies lack task-level and seed-level uncertainty; setting-level tests remain exploratory (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Call counts alone do not establish token, latency, monetary, or total training-cost savings. The reported metric covers held-out synthesis and execution calls (§§4.1, E.2).
- **Source limitation:** Several tables contain captions without cells, prompt templates are missing, and figure references conflict with displayed captions, limiting independent verification.

## Open research questions

- **When should verified guidance persist?** Reliable-knowledge reuse is explicitly left unresolved (Appendix H). Compare discard-all against verified selective retention under matched retrieval and context budgets.
- **Does faulty guidance causally reproduce through trajectories?** The audit establishes associations, not inherited content (§E.3). Randomize matched correct and incorrect parent rules, then trace specific errors into executions and newly synthesized guidance.
- **Do benefits survive longer or shifting task streams?** Longer-term generalization is untested (Appendix H). Extend update horizons and introduce task-distribution shifts while tracking success, retrieval coverage, and memory growth.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and aggregate performance findings, but confidence in the proposed causal mechanism is low.

Material missing information includes full table cells and seed-level results, exact call costs, complete prompt templates, hardware/context configurations, provenance group counts and labeling coverage, audit uncertainty procedures, and auxiliary-study raw observations. Code and artifact availability are not established.
