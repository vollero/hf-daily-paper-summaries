# CoEvo-RSI: When Harnesses Evolve, Which Models Should Follow?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25078) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25078)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoEvo-RSI independently reuses historical harnesses and model checkpoints, then fine-tunes on verified self-generated trajectories, producing competitive domain-specific agents while showing that interface compatibility alone does not ensure task success (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Updating an agent’s harness changes its tools, interfaces, memory, and workflow, potentially creating requirements its model has not learned or constraining capabilities it already has. The authors call this **brain–body mismatch** (§1).

The identified gap is model inheritance: the compared co-evolution frameworks generally continue the current checkpoint or a parent-branch policy, rather than independently choosing historical weights suited to a new harness (§2; Appendix D, Table 10).

## Approach and contributions

- **Mismatch diagnostic:** Turn-level Interface Mismatch Rate (TIMR) measures the fraction of model turns containing observable interface violations. It diagnoses execution friction but does not measure all capability mismatch (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Bidirectional historical inheritance:** A Curator organizes archived designs, checkpoints, and execution outcomes. Harness editors choose historical starting designs; model inheritance independently selects checkpoints or combines them using existing TIES-Merging (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Adaptation and promotion:** Selected pairs generate verifier-accepted trajectories for supervised fine-tuning, including historical replay. Validation compares the incumbent, inherited pair, and adapted pair before promotion (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Empirical contribution:** Evaluation spans failure attribution, role-aware SQL, and simulated smart-home control. The distinctive method contribution is coordinated historical inheritance; TIES-Merging and supervised fine-tuning are reused components (§4; Appendix D).

These author-reported results support domain-specific competitiveness and the usefulness of historical reuse. They do not establish universal superiority: some baselines perform better on particular attribution and SQL metrics. Appendix B explicitly states that the evidence does not isolate lower TIMR as the cause of improved accuracy. Table 1’s caption reports three-run averages for CoEvo-RSI, but its numerical cells and uncertainty estimates are absent from the supplied rendering.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated diagnostic boundary:** TIMR misses failures involving reasoning, insufficient evidence, or underused capabilities when tool calls remain valid. Causal attribution requires controlled interventions (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated SQL trade-off:** Conservative refusal may improve compliance while reducing successful execution of authorized queries (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited component isolation:** The reported ablations do not separately isolate checkpoint selection, merging, harness-history reuse, and training replay (§4.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generality:** Experiments use one task-model family and one meta-agent configuration; smart-home evaluation is simulated. Transfer to other model families or physical deployments is unestablished (§4.1; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—selection and comparison risks:** The same validation set repeatedly guides search and promotion. Tests are held out, but validation overfitting remains possible. Some harness-enhanced baseline results come from their original papers rather than matched reruns (Appendix A; §4.1).
- **Cost boundary:** Reported API expenditure is not a complete monetary accounting of training on four H100 GPUs (§4.4; Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which inheritance component contributes most?** Existing ablations bundle several mechanisms. A factorial experiment could independently disable historical harness bases, checkpoint selection, merging, and replay under matched budgets.
2. **Does reducing interface mismatch itself improve accuracy?** Appendix B leaves causality unresolved. Introduce controlled interface-format changes while holding reasoning access, workflow, and execution budget fixed.
3. **How portable is the framework across models?** Only one task model and meta-agent configuration are evaluated. Repeat the same domain protocols with alternative task models and meta-agents.
4. **How much SQL compliance comes from refusal?** The authors acknowledge this possibility. Compare authorized-query execution, over-refusal, and violations at matched refusal rates on Spider and BIRD.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and headline results, but leaves material verification gaps:

- Numerical bodies of Tables 1–3 and several appendix tables are missing.
- Run-level variation, confidence intervals, and statistical significance are unavailable.
- Exact meta-agent prompts are absent despite Appendix E’s introduction.
- Complete merge configurations, promotion margins, retrieval budgets, and replay-selection rules are unavailable.
- Code, checkpoints, evolved harnesses, and the self-constructed training trajectories have no established release location in the supplied text.
