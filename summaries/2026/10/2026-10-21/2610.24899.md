# HybridResearcher: Bridging the Structured-Unstructured Boundary for Deep Research Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24899) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24899)


## One-sentence takeaway
HybridResearcher trains a 4B agent on explicitly interdependent SQL–search trajectories, raising HybridDeepResearch hard-subset pass@8 from 30.83% to 60.83% while improving measured single-source performance over its base model (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Analytical questions can require a database result to guide web research, or a web-resolved entity to parameterize SQL. The authors identify a training gap: learning SQL and search separately does not reliably teach this dependency, and correctness-filtered synthetic trajectories can bypass intended evidence gathering through memorized facts or leaked entities (§§1, 3.1).

The paper addresses training for this boundary crossing. Its contribution is a full recipe for difficult SQL–search workflows; related work already includes simpler hybrid QA training (§2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Bidirectional task construction.** Extend validated SQL or search seeds into SQL-to-search (SQL2S) and search-to-SQL (S2SQL) tasks. SQL-originated tasks add fabricated documents and intersecting clues; search-originated tasks add populated SQLite databases requiring structured computation (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Checks against shortcuts.** Hide bridge entities, test whether SQL predicates affect results, construct unique clue intersections and near-miss distractors, and apply model-based checks against guessing or answer leakage. These checks operationalize the authors’ claim that both sources are necessary (§3; Appendices C–D).
- **Training and execution recipe.** Combine supervised fine-tuning with curated CISPO-based reinforcement learning, five-vote reward judging, and termination penalties for degenerate tool use. A shared harness and frozen retrieval corpus support trajectory generation, training, and hybrid evaluation (§4; Appendices F–G, J).

These results support substantial improvement under the paper’s evaluation protocol and retention on the tested component benchmarks. They do not establish universal elimination of forgetting, uniform RL improvement, or transfer to other source combinations. The sequential data ablation supports incremental benefits but does not isolate data construction from additional training volume.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-identified bottlenecks:** retrieval failures, incorrect SQL despite resolving the right entity, failure to use retrieved answers, and submissions justified by guesses or perceived budget pressure remain common (§6.1; Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference—limited hybrid coverage:** the central evaluation uses one benchmark’s hard subset. Table 17 also reports verbatim duplicate question pairs, reducing independent coverage.
- **Analyst inference—environment dependence:** hybrid training and evaluation share the local retrieval corpus, although the authors state benchmark questions are excluded from training. Live-web search-only gains do not establish live-web hybrid transfer (§5; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—judge validity:** majority voting reduces disagreement and improves training outcomes, but agreement is not independent evidence of correctness. No systematic human-audited judge accuracy is supplied (§6.3; Appendix J.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and attribution limits:** model families use different decoding/token settings; the base frequently fails to submit answers. Gains therefore include improved episode completion alongside evidence reasoning (Appendices H.3, I).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** SFT Parallel avg@8 differs between Tables 1 and 13. Construction checks also combine deterministic and model-based gates, so their claimed necessity guarantees should not be read as a general proof against all shortcuts.

## Open research questions

1. **Does hybrid competence transfer to live enterprise evidence?** Hybrid testing uses frozen retrieval. Next experiment: evaluate unseen database–live-web questions with independently audited evidence chains.
2. **Which construction checks matter most?** The paper provides data-family and RL ablations, but no isolated gate ablation. Next experiment: remove individual checks under matched data and training budgets, then measure source bypass.
3. **Can evidence-triggered verification reduce premature answers?** Guess/budget language correlates with errors, but continuation is observational (Appendix I.2). Next experiment: randomize verification interventions at flagged commits and compare accuracy and cost.
4. **Can the recipe generalize beyond SQL–web tasks?** Other source combinations remain proposed extensions (§7). Next experiment: construct and evaluate dependent tasks joining databases with internal documents or code repositories.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main results and method, but lacks SFT optimization details, total training compute/cost, repeated-training uncertainty, systematic human validation of judging and construction gates, and explicit public artifact access links. Worked-example sections are present without their promised examples.
