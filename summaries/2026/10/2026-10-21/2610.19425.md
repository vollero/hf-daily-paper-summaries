# Evaluating Agentic Capabilities Before Post-Training: An End-to-End Approach

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19425) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19425)


## One-sentence takeaway
ASAP elicits end-to-end agent behavior from frozen base models through structured continuation, revealing checkpoint differences and execution failures that conventional aggregate benchmarks can miss, although results depend on scaffold and reference-prefix guidance (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Standard agent scaffolds expect instruction following, chat protocols, and stable tool use, making direct evaluation of pretrained base models unreliable. Fine-tuning every checkpoint adds cost and changes the model being measured, while static questions and trajectory-likelihood proxies do not expose the evaluated model’s own interactive behavior. The authors seek a practical way to measure both task success and execution behavior during pretraining (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Completion-based scaffold adaptation:** ASAP combines Markdown reasoning with XML action/observation boundaries, without chat templates, system prompts, or weight updates. The authors implement adaptations for existing software-engineering, terminal, and research scaffolds (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Guided initiation and minimal recovery:** Each task receives a short reference-trajectory prefix, shared across evaluated models. The default uses three interaction steps; the authors report checking that these do not reveal the solution. Empty submissions and repeated actions each receive at most one recovery hint (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix A.4.2).
- **Behavioral pretraining diagnostics:** Generated trajectories support analysis of exploration, editing, testing, recovery, and completion across checkpoints and training stages, supplementing aggregate success scores (§5).

These results support ASAP as a discriminative, behaviorally informative evaluation protocol. They do not isolate intrinsic capability from elicitation choices, establish causal effects of individual training ingredients, or demonstrate general prediction of post-trained performance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged confounding:** Stage3 changes trajectory data and native context capability together; their separate contributions cannot be identified (§5.1.1).
- **Author-acknowledged comparison limit:** Base/post-trained comparisons use different protocols and are descriptive rather than controlled estimates of post-training effects (§5.2).
- **Prefix dependence:** Longer prefixes improve performance, and prefixes beyond the default can expose solution-bearing information. Initial exploration also supplies task-relevant guidance (§3.2; Appendix A.4.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Checkpoint analysis covers one internal model run; format and prefix robustness studies cover two models on a SWE-bench subset.
- **Analyst inference—measurement gaps:** No recovery-component ablation or uncertainty intervals are supplied. Behavioral labels are reported without detailed operational definitions.
- **Audit scope:** The network audit measures attempted external access; it does not establish absence of other contamination or shortcuts (Appendix A.6).

## Open research questions
1. **What drives the stage3 gain?** Data and context length change together. A factorial experiment varying trajectory data and native context training independently could separate their effects.
2. **How much capability comes from prefix navigation?** Prefixes can inspect relevant files. Compare task-specific prefixes with protocol-only demonstrations under matched budgets.
3. **Do recovery hints alter model rankings?** Their independent effect is not ablated. Evaluate both recovery mechanisms separately and jointly disabled across models.
4. **Do diagnostics improve training decisions?** Appendix A.7 proposes targeted data synthesis without testing it. Compare model-specific trajectory mixtures against a generic mixture at matched training cost.

## Confidence and missing information
