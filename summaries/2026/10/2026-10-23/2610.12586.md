# Realism VS Accuracy: Event Sequence Forecasting from a Generative Modeling Perspective

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12586) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12586)


## One-sentence takeaway
On the evaluated event-sequence datasets, aggregating latent-diffusion forecasts with Wasserstein BaryBooster achieves the highest reported TMS accuracy while sacrificing substantial distributional realism (Tables 3, 17).

## Problem and motivation
The paper forecasts multiple future events from a history, where each event contains a timestamp and mixed numerical and categorical attributes. Such forecasts could support planning in finance and other domains. The authors identify two gaps: limited systematic evaluation of heterogeneous, long-horizon event forecasting, and insufficient attention to whether accurate forecasts resemble plausible continuations (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and metrics:** Curates existing datasets and adapts statistical, transformer, diffusion, and matching-based forecasters. Introduces Temporal Matching Score (TMS), using a shared event alignment across heterogeneous features, and adapts classifier two-sample testing into history-conditioned cDFS (§§3–4).
- **Conditional latent diffusion:** Adapts a per-event β-VAE plus sequence diffusion, conditioned on a GRU history embedding and recent event latents. This is an adaptation of existing approaches, rather than an entirely new model family (§3.3).
- **Wasserstein BaryBooster:** Aggregates sampled continuations without retraining, using medoid initialization, alternating Hungarian alignment, and numerical medians/categorical modes. The implementation finds a local solution to a surrogate objective; it does not guarantee a globally TMS-optimal forecast (§5; Appendix F).
- **Empirical finding:** Reports a persistent separation between accuracy and realism, with aggregation moving the observed Pareto frontier (§6.2).

## Evidence and main results

Values within transaction-dataset triples follow **Alphabattle / Gender / Age**; higher scores are better.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| LDM-WB leads reported accuracy | At horizon L=32, TMS is **0.495 / 0.436 / 0.522**, versus DEF’s 0.424 / 0.413 / 0.508 and Repeat’s 0.440 / 0.361 / 0.454. | Table 3 |
| Aggregation reduces realism | At L=32, LDM cDFS is **0.856 / 0.907 / 1.000**; LDM-WB falls to **0.177 / 0.256 / 0.224**. | Table 3 |
| Gains extend beyond optimized TMS | On Age at L=32, LDM → LDM-WB improves amount R1 **0.216→0.438**, time R1 **0.074→0.331**, and MCC F1 **0.323→0.371**. | Tables 4, 9 |
| Transfer tests show similar rankings | At L=32, LDM-WB versus DEF: Retweet TMS **0.663 vs 0.653**, cDFS **0.672 vs 0.017**; Amazon TMS **0.438 vs 0.430**, cDFS **0.243 vs 0.002**. These datasets contain time and one mark, without amounts. | Appendix L, Table 17 |
| Limited repeated runs support the Age accuracy ranking | Age, L=32, three-seed TMS mean ± standard deviation: LDM-WB **0.523±0.001**, DEF **0.505±0.004**, LDM **0.430±0.002**. | Appendix E.5, Table 10 |
| Aggregation adds substantial inference cost | On the full Age test split at L=64, LDM-WB with N=30 takes **52.4 minutes**, versus **1.2 minutes** for LDM under the stated one-job-per-GPU protocol. | Appendix J, Table 14 |

These results support aggregation as an accuracy improvement for the evaluated generators and settings. They do not prove the authors’ stronger characterization of a **fundamental** accuracy–realism trade-off. Likewise, high cDFS establishes difficulty for the tested discriminator, rather than equality of conditional distributions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The main benchmark contains only card transactions. Retweet and Amazon broaden coverage but lack the heterogeneous numerical features central to the task (§7; Appendix L).
- **Author-stated cost and optimization limits:** BaryBooster requires multiple generations and iterative matching, and uses a categorical surrogate with local optimization (§7; Appendix F).
- **Detector validity:** The authors document detector underfitting that inflates Repeat’s cDFS, including at longer horizons. Absolute cDFS values therefore require caution (Appendices D.2, E.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited uncertainty coverage:** Repeated training results cover selected methods on Age, rather than the full benchmark; most reported comparisons lack uncertainty estimates (Appendix E.5).
- **Analyst inference—reporting ambiguity:** LDM’s Age cDFS differs between Table 3 and the default-detector column of Table 5; the supplied text does not reconcile them. Detector evaluation is also described as three seeds in D.1 and three-fold cross-validation in E.5.
- **Coverage limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, preventing complete assessment of preprocessing, sample-count ablations, and other omitted details.

## Open research questions
- **Does the result generalize to heterogeneous non-financial events?** The transfer tests omit numerical side features. Next experiment: evaluate the same methods on a suitably sized non-financial corpus with mixed attributes.
- **Can aggregation preserve more realism at comparable accuracy?** LDM-WB improves TMS but sharply lowers cDFS. Next experiment: compare stochastic or realism-constrained aggregation against BaryBooster at matched sampling budgets.
- **How reliable is cDFS when detectors fail to fit?** Documented failures inflate realism scores. Next experiment: repeat evaluation with detector convergence checks, multiple seeds, and history-grouped splits.
- **How much does the categorical surrogate affect aggregation?** Appendix F establishes only a local surrogate solution. Next experiment: compare it with direct TMS optimization on small horizons where stronger optimization is feasible.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied tables clearly support the central accuracy gain and realism loss, but coverage is incomplete.

Material missing or unresolved information includes the truncated preprocessing/splitting details, complete sample-count results, broad multi-seed uncertainty, reconciliation of cDFS discrepancies, and independent verification of the claimed code release.
