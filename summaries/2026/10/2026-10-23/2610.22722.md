# The LLM-Attacker: A Unified Red-Team Framework for Privacy Evaluation of Split and Distributed LLM Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22722) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22722)


## One-sentence takeaway
LLM-Attack Suite makes split-LLM privacy evaluations inspectable through explicit attacker contracts, replayable observations and linked controls, with controlled GPT-2 results showing that returned gradients substantially increase exact-document reconstruction (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Keeping raw text on a client does not prevent reconstruction from activations and returned training gradients. Comparisons become difficult when studies vary attacker access, model knowledge, calibration data, computation or recovery metrics. Defences can also look effective because the chosen attack already fails without protection (§1).

The authors identify a methodological gap: evaluations need explicit experimental contracts, competent undefended controls, retained predictions and visible records of untested threats.

## Approach and contributions
- **Explicit evaluation protocol:** Record target, attacker privileges, observations, knowledge, budgets, corpus, metrics and controls. Compatible attacks replay the same captured traffic, enabling paired comparisons and later extensions (§§2.1–2.4).
- **Checkable scoring and coverage:** Compare recovery against corpus-specific baselines; require repeated runs and a linked qualifying undefended reference before a defence passes. Report missing tests and implementation qualification separately (§3).
- **Evidence and reusable artifacts:** Retain predictions, configurations, provenance and source snapshots for verification, rescoring and regeneration. Evaluate the procedure through reconstruction studies, an adapted BiSR reference comparison and measurement-code tests (§§4–5; S4).

The central contribution is the evaluation procedure and evidence organization. The paper explicitly treats many attacks as adaptations or qualified implementations rather than new attack families; its main GPT-2 experiment shares evidence with a companion study (§§1, 7; Table S15).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Gradients increase reconstruction | GPT-2 124M, depth six, public client layers, 256 held-out 32-token WikiText-2 documents, ten model seeds and two attack seeds: token recovery **94.20% → 97.38%**; exact-document recovery **13.71% → 37.77%**. Reported unrounded token gain: **3.1714 pp**, 95% bootstrap interval **[2.72, 3.64]**, exceeding the prespecified two-point threshold. | §5.1; Table 1; §§S3.1, S6 |
| Controls support an informational gradient benefit | In that primary setting, shuffled-gradient and random-candidate controls recovered **93.91%** and **93.89%**, versus **97.38%** with true gradients. Candidate count and beam width were matched, but total computation was not. | §§2.2, 4.1, 5.1 |
| Corpus choice changes recovery and baseline | Undefended depth-six GPT-2, public layers, inference, three seeds and 3,072 pooled positions per corpus: code **64.94%** recovery versus **20.31%** baseline; synthetic clinical templates **100%** versus **6.25%**; WikiText-2 **63.25%** versus **2.64%**. | Table 2; §S3.2 |
| Low document recovery can coexist with sensitive-span leakage | Secret mixup yielded **83.01–90.67%** token recovery and at most **1.48%** exact-document recovery, yet **47.85–66.15%** name-span recovery across 716 annotated spans. Geometric-mean perplexity increased **31.8%** at depth three and **26.9%** at depth six. | §5.1; §S3.1 |
| Compression retained recoverable information | Defence-aware optimization recovered depth-mean token rates of **18.85–68.59%** under stable compressors and **23.52–67.68%** under separately specified co-trained bottlenecks; means average three widths and three seeds. | §5.2; Table S7 |
| BiSR conformance does not imply universal superiority | Port and archived reference agreed on all **3,072 paired predictions** under the local single-seam contract. In the depth-six/public-layer adapted comparison, BiSR recovered **93.21%**, versus joint reconstruction’s **97.38%**: difference **−4.17 pp**, 95% interval **[−4.79, −3.53]**. Budgets were attack-specific. | §5.3; §S3.4 |
| Selected measurement checks passed | **37/37** fixed cases passed, covering metrics, controls, observation boundaries, solvers, resampling and evidence validation using constructed inputs and small models. | §5.4; Table S11 |

These results establish substantial leakage and consequential evaluation choices under the declared protocols. They do not establish a universal attack ranking, a general privacy guarantee or equivalent effectiveness across the registry. Reference agreement and measurement-code conformance establish narrower properties than real-target attack effectiveness.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The strongest reconstruction comparisons concern GPT-2 124M, short specified corpora and fixed budgets. Broader Qwen/Mistral studies provide heterogeneous qualification evidence rather than a matched replication (§6).
- **Author-stated scoring limits:** The Wilson-based gate is an operational convention. Token dependence limits its binomial interpretation, and its multiplier supplies no simultaneous confidence guarantee across all modules. Finite-sample floors can also qualify weak controls, requiring inspection (§3).
- **Comparison limits:** Matching candidates and beam width does not equalize computation. Adapted BiSR uses a single split rather than its published two-cut protocol (§§2.2, 4.2; S3.4).
- **Data validity:** Clinical notes are synthetic templates; span annotations are not human gold labels. Several pilot studies use development corpora (§§5.1–5.2; S3.2–S3.3).
- **Reproducibility access:** Source is reported public, but evidence files require Hugging Face login and manual approval. The maintained registry also differs from the reviewed taxonomy (Evidence and Supplementary Material; Table S13).
- **Analyst inference:** A frozen post-training capture does not establish equivalent reconstruction during evolving live training; the main experiment explicitly uses a fixed snapshot (§S2).
- **Evidence dependence:** The companion paper’s main result is shared evidence, not independent replication (§1; Table S3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the gradient benefit persist during live training?** The main campaign attacks frozen snapshots (§S2). Next experiment: capture successive training states and compare paired attacks under fixed knowledge and budgets.
2. **How do attack rankings change with equal computation?** Current comparisons use attack-specific budgets (§4.2). Next experiment: measure recovery curves against shared runtime and work limits.
3. **Can compression preserve utility while resisting stronger attacks?** White-box attacks retain recovery, while one projection defence substantially impairs learning (§§S3.2–S3.3). Next experiment: jointly measure utility and multi-attack recovery across compression settings.
4. **How well do verdicts transfer to larger targets and longer documents?** Strongest matched evidence remains concentrated on GPT-2 and short passages (§6). Next experiment: repeat the paired protocol on larger models and longer held-out texts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this text-grounded summary; broader generalization remains limited.

Unavailable here are independently inspected artifacts, full machine-readable per-condition outputs, an equal-compute attack ranking, human-validated sensitive-span labels and matched large-model replications of the principal experiment. Release availability and reproducibility were reported by the authors, not independently verified.
