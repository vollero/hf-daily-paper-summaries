# Diffusion Language Models Change Speed Across Tasks and Languages

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25835) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25835)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across two adaptive diffusion language models, decoding requires fewer forward passes per token for copied text than open-ended writing, and weighting math answers by final-answer decoding speed improves five-sample voting accuracy by 2.3 percentage points in DiffusionGemma (§§3, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Adaptive diffusion models can commit multiple tokens per forward pass, so equally long outputs can require different amounts of computation. The authors identify a gap in evaluations concentrated on math and code: how decoding effort varies across tasks, languages, and parts of a reasoning response is less understood (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Comparative analysis:** Evaluate DiffusionGemma-26B-A4B-it and DreamReasoner-8B across ten tasks, measuring cost as forward passes per 100 generated tokens, excluding prompt processing (§2; Appendix A).
- **Output-property analysis:** Combine surface-copying novelty and empirical task-level word surprisal into “new information” (NI), then examine its association with decoding cost (§3.1).
- **Reasoning and language analyses:** Study thinking versus final-answer costs, correctness-conditioned math costs, and English-to-25-language translation costs using DiffusionGemma (§§4–5).
- **Answer selection:** Weight candidate answers’ votes by final-answer tokens per forward pass, reusing decoding counts already collected during generation (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Task substantially changes per-token effort | Thinking off: DiffusionGemma grammar correction costs 3.72 versus story generation 11.43 passes per 100 tokens, a reported 3.1× gap. DreamReasoner costs 22.21 and 95.47 respectively, after exclusions. | §3; Table C.1 |
| NI correlates with task-level cost | Across ten task means per model: Pearson r = +0.76, p = 0.011 for DiffusionGemma; +0.85, p = 0.002 for DreamReasoner. P-values are uncorrected. | Table 1; Fig. 2 |
| Copying from thinking accompanies cheaper final answers | DiffusionGemma, thinking on: answer novelty relative to thinking correlates with answer-minus-thinking cost across ten tasks, r = +0.89, p = 0.001. | §4.1; Fig. 4 |
| Incorrect math answers require more effort | DiffusionGemma, thinking on: incorrect final answers require +0.83 passes per 100 tokens when holding problem and output length fixed across seeds. | §4.2, p. 7 |
| Tokenization disadvantage accompanies additional per-token cost | Same 200 FLORES sentences translated into 25 languages; among 20 space-delimited languages, reference tokens per word correlates with generation cost at r = +0.65, p = 0.002. | §5; Fig. 6; Appendix D |
| Speed-weighted voting improves math accuracy | DiffusionGemma, 255 problems, five runs each: five-answer accuracy increases from 71.4% for majority voting to 73.7%; bootstrap 95% CI for gain: [0.3, 4.5] percentage points. Two-answer gain is 4.2 points. | §6; Fig. 7 |

These results establish associations under the tested decoding settings and an answer-selection improvement on the sampled math problems. They do not establish that copying, word rarity, or tokenization causally determines effort. Forward-pass counts also do not directly establish wall-clock speedups or equal computational cost across architectures; Figure 1 explicitly notes throughput differences.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated model scope:** Only two suitable adaptive models are studied; broader scales and training recipes remain untested (Limitations, p. 9).
- **Author-stated measurement gaps:** Surface novelty misses content reuse across languages, while word-level surprisal misses discourse-level unpredictability (§3.1).
- **Exclusion sensitivity:** DreamReasoner excludes 143 of 300 stories that hit the token limit. Including repetitive truncated outputs lowers story cost from 95.47 to 54.7, materially changing the task comparison (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generalization:** Reasoning, multilingual, and voting results use only DiffusionGemma; the paper does not demonstrate their transfer to DreamReasoner or other models (§§4–6).
- **Analyst inference—confounded model comparison:** Architecture, size, block size, and commitment rules differ together. Their individual contributions to absolute cost are not isolated (Table A.1).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Appendix C reports 3,860 generations, while Table B.1 lists 2,468 items per model. DreamReasoner costs also differ between Tables 1 and C.1, including extractive summarization and WildBench. Exact run accounting and some reported means therefore remain ambiguous.
- **Coarse difficulty control:** Contest position is explicitly described as a rough proxy rather than a calibrated difficulty measure (§4.2, footnote 1).

## Open research questions
- **Does decoding speed reliably select answers beyond this setting?** Voting is tested only on DiffusionGemma competition math. Next experiment: compare speed-weighted and majority voting on another adaptive model and on code tasks with executable correctness checks.
- **Which model settings drive task-dependent cost?** Block size and commitment rules vary alongside model architecture. Next experiment: vary these settings within one model while measuring both correctness and decoding cost.
- **Can semantic reuse explain NI’s failures?** Translation and discourse-heavy tasks deviate from the word-based measure. Next experiment: test semantic-reuse and discourse-sensitive predictors alongside NI on held-out tasks.
- **What causes the multilingual cost association?** The translation study correlates reference tokenization with effort without isolating causality. Next experiment: compare matched translation evaluations across models with different tokenizers and decoding settings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main findings have explicit settings and references, but numerical inconsistencies reduce confidence in exact cross-task accounting.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes a code or analysis release, wall-clock and energy measurements, full regression specifications and uncertainty for correctness controls, detailed voting tie handling, and quality-matched comparisons across the ten tasks.
