# SCOPE: Compiling Natural-Language Policies into Neurosymbolic Predicate Programs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31062) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31062)


## One-sentence takeaway
SCOPE jointly optimizes executable policy decompositions and adversarial training examples, achieving best-or-tied performance in 10 of 12 task–executor settings while enabling transfer to non-reasoning models (§5).

## Problem and motivation
Compound policies require semantic judgments, exceptions, counting, and interactions across conversations or agent traces. The authors argue that small monolithic classifiers struggle with this complexity, while frontier judges incur high inference costs and inconsistent verdicts. They identify two gaps in existing neurosymbolic approaches: restricted composition languages and optimization against fixed datasets that may underrepresent policy clauses (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable policy decomposition:** SCOPE represents a policy as self-contained neural classification predicates, deterministic extractors, and Python composition code. Code selects relevant spans, repeats or conditionally invokes predicates, and computes exact counts (§3.1).
- **Joint program and data optimization:** Strong-model agents inspect execution traces and revise both predicate wording and composition. Local adversaries target predicate drift; a systemic adversary targets missing clauses or incorrect interactions. A teacher independently labels generated examples, admitting them only when its label agrees with the generator’s intended label (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Executor-aware compilation:** Search evaluates predicates using a weak model with reasoning disabled, encouraging decompositions that deployment models can execute. The final candidate is selected by performance on the fixed human-labeled seed pool (§3).
- **Benchmark and empirical evaluation:** The authors release four policy-enforcement datasets and evaluate accuracy, executor transfer, repeated-run stability, estimated inference costs, and removal of adversarial generation (§§4–5; Appendices B–D).

The claimed methodological novelty is the combination of expressive code composition, jointly revised predicates, and policy-targeted adversarial data generation—not decomposition or counterexample generation individually (§2).

These results support useful decomposition, transfer, and stability on the supplied benchmark. They do not establish universal policy correctness or production-scale performance. The stability comparison changes both model and decomposition, so it does not isolate decomposition’s causal effect. Cost figures are assumption-based inference estimates, excluding compilation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Correctness depends on the teacher’s interpretation of the policy rather than a formal verifier; verification remains open (§6).
- **Analyst inference—limited coverage:** Evaluation covers four application policies, with test sets of 71, 286, 81, and 124 examples. Several datasets contain manually adapted or authored cases, limiting evidence for broader deployment distributions (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The ablation removes both adversaries together. It does not isolate local versus systemic generation or compare against an equal-sized non-adversarial expansion (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison controls:** Prompt optimizers use the initial pool while SCOPE grows its data; AFlow is restricted to the Custom operator but may elicit intermediate reasoning. These comparisons evaluate configured systems rather than isolate structure alone (§4.2).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** Table 2’s caption broadly claims superiority over all baselines on every task, but its entries show AFlow winning Escalation.
- **Reproducibility gap:** Appendix F contains only a heading in the supplied text; the promised agent prompts are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Which adversary contributes most?** The joint ablation cannot answer this. Compare local-only, systemic-only, combined, and equal-budget non-adversarial generation.
- **How reliable is teacher supervision?** Generator–teacher agreement does not prove correctness (§6). Have independent human annotators audit admitted examples and final program errors.
- **Does decomposition itself improve stability?** Current stability comparisons also change models (§5.1). Compare decomposed and undecomposed execution using identical models and decoding settings.
- **When does compilation pay off?** Only inference costs are estimated (Appendix C). Measure compilation expenditure, serving latency, and total cost across deployment volumes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark findings; **medium** in broader efficiency and deployment conclusions.

Material missing information includes the promised compilation prompts, optimization cost and latency, detailed baseline search budgets, executable program/code release status, and independent validation of teacher-generated labels.
