# TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30043) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30043)


## One-sentence takeaway
TARSphere integrates physics-based robot execution, constrained human interaction, and changing task requirements into a benchmark where stronger model reasoning improves completion but leaves substantial adaptation and safety failures.

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon robots must respond to changing goals, physical obstacles, and human availability while executing actions that can fail. The authors identify a gap between systems evaluated on fixed goals and benchmarks that abstract execution into symbolic actions: neither fully captures how physical execution and evolving social requirements jointly determine success (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Execution infrastructure:** TARSphere-Infra combines simulated robot dynamics, embodied NPCs, spatially constrained dialogue, and an event-driven task FSM. State changes require completed actions or delivered messages; evaluation checks evidence against the requirements active when it was produced (§3.2.1).
- **Frozen-model agent harness:** TARSphere-Harness combines multimodal observations, hierarchical memory, navigation/manipulation skills, and measured execution feedback for repeated planning by a frozen MLLM (§3.2.2).
- **Benchmark and curation pipeline:** TARSphere-Bench contains 360 episodes across 30 indoor scenes, covering navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver. Base tasks have paired perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent understanding variants. Agent synthesis, review, human audit, and simulation validation align semantics, event dependencies, and geometry (§3.3).

The contribution is the integrated framework, evaluation design, and curation process; the paper does not introduce a newly trained foundation model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| General-purpose models can outperform embodied models | Across eight frozen MLLMs on 360 episodes, Gemini-3.8-Flash achieves 40.6% navigate-and-report and 47.8% fetch-and-deliver Success, versus Claude Sonnet-5’s 16.9% and 24.6%. Both evaluated embodied models complete no episodes. | §4.2; Table 2 discussed, cells absent from supplied text |
| Completion coexists with frequent physical failures | Gemini collision incidence is 84% for navigation and 98% for delivery; fall incidence is 14% and 16%, respectively, across successful and failed episodes. | §§4.1–4.2 |
| Execution assistance does not remove social/adaptation gaps | On 30 matched delivery Base tasks and their DA/TU/HC variants, full assistance raises Base Success from 60.0% to 66.7%. TU and HC remain 13.3 and 16.7 percentage points below assisted Base; the DA gap narrows by 3.3 points. | §4.3.2; Table 4 |
| Harness components support average performance | For Gemini on a selected subset balanced across task families and conditions, six component removals reduce average Success from 45.8% to 20.8–41.7%; removing grasp skills causes the largest decline, 25.0 percentage points. | §4.3.1; full ablation table unavailable |
| Isolated skill reliability exceeds end-to-end completion | Under valid calls, grasping succeeds in 229/240 trials. Across 300 visible-target views, grounding detects 285 correct instances and localizes 260 within 10 cm of reference positions. | §4.3.4; supplied Table 5 |
| Review materially improves curation yield | Across 360 matched specifications, the full pipeline produces 96.7% usable episodes versus 45.0% without multi-agent review. | §4.3.5 |
| Semantic scoring broadly agrees with human scoring | On 180 trajectories, plan SRCC/PLCC is 0.86/0.88; inspection/inquiry-report SRCC/PLCC is 0.91/0.92. | §4.4 |

Progress averages dependency-eligible quality across five stages; Success requires completed reporting or accepted handover and excludes terminal falls or object loss. Ordinary contact does not itself invalidate Success (§4.1).

These results support the authors’ claim that execution, adaptation, and interaction require separate assessment. They establish performance within this shared harness and simulator, rather than general superiority of a model family or real-world readiness. The assistance study reports descriptive matched contrasts, without uncertainty estimates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers one robot body and two indoor service task families in simulation. Transfer must address sensing, contact, and human-behavior differences (§5).
- **Analyst inference—restricted social realism:** NPC answers are episode-defined and constrained by authorized topics. This limits conclusions about open-ended human interaction (§3.2.1).
- **Analyst inference—interface-dependent rankings:** Models share the same harness, but failures include tool-interface and task-state errors. Rankings therefore reflect model–harness compatibility as well as embodied reasoning (§§4.1, 4.3.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited intervention generality:** Harness ablations and controlled assistance use Gemini and selected subsets; their effects are not established across all evaluated models (§§4.3.1–4.3.2).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Several tables have captions without cells, and prose cross-references conflict with displayed table numbering. The supplied text also does not explain the relationship between the binary-scored failure dataset and the main quality-scored results (§§4.1–4.4).

## Open research questions

- **Can the framework transfer across embodiments and real environments?** Only one simulated body is evaluated (§5). A next experiment could run matched tasks on the planned Mobile-Franka setup and measure completion, safety, and feedback calibration.
- **What reduces task-update and coordination deficits?** Tested execution assistance leaves their Base-relative Success gaps unchanged (§4.3.2). Compare explicit goal-revision tracking and coordination policies under matched physical assistance.
- **How stable are rankings across skill interfaces?** The paper uses a shared harness and reports substantial interface failures (§§4.1, 4.3.3). Cross the evaluated models with alternative interfaces and controller backends while retaining task requirements and budgets.
- **Can safety improve without sacrificing completion?** Collisions remain frequent even for the strongest model (§4.2). Test safety-aware action selection and recovery against the default harness, measuring both Success and collision/fall incidence.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative supports the central findings, but full main-result and ablation tables, repeated-run uncertainty, valid-run counts by comparison, exact prompts and semantic-judge configuration, detailed assistance implementations, human-rater procedures, and verified release contents are unavailable.
