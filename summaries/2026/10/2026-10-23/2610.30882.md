# Inherited, Not Invented: Tracing the Provenance of Wait in Reasoning Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30882) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30882)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the studied open-model lineages, *wait* usually appears before RL or through trace fine-tuning, while RL changes its frequency and error-signalling properties in recipe-specific ways (§§4–4.1).

## Problem and motivation
Increasing use of *wait* during RL is often interpreted as newly acquired reflection. The authors distinguish the origin of this lexical marker from the origin of reflective behaviour: prior work, they argue, had not traced when the word enters training histories or tested whether its meaning remains stable. This matters for interpreting reasoning gains and calibrating uncertainty heuristics or trace monitors (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Training-stage provenance:** Compare open checkpoints and documented descendants across pretraining, mid-training, trace fine-tuning and RL, supplemented by controlled GRPO runs and marker-seeding experiments (§§3–4).
- **Output-based measurement:** Track occurrence frequency, sentence position, canonical spelling, repetition chains, sentence length and usage-distribution drift. An injected-arithmetic-error test measures marker sensitivity and false alarms without behavioural judges (§3).
- **Recipe comparisons and validation:** Show differing marker trajectories under released RL-Zero recipes and the authors’ GRPO; audit automated revision labels against expert consensus (§§4–4.1).
- **Reported reusable assets:** A provenance map covering 120 endpoints, measurement code, result keys and expert-consensus audits (§1; Ethical Considerations). Availability is reported by the authors, not independently verified here.

## Evidence and main results

All findings below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large accuracy gains usually occur without installing *wait*. | Across 18 counted zero-RL runs from initially marker-free bases, median MATH-500 accuracy gain was 42.9 percentage points; 17 final models remained below 0.05 occurrences/1k tokens, with 95% upper bounds below 0.06/1k. | §4; Tables 1, 13 |
| One counted zero-RL run is a counterexample. | General-Reasoner-Qwen3-4B reached 0.29 *wait*/1k tokens, 95% CI [0.24, 0.34], from a base below 0.01/1k; MATH-500 accuracy rose from 71.2% to 79.0%. | §4; Tables 2, 13 |
| Marker onset aligns with data-training stages. | OLMo-3 rose from 0.04 to 4.03 *wait*/1k during mid-training. Of 14 trace-fine-tuned descendants of marker-free bases, 13 reached at least 0.5/1k. | §4; Tables 1–2 |
| Amplification depends on the RL recipe. | From the same OLMo-3 base, KL-free GRPO at step 400 yielded marker-rate ratios of 0.81 on all responses and 0.93 on finished responses; the released recipe reached 1.58 and 1.70 respectively, under matched evaluation. | §4; Appendix D.2, “Controlled fork” |
| OLMo-3 RL-Zero changes marker form. | Canonical *Wait,* share fell from 88.9% to 37.5%; marker-initial sentences shortened from 11.8 to 6.1 words. | §4; Figure 3 |
| A seeded marker can disappear while accuracy improves. | After fine-tuning Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on 512 marker-bearing traces, GRPO reduced *wait* from 7.52/1k to zero occurrences in 3,066,514 tokens; MATH-500 accuracy rose from 41.0% to 77.6%. A longer-limit control also showed near-elimination among finished responses. | §4; Appendix D.2 |
| Error signalling becomes more sensitive and more liberal. | In OLMo-3 RL-Zero, any-marker d′ rose from 0.57 to 1.09, final 95% CI [0.97, 1.24], while criterion c fell from 0.11 to −0.24 on paired clean/arithmetic-error prefixes. | §4.1; Figure 4 |
| Automated revision trends can reverse human-labelled trends. | In a 60-sentence weighted blind audit, the primary judge’s revision rate fell from 26.0% to 12.9%, while consensus labels rose from 1.3% to 15.9%. | §4.1; Appendix D.2 |
| The causal insertion test is inconclusive. | The value-change detector failed its validity check; judge-based insertion effects remained exploratory because human-audit sensitivity was low. | Appendix D.2, “Operator (H4)” |

These results support separating lexical-marker frequency from reasoning improvement and measuring marker meaning separately at each checkpoint. They do not establish the individual training examples responsible for marker acquisition, prove an internal reflection mechanism, or resolve whether inserting *wait* causes useful revision.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Provenance comparisons are observational and mix differences in data and recipes. Open checkpoint-series intervals capture evaluation sampling, not training-run variability (§3; Limitations).
- **Restricted scope:** The RL evidence concerns English mathematics, outcome rewards and the studied model sizes; frontier-scale models, other languages, domains and process rewards remain untested (Limitations).
- **Measurement validity:** Lexical forms do not measure reflection itself; unlisted markers escape detection, and the alarm test covers injected arithmetic errors only. Consensus audits are small and lack independent double annotation (Limitations).
- **Theory support is partial:** Several preregistered grammaticalization predictions failed; the registered gate sequence was not applied. The insertion experiment cannot support either a causal effect or a reliable null (Table 12; Appendices D.2–D.3).
- **Analyst observations:** Some supplied numerical accounts disagree, including Qwen3.5 rates in prose versus tables. The revision audit is described as comparing the final checkpoint in §4.1 but step 1900 in Appendix D.2. These discrepancies remain unresolved.
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated, so this analysis does not cover the complete paper or appendices.

## Open research questions
- **What data exposure causes acquisition?** Stage-level provenance does not isolate examples. A next experiment could hold the training mixture fixed while varying marker-bearing traces.
- **Which RL components determine marker survival?** The controlled fork establishes recipe differences but does not isolate every driver. A matched, multi-seed factorial experiment could vary KL, length pressure and group size separately.
- **Does *wait* causally improve correction?** The insertion test remains inconclusive. Repeat matched *Wait,*/*Next,* interventions with independently annotated correction outcomes and a validated detector.
- **Does checkpoint-specific calibration transfer beyond arithmetic?** Only injected arithmetic errors were tested. Evaluate matched logical and factual errors across languages and domains.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main lexical findings, but truncation, observational provenance and internal inconsistencies constrain interpretation.

Material unavailable information includes the omitted experimental details and full endpoint records, exact acquisition examples, some training mixtures, complete decoding configurations and independently verified artifact contents. Training-seed uncertainty for released trajectories and a valid causal insertion result are also absent.
