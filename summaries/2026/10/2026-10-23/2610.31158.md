# Beyond Watching and Searching: Benchmarking Agents for Video-centered Deep Research Reports

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31158) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31158)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmniDR-Bench evaluates agents that turn a source video and external multimodal evidence into interactive research reports, finding substantially stronger presentation than evidence coverage and claim support.

## Problem and motivation

Users may need to verify a video’s claims, reproduce its procedures, or make decisions from its demonstrations. These needs require connecting audiovisual details and timestamps to external evidence, rather than merely summarizing speech.

The authors identify a gap between text-centered research reports, multimodal reports without a source video, and video research benchmarks focused mainly on short-answer QA: none evaluates the complete video-centered report setting they propose (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task and reference workflow:** Given a video and user request, produce a cited, interactive HTML report. A seven-stage skill workflow builds an audiovisual timeline, selects research anchors, retrieves multimodal evidence, synthesizes relations, renders the report, and validates it (§2; Appendix D).
- **Evaluation and construction methods:** Omni Atomic Rubric Trees (Omni-ARTs) organize task-specific binary criteria under content, citation, and presentation dimensions and 23 shared directions. Audited Rubric Construction (ARC) independently collects evidence, screens criteria, calibrates behavior, and applies iterative human review (§3.2).
- **Benchmark and analysis:** OmniDR-Bench contains 50 Chinese and English tasks across 19 subcategories and 6,414 criteria. The authors report releasing the benchmark, workflow, evaluator, configurations, and trajectories, and compare 18 agent configurations (§§3–4; Reproducibility Statement).

| External evidence integration is weak | Unweighted means across 18 configuration-level atomic pass rates are 79.9% for direct-video facts, 8.7% for external evidence, 8.8% for independent verification/conflict handling, and 5.0% for multi-fact composition. | §4.2; Appendix F.3, Table 16 |
| Source collection does not ensure claim support | GPT-5.6 Sol under Claude Code passes 98.0% of source-count criteria and 93.5% of multimodal-material criteria, but 23.9% of claim-support criteria. | §4.2; Appendix Table 17 |
| Orchestration improves quality at additional cost | On 15 matched tasks using GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Code, the full workflow exceeds all atomic skills by 7.3 overall points, paired 95% CI [3.4, 11.1], winning 12 tasks. Citation gains 20.7 points; content gains 0.8. Median time rises from 0.7 to 1.6 hours and runner-reported tokens from 2.3M to 4.7M. | §4.3; Tables 5 and 21; Appendix F.5 |

The evidence supports a benchmark-specific gap between polished delivery and supported research. The controlled ablation supports the deployed workflow’s quality–cost benefit, but does not isolate orchestration from the additional computation it induces. Scores are budget-normalized rubric aggregates, not percentages of all audited facts covered (Appendix A.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limits:** The task set is small, bilingual, curated, and not population representative. Subcategory and query-form comparisons are descriptive because groups are small and queries were not randomly assigned (§4.2; Ethics Statement; Appendix I).
- **Author-stated evaluation dependence:** Some directions reflect capabilities emphasized by the reference skills. A fixed judge may retain model-specific bias; human report validation covers only two generators. The common two-pane interface also limits conclusions about unrestricted report design (Appendices A.3, A.6, I).
- **Author-stated experimental limits:** The skill ablation does not hold realized compute constant, and it does not separately measure frames versus transcripts. Cross-harness token accounting is not fully comparable (Appendices B.2, F.5–F.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Requiring calibration reports from one model–harness pair to improve with evidence access may favor tasks responsive to that pair’s behavior. Calibration demonstrates sensitivity under that setup, not universal evaluator validity (§3.2; Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Because coverage is measured against a finite audited fact library, useful findings outside that library may be incompletely reflected in coverage scores. The authors allow alternative sources and organization, but do not quantify this risk (§3.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much do visual and temporal inputs add beyond transcripts?** Curation requires audiovisual dependence, but no separate frames-versus-transcripts ablation is reported. Compare transcript-only, frames-only, and combined inputs on matched tasks and budgets.
2. **Can citation improvements persist at equal compute?** Orchestration improves citation scores while increasing resource use. Compare the workflow with atomic skills under matched token and wall-time budgets.
3. **Does evaluator agreement generalize across systems and layouts?** Human validation uses two report generators, while the benchmark uses a shared interface. Repeat blinded human evaluation across more generators and alternative layouts.
4. **Can agents reliably transfer retrieved evidence into reports?** Trace analysis identifies useful research that never reaches the artifact. Test an explicit evidence-to-claim ledger and measure supported coverage and citation gains.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the central findings and their qualifications, but most tables appear only as captions.

Material missing information includes the full configuration score matrix and intervals, exact scoring weights and saturation/cap values, numerical source budgets and calibration threshold, complete criterion inventories, and detailed per-task results. The authors state that code and data are released; their availability and contents were not independently verified.
