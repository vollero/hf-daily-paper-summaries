# Code2Video-Compass: A Comprehensive Benchmark for General-Purpose Code-Based Video Generation across T2V, I2V, and V2V

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28416) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28416)


## One-sentence takeaway
Code2Video-Compass benchmarks complete video creation through executable code across text, image, and video inputs, finding that execution reliability, reference fidelity, and holistic quality remain distinct challenges.

## Problem and motivation
Generating complete videos through code requires visual construction, temporal coordination, and consistent states across scenes. Visual references additionally require reconstructing and integrating appearance or motion at suitable points in the video. The authors identify a gap in existing benchmarks: most code-based evaluations cover specialized applications or short animations rather than complete videos across diverse creative categories and input modalities (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified task formulation:** T2V, I2V, and V2V tasks produce executable projects rendered through Remotion, supporting HTML/CSS, SVG, Canvas, and Three.js. References must be reconstructed in code rather than embedded or replayed (§3.1).
- **Benchmark dataset:** 2,104 tasks cover 10 categories and 24 subtypes, combining independently constructed tasks with aligned tasks derived from complete source videos. Tasks carry construction, dynamic-animation, and state-dependency complexity annotations (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Evaluation framework:** Twelve objective metrics measure execution, token use, visual quality, semantic alignment, and reference fidelity; five MLLM-judged metrics assess instruction following and holistic quality (§3.5).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Fifteen MLLMs are tested on a fixed subset using single-call generation without execution feedback or repair, followed by reliability, complexity, cost, and judge analyses (§§4–5).

The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation protocol; the paper does not introduce a new trained video-generation model.

These results support the authors’ conclusion that executable output, reference reconstruction, and complete-video quality require separate assessment. They establish performance under this particular generation and rendering protocol; they do not establish rankings for iterative agents or other rendering stacks. The supplied text includes prose summaries but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 2–4, preventing a complete audit of model comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper does not provide a dedicated limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences grounded in its setup**:

- **Protocol dependence:** Single-call generation without repair measures a narrower capability than an iterative production workflow; default reasoning settings also differ by provider (§4.1).
- **Input-format confounding:** GPT and Claude receive frame sequences for V2V, while other models receive native video with nominally matched sampling. The effect of this difference is not isolated (§4.1).
- **Judge dependence:** Gemini assists data construction, judges outputs, and is itself evaluated. Human validation provides useful evidence, but does not rule out model-specific or category-specific scoring bias (§§3.3, 3.5, 5.5).
- **Complexity is observational:** Tasks are grouped by annotated complexity rather than controlled interventions, so declines do not isolate the causal effect of each dimension (§5.3).
- **Scope and reproducibility:** Source videos are screened for code-generation suitability, and evaluation uses a fixed stack. Exact resource limits, complete generation prompts, and executable environment specifications are unavailable in the supplied text (§§3.1, 3.3, 3.5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can execution feedback improve full-set quality efficiently?** Repair is excluded despite substantial failures. Compare single-call generation with a bounded repair loop at matched token or cost budgets (§§4.1, 5.1–5.2).
- **How much do V2V rankings depend on input format?** Formats differ across model families. Evaluate models supporting both formats on identical clips and frame samples (§4.1).
- **Which complexity dimension independently drives failures?** Existing comparisons do not isolate correlated task properties. Construct matched task variants that vary one complexity dimension at a time (§5.3).
- **Does judge agreement hold within categories and model families?** Validation reports aggregate correlations. Expand blinded human evaluation and report agreement and ranking changes by category, complexity, and generator (§5.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the benchmark design and reported headline findings, but lacks complete result tables, uncertainty estimates for model comparisons, detailed rendering/resource specifications, full prompts and judge rubrics, per-group validation results, and confirmation of downloadable asset contents or licensing.
