# Deep Search in Real-World Web Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30181) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30181)


## One-sentence takeaway
OpenDS-World introduces 200 video-anchored, multi-source Web research tasks on which the strongest evaluated model reaches 39.0% answer accuracy under a shared tool harness, exposing persistent evidence-acquisition and execution challenges (Table 1; §3.1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing evaluations insufficiently combine three demands of real Web research: multimodal evidence, heterogeneous resource formats, and interactive access. Relevant information may require navigation, downloads, interface manipulation, and cross-source reasoning before it becomes usable. OpenDS evaluates this combined process through questions with unique factual answers (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task formulation:** OpenDS requires autonomous discovery, access, processing, and integration of evidence whose locations and access methods are initially unknown (§2.1).
- **Benchmark construction:** Annotators identify audiovisual anchors, recursively expand corroborated Web evidence chains, and invert those chains into questions with indirect constraints. Independent reviewers check factual support and answer uniqueness, yielding 200 tasks across eight domains (§2.2).
- **Evaluation infrastructure and analysis:** A shared harness supplies search, browser interaction, artifact inspection, video frames, and subtitles. The authors evaluate 12 models and analyze accuracy, costs, tool use, termination, and recovery, alongside independent human testing (§§2.3, 3; Appendix C).

The contributions concern task design, benchmarking, and behavioral measurement; the paper does not demonstrate a newly trained search agent.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Leading models remain below 40% accuracy | Across 200 tasks, GPT-6 Astra scores 39.0% (78 correct), Claude Opus 5.5 38.0% (76), and Gemini 3.8 Flash 36.5% (73), under a 3,600-second and 100-call research budget per task. | Table 1; §3.1 |
| Human solving is difficult | Fifty participants make 1,000 independent attempts without LLM tools or a uniform per-question time limit: 21.1% accuracy; 85/200 tasks remain unsolved by all five assigned solvers. | §§2.3.1–2.3.2; Fig. 4 |
| Human-calibrated difficulty tracks aggregate model performance | Equally averaged over 12 models, accuracy is 39.66%, 23.50%, and 16.76% on Low, Medium, and High groups, containing 54, 61, and 85 tasks respectively. | Table 1; §3.2 |
| Evidence requirements are heterogeneous | Across 200 tasks, 82.5% involve multiple modalities, 96.0% multiple formats, and 95.5% multiple access types; annotations exclude the supplied anchor video. | Appendix B.2; Fig. 11 |
| More tool use does not consistently accompany better accuracy | GPT-6 Astra averages 51.05 calls per target task at 39.0% accuracy; Qwen3.8-Flash averages 89.94 calls at 12.0%, under the shared harness. | §3.3; Fig. 6; Table 1 |
| Failed calls consume disproportionate execution time | Across evaluated models, failures constitute 2.83%–9.42% of recorded calls but 19.27%–61.90% of recorded tool-execution time. | §3.5; Fig. 8 |
| Natural termination does not establish correctness | GPT-6 Astra terminates naturally on 69.0% of 200 tasks but answers 39.0% correctly. | §3.5; Fig. 8 |

These results establish difficulty under the reported protocol and descriptive differences among models. They do not establish causal effects of tool selection, additional budget, or particular evidence attributes. The human and model protocols differ, so their accuracy comparison does not establish general superiority over human researchers. Answer matching also does not verify the completeness of agents’ supporting evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundary:** Video-duration performance differences do not establish a causal relationship between duration and difficulty (Appendix D.3). The authors also acknowledge that budgets constrain observed trajectories (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Deliberately challenging, video-anchored questions with unique answers may not represent ordinary research or open-ended synthesis (§2.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Model answers receive reference-based LLM judgments, whereas human answers are manually checked. No judge-versus-human agreement study is reported (§§2.3.1, 3.1; Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** The supplied experiments do not ablate budgets, context truncation, or individual tool capabilities; observed failures therefore cannot be assigned cleanly to model reasoning versus harness constraints (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Anchor videos are archived, but changing external sources remain part of execution. Repeated-run uncertainty, evaluation dates, and a release location for benchmark/code are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Which acquisition stage most limits accuracy?** Aggregate accuracy and overlapping attribute subsets do not isolate bottlenecks. Next experiment: supply verified anchors or intermediate evidence separately and measure the resulting accuracy changes.
- **Can adaptive recovery improve accuracy at fixed cost?** Failure latency and recovery choices are descriptive, without intervention tests. Next experiment: compare recovery policies under identical budgets, measuring accuracy and failed-call time.
- **How stable are rankings across Web changes and runs?** Dynamic sources and unreported repeat-run variability leave stability unresolved. Next experiment: repeat evaluations across dates and random seeds while recording source availability.
- **Do correct answers reflect complete evidence chains?** Reference-based judging evaluates answers rather than research support. Next experiment: manually audit cited evidence and intermediate relations for correct and incorrect outputs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark design and reported descriptive results. Material missing information includes release/access details, model sampling configurations, repeated-run uncertainty, judge validation, participant expertise, and evaluation dates. No controlled improvement experiment establishes the proposed recursive self-improvement potential.
