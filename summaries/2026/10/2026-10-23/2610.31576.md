# What Makes a Good Unmasking Policy? A Controlled Study of Learned Unmasking in Diffusion Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31576) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31576)


## One-sentence takeaway
Trajectory-preserving supervision and combined confidence–hidden-state inputs improve learned unmasking efficiency, but OnTrack’s gains weaken at demanding accuracy targets and under combined domain and generation-length shifts.

## Problem and motivation
Masked diffusion language models commit multiple predicted tokens per step. More parallel commitments reduce backbone evaluations, but premature commitments can alter subsequent predictions, and committed tokens are never revised (§3.1).

Existing learned policies simultaneously change supervision, training states, inputs, and capacity, obscuring which choices produce improvements. The authors also investigate when fewer function evaluations (NFE) actually compensate for policy overhead (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory-contraction supervision:** Label only commitments that directly reach a later reference-trajectory state. Unlike agreement with the final output, this preserves the reference path when the teacher’s complete commitment set is applied. Targets require no additional backbone evaluations (§3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Controlled design comparisons:** Cross commitment targets with reference versus teacher-induced training states, then compare confidence, hidden states, probability margins, and combined inputs while holding other settings fixed (§§3.2–3.4).
- **OnTrack and overhead analysis:** Train a lightweight policy using confidence plus final-layer hidden states, binary cross-entropy, and correct reference trajectories from a frozen backbone. Separately profile latency and evaluate accuracy, NFE, and throughput across tasks, caching, backbones, and generation lengths (§§3.5–5).

## Evidence and main results
Unless specified otherwise, downstream comparisons use zero-shot LLaDA-8B-Instruct, generation length 256, and block length 32. Accuracy targets retain a stated fraction of one-by-one confidence-decoding accuracy.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Final-output agreement permits off-reference commitments | Across training reference trajectories, 28.7% of terminal-agreement positives fall outside the contraction set; 56.0% of states contain at least one mismatch. | Appendix B.2, p. 13 |
| Reference states improve code transfer under contraction | At the MBPP 98%-retention target, reference-state training needs 48.6 mean NFE versus 64.8 for teacher-induced training; measured Pass@1 is 52.5% versus 53.7%. | §3.3; Table 13 |
| Hidden states complement confidence on GSM8K | At the 98%-retention target, confidence plus hidden states achieves 77.0% accuracy at 42.1 NFE; confidence alone achieves 77.0% at 52.9 NFE. | §3.4; Table 14 |
| OnTrack improves uncached GSM8K throughput | At 98% of the 78.0% reference accuracy, highest qualifying TPS is 331 for OnTrack, 242 for Learn2PD, 232 for Fast-dLLM, and 221 for EB-Sampler. | Tables 3, 17 |
| Strongest statistical support is on GSM8K | Against Fast-dLLM at the 98% target, NFE speedup is 1.56×, paired-bootstrap 95% CI [1.08, 2.10]. Against EB-Sampler it is 1.59× [0.95, 2.52], leaving that comparison uncertain. | Table 27 |
| Lightweight overhead is modest in profiled settings | Policies up to approximately 7.5M parameters add 2–10% per-step overhead across 128–2048-token synthetic prompts, with or without prefix caching; authors infer roughly 10% fewer NFE suffices to break even. Single H200, batch size 1. | §3.5; Tables 2, 8 |
| Highest accuracy targets expose a gap | OnTrack does not reach 98% retention on LLaDA MATH-500 or HumanEval; training-free methods do. | §5.2; Tables 17, 18, 27 |
| Caching can reverse the throughput ranking | At GSM8K’s zero-shot 98% target with prefix caching, OnTrack achieves 249 TPS versus Fast-dLLM’s 276 TPS. | Tables 3, 19 |
| Transfer has clear boundaries | A separately trained Dream policy largely preserves benefits; the LLaDA checkpoint transfers across lengths on mathematical tasks but loses its advantage when length and code-domain shifts coincide. | §§5.4–5.5; Figures 5, 6, 11 |

These results support improved efficiency at selected accuracy targets, especially on GSM8K. They do **not** establish that leaving a reference trajectory necessarily causes an incorrect answer, or that a learned approximation inherits the teacher’s trajectory-preservation property. Many comparisons outside GSM8K have confidence intervals spanning no improvement (Table 27).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Training uses only mathematical reasoning, one generation length, and one block length. Simultaneous domain and length transfer is unsuccessful (§7).
- **Author-stated accuracy ceiling:** Conservative thresholds do not consistently recover reference accuracy. The authors suggest broader supervision of conservative behavior (§7).
- **Author-stated feature sensitivity:** Caching reduces OnTrack accuracy by about two percentage points in some settings. Perturbed hidden states are a proposed explanation, not a demonstrated cause (§7).
- **Comparison coverage:** LUP and TraceLock are excluded because trained checkpoints were unavailable. Larger latency-test policies match approximate parameter counts rather than those methods’ architectures (§5.1; Appendix B.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Reference-state supervision also provides substantially more training states, so its transfer benefit is not isolated from training-data volume (Table 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Speed conclusions are bounded by batch-one H200 measurements. Throughput uses one decoding pass per prompt without warm-up, and no repeated-timing uncertainty is reported (Appendix C.5).
- Output safety changes are explicitly unevaluated (§8).

## Open research questions
1. **Can broader training repair combined shifts?** Domain-plus-length transfer fails; compare mathematical-only training with matched-budget mixtures of code and multiple generation lengths (§7).
2. **Can supervision recover the highest accuracy targets?** OnTrack sometimes saturates below reference accuracy; test mixed contraction and conservative-commitment targets at matched training budgets (§7).
3. **What causes caching sensitivity?** Hidden-state perturbation is only a hypothesis; compare confidence-only and combined-input policies trained and evaluated with and without caching (§7).
4. **Does state diversity help independently of quantity?** Reference training includes more states; compare equal-sized reference and teacher-induced state sets (Appendix B.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported setup and numerical comparisons; causal explanations and broader generalization remain less certain.

Material information unavailable includes a usable code/checkpoint release location, repeated-training variability, throughput confidence intervals, equal-state-budget supervision ablations, measurements on other hardware or batch sizes, and direct tests of the proposed caching mechanism.
