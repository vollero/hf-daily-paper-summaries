# MolLean: A Process-Verifiable Language for Chemical Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20459) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20459)


## One-sentence takeaway
MolLean makes declared chemical reasoning executable and checkable, improving benchmark performance through verifier feedback while showing that accepted traces can still solve the wrong chemical specification (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Free-form chemical chain-of-thought can invent functional groups, mishandle bonds, or assert property improvements without evidence. The authors identify a missing contract: successive edits and claims need a shared molecular referent and explicit acceptance conditions (§1). Checking execution also needs to remain separate from checking whether the model correctly interpreted the task.

## Approach and contributions
- **State-grounded language:** Molecular transitions, readouts, and assertions operate on a maintained graph state. Accepted transitions commit changes; rejected operations preserve the state. Primitive edits, construction macros, and atomic compound transformations support different action granularities (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Explicit evidence boundaries:** Lean checks native structural predicates; typed external witnesses supply descriptor or lookup results whose acceptance predicates Lean evaluates. This checks claims against provider responses without independently proving those responses correct (§2.3; Appendix A).
- **Shared execution with different feedback regimes:** One-pass executes a complete trace; Refinement rewrites rejected traces from the initial state; MolLean-MCP provides interactive feedback against the current state (§2.5).
- **Matched evaluation:** The authors compare these interfaces with direct CoT across molecular generation, editing, optimization, and reaction tasks, reporting process soundness separately from answer correctness (§3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Refinement improves every main-model task | Qwen3.6-27B, 8,800 matched examples across 12 tasks; up to eight revisions. Authors report gains over CoT on every task and paired bootstrap intervals excluding zero for every primary-score difference. | §§3.1–3.2; Table 2 discussion |
| Large gains on structural generation | Atom-count success improves by 46.8 percentage points and bond-count success by 48.2 points over CoT; each task has 500 examples. | §§3.1–3.2 |
| Interactive verification leads on reactions | On 500 USPTO-50k examples per direction, MCP versus Refinement achieves 66.4% versus 62.8% forward accuracy and 27.4% versus 20.8% retrosynthesis accuracy; scoring requires complete component-skeleton set equality. | §§3.1, 3.4 |
| Refinement leads on description-driven generation | Full ChEBI-20 test set, 3,300 examples: connectivity-match accuracy is 33.1% for Refinement, 23.5% for MCP, and 18.1% for CoT. | §§3.1–3.2, 3.4 |
| Certification and correctness diverge | ChEBI-20 Refinement reaches 79.0% soundness but 33.1% accuracy; 1,533 outputs are sound yet incorrect. Retrosynthesis reaches 88.2% soundness but 20.8% exact set-match accuracy. | §3.3 |
| Smaller-model benefits depend on task | Qwen3-4B Refinement raises atom-count success from 15.8% to 56.8% and component-addition success from 13.4% to 91.4%, on matched 500-example tasks; refined retrosynthesis accuracy remains 0.0%. | §§3.1, 3.5 |

These results support verifier-guided improvement under the reported settings, especially when task requirements map directly to explicit predicates. They do not establish that certification guarantees task fidelity, empirical chemical truth, or faithful disclosure of the model’s internal reasoning. The supplied retrieval contains table captions but omits table bodies; the findings above use values explicitly stated in the prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified specification gap:** Models can omit or weaken obligations during revision. The salt example becomes accepted after dropping required chloride, illustrating successful execution of an incomplete specification (§4; Appendix C.6).
- **Verification boundary:** Only declared operations are covered. External measurements remain provider-dependent, and stereocenter checks count retained annotations rather than establish unrestricted stereochemical correctness (§§2.1–2.4; Appendix A.3).
- **Author-identified proposer dependence:** Weak reference-based results with Qwen3-4B show that verification cannot supply missing candidate-generation knowledge (§3.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confounding:** MCP and Refinement use different contexts, temperatures, and interaction budgets; their performance differences do not isolate feedback timing alone (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Connectivity-based matching does not establish full stereochemical accuracy. No laboratory validation is reported, and bootstrap intervals describe example variation conditional on the generated outputs (§3.1; Appendix E.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can revision preserve task requirements?** Accepted but incorrect outputs expose obligation loss. Next experiment: freeze independently extracted task constraints across revisions and measure sound-but-incorrect outcomes (§3.3; Appendix C.6).
- **Does interactive feedback help under equal resources?** Current interface settings differ. Next experiment: compare MCP and Refinement with matched token budgets, sampling settings, and attempts (Appendix D.2).
- **How much does proposer capability determine benefit?** Smaller-model structural gains coexist with poor reference matching. Next experiment: give both evaluated models identical candidate pools and measure verification-driven selection and repair (§3.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported architecture and prose-supported results; **medium** in causal interpretations of interface advantages.

Material missing information includes numerical table bodies and interval endpoints, full plotted distributions, public code/artifact availability, aggregate runtime and token costs, repeated-run variability, and independent validation of external evidence providers.
