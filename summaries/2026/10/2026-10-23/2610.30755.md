# Is Complex Agentic Memory Always Needed? Naive RAG Is Stronger Than We Thought

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30755) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30755)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that raw-history dense retrieval with reranking beats compared memory systems in six of seven main benchmark–model settings, while additional personalization results and diagnostic caveats limit broader claims of sufficiency (§4.2; Appendix A.3).

## Problem and motivation
Long conversation histories exceed LLM context windows, motivating systems that extract, summarize, update, or organize memories. The authors ask whether these operations improve conversation understanding beyond a carefully configured raw-history retrieval baseline. They identify potentially weak retrieval configurations in prior comparisons and question whether readers can reconstruct changing states directly from retrieved history (§1; Appendix E).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic baseline:** Raw-RAG preserves source text, timestamps, and supplied image captions as text. It normally indexes 256-token windows with 32-token overlap, retrieves 128 candidates by dense similarity, and reranks to 15 items. Benchmark-specific assembly can expand windows to complete turns or restore chronology (§3; Appendix B).
- **Comparative evaluation:** The contribution is an empirical reassessment using standard retrieval components, including retrieval ablations, evidence-budget controls, and ingestion/retrieval timing (§4; Appendix A).
- **Evidence diagnostics:** Mutation interventions, coverage/utilization analysis, dependency-path assessment, and privileged store-access controls investigate why raw retrieval remains competitive (§§1, 5; Appendix C).

These results support Raw-RAG as a necessary comparison baseline for the evaluated tasks. They do not establish universal superiority or prove that memory transformation itself causes every deficit. Coverage, evidence assembly, reader instructions, and representation format affect the diagnostics. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** Evaluations concern offline public histories; interactive operation, broader multimodal settings, explicit erasure, authoritative tool state, and bounded storage remain unestablished (§6.1; Appendix F).
- **Comparisons vary:** Some baselines retain published scores rather than being rerun. Budget controls include earlier hosted configurations, and Raw-RAG-only budget reruns leave comparator budgets unchanged (Appendices A, A.4).
- **Diagnostics are not causal isolation:** Complete-store conditions change selection and readout policy; final-store exports have temporal-provenance limitations. Raw-to-store gaps combine information preservation with assembly and reading effects (Appendix C.5).
- **Measurement uncertainty:** MemoryAgentBench sufficiency is judged from gold-localized excerpts, potentially differing from full-context sufficiency. Conditional utilization uses different question subsets across systems. Confidence intervals condition on observed builds and model outputs (Appendices G.9, C.6).
- **Representation ablations are coupled:** Atomic extraction changes evidence volume, demonstration structure, and reader compatibility alongside content preservation (Appendix F).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Historical LoCoMo judge responses are unavailable, preventing attribution of incorrect scores to grading versus format/request failures; some dependency versions are unavailable (Appendices G.3, D.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Small headline leads require caution because the supplied prose does not establish significance for every main comparison.

## Open research questions
- **When does explicit state maintenance help weaker readers?** Reader dependence remains unresolved (§6.1). Compare mutation and append-only stores across reader capabilities with matched evidence and budgets.
- **Why do structured systems lead on personalization?** PersonaMem-v2 uses differing native profile configurations (Appendix A.3). Cross raw retrieval and profile construction under a shared retrieval backbone and reader.
- **Which transformation effects cause the learning deficit?** Evidence volume and demonstration format remain coupled (Appendix F). Match token budgets and preserve example boundaries while varying extraction.
- **How does the trade-off change during prolonged operation?** Repeated reasoning cost and retrieval quality across history lengths remain unmeasured (Appendix F). Evaluate growing interactive histories under storage and deletion constraints.
