# Verus-Insight: Towards Explainable Artifact Detection in AI-Generated Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30866) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30866)


## One-sentence takeaway

Verus-Insight improves video authenticity classification and semantic recovery of human-annotated artifacts through explicit inspection, verification, and aggregation training, although its main metrics do not test localization accuracy (§§3, 5; Appendix A.4).

## Problem and motivation

A detector can correctly classify a video as AI-generated while missing its actual artifacts or inventing supporting evidence. The authors identify a gap between source classification and reliable diagnosis: detectors must choose relevant inspection targets, distinguish genuine abnormalities from normal content, and ground their conclusions in supported observations (§1; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured detection:** Inspection Planning produces content-specific, outcome-neutral criteria; Evidence Verification checks each criterion and supplies available spatial and temporal evidence; Evidence Aggregation produces the source verdict (§3.1).
- **Supervision and training:** Human artifact annotations are paired with inspection criteria and supplemented with independently verified normal checks. Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct undergoes tracking/planning warm-up, full-process supervised fine-tuning, and GRPO. RL rewards require a valid, correct source verdict, then reward artifact recovery or the absence of false artifact claims on real videos (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Verus-Bench:** A sample-disjoint benchmark contains 192 videos, balanced between real and generated sources, with 326 artifact annotations across 18 categories and frame-wise spatial evidence (§4; Appendix A.1.2).
- **Capability analysis:** Ablations and diagnostics distinguish inspection coverage, artifact recovery, and source accuracy rather than treating classification as a sufficient explanation-quality measure (§5.3).

These reported results support improved classification and recovery of reference artifact descriptions. They do **not** establish accurate spatial or temporal localization: matching explicitly ignores timestamps and boxes. ViF-CoT results use a different historical protocol and are not pooled with the main evaluations (Appendix A.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Reported weakness:** On RewardData, small-region artifact recall remains below VideoVeritas; gains are not uniform across extent bins (Appendix A.3.2).
- **Analyst inference—grounding gap:** Explicit grounding is trained and generated, but semantic F1 cannot validate whether its locations or intervals are correct (Appendix A.4.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluator dependence:** Gemini performs extraction and matching and also supplies RL reward judgments. The authors explicitly state that automated matches and illustrative inspection do not constitute independent human agreement measurements (Appendices A.2.2, A.4.2).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Verus-Bench is small and emphasizes perceptible artifacts in short sampled inputs. Sample disjointness does not establish generalization to unseen generators or artifact-free synthetic videos (§4; Appendix A.1.2).
- **Evidence availability:** The supplied text contains table captions and selected numerical summaries, but omits table bodies, many figure values, and actual prompt templates. Full comparisons and several reproducibility details cannot be audited.

## Open research questions

1. **Are recovered artifacts correctly localized?** Main matching ignores spatial and temporal evidence. Next experiment: jointly score semantic correctness, temporal overlap, and frame-wise box overlap against human annotations.
2. **Do gains survive independent human evaluation?** Automated judging lacks measured human agreement. Next experiment: have blinded annotators assess matched and unmatched claims across models.
3. **Does detection generalize beyond represented artifact conditions?** Benchmark sample separation does not establish generator-held-out transfer. Next experiment: hold out entire generator families and include generated clips with no perceptible artifacts.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and selected results are explicit, but conclusions depend on automated semantic scoring.

Material missing information includes complete result tables, many plotted values, full training hyperparameters, actual evaluator prompts and schema, independent human agreement, localization accuracy, and inference cost.
