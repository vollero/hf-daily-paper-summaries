# EvalResearchBench: Can AI Agents Build Their Own Feedback for Recursive Self-Improvement?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04184) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04184)


## One-sentence takeaway
ERB shows that agents can construct budgeted executable evaluators, but the best observed candidate-order agreement remains 74.87% against a reference-imposed ceiling of 91.20%, without demonstrating downstream recursive self-improvement (Appendix A.1).

## Problem and motivation
Repeated evaluation makes autonomous improvement costly, particularly for long tool-using tasks. Humans currently choose representative tasks, graders, and affordable proxies. The authors identify a gap between automating individual benchmark-construction steps and autonomously designing, testing, and revising an entire evaluator under resource constraints (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Autonomous evaluation-research benchmark:** ERB gives researchers target materials, development references, candidate APIs, and an execution SDK. Agents select or synthesize tasks, implement graders, run pilots, and freeze at least 100 scored questions spanning coding, co-work, and reasoning (§3.1).
- **Independent transfer assessment:** Candidate visibility and target visibility are assigned separately. Frozen evaluators are assessed against development and sealed references using score concordance, candidate ordering, and execution cost (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Empirical design analysis:** Nine researcher configurations each contribute one episode; eight produce complete candidate-domain panels. Offline component deletions, question subsampling, weight changes, and trajectory audits examine what drives measurement quality (§§4–5; Appendices B–E).

Research receives three hours, $100 for researcher inference, and $100 for development/helper calls; assessment separately allows $120 total and $30 per candidate (§4.1).

These results support the feasibility of autonomous evaluator construction and show that ordering, score calibration, and cost are distinct objectives. They do not establish reliable feedback for an actual improvement loop: the experiments assess frozen measurements against published reference snapshots, rather than measuring downstream research progress.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-documented reference conflict:** Targets within a domain disagree in score scale and ordering. One domain score cannot reproduce all references perfectly; compatible-target selection also changes the leading evaluator (Appendix A.1).
- **Author-documented execution effects:** Truncation, incomplete items, concentrated weights, and assessment-budget exhaustion affect measurements. Costs include partial executions and exclude unresolved reservations (§§3.2, 4.3–4.4; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited repeatability evidence:** One episode per researcher configuration leaves episode-to-episode variability unresolved. Reported resampling ranges hold suites and targets fixed, so they do not address that uncertainty (§4.1; Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—confounded comparisons:** Researchers use different harnesses without a reported controlled harness comparison. Offline deletions and reweighting isolate frozen-score effects, but do not establish what redesigned or rerun evaluators would achieve (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—scope of validity:** Matching public benchmark snapshots does not by itself validate sensitivity to improvements within a single model lineage or resistance to optimization against the evaluator (§3.2; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Does evaluator agreement translate into better improvement decisions?** ERB does not run downstream improvement loops. A next experiment could compare model revisions selected by agent-designed, Random120, and full-target feedback.
- **How stable are researcher rankings?** Each configuration has one episode. Repeat episodes under matched harnesses and budgets, then assess fresh candidate and target panels.
- **Can weighting preserve calibration and ordering without concentration?** Grok’s offline reweighting reveals unused signal. Compare uniform, concentration-constrained, and learned weights using development-only selection and sealed assessment.
- **How much signal depends on answer allowances?** Spreadsheet performance coincides with frequent capped responses. Rerun the same tasks across controlled allowances and compare fidelity, completion, and cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the protocol and principal findings, but most referenced tables lack their numerical bodies. Missing information includes the full leaderboard, complete cost breakdowns, numerical uncertainty ranges, some reference-normalization constants, and confirmed code/data release locations. Repeated research episodes and downstream self-improvement outcomes are not reported.
