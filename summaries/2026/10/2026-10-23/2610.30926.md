# Making MLLM Latents Matter: Factor-Aware Predictive Learning for Cinematic Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30926) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30926)


## One-sentence takeaway
FAPL combines latent-only generator training, factor-span interventions, and MLLM adaptation through a frozen video generator to improve cinematic control, with reported ablations supporting distinct editing and temporal-generation benefits (§§3, 4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Cinematic generation must infer action sequences and camera motion from sparse guidance while controlling character, scene, action, and camera independently. The authors identify two gaps: video generators can bypass MLLM features through other conditioning pathways, and ordinary video-level supervision does not explicitly teach selective factor changes or generation-compatible temporal prediction (§1; Figs. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Ground the generator in MLLM latents.** Freeze the MLLM and connector, disable separate text and VAE-reference conditions, and train the DiT using text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations (§3.2).
2. **Supervise selective interventions.** Replace character, scene, action, or camera spans in projected latents between paired examples; train against videos specifying the intended change and preservation. This does not assume fully disentangled representations (§3.3).
3. **Adapt representations through a fixed generator.** Freeze the trained DiT and update the MLLM and connector using both observation modes with shared video targets. Complete-video reconstruction complements prediction from text alone (§3.4).

The method reuses the existing architecture and connector; its central contributions are the training arrangement and structured supervision (§3).

The controlled swapping comparison supports a specific editing benefit, while the observation-mode comparison supports temporal improvements. These results do not establish fully disentangled latents or broad reasoning gains. Missing table bodies and uncertainty estimates limit assessment of effect sizes and robustness.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Performance remains constrained by the underlying models, including MLLM difficulties with 3D blockouts and complex camera reasoning, and DiT limitations in fidelity and realistic acting (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Selective-edit supervision depends on pairs preserving non-target factors; selection, generation, and quality-filtering details are insufficient to assess that assumption (§§3.1.2, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Full quantitative evaluation is centered on UniVideo. Cross-backbone evidence described for Omni-Video 2 concerns Stage I examples, so transfer of the complete procedure remains uncertain (§§4.1, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Source-disjoint CineFactor testing reduces direct source overlap, but uses the same source collections as training; broader distribution transfer is not established (§§4.1–4.2).
- The supplied text does not specify which optional inference-conditioning configuration produced each main result (§3.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does the complete procedure transfer across backbones?** Only Stage I transfer examples are described; run all stages on Omni-Video 2 with matched data and evaluation (§4.4).
- **How sensitive is control to imperfect edit pairs?** Pair preservation is assumed but not quantified; audit non-target changes and test training under controlled pair corruption (§3.1.2).
- **Can factor control handle simultaneous changes?** Training pairs alter one factor at a time; evaluate combinations of character, scene, action, and camera edits with preservation checks (§§3.1.2, 4.2).
- **Does consistency persist over longer narratives?** Evaluation uses independently generated sequences of two to four shots; test longer sequences with repeated identity, scene, and camera requirements (§§3.5, 4.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several controlled comparisons are explicit. Material gaps include Tables 1–2’s numerical bodies, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run results, exact inference configurations, detailed pair construction and filtering, compute requirements, and confirmation of code, checkpoint, or dataset releases.
