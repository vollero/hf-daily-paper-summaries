# Policy-Mask: Budget-Aware Adaptation of Diffusion Features to Discriminative Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28889) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28889)


## One-sentence takeaway
Policy-Mask learns budget-conditioned timestep, layer, and channel selection for diffusion-to-student distillation, reporting improved compact visual representations while leaving the optimal channel budget unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Useful diffusion features vary across denoising timesteps, network layers, and channels. The authors identify a gap in extraction methods that fix these choices or optimize only timesteps: they do not jointly adapt extraction to the image, downstream objective, and feature budget (§§1–2). These choices affect the features stored and used for distillation; the resulting student runs without the diffusion teacher at inference.

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint budgeted selection:** An image- and budget-conditioned policy selects a timestep, layer, and exact-size channel subset. Gumbel top-*b* sampling implements a Plackett–Luce draw without replacement (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Balanced policy updates:** A supervised task decoder supplies the reward. The channel log-score is divided by the subset size to balance its contribution against timestep and layer heads. The authors explicitly describe this as preconditioning, not an unbiased gradient estimator (§3.3).
- **Teacher–student integration:** Selected, aligned teacher features supervise a student alongside its task loss; only the student runs at inference (Algorithm 1).
- **Transformer interface:** The action maps to noise/flow coordinate, depth, and image-token hidden dimensions for transformer teachers, instantiated with PixArt-Σ and SD3.5 MMDiT (§4.4).

The sampling and score-function machinery are established components; the claimed contribution is their joint budgeted extraction formulation and channel-score balancing (§2).

These results support compact feature selection and normalization in the tested settings. They do not establish universal superiority, an independently optimal budget, or a transformer-teacher ranking. The abstract reports 38.9 ADE20K mIoU, but the missing Table 1 cells prevent inspection of its full comparison. The separate protocols should not be pooled.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated budget confound:** KD loss is averaged over the full aligned width, so channel count changes distillation strength. The decline at the largest tested budget cannot establish channel redundancy (§4.5).
- **Author-stated repetition limits:** Action-axis and budget ablations use one seed; transformer evaluations use one evaluation per teacher (§4.5).
- **Alignment instability:** Trainable alignment rapidly reduced KD loss, which the authors interpret as consistent with projection shrinkage. Alignment was consequently frozen for selected comparisons; this does not validate every alignment setting (Appendix A.2).
- **Comparison limits:** DDAE and RepFusion classification entries retain source protocols. Correspondence has no numerical baseline in Table 2 (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** A feature-coordinate budget does not establish proportional compute or memory savings. The supplied text provides no measured resource comparison.
- **Qualitative selection bias:** Gain and loss panels deliberately show extreme outcomes and cannot establish typical behavior (Figures 3 and 6).

## Open research questions
1. **Which budget works best when distillation strength is controlled?** The current sweep couples budget and KD scale. Repeat it with selected-channel normalization and matched KD weighting across multiple seeds.
2. **Does conditioning on each image and budget add value?** The supplied ablations do not isolate these conditioning inputs. Compare against global learned extraction choices and test held-out budgets.
3. **How robust is transformer transfer?** Single teacher-specific evaluations and missing numerical table cells limit assessment. Repeat matched-fraction evaluations with fixed and learned selectors over multiple seeds.
4. **How much practical resource saving results?** Coordinate reduction alone leaves this unanswered. Measure teacher-feature storage, training memory, and runtime under matched student architectures.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The formulation and several results are explicit, but the supplied retrieval omits numerical cells from Tables 1, 3, 4, and 5 despite including their captions. Material missing details include student and policy architectures, candidate timestep/layer grids, most optimization settings, full repeated RepFusion results, measured resource costs, and code/checkpoint availability. Appendix B.1 also references Table 5 for fixed-slot runs although Table 4 carries that caption, creating a reference ambiguity.
