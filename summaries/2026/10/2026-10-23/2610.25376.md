# TILDE: Teacher-in-the-Loop Distillation from Embodied Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25376) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25376)


## One-sentence takeaway
TILDE combines teacher supervision on student-visited states with teacher-executed recovery trajectories, improving real-world manipulation scores over behavior cloning while avoiding repeated large-scale action pretraining (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors seek to transfer pretrained embodied foundation model capabilities into policies without robot action pretraining. Offline distillation supervises demonstration states but misses deviations encountered during deployment; on-policy distillation labels student states without observing the subsequent teacher recovery; human takeover collects recovery experience but requires monitoring and teleoperation (§1).

The student still uses a pretrained vision-language backbone: “without action pretraining” does not mean training entirely from scratch (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Three-source distillation:** Combine human demonstrations, student rollout states, and teacher recovery trajectories. Demonstrations and recovery samples receive executed-action imitation plus teacher supervision; student rollout samples receive teacher supervision only (§3.4).
- **AutoDAgger:** An off-the-shelf Robometer monitor estimates progress and success. Sustained stagnation triggers teacher takeover; only successfully recovered episodes are retained. Student-completed episodes are discarded (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Cross-architecture transfer:** Supervision uses action-generation outputs without intermediate-feature alignment. Same-architecture experiments match flow-matching velocity predictions using shared noise; heterogeneous students use fully denoised teacher actions (Appendix A.4).

The evaluated protocol collects data once with a fixed BC student, then reinitializes and trains the final student; it does not iteratively update the collection policy (Appendices A.4, C).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only one collection round is evaluated; monitor mistakes and teacher-unrecoverable states can reduce collection quality or efficiency; task, embodiment, and architecture coverage is limited (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Retaining only confirmed recoveries restricts learning to the monitor-selected, teacher-recoverable failure distribution; performance on rejected failures remains unresolved (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed real-world test configurations and small trial counts constrain generalization claims (§4.1; Appendix A.2).
- **Evidence limitation:** Tables appear as captions without their numerical bodies. Consequently, full baseline results, confidence intervals, recovery-time distributions, and several training settings cannot be inspected. Cross-references also conflict: recovery prose points to Table 2 although Table 1 is captioned as recovery evaluation; cross-architecture prose points to Table 6 although Table 5 has that caption.

## Open research questions
- **Does iterative collection outperform one-shot collection?** Only one round is tested. Compare repeated policy-update/collection cycles against one-shot collection under equal robot-time budgets (Appendix C).
- **How sensitive is performance to monitoring errors?** The method depends on monitor accuracy, but downstream sensitivity is not quantified. Sweep intervention thresholds and compare with manually verified intervention decisions (§3.3; Appendix B.1).
- **Does recovery-time efficiency translate into lower total cost?** The reported speedup concerns recovery after takeover. Measure complete collection, reset, monitoring, and training costs at matched final performance (§4.3).
- **How broadly does action-space transfer generalize?** Heterogeneous transfer is demonstrated with SmolVLA in simulation. Test additional student families and embodiments with explicitly compatible action interfaces (§4.3; Appendix C).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and principal prose-reported results are clear, but the supplied retrieval omits numerical table bodies and plotted values.

Material missing information includes complete baseline and success-rate results, confidence-interval values, seed variability, task-specific timing thresholds, detailed training hyperparameters, full collection statistics, and confirmed release availability for code, models, or datasets.
