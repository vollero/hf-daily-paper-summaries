# AgentVQA: A Unified Benchmark for Agentic Visual Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31494) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31494)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgentVQA standardizes visual agent evaluation across five domains, with a best reported accuracy of 58.5% and strong ranking agreement with online evaluation in the matched spatial domain, while broader interactive validity remains untested (§§3–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Online agent evaluation captures interaction but requires costly environment setup and execution. The authors identify a complementary evaluation gap: general VQA benchmarks poorly reflect agentic decisions, while existing offline agent benchmarks use fragmented domains, formats, and annotation standards (§§1–2).

AgentVQA aims to make cross-domain VLM comparison faster and more consistent while retaining observations and decisions drawn from agentic scenarios.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified benchmark:** 1,000 multiple-choice questions from 23 existing datasets, balanced at 200 questions per domain: Web Agents, Robotics, Egocentric Videos, Games, and Spatial Planning. Inputs include images, multiple frames, and videos; answers represent actions, coordinates, states, and spatial relations (§3; Table 2).
- **Curation pipeline:** VLMs generate hard distractors and screen candidates; humans repair, select, and review every retained question twice. Approximately 22% of initial candidates survive, emphasizing difficulty and a unique correct answer (§§1, 3.1; Figure 3).
- **Comparative and diagnostic evaluation:** The authors evaluate 22 VLM configurations, test action-history and reasoning effects, compare rankings with general benchmarks and SpatialWorld, and categorize one model’s failures (§4).

The contribution is a curated evaluation asset and analysis, rather than a newly trained agent or model architecture.

These results establish difficulty, uneven domain capabilities, and ranking agreement with one matched online evaluation. They do not establish that improving AgentVQA scores causes better interactive performance. The error analysis supports the authors’ perception-first interpretation for the analyzed model, but does not independently establish the causes of failure across all evaluated models.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged scope:** Correlations are lower outside the matched SpatialWorld domain, limiting any single dataset’s usefulness as a proxy for overall agent competence (§4.4). Connecting offline error patterns to interactive failures is explicitly left for future work (§5).
- **Analyst inference—selection effects:** Heavy filtering for difficulty, unambiguous answers, and offline convertibility produces a deliberately selected test set. Its accuracy should not be interpreted as a deployment success rate (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic generality:** Error attribution uses Gemini 3.1 Pro’s generated reasoning and largely automated categorization. Only a subset receives manual verification; agreement statistics and per-domain error sample counts are unavailable (§4.5; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—scoring comparability:** Unparseable outputs are skipped and marked unevaluated. Without per-model skip counts and scoring denominators, the effect on comparisons is unclear (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—robustness claims exceed visible evidence:** Option-order tests cover a subset, and no uncertainty intervals or repeated-run statistics accompany Table 1. The lowest overall score, 28%, also equals the constant-A baseline; exceeding the 25% average guessing baseline alone does not prove meaningful task engagement (Appendix F).
- **Cost evidence:** Lower evaluation cost is argued from avoiding environment execution, but no measured runtime or monetary comparison is supplied (§4.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Do offline improvements transfer to interactive success?** Matched-domain ranking correlation does not test intervention effects. Next experiment: improve grounding or state tracking, then measure paired gains on AgentVQA and closed-loop tasks (§§4.4–5).
2. **Do the error profiles generalize across models?** Detailed attribution covers one model. Next experiment: apply the taxonomy to several model families using blinded human labels and report agreement and category distributions (§4.5).
3. **How much does answer format shape rankings?** The paper tests option order but not equivalence to open-ended action production. Next experiment: evaluate the same observations as MCQs and executable open-ended actions, with manually validated scoring (§3; Appendices F and H).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark design and headline results, but several supporting artifacts are unavailable.

Material gaps include the actual prompt templates promised in Appendices K–M, numerical Table 3 contents, the claimed frame-count ablation, per-model unevaluated counts, repeated-run uncertainty, error-label agreement statistics, and measured evaluation costs. Code and data are linked in the paper but were not inspected.
