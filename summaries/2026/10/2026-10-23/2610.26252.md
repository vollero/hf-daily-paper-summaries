# SEAM: Spectral Embedding Alignment and Merging for Low-Resource TTS

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26252) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26252)


## One-sentence takeaway
SEAM initializes unseen TTS token embeddings through phase-preserving spectral features and closed-form ridge alignment, with reported low-resource CER gains and selected comparisons suggesting comparable performance using one-quarter of the fine-tuning audio (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Expanding a pretrained TTS vocabulary introduces embeddings that must adapt with scarce paired speech. The authors argue that random initialization requires substantial adaptation, while averaging known subtoken embeddings discards their order. SEAM targets this initialization bottleneck rather than replacing subsequent audio-based fine-tuning (§§1–2, 6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Spectral merging:** Treat ordered subtoken embeddings as discrete signals, apply a Gaussian low-pass filter, and retain real and imaginary Fourier coefficients to preserve phase information (§3.2).
- **Audio-free calibration:** Fit a linear readout through closed-form ridge regression using decompositions of existing vocabulary tokens and their pretrained embeddings as targets. New embeddings are normalized to the average pretrained embedding norm (§3.3).
- **Empirical and theoretical support:** Compare initialization methods across languages, budgets, vocabulary sizes, and two backbones; provide a sufficient-condition order-sensitivity proof. Acoustic stability rests on an assumed Lipschitz relationship, rather than a demonstrated guarantee for the evaluated models (§4; Appendices A–B).

Non-Latin tokens require transliteration or phonemic normalization before decomposition. “Zero-audio” describes initialization; downstream adaptation still uses paired target-language speech (§§3.3, 6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lower CER at the intermediate budget | Chatterbox, 25h: reported CER reductions versus mean initialization are 2.22 percentage points for Russian, 10.63 for Kazakh, and 0.87 for French; versus random, 9.16, 27.34, and 3.77 points. Results are reported averaged over three seeds. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 referenced |
| Selected comparisons suggest reduced audio requirements | Authors report SEAM trained on 25h approaches or matches baselines trained on 100h, corresponding to up to 4× less audio. Full numerical rows are absent from the supplied text. | §4.2; Table 1 caption |
| Benefit transfers to a second backbone | VibeVoice, Russian, 5h: CER is 6.14% for SEAM, 8.34% for mean, and 9.26% for random initialization. | Appendix B.6; Table 5 referenced |
| Order disruption worsens performance | Russian, 5h: CER rises from 14.88% to 19.67% with reversal and 22.91% with shuffling. Shuffling raises WER from 34.41% to 53.66%. | §4.6; Table 3 referenced |
| Advantage over additional initialization baselines is uneven | Russian, 5h: SEAM’s 14.88% CER compares with learned attention at 15.30%, fixed-query attention at 20.14%, and position-aware pooling at 23.37%; uncertainty for these differences is unavailable. | §§4.5–4.6; Tables 2–3 referenced |

The evidence supports improved initialization efficiency in the tested settings, especially against random and mean pooling. It does not establish universal fourfold savings or broad architectural generality. The order ablation supports the importance of ordered inputs, but does not independently establish that low-pass filtering is the primary cause of improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reliable subtoken decomposition and phonemic mappings are required; mapping errors may propagate. Paired audio remains necessary for adaptation (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Lexical overlap can produce non-monotonic budget comparisons, particularly for Kazakh. The authors attribute this to dataset composition, but supporting overlap measurements are not supplied (Appendix B.5).
- **Analyst inference:** No isolated filter-removal or ridge-only ablation establishes the separate contribution of spectral smoothing. Order perturbation changes a different factor (§§4.5–4.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence covers three languages, with the second backbone tested only on Russian at one budget (§4; Appendix B.6).
- **Analyst inference:** The human-study analysis aggregates repeated judgments from annotators and utterances; no dependence-aware statistical analysis is described (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Human-recording ASR error is a useful reference, but the text does not establish it as a strict lower bound or justify subtracting it to isolate synthesis error (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which component drives the gains?** Component contributions remain unresolved. Compare ridge-only, unfiltered Fourier, and full SEAM initialization using identical calibration pairs and fine-tuning schedules.
- **How robust is SEAM to imperfect mappings?** Transliteration dependence is acknowledged but unquantified. Introduce controlled mapping errors and measure CER and pronunciation quality.
- **Do audio savings survive lexical-overlap control?** Kazakh budget comparisons are composition-sensitive. Repeat with nested training subsets, measured lexical overlap, and multiple held-out sets.
- **Can audio-guided calibration work with norm constraints?** One unstable refinement variant does not settle this question. Test norm-constrained refinement and report held-out CER alongside training loss (Appendix B.7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several numerical comparisons are explicit, but many referenced tables appear only as captions.

Material missing information includes complete budget results and seed-level uncertainty; human preference counts and confidence intervals; plotted vocabulary-size results; calibration-pair counts and decomposition construction details; handling of variable-length spectral features within the fixed-size readout; exact phonemic mapping resources; and a verifiable code-release location.
