# PAMI: Part Anchored Motion for Text to Human-Object Interaction Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38466) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38466)


## One-sentence takeaway
PAMI combines learned body-part votes for object translation with latent flow generation and local geometric refinement, reporting improved text-conditioned human–object interactions on InterAct, although the supplied text omits the main numerical table contents.

## Problem and motivation
Text-conditioned full-body human–object interaction generation must coordinate human motion and object trajectories while preserving the requested action and precise contact. The authors argue that representing human and object motion separately makes their changing relationship difficult to learn, producing drift, missed contact, and penetration (§1). Their proposed gap is a latent representation that explicitly preserves this coupling, followed by refinement of detailed contact geometry.

## Approach and contributions
- **Part-anchored interaction representation:** Object translation is expressed relative to the root, wrists, ankles, and an independent free anchor. A decoder predicts frame-wise softmax weights to combine their translation votes; object rotation is decoded separately. PamiVAE combines part-specific human encoders and an object encoder through spatiotemporal attention (§3.1; §B.2).
- **Coarse latent generation:** PamiGen uses text- and mesh-conditioned rectified flow to generate interaction latents. Decoded-space trajectory and alignment losses supplement latent supervision (§3.2; §B.3).
- **Fine geometric refinement:** PamiRefiner recursively updates human and object poses using long-range body-to-surface probes and short-range hand-surface neighborhoods. Training mixes corrupted ground-truth reconstructions with generated motions supervised through geometric objectives (§3.3).
- **Transferable refinement:** The authors report applying the frozen refiner to InterAct and LIGHT outputs after inverse-kinematics conversion, improving their interaction geometry (§4.2.2).

The authors claim the first part-based latent framework for this task (§1); the supplied text does not independently establish priority. Code and model release is promised, rather than confirmed (§5; reproducibility statement).

The supplied evidence describes a coherent method and component-wise ablations, but most performance findings remain author-reported summaries: Tables 1–10 contain captions without numerical bodies. Consequently, effect sizes, uncertainty, trade-offs, and the broad “best on almost all metrics” claim cannot be checked. Cross-generator refinement establishes a reported transfer setting, not generalization to unseen object shapes or other datasets.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers one human and one rigid object on InterAct; accurate canonical geometry is required. Novel-shape generalization and multiple-object interactions remain future work (Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Bounded local refinement cannot repair an incorrect action, severe temporal misalignment, or a large trajectory error (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed contact masks derived from coarse generation may constrain recovery when the coarse contact assignment is wrong; masks remain unchanged during recursion (§B.5). This failure mode is not separately quantified.
- **Analyst inference:** Improved contact and penetration metrics do not establish physical executability or robotics performance; the supplied evaluation reports motion and geometric metrics (§4).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** §3.1 describes softmax normalization of contact targets, whereas §B.2 describes normalization with the free branch receiving the full vote when detached. Exact target construction needs clarification.
- **Reproducibility gap:** Main numerical results, detailed dataset splits, and confirmed released artifacts are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
1. **Does PAMI generalize to unseen object geometry?** Appendix C identifies this as future work. A plausible experiment would hold out object shapes and report semantic alignment and contact quality separately.
2. **Can refinement recover incorrect coarse contact assignments?** Contact masks are fixed and large-error recovery is explicitly limited (§B.5; Appendix C). Compare fixed versus recomputed masks under controlled contact and trajectory corruptions.
3. **Can anchor voting scale to multiple objects?** Only single-object interactions are evaluated (Appendix C). Extend voting to separate object streams and test coordination and contact assignment on multiple-object sequences.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method structure and stated scope are clear; quantitative conclusions are less secure because the supplied retrieval omits numerical result tables.

Material missing information includes absolute baseline and ablation scores, the exact meaning and scope of the recall gain, the reconstruction metric behind the reported reduction, dataset split details, runtime comparators, and verified code/model release status.
