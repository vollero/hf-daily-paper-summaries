# Q-Learning with Scalar Adjoint Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10437) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10437)


## One-sentence takeaway
SQAM replaces expensive per-step adjoint propagation with a time-scaled critic gradient and a policy-action value penalty, improving aggregate offline OGBench success and three real-robot tasks while underperforming on some benchmark domains (Sections 3–4, Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Off-policy RL could improve pretrained flow policies beyond their training data, but actions emerge through multiple flow steps while the critic evaluates only the endpoint. Exact adjoint matching propagates the endpoint gradient through every step, requiring costly policy vector–Jacobian products. The authors identify a gap between directly updating an expressive flow policy and making that update computationally practical; frozen-policy residual and noise-selection approaches place improvement in a separate component (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Scalar adjoint:** Under an isotropic velocity-Jacobian assumption, the authors derive a closed-form adjoint. Their practical approximation drops its exponential scale factor and uses \(\hat a_\tau=-\tau\nabla_a Q(s,a_\pi)\), requiring one endpoint critic gradient and no policy vector–Jacobian products (Section 3.1, Proposition 1).
- **Policy-action value penalty:** SQAM adds \(c[Q(s,a_\pi)-Q(s,a_{\mathrm{data}})]\) to the critic loss, with the sampled policy action detached. It reuses the actor’s action sample and regularizes the value where the scalar update obtains its gradient (Section 3.2, Appendix C).
- **Integrated fine-tuning procedure and evidence:** SQAM samples endpoints with an ODE sampler, constructs re-noised linear bridge states, and ordinarily retains TRQAM’s inherited path-space trust region. Experiments examine performance, critic regularization, approximation behavior, and robot adaptation; bridge-state construction and the trust region draw on prior methods (Appendix A, Sections 4.1–4.5).

These results support aggregate benchmark improvement and task-specific robot adaptation. They do not establish universal superiority or exact adjoint equivalence. The authors report that gains persist online on OGBench, but numerical online endpoints are absent from the supplied text. Critic-error explanations and the proposed reasons for EXPO-FT’s weakness remain hypotheses rather than demonstrated causal mechanisms (Sections 3.2, 4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The cause of diagonal Jacobian concentration is unexplained; purpose-built value learning and joint multi-task fine-tuning remain open. Robot policies were fine-tuned separately per task (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Diagonal dominance of a **batch average** does not establish isotropy for individual states. The practical method additionally drops a theoretically present scale factor, and bridge states only approximate rollout distributions away from the optimum (Section 3.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Robot evidence covers fixed initial configurations, operator-judged success, and a small task set; training-seed replication and confidence intervals are not reported (Appendix E.2; Table 2).
- **Comparison boundary:** Robot EXPO-FT omits human corrective actions, freezes the base policy, and replaces its separate encoder with shared VLM features. Its results therefore evaluate this adapted protocol (Appendix E.2).
- **Analyst inference:** SQAM changes loss states and critic training as well as the adjoint. Supplied ablations do not separately isolate bridge-state effects; domain-specific tuning and benchmark regressions also qualify attribution and generality (Appendices A, D.2; Table 1).

## Open research questions
1. **When does the scalar approximation remain reliable?** Batch-average structure lacks a theoretical explanation (Appendix H). Next experiment: measure per-state Jacobian anisotropy and adjoint error across domains and training checkpoints, then relate them to success.
2. **Does the penalty improve critic accuracy or mainly suppress harmful gradients?** Critic error is hypothesized, without direct accuracy measurements (Section 3.2). Next experiment: compare value and gradient estimates against rollout-based references under each regularizer.
3. **Can one policy improve across multiple robot tasks?** Separate-task adaptation leaves the claimed multi-task benefit untested (Appendix H). Next experiment: jointly fine-tune one policy and measure per-task improvement and retention.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main offline and robot results are explicitly tabulated, but causal explanations and broader applicability are less established. Missing information includes exact compute timings and measurement hardware, numerical OGBench online endpoints, robot training-seed variability, direct critic-error measurements, isolated bridge-state ablations, and verified implementation availability.
