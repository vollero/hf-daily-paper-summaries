# Improving Proactive AI Assistance with Hierarchical Procedural Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06505) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06505)


## One-sentence takeaway
Joint phase–step–action supervision improves proactive video guidance over step-only training on ProactiveCoachBench, while a separate guidance router supports requested granularity changes, although evaluation uses ground-truth dialogue histories (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
A proactive assistant must understand procedural progress, decide when to speak or remain silent, and provide an appropriate level of detail. The authors argue that existing datasets emphasize event detection or fixed-granularity guidance, leaving insufficient supervision for tracking completion across procedural levels and changing guidance granularity (§1).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Hierarchical training data and benchmark.** ProactiveCoach-Instruct and ProactiveCoachBench provide task goals, egocentric videos, phase–step–action hierarchies, and guidance timestamps. Construction combines model-assisted filtering, video recaptioning, hierarchy refinement, and rule-based timing assignment (§§3.1–3.2; Appendix B).
2. **Joint hierarchical guidance learning.** A single VLM learns level-tagged guidance with parent–child IDs and response/silence decisions. Training uses ground-truth output histories and selectively supervises silent chunks to reduce silence dominance (§4).
3. **Separate guidance-level selection.** The trained model generates multi-level guidance; a lightweight LLM router selects the level requested by the user without further guide-model fine-tuning (§5.3; Figure 6).

These results support hierarchical supervision under the paper’s anchor-based protocol: models receive the preceding 10 seconds of video and complete ground-truth dialogue history (§5.1). They do not establish autonomous, continuous assistance with accumulated model errors or improved user task outcomes. The adaptation comparison establishes a system-level advantage; the supplied description does not clearly isolate the router’s contribution from differences in guide training and history representation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Ground-truth histories leave error accumulation and plan revision untested; adaptation requires explicit user requests; alternative valid execution orders need better evaluation (Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference:** Predefined response anchors and centrally sampled silent anchors limit evidence about timing throughout an uninterrupted stream (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Model-generated annotations receive human review on only 50 test examples, with action-level alignment weakest; annotation reliability across the full dataset remains uncertain (§3.2; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** PQS depends on GPT-5.2 judgments. It must be interpreted alongside gF1 because correct silence earns full credit; sF1’s temporal cost affects matching but does not directly penalize its final score (Appendix F).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Several §5.3 cross-references incorrectly point to Table 6. Table 2’s flattened base-model rows are partly ambiguous, although captioned ablation tables remain readable. No uncertainty estimates or repeated-run results are supplied.

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains survive self-generated histories?** Appendix I leaves error accumulation open. Run full-video rollouts with model histories and measure missed guidance, repeated guidance, and recovery.
2. **Does hierarchical structure help beyond additional supervision volume?** The ablations add guidance levels but do not report a matched-volume control. Compare hierarchical targets against equally sized targets with parent–child structure removed.
3. **Can granularity adapt automatically to user needs?** Current adaptation follows explicit requests. Test expertise estimation and automatic level selection against requested-level routing in a user study.
4. **Can evaluation accept different valid procedures?** Appendix I identifies alternative execution orders as unresolved. Test videos with reordered valid steps using multiple-reference annotations and order-aware scoring.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main method and ablation conclusions, but material gaps remain: router identity and adaptation implementation details; matched-training adaptation baselines; reviewer count, agreement, and explicit scoring scale; repeated-run uncertainty; latency and deployment cost; verified artifact availability; and the contents of prompt figures provided only as captions or image links.
