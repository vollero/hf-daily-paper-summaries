# SWE-Game: Can Coding Agents Build the Games We Want?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33678) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33678)


## One-sentence takeaway
SWE-Game evaluates coding agents on 247 reference-grounded game-development tasks, finding construction scores below 60/100 and stronger agreement with human behavioral judgments from executable checks than from video judging (Tables 3 and 5).

## Problem and motivation
Building a game requires coordinating mechanics, assets, progression, and feedback. A convincing appearance or reachable victory screen does not establish that required interactions work. The authors identify a gap in connecting gameplay specifications to behavioral evaluation across construction, repair, and engine migration (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reference-grounded benchmark:** 41 executable Godot games support brief-to-game, GDD-to-game, skeleton completion, bug repair, and Godot-to-Unity porting tasks. Requirements permit alternative implementations while defining observable targets (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Evaluation across independent implementations:** Semantic object roles, input bindings, and state observations let evaluator-owned probes check mechanics. Reference-input replays and agent-authored demonstrations use matched no-input controls to test action dependence; repair checks restoration and preservation (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Capability and evaluator-validity study:** Six model–framework configurations are evaluated, with error analysis, a reference-video ablation, and human validation. Construction presentation is assessed separately using game-specific visual rubrics adapted from prior work (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad task coverage over a modest corpus | 247 tasks from 41 games across 13 categories: 24 2D and 17 3D games; 41 tasks per non-repair mode and 83 repair cases. | §§3.1–3.2; Appendix A |
| Construction remains incomplete | Across six configurations, Opus5 leads every mode. Its mean construction totals are 50.38 for Brief-to-Game, 59.68 for GDD-to-Game, and 54.06 for Skeleton Completion, all on a 0–100 scale. | Table 3 |
| Repair and porting have different performance profiles | Opus5 leads with mean totals of 83.46 for repair and 72.40 for porting. Repair averages per-case products of restoration and preservation factors; porting uses a weighted aggregate. | Table 3; Appendix D.1 |
| Executable checks better match human behavioral labels | On 1,200 assertions from 100 construction-mode games, executable checks achieve 92.59% balanced accuracy versus approximately 78.41% for video VLM judgments. Defect detection is 94.25% versus 75.40%. | §4.5; Table 5 |
| Visual rubric scores track human ratings | On 200 clips, Spearman correlation is 0.829; mean absolute error is 0.103 and repeated-score SD is 0.034 on a [0,1] scale. | §4.5; Table 5 |
| Reference video improves presentation scores in the tested setting | Across the same 41 Brief-to-Game tasks, video raises VLM totals by 5.60 points for GPT-5.6 Luna and 2.23 for Opus5; other rubric groups change in mixed directions. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Error patterns differ across reviewed configurations | Requirement omissions account for 56.4% of GPT’s and 36.9% of Opus’s categorized problems; gameplay logic accounts for 52.2% of GLM’s. Denominators are categorized problems, with multiple problems possible per game. | §4.3; Figure 4(d); Appendix F |

These results support the authors’ division between executable behavioral checks and visual assessment within this benchmark. They do not isolate model capability from framework effects, establish comprehensive gameplay correctness beyond tested behaviors, or demonstrate that visual scores measure player enjoyment. Scores across task modes use different criteria and should not be treated as directly comparable difficulty measures.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The corpus has limited coverage of large projects and long-term development. Each model uses one framework, porting is an initial migration study, and broader human assessment is needed (limitations, p. 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Certified routes retain reference timing, so a failed replay can reflect sensitivity to implementation timing as well as missing behavior; alternative implementations are permitted (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Functional human validation covers Mechanics and Content assertions in construction modes, leaving repair, porting, and other score components without equivalent reported validation (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** The visual prompt’s scoring override requires numeric ratings despite incomplete footage, creating a risk that missing observation lowers scores (Appendix H.6).
- Error-analysis sample sizes and selection details are not reported; its percentages cannot establish population-wide failure frequencies (Appendix F).

## Open research questions
- **How much of the ranking comes from frameworks?** Single-framework evaluation leaves this unresolved. Next experiment: cross models with multiple frameworks under matched budgets.
- **Can replay checks accommodate equivalent gameplay with different timing?** Fixed reference timing leaves this boundary unclear. Next experiment: compare fixed replays with adaptive semantic drivers on human-verified equivalent implementations.
- **Does evaluator validity extend to repair and porting?** Reported functional validation covers construction only. Next experiment: human-label restoration, preservation, and cross-engine behaviors, then measure agreement.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and descriptive results; causal interpretation and generalization remain limited.

Material missing information includes framework identities and detailed run budgets, run-to-run uncertainty, evaluator VLM identity, human-rater procedures and agreement, error-review sample counts, and a confirmed public artifact release location.
