# Mobile-4DGS: Unified Static-Dynamic Real-time Mobile Gaussian Splatting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05289) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05289)


## One-sentence takeaway
Mobile-4DGS combines baked first-order appearance, multi-view primitive control, explicit motion, and reusable depth orders for mobile static and dynamic rendering, but the supplied text omits the numerical tables needed to quantify its reported advantages.

## Problem and motivation
Gaussian scene representations burden mobile devices with appearance storage, redundant primitives, deformation inference, and repeated depth sorting. The authors identify a gap between fast sorting-free rendering with compositing artifacts and sorted pipelines that repeatedly rebuild ordering during dynamic playback (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Compact appearance:** Monte Carlo statistics of positive high-order radiance residuals feed a learned first-order SH mapping; an attribute-conditioned network refines coefficients. Both mappings are baked before rendering (§IV-A).
- **Multi-view density control:** Stratified camera sampling and alpha-weighted reconstruction errors supplement gradient-based cloning, splitting, and opacity-based pruning (§IV-B).
- **Unified explicit dynamics:** Quadratic center trajectories, temporal opacity windows, and a learned binary static–dynamic partition avoid runtime deformation networks. Dynamic primitives carry eight temporal scalars, with the partition stored separately (§IV-C).
- **Depth-order reuse:** Static ordering is cached per camera; a motion bound certifies unchanged quantized ordering or supports optional approximate reuse, skipping ordering work and index uploads (§IV-D).

The paper explicitly extends Mobile-GS and Flux-GS: Monte Carlo aggregation already appeared in Flux-GS. Its stated extensions include unified dynamics, temporal optimization/compression, and partition-enabled depth-order reuse (§I, relationship to prior versions).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Exact DOC is conservative in dense scenes; relevant depth-key ties reject certification. Approximate reuse can change ordering (§IV-D).
- **Author-stated scope:** The method prioritizes efficiency–quality trade-offs; Real-Time4DGS achieves higher overall dynamic reconstruction fidelity (§§V-B, VI).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed covariance and appearance, plus quadratic trajectories, restrict each primitive’s temporal expressiveness; no targeted stress test of these restrictions is supplied (§IV-C).
- **Analyst inference:** The relaxed budget bounds permitted depth travel, but the supplied derivation does not establish an image-error bound or guarantee that every ordering change involves overlapping footprints (§IV-D).
- **Evidence gaps:** No separate dynamic motion, gating, temporal-support, or temporal-quantization ablations are presented in the supplied text. Mobile evaluation uses different hardware for static and dynamic scenes, limiting direct comparison (§§V-A–V-C).

## Open research questions
- **How useful is exact DOC during camera navigation?** Camera-depth changes invalidate its cache (§IV-D). Compare reuse rate and end-to-end latency under fixed views and controlled moving-camera paths.
- **What visual errors does budget reuse permit?** Depth drift does not establish image fidelity. Sweep reuse budgets against fresh sorting and measure image differences and temporal artifacts.
- **Where does explicit temporal modeling lose fidelity?** Its motion, covariance, and appearance restrictions lack isolated evaluation. Compare quadratic versus richer trajectories and time-varying attributes on controlled dynamic sequences.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method descriptions are detailed, but quantitative advantage sizes cannot be assessed. Missing information includes Tables I–VI cells, plotted measurements, rendering resolutions, benchmark splits, run counts and variability, detailed timing procedures, several temporal hyperparameters, and independently verified release contents.
