# Real Long-Term Memory for AI: A 50-Million-Token Window That Is Faster and Cheaper Than Recompute

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10845) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10845)


## One-sentence takeaway
The paper reports faster, lower-GPU-energy restoration of stored KV blocks across a 50-million-token corpus on two Gemma models, while leaving passage selection and simultaneous attention across that corpus outside scope (§§6–8).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address repeated prefill computation and loss of access to tokens outside a model’s active context. Their proposed gap is durable reuse of previously computed attention state beyond the native window, without keeping the whole corpus in GPU memory (§1). The claimed benefit depends on restoring a requested block; finding that block is not evaluated.

## Approach and contributions
- **Durable KV storage:** `galahad-kv==1.31.4` integrates with vLLM through `GalahadConnector`, depositing block KV state into AES-256-GCM-encrypted local storage and restoring it for subsequent requests. One block remains resident at a time (§3).
- **Scale and serving evidence:** Relative to the authors’ prior work, this study extends the corpus to 50 million tokens, evaluates under vLLM, adds encryption at rest, and measures restoration against prefill (§§2, 5–6).
- **Evaluation protocol and harness:** Runtime-mutated facts, blind ingestion, decoys, storage audits, separate restore/recall metrics, and absent-record controls aim to reduce benchmark shortcuts (§4; Appendix A).

Byte-exact grafting itself is attributed partly to companion work; this paper explicitly does not repeat logit-equality validation (§6.1).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported, using one H100 80 GB with local NVMe, Gemma 4 12B bf16 and 31B FP8, and temperature zero (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Restoration succeeded at sampled depths | Both models: 100/100 zero-recompute restores; sampled depths span 0–49,488,000 tokens in a 50-million-token corpus. | §6.1 |
| Recall remained model-dependent | Exact planted-code recall: 82/100 for 12B; 98/100 for 31B. Misses selected decoys or other numbers present in the block. | §§6.1, 6.4 |
| No fabrication observed in these probes | Neither model emitted an absent code; each fabricated answers in 0/20 absent-record controls. | §6.1 |
| Restore beat full prefill | Against a previously unseen 16,000-token block with the same request shape: 12B had 2.8× speedup and 8.8× lower GPU energy; 31B had 4.25× speedup and 12.3× lower GPU energy. Energy was idle-subtracted using NVML. | §§4, 6.2 |
| GPU memory and restore time reportedly stayed flat | VRAM remained flat during ingestion of the full corpus; restore time reportedly did not grow with depth. No detailed curves or absolute VRAM values appear in the supplied text. | §§6.2–6.3 |
| Storage cost was substantial | Authors report 1.86 TB for 12B and 6.25 TB for 31B, with all 3,125 encrypted blocks retained in each store. | §5.1; Appendix B |

The evidence supports sampled block reuse and a comparison against recomputation in this configuration. It does not establish a simultaneous 50-million-token attention window, autonomous retrieval, general absence of hallucinations, or superiority over other KV-offloading systems. Byte-exact logit equivalence is asserted through companion work rather than demonstrated here (§6.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only one block is resident; learned retrieval is excluded. Local NVMe is required for the reported performance, and disk requirements are substantial (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Restore probes take approximately 0.3 seconds, near NVML counter resolution; absolute energy estimates have wider uncertainty (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** Two models on one hardware configuration provide limited support for broad model-agnostic or model-size-scaling claims (§§5–6).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text lacks absolute timing, uncertainty estimates, quantified deposit cost, and comparisons with named offloading alternatives. End-to-end cost and competitive advantage remain unclear (§§2, 6).
- **Internal ambiguity:** Section 4 describes one needle per block, whereas §5 specifies 100 needles, one per 500,000 tokens. Probe coverage cannot be reconciled from the supplied text.
- **Internal ambiguity:** Section 6.4 discusses stronger-model escalation using “identical stored blocks,” but does not demonstrate cross-model KV compatibility.
- **Reproducibility gap:** Appendices name scripts and artifacts without supplying their contents or download locations; §9 also requires a licence.

## Open research questions
- **Does retrieval preserve the savings?** Passage selection is excluded (§8). A next experiment could measure retrieval accuracy and end-to-end latency with a selector over the stored corpus.
- **When does storage become economical?** Deposit and full-system costs are unquantified (§6.3). Measure ingestion, storage, CPU/I/O energy, and repeated reuse to determine break-even points.
- **How broadly does restoration generalize?** Evaluation covers two Gemma variants (§5). Repeat the protocol across architectures and precisions, including direct logit-equality checks.
- **Does memory support richer reasoning?** Evaluation tests planted-code recall (§6.1). Test multi-fact questions requiring information from several separately stored blocks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the mechanism and reported outcomes, but provides limited underlying measurement detail.

Missing information includes raw results and runnable harness contents, absolute latency and energy measurements, variability, deposit costs, block-selection procedures, resolution of needle-placement inconsistencies, direct byte/logit-equality evidence, and comparisons against other storage-based KV systems.
