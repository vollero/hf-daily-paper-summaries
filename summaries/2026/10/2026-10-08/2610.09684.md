# From Pareto to Preference: Personalized Test-Time Scaling via Amortized Agentic Policy Discovery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09684) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09684)


## One-sentence takeaway
PersonTTS searches for executable reasoning controllers that jointly meet accuracy, latency, and compute requirements, with authors reporting improved replay performance and lower discovery overhead through reuse of prior controllers and design guidance.

## Problem and motivation
Existing test-time scaling methods typically optimize accuracy against cost or latency separately. The authors argue that users require all three constraints to hold simultaneously, and different requirements can favor different controller behavior (§1).

Personalized controller discovery addresses this mismatch, but repeating program search for every requirement profile adds overhead. Source policies also require target-specific evaluation because changing requirements can change both acceptance thresholds and controller execution (§§3.1, 3.4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint-requirement formulation:** Joint satisfaction rate (JSR) measures the fraction of replay seed batches meeting an accuracy floor, maximum per-question latency ceiling, and mean per-question compute ceiling simultaneously (§3.1).
- **Amortized controller discovery:** PersonTTS retrieves a source controller using standardized accuracy and log-resource coordinates, then evaluates it under target requirements. A frozen Guide distilled from source search histories informs subsequent code revisions. Five new candidates are evaluated, retaining the highest discovery-set JSR; agent parameters remain unchanged (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix B.4).
- **Conditional transfer analysis:** The paper separates threshold changes from execution changes and derives target-JSR bounds conditional on execution-deviation bounds. This supports target replay rather than guaranteeing nearest-neighbor transfer (§3.4; Appendix E).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Experiments compare personalized discovery and reuse ablations on mathematical reasoning benchmarks with separate source/target profiles and discovery/held-out problems (§4).

The offline replay foundation and executable controller synthesis build on AutoTTS; the proposed additions concern the joint objective and cross-profile experience reuse (§§2, 3.2).

These findings support the authors’ account of useful personalization and experience reuse **within the evaluated replay setting**. However, the supplied text contains table and figure captions without their numerical bodies or plotted values, preventing independent assessment of JSR effect sizes and variability. JSR is batch-level constraint satisfaction, not subjective user satisfaction or per-request success probability (§3.1; Ethics Statement).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation uses mathematical problems, simulated operational profiles, and offline replay; deployment requires workload-specific validation and resource accounting (Ethics Statement).
- **Author-observed generalization limits:** Discovery JSR differs from held-out JSR, and retrieval-bank expansion can hurt held-out performance (§4.4).
- **Accounting limitation:** Latency is reconstructed from cached timings, not measured batch wall time. Inference “cost” is parameter-weighted token compute, distinct from discovery-agent dollar expenditure (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Unseen profiles are sampled through the same calibration procedure as source profiles, so these experiments do not establish robustness to substantially different requirement distributions (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The 1,000 replay seeds reorder cached branches and are explicitly not independent discovery runs; they do not establish search reproducibility across independent agent runs (Appendix B.2).
- **Missing evidence:** Numerical table bodies, uncertainty estimates, baseline configuration details, and prompt/API figure contents are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does replay improvement survive live execution?** The paper evaluates reconstructed resources. A next experiment could execute frozen controllers live and compare measured latency, billed cost, and JSR with replay predictions.
- **How should retrieval handle poor coverage?** Larger banks hurt HMMT held-out performance. Compare nearest-neighbor retrieval with coverage-aware or confidence-based initialization on shifted requirement profiles.
- **When does reuse repay its preparation cost?** Reported savings concern target agent calls, while source searches and Guide construction require preparation. Measure total lifecycle expenditure across increasing numbers and frequencies of target profiles.
- **How stable are the gains across discovery agents and runs?** Evaluation uses one specified discovery agent, and replay seeds do not represent independent searches. Repeat discovery with independent runs and alternative agents under matched evaluation budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method, metric, splits, and scope are clearly described; performance conclusions rely largely on author narrative.

Material missing information includes exact JSR scores, absolute agent time/cost, variability and statistical uncertainty, numerical cross-benchmark results, full baseline settings, source-bank/Guide preparation overhead, and the contents of prompt, API, and Guide figures.
