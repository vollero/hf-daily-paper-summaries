# Agentic RAG Evaluation: Budget Allocation Across Questions, Trajectories, and Reads

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05034) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05034)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the tested agentic RAG workflow, broader question coverage improves policy-comparison precision at matched model-token cost, while monetary efficiency and optimal repetition counts depend on search fees, model behavior, and audit uncertainty.

## Problem and motivation
Agentic RAG evaluation divides resources among questions, search trajectories, and repeated answers from fixed evidence packets. These choices affect both comparison uncertainty and cost. The authors identify a practical gap: established variance-based allocation principles need validation against agentic planning, retrieval, and reading workloads, including whether a small audit predicts precision when repetition counts change (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Measured allocation comparison:** Compare Open search, which plans without retrieval feedback, against Adaptive search, which uses earlier results. Both use four queries and five final documents. Evaluate the Adaptive-minus-Open answer-F1 contrast on equally weighted HotpotQA and MuSiQue panels, averaging RRF and round-robin selectors (§§3–4).
- **Forecast validation:** Fit question, trajectory, and reader variance components from a separate audit; compare their precision forecasts with a simpler question-only scaling baseline on new allocations and saved repetition subsets (§4.1).
- **Complete cost and repetition analysis:** Measure planning/reading tokens, cached search requests, fee sensitivity, fitted one-read efficiency loss, direct one-versus-two-read efficiency, and a fixed-packet temperature control (§§4.2–5.4).
- **Reusable evidence:** The authors report an artifact containing scored outcomes, costs, audit data, forecast provenance, and standalone analysis scripts (§6).

The contribution is empirical validation and scoped allocation guidance; the underlying generalizability framework comes from prior work (§2).

## Evidence and main results

Intervals below are reported 95% intervals.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| More questions improve precision at similar token cost | A/B/C use questions/trajectories/reads of 446/2/1, 174/2/5, and 298/3/1. At 34.20/34.14/34.39M model tokens, SEs are 1.03/1.54/1.18 F1 points. A reduces SE by 33.3% versus B and 12.6% versus C; both ratio intervals favor A. | §§3.2, 5.1–5.2; Figs. 1, 4 |
| Initial forecasts are accurate under both models | Archived nested forecasts are within 4.0% of measured A/B/C SEs; Q-only forecasts are within 3.5%. B predictions coincide by construction. Parameters were fixed before extension, but numerical forecasts were computed retrospectively. | §§4.1, 5.1 |
| Deeper-trajectory forecasting advantage is unestablished | On 48 questions, across both read settings, nested/Q-only MAEs are 0.18/0.37 F1 points; advantage 0.19 [−0.06, 0.36]. For trajectories 3–5, MAEs are 0.16/0.13, with advantage interval [−0.25, 0.25]. | §5.1; Fig. 3 |
| Question-versus-trajectory fee efficiency favors A within the tested price range | C/A variance-times-total-fee ratio is 1.31 [1.05, 1.61] with free search and 1.25 [1.01, 1.54] at $1 per 1,000 requests. Its lower bound stays above one throughout that range under recorded model fees/cache conditions. | §5.2; Fig. 4 |
| Question-versus-read monetary ranking remains unresolved | B/A variance-times-total-fee ratio is 1.01 [0.62, 1.40] with free search and approaches 0.88 [0.55, 1.23] as search charges dominate; intervals include one at every nonnegative price. | §5.2; Fig. 4 |
| One-read efficiency is model-dependent and uncertain | Across 15 model/panel/selector settings, fitted one-read excess variance at the same token budget is 0–9.9%. Pro/RRF’s 9.9% fixed-trajectory penalty has a bootstrap range of 0–57.1%. Direct one/two-read variance-times-token ratios are 0.736 [0.727, 0.761] for the Flash check and 1.134 [0.913, 1.636] for Pro/RRF. | §5.3; Fig. 5 |
| Lower temperature improves consistency without demonstrated precision gain | On fixed packets in the prior 96-question panel, temperature zero lowers answer disagreement from 14.3% to 3.4%. Typical one-read SE is 2.38 versus 2.36 F1 points at temperature 0.7; ratio 1.01 [0.91, 1.14]. | §§3.2, 5.4; Fig. 6 |

These results establish allocation efficiency for the recorded policy comparison, not a universal optimum. They also do not establish Adaptive’s superiority: allocation A’s mean feedback contrast is +1.66 F1 points with interval [−0.27, +3.65] (§5.2). The authors’ strongest practical recommendation is broader coverage and a single-read starting point for the tested Flash workflow; Pro remains unresolved (§6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One policy comparison, one model family, one FineWeb endpoint, and two QA datasets with F1 around 30 limit generalization (§6).
- **Forecast uncertainty:** Small trajectory counts destabilize component estimates. Subset-validation intervals condition on recorded runs and exclude prior-audit parameter uncertainty; A/B/C share cells and constitute one allocation check (§4.1).
- **Cost validity:** Independent equal-dollar allocations were not run. Fee conclusions hold recorded tariffs, caching, and query reuse fixed (§§4.3, 6). Fitted optimization permits continuous allocation, so integer deployment choices can change costs (Appendix D).
- **Reproducibility:** No immutable model or index snapshot is available. The supplied text describes an artifact but provides no direct artifact access link (§§3.1, 6).
- **Evidence and contamination:** Lexical alias presence does not establish evidence sufficiency; an oracle F1 ceiling is unmeasured. Benchmark-URL masking finds no matches, but other contamination remains possible (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The narrow averaged Flash repetition interval should not be read as equally strong evidence for every single-read realization: the authors report cancellation between opposing batch fluctuations and wider individual-batch intervals (§5.3).

## Open research questions
- **How deep must an audit be to forecast trajectory scaling reliably?** Two-trajectory calibration shows no clear extrapolation advantage (§5.1). A next experiment could fit audits at several depths and test forecasts on independently executed deeper trajectories.
- **Which allocation wins under an actual dollar budget?** Fee curves leave questions versus reads unresolved, and equal-dollar runs are absent (§§5.2, 6). Execute allocations at fixed monetary budgets under controlled cache and search-price conditions.
- **When does Pro benefit from repeated reads?** Its efficiency intervals are broad and the authors reject a reliable default (§§5.3, 6). Expand the Pro panel and independently repeat paired one-read/two-read comparisons for each selector.
- **Does broader coverage remain preferable in other workflows?** Current evidence is restricted to one policy pair and model family (§6). Repeat matched-budget comparisons across additional policy pairs, model families, and retrieval endpoints.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this text-based summary; generalization beyond the tested setting remains uncertain.

Material unavailable information includes direct artifact access, immutable model/index snapshots, independent equal-dollar results, evidence-sufficiency or oracle-ceiling measurements, and forecast intervals incorporating prior-audit parameter uncertainty.
