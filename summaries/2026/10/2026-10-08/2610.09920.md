# Inverting Multi-Vector Visual Document Indices

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09920) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09920)


## One-sentence takeaway
On ViDoRe v3, the authors reconstruct substantial text and page identity from one retriever’s stored patch vectors alone, while showing that shuffling is reversible and pooling resists only the attacks tested (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Visual document retrievers store a page as many contextualized patch vectors, often separately from the original document. The authors ask whether access to these indices exposes readable content despite their apparently opaque representation.

The identified gap is inversion of visual late-interaction indices into document images containing text and layout; prior attacks discussed in the paper concern text embeddings or natural-image features (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Document-image inversion:** An encoder-specific conditional flow-matching transformer generates page images from indices through a frozen VAE. Ordered conditioning supplies spatial positions; set conditioning omits them. Training uses public page–index pairs without auxiliary OCR, perceptual, or adversarial losses (§4.1).
- **Inference from the index:** The pipeline identifies a public encoder using structural checks and reference-vector similarity, estimates page shape using sequence periodicity or a set classifier, and restores shuffled positions using a permutation-equivariant transformer followed by Hungarian assignment (§§4.2–4.4).
- **Leakage and protection evaluation:** The study measures text recovery and source-page re-identification, includes an independent encoder judge, tests pooling and shuffling, and repeats the recipe with separately trained models for a second retriever (§§5–6). The authors claim this extends embedding-inversion risk to visual multi-vector indices.

## Evidence and main results
Unless noted otherwise, results use 2,000 fixed evaluation pages, balanced across eight ViDoRe v3 domains; OCR metrics cover 1,927 pages with at least 20 reference words. Re-identification ranks against 19,252 source-corpus pages (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Raw indices expose substantial text under the primary encoder | Word recall **47.4%**, sensitive-token recall **45.0%**, NED **0.292**, versus kNN **23.4%**, **14.6%**, and **0.858**, respectively; inferred shapes | Table 1; §6.2 |
| Raw reconstructions preserve source identity | Top-1 **98.4%**, versus kNN **19.9%**; independent encoder judge **97.7%** | Table 1; §6.2 |
| Pooling substantially weakens these attacks while retaining retrieval utility | Factors 3/9: word recall **7.6%/8.1%**, top-1 **1.1%/0.5%**, retaining **99.2%/97.8%** of raw nDCG@10; utility uses English queries within each subset | Table 1; §6.3; Appendix L |
| Learned ordering reverses much of shuffling’s identity protection | Without/with position recovery: top-1 **3.8%/93.5%**, word recall **8.4%/23.1%**; restored independent-judge top-1 **87.7%**. The unrecovered setting receives true shape | Table 1; §6.3 |
| Restored order adds sensitive-token leakage beyond kNN | Sensitive-token recall **21.2%** versus **14.6%**; paired bootstrap difference excludes zero. Word-recall difference from kNN does not | Table 1; Appendix J |
| Second-encoder transfer supports identity leakage more than readable-content leakage | Raw inferred-shape top-1 **70.2%** versus kNN **15.4%**; independent judge **60.5%**. Word recall **7.9%** versus kNN **22.3%** | Table 2; §6.4 |
| Attack prerequisites can be inferred in the tested setting | Encoder correctly identified in **26,000 single-page decisions** across 13 candidate stores; primary-encoder raw shape accuracy **99.2%** | §6.1 |

These results establish leakage for the tested encoders and settings, with independent judging reducing concern about circular matching. They do not establish exact document recovery: primary-encoder raw word precision is only 47.5% (Appendix C). Re-identification measures fidelity, not readable-text recovery or a standalone practical attack. Pooling’s failure against these inverters is not evidence of general safety (Appendix N).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Both attacked retrievers belong to the Qwen-based ColPali-style family. Private encoders and deployed stores were not attacked (§8; Ethical Considerations).
- **Author-stated attack limits:** Training losses were still decreasing, configurations were largely untuned, and pooled-vector position recovery was not attempted. Other protections were not evaluated (§8; Appendix N).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** OCR references can contain errors; heuristic “sensitive” tokens were not validated through a human study. Generated content cannot be certified as correct (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** Encoder differences remain confounded by resolution, vector count, backbone, and training characteristics; the paper explicitly cannot isolate the cause of lower second-encoder leakage (§6.4).
- **Reproducibility risk:** Each model was trained once with unseeded initialization. The same-data control uses the same collections but not identical page sets: Appendix B reports partial overlap. Release availability is promised rather than demonstrated (Appendices B, O).

## Open research questions
- **Can pooled vectors support stronger reconstruction?** No pooled-position recovery was tested. Train centroid-to-slot distributions or an end-to-end transport-based inverter and compare against pooled kNN/null controls (Appendix N).
- **Which encoder properties determine leakage?** The second retriever changes several properties together. Hold training pages and attack budget fixed while varying resolution, vector count, and encoder components (§6.4).
- **Can repeated samples distinguish recovered text from invention?** Seed agreement is suggestive, but correctness remains unverifiable. Evaluate consensus-token precision and recall across multiple samples against human transcripts (§8).
- **Does inversion extend beyond public Qwen-based retrievers?** Current scope is narrow. Test another model family and a withheld-encoder setting with explicitly defined attacker access (§8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported settings; broader conclusions remain limited.

The supplied retrieval omits several referenced table bodies, exact sensitive-token regexes, detailed inverter configuration fields, and exact bootstrap intervals. It provides no confirmed release status, human sensitivity validation, repeated-training variability, or evidence for private encoders and real deployments.
