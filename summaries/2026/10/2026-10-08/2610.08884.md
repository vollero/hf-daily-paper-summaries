# Task-Sufficient Contraction: Source Selection for Machine Information Interfaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08884) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08884)


## One-sentence takeaway
For a fixed consumer, preserving every feasible action’s regret certifies a source reduction that preserves the entire finite one-step rate-regret curve, while quadratic scheduling admits an exact centered-load reduction even when retaining only the optimal action fails (§§4, 6.3).

## Problem and motivation
Task-aware compression typically selects a representation for a particular operating point. This paper asks whether the task can instead determine a reduced source **before** selecting the rate, distortion target, encoder, or codebook, while preserving the declared optimization family (§§1–2).

The motivation is machine-to-machine communication: a receiver might need task-relevant distinctions rather than a producer’s complete internal representation. The identified gap is a certification framework separating exact source reduction from architectural restriction, approximation, and reductions that retain the optimum but lose the costs of mistakes (§§1–2, 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Certification framework:** Task-Sufficient Contraction requires full-source solutions to have reduced counterparts with preserved task value and no worse resource use, reduced solutions to pull back faithfully, and claimed constraints to survive (§2). It distinguishes five contraction regimes (§4.1).
- **Consumer-specific regret quotient:** For a fixed action set and loss, \(G_C\) merges states exactly when every action has identical conditional regret. It is the coarsest deterministic statistic preserving these regrets pointwise; with finite sources and actions, it preserves the complete one-step rate-regret curve (Proposition 1; Theorem 1).
- **Quadratic characterization:** For \(f(t,a)=\|t+a\|^2\), the exact quotient is projection onto the span of feasible action differences. Fixed-energy scheduling therefore retains centered load rather than merely the optimal water-filled action (Theorem 2; §6.3).

The Information Bottleneck, semantic rate-distortion, and earlier quantization results are explicitly retrospective examples, not new contributions (§6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| \(G_C\) is coarsest for pointwise regret preservation | Every deterministic regret-complete statistic determines \(G_C\). This is not minimality among all summaries preserving an optimized curve. | Proposition 1, §3.1, p. 5 |
| Full and reduced rate-regret curves coincide | \(R_X(D)=R_G(D)\) for every achievable \(D\), with finite source and action sets and a fixed consumer. Fibre averaging preserves expected regret without increasing mutual information; pullback preserves both. | Theorem 1, §4, p. 8 |
| The optimal-action quotient characterizes the zero-regret endpoint | Under unique Bayes actions in the finite setting, \(R_X(0)=H(B_C)\); this endpoint result is attributed to earlier work. | §4, p. 8 |
| Quadratic regret equivalence has an exact geometric characterization | For a nonempty feasible set with affine hull \(a_0+V\) and finite objective infimum, equal regret profiles occur exactly when \(t'-t\in V^\perp\). | Theorem 2, §6.3, p. 13 |
| Optimal actions can lose essential mistake costs | With energy budget \(E=1\), loads \((0,10)\) and \((0,20)\) both yield optimum \((1,0)\), but feasible action \((0,1)\) incurs quadratic regrets \(20\) and \(40\), respectively. | §6.3, pp. 13–14 |
| Centered load supports scheduling assignment and update | Common load shifts add the same assignment cost to every representative; water-filling cell updates are also invariant to those shifts. | §6.3, p. 14 |

These are mathematical results and a constructed counterexample, not measured compression or system-performance gains. They establish exact preservation within the stated optimization settings. They do not establish practical savings in transmitted bits, latency, compute, memory, or energy; the paper proposes those comparisons for future systems work (§7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Exactness depends on correctly specified actions and loss. Hidden criteria or additional consumers may require a finer source (§8).
- **Author-stated:** The theory is static. State transitions, learning, changing action menus, or altered dependence structures can invalidate a currently sufficient reduction (§§1, 8–9).
- **Author-stated:** The rate-regret theorem assumes finite actions; the continuous quadratic result preserves regret functions directly rather than extending that coding theorem (§4).
- **Author-stated:** Smaller external information requirements do not imply inexpensive construction or a cheaper internal implementation (§§7–8).
- **Analyst inference:** Deployment validity remains untested because the supplied paper provides no empirical interface comparison or procedure with guarantees for estimating regret equivalence from finite data.

## Open research questions
1. **Can certified interfaces deliver practical savings?** §7 leaves this empirical claim open. Compare full-source or aligned-representation communication against a verified reduced interface, measuring task loss, communication volume, latency, and compute.
2. **How robust is contraction to estimated regrets?** Exact equality is defined using the source law, but finite-data certification is not developed here. Estimate regret profiles at increasing sample sizes and measure excess regret against the exact quotient.
3. **How should the quotient change after actions alter the problem?** Recursive preservation is explicitly excluded (§8). Test sequential scheduling with changing constraints, comparing a fixed quotient against one recomputed at each decision.
