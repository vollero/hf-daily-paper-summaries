# Beyond the Parameter Monolith: Reconstructive Memories, Executable Skills, and Residual Assembly for Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04012) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04012)


## One-sentence takeaway
FEM-ASM demonstrates controlled interfaces between a neural language core, reconstructive document memories, and deterministic arithmetic skills, but does not establish effective unrestricted QA or an advantage over simpler retrieval and tool systems (§§6–8).

## Problem and motivation
Document storage, contextual language computation, and exact execution have different update and verification requirements. The author investigates how independently managed components can contribute to a shared neural state without placing every capability in one parameter system. The identified gap is in coordinating and evaluating these interfaces separately: successful storage or retrieval need not produce a correct answer (§§1, 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Typed residual assembly:** Type-shared adapters map external states to proposals at selected neural nodes. A weighted residual reconciles them before a bounded learned correction. Only the deterministic substep has a non-increasing consistency-energy guarantee; the complete iteration does not (§3; Appendix C).
- **Versioned reconstructive memory:** A shared codec initializes document cells, workers optimize local residual states, and immutable versions carry provenance and checksums. Separate lexical indices make stored elements addressable (§§4.2, 4.4).
- **Controlled interface diagnostics:** Experiments separate reconstruction, retrieval, arithmetic execution, and neural rendering, including failed pooled retrieval and global-result interfaces (§6; Appendix F).

The claimed contribution is this combination and its controlled analysis. The author explicitly excludes novelty claims for fixed inputs, summation, external memory, or tool use alone (§2).

These results establish working controlled interfaces and expose failures between retrieval and answer production. They do not establish semantic question-only retrieval, lossless memory, unaided arithmetic, competitive general capability, or efficient full-store serving. The standalone prototype’s MMLU estimates are described as near chance, but its benchmark scores are unavailable in the supplied table text (§6.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** Reconstruction is incomplete and storage-heavy; retrieval uses privileged coordinates; identity-based splits do not guarantee content disjointness or exclude initialization exposure. Arithmetic learns only operation selection, while positional buffers expose answer identities and determine termination. Matched raw-text retrieval baselines, seed robustness, and end-to-end systems measurements remain missing (§8).

Rendering also has training–inference attachment differences, and numeric exact scoring extracts the first signed integer rather than requiring whole-output equality. The supplementary package does not reproduce every reported experiment (§6.4; reproducibility statement).

**Analyst inference:** Because wrong evidence substantially improves NLL over no evidence, the likelihood results alone provide limited evidence that gains depend on correctly retrieved content. This follows from the reported control comparison, consistent with the author’s discussion of generic in-domain conditioning (§6.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can retrieval work from ordinary questions?** Current recall relies on evidence-derived cues and offsets. Next experiment: remove those fields and evaluate held-out question-only retrieval and downstream QA (§§4.4, 6.3).
- **Does reconstructive memory justify its cost?** Raw-text superiority is untested and latent storage is larger. Next experiment: compare matched retrieval systems on answer quality, total storage, latency, and active memory (§§5.2, 8).
- **Can exact execution become reliable complete text output?** Current rendering supplies token identities and length, with protocol mismatches. Next experiment: preserve historical attachments consistently and measure strict sequence equality and learned termination separately (§6.4).
- **Does assembly add value beyond simpler coordination?** The consistency guarantee excludes learned correction. Next experiment: compare residual assembly against direct attachment and matched aggregation under identical evidence and compute budgets (§§3.2, 7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly supports the architecture and its boundaries, but Tables 1–5 contain captions without numerical bodies.

Material gaps include detailed benchmark scores and sample counts, rendering variant scores, arithmetic ranges and test sizes, reconstruction accuracy breakdowns, attachment-condition endpoint metrics, complete evaluation manifests, uncertainty estimates, and complete artifact identifiers. Full reproduction and end-to-end serving costs are not established.
