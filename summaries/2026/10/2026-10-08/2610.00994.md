# VIEScore2: Unified Image Evaluation with Spatially Grounded Explanations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00994) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00994)


## One-sentence takeaway
VIEScore2 jointly scores generated or edited images and localizes defects through textual grids, achieving higher aggregate score correlation than matched-input general-purpose VLM baselines while showing localization gains and increased false alarms (§§4.2–4.4, E.1).

## Problem and motivation
Scalar image-quality scores do not identify where an image fails, and free-form explanations may lack spatial grounding. The authors identify a gap between scoring evaluators and spatial evaluators, particularly for editing and generation with conditioning images; manual defect identification is costly to scale (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified evaluation interface:** A fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B predicts overall or perceptual-quality/semantic-consistency scores, defect locations, or both in one autoregressive pass. Inputs include the generated image, prompt, optional conditioning images, and an evaluation instruction (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Textual spatial representation and post-training:** Sparse coordinates on a default 16×16 grid accommodate heterogeneous spatial annotations. SFT is followed by GRPO using coverage-weighted cell-level Dice, score-accuracy, and format rewards; no learned reward model is used (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Mixed supervision and grounded feedback:** Approximately 38K examples from five existing sources combine score-only, localization-only, and joint supervision. A fixed parser turns predicted scores, categories, and connected defect regions into explanations; these describe predictions rather than independently verifying them (§§3.3, 3.5).

The reported evidence supports aggregate scoring and localization improvements within these settings, especially the contribution of Dice-based GRPO. It does not establish superiority on every task, image, or representation, nor explanation correctness. Most referenced tables lack their actual rows in the supplied text, limiting independent inspection of complete comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only one backbone and scale are validated; released-evaluator comparisons confound architecture, data, and training procedure (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Fixed grids lose small defects and approximate boundaries coarsely. Explanations neither verify predictions nor identify defect causes (§5).
- **Reported trade-offs:** GRPO increases some clean-image false alarms, lowers coverage-mark F1, and reduces PQ SRCC from 0.580 to 0.558 on the 700-example PQ/SC support (§D.1; E.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Union-based Dice does not penalize artifact/misalignment category swaps, so aggregate localization gains cannot establish better category attribution (§3.4).
- **Evaluation boundaries:** The primary suite shares generators with training; ImagenWorld task comparisons use small task subsets. Figure 3 explicitly selects favorable examples (§§A.2, D.1, F).
- **Analyst inference:** Missing table rows, statistical uncertainty estimates, and release details constrain comparison auditing and reproducibility.

## Open research questions
- **Can category-aware rewards improve semantic grounding?** Union Dice ignores category swaps and misalignment localization remains weak (§3.4; E.1). Compare union rewards with category-specific rewards under matched training budgets.
- **Can finer localization avoid excessive output length?** Small defects vanish, while higher grid resolution reduces trained localization performance (§E.2). Test adaptive grids against fixed grids and boxes with matched compute.
- **Can clean-image calibration preserve localization gains?** GRPO increases PAL4VST false alarms (§E.1). Vary clean-example sampling or false-positive penalties and measure both defective-image IoU and clean-image false alarms.
- **Do gains transfer across backbones and unseen generators?** Backbone generality is untested and the primary suite shares generators with training (§5; A.2). Replicate training across backbones and evaluate generator-held-out scoring and localization.
