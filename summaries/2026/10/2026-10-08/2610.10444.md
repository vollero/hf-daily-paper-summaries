# RunningTab: Direct Workspace Interaction with Environment-Side Tabs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10444) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10444)


## One-sentence takeaway
RunningTab improves terminal-based workspace agents by having the environment preserve read excerpts, unopened file candidates, and requirement status outside the context window, achieving the highest reported scores across all tested models and benchmarks (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Workspace agents must assemble deliverables from scattered files over many steps. Finding relevant material does not ensure it reaches the final output: requirements, previously read facts, and promising unopened files can disappear from the agent’s usable context.

The authors identify a gap between file access and persistent task tracking. Their failure analysis finds content missing even after the agent has seen it, motivating an environment-maintained record rather than relying on model-written plans or final self-review (§3.1, Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Setting and diagnosis:** The authors formalize direct workspace interaction (DWI): terminal access to raw workspace files for producing a deliverable, and analyze failures involving content already encountered (§3.1).
- **Environment-side tab:** The agent states requirements; the environment automatically stores observed file content with path and command provenance, plus listed-but-unopened candidates. The tab resides outside both the context window and workspace (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Review and completion mechanisms:** BM25 ranks excerpts and candidates against requirements. Requirements are resolved through a cited excerpt or an “unavailable” reason; a lexical overlap check screens citations, and a single finish check prompts further work (§3.4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Comparisons, ablations, and masked-value recovery experiments test whether retaining and resurfacing evidence improves deliverables (§§4–5). No new trained model or benchmark is established.

## Evidence and main results

All main comparisons below are reported means over three runs. Model order is **GPT-5.4 nano / DeepSeek V4 Flash / Gemini 3.8 Flash**; arrows show **DCI → RunningTab**.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Already-seen facts can be omitted | DCI with GPT-5.4 nano on Workspace-Bench: among attempts producing a deliverable, 19.8% of 891 distinctive expected numeric values appearing in observations were absent from the deliverable and associated with failed rubrics. | §3.1, Figure 2 |
| Better workspace deliverables | Workspace-Bench Rubric Pass Rate (%): 41.63 → 44.73 / 53.28 → 59.99 / 31.42 → 46.91. | Table 1 |
| Better company-task completion | TheAgentCompany file-server-only subset, Success (%; all checkpoints pass): 17.86 → 23.81 / 21.43 → 29.76 / 19.05 → 22.62. | §4, Table 1 |
| Better exact numerical QA | OfficeQA Pro subset requiring no external data, Acc@0% (%; zero allowable absolute relative error): 53.53 → 60.58 / 65.71 → 68.59 / 43.08 → 55.77. | §4, Table 1 |
| Highest reported means throughout | RunningTab exceeds DCI and all three model-managed baselines on every reported metric for each tested model and benchmark. | Table 1 |
| Stored evidence remains recoverable | GPT-5.4 nano on Workspace-Bench: the tab retains 94.8% of expected numeric values seen but omitted from failed deliverables. In the masked-value experiment, recovery of held values is 8.4% without the tab, 45.8% with review plus selected entries, and 78.1% with the full read log. | §5, Figure 4 |
| Ablations support the combined mechanism | GPT-5.4 nano, Workspace-Bench, three runs: Pass Rate is 44.73% for full RunningTab, 41.36% without environmental capture, 41.87% without review, and 42.89% without either evidence check or finish check individually; DCI scores 41.63%. | Table 4 |

These results support improved benchmark performance and the usefulness of preserved observations. They do not establish that resolved requirements actually appear correctly in a deliverable: the resolution check verifies lexical overlap with captured evidence, not output correctness (§3.4). The recovery experiment tests access to omitted values separately from end-to-end task completion. Statistical significance tests are not supplied.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The read log excludes computed values and values seen only in listings (§5, Figure 4 footnote). Agents may retain model biases and errors, and stored excerpts may contain sensitive information (Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference—completion guarantees:** Agent-written requirements can omit obligations. Lexical citation checks and reason-based “unavailable” resolutions do not establish semantic support or successful delivery (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Removing environmental capture also removes review and evidence checks. Its ablation therefore cannot isolate capture’s independent effect (§5, Table 4).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Ablations and numeric recovery use one model on Workspace-Bench. Other benchmarks exclude tasks needing external services or data; all methods use one harness with observation truncation and clearing (§§4–5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison strength:** Model-managed baselines are prompting variants; the evaluation does not establish superiority over a model-written persistent external-memory system (§4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The prose sometimes cites Table 3 for tab-content statistics presented under Table 2. Appendix B supplies figure captions, but the actual prompt and tool-schema contents are unavailable in the retrieved text.

## Open research questions
- **Does the advantage persist with less aggressive context clearing?** All evaluations use the same context-management setting (§4). Next experiment: vary truncation and clearing while comparing DCI and RunningTab under matched budgets.
- **Can semantic evidence checks improve actual delivery?** Current resolution uses term overlap rather than verifying entailment or output inclusion (§3.4). Next experiment: compare lexical checks with semantic support and deliverable verification, measuring false resolutions and rubric scores.
- **How much does automatic capture add over persistent model-written notes?** Existing baselines do not test that comparison (§4). Next experiment: give both methods external storage and comparable review tools, varying who writes the evidence record.
- **Can the tab retain computed results reliably?** Computed values fall outside current capture (§5). Next experiment: log derived values with their inputs and computation provenance, then evaluate computation-heavy OfficeQA tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark comparisons; causal attribution and broader generalization remain less certain.

Material information unavailable includes a RunningTab code-release link, exact capture-detection implementation, complete prompts and tool schemas, token/runtime/storage overhead, statistical significance tests, benchmark subset identifiers, and human-validation sample details for the failure annotations.
