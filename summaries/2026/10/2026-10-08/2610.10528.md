# Long-WAM: Scaling the Context of World-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10528) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10528)


## One-sentence takeaway
Long-WAM reports stronger gains from visual history with autoregressively pretrained video foundations, combining prediction-conditioned robot actions with an optimized deployment stack that preserves future-latent prediction (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Robot controllers need visual history to infer object motion and task progress, but processing that history can delay action. The authors identify a gap between **providing memory access** and **training a foundation that can exploit history**: causal adaptation of a bidirectionally pretrained video generator may not yield the same context benefits as autoregressive (AR) pretraining (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Robot-domain predictive foundation:** LongLive2.0-Robot continues an existing AR checkpoint on robot and egocentric videos without action labels. Its approximately 10,000 hours are *window-equivalent*, not unique footage (§3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Causal-to-causal action adaptation:** A video expert predicts partially denoised future latents; an action expert then generates action chunks conditioned on observed history, predicted futures, language, and robot state. Future latents remain undecoded, and visual KV is reused during action denoising (§3.2).
- **Context-scaling study:** Separately trained context-length variants compare bidirectional, general AR, and robot-domain AR initializations while holding forecast and action horizons fixed within each benchmark (§3.3).
- **Deployment integration:** Streaming observation encoding, asynchronous chunk execution, video-expert NVFP4 quantization, and device-specific tuning reduce inference cost while retaining prediction (§4).

The contribution is the staged predictive-pretraining route, empirical context study, and integrated deployment framework; several training, coupling, and acceleration components explicitly build on prior methods (§§2–4).

## Evidence and main results

All figures below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Longer history benefits robot-domain AR control | RoboCasa GR-1 success rises from **63.3% to 78.7%** with **0.0→19.2 s** history; its lead over bidirectional initialization grows from **3.3 to 17.1 percentage points**. | §1; §5.3; Fig. 6 |
| Context benefits depend on task | LIBERO-Long success rises **94.5%→99.5%** with **0.0→2.4 s** history using LongLive2.0-Robot. | §5.3; Fig. 6 |
| Predicting futures improves the tested action strategy | LIBERO-Long: IDM **99.5%**, action generation without future prediction **94.5%**, co-denoising **97.8%**. | §5.3 |
| Strong simulation results | RoboTwin 2.0 average SR **94.4%**, exceeding ABot-M0.5 by **0.3 points**; after dynamic-data fine-tuning, DOMINO reaches **34.9% SR / 45.1 MS**, exceeding Fast-WAM by **15.0 SR points** and PUMA by **10.1 MS points**. | §5.2 |
| Dynamic physical manipulation succeeds | G1 grasping at **7.5 cm/s**: **90%** success versus **0%** for Fast-WAM and π₀.₅; moving-cup stacking at **3 cm/s**: **19/20** versus **0/20** for each baseline. Each condition uses 20 trials. | §6.1; Fig. 7 |
| Sustained physical execution | YAM success: **80%, 80%, 85%** across sorting, dumpling placement, and bowl stacking; **81.7%** average, 20 trials per task, tasks averaging over 40 s. | §6.1; Fig. 8 |
| Planner augmentation improves composition | On RoboCasa365’s 50-task overall evaluation, the unchanged Long-WAM checkpoint rises **31.4%→54.4%** with GPT-6 Astra; planner alone reaches **25.2%**. | §6; Table 5 caption |
| Acceleration retains most measured success | RTX 5090 V4/A4 inference takes **107.4 ms**, a **3.3×** speedup over BF16 eager; RoboTwin mean SR changes **94.4%→93.5%**. Timing includes full observation VAE computation but excludes controller/IPC and other overhead. | §6.2; Appendix F |
| Asynchrony has little measured success cost | RoboTwin 2.0 IDM SR changes **94.4%→94.2%** at stride **S=12**, execution horizon **R=24**; Fast-WAM and LingBot-VA lose **15.4** and **45.5 points**. | §6.2; Table 7 |
| Longer context increases compute cost | Optimized RTX 5090 chunk latency rises **107.4→341.0 ms** with **2.4→19.2 s** history. | Appendix G; Table 12 |

These results support useful history conditioning, predictive action generation, and responsive execution in the tested settings. They do not establish a universal context optimum or isolate AR pretraining as the sole cause of improvement. Inference latency is also distinct from complete online response time and robot control frequency (Appendices A, F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Each context length requires a separately trained model; adaptive test-time context remains future work (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Performance declines at the longest tested history window, which contains extensive padding. Insufficient real history is the authors’ hypothesis, not a demonstrated explanation (§5.3).
- **Author-stated:** Action training uses noised ground-truth futures, whereas inference uses predicted futures; these conditioning distributions are not identical (Appendix B.2).
- **Author-stated:** Action-free pretraining does not itself establish transfer to unseen embodiments (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparing different pretrained foundations leaves objective, data, and training-history effects incompletely separated (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Physical context-length scaling remains untested here, and real-world rates are based on 20 trials per condition (Appendix A; §6.1).
- **Comparison limits:** Baseline latency configurations and denoising budgets differ; IDM/CoD timing likewise does not isolate execution order alone (§6.2; Appendix F).
- **Evidence availability:** Retrieved tables largely contain captions without cells, and some table references conflict. Reported prose supports the summarized results, but full comparisons cannot be reconstructed.

## Open research questions
- **Does AR pretraining itself cause the larger history gains?** Initialization comparisons do not fully isolate pretraining factors. Next experiment: match architecture, data, and compute while varying the temporal training objective.
- **Can one policy allocate context dynamically?** Current windows use separate models (Appendix A). Next experiment: train variable-context policies and compare success–latency trade-offs against fixed-window variants.
- **Can denser histories extend the useful window?** The longest-window decline coincides with heavy padding (§5.3). Next experiment: repeat the sweep on longer trajectories while controlling padding.
- **Do simulation context gains transfer to physical robots?** The authors identify real-robot context sweeps as future work (Appendix A). Next experiment: vary history on G1 and YAM while recording success and actual handoff delays.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are explicit, but complete numerical table cells, simulation trial counts and uncertainty estimates, matched-pretraining controls, detailed real-world training protocols, and checkpoint-release status are unavailable in the supplied text.
