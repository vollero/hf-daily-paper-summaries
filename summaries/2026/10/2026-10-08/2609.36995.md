# Salt++: Context-Aligned Post-Training for Few-Step Streaming Multimodal Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36995) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36995)


## One-sentence takeaway

Salt++ combines causal representation alignment with context-matched distribution distillation to generate streaming audio–video in four steps, with reported visual and motion quality gains over OmniForcing on JavisBench-mini (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Streaming audio–video generation needs both causal temporal conditioning and fewer sampling steps. The authors identify two training gaps: standard teacher forcing supervises useful history representations only indirectly, while bidirectional score models evaluate contexts different from those available to a causal generator. Existing recipes also switch objectives between causal training, step distillation, and rollout refinement (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Causal Self-Flow (CSF):** A student receives corrupted audio–video history and aligns intermediate target-block features with a clean-history EMA teacher. Both receive the same noisy target block, isolating history as the source of asymmetry. Representation alignment supplements flow matching; the EMA branch and projection heads are discarded at inference (§3.2).
- **Context-aligned autoregressive DMD:** Generator sampling, fake-score training, and real-score evaluation share the causal mask and prefix. Distillation begins with clean histories and continues with generated histories under the same block-conditional objective. Teacher guidance is calibrated separately from consistency-distillation settings (§3.3).
- **Scale-wise extension:** Two low-resolution and two high-resolution generator evaluations, connected by a causal latent upsampler, produce 1664 × 960 output. This separate extension uses bidirectional scoring of completed rollouts rather than the core method’s block-conditional AR–AR scoring (§3.4; Appendix A.6).

## Evidence and main results

All findings below are author-reported; the supplied text includes table captions and narrative results, but omits the numerical table bodies.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better official-prompt visual and motion quality | On 1,000 JavisBench-mini prompts at 832 × 480, four-step causal AR–AR Salt++ improves VQ by **57.1%** and MQ by **44.5%** over four-step causal OmniForcing; Salt++ inference CFG = 1. | §4.2; Table 1(a); Appendices A.2, A.4 |
| Advantage persists with rewritten prompts | On the same 1,000 shared LTX-2-enhanced prompts at 480p, AR–AR Salt++ improves all seven reported metrics over OmniForcing; VQ/MQ increase **59.0%/64.6%**. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Aligned score contexts improve initialization | Before on-policy adaptation, official-prompt 480p DMD VQ/MQ are **0.837/0.140** for BI–BI, **1.131/0.157** for BI–AR, and **3.147/1.351** for AR–AR. | §4.3; Table 3(a) |
| DMD initialization exceeds the dCM comparator | In the initialization comparison, AR–AR TF-DMD improves all seven reported metrics over TF-dCM; VQ/MQ rise from **1.916/0.611** to **3.147/1.351**. | §4.3; Table 3(a) |
| Guidance calibration materially changes results | At matched 3.2k iterations and four-step inference CFG = 1, independent audio/video guidance sampled from U(1.0, 3.5), versus fixed 4.0, improves VQ **91.5%** on official and **82.9%** on rewritten prompts; MQ more than doubles in both. | §4.3; Table 3(b); Appendix A.5 |
| High-resolution extension beats most reference metrics | At 1664 × 960, four-call Salt++ exceeds 40+3-step LTX-2 on six of seven metrics: VQ/MQ **2.730/0.957** versus **2.231/0.607**; DeSync is the exception. | §4.2; Table 1(b) |
| CSF improves teacher alignment | With 40-step inference on JavisBench prompts, CSF improves IB-AV, AVHScore, and JavisScore after initial optimization; CLAP gains emerge later. Exact values are unavailable. | §4.3; Figure 5 |

These results support benchmark quality gains and the usefulness of aligned score contexts in the reported setup. They do not establish equal wall-clock cost, long-duration streaming reliability, or uniformly better audiovisual synchronization. The complete AR–AR route beats TF-dCM on eight of eleven metrics, but TF-dCM retains IB-AV, JavisScore, and DeSync advantages; OmniForcing leads in aesthetics (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** VBench consistency measures concern evaluated clips, not extended rollouts. Generator-call counts do not measure equal runtime across systems or resolutions (Appendices A.3, A.6).
- **Author-stated metric caveat:** Nearly static video can obtain favorable DeSync despite poor visual and motion quality; synchronization scores require joint interpretation with VQ/MQ (Appendix A.3).
- **Author-stated comparison boundary:** OmniForcing at 960p is resolution extrapolation, not a resolution-matched trained baseline. Selected high-resolution stills cannot establish temporal or audio quality (Appendices A.4, B.3).
- **Analyst inference—confounded ablations:** Guidance experiments jointly change range and randomization. Full-route comparisons also differ in continuation length, score visibility, and guidance, so they do not isolate distillation objective alone (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and provenance:** Training uses internal data without supplied size or composition. High-resolution qualitative checkpoints in Appendix B.3 differ from the initialization/schedule described in A.6; their correspondence to aggregate results is unclear.

## Open research questions

- **Does quality persist during long streaming rollouts?** Evaluation uses approximately five-second clips. A next experiment could measure quality, synchronization, and drift over progressively longer generated histories (Appendices A.3–A.4).
- **Which guidance change drives the gains?** Lower range and randomization are bundled. Compare fixed low guidance, randomized high guidance, and independently versus jointly sampled modality guidance at matched training budgets (§4.3).
- **How much does CSF contribute to the final four-step model?** Reported CSF analysis evaluates the multi-step teacher. Distill matched teachers with and without representation alignment through the same AR–AR route (Figure 5; §4.3).
- **Can high-resolution training retain aligned causal scoring?** The extension switches to bidirectional rollout scoring because the CSF teacher is trained at 480p. Compare against a high-resolution causal teacher under matched compute (§3.4).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported settings are well described, but numerical table bodies and plotted values are absent. Material missing information includes training-data size/composition, statistical uncertainty or repeated-seed results, runtime and memory measurements, long-rollout evaluation, confirmed code/checkpoint availability, and clarification of high-resolution checkpoint provenance.
