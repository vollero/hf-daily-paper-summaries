# Internalizing Agent Experience into Diffusion Model Weights via On-Policy Context Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07250) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07250)


## One-sentence takeaway
D-OPCD transfers part of an image-generation agent’s prompt-mediated benefit into Z-Image-Turbo weights, improving query-only generation while enabling a further round of harness adaptation (§4.2; Appendix B).

## Problem and motivation
Text-to-image agents improve generation through prompt rewriting, reusable skills, verification, and refinement, but these improvements normally remain in an external harness. Repeatedly applying that harness costs inference work, and accumulating guidance complicates retrieval. The authors target a gap: transferring the benefit of harness-produced prompts into the generator itself, rather than improving only the agent that supplies its inputs (§1).

The intended transfer covers prompt-mediated gains; it does not directly capture verification or stochastic image-selection benefits (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **D-OPCD:** An EMA teacher receives the original query plus the harness-selected prompt; the student receives only the query. Training matches their velocity predictions at detached states along the student’s own denoising trajectory. Selected images support record eligibility but are not loss targets (§3.2; Appendix D.2).
- **Auto Skill Evolver (ASE):** Completed task episodes become evidence-backed insights, then reusable initial-prompt rewriting skills. Frozen replay of a selected skill library supplies query–prompt training pairs (§3.3; Appendix C).
- **Harness–generator co-evolution:** Select a harness on held-out tasks, replay it for generator training, update the generator, clear accumulated skills and experience, and evolve the harness again. Experiments demonstrate one generator update followed by renewed skill evolution, rather than sustained repeated cycles (§3.4; Appendix B).

These results support partial transfer into query-only generation and renewed harness adaptation in the tested setting. They do not establish replacement of the full harness: standalone performance remains well below the original skill-equipped agent. Likewise, matching the old agent with an updated **skill-free harness** still involves verification and refinement. The supplied text includes table captions but omits the bodies of the main result tables; the numerical findings above therefore rely on accompanying prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Standalone gains are modest; experience useful for immediate generation may be difficult to internalize from the original query alone (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** One generator update and one subsequent skill-evolution round do not establish long-term co-evolution, retention, or transfer across generators and task distributions (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** Final text prompts cannot fully represent tool choices, retrieved information, or reference-image evidence; those settings are outside this evaluation (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** Improvement filtering changes training-set size and composition, preventing attribution solely to better contexts. Its image comparison can also involve different seeds (§4.3.3; Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Statistical reliability is unresolved: supplied results lack confidence intervals or repeated-training variability, and in-distribution evaluation uses one image per task (Appendices D.4 and E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Proposed inference-cost savings remain unquantified; hardware is specified, but training time, harness cost, and amortization measurements are unavailable (Appendices B and D.4).

## Open research questions
- **Which contexts transfer best?** Filtering yields little average change, while increased wording diversity lowers the average (§4.3.3). Next experiment: compare equal-size, task-matched context sets while controlling prompt effectiveness, length, and diversity.
- **Does co-evolution remain beneficial over repeated cycles?** Only one update-and-reset cycle is tested (Appendix B). Next experiment: run multiple cycles and track earlier-task retention, new-task transfer, and total cost.
- **How dependent are gains on the generator and harness?** Evaluation uses one base generator and one scaffold (§4.1; Appendix B). Next experiment: repeat matched distillation comparisons across additional generators and prompt-producing harnesses.
