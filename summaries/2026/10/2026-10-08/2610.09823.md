# UltraText Bench: A Comprehensive Bilingual Benchmark for Evaluating Visual Text Rendering in Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09823) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09823)


## One-sentence takeaway

UltraText Bench evaluates dense English and Chinese text generation with complete region references, revealing differences between legibility and content fidelity while leaving judge reliability insufficiently quantified (§§3–5, Appendix H).

## Problem and motivation

A convincing image can contain a correct headline alongside missing, misspelled, unreadable, or misplaced supporting text. Such errors matter for information-bearing scenes such as receipts, packaging, forms, and interfaces (§1).

The authors identify a gap in combining broad scene coverage, dense multi-region text, and consistent evaluation of content, placement, and carrier integration. They acknowledge that earlier benchmarks already cover long text, multiple regions, and bilingual generation; their contribution is the combined design and uniform reference scheme (§2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 432 manually reviewed prompts, evenly split between English and Chinese, spanning 24 categories and three workload levels. Each requests 4–12 annotated text regions. Prompts were drafted with Claude Opus 4.6 and revised by humans (§§3.2–3.7, Appendix A).
- **Reference and evaluation protocol:** Generators receive only the natural-language prompt containing exact target strings. Evaluation references additionally specify coarse position, relative size, type, carrier, and importance. The existing Q-Judger model applies the new rubric, producing six image-level ratings grouped into fidelity, clarity, spatial quality, and scene quality (§§3.1, 3.6, 4.1).
- **Comparative analysis:** Evaluation of 24 model configurations examines workload, language, and Base/Turbo differences. Ten people participated in human evaluation, but quantitative agreement results are absent (§§4.2, 5).

The composite weights fidelity/clarity/spatial/scene at 60/30/5/5. Fidelity averages accuracy and completeness; spatial quality averages position and layout ratings. These are subjective VLM ratings, not character-accuracy percentages (§4.1, Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

All quality scores below are Q-Judger ratings on a 0–100 scale. Overall results equally average English and Chinese prompt-macro means; workload results are language-specific (§5.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity and fidelity distinguish models differently | Boogu-Image-0.1-Base: Fidelity 75.77, Clarity 78.31; Qwen-Image-2512: Fidelity 59.30, Clarity 79.89, under standard configurations. | §5.2, reported Table 5 results |
| Higher workloads expose substantial performance declines | Qwen-Image-2512 Composite: EN 86.50 at L1 → 42.86 at L3; ZH 89.47 → 49.90. Levels contain different prompts with jointly changing text loads and region counts. | §5.2 |
| Turbo variants show configuration-dependent tradeoffs | Relative to Base, Z-Image-Turbo loses 14.76 Fidelity points and gains 3.81 Clarity points; LLaDA-Image-Turbo loses 7.62 and gains 7.37, respectively. Boogu-Image-0.1-Turbo loses 9.50 Fidelity and 13.45 Clarity points. | §5.2 |
| Highest reported performance also shows score saturation | GPT Image 2 [Low] leads with Composite 99.35; 1,440/1,723 valid images (83.6%) receive 100 on every raw dimension. | §5.3, Appendix G |
| Coverage qualifies that leading score | GPT Image 2 [Low]: valid images EN 859/864, ZH 864/864; represented prompts EN 215/216, ZH 216/216. Failed outputs are excluded from quality means. | §5.3, Appendix G |
| Maximum scores can miss local defects | GPT Image 2 [Low], E4_L3_EN_003 sample 2: all six ratings are 100 despite author-observed degraded small text; input requests 5,230 characters across eight regions in a 1024×1024 image. | Figure A9 |

The evidence supports descriptive differences among the evaluated configurations and illustrates why content fidelity needs separate reporting from visual clarity. It does not establish exact transcription accuracy, statistically reliable close rankings, or causal effects of language, text length, or acceleration. Selected qualitative disagreements demonstrate possible judge failures without measuring their prevalence (§6, Appendices F–H).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated evaluation limits:** One VLM supplies image-level scores without region transcriptions or auditable region-by-region comparisons. No quantitative human–judge or inter-rater agreement is reported (§6, Appendix H).
- **Authors’ stated comparison limits:** Generation resolution, aspect ratio, and API behavior differ across configurations. Independently authored language splits differ in content and text load; workload levels change character load and region count together (§§3.2, 3.5, 5.1).
- **Authors’ stated statistical limits:** Each category–level–language cell has three prompts. Means lack confidence intervals, and repeated images share prompts. The suite is a stress test rather than a frequency-weighted sample (§3.2, §6).
- **Reproducibility limits:** Released GPT Image 2 judge records have null checkpoint revision and artifact-hash fields. They do not establish other configurations’ coverage or score distributions (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Frequent maximum ratings may reduce discrimination among strong models. Figure A9 shows that saturation can coexist with local errors, but its frequency remains unknown.
- **Protocol boundary:** Spatial references lack bounding boxes and within-cell ordering; the rubric specifies no separate penalty for extra, unrequested text (Appendices A.1 and C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How often does Q-Judger miss character errors, particularly at maximum ratings?** Agreement and error-rate estimates are absent. Next experiment: obtain blinded region transcriptions and independent ratings on a representative sample stratified by automatic score (§4.2, Appendix H).
2. **What separately drives degradation: text length or region count?** Both change together. Next experiment: vary these factors independently while holding scene, content style, and generation settings fixed (§3.2).
3. **How much of the EN–ZH difference persists under matched workloads?** Current splits differ in content and character-load distributions. Next experiment: build paired scenes with controlled region count, layout, and text load (§3.5).
4. **Do Base/Turbo fidelity differences persist under controlled settings?** Standard configurations differ. Next experiment: compare matched resolutions and aspect ratios, document sampling settings, and estimate uncertainty across shared prompts (§§5.1–5.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Benchmark construction and reported descriptive results are well specified; judge reliability and close comparisons remain uncertain.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes full table bodies, quantitative human-alignment results, detailed human-evaluation sampling and annotation procedures, confidence intervals, exact judge checkpoint provenance, and complete configuration-specific settings, coverage, and raw records for the remaining leaderboard models.
