# Inherit-MAS: Test-Time Evolution of Multi-Agent Systems through Workflow and Execution Inheritance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02396) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02396)


## One-sentence takeaway

Inherit-MAS combines validated workflow refinement with exact-request execution reuse, reporting stronger benchmark performance than three evolving-agent baselines and lower token usage than independent reruns without reuse, while leaving the causal contribution of workflow inheritance unresolved.

## Problem and motivation

Test-time multi-agent workflow revisions can disturb useful components and repeat completed computations. Preserving an agent’s configuration alone does not ensure that its previous output remains valid when upstream inputs change. The authors address both problems through selective workflow preservation and explicit verification of reusable execution results (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Workflow inheritance:** A meta-model synthesizes a typed DAG; a separately prompted judge assesses its output and trace. Ordinary refinement starts from the latest completed candidate, optionally prunes unhelpful removable nodes, and applies one validated edit to the retained workflow. Pruning can remove several nodes, so the overall transition need not be a single change (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Execution inheritance:** A task-local store restores eligible results only when the complete resolved request—including ordered upstream records, model settings, tool permissions, limits, and environment fingerprint—matches an integrity-verified record. State-changing operations always execute live (§4.4).
- **Search and evaluation:** Completed candidates remain available for final ranking; a stagnation-triggered final restart can replace workflow inheritance. Experiments compare two worker backbones on workplace tasks and multi-hop QA (§§4.3–4.5, 5.1).

The distinctive contribution is the combination of scoped workflow evolution and verified result reuse. Change localization supports accounting and auditability; the authors explicitly attribute avoided model calls to request verification rather than localization itself (Appendix E.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest reported GPT-4o-mini MAS performance | On 130 WorkBench tasks, 55.4% completion: 13.8 percentage points above Single ReAct and 20.8 above TacoMAS. On 300 HotpotQA FullWiki questions, 49.7% joint F1: 3.7 points above Single ReAct and 1.9 above TacoMAS. | Abstract; §5.2; Appendix D |
| Advantage also reported with Qwen3-32B workers | WorkBench completion is 46.2%, matching Single ReAct and exceeding the best evolving-MAS baseline by 11.5 points. HotpotQA joint F1 exceeds Single ReAct by 5.6 points and TacoMAS by 2.8 points. | §5.2 |
| Execution reuse reduces live inference | GPT-4o-mini worker tokens fall from 2.03M to 1.44M on WorkBench and 5.50M to 3.60M on HotpotQA: reported reductions of 29.1% and 34.6%. Total-token reductions are 5.3% and 18.1%. Comparators are independent reruns of the same controller without execution inheritance; WorkBench cost averages two reruns. | §5.4; Figure 4; Appendix E.4 |
| Ranking matters, but misses better candidates | WorkBench online ranking returns 55.4% completion versus 50.0% for the last candidate; an offline oracle solves 82 tasks versus 72 returned. HotpotQA online ranking yields 49.7% joint F1 versus 44.4% for the last candidate and 56.3% for the oracle. | Appendix B.1 |
| Locality is not established as a quality predictor | Only 8/24 improving WorkBench transitions and 37/134 improving HotpotQA transitions leave any node outside the affected region. Edit-family associations are explicitly descriptive, not randomized effects. | Appendix B.2; Table 3 caption |
| Execution equivalence holds in a restricted audit | Across 240 frozen Qwen3-14B/LiveCodeBench candidate-problem pairs, full execution and both inheritance schedules produce byte-identical outputs and scores; inheritance uses 17.7% of full-execution worker-token volume. | Appendix E.4 |

These results support an observed performance advantage under the reported configurations and substantial execution-reuse savings. They do not isolate workflow inheritance from ranking, restarts, additional retrieval exposure, or controller quality. Monotonic returned-prefix performance also does not mean each generated workflow improves: raw candidate performance fluctuates (Appendix B.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Structural validation guarantees valid scoped edits, not successful repairs; judge ranking does not guarantee higher official utility (§§4.3, 4.5).
- **Author-stated:** HotpotQA gains may partly reflect broader retrieval exposure across candidates; this is not separated from topology effects (Appendix B.2).
- **Author-stated:** Baselines differ in search budgets, retrieval allocation, adaptations, persistent memory, and model routing. Cost comparisons are observed operating points rather than matched-budget causal interventions (Appendices D.1–D.2, E.1).
- **Author-stated:** Remote-model reuse restores recorded outputs without guaranteeing fresh-call identity. Deterministic equivalence is qualified on a separate local substrate (Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied results do not independently ablate node selection, the one-edit restriction, or restarts, limiting attribution of the workflow-design gains.
- **Analyst inference:** Two task benchmarks and a shared GPT-5.4-mini controller/judge limit conclusions about broader domains or fully local systems. Confidence intervals and repeated main-performance runs are not supplied.

## Open research questions

1. **Which workflow components drive improvement?** Their effects are not independently isolated. Run matched-budget ablations of selection, scoped editing, and final restarts.
2. **Can a better judge close the selection gap?** Stored-candidate oracles outperform online returns. Compare calibrated judges or ensemble rankers on the same sealed trajectories.
3. **How much QA improvement comes from retrieval exposure?** The authors leave this confound unresolved. Hold total retrieved evidence across the trajectory fixed while varying workflow refinement.
4. **Does reuse preserve quality across repeated remote runs?** Deterministic equivalence is established only locally, and the supplied text gives no HotpotQA no-reuse quality result. Run repeated paired remote evaluations reporting quality, token usage, and trajectory divergence.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed mechanisms, protocols, and numerical prose, but most referenced tables contain captions without their data.

Material unavailable information includes complete main-result rows, exact Qwen HotpotQA scores, most ablation and cost-table entries, uncertainty estimates, component-isolation ablations, and HotpotQA quality with execution inheritance disabled. Appendix F claims a code and artifact release; its contents were not inspected.
