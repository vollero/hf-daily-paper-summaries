# RoboQuest: Generalist Physical Agents that Search, Inspect and Test

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10388) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10388)


## One-sentence takeaway
RoboQuest exposes weak evidence gathering in simulated mobile manipulation: the best evaluated agent achieves 23.2% success, while the authors’ attribution procedure assigns most failures to missing evidence or wrong decisions rather than execution (§§4.2, 5.1).

## Problem and motivation
Robots in unfamiliar environments must discover object locations, inspect hidden properties, and test mechanisms before acting. The authors argue that existing manipulation benchmarks insufficiently test autonomous, multistep investigation where discoveries change subsequent actions and exploration can damage the goal state (§§1–2).

The central question is whether strong manipulation skills translate into knowing what to investigate, how to interpret evidence, and when to commit.

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** Ten tasks covering search, manipulation-based inspection, and interactive testing in RoboCasa365/MuJoCo kitchens. Agents receive physical goals without hidden answers or prescribed investigation strategies; a physical SUBMIT press commits the result (§3.1; Table 2).
- **Exploration demonstrations:** A reported release of 5,000 successful scripted episodes, totaling 366.4 hours, with frame-level stage and subtask annotations. Oracles know the private specification but perform scripted information gathering; evaluation instances, kitchen styles, and some configurations are held out (§3.2; Appendix H, Table 13).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Five zero-shot multimodal agents, separately task-fine-tuned π₀.₅ policies, isolated execution tests, and deterministic failure attribution distinguish evidence acquisition, decision, disturbance, and execution problems (§§4–5; Appendix C).

The contribution is principally an evaluation suite and dataset, rather than a new exploration policy.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Full-task success remains low | Same 500 episodes per frontier agent: Astra 23.2%, Opus 13.8%, Sol 12.2%, Fable 11.4%, Gemini 2.0%; Astra mean progress 48.0%. | §4.2, Table 3 |
| Puzzle Box strongly raises the aggregate | Astra achieves 96.0% success on Puzzle Box; excluding it lowers overall success to 15.1%. Every model achieves 0% on Blackout Search. | Table 3 |
| Execution competence exceeds full-task success | Across eight isolated skills, 20 scenes per skill, Astra/Opus/Sol achieve 80%/81%/72% success with hidden information supplied, no SUBMIT, and a 50-decision limit. | §4.3, Table 4 |
| Evidence and decisions dominate attributed failures | For Astra/Opus/Sol, missing evidence accounts for 43%/43%/46%, wrong decisions 23%/31%/25%, execution 21%/19%/18%, and side effects 13%/7%/12%; failed episodes are weighted equally across unmet requirements. | §5.1, Figure 5 |
| Occlusion worsens completion | Across four tasks and the three strongest models, pooled success falls from 19.7% in Visible to 9.7% in Look and 12.0% in Uncover. | Appendix I, Table 14 |
| Hidden dependencies affect models differently | Puzzle Box, chain lengths 4–5: uncovered→covered success is Astra 94.4%→96.4%, Opus 94.4%→36.8%, Sol 72.2%→60.1%. | §5.2, Table 5 |
| Fine-tuned π₀.₅ rarely completes tasks | On held-out instances, Puzzle Box success is 2.0% with 43.3% mean progress; all other tasks have 0% success. | §4.4, Figure 4 |
| Evaluation is costly | At reported list prices, mean cost per success is $16.3 for Sol, $48.7 for Astra, and $49.5 for Opus, over 500 episodes each. | Appendix B, Table 7 |

These results support the authors’ diagnosis that exploration and evidence use are major weaknesses in this setting. They do not establish a universal causal bottleneck: execution tests simplify tasks, and attribution uses observable events rather than direct access to agents’ understanding.

The claim that occlusion adds failures “entirely” through missing evidence is stronger than Table 15 supports: wrong-decision units increase slightly, although missing evidence shows the largest increase.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ acknowledged ambiguity:** Scene disturbances cannot reliably distinguish unforeseen consequences from accidental collisions (§5.1; Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation covers simulated kitchens and one robot platform; transfer to physical robots or other environments is unestablished (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic limits:** Camera visibility thresholds operationalize “observed evidence,” but visibility does not prove recognition or comprehension (Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference—baseline confounding:** π₀.₅ differs from frontier agents in memory, image resolution, action interface, and control frequency. Its failure does not isolate memory or OOD generalization as the cause; no matched memory or distribution ablation is supplied (§4.1; Appendices B–C).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Full-task skill rates condition on attempted actions, whereas isolated tests use simplified scenes. Their difference cannot establish the authors’ suggested adaptation-to-controls explanation (Appendix G).
- No confidence intervals or repeated training/evaluation runs are reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does explicit evidence tracking improve completion?** Missing evidence dominates attribution, but no tracking intervention is tested. Compare the same agent with and without a structured record of inspected locations, observations, and unresolved goals.
- **What causes π₀.₅’s collapse?** Memory and distribution shift are proposed explanations without separation. Compare memoryless and recurrent policies on matched training-distribution and held-out instances.
- **Can agents explore while preserving recoverability?** Unrepaired disturbances are documented, but prevention strategies are not evaluated. Test explicit safe-storage and recovery planning on Marked Mugs and Wobbly Stand.
- **How much does the decision budget constrain exploration?** Workload increases missing evidence, but budget scaling is untested. Repeat matched instances with larger decision budgets and measure evidence coverage, success, and cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported descriptive findings; **medium** in causal explanations.

Material unavailable information includes training duration and compute, exact VLA task timeouts, checkpoint identifiers, repeated-run variability, attribution validation against human judgments, and a direct benchmark/dataset release location. The supplied text also lacks an in-distribution VLA comparison and physical-robot evaluation.
