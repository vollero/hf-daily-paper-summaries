# Tetris3D: 3D Scene Generation With Objects That Fit Together

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10539) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10539)


## One-sentence takeaway
Tetris3D conditions single-image 3D object generation on neighboring surfaces and physical relations to improve scene coherence under occlusion, although the supplied text omits the numerical results needed to verify the reported gains.

## Problem and motivation
Reconstructing separate objects from an image can produce plausible individual shapes that penetrate, float, or fail to fit their neighbors. Contact regions are often occluded, making support and containment difficult to recover from visible evidence. The authors identify insufficient explicit guidance for local geometric compatibility in independent-object pipelines and methods that couple objects implicitly through attention (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interaction-conditioned generation:** Extend TRELLIS.2 with voxel-aligned neighboring-surface distances, directions, normals, and physical-relation embeddings. Depth occupancy and back-projected image features guide generation directly in scene pose (§§3.2, 2).
- **Occluded-region completion:** Add visible-to-invisible cross-attention, with invisible tokens querying visible tokens at each generation layer (§3.3).
- **Autoregressive scene assembly:** Generate supporting objects before dependents using VLM-inferred relations. Appendix B.4 refines the main text’s topological-sort description with confidence-weighted ordering that resolves conflicting dependencies.
- **ComOb dataset:** Construct simulation-settled scenes containing target objects and neighboring primitives, with meshes, RGB images, masks, depth, and relation annotations (§3.4; Appendix B.3).

The backbone, image encoder, depth estimator, segmentation model, and VLM are existing components; the contributions concern their interaction-aware integration, completion mechanism, and training dataset.

The text establishes the evaluation design and author-reported directions of improvement, but Tables 1–5 contain captions without numerical entries. Magnitudes, variability, and statistical significance cannot be assessed. Scene metrics use a shared rigid ICP alignment; object metrics normalize and align objects individually. Stability is assessed in MuJoCo rather than through real physical trials (Appendix C).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ limitations:** Conditioning provides learned guidance without guaranteeing physical validity. Errors in depth, camera estimation, segmentation, or relation reasoning can distort generation and propagate through sequential reconstruction (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—synthetic distribution overlap:** Training and synthetic evaluation share the ComOb generator, despite disjoint target-object sources. This supports generalization to unseen objects within that construction process more directly than to arbitrary interactions (§4; Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The supplied ablation removes interaction conditioning jointly; separate contributions of surface geometry and relation labels are not established (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** The agentic comparison is qualitative and does not establish broad superiority over agent-based reconstruction (Appendix D.3).
- **Evidence availability:** Figure links and descriptions do not permit independent visual inspection here; missing table values prevent quantitative verification.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is sequential reconstruction to upstream errors?** Appendix E identifies propagation but does not quantify it. Next experiment: perturb depth, masks, relations, and earlier meshes independently, then measure downstream reconstruction and stability.
- **Which interaction signals drive improvement?** The joint ablation does not separate geometry from relation semantics. Next experiment: compare geometry-only, relations-only, combined, and neither under matched training.
- **Does primitive-based training transfer to complex mutual support?** ComOb uses one target with fixed neighboring primitives. Next experiment: evaluate scenes containing multiple irregular objects and cyclic support relationships.
- **Can hard constraints reduce remaining physical failures?** The authors explicitly acknowledge no validity guarantee. Next experiment: add constrained refinement and compare physical violations, image fidelity, and runtime.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and evaluation protocol are well described, but comparative outcomes remain author claims without numerical verification.

Material missing information includes table values, evaluation sample counts and uncertainty estimates, detailed simulation material/contact settings, inference runtime, confirmed code/model/data release availability, and systematic failure rates.
