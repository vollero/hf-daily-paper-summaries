# RobotWorld: Benchmarking Multimodal Agents for Robot Use Across Diverse Tasks and Embodiments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10409) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10409)


## One-sentence takeaway
RobotWorld evaluates five multimodal agents on 84 simulated robot tasks, finding at most 19.0% success under its budgets despite demonstrated perception, computation, and feedback-control workflows (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether general-purpose agents’ digital tool-use capabilities transfer to reliable physical execution. Individual demonstrations do not establish competence across contact, sustained balance, moving targets, and different robot interfaces. They identify a need for broad evaluation that connects executable task outcomes to the decisions and failures producing them (§1).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Benchmark and interaction framework:** 84 tasks from 20 source projects span manipulation, mobile manipulation, locomotion, driving, and aerial control. Task contracts specify observations, actions, controller assistance, budgets, and executable success checks while preserving source physics and control semantics (§§3–4).
2. **Empirical capability analysis:** Five models are evaluated through direct robot calls, with environment-side code control disabled. Recorded trajectories support comparisons of success, resource use, recovery, state tracking, and completion assessment (§§5–6).

The contribution is a benchmark and observational analysis, rather than a newly trained robot policy. Optional generated-program control belongs to the framework but was not evaluated (§§3.3, 5.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall completion is low | One scored episode per model–task pair: Astra 16/84 (19.0%); Opus 5.5 13/84 (15.5%); Kimi K3 2/84 (2.4%); DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash each 1/84 (1.2%). | §5.3; Fig. 1; Appendix D.1 |

These results establish performance of the evaluated model–harness combinations under the declared task contracts. They do not estimate repeated-run reliability, isolate individual capabilities causally, or establish real-world performance. The authors explicitly treat their explanation of model differences as exploratory (§6.6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One episode per pair provides no repeat-trial variability estimate. Coverage is finite and uneven; manipulation and mobile manipulation comprise 69.0% of tasks (§§4.1, 5.2, 7).
- **Author-stated:** Simulation pauses during reasoning, removing real-world latency constraints. Controller assistance differs across tasks, and hardware transfer remains untested (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Reference solution coverage is incomplete; checker validation does not demonstrate every task’s reachability. Temporal checks establish conditions only at sampled ticks (§4.4; Appendix B.7).
- **Author-stated:** Comparisons are observational, with possible provider, collection-time, and build-metadata confounders. Selected cases do not measure failure prevalence, and training-data exposure is possible (§7; Appendices F–G).
- **Analyst inference:** Non-action tiers calibrated from Astra records could influence comparative rankings; identical limits do not establish model-neutral calibration (§5.2).
- **Evidence limitation:** Some invocation accounting and terminal feedback are incomplete; resource logs can extend beyond scoring boundaries (Appendices B.6, H). The supplied source is truncated, so this analysis does not provide complete coverage.

## Open research questions
1. **How stable are the rankings across repeated trials and initial states?** Single episodes leave this unresolved (§5.2). Next experiment: repeated matched-seed runs with varied initialisations.
2. **Which inputs and control support explain the contrasting profiles?** Current comparisons do not isolate vision, computation, or controller effects (§7). Next experiment: paired ablations of those permissions while holding tasks and budgets fixed.
3. **How sensitive are outcomes to non-action budgets?** Late stacking success demonstrates a meaningful boundary effect (Appendix H.4). Next experiment: rerun matched tasks across independently chosen budget tiers.
4. **Do successful strategies survive real-time execution?** Paused simulation excludes inference-delay demands (§7). Next experiment: latency-constrained simulation, followed by matched hardware trials.
