# CADFather: Autonomous CAD Reconstruction through Coordinated Tool Use

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09127) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09127)


## One-sentence takeaway
CADFather coordinates frozen learned proposals, geometric proposals, and parameter refinement to improve executable mesh-to-CAD reconstruction, with ablations supporting tool diversity but leaving the assistant’s selection policy unisolated (§§4.3–4.6).

## Problem and motivation
Meshes describe geometry without exposing construction operations or editable feature parameters. Recovering executable CAD programs could make scanned or legacy components easier to inspect and modify. The authors identify a gap: learned generation, geometric fitting, and numerical refinement have complementary strengths, but their usefulness changes across parts and reconstruction stages (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Candidate-and-tool coordination:** A frozen Qwen3.8-27B vision-language assistant examines renders, scores, and execution history, then selects candidate programs, tools, proposal counts, or termination (§§3.1–3.3, 4.1).
- **Shared reconstruction search:** Frozen CADENA-RL generates next-operation alternatives; an algorithmic tool fits revolutions and extrusions, including additions and cuts; numerical optimization adjusts supported parameters. All use a CadQuery-based DSL and a persistent candidate pool (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Protected output and evaluation:** Programs undergo execution, watertightness checks, measurement, and visual acceptance. The system retains the best valid candidate by mean available IoU/GMS and evaluates the framework on six datasets with tool-removal ablations (§§3.5–4.6).

The claimed novelty is this integration and branching search process; the authors explicitly do not claim a new numerical optimizer or tool use itself (§§1–2).

The evidence supports better measured geometric reconstruction and a substantial robustness benefit from algorithmic proposals under the tested configuration. It does not establish recovery of engineering intent, a compute-matched advantage, or superiority of the assistant’s policy over a fixed scheduler. Frontier comparisons use different inputs and subsets, so their cost and quality rankings are contextual.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Unsupported operations, unsuitable initial topology, ungenerated alternatives, and resource limits can prevent recovery. Parameter optimization cannot change operation structure (§5; Fig. 7).
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Evaluation addresses geometry and execution validity, not original design history, manufacturing intent, assemblies, tolerances, or simulation-validated designs (§5).
- **Comparison limits:** Baselines mostly come from published results; validity rules and evaluated subsets differ. Ablations match configured budgets rather than consumed compute, and direct CAD-agent comparisons remain future work (§§4.2, 4.6, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Best-candidate selection explicitly optimizes the reported IoU/GMS objective. These gains therefore provide limited evidence about unmeasured editability or feature completeness, especially given documented high-overlap results missing surface details (§3.5; Fig. 7).

## Open research questions
- **Does adaptive assistant selection outperform fixed scheduling?** The policy is not isolated. Compare adaptive, fixed, and randomized schedules with identical tools and matched consumed compute (§4.6).
- **How useful are reconstructed programs for engineering edits?** Geometry and validity do not establish design intent. Test feature-parameter edits against expert-defined requirements (§5).
- **Can search escape incorrect topology and missing surface features?** These remain documented failures. Test targeted root diversification and surface-discrepancy proposals under matched budgets (Fig. 7).
- **How robust is reconstruction to imperfect scans?** CADBench uses clean meshes, and no controlled scan-corruption study is reported. Evaluate graded noise, missing surfaces, and mesh density with fidelity, validity, and runtime measurements (§§4.1, 4.5).
