# DSReg: Provably Recovering Individual World Latents without Reconstruction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09457) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09457)


## One-sentence takeaway

DSReg selects a dependency-sparse rotation of an already linearly identified representation, with signed-permutation recovery guaranteed under distinct dependency footprints and functional no-cancellation, without reconstruction or labels (§3.2, Theorem 2).

## Problem and motivation

A representation can retain the full world state while mixing physical factors across coordinates, preserving dense prediction but hindering selective editing, sparse control, and monitoring. The authors identify the unresolved rotation in Gaussian LeJEPA representations as the gap: avoiding collapse and achieving linear identifiability do not isolate individual factors (§§1–3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Identifiability theorem:** Given orthogonal linear ambiguity, minimizing observation–latent Jacobian support recovers individual factors up to sign and permutation under Structural Diversity and functional no-cancellation (§3.2, Theorem 2).
- **Weaker structural requirement:** Pairwise-distinct footprints permit nested supports and single-feature differences. Proposition 1 establishes strict weakening relative to four specified footprint conditions; it does not compare every assumption of prior methods (§3.2).
- **Practical estimator:** Whiten and freeze the representation, estimate local Jacobians using nearest-neighbor ridge regression, and optimize an orthogonal rotation with an anchor-averaged ℓ₁ objective. No global decoder is trained (§3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **Robustness and scaling:** A thresholded population-support theorem gives approximate recovery under bounded Jacobian errors; factored and row-sampled evaluation reduce storage and computation (§3.3, Theorem 3; §C.2).

The authors claim first-of-its-kind recovery without reconstruction, labels, or distributional asymmetry; priority cannot be independently established from this source.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Exact component recovery is conditional | Every global support-minimizing rotation is a signed permutation under the linear premise and both structural assumptions. | §3.2, Theorem 2; §A.2 |
| Dense information can remain unchanged while sparse access improves | On the learned MLP benchmark at latent dimension 8, dense R² is 0.988 for both LeJEPA and DSReg; individual recovery, few-shot readout, and sparse editing improve qualitatively. | §C.3, Fig. 10 |
| Pixel-trained sparse probes improve | LeJEPA → DSReg: control error 0.63 → 0.52; five-step rollout R² 0.74 → 0.81; surprise AUROC 0.995 → 0.999, ten-run means. | §C.5, Fig. 13 |
| Identical footprints preserve a subspace, not individual recovery | On analytic orbits with exact Jacobians, latent dimensions 8–128 and five runs per point, the identical pair’s span has CCA ≥0.998. | §C.3, Fig. 7 |

| DSReg exceeds swept VAE baselines on restricted dSprites | With dense R² ≈0.53, twenty DSReg runs outperform β-VAE and β-TCVAE at their best swept β; one-sided Mann–Whitney p-values are 8×10⁻⁵ and 4×10⁻⁵, respectively. Actual MCC values are unavailable. | §C.7, Fig. 18 |

These results support conditional identification and improved sparse use in constructed or rendered settings. They do not establish arbitrary-world recovery: the exact theorem concerns support minimization, whereas experiments predominantly optimize ℓ₁. Several aggregate tables are present only as captions, preventing assessment of effect sizes and uncertainty.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** All benchmarks are simulated or rendered; no physical-robot evaluation is reported (§5).
- **Author-stated premise dependence:** DSReg inherits linear identifiability and cannot restore factors absent from the encoder’s linear span (§4.1, §C.7).
- **Author-stated objective mismatch:** The ℓ₁ relaxation can globally prefer a slightly mixed rotation even when the support criterion identifies the correct factors (§3.3, §C.8).
- **Structural boundary:** Identical footprints remain indistinguishable from support alone; cancellation can also invalidate recovery (§3.2, Fig. 12).
- **Resource boundary:** Local regression requires sufficiently large neighborhoods; its memory cost limits scaling (§C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Overwidth experiments do not establish automatic discovery of latent dimension, because subspace selection still uses the true dimension (§C.5).
- **Comparison caveat:** Gaussian 3DShapes VAE references use untuned β=4 and i.i.d. images, whereas dSprites uses a β sweep; these comparisons differ in training protocol (§§C.6–C.7).

## Open research questions

- **Does sparse control improve on physical robots?** This remains untested (§5). Next experiment: compare frozen encoders before and after DSReg in real manipulation under matched data and controller budgets.
- **Can the practical objective inherit the support theorem’s guarantee?** The ℓ₁ mismatch remains explicit (§C.8). Next experiment: compare ℓ₁, thresholded support, and support refinement under systematically varied derivative magnitudes.
- **Can extra signals resolve identical footprints?** Support alone cannot (§3.2). Next experiment: add temporal information or limited interventions to identical-footprint worlds and measure individual recovery.
- **Can DSReg choose latent dimension automatically?** The overwidth protocol selects a known-size subspace (§C.5). Next experiment: estimate dimension from Jacobian spectra and evaluate both dimension accuracy and recovery.
