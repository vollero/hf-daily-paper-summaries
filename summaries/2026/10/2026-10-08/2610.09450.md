# Iris-3B: Going Beyond the Latent with Pixel-Space Diffusion Training, Conversion and Fine-Tuning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09450) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09450)


## One-sentence takeaway
Iris-3B demonstrates competitive pixel-space text-to-image generation, but the tested depth and restoration fine-tunes show no clear advantage over latent backbones under comparisons limited by conversion quality and unmatched pretraining (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Latent generators depend on a lossy VAE that can discard spatial detail before generation and constrain dense outputs during decoding. The authors ask whether removing that bottleneck improves transfer to monocular depth estimation and image restoration—an advantage not established by prior evaluations focused on generation or understanding (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **From-scratch model:** Iris-3B combines a hybrid text/image transformer with a PixelDiT pixel-transformer head, operating on RGB patches. It uses flow matching, a resolution curriculum, and DINOv2 representation alignment during the initial stage; the authors report releasing weights and training code (§§3.2–3.3, 6).
- **Recipe ablations:** Matched-step experiments compare prediction targets and REPA variants on a smaller pixel-space model. They select velocity prediction and DINOv2 REPA; the prediction-target comparison also changes the timestep distribution (§3.1).
- **Conversion recipe:** FLUX.2 Klein base 4B is converted by replacing its VAE interface with pixel projections and a pixel head. Gain-matched ridge initialization and a reduced trunk learning rate address early conversion failure (§4).
- **Downstream evidence:** Common fine-tuning recipes test depth and restoration against latent counterparts, documenting negative results, conversion confounds, and patch-boundary artifacts (§5).

The architectural components largely build on cited methods; the contributions are their scaled implementation, conversion adjustments, and empirical transfer study.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive generation | Iris-3B reaches GenEval **0.798** and DPG **86.52**. Released EMA checkpoint evaluated at **1024²**, CFG **3**, **100** sampling steps, **4** samples per prompt; official prompts without rewriting, all samples scored. Authors report OneIG-EN overall matching Qwen-Image, but its score is absent. | §1; §3.4; Table 2 discussion |
| Selected pretraining recipe | Velocity and clean-image prediction tie on GenEval; clean-image prediction is worse on DPG and FID. DINOv2 REPA is strongest overall among tested alignment variants. Prediction targets also use different timestep distributions. | §3.1; Figs. 2–3 discussion |
| Conversion recovery | At **1024²**, global batch **8**, after **1K** steps, ridge initialization plus scaled trunk LR produces prompt-aligned photorealistic samples where the unmodified recipe produces patch noise. Either R²-tuned ridge initialization or LR scaling alone more than halves probe loss but does not yield coherent images. | §4.2; Fig. 5 |
| No clear depth advantage | After the same **10K-step**, batch-**32** direct-regression recipe, Iris-3B matches latent Klein on mean AbsRel and δ₁ across five zero-shot benchmarks and has lower Hypersim SEE; converted Klein trails on all benchmarks except a KITTI tie. Exact scores are absent. | §5.2; Table 4 discussion |
| Pixel models exhibit depth grids | Patch-border curvature diagnostic uses **60 images per benchmark**, comparing pixels adjacent to **16×16** patch boundaries with other pixels. Authors report artifacts in both pixel models, strongest in Iris-3B; numerical ratios are absent. | §5.2; Table 5 caption/discussion |
| No restoration advantage | Matched Klein arms evaluated on identical DIV2K validation pairs with native **4× Real-ESRGAN** degradations: converted pixel Klein is worse on most metrics, especially SSIM and NIQE, and level on LPIPS. Unmatched Iris-3B leads Table 6 methods on MUSIQ but trails both Klein arms on fidelity and DeQA. Exact scores are absent. | §5.3; Table 6 discussion |

These results support feasibility and the absence of an observed downstream advantage in the tested recipes. They do **not** establish equivalence or a general disadvantage of pixel space: downstream experiments have single runs and no significance tests. Generation baseline scores were copied from other papers, with some using prompt rewriting (§3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Iris-3B has no latent twin; model size, pretraining data, and compute confound its depth comparison. Its restoration comparison additionally differs in budget, schedule, EMA scoring, and inference processing (§§5.1–5.3).
- **Author-stated:** Both matched comparisons use one briefly converted parent whose generation quality was not benchmarked. A weaker converted prior is a hypothesis, not a measured explanation (§§4.3, 5.2).
- **Author-stated:** Short, single-run downstream experiments lack significance tests; pixel models retain faint patch artifacts (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Conversion initialization benefits were not tested at the larger batch used in the referenced conversion study (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Depth’s per-image ground-truth alignment limits conclusions about absolute metric-depth accuracy. Restoration benchmark results establish performance under the specified synthetic degradations; selected real-world examples lack ground truth (§5.2; Appendix C).
- **Evidence limitation:** The supplied text contains table captions and narrative summaries, but no table bodies or plotted ablation values, preventing independent assessment of effect sizes.

## Open research questions

1. **Does pixel space help when pretraining is controlled?** Iris-3B lacks a latent twin. Pretrain pixel and latent models on identical data with matched compute, then repeat downstream fine-tuning across seeds (§6).
2. **Is conversion quality responsible for Klein’s deficit?** Converted generation quality is unmeasured. Benchmark successive conversion checkpoints, then compare downstream performance using otherwise fixed recipes (§§4.3, 5.2).
3. **Can overlapping output heads improve dense detail?** Patch artifacts remain despite removing the VAE. Compare PiT with overlapping convolutional or PixelShuffle heads, measuring both SEE and patch-grid ratios (§6).
4. **Does pixel space improve editing fidelity?** Editing is proposed but untested. Train matched pixel/latent editing models and measure preservation of unchanged source regions (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The protocols and qualitative conclusions are explicit, but material information is missing: numerical contents of Tables 1–6, plotted ablation values, exact pretraining dataset identities and sizes, total compute, uncertainty estimates, converted-model generation scores, and release locations or licensing details.
