# Composing What Each Teacher Learned: Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation through Teacher-Relative Shifts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10460) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10460)


## One-sentence takeaway
Δ-MOPD transfers teacher-minus-precursor logit shifts anchored at the student’s initialization, improving three-teacher Math composition over endpoint supervision in the tested setting while yielding comparable interleaved-routing performance (§§3, 5).

## Problem and motivation
A post-trained teacher’s endpoint contains both its post-training changes and preferences inherited from its base. When that base differs from the student’s initialization, endpoint distillation transfers both, potentially distorting the balance among teachers. The authors identify an untested question: whether teacher-relative differences improve multi-teacher on-policy distillation when teacher selection is held fixed, under either simultaneous composition or domain routing (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Target construction:** At each student-generated prefix, form `softmax(z_anchor + Σ(z_teacher − z_precursor))` and train with token-level reverse KL. Each precursor is the exact checkpoint entering the teacher’s post-training stage. Same-origin teachers reduce exactly to endpoint supervision (§§2–3).
- **Controlled multi-teacher study:** Compare shift and endpoint targets under composition, interleaved routing, and phased routing. In paired experiments, only Polaris’s representation changes; Nemotron and JustRL have identical terms across arms (§4).
- **Mechanism and extension:** Diagnose inherited-base gradient pull, teacher-term balance, and target distance; additionally test an unpaired fourth-teacher extension using token-string overlap projection (§5; Appendix F).

The contribution is applying and testing relative shifts in multi-teacher settings; the authors acknowledge earlier single-teacher difference-based methods (§7).

## Evidence and main results
All results below are author-reported; pp denotes percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Base subtraction improves measured composition geometry | Two-teacher mechanism run, late-training averages: teacher-term norm ratio falls from **5.2:1 to 1.44:1**, and full-vocabulary target-to-student forward KL from **0.152 to 0.031**. | §5.1, Figure 2; Appendix C.2, Table 7 |
| Earlier single-teacher Math acquisition offsets scoring overhead | Polaris-only acquisition: shift English-Math macro reaches **47.90% at step 100**, versus endpoint’s best evaluated **46.54% at step 195**; allocated compute is **28.0 versus 42.9 H100-hours**, a **34.7% reduction**. AMC/AIME use Avg@16; MATH-500 uses greedy decoding. | §5.2; Appendix E, Equation 14 |
| Three-teacher composition improves aggregate accuracy | At step 100 on BigMath training prompts, shift composition exceeds endpoint by **4.11 pp Math** and **1.95 pp five-suite macro**, using greedy pass@1. Science/IF is **1.29 pp lower**. | §5.3, Table 2; Appendix C.3, Table 9 |
| Interleaved routing produces small differences | Matched-update greedy pass@1: shifts lead by **0.62 pp five-suite**, **0.91 pp Math**, and **0.19 pp Science/IF**. | §5.4, Table 3; Appendix D |
| Phased routing improves reported means and reduces order sensitivity | Step-300 greedy pass@1: shift five-suite gains are **5.68 and 1.60 pp** across the two orders; the order gap falls from **10.50 to 6.42 pp**. No benchmark-by-order comparison survives the stated multiple-comparison correction. | §5.4; Appendix D.1, Table 10 |
| A projected fourth shift adds Math gains | Unpaired step-100 greedy comparison against three-teacher shift composition: Math macro **33.50→36.82%**, five-suite macro **26.19→28.04%**. All three Math benchmarks improve; Science/IF does not. | Appendix F, Table 13 |

The paired comparisons support target construction as a consequential design choice in these experiments. They do not establish universal superiority: two-teacher aggregate accuracy is similar (§5.3), interleaved-routing differences are small, and phased-routing statistical evidence is limited. The fourth-teacher experiment demonstrates gains from adding a projected shift, without establishing superiority over a corresponding endpoint target.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers public reasoning specialists, with common-domain composition trained on Math prompts. Broader specialist families and domain-mixed composition remain untested (§9).
- **Author-stated requirements:** Exact precursors and compatible tokenizers—or explicit projection—are necessary (§9). Shifts include style and calibration changes, not isolated capability (§2).
- **Author-stated calibration issue:** Base subtraction does not normalize checkpoint temperature or confidence differences (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond the paired Polaris substitution is uncertain; the second cross-origin teacher appears only in an unpaired extension (§4; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Phased results cannot separately identify teacher effects and domain-curriculum effects because each teacher is coupled to its domain (§5.4).
- **Evidence limits:** Some runs approximate the target partition function, and diagnostic pooling differs between runs (Appendix B). Compute measures allocated device time, excludes evaluation, and does not correct for utilization (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
- **Does the advantage generalize across student origins and specialist families?** The paired evidence uses one student initialization and one cross-origin substitution. Repeat paired experiments across several students and unrelated teacher lineages (§§4, 9).
- **How much benefit comes from removing base preferences versus changing signal scale?** Calibration remains unresolved. Compare shifts against temperature-calibrated and norm-matched endpoint controls (§6).
- **Do shifts improve domain-mixed composition?** Common-domain composition uses Math prompts. Test identical teacher sets on mixed Math, Science, and instruction-following prompts (§9).
- **Can general cross-tokenizer transfer retain the gains?** The extension uses near-complete token-string overlap and lacks an endpoint control. Test more divergent tokenizers with explicit alignment and paired target constructions (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the method and reports the main outcomes, but referenced tables contain captions without their data bodies.

Material gaps include complete training configurations, exact checkpoint identifiers and revisions, per-benchmark scores and seed variability, support settings for each approximate-normalization run, and code or trained-student release details. These omissions limit independent assessment of reproducibility and statistical strength.
