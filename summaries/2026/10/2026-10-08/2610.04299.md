# Questioning the Questions: Sustaining Self-Evolution in Reasoning Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04299) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04299)


## One-sentence takeaway
R-Quest combines learned question-validity checks with sampled task-novelty judgments to sustain mathematical self-training through ten rounds on Qwen3-4B-Base, reaching a seven-benchmark average of 51.92% versus R-Zero’s 34.60% at the same round (Figure 5).

## Problem and motivation
Questioner–solver systems generate their own training problems and learn from majority-voted answers, but continued training can reverse early gains. The authors identify two weaknesses: invalid questions can receive difficulty rewards and survive answer-consistency filtering, while lexical diversity penalties miss repeated mathematical tasks expressed differently (§2.2).

The proposed gap is therefore in assessing training questions themselves: answer disagreement does not establish useful difficulty, and different wording does not establish a new task.

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure diagnosis:** Sampled-question audits identify increasing invalidity and task concentration in R-Zero. A matched original-versus-repaired training comparison supports invalid questions as one contributor to degradation (§2.2).
- **Validity feedback:** R-Quest initializes the solver using externally annotated valid and invalid questions. During self-evolution, majority-voted rejection judgments penalize invalid question generation and filter solver data; initialization-data replay preserves this capability (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Task-level novelty feedback:** A frozen copy of the base model compares each candidate with sampled questions, withholding difficulty rewards when it detects the same exercise pattern. Sampling reduces comparison count from quadratic to \(O(BK)\), with eight comparisons per candidate by default (§3.2).
- **Combined evaluation:** Alternating GRPO updates test the two feedback mechanisms across two backbones, three evaluation domains, and an extended ten-round Qwen experiment (§4).

Stronger external-model annotation is required for initialization; subsequent self-evolution feedback comes from the solver and frozen base model.

These results support improved performance and training-data quality in the tested settings. They do not establish indefinite improvement, universal benchmark superiority, or the authors’ suggested mechanism for cross-domain transfer. Domain results use separately selected best checkpoints rather than necessarily one shared checkpoint (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author interpretation:** Stronger novelty suppression may reject harder variants of useful recurring patterns; the larger-comparison-budget experiment supports a diversity–learning trade-off (§4.3.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Ten-round sustainability is demonstrated only on Qwen3-4B-Base; larger models, longer runs, and non-mathematical training curricula remain untested.
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Validity, reference-answer verification, and exercise grouping rely substantially on GPT-5.6-Sol. Uncertain judgments are mapped to invalid during annotation, so operational invalidity can include evaluator uncertainty (Appendix A.2). Independent judge-agreement measurements are not reported.
- **Analyst inference—comparison:** Candidate-question budgets do not establish matched compute. R-Quest adds validity and novelty inference, OCNR doubles candidate generation, and R-Diverse uses additional models (Appendices C–D).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Training-seed variability and benchmark-score confidence intervals are not reported. Initialization and replay effects are not separately isolated in the reported component table.

## Open research questions
- **Does improvement survive beyond ten rounds and across backbones?** Only Qwen receives the extended test. Next experiment: longer runs on both backbones with repeated training seeds.
- **How accurate are the internal judges?** The paper measures solver validity assessment but does not report independent semantic-pair judgment accuracy. Next experiment: evaluate both judges against manually verified, held-out examples and trace errors into training selection.
- **Can novelty control preserve useful difficulty progression?** Stricter suppression lowers concentration and performance. Next experiment: compare fixed novelty gates with difficulty-aware gates that retain harder variants.
- **Which parts of validity training are necessary?** Initialization, evolving judgments, and replay are bundled. Next experiment: independently ablate initialization and replay under matched inference and training budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and descriptive results; **medium** in causal and broader sustainability conclusions.

Material missing information includes repeated-seed uncertainty, end-to-end compute and runtime comparisons, independent annotation agreement, isolated initialization/replay effects, results beyond ten rounds, and confirmed downloadable code, dataset, or checkpoint locations.
