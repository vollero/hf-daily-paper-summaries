# SkillForge: Co-Evolving Skills and Agents via Dynamic Skill Lifecycles

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09832) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09832)


## One-sentence takeaway
SkillForge combines retirement-aware fine-tuning with fitness-driven skill retirement and mutation during agentic RL, reporting higher aggregate performance than SkillRL across three benchmarks while limiting library growth (Table 2; Appendix D, Table 10).

## Problem and motivation
Retrieved procedural skills can guide agents, but obsolete or harmful instructions can accumulate as the policy improves. The authors identify **delayed obsolescence**: skills that previously qualified as stable later fall below the retirement threshold. Their proposed gap is that existing skill-augmented RL methods accumulate skills without adequately removing or revising them (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Retirement-aware initialization:** Evaluate SkillRL seed skills using base-model rollouts, remove sufficiently used low-fitness skills, and fine-tune only on successful trajectories that retrieved no subsequently retired skills (§4.1).
- **Online skill lifecycle:** Track episode success for retrieved skills and apply trial, active, stable, and retired states alongside GRPO training. Usage gates, demotion thresholds, retirement budgets, and library caps govern retention; an external teacher rewrites moderately performing skills using failure examples (§§4.2–4.4).
- **SkillFurnace:** The authors report releasing trajectories, annotated skill snapshots, and human-labeled retirement events with joinable identifiers and a CC-BY-4.0 license (Appendix C).

The central methodological contribution is lifecycle management coupled to an evolving policy; GRPO and the seed skill library are inherited components (§§3–4).

These results support the complete pipeline’s benchmark gains and a benefit from controlling library accumulation. They do not establish individual skills’ causal effects: fitness measures success when a skill is retrieved, rather than improvement relative to withholding it. The headline 7.8% gain is specifically WebShop’s relative success improvement, not a pooled metric across environments.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** All retrieved skills share one binary episode reward, preventing individual contribution attribution. Experiments use one 7B base model, and mutation quality depends on the external teacher (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** QA baselines are imported from source papers; recomputing weighted averages does not establish matched retrieval, decoding, or training conditions (Table 10). Appendix H’s significance tests use a fixed SkillRL estimate or assumed baseline variance, rather than observed matched baseline runs.
- **Analyst inference—mechanism isolation:** Removing retirement also removes size capping, so that ablation does not separate fitness-based removal from generic capacity control. Teacher alternatives and cumulative-versus-recency fitness estimators are discussed but not experimentally compared (Table 3; Appendices B and H).
- **Analyst inference—internal ambiguity:** Trial skills belong to the retrievable non-retired library, conflicting with the case-study claim that the trial gate prevents harmful variants from affecting it (§§4.3–4.4; Appendix E.3). Appendix H also explains that some Table 3 “final” library counts instead represent pre-retirement seed counts.
- **Analyst inference—failure interpretation:** Moderate initial annotation agreement and selection of already-retired skills limit how broadly the failure taxonomy can be generalized. Historical fitness decline alone does not prove that skills became causally harmful (Appendices C and E.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can fitness identify causal skill utility?** Shared credit leaves attribution unresolved. Compare current fitness with randomized skill withholding or matched counterfactual rollouts.
- **How quickly should obsolescence be detected?** The cumulative estimator intentionally trades responsiveness for stability without a direct comparison. Test cumulative, sliding-window, and exponential estimates under controlled policy or task shifts (Appendix H).
- **How dependent are gains on model and teacher choice?** Only one base model and mutation teacher are evaluated. Repeat matched-budget experiments across policy sizes and teacher strengths (Appendix G).
- **Does selective retirement outperform simple capacity control?** The retirement ablation also disables capping. Compare fitness-based retirement with random or age-based eviction at identical library sizes (Table 3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial method, evaluation, and dataset detail, but causal interpretation and comparison fairness remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes a direct release/download location, the actual mutation prompt, detailed SFT settings, exact pre-retirement counts per environment, baseline training and evaluation variance, and results across independent training seeds. Figure curves are represented mainly by captions and textual descriptions.
