# STEPQuant: When and Where Errors Matter in Delta-Rule Recurrent State Quantization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38169) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38169)


## One-sentence takeaway
STEPQuant combines lifetime-aware precision allocation with readout-aware spatial scaling to preserve near-FP32 accuracy at nominal six-bit recurrent-state precision on two evaluated language models, while reducing state storage by roughly fivefold (Tables 1–2; Appendix D; §5.6).

## Problem and motivation
Fixed-size linear-attention states still consume substantial serving memory because each concurrent request needs persistent state slots. Uniform low-bit quantization introduces errors that feed back through successive Delta-rule updates and can sharply reduce generation accuracy (§§1–2).

The authors identify two gaps: precision allocation should account for how long errors persist, and spatial scaling should account for both unequal key-row readout sensitivity and magnitude variation across rows and columns (§§3–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Error analysis:** Derives conditional error propagation under identical inputs and gates, and experimentally relates accumulated error to gate lifetime and readout error to key-row location (§3.1; Appendices A–B).
- **Temporal quantization:** Allocates mixed precision using lifetime-weighted reconstruction distortion, with sparse FP16 protection for high-risk units. Allocation operates on whole heads for Qwen and key rows for Kimi, and remains fixed across requests (§3.2; Appendix C.2).
- **Spatial quantization:** Combines dynamic row-magnitude scales with calibrated row-impact factors, then fits shared value-column scales using weighted reconstruction error (§4.3).
- **Serving implementation:** Integrates packed states into SGLang, fusing reconstruction, recurrent update, and readout while overlapping scale fitting and writeback with later model computation (§4.4; Appendix F.1).

The contribution is this combination for Delta-rule recurrent states; mixed precision and dual-axis scaling themselves have precedents acknowledged by the authors (§6.2).

All results below are author-reported.

| Compatible with quantized weights | Seven-task mean accuracy with AWQ four-bit weights, STEPQuant@6bit versus FP32 states: Qwen 79.27% versus 79.32%; Kimi 58.62% versus 58.95%. | Table 3 |

These results support effective compression in the tested architectures and workloads. They do not establish universal accuracy preservation, statistically significant superiority over FP32, or equivalent gains under dynamic serving. The DAMP comparison is cross-study and uses different generation settings, so it does not establish controlled superiority (Appendix G).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Gate-only lifetime weighting approximates persistence without fully modeling time-varying, key-dependent transitions (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Four-bit KDA loses long-generation accuracy; Qwen can generate longer outputs despite retaining average accuracy (Appendix H; Tables 1 and E4–E5).
- **Author-stated:** Only two models and fixed hardware/workload settings are evaluated; other architectures and dynamic workloads remain unverified (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Conditional replay analysis isolates state distortion but does not fully model feedback from changed generated tokens into future inputs (Proposition 1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Component ablations show benefits from combining components, but do not separately isolate lifetime weighting from distortion-only allocation, or row-impact weighting from all other spatial-fitting changes (Tables 4 and E6).
- **Analyst inference:** Small accuracy differences are difficult to interpret without reported uncertainty estimates. Serving memory gains also depend on state-slot reservation and memory accounting (Appendix F.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Would transition-aware allocation improve long-generation robustness?** Gate-only weighting omits key-dependent dynamics (Appendix H). Compare it with a transported-readout objective under the same effective storage budget.
2. **How robust is frozen calibration under distribution shift?** Lifetime rankings are stable across the tested WikiText, C4, and coding streams, but broader shifts remain untested (Appendix B.6). Recalibrate across domains and measure accuracy, allocation stability, and generation length.
3. **Do gains persist under dynamic serving?** Existing efficiency tests hold batch size and sequence lengths fixed (Appendix F.2). Test mixed lengths, request arrivals, slot reuse, and latency alongside memory and throughput.
