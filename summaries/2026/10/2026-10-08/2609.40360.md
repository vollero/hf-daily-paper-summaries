# Semifactual Credit-Augmented Policy Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40360) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40360)


## One-sentence takeaway
SCAPO reduces GRPO credit for tokens sensitive to answer-preserving prompt changes, with reported AIME 2024–2026 accuracy gains of 5.63 and 4.17 percentage points on Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-1.7B-Base, respectively (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Outcome-based RLVR provides coarse credit: GRPO assigns every response token the same outcome-derived advantage. The authors argue that this can reinforce dependence on irrelevant prompt features alongside useful reasoning. They investigate whether token probability changes under answer-preserving prompt interventions can supply finer credit without process annotations or an external reward model (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Token sensitivity diagnostic:** Hold sampled responses fixed and measure token probability drift under paraphrases, minor typos, irrelevant scenario wrapping, and appended irrelevant context. A frozen-model decoding experiment tests whether suppressing high-drift candidates improves accuracy (§2; Appendix C).
- **SCAPO credit assignment:** Teacher-force sampled responses under original and perturbed prompts using the rollout policy. Standardize negative drift within each prompt group, aggregate across perturbations, and add only negative stability scores to GRPO advantages, with gradients stopped. Stable tokens receive no extra credit (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Early-training augmentation:** Apply the correction initially, then revert to standard GRPO. Experiments compare reasoning accuracy, transfer, credit-source/sign ablations, and runtime; Appendix D provides a conditional optimization bound, rather than a guarantee of improved accuracy (§§3.4–4; Appendix D).

The reported comparisons support the usefulness of stability-informed credit in these settings, including transfer beyond mathematics. They do not establish that drift identifies causally spurious tokens or that SCAPO generalizes to other architectures. Fewer optimization steps and a small probing runtime share also do not establish a matched wall-clock speedup over GRPO.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Training covers only dense Qwen3 models at 1.7B and 4B on DAPO-Math-17K. Larger models, other architectures, broader training domains, and larger data scales remain untested (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—proxy validity:** Probability drift measures sensitivity, not token correctness or a demonstrated causal mechanism. The paper itself describes it as a proxy for potential spurious dependence (§§1–3).
- **Analyst inference—intervention validity:** Perturbations are generated with instructions to preserve answers, but the supplied text does not report an independent preservation audit or failure rate (§2; Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and ablations:** No training-seed counts or confidence intervals are available. The supplied experiments do not establish sensitivity to perturbation type, correction strength, or augmentation duration; the reported source/sign ablations use only the smaller model (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Evidence availability:** Numerical table bodies, several figure contents, and prompt templates are missing from the retrieved text despite being referenced. This limits comparison and reproducibility assessment.

## Open research questions
- **Which perturbations provide reliable credit?** The method combines four types without a reported type-specific ablation. Next experiment: independently audit answer preservation and compare each type against their combination.
- **How robust is the early-phase schedule?** The paper uses fixed correction strength and duration without a supplied schedule sweep. Next experiment: vary both across repeated seeds and compare accuracy under matched training time.
- **Does the method generalize across architectures and domains?** Appendix A explicitly leaves this open. Next experiment: repeat matched SCAPO–GRPO comparisons on larger and alternative architectures with non-mathematical training data.
- **Does SCAPO expand solvable-problem coverage?** Higher Pass@128 is reported, but numerical curves are unavailable. Next experiment: measure per-problem coverage across sampling budgets and compare which problems each method solves.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are clearly described, but the supplied text lacks full result tables, uncertainty estimates, training-seed counts, most benchmark sample counts, complete hyperparameters and generation templates, intervention-validation statistics, hardware and absolute runtime, and schedule/type ablations. Code and data availability are author claims, unverified here.
