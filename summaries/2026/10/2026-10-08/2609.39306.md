# ReSAIL: Mitigating Collapse in Iterative Agent Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39306) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39306)


## One-sentence takeaway
ReSAIL combines PI-sensitive step selection, trajectory-balanced distillation, and privileged-view retention to sustain agent success over three self-distillation cycles, with a reported average final-cycle improvement of 22.5 percentage points over SDPO and OEL (Table 1; §4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Iterative self-distillation trains an agent using privileged information (PI), then deploys it without that information and makes it the next cycle’s teacher. The authors observe declining deployment performance and PI-conditioned competence in existing methods. They identify two gaps: uniform training does not prioritize informative interaction steps, and ordinary-view distillation does not explicitly preserve the privileged-view behavior needed for future supervision (§§1, 3.1; Figure 1).

The target setting permits new deployment trajectories between cycles but no additional environment interaction during offline training (§§2, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory-Balanced Selective Distillation:** Rank interaction steps by the frozen teacher’s token-averaged Jensen–Shannon divergence between predictions with and without PI. Select the highest-scoring steps across each batch, average losses within each represented trajectory, and normalize over the full trajectory batch (§3.2).
- **Privileged Retention:** Regularize the student’s PI-conditioned predictions toward the frozen teacher’s PI-conditioned predictions at both selected and unselected training steps. Combine this with selected ordinary-to-privileged distillation (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Empirical contributions:** Demonstrate sustained three-cycle improvements when augmenting offline SDPO and OEL, and separately test sensitivity-based filtering for multimodal GUI action prediction (§4). The authors’ “first evidence” priority claim cannot be independently established from the supplied paper.

PI consists of trajectory-derived summaries in these experiments. Distribution comparisons use a Top-20-token-plus-residual-tail approximation (Appendix B.1–B.2).

## Evidence and main results
Text success results report means and standard deviations across three **decoding** seeds; these do not measure independent training-run variability (Appendices A.3, C.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sustained deployment gains | Both augmented methods outperform their parents at every reported cycle and have non-decreasing mean success across all six model–benchmark settings. Reported average final-cycle gain: 22.5 percentage points across SDPO/OEL comparisons. | Table 1; §§1, 4.2 |
| Strong final-cycle OOD performance | Qwen3-8B, ALFWorld OOD, without PI: SDPO rises from 49.2% (2.8) to 75.3% (1.2); OEL from 41.4% (1.4) to 78.4% (1.2). RFT reaches 63.8% (5.3). | Table 1 |
| Selection criterion matters | OEL/Qwen3-4B, Cycle 1, selection ratio 0.05 with balancing and retention fixed: Top versus Random gives 62.2% versus 49.7% ID success and 57.3% versus 45.8% OOD success. | Table 3(a) |
| Retention supports later cycles | OEL/Qwen3-4B, Cycle 3: adding PR to selective, balanced distillation raises ID success from 56.5% to 74.2% and OOD success from 45.6% to 67.2%. | Table 2 |
| Better preserved PI-conditioned competence | Fixed summaries and initial states on 128 ALFWorld OOD tasks: privileged-view success falls from 61.5% to 44.8% between Cycles 1 and 3 without retention, but rises from 58.9% to 70.3% with PR. | Figure 3; Table 9 |
| Modest offline GUI improvement | Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, 100 updates, fixed top-80% filtering: AITZ accuracy is 65.95% versus 64.89% for OEL on 843 non-stop steps from 101 episodes, using one decoding seed. | §4.4; Appendices A.3, C.2 |
| Full method costs more | Approximate Cycle 1 ALFWorld training-loop cost on eight H800 GPUs: OEL 4.33 GPU-hours; SGS-only augmentation 4.08; full ReSAIL 5.32. Deployment collection is outside this measurement. | Figure 5(a); Table 8 |

The evidence supports mitigation over the tested three-cycle horizon and complementary roles for selection and retention. It does not establish indefinite recursive improvement. Fixed-PI evaluation isolates competence under constant guidance; the authors explicitly state that it does not assess summary generation or subsequent teaching effects (Appendix D.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Explicit measurement limits:** AITZ evaluates reference-history action prediction without executing actions and excludes reference stop steps. It therefore does not establish end-to-end GUI task success (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generality:** Results cover two text benchmarks, two sizes from one model family, and three cycles. The detailed ablations use OEL/Qwen3-4B on ALFWorld (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain robustness:** Reported text error bars capture decoding variability, and AITZ uses one decoding seed. Independent training-run variability is unavailable (Appendices A.3, C.1).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism remains partly unresolved:** Ordinary-view retention also improves deployment substantially. PR’s larger PI-conditioned advantage supports its rationale, but does not isolate how much later improvement comes from better teachers versus general regularization (Table 3(b); Appendix D.2).
- **Comparison scope:** SDPO and GRPO are offline adaptations; conclusions apply to these implementations. Matched update budgets do not imply matched compute costs (Appendix B.3; Table 8).

## Open research questions
1. **Does mitigation persist beyond three cycles?** The reported horizon ends there. Extend training across additional cycles while tracking both input views and independent training seeds.
2. **Does retained competence produce better subsequent supervision?** Fixed-PI testing does not assess teaching effects. Compare summaries and distillation targets generated by checkpoints with different retention strategies using a common student.
3. **Does SGS improve executed GUI task success?** AITZ evidence is offline step accuracy. Test filtered and unfiltered models in executed episodes, including termination decisions.
4. **How sensitive is selection to the probability approximation?** Training uses Top-20-plus-tail comparisons without a reported approximation ablation. Compare larger supports or full-vocabulary scoring under matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported three-cycle benchmark findings; lower for long-term improvement and causal explanations.

Material information unavailable includes independent training-run uncertainty, results beyond three cycles, end-to-end GUI success, probability-approximation ablations, and a stated release of ReSAIL code, checkpoints, or generated summaries.
