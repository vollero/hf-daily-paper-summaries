# NAMVIS: Next-Scale Autoregressive Multi-View Image Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04722) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04722)


## One-sentence takeaway
NAMVIS uses camera-conditioned next-scale autoregression to synthesize novel object views, reporting better image fidelity than evaluated diffusion baselines and approximately 3.3× faster inference at 256×256 resolution (§4.3).

## Problem and motivation
The task is to generate unseen viewpoints from a few posed images without per-scene optimization or explicit 3D reconstruction. Sparse observations leave geometry and appearance ambiguous, while diffusion-based generation incurs repeated denoising costs. The authors identify an opportunity to adapt next-scale image generation to geometry-aware multi-view synthesis (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint next-scale generation:** Built on Infinity, NAMVIS predicts discrete bitwise residual tokens coarse to fine, generating all tokens within a scale and across target views in parallel. A block-causal mask permits communication across views while preventing attention to future scales (§3.2).
- **Multi-scale Projective Pose Encoding:** Adapts existing ProPE to changing token resolutions and routes source and target camera transformations through target self-attention and source-to-target cross-attention (§3.2).
- **Dual conditioning:** Frozen VQ-VAE source features provide both pooled global conditioning through a start token and adaptive normalization, and dense geometry-aware cross-attention (§3.2; Figure 2).
- **Data assets:** The authors report releasing filtered scene identifiers, rendered images, camera parameters, masks, point maps, captions, and preprocessing scripts for approximately 200K objects (§4.1). Availability is not independently verified here.

The contribution is the geometry-aware multi-view adaptation and integration; Infinity’s bitwise generation and the underlying ProPE formulation are inherited methods.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The checkpoint is limited to 256×256; higher-resolution inference requires further training. Attention sequence length and cache costs motivate more efficient designs (§4.5).
- **Author-stated:** Object-centric training may not transfer directly to complex real-world scenes. Pose-noise robustness remains future work (§4.5; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons are protocol-dependent: Wonder3D uses a different elevation, and single-view baselines receive best-PSNR selection across available source views (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Reduced-data ablations support architectural choices under their fixed budget, but do not establish their effects at full training scale. No isolated removal of target-to-target attention is reported (Table 5; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty estimates or repeated-run results are supplied; small consistency differences therefore have unclear statistical reliability.

## Open research questions
1. **How robust is geometry conditioning to camera errors?** Pose-noise robustness is explicitly deferred (§5). Perturb camera rotations, translations, and intrinsics, then measure fidelity and registration degradation.
2. **Can scale-local attention preserve quality at higher resolution?** The authors propose it but provide no experiment (§4.5). Compare full and scale-local attention after matched higher-resolution training, measuring quality, latency, and memory.
3. **Does the method generalize to complex scenes?** Object-centric evaluation leaves this unresolved (§4.5). Compare object-only and mixed object/scene training on held-out scene-level views.
4. **How much consistency comes from target-view communication?** Existing ablations do not isolate it (Table 5). Disable cross-view target attention while retaining source conditioning and compare COLMAP outcomes and per-view fidelity.
