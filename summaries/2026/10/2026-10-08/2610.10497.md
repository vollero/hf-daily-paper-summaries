# QuadTok: Quadtree Visual Tokenizer for Autoregressive Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.10497) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10497)


## One-sentence takeaway
QuadTok combines spatially adaptive quadtree tokenization with topology-conditioned autoregressive generation, reducing reconstruction token counts while enabling measurable zero-shot subject placement on ImageNet.

## Problem and motivation
Fixed-grid tokenizers allocate equal capacity to regions with unequal visual complexity. Flexible one-dimensional tokenizers address token budgets but, the authors argue, often lack explicit token–region correspondence. QuadTok aims to preserve spatial grounding while allocating additional tokens where refinement improves reconstruction (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Quadtree tokenizer:** Each discrete token represents a spatial region. A ViT and Transformer aggregator encode region instructions; a shared codebook quantizes them; hierarchical residual decoding reconstructs the image. Breadth-first serialization supports variable-length, coarse-to-fine representations (§3.1).
- **Region-Wise Complexity Guidance:** Two complementary probing trees estimate each region’s reconstruction benefit using LPIPS. Regions exceeding a threshold receive finer tokens (§3.3).
- **Kinship Causal Mask:** Within the tokenizer, coarse tokens interact globally, fine tokens read coarser levels, and same-level fine interactions stay within sibling groups. The generator instead uses ordinary causal attention (§3.2).
- **Topology-conditioned generation:** A decoder-only generator predicts visual codes under a tree supplied before sampling. Expanding a requested region provides spatial control without layout-specific retraining (§§3.4, 4.3).

Content-adaptive tree selection applies to encoding existing images; benchmark generation uses independently sampled trees rather than predicting image-dependent topology.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer reconstruction tokens with a fidelity tradeoff | At 256×256 on ImageNet-1K, QuadTok uses **230 mean tokens**, rFID **1.46**, PSNR **20.37**, versus LlamaGen f=16 at **256**, **2.19**, **20.79**, respectively. | Table 1 |
| Reconstruction transfers to COCO | Without fine-tuning, QuadTok uses **232 mean tokens**, rFID **7.88**, PSNR **19.30**, versus LlamaGen f=16 at **256**, **8.11**, **20.42**. | §4.1, Table 1 |
| Competitive class-conditional generation | On ImageNet at 256×256, **947M** QuadTok achieves gFID **2.08**, versus **2.15** for **1.4B** RandAR-XXL; several listed systems achieve lower gFID. Settings differ across systems. | Table 2 |
| Mask improves downstream generation | With the same **697M generator and CFG**, using the mask changes gFID from **3.17 to 2.23** and reconstruction PSNR from **19.16 to 20.37**, while rFID worsens from **1.41 to 1.46**. | §4.4.1, Table 4 |
| Guided allocation approaches full-tree reconstruction with fewer tokens | On ImageNet reconstruction, guided trees use **230 tokens**, rFID **1.46**, PSNR **20.37**; full trees use **320**, **1.50**, **20.39**. | Table 5 |
| Prescribed layouts improve localization | At **192 tokens**, **1,000 images per condition** across **50 ImageNet classes**, prescribed versus random trees improve hit rates across all four directions: **17.5–28.7 percentage points** for Grad-CAM, **4.3–7.1** for boxes, and **4.9–7.9** for masks. FID-50cls changes by **−0.15 to +0.12** against **2,500** class-matched real images. | Table 3, Appendix F |
| Native high-resolution generation is demonstrated | At ImageNet 512×512, a **344M** generator achieves gFID **2.734** over **50,000 samples**, versus **3.04** for **675M** DiT-XL/2 and **4.38** for approximately **431M** CAT, under different system settings. | Table 7, Appendix D.1 |
| Reduced generation latency comes with preprocessing cost | On an A100-80GB, BF16, batch **32**, CFG **2**, **343M** QuadTok at **189.2 mean tokens** takes **7.504 s/batch** for sampling versus LlamaGen’s **8.404 s** at **256 tokens**. Separately, batch-128 pretokenization costs **6.186 ms/image** for QuadTok versus **2.885 ms** for LlamaGen VQ-16. | Tables 9–10, Appendix E |

The evidence supports adaptive reconstruction, benefits from the tokenizer mask, and limited spatial steering using frozen models. It does not establish universal quality superiority: reconstruction trades PSNR against rFID, generation comparisons use different settings, and timing uses randomly initialized LlamaGen generators. The layout results concern predefined half-planes within existing ImageNet classes, rather than arbitrary multi-object layouts.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Compute constraints limit generation experiments to class-conditional ImageNet; open-domain text-to-image generation remains future work (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated layout limitation:** Object-centric, single-subject training compositions constrain complex multi-object and relational control (Appendix H).
- **Author-acknowledged comparison limits:** Generation tables are system-level comparisons. High-resolution reconstruction also includes a LlamaGen baseline evaluated after resizing to lower resolution (Tables 2, 6–7).
- **Analyst inference—depth is confounded with capacity:** The deeper-tree reconstruction experiment increases token budget alongside depth, so it does not isolate hierarchy depth (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—control evidence is narrow:** Localization uses automated proxies; SAM 2 is prompted by the detected box, so box and mask evidence are not independent. No human localization assessment or uncertainty intervals are reported (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency is conditional:** Adaptive encoding adds probing cost, and maximal tree expansion does not yield the best generation quality. Token savings alone therefore do not establish an overall compute advantage (Figure 4, Appendix E).

## Open research questions
- **Can topology control compose multiple subjects and relations?** Current evidence covers single target half-planes and ImageNet compositions. A next experiment could train on scene-level data and evaluate multiple boxes plus relational constraints (Appendices F, H).
- **Does deeper hierarchy help at a fixed token budget?** Depth and budget rise together in the reported experiment. Compare two- and three-level tokenizers at matched token counts, model capacity, and training compute (§4.1).
- **Can topology be selected automatically during generation?** The current generator requires a tree beforehand. Compare learned topology prediction with random and prescribed trees at matched budgets and sampling settings (§3.4).
- **When do token savings repay probing costs?** The paper measures preprocessing and sampling separately. Measure complete training-and-generation workloads across dataset reuse rates, token budgets, and matched output quality (Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and results; broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes repeated-run uncertainty, human layout assessments, verified repository/checkpoint contents, explicit numerical loss weights for the default tokenizer, total training compute, and complete sampling settings tying each 256×256 generation-table result to its topology expansion probability and CFG configuration.
