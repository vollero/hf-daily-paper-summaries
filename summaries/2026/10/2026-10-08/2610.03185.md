# Gains and Collapse in On-Policy Distillation:A Reinforcement Learning Perspective

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.03185) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03185)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the tested mathematics settings, on-policy distillation improves sampling of existing student solutions but can amplify teacher-favored repetition, with loss masking and SFT initialization mitigating the observed collapse.

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) supervises student-generated responses using teacher token probabilities. It can improve accuracy yet also produce excessive length and repetition. The authors investigate what OPD learns and why successful optimization can yield poor generations, arguing that teacher feedback on student rollouts deserves separate scrutiny from the teacher’s own generation quality (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Optimization interpretation:** The authors express conditional reverse-KL minimization at fixed student-generated prefixes as teacher-log-probability reward maximization with entropy regularization. The implemented update uses stop-gradient token advantages, PPO-style clipping, and reference-KL regularization (§2, Eqs. 1–4).
- **Capability diagnosis:** Increasing-budget pass@k evaluation and targeted manual audits suggest that successful OPD makes existing solutions easier to sample without expanding the observed solvable set in the tested settings (§3).
- **Collapse diagnosis:** Training dynamics, likelihood shifts, and advantage-ranked responses support the authors’ interpretation of collapse as reward hacking: students increasingly favor teacher-preferred responses while generation quality deteriorates (§§4–5.1).
- **Diagnostic interventions:** Masking truncated or failed responses and loading an existing SFT checkpoint mitigate collapse while retaining the same teacher. These are tests of the proposed explanation, rather than claimed general-purpose solutions (§5.2; Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy gains shrink with sampling budget | JustRL and DeepScaleR teachers supervise DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B; gains are largest at small k and disappear at the largest tested budgets: 256 on AIME24–26 and 1,024 on AMC23. | §3; Figure 2 |
| No validated OPD-only problem survives the audit | Across 90 AIME24–26 problems, the pooled initial-student set covers 73; trained students cover 65 with JustRL and 63 with DeepScaleR. Initial-student sampling is conditionally extended from 256 to 1,024 responses, with targeted manual review. | Figure 3; Tables 6–7; Appendix A.3 |
| Existing solutions become easier to sample | Among initially solvable problems, per-problem success rates improve on 90.6% with JustRL and 84.4% with DeepScaleR. The reported average mean@256 increases are 22.6% and 14.7%; the text does not explicitly distinguish relative increases from percentage-point changes. | §3; Figure 4 |
| Collapse coexists with improved answer accuracy | Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B-Base on AIME24–26: endpoint accuracy rises from 0.2% to 5.4%, while mean length reaches 8.16k tokens, truncation reaches 99.4%, and severe repetition reaches 38.0%. The teacher has 2.1% truncation and 0% repetition. | Table 2; routine endpoint protocol in Appendix A.3 |
| Teacher-preference fitting strengthens during collapse | On fixed initial-student responses, the NLL selection gap between the teacher’s least- and most-preferred deciles grows to approximately 26 in the collapsed Qwen setting, versus approximately 0.07 with JustRL. Higher-advantage Qwen responses are longer and more repetitive before training. | §4.2; Figures 6–7 |
| Masking improves benchmark-average accuracy | Four-benchmark mean@4 accuracy rises from 10.16% to 13.24% with the 4B teacher, 10.41% to 10.92% with 8B, and 10.49% to 13.07% with 30B-A3B. Gains are not uniform across benchmarks. | Table 3; §5.2; Appendix A.3 |
| SFT initialization avoids the observed failure mode | With the 4B teacher, the released SFT initialization reaches 16.38% four-benchmark mean@4 accuracy, versus 10.16% for Base initialization; it also begins from a stronger student. | Figure 8; Table 3; Appendix F |

The evidence supports probability redistribution and teacher-feedback exploitation in these particular runs. It does not prove that OPD can never expand capability, establish equality of model supports, or isolate teacher bias as the sole possible cause of collapse. The audited coverage totals are explicitly unequal-budget measurements; their difference overstates the coverage gap (Appendix A.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Computational constraints restrict experiments to small models and mathematics; larger-model and cross-domain generalization remain untested. The interventions target the observed length and repetition failures (Limitations, p. 10).
- **Author-stated audit asymmetry:** Extra sampling and manual review target OPD-only candidates, and initial-student recoveries are pooled across teacher settings. This favors eliminating OPD-only cases and prevents interpreting the totals as a symmetric comparison (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—intervention attribution:** Warmup changes both initialization and the fixed reference, and starts with higher accuracy. Its final advantage does not isolate an OPD-specific benefit from the stronger starting checkpoint (Appendix F).
- **Measurement limits:** Integer-answer verification can accept invalid derivations; targeted review catches examples, but a comprehensive derivation audit is not reported. Min-10NN measures character similarity, not semantic diversity or quality (Appendices A.3, B–C).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text gives substantial training details but no repeated-run uncertainty estimates or explicit severe-repetition detector definition. The abstract announces code availability, while the reproducibility statement describes evaluation and analysis scripts as a planned release.

## Open research questions
1. **Does capability expansion appear under a symmetric audit?** The current search disproportionately strengthens initial-student coverage. A next experiment could use equal expanded budgets and blinded derivation review for both students across alternative decoding settings.
2. **How broadly does feedback bias predict collapse?** Evidence is restricted to the studied model pairs and mathematical tasks. Test pretraining advantage–quality alignment across larger students and other domains, then compare it with subsequent collapse.
3. **Which part of masking causes recovery?** Masked responses retain a role in normalization but lose policy, reference-KL, and entropy gradients (Appendix F). Ablate these components separately and compare cap-based masking with direct repetition filtering.
4. **How much does OPD add beyond SFT initialization?** A standalone SFT endpoint comparison is not reported in Table 3. Evaluate the same SFT checkpoint before and after OPD, including matched-reference controls and repeated runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the descriptive results and proposed mechanism, but finite asymmetric coverage measurement and limited uncertainty reporting constrain stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, the severe-repetition detection rule, some teacher-scoring and Qwen evaluation-temperature details, a standalone SFT benchmark result, and confirmation of which announced scripts and checkpoints were actually released.
