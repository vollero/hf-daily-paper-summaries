# On-Policy Distillation with Negative-Policy Rollouts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07874) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07874)


## One-sentence takeaway
NP-OPD improves reported language-model reasoning results by applying unchanged teacher supervision to weaker-model rollouts, although gains depend on the rollout mixture and are not universal across domains (§§3–4; Appendix C.2).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) supervises a student’s own generations using a stronger teacher’s token probabilities. The authors argue that limited teacher–student distributional overlap can weaken this supervision, while existing improvements mainly change rewards or teacher configurations. They propose supplying a weaker policy’s outputs as an explicit negative reference, without changing the distillation reward (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Rollout-stage modification:** NP-OPD draws a proportion α of trajectories from a lower-capability negative policy and the remainder from the student. Both receive the same teacher–student token-level signal. At α=1, training uses only negative-policy trajectories (§3.1).
- **Conditional mechanism:** Under the assumption \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_n\|\pi^*)>D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_n\|\pi_\theta)\), negative-policy tokens have negative expected standard-OPD reward. Teacher-preferred tokens can still be reinforced; suppression is not unconditional (§3.2, Eqs. 11–12).
- **Empirical and diagnostic contributions:** Experiments combine the rollout modification with OPD, ExOPD, and OPD 2. The authors introduce negative-policy suppression precision (NSP) and negative-policy overlap reduction (NOR) to characterize updates, and demonstrate training-time savings from reusable rollouts (§4; Appendix C).

The evidence supports improvements across several tested configurations and a teacher-relative change in token probabilities. It does not establish universal gains, causal mediation by NSP/NOR, or end-to-end compute savings including rollout preparation. NOR explicitly measures relative top-token shifts at disagreement positions, not absolute distributional overlap (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated qualifications:** Gemma OPD training was less stable and required KL regularization; some Qwen non-thinking rollouts required short-answer filtering. These conditions matter when reproducing comparisons (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—selection risk:** Representative α values were chosen using domain improvements and overall benchmark averages. A separate validation-based selection procedure is not described, limiting interpretation as performance from a prespecified configuration (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—theory scope:** Weaker task performance does not itself establish the required KL ordering. Extension of the negative-expected-reward argument to ExOPD and OPD 2 is presented as an expectation, rather than derived equivalently (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and scope:** Accuracy tables lack training-seed variability or significance estimates. NSP repetitions concern analysis-trajectory sampling, not independent training runs. Evaluations cover two model families and reasoning domains, leaving broader language capabilities unestablished (Appendices C.1–C.4).
- **Source ambiguity:** Main-text figure references conflict with supplied captions. Appendix D introduces a persona prompt but omits its actual text. Code and rollout release availability are not established.

## Open research questions

1. **What predicts a useful negative policy?** Capability-based selection does not verify the KL assumption (§3.2). Compare same-family and cross-family candidates while measuring reward signs and KL ordering on shared prefixes.
2. **Can α be selected without benchmark-result selection?** Optimal mixtures vary by domain (Appendix C.2). Select α on separate validation prompts, then evaluate once on held-out benchmarks.
3. **Does targeted suppression cause the accuracy gains?** NSP–accuracy correlation and NOR shifts support association (§4.3). Hold rollout token budgets and lengths constant while varying exposure to teacher-disfavored tokens.
4. **What are the full efficiency and reuse tradeoffs?** Timing excludes pre-generation (§4.2). Measure preparation, storage, teacher scoring, and training costs across different rollout-reuse schedules.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method and benchmark results are well specified, but mechanism and generalization claims remain conditional. Material missing information includes independent training-run uncertainty, validation-based α selection, complete pre-generation costs, the exact persona prompt, the ExOPD extrapolation setting, and confirmed availability of promised code and rollouts.
