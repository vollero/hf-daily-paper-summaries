# OmniShowcase: Omnidirectional Subject-Consistent Video Generation from Multi-View Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35684) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35684)


## One-sentence takeaway
OmniShowcase combines mesh-based training videos with reconstructed surface-normal conditioning to improve subject identity and 3D consistency across camera viewpoints, although the supplied text omits the numerical tables needed to quantify those gains (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Subject-to-video models can change an object’s shape or appearance when the camera reveals unseen surfaces. Multiple reference images reduce ambiguity, but the authors argue that existing methods neither explicitly connect those views through geometry nor guarantee geometrically consistent training videos (§1).

The target is prompt-guided video generation that preserves a specific subject across substantial viewpoint changes, including full orbits.

## Approach and contributions
- **Automated training-data pipeline:** Render references and camera trajectories from the same mesh, then generate backgrounds through video inpainting while preserving rendered subject pixels. Automated gates filter assets, composited videos, and reconstructions (§3.2).
- **Geometry-aware conditioning:** Reconstruct a subject from four reference views and render a 25-frame surface-normal turntable. Reference images, normals, and target latents occupy separate temporal spans in a LoRA-tuned video diffusion transformer. This avoids requiring correspondence between normal frames and the prompted camera path (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Dataset and benchmark:** Showcase-4K contains 4,000 videos covering 2,593 assets and 620 categories; its trajectory vocabulary has 88 configurations. ShowcaseBench provides held-out subjects with source geometry and camera parameters. Public release of data and synthesis code is promised, rather than verified by the supplied text (§1; Appendices G.1–G.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Backgrounds can appear synthetic because compositing does not establish consistent light transport between subject and scene (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Non-rigid references are unsupported. A single reconstructed shape can average incompatible poses, and motion prompts can produce tangled transitions (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Identity scores select the best generated match for each reference, so they do not establish identity preservation in every frame (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** GT-CD measures geometry after separate centering, scale normalization, and rigid alignment; it does not establish absolute scale or camera-placement accuracy. It also depends on geometry reconstructed from generated frames (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative evaluation emphasizes full-orbit trajectories; broader camera motions receive qualitative demonstrations rather than clearly reported separate quantitative results (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Evidence gap:** The supplied conditioning ablations hold training data fixed, so they do not isolate the downstream benefit of the synthesis pipeline itself (§4.3).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is conditioning to reconstruction errors?** Training reconstructions are filtered, while the reconstruction gate is absent at inference (Appendix I.1). Next experiment: stratify results by reconstruction quality and perturb the normal condition.
- **Can geometry conditioning support deformation?** Static reconstruction fails on inconsistent poses (Appendix H). Next experiment: compare static normals with time-varying geometry conditions on articulated subjects.
- **How much improvement comes from training data versus conditioning?** The presented conditioning ablations use fixed data (§4.3). Next experiment: cross mesh-based versus generated training videos with geometry conditioning enabled versus disabled.
- **Does camera control generalize beyond orbit benchmarks?** Extended trajectories are shown qualitatively (Appendix A). Next experiment: evaluate identity, geometry, and trajectory accuracy separately across the full motion vocabulary.
