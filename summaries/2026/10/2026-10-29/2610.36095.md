# Instruction Capacity: Behavior and Degradation of Language Models under Controlled Load

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36095) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36095)


## One-sentence takeaway
A controlled word-substitution test yields a model-derived instruction capacity of roughly 37–38 rules for GPT-4o mini at 95% correct replacements under reference conditions, falling to roughly 18–19 at full replacement density (§5.5; Table 6).

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses how to measure the load at which language models stop reliably following simultaneous instructions. The author argues that existing evaluations confound instruction count with semantic difficulty, interactions among constraints, or evaluator error, and lack a comparable capacity expressed in load units with uncertainty (§§1–2).

The proposed solution uses independent substitution rules as conventional load units. Its practical motivation is to support model comparison and eventually calibrate a measure of arbitrary prompts’ instruction load; that latter application remains proposed (§6.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled evaluation instrument:** Generate meaningless streams of single-token words and disjoint replacement pairs. Three random seeds determine rule selection, text construction, and rule order. A deterministic reference answer supports scoring each required replacement and distinguishing incorrect substitutions, local structural damage, and answer collapse (§3).
- **Statistical measurement procedure:** Fit a logistic mixed model with request-level random effects, first estimating binned profiles and then smooth laws. The final VS-PMR model combines text length and answer position in a joint grid, alongside replacement density, rule count, and rule position. A shift correction defines a reference level independent of arbitrary bin coding (§§4–5.3).
- **Capacity measures and model characterization:** Derive instruction, fatigue, and full-density instruction capacities with delta-method uncertainty. Importantly, the final instruction-capacity definition accumulates costs from both table size and each rule’s position; it is a constructed summary of fitted effects (§5.5).
- **Reported public assets:** The author reports releasing task-generation and analysis code, vocabulary, prompts, answers, and measurement records. The adaptive procedure that selected experimental points remains closed (§8).

## Evidence and main results
All ± values below are reported half-widths of 95% confidence intervals.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial controlled measurement on one model | GPT-4o-mini-2024-07-18, temperature zero: 2,958 requests, 642,430 replacement trials; texts of 20–10,240 words and tasks with 1–256 rules. | §§5.1, pp. 27–28 |
| Reference instruction capacity near 37–38 rules | At quality level 0.95: **36.58 ± 5.41** rules from bins; **38.06 ± 6.13** from smooth laws. Reference conditions use short-text/early-answer and sparse-replacement limits; cumulative cost includes table size and rule positions. | §5.5.1; Table 6 |
| Full density substantially lowers capacity | At density 1 and quality level 0.95: **18.69 ± 3.62** rules from bins; **17.55 ± 4.04** from laws, with other axes at the reference point. | §5.5.3; Table 6 |
| Length and answer position interact | In the fitted grid law, long-text plateau height decreases **1.60 ± 0.43 logits per length doubling**; deep-answer contribution decreases **2.67 ± 0.31 logits per position doubling**. | §5.3.1; Table 4 |
| Dense replacements impose a large fitted penalty | Full density reduces the correct-replacement logit by **9.35 ± 1.01** relative to the sparse-density asymptote, holding other modeled contributions fixed. | §5.3.2, p. 39 |
| Fatigue capacity includes an extrapolated result | From the grid’s positional asymptote: **3,277 ± 571 words** at quality 0.95; **11,890 ± 1,839 words** at quality 0.5, explicitly beyond the measured text-length range. | §5.5.2; Table 6 |

The evidence supports regular, protocol-specific degradation patterns and derived capacity estimates for this model. Agreement between bin-based and smooth-law capacities supports the chosen approximations within their reported uncertainty; it is not independent validation of the capacity construct. These results do not establish equivalent limits for natural-language requirements or other models.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only GPT-4o mini is characterized; the author explicitly cautions that other models may exhibit different laws or significant axes (§6.1).
- **Unvalidated practical transfer:** Converting arbitrary prompt interference into equivalent replacement rules assumes that both affect replacement logits comparably. The author says this requires experimental validation (§6.2).
- **Model-dependent reference conditions:** Capacity uses asymptotic reference levels, including vanishing replacement density and mathematical continuation of answer position. Some transition-width parameters are not identified (§§5.3.1–5.3.2; Tables 4–5).
- **Partial reproducibility:** Published records reportedly permit recomputation, but the closed adaptive planner prevents reproducing point selection from its disclosed rules (§8).
- **Analyst inference—interpretation of capacity:** The final cumulative rule-cost definition differs from a directly observed maximum rule count achieving a given accuracy. “Guaranteed” capacity is a bound over replacement density under specified reference conditions, not a guarantee of flawless answers (§§5.5.1, 5.5.3).
- **Analyst inference—scoring scope:** Required substitutions are the scored trials; the validator can also remove answer framing. This metric therefore does not establish compliance with every formatting and unchanged-background requirement (§3.4).
- **Incomplete source:** A substantial middle portion was truncated, limiting assessment of rule-position laws, collapse analysis, and validation details.

## Open research questions
1. **Do the laws and capacity rankings generalize across models?** Only one model is measured. Repeat identical seeded tasks across several models and test fitted laws on held-out load points (§6.1).
2. **Does substitution capacity predict practical instruction following?** Prompt weighing is unvalidated. Add controlled natural-language tasks and compare observed interference with predicted equivalent-rule costs (§6.2).
3. **How stable are estimates under experimental design changes?** Point selection is adaptive and undisclosed. Compare the published estimates with independently sampled grids and fresh random seeds (§§4, 8).
4. **What causes positional degradation?** Distance from the rule list is offered as a hypothesis, not an established mechanism. Move or repeat the rule list while controlling text length, density, and answer position (§5.3.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the instrument design and reported capacity estimates, but coverage is incomplete because the source was truncated.

Material gaps include detailed portions of §§5.3–5.4, numerical held-out prediction results not visible in the excerpt, the adaptive planner’s selection rules, independent replications, cross-model measurements, and experimental validation of arbitrary-prompt load conversion. Repository availability and reproducibility claims were not independently checked.
