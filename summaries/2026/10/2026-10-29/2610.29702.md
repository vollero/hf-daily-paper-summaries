# CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29702) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29702)


## One-sentence takeaway
CausGround supplies frozen multimodal planners with observation-grounded action preconditions and effects, with authors reporting improved simulated planning and interaction reasoning, alongside qualitative robot demonstrations (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Embodied planners must determine whether actions are executable and anticipate their effects; missing prerequisites can cause repeated failures over long tasks. The authors identify a gap between costly environment-specific parameter training and unstructured experience memories that are difficult to reuse or ground in visual observations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared state representation:** Object attribute–value pairs and typed inter-object relations represent observations, action preconditions, and effects in the same schema (§3.1).
- **Action-centered knowledge construction:** Interaction outcomes identify candidate preconditions, while successful state transitions identify effects. Rules are organized into generic and instance-specific knowledge and connected through effect–precondition dependencies. VirtualHome uses controlled interventions; RoboCasa and SWITCH use consistency across recorded trajectories (§3.2; Appendix C.3.1).
- **Task-grounded planning:** Backward retrieval from interpreted goals combines with semantic retrieval. The retrieved graph remains fixed during an episode, while current visual states repeatedly ground its conditions for a frozen planner (§3.3; Appendix C.3.2).
- **Evaluation adaptations:** The authors construct a repaired-image VirtualHome planning suite and adapt RoboCasa trajectories into high-level planning interfaces; they also evaluate selected SWITCH subtasks and demonstrate robot execution (Appendices A, E).

The evidence supports improved high-level planning under the authors’ adapted protocols. It does not establish reliable low-level robot control, broad real-world robustness, or formally identified causal mechanisms in trajectory-only settings. Exact per-backbone comparisons against SelfReVision and LookPlanGraph cannot be checked from the missing table bodies.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no explicit limitations section.

- **Analyst inference—causal identification:** Consistency among successful recorded executions does not independently establish that a factor is necessary. This affects RoboCasa and SWITCH, unlike VirtualHome’s controlled intervention procedure (Appendix C.3.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation selection:** Excluding RoboCasa cases whenever any method encounters an unsupported trajectory branch may restrict the evaluated population. Retained counts and exclusion rates are unavailable (Appendix A.2.3).
- **Analyst inference—visual benchmark validity:** VirtualHome images are edited using simulator transitions and manually validated. Shared observations support matched comparisons, but robustness to native rendering or editing artifacts is not established (Appendix A.1.3).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** Robot evidence consists of two illustrated tasks using an existing controller; repeated-trial success and recovery performance are unreported (Appendix E).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §4.5 states six evaluated models, while Appendix C.1 names three main backbones plus four additional models; the exact averaging membership is unclear.

## Open research questions
- **How reliable are trajectory-derived preconditions?** Necessity is not intervention-tested for RoboCasa or SWITCH. A next experiment could violate retained factors individually and measure rule precision and planning effects.
- **How much does RoboCasa branch filtering affect results?** Unsupported branches remove cases from evaluation. Re-evaluate with an executor supporting alternative valid actions and report excluded-case outcomes.
- **How robust is grounding to observation errors?** State extraction is central, but its error sensitivity is unquantified. Test controlled occlusion and attribute/relation corruption while measuring task success.
- **Does robot performance persist across repeated trials?** The demonstrations lack reliability estimates. Repeat both tasks under varied placements and injected execution failures, recording completion and recovery rates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and aggregate claims are clear, but the supplied text lacks numerical table bodies, uncertainty estimates, repeated-run details, retained RoboCasa sample counts, actual prompt templates, library/test separation details, construction and inference costs, quantitative robot results, and confirmed code/data release availability.
