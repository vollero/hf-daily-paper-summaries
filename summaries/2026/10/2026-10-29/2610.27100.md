# LOB-Impact Benchmark: Emergent Market Impact in Generative Limit Order Book Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27100) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27100)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across 25 neural model–stock pairs, injected metaorders produce identifiable responses beyond book mechanics, but impact is steeper than the square-root reference and never passes the benchmark’s relaxation test (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Generative limit order book (LOB) simulators may reproduce historical message distributions while responding incorrectly to a trading agent’s orders. Such errors could distort execution policies learned inside them. The authors identify a gap in existing evaluation: sustained metaorder responses need testing separately from single-event responses, with controls distinguishing mechanical liquidity consumption from the generator’s learned reaction (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Three-phase intervention benchmark:** measure free-generation dynamics, inject metaorders to estimate impact, then stop execution to measure relaxation. Stock-specific calibration and evaluation rules are fixed before testing models (§2.1).
- **Attribution through paired controls:** visible and blind injections share windows and seeds; blind orders execute in the simulator but are hidden from model inputs. Buy/sell antisymmetrisation removes direction-independent drift (§2.1).
- **Identification before estimation:** test whether an impact law exists before reporting its exponent and amplitude, using replay controls, planted laws, and day-clustered uncertainty estimates (§2.1).
- **Evaluation and reusable implementation:** compare five neural state-space generators with eight non-neural baselines across five stocks. The authors report an open-source package containing calibration, estimators, controls, and reporting rules (§§2.2, 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Neural impact is identifiable and exceeds mechanics alone | All 25 neural model–stock pairs pass existence and attribution tests; visible peaks exceed blind floors by 3.4–38×. Campaigns use 1,024 rollouts per direction per phase over 20 trading days. | §2.1; Table 3 |
| Impact is steeper than the square-root reference | For 100-child build-ups at target participation of 10%, neural δ spans 0.63–1.21. All 95% day-clustered bootstrap intervals exclude 0.5; interval half-widths span 0.04–0.14. | §§2.1, 3.4; Table 3 |
| AMD impact amplitude is excessive relative to the cited empirical reference | Neural Y spans 2.24–8.57 versus the paper’s literature reference of approximately 0.5; corresponding 100-child price moves are reported as 17–70 bps versus at most 11 bps among baselines. | §3.2; Table 3 |
| No neural model passes credible relaxation | Following 10 children and 100 silent blocks, all neural cells fail C5. AMD end-over-peak spans 0.88–1.36; the apparent decline for GDN is confounded by −127 ticks of free drift. | §§2.1, 3.3; Table 3 |
| Identification controls support the estimator | Historic replay yields no identified law on any stock. In 500 planted-null trials, no false law is identified; planted exponents from 0.5–1.0 show downward bias of at most 0.11 at neural amplitudes. | §§2.1, 3.4 |
| The out-of-distribution stock has especially large amplitudes | On EA, the sole stock outside the training corpus, neural Y spans 12.23–49.52; impact remains identifiable. | §3.4; Table 3 |

These results establish intervention-sensitive neural responses and failures under the specified benchmark. They do not establish realistic execution-agent performance, which is not tested. Square-root and amplitude targets come from cited empirical literature rather than directly observed historical counterfactuals for these injected orders. Declining prices alone do not establish resilience when free drift is substantial.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** one month, one exchange, five stocks, one participation rate, one child-size rule, aggressive orders, and a restricted execution schedule. Extreme-volume behavior is untested, and estimated exponents depend on the operating point (§4).
- **Author-stated temporal uncertainty:** an atypical month could shift exponents beyond reported interval half-widths; additional rollouts cannot replace independent trading days (§§2.1, 4).
- **Unproven mechanism:** limited memory is the authors’ explanation for missing replenishment. Longer-context variants also fail, but their windows remain shorter than metaorders; this does not isolate context length causally (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** the larger EA amplitudes cannot establish a general out-of-distribution effect from one held-out stock (§3.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 1’s caption broadly describes neural measurements as in-distribution, whereas Table 3 and §3.4 explicitly identify EA as out-of-distribution. The latter distinction is used here.
- **Reference limitation:** AMD receives detailed plots; other stocks receive tabulated summaries. Some relaxation cells are marked duration-mixed (§§2.1, 4; Table 3).

## Open research questions
- **Does longer memory restore resilience?** The proposed explanation remains untested at metaorder-scale context lengths. Compare otherwise matched models with progressively longer contexts using all three phases (§4).
- **How stable are impact laws across execution settings?** Only one operating point is evaluated. Sweep participation, child size, and aggressive versus passive execution while retaining blind controls (§4).
- **Do findings persist across regimes and unseen stocks?** Evaluation covers one month and one out-of-distribution stock. Repeat across months and a larger held-out stock panel (§§3.4, 4).
- **Do benchmark failures predict agent failures?** Execution consequences are argued rather than measured. Train matched execution agents in simulators with differing impact fidelity and evaluate them in a common controlled environment (§4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark outcomes; **low** for the proposed memory-based causal explanation.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes Table 2’s stock-calibration rows, detailed neural training and checkpoint-reproduction settings, relaxation uncertainty intervals, multi-month evaluations, and downstream execution-agent experiments. The repository release is reported by the authors but was not independently inspected.
