# PreView: Enhancing GUI Grounding with Preprocessed Views and Cues

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33645) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33645)


## One-sentence takeaway
PreView trains an auxiliary screenshot preprocessor to improve frozen GUI grounders, reporting average accuracy gains of 1.5–13.9 percentage points across five benchmarks, while sometimes harming predictions through lost context or misleading cues (§5.1; Limitations).

## Problem and motivation
GUI grounding maps an instruction to a clickable screen location. Small controls in dense screenshots require both precise localization and understanding of an element’s identity, function, and relationships. The authors argue that zooming improves visibility but can remove context or leave target ambiguity; they investigate jointly preparing focused views and semantic cues for an existing grounder (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Learned input preparation:** An auxiliary Qwen3.5-0.8B model is jointly trained for center prediction and candidate verification with description generation, using GroundCUA screenshots and annotations. The downstream grounder remains frozen (§§3.1, 4.1, 4.4).
- **Complementary views and cues:** Initial center prediction followed by refinement selects a crop retaining 12.5% of screenshot area, enlarged 2× per dimension. A red-circle marker and location hint provide spatial guidance; an accepted candidate description supplies semantic guidance (§3.2).
- **Cue routing:** Center consistency determines whether cues are supplied. Rejection removes the description; failed consistency removes both cue types while retaining the crop. One downstream grounding call produces a click mapped back to the original screenshot (§3.2).
- **Empirical analysis:** Experiments separate contributions from views, cues, refinement, and visual budgets, and document failures from excluded targets and incorrect guidance (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

These reported results support input preparation as a useful intervention for the tested frozen grounders, with strongly task-dependent benefits. They do not establish universal improvement, lower total inference cost, or better interactive task completion. Most table bodies and figure data are absent from the supplied text, so the narrative results cannot be fully checked against their underlying tables.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Cropping can exclude targets or necessary context; incorrect cues can redirect otherwise correct predictions. Consistency filtering retains the same crop and therefore cannot repair crop-induced failures (§5.5; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Desktop-only training limits mobile generalization; offline evaluation does not establish multi-step reliability. Auxiliary inference adds cost and latency, and each configuration has only one reported run (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** One downstream call is insufficient evidence of efficiency because end-to-end latency and compute measurements are unavailable in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons require caution: Full scores mix reproduced evaluations and model-card results (§4.3). Missing table bodies prevent auditing the broader baseline comparisons.
- **Evidence ambiguity:** §5.3 describes a 2.5-point SSPro cue gain for 2B, while its stated endpoints, 56.9% and 59.3%, imply 2.4 points. The supplied text does not resolve this discrepancy.

## Open research questions
1. **Can coverage-aware cropping prevent information loss?** Fixed crops sometimes exclude the target (§5.5). Compare fixed cropping with adaptive crop sizes and full-screen fallback, measuring target containment and final accuracy.
2. **Can cue reliability be predicted more effectively?** Center consistency still admits harmful cues (§5.5). Compare consistency routing with a learned reliability gate, reporting recovery and regression separately.
3. **Do gains survive interactive execution at acceptable cost?** Evaluation isolates single-step grounding and lacks measured latency (Limitations). Test complete agent tasks while recording completion rate, erroneous actions, and end-to-end latency.
4. **Does mobile supervision address platform regressions?** Desktop-trained preprocessing regresses on both iOS subsets for 2B (§5.5). Compare desktop-only and mixed-platform auxiliary training on held-out mobile interfaces.
