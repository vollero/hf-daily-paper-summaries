# RealCLI: Toward a Unified Evaluation of Human-Agent Collaboration for CLI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22507) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22507)


## One-sentence takeaway
RealCLI pairs autonomous and scripted-user CLI evaluation, finding that verified task completion frequently coexists with failures in authorization, constraint retention, and risk communication (§§2, 4.5).

## Problem and motivation
CLI benchmarks commonly provide fixed specifications and judge final environment states, leaving runtime coordination poorly measured. The authors argue that practical workflows also require agents to absorb evolving requirements, accept corrections, communicate risks, and preserve user control. Their identified gap is a unified evaluation of task outcomes and collaboration quality on matched tasks and environments (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired benchmark:** RealCLI contains 101 tasks across 10 categories and 52 subdomains. EXEC supplies complete specifications upfront; COLLAB progressively supplies information and scripted interventions. Both share goals, environments, and outcome verifiers (§§2.1, 3).
- **Collaboration framework:** Five dimensions cover understandability, steerability, observation and recovery, trust calibration, and safety boundary management, refined into 22 subdimensions. The main text specifies 51 scenario templates plus six trajectory checks; Appendix B.1 inconsistently reports 57 scenarios plus six checks (§§1–2.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Evidence-based scoring:** Binary checklists combine transcripts and environment evidence, with environment evidence taking precedence. Scenario pass rates are thresholded at 75%, then averaged through subdimensions and dimensions. The joint score is \(J_{\mathrm{COLLAB}}=R_{\mathrm{COLLAB}}S_{\mathrm{COLLAB}}\) (§2.4; Appendix B.3).
- **Evaluation infrastructure and analysis:** Log-derived task construction, containerized execution, scripted simulation, and separate simulator audits support evaluation of 33 model–scaffold configurations (§§1, 3; Appendix D).

The reported evidence supports separating artifact correctness from collaboration behavior within this benchmark. It does not establish equivalent failure rates with real users or isolate why interaction helps some configurations and harms others. The joint score multiplies aggregate quantities; it is not the observed fraction of trajectories that jointly satisfy every task and collaboration requirement (§2.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no dedicated author limitations discussion. The following are **analyst inferences grounded in the described protocol**:

- **Human validity remains untested:** GLM-5.3 simulates users, and Claude-Opus-4.8 judges collaboration. No real-user comparison or human–judge agreement study is reported (Appendix D).
- **Mode differences complicate attribution:** COLLAB combines withheld specifications, probes, and interaction, while allowing ten hours versus six for EXEC. These results do not isolate the effect of user participation alone (§§2.3, 4.1).
- **Scores depend on rubric choices and exposure:** All-or-nothing checks, the 75% scenario threshold, equal weighting, and exclusion of untriggered scenarios can affect comparisons. No sensitivity analysis is supplied (§2.4; Appendix B.3).
- **Scope is curated:** Tasks are synthesized from logs, filtered for difficulty, and selected through pilots and expert review; representativeness of ordinary CLI workloads is not established (§3).
- **Reporting ambiguities limit verification:** Headline scores and scenario counts conflict. Tables 3–5 lack numerical bodies, and “held-out split” captions are not reconciled with evaluation on all tasks (§4.1).
- **Causal explanations remain hypotheses:** Claims attributing dimension strengths to RLHF or improvements to mitigation of long-context degradation lack controlled supporting experiments in the supplied text (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does scripted-user performance predict human collaboration quality?** No real-user validation is reported. Run matched tasks with human users and compare task outcomes, checklist judgments, and user assessments.
2. **Which interaction component causes gains or regressions?** Progressive disclosure and probes are combined. Ablate disclosure, probes, and user participation separately under matched budgets.
3. **Can explicit constraint tracking improve delivered artifacts?** The paper documents dialogue–artifact mismatches without testing a remedy. Compare baseline agents with persistent constraint records and final artifact checks.
4. **Are rankings robust to scoring and judging choices?** Threshold and judge sensitivity are unreported. Recompute scores across thresholds and obtain blinded human judgments on a stratified trajectory sample.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The protocol and several findings are well described, but material gaps prevent full verification:

- Complete numerical result tables and uncertainty estimates.
- Definition of the headline “average score” and reconciliation of conflicting values.
- Definitive scenario count and held-out split definition.
- Human validation of simulator realism and judge reliability.
- Exact handling of contaminated runs in final aggregation.
- Budget-matched ablations and scoring-sensitivity results.
