# TauIndianBankBench: Procedure-Aware, Action-Oriented Evaluation of Tool-Using Conversational Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30161) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30161)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across six models on synthetic Indian banking tasks, checking required tool calls and unrequested writes catches procedural violations that final-state scoring misses in 6.5–12.0% of sessions (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Correct final records do not establish that a banking assistant followed required procedures: looking up a closure penalty or checking card status can leave no database trace. The authors identify a gap in evaluating customer servicing under Indian banking rules, beyond financial knowledge, arithmetic, or tool selection (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Banking benchmark:** A synthetic bank, written policy, searchable knowledge base, servicing tools, and 1,000 tasks across sequencing, edge-case, rare-tool, and control categories (§3.1).
- **Combined scoring:** Deterministic final-state comparison against gold-chain replay, plus an action check requiring specified calls—including reads—and rejecting unmatched writes, even when later reversed. Required-call matching builds on τ²-bench; policy-mandated reads and write purity are the stated additions (§3.2).
- **Evaluation and release:** Reference results for six open-weight models, scorer audits, scripted baselines, and released code, tasks, and adaptation splits (§§3–5).

The action check matches call presence rather than order or multiplicity; ordering is encouraged through tool dependencies and the customer’s staged requests (§3.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Required reads are widespread | Of 1,000 released tasks, 962 require a read and 114 require no write. | §1 |
| Combined scores vary substantially | One attempt per base-split task, same MiniMax-M2.7 customer: Qwen3.8-27B 0.849; Gemma 4 31B 0.776; 26B-A4B 0.714; 12B 0.699; E4B 0.607; E2B 0.479. | §4; Table 1 |
| Final-state scoring misses violations | Re-scoring the same sessions using final records alone increases pass rates by a reported 6.5–12.0 percentage points across models. | §5.2; Table 2 |
| Both checks catch distinct failures | For Qwen, 71 sessions pass final-state checking but fail action checking; 13 show the reverse. For E2B, the reported counts are 118 and 180. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| Rare-tool tasks remain difficult | Combined pass rates range from 0.388 for E2B to 0.757 for Qwen; this is every model’s lowest-scoring category. | Table 1; §5.4 |
| Scorer implementation is consistent | Independent implementation agrees on all 600 audited sessions and 705 paired calls; packaged scorer reproduces scores on 5,000 recorded sessions. | §3.2; Table 8 |
| Aggregate stability hides task variation | Across two attempts for five Gemma models, overall pass rates change by at most 2.7 points, while roughly 14–26% of tasks change outcome. | §5.8; Table 14 |

The paired re-scoring directly supports the claim that final records alone miss required actions. It also supports retaining final-state checks because required-call presence can coexist with incorrect records. These results establish behavior within this environment, not deployment readiness or complete procedural correctness.

There is a small internal inconsistency: E2B’s displayed final-state-only and combined rates differ by 119 sessions, while Table 2 reports 118 procedural violations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Results depend on one customer simulator whose error rate was not measured. Synthetic edge-case frequencies need not reflect bank traffic; coverage is English-only; live multi-turn evaluation is costly (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—procedure coverage:** Order-insensitive matching and allowing one call to satisfy repeated gold calls leave temporal requirements incompletely checked. Appendix C explicitly permits one balance lookup to satisfy both the initial and final lookup (§3.2; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—communication:** Required calls do not prove that their results were correctly explained. Communication and judge assertions do not affect the released score (Table 8).
- **Analyst inference—external validity:** Gold-chain replay validates task executability, but the text does not report independent banking-expert validation of policy completeness or task realism (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—control interpretation:** The absence of a model-size trend on simple controls does not by itself exclude general capability as an explanation for graded-task performance; task difficulty and composition also differ (§5.6; Table 6).

## Open research questions
- **Does simulator choice change rankings or scoring gaps?** Only one simulator is evaluated. Repeat paired scoring with multiple simulators and human customers.
- **How much does explicit temporal checking change scores?** Current matching ignores order and repeated-call counts. Compare it with dependency- and multiplicity-aware scoring on audited trajectories.
- **Do procedural calls lead to correct customer communication?** Communication is outside the headline score. Add expert assessment of explanations, disclosures, and confirmations.
- **Does benchmark adaptation transfer beyond its task families?** Splits are supplied, but no training experiment is reported. Evaluate adaptation on held-out request families and independently authored scenarios.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and within-setting findings; lower in broader validity claims.

Material missing information includes simulator error measurements, human-customer evaluation, independent policy validation, temporal-scoring ablations, full serving and sampling configurations, and adaptation results. The E2B scoring discrepancy also remains unresolved.
