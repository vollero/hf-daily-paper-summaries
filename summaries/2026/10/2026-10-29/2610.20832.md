# Amortizing Sequential Experimental Design in Reasoning LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20832) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20832)


## One-sentence takeaway
AR-BED trains reasoning LLMs with externally defined expected information-gain rewards, improving Twenty Questions performance and simulated clinical questioning, while remaining sensitive to hypothesis coverage and showing modest lookahead gains in open-ended questioning.

## Problem and motivation
Adaptive information gathering requires choosing questions that distinguish plausible hypotheses and responding to new evidence. The authors argue that existing LLM approaches either repeatedly perform expensive Bayesian experimental design (BED) at inference or train question selection using potentially unreliable distributions induced by the policy itself (§§1–3).

Their proposed gap is learning transferable information-gathering reasoning against an independently specified experimental model, including rewards for information acquired through future interactions.

## Approach and contributions
- **Reasoning-policy amortization:** AR-BED jointly trains a reasoning trace and subsequent question using GRPO and information-gain returns; deployment directly generates questions without explicit BED search (§4, Eq. 5).
- **Independent reward model:** A fixed uniform prior over finite hypotheses and a separate GPT-5 likelihood evaluator define question value. Enumerable hypotheses and outcomes allow exact one-step EIG under this specified finite model, subject to implementation approximations (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Expected and multi-step rewards:** Rewards marginalize over hypotheses and answers, then accumulate over short policy rollouts. The paper distinguishes variance reduction under matched sampling from objective differences when training targets cover only part of the contrastive prior (§4.2; Appendices D.4–D.5).
- **Empirical design study:** Comparisons examine reasoning, reward estimation, hypothesis coverage, rollout horizon, computational cost, and transfer. The contribution builds on prior amortized BED rather than introducing amortization itself (§§2–3, 5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning training improves transfer | AR-BED₁ reports the best final solve rate across all four TQ domains against base/direct-policy controls; highest information gain on Animals, Plants, and Countries. Exact values are unavailable in supplied figure content. | §5.1, Fig. 1 |
| Lower reported computational cost | BED-LLM is approximately **61× slower per game at inference**; ASIG approximately **2.9× slower per training step**, compared with AR-BED₁. Absolute timings and Table 2 cells are absent. | §5.2, Table 2 discussion |
| Expected rewards usually outperform realized rewards | With matched one-step reasoning policies and a 200-animal contrastive set, Animals information gain rises **0.290→0.330 nats/question**, and solve rate **34.0%→43.3%**. Celebrities reverses the solve-rate advantage: realized **23.3%**, expected **17.3%**. | Appendix B.2, Fig. 5 |
| Broader hypothesis coverage matters | Restricting both contrastive hypotheses and training targets from 100 diverse Animals to 100 Mammals reduces animal-evaluation information gain **0.330→0.245** and solve rate **41.3%→16.7%**. | Appendix B.1, Fig. 4 |
| Controlled non-myopic behavior is learnable | In Cube-NM, five-test Bayesian MAP solve rate is **75.0% for K=2 versus 30.2% for K=1**; evaluated greedily on all 96 disease–context pairs, with matched single-training-seed, no-reasoning policies. | Appendix B.3.1, Fig. 6 |
| Open-ended lookahead gains are small | Across 180 matched held-out animal-game states, K=2 improves three-question cumulative expected IG divided by horizon by **0.0116 nats/question** versus K=1; K=3 improves it by **0.0080**. | Appendix B.3.5, Fig. 10 |
| Medical TQ training transfers to MediQ | After five questions with a fixed GPT-5 Patient and separate GPT-5 decider, accuracy rises **90.5%→94.7%** on 95 held-out-label dermatology cases and **84.8%→90.2%** on 92 other-specialty cases. | §5.4; Appendix C.4; Table 3 discussion |

The evidence supports improved questioning within the tested simulations and transfer settings. Crucially, MediQ uses a **separately trained medical policy**, not the animal-trained checkpoint (Appendix C.4.1). Results do not establish clinical effectiveness, globally optimal experimental design, or consistent benefits from longer horizons.

There is also a reporting ambiguity: §5 calls the full-game metric average EIG, whereas Appendix C defines it as cumulative **realized** information gain divided by questions asked. Matched-state lookahead evaluation explicitly uses expected information gain.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Rollout horizons are short, and performance depends on contrastive-set coverage; effects of composition and prior weighting remain unresolved (§6).
- **Implementation qualification:** TQ prunes low-weight hypotheses and advances with the oracle’s most likely answer. Consequently, implemented rollouts approximate the fully stochastic multi-step objective (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation-repeat SEM does not establish robustness across training runs. Main TQ checkpoints lack independent training-seed replication, and Cube-NM reports a single matched training seed (Appendices C.1, B.3.1).
- **Comparison scope:** ASIG excludes its additional game reward to isolate information-only training; conclusions therefore concern this evaluated configuration (§5.2).
- **Causal ambiguity:** Expected-versus-realized reward gains cannot be attributed solely to variance reduction because training targets and contrastive-prior support differ (Appendices B.2, D.5).
- **Clinical scope:** MediQ uses simulated patients and model-based decisions; reported uncertainty excludes Patient/decider sampling variation. Held-out labels do not imply absence from pretraining (Appendix C.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does longer-horizon training reliably improve questioning?** Current open-ended gains are modest and non-monotonic. Compare longer horizons under matched compute and independent training seeds.
2. **How robust is learning to imperfect likelihoods and priors?** Coverage sensitivity is demonstrated, but evaluator reliability is not systematically isolated. Vary likelihood noise, evaluator models, and prior weights independently.
3. **What produces the expected-reward advantage?** Variance reduction and target-distribution mismatch are entangled. Repeat comparisons with matched target/prior distributions and measure reward variance directly.
4. **Does medical transfer survive changes in simulation?** Evidence uses fixed GPT-5 Patient and decider roles. Evaluate with alternative simulators and blinded human assessment of acquired evidence.
