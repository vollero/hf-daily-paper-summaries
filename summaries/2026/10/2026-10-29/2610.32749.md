# Efficient Streaming Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction for Real-World Acoustic Scenes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32749) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32749)


## One-sentence takeaway
Falcon combines a single-pass multi-scale separator with training-only speaker contrastive learning to report competitive audio-visual target extraction at substantially lower computation, while RealSSA adds controlled acoustic diversity and held-out real recordings (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Audio-visual target speaker extraction uses a target’s mouth video to recover their speech from a mixture. The authors identify two gaps: fixed two-speaker benchmarks omit important deployment conditions, and strong causal models require substantial computation while lightweight iterative models repeatedly invoke their separator. They also argue that reconstruction loss alone insufficiently discourages near-field speaker leakage (§1).

Video conferencing and hearing assistance motivate low-latency operation, but neither application is directly evaluated as a deployed system (§1; Appendix E).

## Approach and contributions
- **RealSSA benchmark:** RealSSA-Sim varies near-field interferer counts, far-field interference, reverberation, and continuous/transient noise across four scene types. RealSSA-Real supplies independent recordings exclusively for evaluation (§3; Appendix A).
- **Falcon separator:** An asymmetric U-Net performs frequency, temporal, and joint time-frequency modeling across four resolutions in one encoder–decoder traversal. The pipeline applies an audio block before fusion and a separator after fusion; “single-pass” describes each application, rather than the entire pipeline containing only one block (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Encoder-space speaker contrastive loss:** Estimated target features are attracted toward clean target features and repelled from individual near-field interferers, alongside SI-SNR reconstruction loss. Reference paths use stop-gradient; the contrastive branch is removed at inference (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Competitive real-recording quality | After RealSSA-Sim training, RealSSA-Real DNSMOS **3.276**, tied with Swift-Net-12, versus **3.030** for AV-TFGridNet. | §6.1; Table 3 discussion |

These results support favorable quality–computation tradeoffs under the reported protocols and useful simulation-to-real transfer. They do not establish end-to-end streaming latency or universal superiority: real-set WER favors another model, and real recordings lack isolated target waveforms. Most table bodies are absent from the supplied extraction, so the numerical evidence above comes from accompanying prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Falcon is single-channel; multi-channel extensions remain future work. Contrastive training requires clean near-field source waveforms, limiting direct use on unlabeled recordings (§7).
- **Author-stated visual dependence:** Sustained or complete visual loss causes severe degradation. Mouth-crop displacement and tracking the wrong speaker remain untested (Appendix M).
- **Real evaluation coverage:** The real set contains approximately 84 minutes, 10 participants, eight environments, and one recording-device model. **Analyst inference:** broader population and device generalization remains uncertain (§7; Appendix A.4).
- **Latency evidence:** The visual branch uses **120 ms lookahead**. Timing covers complete two-second forward passes; the authors explicitly defer per-hop overhead and end-to-end latency measurement to a streaming implementation (Appendix E).
- **Comparison and attribution limits:** LRS2/LRS3 baseline scores are imported from Swift-Net, whereas other baselines are retrained. No uncertainty estimates are supplied, and the aggregate RealSSA-Sim advantage over AV-TFGridNet is small. The training-data comparison does not isolate individual acoustic factors (§§6.1, 6.3).
- **Simulation assumptions:** Outdoor propagation uses a highly absorptive enclosure approximation; far-field interferers are mixed at an applied SIR of **20–35 dB**. **Analyst inference:** conclusions about harder far-field conditions require further testing (Appendices A.1–A.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does Falcon meet sustained streaming budgets on edge devices?** Whole-clip timings leave scheduling overhead and long-stream behavior unresolved. Next experiment: measure end-to-end latency, memory growth, and deadline misses during continuous device inference (Appendix E).
2. **Can contrastive supervision work without clean interferer stems?** The present loss depends on simulated source references. Next experiment: compare supervised training with pseudo-reference adaptation on unlabeled real recordings (§7).
3. **How can extraction survive prolonged visual failure?** Contiguous missing video causes substantial degradation. Next experiment: train with sustained occlusions and test fallback behavior, crop displacement, and wrong-speaker tracking (Appendix M).
4. **Which simulation factors drive real-world gains?** RealSSA-Sim changes several acoustic conditions together. Next experiment: matched training ablations removing reverberation, speaker-count variation, or transient noise individually (§6.3).
