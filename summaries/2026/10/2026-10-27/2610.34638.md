# Intrinsic Goals for Autonomous Agents: Hierarchical Exploration Captures Future Volitional Action in Drosophila

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34638) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34638)


## One-sentence takeaway
Among 20 intrinsic objectives tested in a simulated fly, hierarchical DIAYN best jointly reproduced selected behavioral and neural signatures of spontaneous turning, supporting a computational hypothesis rather than establishing the biological mechanism (§4).

## Problem and motivation
Intrinsic motivation algorithms are usually evaluated through exploration or downstream task performance, leaving unclear whether they explain biological autonomy. The authors instead require models to match both spontaneous fly behavior and IPS activity predictive of later turns, since similar average behavior can arise from different internal computations (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Embodied comparison framework:** Recurrent PPO policies select among seven fixed motor primitives in NeuroMechFly/FlyGym. Twenty intrinsic objectives share the training architecture, environment, and decision budget; frozen policies then undergo a virtual tethered-ball assay (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Joint behavioral and neural evaluation:** The study combines future-turn decoding, turning-bias variability, and low-dimensional activity–kinematics relationships, with random and extrinsically trained controls (§4; Appendix A.7).
- **Empirical finding:** DIAYN’s learned skills provide the strongest joint correspondence across these measures. The contribution is its hierarchical implementation and biological comparison; DIAYN itself is an existing method (§§2.2, 4).
- **Interpretive hypothesis:** Persistent skills could explain both individual exploratory biases and prospective activity; population-level coverage is proposed as an explanation, not experimentally established (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| DIAYN has strong prospective internal decoding | Future left/right turns decoded from activity **−15 to −8 s** before turns: DIAYN actor LSTM **82.1 ± 0.9%**, fly IPS **66.5 ± 1.4%**, NGU **65.4 ± 1.8%** balanced accuracy. Uncertainty is SD across stratified cross-validation folds. | §4; Fig. 3a; Table 7; A.10 |
| Behavior alone is also predictive | Same window and decoding metric, using mean angular and forward velocity: DIAYN **74.7 ± 1.4%**, flies **60.5 ± 0.9%**, MAX **92.2 ± 5.9%**; fold SD. MAX’s selected internal representation scores **50.0 ± 0.0%**. | §4; Fig. 3c–d; Table 7 |
| Many objectives fail the turning assay | **Nine of 20** intrinsic methods produce no turns or only one turn direction, preventing prospective decoding. Evaluation uses **nine 30-minute rollouts per policy**, matching the biological recording duration. | §§3.3–4; Fig. 3e; Table 7 |
| DIAYN captures broad turning-bias variability | Across rollouts, turning bias log(Nᵣ/Nₗ) is **−0.37 ± 2.27** for DIAYN, **0.17 ± 1.90** for flies, and **−0.05 ± 0.34** for Random-DIAYN. DIAYN holds one skill per rollout. Table 7 does not explicitly define these bias uncertainty terms. | §4; Fig. 4; Table 7 |
| Selected DIAYN components resemble the IPS sum–difference relationship | Pearson correlations for component difference with angular velocity / component sum with forward velocity: DIAYN **0.153 / 0.413**, fly IPS **0.191 / 0.429**, Random-DIAYN **0.021 / 0.113**. Model component pairs are selected to maximize the relationship on pooled evaluation activity. | §4; Fig. 5; A.11 |

These results support DIAYN as the strongest joint match **within the tested implementations and assay**. Its neural decoding exceeds the fly value rather than matching it exactly; the authors emphasize the accompanying behavioral relationship and variability. Separate neural and behavioral decoders do not establish that internal activity adds information after conditioning on behavior, nor do predictive correlations establish planning or causal control.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Fixed primitives constrain the repertoire mainly to planar locomotion. IPS-focused evaluation and a simple environment leave whole-brain dynamics and richer ecological behavior untested (Limitations and Future Work).
- **Training robustness:** Each method is trained once with seed 0. Fold-level uncertainty does not measure variation across independent training runs (A.5, A.10).
- **Biological correspondence:** The released data lack the original premotor supercluster, so decoding uses whole anatomical IPS. Some biological turning bias may reflect mounting asymmetry (A.8).
- **Analyst inference—selection and dependence:** The best network feature set and PCA component pair are selected from evaluation data without a reported independent confirmation set. Cross-validation splits individual pooled turns rather than holding out flies or rollouts, leaving generalization across individuals and temporally related events uncertain (A.10–A.11).
- **Implementation ambiguity:** §3.2 describes DIAYN’s per-step reward with an action-entropy term, while A.4 specifies discriminator reward plus PPO’s entropy bonus only. Several other algorithms also depart from their original formulations; rankings therefore concern these implementations (A.4).
- **Analyst inference—causal scope:** No hierarchy ablation or direct test of population-level optimization is reported. The evolutionary interpretation remains speculative (§5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does DIAYN’s advantage survive training variability?** Only one training run per method is reported. Repeat training across seeds and evaluate on held-out rollouts with prespecified representation choices.
2. **Does internal activity predict turns beyond behavioral history and skill identity?** Separate decoder accuracies cannot resolve this. Compare behavior-only and combined decoders while conditioning on skill and holding out entire rollouts.
3. **Which components produce the correspondence?** Fixed hierarchy, recurrence, skill persistence, and the intrinsic objective are not separately isolated. Ablate these factors under matched training budgets.
4. **Does the explanation extend to richer behavior?** Evaluation targets tethered turning and IPS signatures. Test learned primitives and richer terrain against additional behavioral and whole-brain recordings, as the authors propose.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main empirical comparisons, but biological and causal interpretations remain provisional.

Material gaps include full contents of Tables 1–6 and 8, independent training replications, independent validation of selected representations, quantitative population-coverage tests, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability. The DIAYN reward descriptions also require reconciliation.
