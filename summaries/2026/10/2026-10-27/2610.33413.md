# AV-FABLE: Audio-Visual Manipulation Forensics in Long-Form Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33413) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33413)


## One-sentence takeaway

AV-FABLE evaluates sparse video manipulations on separate audio and visual timelines, and the authors report that supervised, modality-separated readouts of frozen AV-LLM features substantially outperform prompted language outputs (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation

Brief edits to speech, sounds, actions, or objects can change a long video’s meaning while leaving most content authentic. Video-level classification cannot identify the manipulated interval or affected modality. The authors identify a gap between predominantly talking-head audio-visual benchmarks and broader long-video benchmarks focused on visual forensics (§1).

Separate timelines matter because edits can affect one modality or occur asynchronously: matching the correct time on the wrong track can receive credit under joint-timeline evaluation (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark and construction pipeline:** AV-FABLE contains 16,650 pristine–manipulated twin pairs, totaling 33,300 videos and 998.1 hours, across 27 editing configurations. SEE-Forge plans edits around existing events, executes and reinserts them, and applies matched processing to pristine twins. Annotations include separate timelines and human-reviewed evidence records (§§3–3.2; Figure 1).
- **Evaluation design:** Native-track evaluation requires both correct timing and modality. Source-disjoint splits test transfer to new sources, generators, and a held-out generation route; decision thresholds remain calibrated on source validation data (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Representation finding:** Supervised probes recover forensic discrimination from frozen perception features despite poor zero-shot language outputs. This establishes accessibility under supervision, rather than a general ability to explain manipulation (§§4.1, 5.1; Appendix D).
- **NFSR:** Frozen Qwen3-Omni intermediate and interface features pass through gated projection, host-conditioned FiLM, and independent temporal decoders. Each decoder receives only its own modality; proposal scores also support detection and attribution (§4; Figure 2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limits:** Broader real-world editing processes, perceptual boundary annotations, corruption robustness, and faithful explanations remain future work (§6).
- **Annotation validity:** Labels mark executed edit intervals rather than perceptually observable changes; reviewers do not redraw boundaries. Localization scores therefore measure agreement with execution coordinates (§3.2).
- **Residual shortcuts:** Matched twins control content and export differences, but file-level probes cannot exclude local resampling or generator residue as predictive cues (§3.2). **Analyst inference:** strong discrimination need not imply understanding the semantic edit.
- **Confounded transfer settings:** Source transfer changes domain and modality composition; held-out TI2V changes both route and generator. The authors explicitly caution against interpreting settings as isolated shift factors (§5.2; Appendix C).
- **Comparison and scaling limits:** Baselines use different output forms and adaptation schedules. Training-fraction experiments vary optimizer updates alongside data volume, preventing isolation of data quantity alone (§5; Appendices C.4, F.3).
- **Reproducibility and coverage:** Release is promised, with licensing restrictions requiring partial reconstruction rather than unrestricted source-media redistribution. The supplied source is truncated, so this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions

1. **Do readouts detect authentic forensic cues beyond generator residue?** Local synthesis traces remain uncontrolled (§3.2). A next experiment could evaluate matched edits after artifact-reducing processing and on additional real-world editing workflows.
2. **How much do execution labels affect measured boundary quality?** Observable changes may occupy only part of an annotated interval (§3.2). Independently redraw perceptual boundaries on a subset and compare localization scores and model rankings.
3. **Can visual coverage improve under degradation without increasing false alarms?** OOD coverage and 360p robustness remain weak (§5.2; Appendix F.5). Test corruption-aware training against the current model using identical, fixed source calibration.
4. **Can explanations become faithful while avoiding excessive abstention?** Accurate event conditioning still leaves semantic identification weak (Appendix H). Compare evidence-focused supervision on fixed annotated events, measuring semantic correctness, unsupported support, and manipulated-event abstention.
