# ProAS: A Progress-Aware Advantage Shaping Framework for Multi-Turn Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18683) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18683)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProAS uses reference graphs of successful task progress to reshape action advantages without a critic, improving most tested aggregate results on ALFWorld and WebShop while leaving its broader applicability and causal credit interpretation unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Critic-free group-based RL can assign the same trajectory advantage to actions with very different contributions: successful trajectories can reinforce errors, while failed trajectories penalize useful and harmful actions alike. The authors call this the **uniform credit assignment dilemma** and seek finer credit assignment without training a separate critic (§§3.2–4).

Their intervention studies suggest that irrelevant action history can reduce subsequent success and that suppressing actions with more downstream stagnation can help recovery. These are motivating case studies rather than general causal demonstrations (§4.2; Appendix D).

## Approach and contributions
- **Agentic Reference Graph (ARG):** Build a graph from successful trajectories, organize progress into ordered sub-goals with permutation-invariant requirements, and expand it using purified, newly successful rollouts. Graph matching labels actions as reasonable and progress-advancing (§4.1).
- **Asymmetric advantage shaping:** Mask unreasonable actions’ positive advantages through Masking Unreasonable Incentives (MUI). For negative advantages, Asymmetric Penalty Redistribution (APR) weights penalties by normalized **Future-Drift**, the inclusive count of subsequent non-advancing actions (§4.3, Eqs. 6–7).
- **Integration and empirical analysis:** Apply the shaping to GRPO, DAPO, and GiGPO; compare against imitation and RL baselines; and test component choices and reference quality (§6; Appendix B.2). These constitute the paper’s proposed contributions, rather than a new environment or model architecture.

## Evidence and main results

Main results report mean ± sample standard deviation across three seeds, using the checkpoint with highest validation success rate on a shared, training-disjoint validation set of 128 tasks per environment (§5.1.2; Appendix B.1).

The evidence supports useful shaping across the tested algorithms and environments, particularly for weaker baselines. It does **not** establish universal improvement: aggregate WebShop SR falls slightly in one configuration, some category results decline, and reported standard deviations overlap in several comparisons. “State of the art” is the authors’ characterization of their comparisons. Steps-to-target gains also do not directly establish equivalent wall-clock savings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ explicit qualification:** Future-Drift interventions use different prefixes and remaining horizons; Appendix D.2 cautions against treating the case as a universal causal estimate.
- **Analyst inference—limited scope:** Evaluation covers two environments and two sizes within one model family. Broader agent tasks, stochastic environments, and less structured workflows remain untested (§5).
- **Analyst inference—reference dependence:** Graph membership is not identical to actual action validity. Table 9 labels a novel, successful action unreasonable before graph expansion. Random annotations substantially degrade performance, illustrating sensitivity to guidance quality (§6.2.2).
- **Analyst inference—formalization ambiguity:** The graph definition prunes self-loops, while Table 10 permits reasonable but non-advancing repeated clicks. How the formal graph implements these distinctions is not fully resolved by the text (§4.1; Appendix D.4).
- **Analyst inference—stronger claims than ablations warrant:** Full ProAS does not uniquely win every ablation metric: disabling APR ties WebShop SR, and a static ARG exceeds the evolving ARG on WebShop SR. Claims of universal component necessity require qualification (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation and cost limits:** Checkpoints are selected on the same validation set used for reporting; no separate final test evaluation is reported. Wall-clock analysis averages algorithm runs under one seed, and exact plotted times are unavailable in the retrieved text (§5.1.2; Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does Future-Drift capture responsibility beyond position and recovery opportunity?** The intervention varies both prefix and remaining horizon. A next experiment could compare matched decision states and budgets while independently varying downstream stagnation (Appendix D.2).
2. **Can ProAS work with weak or absent task structure?** Existing tests start from official or teacher-generated successful references. A next experiment could progressively reduce reference coverage and evaluate on tasks without predefined sequential sub-goals (§§4.1, 6.2.2).
3. **When are APR and online evolution beneficial?** Their gains vary by environment, including WebShop ties or reversals. A next experiment could repeat factorial ablations across model sizes and larger held-out task sets (Table 2).
4. **Do step-efficiency gains translate into total compute savings?** Current speedups measure RL steps, while graph construction and updates add work. A next experiment could report end-to-end time and compute to target, including reference generation, across seeds (Appendix C.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and tabulated comparisons, but broader causal and generalization claims remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes exact wall-clock figure values and GPU models; complete graph-matching and purification implementation details; precise GiGPO shaping thresholds and clustering rules; statistical significance tests; a separate final test evaluation; and verified repository contents or released model artifacts.
