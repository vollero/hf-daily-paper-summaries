# miniTE: Diagnosing and Bridging the Execution Gap in Native NVFP4 Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23928) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23928)


## One-sentence takeaway
miniTE combines execution diagnostics and optimized native NVFP4 expert computation to improve mathematics evaluation over standard TE NVFP4 and accelerate learner updates, while leaving end-to-end RL speedup unestablished (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Separate learner and sampler engines can assign different probabilities to identical tokens even with frozen, matching weights and nominal numerical formats. These differences affect RL importance ratios and clipping. The authors identify an execution gap beyond bit width and expert routing: scaling, rounding, accumulation, casts, and operator composition also require specification (§§2–3).

Native four-bit arithmetic also requires quantization, packing, and dispatch preparation; faster GEMMs alone need not produce faster learner updates (§4.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Execution-gap diagnosis:** Frozen-policy scoring, routing controls, and common-input replay expose residual numerical differences. A strict execution reference aligns kernels, formulas, reduction orders, and surrounding operations (§§3, 4.1).
- **Practical learning path:** Native W4A4 expert forward uses per-token activation scales, per-output-row weight scales, FP32 epilogue rescaling, and BF16 backward on saved original operands. Non-strict runs retain the existing TIS-corrected objective (§4.2).
- **Integrated implementation:** Contiguous packed buffers, device metadata, producer-side maxima, fused scale layouts, and specialized grouped GEMMs reduce preparation costs. The authors report releasing kernels, diagnostics, configurations, Dockerfiles, and launch scripts (§§1, 4.3).

The contribution is the combined diagnosis and implementation; per-token NVFP4 and high-precision backward are acknowledged as existing approaches (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| NVFP4 enlarges frozen execution discrepancies | On 32 prompts and 69,088 matched tokens, P99 absolute learner–sampler log-probability difference rises from 0.1271 nats for BF16 to 0.2911 for NVFP4; paired increase 0.1641, 95% CI [0.1311, 0.2005]. | §3.1; Fig. 1 |
| Matching target-layer routing is insufficient | Across 11 depths, FC1 symmetric relative-L2 discrepancies remain 0.0381–0.6124 and FC2 discrepancies 0.0810–0.7624; upstream inputs can still differ. | §3.2; Fig. 1; App. C |
| Mathematics improves, with task-dependent tradeoffs | At step 999, miniTE exceeds naive NVFP4 by 10.00/3.88 percentage points on AIME 2024/2025 and 0.55/0.08 on MATH-500/GSM8K. | Complete learner updates accelerate | Eight B300 GPUs, 524,288 fixed-text tokens/update: optimized miniTE reaches 33,540 tokens/s, 1.39× BF16 and 1.47× TE NVFP4. | Strict alignment supports near-identical scoring | Approximately 10⁻⁸ log-probability difference with maintained reward; one run per configuration, jointly changing operators and removing TIS. | §5.4; Fig. 4 |

The evidence supports an execution gap and gains for the tested learning and implementation configurations. It does not isolate which numerical choices cause the quality gains, establish multi-seed reliability, or demonstrate faster complete RL iterations. BF16 checkpoint comparisons also change inference precision; the two NVFP4 paths share evaluation precision (App. E.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Learning evidence covers one MoE model with one training run per configuration; implementation gains are evaluated on Blackwell hardware (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Remaining quality differences do not separate quantization error from residual execution mismatch; practical miniTE remains below BF16 in average evaluation quality (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation point estimates lack per-round dispersion; repeated checkpoint evaluations are not independent training seeds (App. E.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Strict alignment cannot identify the effect of alignment alone because it also removes TIS and changes multiple operators (App. E.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Combined optimizations and different timing boundaries prevent attributing complete learner gains to individual kernel changes (§5.3; App. F.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Which execution differences drive policy quality?** Similar mean discrepancies accompany different reward trajectories (§5.2). Next experiment: ablate scaling, composition, and kernel alignment independently while holding TIS fixed.
2. **Can component gains yield faster complete RL iterations?** Conversion and synchronization are costly, and total iteration timing is absent (§8). Next experiment: measure all stages before and after cached or incremental weight packing.
3. **How robust are the gains?** Model, seed, and hardware coverage remains narrow (§8). Next experiment: repeat matched training across independent seeds, additional MoE models, and tasks.
