# S3AC: Stabilized, Selective, and Scalable Actor-Critic for LLM Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35518) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35518)


## One-sentence takeaway
S3AC combines a critic-training recipe with critic-guided prompt sampling to improve reported reasoning scores and sustain single-rollout learning, at the cost of additional computation and with limited evidence on training-seed variability.

## Problem and motivation
Critic-free RL with verifiable rewards typically assigns one advantage to an entire response, limiting token-level credit assignment. Grouped rollouts supply prompt-specific baselines but require multiple generations; single-rollout batch baselines do not account for prompt difficulty. The authors identify two obstacles to using critics instead: insufficient coverage of long, correct trajectories and early optimization dominated by a newly initialized value head (§§1, 3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Critic-training recipe:** Pretrain on trajectories from policies of different strengths, use small value-head initialization, and relax critic gradient clipping. Update the critic online more aggressively than the actor (§3.3).
- **Unified learning signals:** Use the critic for prompt-conditioned baselines and token-specific, length-adaptive GAE advantages. GAE, CISPO, and several training components are adopted from prior work; the contribution is their integration with the proposed critic recipe (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Difficulty-Aware Prompt Sampling (DAPS):** Combine prompt-level critic predictions with decaying outcome counts, then preferentially accept prompts of intermediate estimated difficulty before generation. This reuses the policy-learning critic without separate offline difficulty initialization (§3.4).
- **Empirical study:** Evaluate dense and MoE models under grouped and single rollouts, with critic-pretraining diagnostics and matched DAPS comparisons (§4).

The evidence supports improvements for the evaluated training recipes and settings. It does not isolate token-level credit assignment as the sole cause: main comparisons combine several changes, and DAPS is included in most S3AC runs. Equal actor-update budgets also do not establish equal-compute superiority.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers verifiable math, science, and multimodal reasoning, excluding agentic multi-turn tasks. Independent RL training seeds are not systematically assessed. Critic training and inference add substantial overhead (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Small R1-Distill score differences require caution because repeated evaluations vary; policy-loss robustness checks use single seeds with different restart histories (Appendices A.2, B).
- **Analyst inference:** The coverage-pretraining comparison confounds trajectory diversity with data volume, so it cannot establish the independent benefit of diversity (§4.3; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** In-house MoE training prompts and missing configuration-table contents limit reproducibility from this text. Missing per-benchmark table cells also prevent independently checking the reported aggregate improvements (§4.1; Tables 1–3, 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Qualitative value traces illustrate plausible reasoning progress but do not establish causal correctness of token-level credit assignments (§3.2; Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does trajectory diversity help independently of corpus size?** The comparison changes both. Next experiment: match pretraining token budgets while varying policy-strength diversity.
- **Does S3AC improve performance per unit of compute?** Scores are mainly compared by actor steps despite added overhead. Next experiment: compare learning curves at matched wall time and total compute, including critic preparation and prompt scoring.
- **Which critic signal drives the gains?** The reported recipe combines prompt baselines, token advantages, and selection. Next experiment: hold the recipe fixed while separately ablating token-specific advantages and DAPS.
- **Do the benefits transfer to multi-turn agents?** This setting is explicitly unevaluated. Next experiment: test credit assignment across tool calls and environment turns with verifiable outcomes.
