# Trust Mechanisms for Offline Generative AI at the Edge: A Connectivity-Regime Framework and an Empirical Study on a Raspberry Pi 5

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33732) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33732)


## One-sentence takeaway
On a network-isolated Raspberry Pi 5, layered checks improved abstention and update security, but neither signed knowledge packs nor the tested inference-time checks protected against validly signed false evidence (§§5.1–5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Disconnected generative assistants must answer from local knowledge without cloud verification or continuous updates. The authors identify a gap between research on efficient edge inference and research on trustworthy RAG: their joint behavior, interactions, and hardware costs under disconnection remain insufficiently measured (§§1–2).

The practical challenge spans both inference and updates: deciding when evidence supports an answer, keeping local knowledge current, and preventing malicious or outdated packs from being activated.

## Approach and contributions
- **Connectivity framework:** Proposed C0–C4 deployment regimes connect cloud availability and inference location to feasible updates, threats, and assurance mechanisms. This is an author-proposed taxonomy, not an established standard (§3.1, Table 1).
- **Trust and update architecture:** Organizes inference-time safeguards by locus and generator dependence, and motivates signed, versioned document-and-index packs for disconnected updates. It distinguishes authenticity, freshness, and content trust (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Joint empirical evaluation:** Tests self-abstention, citation-only output validation, calibrated retrieval gating, NLI verification, model/context choices, update attacks, and signed poisoning on one CPU-only device. The contribution is their combined evaluation, rather than invention of offline RAG or these established method families (§§2.5, 4–5).
- **Reported reusable artifacts:** Releases benchmark scripts, sampled questions, prompts, per-query results, analysis outputs, and pack-building code (Data and Code availability, p. 17).

## Evidence and main results

All QA comparisons below use the network-isolated Raspberry Pi 5 pipeline. The main sample contains 100 questions each for answerable, in-topic unanswerable, and out-of-corpus conditions (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval similarity detects corpus gaps better than in-topic unanswerability | Answerable-vs-out-of-corpus AUROC **0.97**, 95% CI **[0.94, 0.99]**; answerable-vs-in-topic-unanswerable **0.68 [0.60, 0.76]**, using top-1 cosine scores. | §5.1; Fig. 3 |
| Output validation substantially changes measured risk for the smallest model | For Qwen2.5-1.5B, k=3, converting citation-only outputs to abstentions reduced answered-query risk **0.81→0.46**, with no observed correct-answer loss. This reroutes malformed outputs rather than correcting substantive claims. | §5.2; Table 4 |
| Gating trades correct answers for additional abstention | For 7B, k=3, adding gating after validation reduced risk **0.29→0.24**, while removing **7 percentage points** of correct answers; paired 95% CI for correctness change **[−13, −2]**. | §5.2 |
| Equal gold containment conceals a major quality difference | At k=3, 3B and 7B both achieved **75%** gold-answer containment, but token-F1 ≥0.5 rates were **31% versus 74%**. Median latency was **22 versus 50 s** and estimated energy **174 versus 399 J/query**. | §5.3; Table 4 |
| Additional context reduced correctness | For 3B, increasing k from 3 to 5 reduced gold containment **75%→52%**, paired difference **−23 points [−32, −14]**, despite unchanged recall@k of **98%**; median latency increased **22→36 s**. | §5.3 |
| The tested NLI verifier added limited value | For 3B, k=3, correct/incorrect separation AUROC was **0.64 [0.54, 0.73]**. Adding it after other layers increased in-topic abstention **72%→77%**, reduced correct answers **67%→62%**, and added median **2.4 s/answer**. | §5.2; Table 5 |
| Layered update verification was cheap and stopped additional specified attacks | Layered verification rejected **all nine** tested update attacks; signature/hash checks accepted **six**. Valid-pack median verification times were **4.55 versus 4.53 ms**. Separately, embedding 950 passages took **28.8 min**. | §5.4; Table 6 |
| Validly signed poisoning defeated the tested trust stack | For 30 previously correct 3B, k=3 targets, adding 150 poisoned passages reduced correctness **30/30→0/30**. Injected answers appeared for **14/30** targets; **13/30** survived gating plus span and entailment checks. Both pack verifiers accepted the pack. | §5.5; Table 6 |

These results establish configuration-specific trade-offs and concrete failures of the tested mechanisms. They do not establish that 3B and 7B are equivalent, that NLI verification generally fails, or that the poisoning budget is minimal. Cryptographic acceptance authenticates artifacts; agreement with retrieved text does not establish that text’s truth.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** One device, one model family, one English Wikipedia-based dataset, and one article split limit generalization. Network namespaces test network independence, not operational air-gap security (§6.3).
- **Label ambiguity:** Unanswerable labels apply to original passages, not the whole pack; questions were not re-annotated against all passages. **Analyst inference:** Some measured errors or abstention failures may therefore reflect label mismatch rather than unsupported answering (§4).
- **Metric dependence:** Gold containment can reward verbose answers containing incorrect material; token-F1 penalizes verbosity. Equal containment rates do not establish equal answer quality (§§5.3, 6.3).
- **Cost uncertainty:** Sequential runs, enabled prompt caching, and no warm-up introduce potential confounding. Energy uses onboard power estimates without idle subtraction; verification timing used a warm page cache (§§4, 5.4).
- **Verifier confounding:** Rule-based rewriting and a small NLI model were not separately ablated, limiting conclusions about answerability-aware verification (§5.2).
- **Security boundaries:** Poisoning used selected targets and templated passages. Update tests did not exercise state reset, authenticated trust-store updates, activation-byte binding, or crash-safe persistence (§§5.4–5.5).

## Open research questions
1. **Can edge-feasible verification recognize whether evidence answers the question?** The tested verifier conflates rewriting and classification weaknesses. Next experiment: compare question-conditioned verifiers and rewriting methods on manually re-annotated whole-pack labels, measuring accuracy and CPU cost (§§5.2, 6.4).
2. **Does retrieval calibration transfer across corpora and update windows?** Evidence comes from one article split, and the reported mean threshold has no formal conformal guarantee. Next experiment: calibrate on one pack and evaluate successive domain-shifted packs, tracking retention and selective risk (§§4, 6.4).
3. **Which publisher-side checks resist signed poisoning?** All activation checks accepted the poisoned pack. Next experiment: vary poison budget and attack style while comparing provenance review, content screening, and adversarial pack testing (§§5.5, 6.4).
4. **Can update guarantees survive device failures and hostile state changes?** State preservation and crash-safe activation were assumed. Next experiment: interrupt activation and attempt sequence-state rollback, clock manipulation, and key-revocation inconsistencies (§5.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this account of the supplied study; broader applicability remains uncertain.

Material missing evidence includes whole-pack answerability adjudication, independent energy measurements, isolated cache effects, verifier-component ablations, multiple corpus/device/model replications, poisoning-budget sweeps, and fault-tested update-state persistence. The paper reports a public release, but its contents were not independently inspected.
