# AgentProofBench: Benchmarking Reusable Verifiers for Web Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34634) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34634)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgentProofBench tests whether frozen Web-agent verifiers follow changed initial-state requirements and support correct delivery, finding a strong exploratory association between deployment-panel correctness and execution outcomes.

## Problem and motivation
A fixed instruction can require different actions when initial business data change: a name may identify a different user, a collection may gain required items, or a preserved value may differ. Generated verifiers can instead memorize their construction-state answers.

Verifier decisions also affect execution: rejecting completed work can trigger harmful edits or withhold a valid result. The authors identify a gap between testing acceptance decisions and measuring what happens when an agent follows them (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired reuse evaluation:** Freeze the instruction and verifier, then vary source, unrelated-background, and requirement-changing initial states. Matched outcome panels test acceptance; replay witnesses test whether an earlier valid decision becomes correctly rejected under changed requirements (§3.3).
- **Linked execution evaluation:** Restart policies from identical saved histories and business states. Compare actor stopping, neutral continuation, frozen-program gating, and fresh online judgment, recording state changes and delivery separately (§3.4).
- **Executable benchmark resource:** The authors report releasing 97 tasks from 79 families across Linear, Slack, Box, and Calendar, with reference predicates, legal alternatives, reconstructable states, verifier programs, and trajectories (§§3.2–3.3, 5; Appendix A).

The interface is `verify(task, before, after)`, using ordinary authenticated APIs in a bounded, read-only sandbox. The reported experiments use DeepSeek Flash/Pro with thinking disabled (§3.1; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reuse performs slightly below rebuilding on mean balanced accuracy (BA), with uncertain differences. | Across 48 families and two program replicas per model, related-state reuse BA is 93.23% Flash and 90.36% Pro, versus 94.79% and 95.31% for rebuilding. Reuse-minus-rebuild differences are −1.56 and −4.95 percentage points; 95% family-bootstrap intervals are [−5.47, 1.57] and [−10.16, 0.00]. | §4.1, Table 1, Figure 3 |
| Replay witnesses reveal errors missed by main panels. | Related main-panel correctness holds for 83/96 Flash and 81/96 Pro source programs; requiring correct panel and witness verdicts reduces these to 82/96 and 79/96. | §4.1, Figure 3 |
| Deployment-panel correctness strongly separates delivery outcomes. | Across 44 program assignments from 22 families, both saved openings are correctly delivered for 32/33 assignments with error-free related panels versus 1/11 with panel errors. Exploratory assignment-level gap: 87.9 percentage points, pointwise 95% family-cluster interval [68.8, 100.0]. | §4.2, Figure 4; Appendix B |
| Program-gate harms concentrate among assignments with panel errors. | All 17 program-gate withholdings and all three reference-defined regressions occur in the panel-error group; 13 withheld terminal states nevertheless satisfy the reference. | §4.2 |
| Neutral continuation provides the strongest tested delivery baseline. | At checkpoints, correct delivery is 20/22 Flash and 22/22 Pro under neutral continuation, versus 17/22 and 16/22 under program gating, and 20/22 and 21/22 under online gating. | §4.2, Figure 4 |
| Generated programs trade valid acceptance for stronger failure rejection. | On identical collection-test states spanning 36 tasks and 29 families, Flash B12 false acceptance is 3.5% versus API12’s 31.7%; valid-anchor acceptance is 77.8% versus 98.6%. | §4.3 |
| Frozen checks reduce repeated assessment latency and can amortize construction fees. | On the 24-state timing panel, median standalone assessment takes 0.58 seconds for B12 versus 20.69 seconds for API12. Matched API-fee comparisons yield median construction break-even horizons of 12 assessments for Flash and 10 for Pro. | §§4.3–4.4 |
| A separate semantic audit largely supports the reference labels. | GPT-6 Astra agrees with 191/192 resolved labels among 195 audited states across 62 tasks; three cases remain indeterminate. | §3.2; Appendix A |

These results establish behavior on the benchmark’s controlled outcomes and saved execution branches. They do not establish reuse–rebuild equivalence: the intervals include zero but permit meaningful reuse losses. The panel–delivery result is exploratory association, not evidence that improving panel scores alone causes better delivery. Policy results apply to the tested feedback and stopping rules.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Reference-dependent validity:** Scores measure conformity to explicit reference predicates. The audit finds one resolved disagreement concerning label uniqueness and three indeterminate cases; benchmark scores retain the frozen labels (Appendix A).
- **Post-inspection changes:** Three tasks are excluded from the explicit-acceptance view after inspection of earlier results, and preservation-label revisions affect seven Linear tasks. Original outputs and alternate views are retained (Appendix A).
- **Selected evaluation scope:** Reuse uses qualified requirement-changing cases, and execution uses families with suitable mutation traces (§3.3; Appendix A). **Analyst inference:** Results may not represent arbitrary workflow changes or tasks lacking reconstructable state.
- **Limited execution generalization:** Experiments use Flash/Pro without thinking and generic rejection feedback. The evaluated unit combines verifier decisions, feedback, and stopping (§§3.4, 4.2). **Analyst inference:** The study cannot isolate verifier quality from those policy choices.
- **Incomplete outcome coverage:** The Box141 replay exposes a collection branch missed by otherwise correct main panels (§4.1). **Analyst inference:** Passing all supplied tests does not establish verifier correctness beyond those tests.
- **Cost completeness:** One online-policy fee is unknown after a timeout; reported fee comparisons use complete-usage records (Figure 6; Appendix C).

## Open research questions
1. **Can generators reliably derive requirements from runtime state?** Memorized identities and preservation values cause documented transfer errors (§4.1). A next experiment could compare explicit runtime-binding construction strategies against the existing generator on frozen reuse panels and witnesses.
2. **Can informative feedback make gating outperform neutral continuation?** Formal gate feedback contains no failed checks or entity identifiers (§3.4). A next experiment could branch from the same openings with generic rejection, check-specific evidence, and neutral feedback.
3. **How much test coverage predicts execution reliability?** Replay witnesses catch failures missed by main panels, while the delivery association uses a selected cohort (§§4.1–4.2). A next experiment could vary panel composition and test predictive performance on held-out families.
4. **Do the findings transfer across models and environments?** Reported experiments use two model configurations and four service replicas. A next experiment could repeat the paired protocol with additional model families and services while preserving the evidence contract.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in summarizing the supplied results; **medium** in their broader implications.
