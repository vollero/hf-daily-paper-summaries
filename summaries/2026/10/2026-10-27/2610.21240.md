# Representation Redistribution in Sparse Autoencoders

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21240) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21240)


## One-sentence takeaway
RSS identifies sparse feature changes that recur across prompt variants and explain substantial variation in frozen safety readouts, while showing that representation change alone does not establish decision failure.

## Problem and motivation
Prompt transformations intended to preserve a request can redistribute activity across SAE features. Tracking individual features misses broader changes, while aggregate distances do not identify which features contribute. The authors identify two gaps: establishing whether changes persist beyond the variants used to discover them, and distinguishing persistent changes from downstream effects and errors (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Readout-independent feature selection:** RSS ranks features by average absolute activation change, filters for recurrence and directional consistency, and retains the largest eligible responses. Selected supports and directions remain fixed during evaluation on excluded variants; MeanRSS summarizes support response magnitude (§3.1).
- **Exact readout accounting:** For a fixed linear readout, the authors factor signed margin erosion into response magnitude, readout weight, selected direction, and directional alignment. Combining erosion with the full clean margin separates representation change from threshold crossing (§§3.2–3.3; Supplement S1).
- **Empirical validation:** The paper evaluates redistribution across pretrained SAEs, then tests contribution retention, logit reconstruction, readout-only restoration, and offline review utility in frozen safety probes (§4). A supplementary augmentation experiment reports mixed evidence for training benefits (Supplement S6).

## Evidence and main results
Unless stated otherwise, paired functional results below are **Gemma 3 4B / Llama 3.1 8B**. The safety study uses 1,800 balanced WildGuardMix prompts split 1,200/300/300 for training/validation/test. Five variants select supports; ten excluded realizations of the same operators evaluate them. Recurrent failure means at least two negative held-out margins. The filtered cohorts contain 1,524/1,445 prompt–setting cases (§4.2).

| Selected directions persist beyond selection | Representation-study held-out response rate in the selected direction: **0.952 RSS vs 0.758** for random supports matched by size and candidate pool. | §4.1; Tables S16–S17 cited there |

The evidence supports recurring feature responses and their relevance to the tested linear computations. It does not establish that MeanRSS alone predicts failure, that RSS maximizes contribution retention, or that restoring these features changes language-model generation. MCR measures absolute averaged contribution; signed fidelity also depends on cancellation and transfer (Supplement S1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Functional conclusions concern frozen linear readouts, with the main holdout testing new realizations of the same operators. Restoration changes only readout inputs (§§3.4, 5).
- **Offline label dependence:** Failure rankings and review results use known class labels. **Analyst inference:** these results do not establish a directly deployable label-free review policy (§4.3).
- **Incomplete localization:** RSS retains only part of absolute contribution and logit variation; readout-aware selection retains more contribution (§§4.2–4.4).
- **Conditional cohort:** Functional analysis excludes clean-incorrect cases, unavailable supports, and incomplete outcomes. **Analyst inference:** its reported performance need not extend to those excluded cases (§4.2).
- **Validity of preserved requests:** The protocol fixes target spans and transformation rules, but the supplied text does not report independent semantic-equivalence validation (Supplement S3).
- **Training extension:** The authors explicitly conclude that augmentation results do not establish a consistent AUROC advantage over uniform sampling (Supplement S6).

## Open research questions
1. **Does functional relevance transfer to unseen operators?** Main functional holdouts reuse operators. Next experiment: exclude entire operators during selection and measure residual-margin AUROC and reconstruction.
2. **Do selected changes affect generated behavior?** Restoration is confined to the readout. Next experiment: intervene on corresponding model representations and measure behavioral outcomes alongside probe changes.
3. **Can audit utility survive without known labels?** Current residual margins use class signs. Next experiment: evaluate a label-free ranking against the same held-out errors.
4. **When does MeanRSS-guided augmentation help?** Training AUROC gains are inconsistent. Next experiment: repeat matched-budget augmentation across more seeds and readouts, reporting uncertainty across runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported core findings within their stated scope; generalization remains uncertain.

The supplied text omits the bodies of supplementary tables, leaving many confidence intervals, checkpoint specifications, clean-probe results, and ablation values unavailable. Exact transformation templates, independent semantic-preservation checks, and the contents of the claimed code release are also unavailable.
