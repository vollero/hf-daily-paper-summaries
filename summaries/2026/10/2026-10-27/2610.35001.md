# More Memory, Better Videos? A Controlled Study of Visual Memory in Long-Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35001) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35001)


## One-sentence takeaway
In this controlled long-video testbed, retrieving visual history improves entity recall and historical resemblance but reduces precision and forbidden-entity avoidance, showing that continuity alone does not establish current-story correctness (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Visual memory can restore returning characters and objects, yet also revive deceased characters or preserve outdated outfits. The authors identify a measurement gap: retrieval relevance and visual consistency do not distinguish useful historical information from obsolete states, unwanted entities, or appearance information already supplied by the prompt (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Two-stage diagnostic framework:** VMem-Bench separates reference selection on 92 real films comprising 16,548 segments (Track-A) from closed-loop generation on 30 authored stories comprising 2,824 segments (Track-B). The tracks use different instances (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Controlled policy comparison:** Four retrieval policies share a FLUX.2 keyframe compositor and Wan2.2 video generator, with no-memory and privileged gold-ID last-seen controls. Each policy builds its own generated history, so comparisons concern complete policies (§4.1; App. C.3).
- **Distinct utility measurements:** The study measures entity presence, historical appearance continuity, current-state correctness, and exclusion separately. An appearance-stripping intervention tests whether long-gap success depends on prompt information (§§3.3, 4.2).
- **Reusable evaluation assets:** The paper provides dataset and code links, frozen scoring protocols, and reproduction instructions. It contributes a diagnostic study rather than a newly trained generator or validated memory controller (Abstract; Apps. C.7, H.2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval improves presence but worsens exclusion | Across four retrieval policies on the shared generator, pooled character/prop recall rises from **0.559 to 0.628–0.693**; precision falls from **0.977 to 0.915–0.938**; Avoid-OK falls from **0.968 to 0.845–0.898**. | §§4.2–4.3; Table 3; Fig. 3 |
| Seg+DINO-key demonstrates the trade-off | Relative to no memory: recall **+0.133** (95% CI **+0.111 to +0.155**), precision **−0.057** (**−0.066 to −0.048**), Avoid-OK **−0.114** (**−0.132 to −0.098**). Intervals use paired story bootstrap over the same authored cohort. | §4.3 |
| Long-gap success depends strongly on prompt content | On **62** long-gap character returns, stripping appearance descriptions lowers no-memory presence from **0.984 to 0.565**. Seg+DINO-key then adds **0.016**, with its 95% CI spanning zero. | §4.2; Table 4 |
| Historical resemblance can reward stale states | On **61** changed character returns after at least **30 segments**, historical DINOv3 similarity rises from **0.19** by **0.15–0.20** with retrieval. Similarity conditions on detection in both appearances. | §4.3; Table 5 |
| Reference coverage and precision favor different policies | On BlenderOpenMovies/LSMDC respectively, Seg+DINO-key achieves reference recall **0.923/0.849**, but precision **0.579/0.484**, versus Text-frame’s **0.692/0.630** precision. Text-frame has the best F1, **0.817/0.670**. | §4.4; Table 7 |
| Aggregate directions have partial robustness | A second judge supports recall gains and Avoid-OK losses for all four policies, but precision losses for only three. Replacing Wan with H3 while retaining FLUX keyframes preserves all three Seg+DINO-key effects on eight stories. | §4.6; Tables 21–22 |

The evidence supports a policy-level trade-off under the tested settings. It does **not** isolate the causal effect of individual retrieved frames, establish a memory-capacity scaling law, or demonstrate that better Track-A retrieval predicts better Track-B generation. Those tracks use unmatched instances (§4.4; App. H.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ internal-validity limits:** Histories diverge across policies; last-seen uses privileged IDs and a variable reference budget. One rollout per story means confidence intervals omit generation-seed variability (App. H.3).
- **Authors’ measurement limits:** Presence can count a description-matching stranger. Identity metrics exclude undetected returns and may reward obsolete appearances. Judge choice changes absolute rates and some stress-level conclusions (§§3.3, 4.6; App. D.2).
- **Authors’ external-validity limits:** Authored stories do not establish genre representativeness. The main keyframe-first pipeline may pull later-needed content into the first frame; alternative-backend coverage is narrow (App. C.3; App. H.3).
- **Audit limits:** Failure attribution comes from one author examining selected units from an earlier rollout; reference/output co-occurrence is observational, not causal (App. G).
- **Analyst inference:** The scarcity of unchanged long-gap character returns prevents a clean estimate of long-range identity recovery independent of state changes and descriptive prompts (§4.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does stale reference content directly cause state errors?** Divergent histories leave this unresolved. Branch from an identical prefix and swap current-state versus stale-state references while fixing count and generator state (App. H.1).
- **Can memory recover unchanged identities over long gaps without descriptive prompts?** Existing long-gap character returns mostly change appearance. Construct balanced gap-by-state-by-prompt-detail probes and score presence and identity separately (§4.2).
- **Can state-aware filtering improve exclusion without sacrificing recall?** Proposed remedies are unvalidated. Compare filtered and unfiltered references under matched histories and budgets, reporting all three policy outcomes (App. H.2).
- **How stable are the effects across seeds and generators?** Current uncertainty excludes rollout variability. Repeat the cohort across generation seeds and broader reference-conditioning architectures (App. H.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported aggregate trade-off within this testbed; lower in causal mechanisms and broad generalization.

The supplied extraction omits many table bodies, leaving complete per-policy results, calibration values, and some confidence intervals unavailable. It also does not establish public download availability for all frozen artifacts. Multi-seed outcomes and fixed-history reference interventions were not measured (App. C.7; App. H).
