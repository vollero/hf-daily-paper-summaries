# World-in-Context: Interactive Video World Modeling with In-Context Conditioning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33169) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33169)


## One-sentence takeaway
World-in-Context combines view, action, and camera tokens with control-aware temporal attention, reporting competitive WBench navigation and strong perspective switching after targeted switching supervision (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Interactive video models must coordinate viewing perspective, character actions, and camera motion. The authors identify three gaps: perspectives often remain implicit in captions, camera trajectories substitute for distinct character actions, and separate conditioning pathways coordinate controls only indirectly. These issues matter because identical camera motion can have different meanings in free-camera, first-person, and third-person settings (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified conditioning:** In-Context Conditioning (ICC) prepends explicit view, action, and camera tokens to visual latents in a shared self-attention sequence. World-in-Context uses a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B flow-matching backbone (§§4.1, 5.1).
- **Explicit interaction structure:** Learned view tokens distinguish Free, Fpp, and Tpp regimes; separate action and camera tokens represent character actuation and viewpoint motion. Experiments examine emergent switching and complementary control roles (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Control-aware temporal attention:** Interaction-Transport RoPE (IT-RoPE) accumulates ordered action and camera transformations between frames and adds gated, centered corrections to standard spatiotemporal attention (§4.4; Appendix A.3).
- **Extensions:** Causal generation adds initial and recent historical latents to the context; a gameplay variant adds firing, crouching, and walking tokens. Gameplay evidence is qualitative (§6).

## Evidence and main results
All figures below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive navigation at smaller model size | WBench Navigation average: World-in-Context-5B **81.5**, Zing-0.5-5B **81.0**, EchoWM-22B **81.6**. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Strong supervised perspective switching | WBench Fpp↔Tpp success: **93.8%**, versus **18.8%** for the best listed 5B baseline, DreamX-World; default training includes **500 switching sequences**. | Table 1; §§4.5, 5.2 |
| Switching emerges before switching supervision | Single-view-trained model: **37.5%** WBench switching success versus **6.25%** for text-only view specification; adding **500 transition sequences** raises success to **93.8%**. | §4.2; Figure 3 |
| Action and camera conditioning complement each other | On WBench Navigation excluding Tpp, joint training obtains translation accuracy **89.3**, rotation accuracy **90.6**, and geometric consistency **90.8**. Action-only improves translation/rotation over camera-only by **6.5/3.9 points**; camera-only improves geometric/photometric consistency by **2.1/1.9 points**. | §4.3; Figure 5; Appendix B.2 |
| Ordered transport gives modest controlled gains | With fixed view+action+camera inputs before switching consolidation, IT-RoPE versus 3D-RoPE improves Navigation **80.5→81.2** and Interaction **86.2→86.8**; endpoint-only encoding scores **80.5/86.6**, respectively. | §5.4; Figure 12; Appendix B.3 |
| Better data efficiency than additive injection in controlled comparisons | ICC with **50K navigation+250 switching clips** reaches **75.0%** switching success versus additive injection’s **71.9%** with **100K+500**. ICC at **100K+500** also exceeds additive injection at **125K+750**: Navigation **81.5 vs. 80.3**, switching **93.8% vs. 78.1%**. | §5.5; Figure 13 |

The controlled comparisons support benefits from shared conditioning and accumulated control transitions within the authors’ setup. The headline switching result requires switching supervision; it is separate from the weaker emergent result. Main-table comparisons do not isolate architecture from training-data differences. Causal benchmark scores establish task performance, while long-horizon landmark retention and gameplay transfer receive qualitative support.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The report emphasizes conditioning algorithms rather than a comprehensive system. Competing models’ training-data scales are insufficiently disclosed for matched cross-method data comparisons (§7).
- **Annotation constraints:** Actions are mainly inferred from estimated camera trajectories. The authors acknowledge difficult action discretization in Tpp and camera-estimation artifacts; “joint training” therefore mixes action-only, camera-only, joint, and unconditioned samples (Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Analyst inference—control independence:** Camera-derived action labels limit how strongly the experiments establish independently grounded character actuation.
- **Analyst inference—evaluation breadth:** Quantitative evidence centers on WBench. Gameplay transfer uses qualitative examples, including realistic environments initialized from synthetic images; it does not establish performance on real recorded interactions (§6.2).
- **Reporting gaps:** The supplied text gives no uncertainty estimates or repeated-run statistics, preventing assessment of small score differences. Table 2 and Table 3 bodies, along with numerical mixture schedules shown only in figures, are unavailable in the retrieved text.

## Open research questions
1. **Does complementarity persist with independently measured actions?** Most action labels derive from camera motion. Compare camera-inferred labels against synchronized engine telemetry while varying character and camera motion independently.
2. **How reliable is causal memory over longer revisits?** Loop closure is demonstrated qualitatively (§6.1). Evaluate landmark and layout retention across increasing rollout lengths and revisit delays.
3. **What is the computational cost of richer context?** The paper describes efficient transport factorization but supplies no measured latency or memory results. Benchmark ICC against additive injection and AdaLN across control-token counts and history lengths.
4. **How broadly do gameplay controls transfer?** Training uses a single map and transfer evidence is qualitative (§6.2). Measure action fidelity and scene consistency on held-out maps and recorded real-world inputs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and reported comparisons, but independent reliability and reproducibility remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes training hyperparameters and compute, inference latency and memory, repeated-run variability, exact evaluation subset sizes, full data-source and conditioning-mixture proportions, gameplay training-set size and quantitative results, and code/checkpoint/data release availability.
