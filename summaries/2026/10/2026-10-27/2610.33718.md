# RoboJev: An Open Vision-Language Judge for Robot Success Detection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33718) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33718)


## One-sentence takeaway
RoboJev improves robot success detection over its matched-input base model on held-out SO-101 runs, but does not establish better transfer to unseen domains, where calibration also deteriorates.

## Problem and motivation
Robot manipulation videos often lack machine-readable success labels, limiting policy evaluation, episode review, and terminal-reward construction. The authors target a reusable, task-conditioned detector instead of separate classifiers for each task and environment, with explicit input protocols and inspectable predictions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Episode judge:** RoboJev-27B takes a task instruction, ten evenly spaced primary-camera frames, and available final auxiliary-camera views. It scores `false` and `true` tokens in one forward pass, then applies released biases and temperature scaling to produce a terminal-success probability (§3.1).
- **Adaptation recipe:** The authors adapt JEV-27B-VL using language-component rank-32 LoRA while freezing the vision encoder and merger. Training uses 52,598 episode records, supervised binary or soft targets, source balancing, augmentations, and a base-model KL penalty restricted to selected research sources (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Reusable release and evaluation:** Contributions are the open-weight judge, training/inference recipe, and evaluation artifact containing 20,628 predictions across 29 model/protocol entries. The paper explicitly does not claim a new architecture or demonstrate downstream policy-learning gains (§2; §4.2; §6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptation improves SO-101 performance | On 268 held-out runs, RoboJev achieves 95.2% balanced accuracy and 0.993 AUROC versus 88.1% and 0.979 for JEV with identical inputs. Paired balanced-accuracy gain: 7.08 percentage points, 95% interval [3.04, 11.45]. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| Entirely withheld tasks remain harder | On 80 episodes from two withheld SO-101 tasks, RoboJev reaches 92.50% balanced accuracy versus 91.25% for the matched-input base. Four-frame Astra and Sol each reach 96.25%, under a different input protocol. | Appendix B.1 |
| No demonstrated transfer improvement | Across 459 episodes from eight unseen domains, RoboJev obtains 0.890 AUROC and 78.2% balanced accuracy versus 0.900 and 79.4% for the matched-input base. Paired AUROC difference: −0.0092, 95% interval [−0.0218, 0.0026]. | §5.2; Table 3 |
| Strong accuracy on clear community outcomes | RoboJev labels 126/135 episodes correctly: 93.3% accuracy, 0.984 AUROC. Four-frame Opus achieves 94.1% accuracy. Another 65 ambiguous community clips are excluded from binary metrics. | §5.3; Table 4 |
| Calibration changes substantially by domain | RoboJev’s ten-bin ECE/Brier score is 0.032/0.033 on SO-101, 0.148/0.162 on unseen domains, and 0.027/0.052 on clear community episodes. | §5.5; Fig. 5 |
| Anchoring and temporal evidence have supporting diagnostics | Unseen-domain AUROC is 0.890 for the released anchored variant versus 0.847 without anchoring; each uses one training run. Final-frame-only inference yields 0.776 AUROC for the released model. | §5.4 |
| Local scoring has a low reported compute cost | Serial inference on one H100 takes a median 0.257 seconds per episode, implying approximately $0.192 per 1,000 predictions at $2.69/GPU-hour, excluding media processing and serving overhead. | §6; Eq. 3 |

The strongest evidence is the matched-input adaptation comparison: gains are clear on the full SO-101 split, while unseen-domain improvement is unsupported. Frontier comparisons use fewer frames and cannot isolate model capability. The anchoring diagnostic is suggestive rather than replicated evidence. Reported arithmetic was recomputed from released records; this does not independently verify training provenance or all historical inference calls (Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Evaluation reuse:** The authors inspected published test sets during model development and used related benchmarks in exploratory experiments; these are not fully blind, prospective tests (§3.3; §7).
- **Limited generalization evidence:** The full SO-101 split includes familiar tasks and environments. Unseen-domain testing covers eight domains; episode bootstrap intervals do not account for within-domain correlation or uncertainty over new labs (§§4.1, 4.3, 7).
- **Label uncertainty:** Community training targets are teacher-generated, with teachers overlapping the API baseline panel. Community test labels come from one annotator without agreement measurement, and ambiguous clips are excluded (§§3.2, 4.1, 7).
- **Observability and robustness:** Occlusion, premature video endings, and sparse sampling can hide decisive events. Wrist-camera and long-horizon tasks remain difficult; instruction-paraphrase sensitivity was not tested (§7).
- **Reproducibility and provenance:** Training inputs were not completely pinned, the public training manifest is incomplete, and a community-record count discrepancy remains unexplained. Licensing and availability are incomplete for some sources (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** High accuracy on the clear community subset cannot establish performance on ambiguous outcomes or all community videos; the paper explicitly declines that extrapolation (§5.3).
- **Unvalidated downstream use:** Terminal-reward integration exists, but policy-learning benefits and closed-loop behavior were not evaluated (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Does adaptation help across genuinely new labs?** The matched-input transfer comparison shows no established gain. Next experiment: preregister new lab/robot holdouts and repeat base, anchored, and unanchored training comparisons across seeds.
- **What independently drives gains from temporal inputs?** Historical frame-count comparisons also change training data or prompts (§5.4). Next experiment: hold checkpoint, prompt, and episodes fixed while varying frame count and auxiliary views.
- **Can calibration and abstention handle uncertain outcomes?** Transfer calibration worsens, and ambiguous clips receive no binary performance assessment. Next experiment: collect multiple human judgments and test local recalibration plus abstention on clear and ambiguous episodes.
- **Does the judge improve learned policies without reward exploitation?** Integration alone does not answer this (§6). Next experiment: compare policy training with and without its terminal reward, using independent human outcome evaluation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-artifact findings and their stated scope; lower in broad deployment generalization.

Material missing evidence includes repeated-seed results, fully matched frontier comparisons, independent blind evaluation, inter-annotator agreement, paraphrase robustness, downstream policy-learning outcomes, and a complete reconstructable training manifest and license chain.
