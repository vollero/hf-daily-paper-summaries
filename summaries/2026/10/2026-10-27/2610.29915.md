# CausalAlign: Causal Alignment of Video Generation Models for Consistent Ego-Motion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29915) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29915)


## One-sentence takeaway
CausalAlign uses probes and interventions to locate weakening ego-motion representations, then aligns those locations with learned 3D-teacher transition tokens, improving Wan2.2’s measured geometric consistency and downstream Fast-WAM success (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Video generators can produce plausible images while camera trajectories drift or jitter. The authors identify a gap between representing camera motion internally and retaining its influence through later denoising stages; inconsistent geometry may also impair action prediction when video generators support robotic policies (§1).

Their diagnosis calls this pattern “geometric state forgetting”: ego-motion decodability and intervention effects rise and then decline across layers and denoising progress (§4.1). This is an interpretation supported by their measurements, rather than proof that all motion information disappears.

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnose alignment locations.** Relative-pose probes and four hidden-state interventions measure motion accessibility and influence. Declines in their combined profiles select the layers and denoising locations for supervision (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Learn and transfer motion transitions.** A frozen LingBot-Map teacher supplies consecutive geometric states. Learned delta tokens encode their transitions, with structured motion, geometry, and visual regions. Projectors map generator transitions into this space; pose and token losses then train selected LoRA adapters (§3.3; §4.3; Appendix C.1). The contribution adapts an existing delta-token idea rather than introducing delta tokens themselves (§1).
- **Demonstrate deployment and policy transfer.** Auxiliary teacher modules are removed at inference. The adapted generator also initializes Fast-WAM’s video expert for mobile manipulation (§§3.4–3.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified interpretation:** Linear and small-MLP probe failures do not establish that every nonlinear representation is unrecoverable. Whole-state interventions can also affect appearance; motion-specific controls address this concern partially (Appendix A.3).
- **Author-stated metric boundary:** Motion proximity measures closeness to real-data mean speeds, not agreement with the full motion distribution (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Trajectory-conditioned baselines receive additional inputs, so the main ranking does not isolate method quality under identical conditioning. The authors explicitly separate these protocols (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Geometry conclusions depend on frozen reconstruction estimates. Some representation and placement studies also use teacher-derived geometry as references, leaving accuracy against independent physical measurements unresolved (§4.2; Appendices C.1–C.2).
- **Observed tradeoffs:** A full-spatial-grid teacher control outperforms delta tokens on several geometry/visual readouts; temporal-modulation benefits are uncertain; stronger combined objectives need not improve every metric group (Appendices C.1–C.3).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Experiments cover Wan variants and one downstream policy framework, without establishing transfer to unrelated generator families or physical robots.

## Open research questions
- **Does target selection generalize across architectures?** Evidence centers on Wan variants. Repeat diagnosis and matched adapter-placement experiments on an unrelated video generator.
- **Does better geometric retention cause the policy improvement?** Fast-WAM transfer improves, but does not isolate the mediating representation. Compare matched checkpoints with similar visual quality and differing measured motion retention.
- **Does alignment preserve motion diversity?** Mean-speed proximity does not measure distribution coverage. Compare complete motion distributions and diversity across repeated generations.
- **Are timestep-specific updates necessary?** Temporal-modulation confidence intervals include zero. Run a larger matched study with more scenes and seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and headline comparisons, but leaves material details unavailable: most complete table entries, main-result uncertainty and seed counts, exact generation lengths and resolutions, optimizer and compute budgets, full module architectures and LoRA settings, and verified checkpoint/code availability. The diagnostic subset is excluded from evaluation, but its precise relationship to the stated dataset splits is not fully specified.
