# Skill^{infty}: Recursive Self-Improvement for Agent Skill Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35434) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434)


## One-sentence takeaway
Skill^{infty} improves an editable skill optimizer using its own procedure, then reuses it to achieve 60.7% pass rate on 83 SkillsBench tasks versus 55.9% without optimizer self-improvement and 56.4% with human-curated skills (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Agent skills package reusable instructions, scripts, and references, but the authors argue that existing optimizers largely retain fixed optimization procedures or evolve task-bound meta-skills. This limits reuse of improvements to the optimizer itself. They also identify a deployment mismatch when optimization injects skills into context instead of testing whether agents discover and load them independently (§1).

The goal is to improve both task skills and the procedure that produces them, while sharing the resulting optimizer across tasks.

## Approach and contributions
- **Recursive optimization of a skill-based optimizer.** A meta agent follows an editable meta-skill to diagnose execution traces, propose candidates, evaluate them, obtain independent reviews, and select revisions. The same procedure optimizes the meta-skill itself; its reward is the average improvement it produces in inner task-skill optimizations (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Reusable optimization knowledge.** The meta-skill is refined across skill optimization tasks from different domains, then reused for subsequent task skills, amortizing its refinement cost (§3.4).
- **Optimization under native discovery.** Agents initially see skill names and descriptions and decide whether to load them. Descriptions, instructions, scripts, and references are editable; scoring remains outside the self-improvement process (§§3.3–3.5).

These are the authors’ proposed distinctions from prior work. The supplied evidence supports improvements within this framework, rather than unrestricted recursive capability growth.

## Evidence and main results

SkillsBench uses avg@5: the proportion of five trials per task with positive reward. The authors state that reported results are averaged over five independent runs (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported overall SkillsBench pass rate | On 83 tasks across eight domains, Codex CLI + GPT-6 Luna: **60.7%**, versus human-curated **56.4%**, SkillEvolver **54.5%**, and no skill **45.5%**; default budget capped at four candidates per task skill. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 referenced |
| Refined meta-skill improves over its seed | Same SkillsBench setting: **60.7%** with self-improvement versus **55.9%** using the seed meta-skill. | §4.3 |
| Better correctness-weighted kernel speedup | Three KernelBench level-3 problems, Codex CLI + GPT-6 Luna, NVIDIA A100: mean speedup over PyTorch **1.62**, versus SkillEvolver **1.18** and no skill **1.02**; incorrect implementations receive zero. | §§4.1–4.2; Fig. 3 |
| Advantage persists in two alternative configurations | SkillsBench avg@5: replacing the model with GPT-5.6 Luna gives **66.5%** versus human-curated **61.2%**; replacing the harness with OpenCode gives **56.9%** versus **53.3%**. | §4.4; Fig. 4 |
| Larger search budgets help a selected subset | On tasks selected because the default budget substantially improves their seeds, avg@5 rises from **47.5%** with one candidate to **82.5%** with eight; no skill scores **12.5%**. | §4.6; Fig. 5 |
| Optimization has substantial token cost | Mean per task skill across 83 tasks: **8.7M input / 66k output tokens**, versus SkillEvolver **8.1M / 76k**. Self-improvement adds **less than 15%** when spread across those tasks. Input counts include cached tokens; test trials are excluded. | §4.6; Appendix E caption |

The results support the usefulness of the refined meta-skill in the tested settings. Appendix C additionally documents a changed selection rule and corresponding changes in candidate-selection behavior. They do not establish sustained improvement over many recursive generations, universal harness portability, or statistical significance: uncertainty estimates and significance tests are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The backbone remains fixed, so improvements are bounded by what skill packages can express. Reliable automatic rewards are required. Procedure adherence depends on the model following long instructions, without tool-enforced compliance (Limitations).
- **Author-stated deployment concern:** Skills can contain executable scripts and activate without user approval; the authors recommend reviewing their behavior before deployment (Ethical Considerations).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** The seed-versus-refined ablation tests the refinement package as a whole. Separate effects of independent review, adaptive budgeting, description optimization, and individual procedural changes are not isolated in the supplied experiments (§§4.3–4.6).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generality:** KernelBench covers only three problems, and budget scaling uses a deliberately selected subset. These findings do not establish broad kernel coverage or representative scaling (§§4.1, 4.6).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and split uncertainty:** Training and validation interfaces are described, but exact meta-training task identities and their overlap with final evaluation tasks are unavailable. Table bodies and complete implementation artifacts are also absent from the retrieved text.

## Open research questions
1. **Does improvement continue across repeated recursive cycles?** Self-improvement permits at most four meta-skill candidates (Appendix B). A next experiment could extend successive refinement cycles and evaluate each generation on a fixed, held-out task set.
2. **Which optimizer components drive the gains?** The reported ablation replaces the entire refined meta-skill (§4.3). Matched-budget experiments could independently vary review, selection rules, adaptive budgeting, and description editing.
3. **How well does optimization knowledge transfer to unseen domains?** Cross-domain refinement is described, but exact domain separation is unavailable (§3.4). Leave-one-domain-out meta-training could measure transfer and negative transfer.
4. **How sensitive is optimization to imperfect verifiers?** Reliable rewards are an explicit prerequisite (Limitations). Controlled experiments with noisy or incomplete verifiers could measure erroneous candidate acceptance and downstream regressions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline comparisons are clearly described, but evidence completeness limits assessment.

Material missing information includes full table values, uncertainty estimates, exact meta-training tasks and splits, budget-scaling subset composition, complete reviewer policy and scripts, harness/model version details, and confirmation of released refined skills or code.
