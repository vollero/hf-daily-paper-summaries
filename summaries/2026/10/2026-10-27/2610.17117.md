# Gluing Local Contexts into Global Meaning: A Sheaf-Theoretic Decomposition of Transformer Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17117) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17117)


## One-sentence takeaway

A paraphrase-based spectral decomposition extracts fact-discriminating subspaces from frozen transformers, while a separate multilayer harmonic-mass diagnostic correlates with steering fragility across six architectures (§§5.2–5.4).

## Problem and motivation

Transformer activations mix semantic content with phrasing. The authors argue that existing feature decomposition, probing, and representation-similarity tools do not directly test whether information remains consistent across meaning-preserving context changes. They seek a common framework for identifying stable content, locating influential variation, and diagnosing architecture-dependent steering failures (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Content decomposition:** Project paired activations through a shared map \(P\), then eigendecompose their projected difference second-moment matrix \(P^\top C_WP\). Bottom eigenvectors approximate invariant readouts; top eigenvectors capture paraphrase variation. This analyzes frozen models without updating their weights (§3).
- **Retrieval and intervention evidence:** Test low-energy subspaces against LEACE and classical retrieval baselines; test high-variation coordinates and spectral subspaces through activation ablations. Joint paraphrase constraints improve retrieval, but cycle closure itself provides no established additional benefit (§§5.2–5.3).
- **Depth-based diagnostic:** Construct multilayer paraphrase grids and measure the harmonic energy of an edge signal incorporating fitted Procrustes transports. Its architecture ranking tracks steering fragility (§§4.1, 5.4).
- **Training extension:** Explore covariance-regularized consistency training. The appendix finds no consistent benefit from the specific transition and cycle terms beyond simpler multilayer projections (Appendices B.14–B.15).

The mathematical distinction matters: with shared \(P\) and identity transports, the empirical operator reduces to projected difference covariance. Its top eigenspace, called “\(H^1\) directions,” is not algebraic first cohomology; the matching graph has algebraic \(H^1=0\). The practical “\(H^0\)” uses low-energy eigenvectors rather than necessarily an exact kernel (§3).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stable subspaces improve held-out retrieval | On 500 CounterFact facts split 70/30, with 150 test facts and same-relation distractors, 20D \(H^0\) beats full-dimensional LEACE-preserved representations across eight architectures: mean **+17.1 percentage points**; versus LEACE-20D, mean **+48.0 points**. | §5.2; Table 2 |
| Kernel selection matters more than the projection alone | Same hard retrieval protocol: eight-model mean accuracy is **81.6%** with PCA-\(P\), **74.2%** with random-\(P\), and **77.5%** with identity-\(P\), versus **30.5%** for PCA-20 without kernel selection. | §5.2; Appendix B.4, Table 11 |
| Joint constraints help; cycles are not established as the cause | Rings improve hard retrieval over pair-only construction by mean **+6.5 points** across eight architectures. In an edge-count-matched subset control, ring-minus-path averages **+0.5 points**, with confidence intervals including zero on every architecture. | §5.2; Appendix B.3, Table 10 |
| Variable coordinates have greater ablation effects | Across nine models, output-change ratios are **5.6–26.5×** versus controls selected for similar per-coordinate variability. Protocols differ: 1,000 sentences/64 coordinates/logit change, or 100 sentences/20 coordinates/final-hidden-state change, reporting the best of three depths. | §5.3; Table 3; Figure 2 |
| Spectral subspace ablation also changes predictions | Removing top-20 eigenvector subspaces produces **17.8–398×** the next-token KL of equal-dimensional random subspaces, as layer-median ratios across nine models on 100 held-out MRPC sentences; random controls are not activation-energy matched. | §5.3; Appendix B.6, Table 13 |
| Harmonic mass tracks architecture fragility | Two-layer grids built from 30 MRPC pairs at edge dimension 8 yield masses around **\(10^{-4}\)–0.08**. Across six architectures, the reported fragility ranking correlation is **\(\rho=0.90\)**, exact one-sided **\(p=0.014\)**; fragility uses CounterFact random steering at relative norm 0.3. | §§4.1, 5.4; Table 1; Appendix A.3 |
| Steering gains depend on architecture | CounterFact, 1,000 prompts/model, relative norm 0.3: Llama-2-7B contrastive sheaf preserves **31.0%** of facts versus random **4.2%**. Mistral-7B CCA sheaf obtains **41.6%** versus **39.4%**, a statistical tie (**p=0.19**). | §5.4; Table 4 |
| Explicit sheaf training terms do not consistently help | STS-B seed-paired Spearman differences for MLST-section versus the simpler no-transition/no-cycle concatenation are **−0.010**, **p=0.0009**, on GPT-2. Authors characterize transition/cycle contributions across GPT-2 and Mistral-7B as neutral to slightly negative. | Appendix B.15; Table 18 |

The evidence supports useful invariance-based readouts and intervention-sensitive variation. It does not establish a complete semantic-content/surface-form separation, universal steering superiority, or a validated prospective fragility predictor. Algebraic cycles alone do not demonstrate model-specific semantic structure.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Restriction maps depend on architecture and domain. On held-out hard CounterFact retrieval, out-of-domain maps average 55–60% versus 81.6% in-domain across eight architectures (Appendix B.1).
- **Author-stated:** Coordinate controls remove less total variability than the selected coordinates; eigenbasis random controls lack activation-energy matching. These confounds limit causal interpretation (§5.3; Appendix B.6).
- **Author-stated:** The diagnostic covers only six architectures. Relation-hypergraph holonomy does not significantly exceed shuffled nulls, and its node-space features fail to improve retrieval (§7; Appendices B.12–B.13).
- **Analyst inference:** Changed output is a weak style-success measure: the paper scores any departure from baseline as style change. Entity inclusion likewise does not establish full factual correctness (§5.4).
- **Comparison/reproducibility ambiguity:** PIXEL is simplified, and Appendix B.11 specifies 100 generated tokens while §5.4 specifies 50. The supplied text does not reconcile this discrepancy.

## Open research questions

1. **Does harmonic mass predict unseen models?** The evidence is a small architecture-ranking study. Next experiment: preregister the diagnostic and test held-out architectures across perturbation strengths (§7).
2. **How much influence remains after matching removed activation energy?** Existing controls leave acknowledged mismatches. Next experiment: compare spectral ablations with controls matched jointly on dimension and removed energy (§5.3; Appendix B.6).
3. **Can cycles provide value beyond simpler consistency methods?** Cycle closure adds no significant retrieval gain, and training terms show no consistent advantage. Next experiment: ablate topology and transports under identical data and regularization on harder equivalence tasks (Appendices B.3, B.15).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text describes methods, controls, and negative findings clearly, but most numbered tables contain captions without their numerical bodies. Consequently, many per-model estimates, confidence intervals, and seed-level results are unavailable. Code release is asserted but unverified; the decoding-length discrepancy and exact pair-only graph implementation remain unresolved.
