# How Far Can Coding Agents Go in Visual Generation?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19025) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19025)


## One-sentence takeaway
Coding agents generate structurally precise visuals and usable SVG representations for question answering, but their weaker naturalistic rendering and exploratory consistency probes leave the authors’ proposed internal visual representation unproven.

## Problem and motivation
Specialized image generators leave image construction implicit in learned parameters. The authors investigate whether general-purpose coding agents can instead construct images through executable programs, how drawing interfaces affect their capabilities, and whether those programs support visual understanding. They also ask whether recurring scene choices across interfaces and viewpoints indicate reusable internal scene representations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and capability study:** Introduce AgentDrawBench, combining prompts from four existing benchmarks, and evaluate zero-shot drawing through programmable and GUI interfaces using structural checklists, perceptual comparisons, and holistic preferences (§§3–4).
- **External visual representation:** Test generation without image feedback, question answering from reconstructed SVG, extended refinement, and qualitative reconstruction guided by procedures extracted from human drawing demonstrations (§5).
- **Internal-representation hypothesis:** Probe shared scene content through independent drawings across interfaces, separately queried pixel colors, and consistency of self-arranged rooms across viewpoints and context-removal conditions (§6). These are behavioral tests, not direct measurements of internal representations.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong structural adherence | On 200-prompt AgentDrawBench, GPT-6-Astra with Canvas 2D scores **85.8%** checklist pass rate, versus **68.8%** for gpt-image-1 and **90.7%** for gpt-image-2. | §1; §4, Table 1 discussion |
| Preference depends strongly on task | Against GPT-image-1, Astra/Canvas 2D achieves **92.0%** holistic preference on design/symbolic prompts, **13.0%** on scenes, **6.0%** on people/organic subjects, and **47.7%** overall. Gemini 3.5 Flash judges both image orders; ties receive half credit. | §§3.2, 4; Table 3 discussion |
| Naturalistic rendering remains weak | Against GPT-image-1, every reported configuration scores below **50%** perceptual wins on anatomy, perspective, light transport, material, and scene physics. Astra/Canvas 2D scores **77.6%** on typography. | §4; Table 2 discussion |
| Image feedback adds a modest structural gain | Astra/Canvas 2D scores **83.0%** without rendered feedback versus **85.6%** with it on AgentDrawBench. | §5.1.1 |
| SVG retains useful question-answering information | On **200 WorldBench items**, Astra exact-match accuracy is **69.0%** from SVG source, **65.0%** from its render, and **74.0%** from the original image. Reconstruction precedes disclosure of the question; answering uses fresh contexts. | §5.1.2, Fig. 3; Appendix B |
| More refinement gives little holistic benefit | A target **4×** weighted-token budget raises structural fidelity from **85.6% to 86.8%**; extended outputs achieve **50.5%** holistic preference against standard outputs. | §5.2.1 |
| Coarse appearance recurs across interfaces | On AgentDrawBench, different 2D harnesses average layout correlation/palette cosine/DreamSim distance of **0.60/0.81/0.19**, versus **0.66/0.84/0.14** for independent SVG runs and **0.01/0.31/0.77** for different-prompt controls. Lower DreamSim means greater similarity. | §6.1.1, Fig. 7 |
| Independent pixels recover coarse structure | Across **five subjects**, assembled pixel responses have luminance Moran’s I **0.85–0.96**, versus **0.01** after color shuffling; luminance correlation with whole-image Canvas 2D drawings is **0.51–0.88**. Fine details fail. | Appendix C |
| Room arrangements persist after visible SVG removal | Across **30 rooms**, deleting earlier drawings yields revisit piece-set Jaccard **0.99**, relation agreement **0.96**, wall–plan agreement **0.99** versus chance **0.31**, and box IoU **0.71**, versus **1.00** with drawings retained. Opaque reasoning history remains. | §6.2, Table 4 discussion; §7 |

These results support useful programmatic drawing and SVG-based information access under the tested conditions. The authors interpret the consistency probes as support for internal visual imagery. **Analyst inference:** recurring outputs do not uniquely identify a shared internal representation; the experiments do not isolate that explanation from other mechanisms producing consistent scene choices. Room scores measure agreement with the agent’s own arrangement, not accuracy against an external scene.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Most ablations use one primary agent; evaluation relies heavily on automated judges with limited human verification. Artistic procedural guidance receives qualitative evaluation only (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Model weights and provider internals are inaccessible. Internal-representation claims are exploratory; pixel subjects, interfaces, and transformations have limited coverage, and room experiments retain opaque reasoning history (§7).
- **Comparison limits:** Harness compute and feedback are not uniformly matched, preventing a controlled causal estimate of interface effects (Appendix A.1).
- **Statistical limits:** WorldBench domain samples are small, and domain rankings have no statistical-significance claim. Answer changes do not prove information is absent from an artifact (Appendix B).
- **Reporting/reproducibility gaps:** Supplied Tables 1–5 contain captions but no table bodies. The WorldBench selection script is untracked, although selected indices are supplied. Standard structural fidelity is reported as 85.8% in §4 and 85.6% in §5 without reconciliation. Appendix A.3 describes dollar matching, whereas §5.2.1 specifies weighted-token scaling.

## Open research questions
- **Does procedural guidance improve quality causally?** Current evidence is qualitative. Compare identical references and budgets with and without guidance, using blinded human assessment (§5.2.2; Appendix A.1).
- **What explains cross-interface consistency?** Behavioral agreement does not identify its mechanism. Test novel, explicitly randomized layouts and controlled attribute changes across fresh sessions (§6).
- **How much room coherence depends on retained reasoning?** The paper acknowledges opaque retained history. Compare full-history, reasoning-reset, and explicit scene-record conditions using externally specified arrangements (§7; Appendix D).
- **Do SVG question-answering benefits generalize?** Evidence comes from one model and a limited sample. Repeat paired evaluation across models and larger domain samples, reporting uncertainty (Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main descriptive findings, but confidence in the internal-representation interpretation is lower.

Material missing information includes full table contents, uncertainty estimates for most comparisons, complete judge coverage counts, exact retained reasoning contents, executable evaluation code, and confirmation that the promised benchmark and recorded artifacts are publicly available.
