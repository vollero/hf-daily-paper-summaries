# ThinkBit: Search Codewords, Recalibrate Scales, Select by Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34886) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34886)


## One-sentence takeaway
ThinkBit combines scalar low-bit quantization, scale recalibration, and reasoning-based checkpoint selection to improve Qwen3 reasoning per stored byte, while showing that likelihood metrics can conceal substantial reasoning losses (§§4–7).

## Problem and motivation
Long reasoning outputs make model-weight memory traffic costly. The authors ask whether a simple scalar format can preserve difficult reasoning at approximately two-bit precision, without vector codebooks, rotations, or zero-points. They identify an evaluation gap: perplexity and multiple-choice accuracy can rank quantized configurations differently from sampled reasoning accuracy (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated quantization pipeline:** assign ternary, two-bit, or three-bit tiers using KronQ sensitivity; initialize and search codewords using GPTQ and GSQ-based optimization; then recalibrate group scales and RMSNorm gains against the full-precision teacher while freezing codewords (§§2.1–2.4).
- **Optional codeword adaptation:** straight-through QAT trains latent weights, scales, and gains using output-distribution and hidden-state distillation. A longer variant adds complete reasoning traces (§2.5; §3).
- **Reasoning-based selection:** compare recalibrated and QAT checkpoints on held-out MATH training problems using paired sign-flip tests, rather than selecting solely by likelihood (§2.6).
- **Evaluation contribution:** stage ablations and whole-model byte accounting demonstrate reasoning differences hidden by standard metrics (§§6–7).

The contribution is chiefly the combined framework and its empirical evaluation; the paper explicitly builds on existing KronQ, GPTQ, GSQ, and gradient estimators (§8).

## Evidence and main results

All numerical results below are author-reported. Primary MATH-500 evaluation samples four answers per problem, with a 32k-token generation limit, and requires both a closed reasoning trace and a correct answer (§3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen-codeword recalibration substantially recovers reasoning | Qwen3-1.7B, bf16 head/embedding: sampled MATH-500 rises from 14.8 after search to 47.5 after recalibration; WikiText-2 perplexity falls from 46.91 to 19.46. | §4; Table 4 |
| Better likelihood need not mean better reasoning | Same setting: base-mixture QAT raises seven-task mean from 51.5 to 54.6, but lowers sampled MATH-500 from 47.5 to 43.0. | §§4, 7; Table 4 |
| Search and recalibration matter beyond standard metrics | Removing search, recalibration, both, or mixed allocation lowers sampled MATH-500 from 43.0 to 28.4–37.0, while seven-task means remain 53.7–54.8. | §7; Table 8 |
| Recalibration beats approximately byte-matched QAT baselines | Qwen3-1.7B Rec-h2: 46.7 sampled MATH-500 at 0.52 GB; official EfficientQAT and BitDistiller with two-bit head/embedding: 30.0 and 25.3 at 0.54 GB, trained on the same base mixture and token budget. | §§5–6; Table 7 |
| Longer reasoning-mixture QAT produces the largest gain | Qwen3-1.7B, unchanged 0.52 GB format: Rec-h2 46.7 → QAT+R-h2 73.0 sampled MATH-500; paired difference +26.3 points, 95% CI [23.3, 29.4]. | §5 |
| Larger models retain more math accuracy | Selected 14B checkpoint: 94.8 sampled MATH-500 at 3.74 GB versus bf16 97.5 at 29.5 GB; AIME@16 remains 57.7 versus 73.6. | §5; Table 6 |
| Serving gains are measured with wider containers | One H100, batch one: 8B/14B decode at 212/150 tokens/s versus bf16 146/86, using four-bit containers and bf16 head/embedding. | §6; Appendix M |

These results support improved reasoning per byte within the evaluated Qwen3 settings. They do not establish lossless compression, general superiority across tasks, or proportional speedups from native two-bit storage. The reasoning-mixture result also changes training duration and data, so its gain cannot be attributed solely to codeword adaptation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** other model families, mixture-of-experts models, sizes beyond 14B, and code/tool-use selectors remain future work (§9).
- **Author-stated deployment limit:** current kernels read four-bit containers; native two-/three-bit bandwidth benefits remain unmeasured (Appendix M).
- **Scoring sensitivity:** earlier small checkpoints beat Q2_K chiefly by finishing more traces; forced-answer evaluations tie. This limits claims about improved problem-solving independently of termination (§6; Appendix K).
- **Analyst inference—unequal optimization:** some stage ablations use fewer training tokens, and held-out KL is still falling; stage effects are not completely separated from optimization budget (§7).
- **Internal inconsistency:** §2.6 and Algorithm 2 prescribe recalibration for equal-size, nonsignificant comparisons, but §5 retains QAT+R-h2 at 14B despite a nonsignificant selector result.
- **Reproducibility:** many supplied tables contain captions without cells, and code availability is only promised (§9).

## Open research questions
- **How much gain comes from reasoning data versus longer training?** Both change together (§§3, 5). Next experiment: cross data mixture and training duration at matched token budgets.
- **Can termination improve without harming code performance?** Incomplete traces persist, while reasoning-mixture QAT reduces HumanEval at larger sizes (§§4–5). Next experiment: compare termination-aware objectives while tracking math and code jointly.
- **Does the selector generalize beyond math?** Current selection uses MATH problems (§2.6). Next experiment: evaluate separate math, code, and tool-use selectors on disjoint test suites.
- **What performance do native low-bit kernels deliver?** Current serving uses wider containers (Appendix M). Next experiment: benchmark native packed kernels against the existing implementation across batch sizes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main findings, but lacks many table entries, complete training hyperparameters and compute costs, detailed decontamination procedures, demonstrated code/checkpoint availability, and a consistent explanation of the final 14B selection rule.
