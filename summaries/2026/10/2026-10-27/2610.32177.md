# Ψ-0.6: Adaptive Streams of Thought in a Unified Vision-Language World Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32177) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32177)


## One-sentence takeaway
Ψ-0.6 unifies visual prediction, geometry, and language in one model, with reported spatial-reasoning gains from composing its predictions and transferable video-QA gains from learning which geometric intermediates to use (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Physical reasoning often requires moving between language, spatial geometry, and imagined outcomes. The authors identify a gap between specialized perception, vision-language, and world models: separate interfaces hinder composition, while fixed external tools prevent joint optimization of their predictions and the reasoning that uses them (§1).

Their goal is to make intermediate representations selectable reasoning operations within a shared model, so task feedback can improve how those operations are used.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified predictive model.** An approximately 8B-parameter autoregressive transformer processes images, text, camera poses, depth, optical flow, and point tracks through a common token interface. The work extends PSI to language-grounded reasoning; patch control and probabilistic prediction are inherited capabilities (§§2.1–2.4, B.1).
- **Composable streams of thought.** Generated geometry or images become context for subsequent predictions—for example, estimating a camera pose, selecting or generating the corresponding view, and answering a spatial question (§§2.2, 4–5).
- **Learned representation selection.** A policy selects and orders depth, camera-motion, and flow intermediates for video QA. Scored-search initialization precedes LoRA-based Dr.GRPO optimization, rewarded by reduced reference-answer loss relative to RGB-only answering (§C.3).
- **Controlled counterfactual prediction.** Motion-probability patch ordering and text-conditioned classifier-free guidance improve sparse object-motion prediction, with ablations examining their effects (§§5, A.6).

The authors frame joint optimization of intermediate selection and content as a broader benefit of unification; the documented RL experiment most directly demonstrates learned stream selection.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Composed spatial reasoning improves over direct answering | On MindCube, inverse-dynamics prompting reaches **57.7% accuracy**, versus **51.3% zero-shot**; it requires the requested viewpoint among the inputs. | §4, Fig. 4 |
| Geometry accompanies stronger fine-tuned spatial QA | On the held-out MindCube Tiny split, SFT reaches **68.7% RGB-only** and **76.3% RGB+camera** accuracy. Displayed SFT+RL comparators include 3DThinker at **76.0%** and GeoThinker at **83.0%**. | §4; §A.1 specifies direct answering |
| Strong counterfactual flow prediction | Across **622 samples**, best reported Ψ-0.6 flow accuracy is **56.4%**, versus **16.4%** for the strongest image editor and **50.8%** for the modular pipeline. Accuracy means moving-pixel relative endpoint error **≤0.4**; baseline inputs/readouts differ. | §5, Table 1 caption, §§A.3–A.4 |
| Simulation fine-tuning substantially improves counterfactual QA | FlowBench-Sim overall accuracy rises from **24.5% to 73.1%**; same-data fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B reaches **76.3%**. | Table 2, §A.5 |
| Sim-to-real transfer occurs, but trails the fine-tuned comparator | With no FlowBench-Real training examples, Ψ-0.6 overall accuracy rises from **37.5% to 51.4%** after simulation fine-tuning; fine-tuned Qwen reaches **59.6%**. | Table 3 |
| Learned selection transfers beyond its training sources | Mean **relative accuracy gains over video-only QA** are **5.6%** on held-out training-distribution examples and **9.0%** across four unseen benchmarks. Absolute scores are unavailable in the supplied figure text. | §6, Fig. 6; benchmark identities in §A.7 |
| Motion-aware decoding contributes materially | On the **622-sample flow evaluation**, pMotion raises accuracy from **31.8% to 44.2%**; adding CFG at scale **1.5** raises it to **54.0%**. | §A.6, Table 4 discussion |

These results support useful composition, learned representation selection, and simulation-to-real QA transfer in the tested settings. They do not establish universal superiority: a fine-tuned Qwen comparator leads aggregate FlowBench QA, and the authors explicitly do not isolate training-time synergy between capabilities (§7.2). Physical control remains future work (§7.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Training scale is smaller than contemporary frontier systems, and positive versus negative transfer among unified capabilities is not isolated (§7.2).
- **Author-stated:** MindCube Rotation remains near chance in some configurations; limited training-scene diversity is offered as a hypothesis (§A.1).
- **Author-stated:** Adaptive-stream experiments optimize QA, not physical control; intermediate representation types are predefined (§7.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Flow prediction evidence has a narrow scope: static cameras and sparse, single-object motions. It does not establish performance under dense motion or moving cameras (§A.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Flow comparisons require care because editors receive ground-truth masks and the modular pipeline uses privileged metric information. The authors acknowledge that the groups are not directly comparable (§A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied RL results do not isolate intermediate-content co-adaptation from selection gains, or report an equal-compute comparison against alternative additional reasoning. Caption-derived QA also leaves independent visual grounding of those training answers uncertain (§§A.7, C.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does shared training improve individual capabilities?** The authors do not isolate synergy or interference (§7.2). Train matched unified and specialized models with controlled data and compute, then compare each capability.
2. **How much benefit comes from selection versus additional computation or content adaptation?** The RL proof of concept emphasizes notation selection (§C.3). Compare learned, random, and fixed streams under equal budgets, with intermediate generation separately frozen or updated.
3. **Does the approach extend to embodied control and broader motion?** Current evidence concerns QA and sparse counterfactual motion (§§A.4, 7.3). Test closed-loop manipulation with action tokens, moving cameras, and multiple moving objects.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose and QA tables support the central findings, but important details remain unavailable:

- Full numerical contents of several tables and figures, including per-benchmark RL scores and uncertainty estimates.
- Exact evaluated checkpoints, context lengths, and inference costs for each result.
- Controlled evidence separating selection, content adaptation, and training-time synergy.
- Confirmed model/code availability; the data-and-materials availability heading is empty.
