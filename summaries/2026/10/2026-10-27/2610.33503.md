# Novel or Not? PatEx-Gym: Agentic Prior-Art Search for Novelty Assessment in Patent Examination

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33503) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33503)


## One-sentence takeaway
PatEx-Gym benchmarks iterative patent prior-art search and shows that training a 4B agent on quality-filtered synthetic trajectories improves retrieval and examination reports, while recovering and selecting examiner-cited evidence remains difficult.

## Problem and motivation
Patent examination requires finding earlier disclosures, inspecting technical details, comparing claims, and writing an evidence-grounded Office Action (OA). The authors identify a gap in benchmarks that supply relevant documents, restrict candidate pools, or evaluate these stages separately: they do not capture adaptive search coupled with examination reasoning (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and runtime:** PatEx-Bench contains 1,000 tasks based on originally filed applications and first USPTO OAs. Its searchable corpora contain 1,011,901 patents and 52,105 scientific papers. The harness supports Boolean, BM25, and dense search, document inspection, and report generation, enforcing publication before the application’s filing date (§3).
- **Trajectory synthesis and filtering:** PASS treats document visits as implicit relevance signals and turn boundaries. It ranks teacher trajectories using within-turn retrieval behavior, query progression, evidence grounding, and outcome quality. Approximately 3,000 trajectories selected from 10,000 GLM-5.3 rollouts form PatEx-SynData (§4).
- **Trained baseline:** PatEx-Agent fine-tunes Qwen3.5-4B on those trajectories, masking environment messages from the training loss. Evaluation combines OA quality, decision accuracy, statutory-ground F1, and examiner-reference recall at search, visit, and selection stages (§§3.3, 4.2, 5.1).

The authors claim the framework is the first of its kind; the supplied benchmark comparisons support its broader integrated scope but do not independently establish priority.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Strong agents still miss much examiner-cited evidence | Claude-Opus-5-max leads OA Score at 7.34/10, with Search-R 48.7%, Visit-R 35.6%, and Select-R 32.4%. | Table 2 |
| Decision accuracy gives a different ranking | Qwen3.6-27B leads decision accuracy at 87.6%, versus Claude’s 87.0%, but has lower OA Score, 5.96 versus 7.34, and Select-R, 19.5% versus 32.4%. | Table 2; §5.2 |
| Agentic search does not beat the strongest standalone retriever at the reported patent cutoff | Patent R@100 is 45.98% for PatEmbed-Large, 45.71% for Claude-Opus-5-max, and 36.36% for PatEx-Agent under the table’s matched returned-document budget. | Table 3 |
| PASS selection outperforms random trajectory sampling | With the same backbone, training settings, and approximately 3,000 trajectories, PASS yields 18.24% Select-R versus 11.96% for random sampling; rounded patent R@100 is 36.4% versus 26.6%. | §5.4.1; Figure 6 |
| Paper retrieval improves, but selection remains weaker | PatEx-Agent obtains paper Search-R 69.4%, Visit-R 37.6%, and Select-R 27.7%; Qwen3.8-Max obtains 69.6% Search-R. | Figure 5; §5.3 |

These results support training and trajectory-selection benefits within this benchmark. They do not establish comprehensive legal correctness, deployment efficiency, or generalization beyond its setting. Reference recall measures agreement with examiner citations; it does not directly evaluate the legal adequacy of alternative references.

The paper’s claim that selection improves disproportionately is also questionable: its reported relative Select-R gain is smaller than its Search-R gain. Those figures alone do not establish better conversion of retrieved references into selected evidence (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed bottlenecks:** Retrieval, inspection, and evidence selection remain difficult; stronger retrieval sometimes fails to improve final selection (§§5.2–5.4).
- **Analyst inference—label scope:** Recall rewards examiner-cited references. Figure 8 includes alternative evidence, but the paper does not establish whether such alternatives are legally sufficient.
- **Analyst inference—decision imbalance:** The benchmark contains 882 rejection and 118 allowance tasks. Consequently, decision accuracy needs class-specific interpretation; Table 2 does not provide it (Figure 2).
- **Analyst inference—judge validity:** Strong agreement among LLM judges supports ranking consistency, but independent human validation is not reported (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Random-sampling and tool ablations support the overall recipe, but individual PASS scoring dimensions and tournament ranking are not separately ablated (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference—scope and reproducibility:** Evaluation covers first USPTO OAs and scientific papers as NPL. The text lacks complete judge prompts, precise scoring implementations, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed asset-release details.

## Open research questions
- **Does PASS improve evidence conversion independently of retrieval coverage?** The reported gains do not isolate this. Compare trained and base agents using identical retrieved candidate sets, then measure inspection, selection, and expert-rated support.
- **Which PASS components drive improvement?** Only the combined filter is compared with random sampling. Ablate each scoring dimension and compare tournament ranking with simpler selection rules at a fixed training-data budget.
- **How often are uncited alternative references legally adequate?** Examiner-reference recall cannot answer this. Have patent experts blindly assess agent-selected alternatives and claim-level justification.
- **Do gains extend beyond the benchmark’s prosecution setting?** Only first USPTO OAs are evaluated. Test held-out later prosecution stages or another jurisdiction using appropriately annotated outcomes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly documents the framework and main comparisons, but legal validity and reproducibility remain less established.

Material missing information includes complete prompts and scoring formulas, standalone-retriever query construction, class-specific decision results, repeated-run variability, human legal validation, inference cost and latency, and verified access to datasets, code, and model weights.
