# RoboHarness: A Simple Harness Outperforms VLA and World Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14018) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14018)


## One-sentence takeaway
RoboHarness connects unchanged LLMs to geometric robot-control tools and reports stronger simulated manipulation results than learned-policy baselines, although missing result tables, different embodiments, and task-specific human procedures limit the comparison (§§3, 5–6).

## Problem and motivation
The authors seek to connect LLM planning and visual understanding to robot manipulation without collecting robot-action training data or adding an action head. They argue that existing callable skill libraries restrict agents to predefined behaviors, while geometric primitives could provide a more expressive interface (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Geometric control interface:** An LLM selects image keypoints and specifies spatial constraints. The harness samples candidate actions, checks inverse-kinematics feasibility, plans trajectories, and executes them; direct adjustment tools support recovery (§3.2).
- **Persistent spatial state:** Optical-flow tracking and depth back-projection convert selected image points into coordinates in a shared camera frame (§3.1; Figure 3).
- **Representational argument:** The authors present completeness propositions for grasp poses and relative poses of rigid objects. These concern geometric representation, rather than guarantees of successful execution (§4).
- **Empirical demonstration:** Unchanged LLMs use the harness on household tasks and an object-lifting evaluation. The authors describe an open-source implementation and shipped skill catalog, but the supplied text provides neither a repository link nor the catalog entries (Abstract; §§5–6; Appendix B).

The supplied prose supports the reported object-lifting range and qualitative household-task ranking. It does not supply enough numerical detail to reconstruct the full comparisons. Different embodiments and starting configurations prevent attributing the object-lifting advantage solely to the harness. The evidence also does not establish causal claims that multimodal capability matters more than parameter count or robot-data volume.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Physical grounding failures include knocking objects over, falling, and getting stuck. Household evaluations mitigate these with human-written instructions specifying procedures and behavioral boundaries (§6).
- **Evaluation scope:** Household results cover a fixed five-instance sample per task. Object tests concern lifting rigid objects, mostly filtered by size and mass, with two stated exceptions (§5; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—comparison validity:** Floor versus tabletop placement and different embodiments introduce confounds. No matched-embodiment comparison is supplied (§5.2; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No supplied ablation isolates tracking, constraints, adjustment tools, or procedural prompts.
- **Analyst inference—proof scope:** The uniqueness arguments leave geometric conditions unclear: §4.1 asserts a unique surface intersection, while §4.2 treats line coincidence through aligned direction vectors. The supplied text does not fully resolve these conditions.
- **Reproducibility:** Table bodies, exact prompts, solver settings, and executable release links are unavailable. Robot-data-hour comparisons also combine heterogeneous estimates and exclude web-video pretraining (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
1. **How much performance comes from human procedures?** Their contribution is not isolated. Compare goal-only prompts with procedural prompts under identical rollout budgets (§6).
2. **Does the advantage persist with matched embodiments and scenes?** Current object tests differ in both. Evaluate harness and learned policies on the same robot, placements, observations, and action budgets (§5.2).
3. **Which harness components drive reliability?** No component ablation is supplied. Remove persistent tracking, constraint solving, and adjustment tools separately.
4. **How robust is geometric control under imperfect sensing and contact?** Completeness arguments do not establish execution robustness. Test controlled depth noise, occlusion, and grasp slippage (§§3–4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and evaluation protocols are reasonably explicit, but quantitative superiority is only partly assessable.

Material missing information includes full result tables, model-specific lift totals, household q-score margins, uncertainty estimates, task prompts, complete tool and skill definitions, solver details, release links, and real-robot evaluation results.
