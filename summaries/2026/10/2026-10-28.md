# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-10-28

## Executive summary

- **Memory quality depends on preserving decision-relevant evidence.** HABIT-Bench (`2610.24520`), Memory Tax (`2610.34073`), and CrossProfile (`2610.37624`) question whether constructed memories improve on retrieval or retain the evidence balance needed for correct decisions.
- **Additional computation needs an explicit value test.** Effort audits (`2610.34491`), SearchHorizonBench (`2610.34714`), and h1 (`2610.35962`) examine success alongside spending, verification, and persistence. Their settings do not support a universal compute-allocation rule.
- **Future representations offer an alternative to generating video during robot control.** JAM (`2610.37219`), PDRVLA (`2610.37381`), and ThinkWorld (`2610.37666`) use predictive supervision during training while removing auxiliary prediction machinery at deployment.
- **Agent improvement remains difficult to attribute.** HaMo (`2610.34724`), AdaPRA (`2610.33245`), RSI Arena (`2610.33624`), and The Trinity (`2610.36077`) combine useful mechanisms with additional training, supervision, preparation, or selection resources.
- **Evaluation increasingly separates successful delivery from substantive correctness.** VibeVideoBench (`2610.35864`) and CodeCap-Bench (`2610.37341`) distinguish renderable artifacts from content and temporal fidelity; Omni-InteractScope (`2610.35439`) additionally tests intervention timing.
- **Efficiency claims require different accounting across methods.** SPOT’s target-pass reduction (`2610.22543`), MoE expert-read savings (`2610.37386`), and MatAnyone-E’s matting acceleration (`2610.35724`) measure different bottlenecks and cannot be ranked by headline magnitude.
- **Scientific discovery remains a demanding evaluation target.** LongChang (`2610.37649`) reports low experimentally supported discovery under free exploration, while TTME (`2610.32046`) studies the narrower setting of verified mathematical construction search.
- **Evidence is broad but incomplete.** All 51 current analyses and all 50 historical evidence items are compacted. Missing tables, uncertainty estimates, and component controls limit many numerical and causal conclusions.

## Main results worth noting

- **Memory versus retrieval — `2610.24520`.** On HABIT-Bench’s synthetic four-domain, four-choice evaluation with **GLM-5.3-Flash**, the best tested memory method achieves **33.61% macro accuracy**, compared with **33.21% for dense retrieval**: a **0.40 percentage-point observed difference**. Missing uncertainty estimates prevent treating this as an established advantage.
- **Search-agent training — `2610.30435`.** SARL reports an **average judge score of 0.429 versus 0.345 for ASearcher** across its reported evaluation aggregate, with assessment removed at inference. Increased search activity means the result does not isolate improved search-state awareness from additional acquisition.
- **Speculative diffusion decoding — `2610.22543`.** SPOT reports **up to 6.8× fewer target passes relative to its specified reference decoder**. The result concerns pass count; distribution preservation holds under its stated shared-randomness and exact-verification assumptions. Complete per-setting throughput results are unavailable.
- **Physical robot improvement — `2610.36068`.** Rollback reports **10.67–11.85 percentage-point success gains over its same-world-model RISE reproduction across three real-world tasks**. Missing independent-run uncertainty and incomplete reward/resource audits limit stronger conclusions.
- **MoE read efficiency — `2610.37386`.** The weight-derived reader retains **98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads** on the tested frozen models. These are relative accuracy and read-volume measures; they do not imply an equivalent end-to-end speedup.
- **Free physical-law discovery — `2610.37649`.** In LongChang’s unguided simulated exploration setting, authors report **16.7% discovery for GPT-6-Astra and 0% for each of eleven other evaluated LLMs**, requiring both law correctness and experimental support. Judge validation, run denominators, and uncertainty are missing.
- **Budget-aware RL interpretation — `2610.34894`.** Across **twenty released RL–base pairs on physics and math**, the reported pass@1 changes primarily reflect finishing within budget or correctness after finishing, with limited net reach under the paper’s sampled-coverage definition. This is not evidence that outcome RL can never expand capability.
- **Training-only future supervision — `2610.37219`.** JAM’s matched simulation experiments replace visual-generation supervision with frozen future-representation prediction while retaining policy architecture, data, optimizer, training budget, and deployment path. This controlled comparison is more informative about the objective than unmatched aggregate robot rankings.
- **Harness adaptation — `2610.37255`.** Restricted-control experiments demonstrate that frozen-checkpoint interface rankings can change after adaptation. The practical implication is editorial: frozen-model evaluation measures compatibility as well as interface potential.

WiD’s **FID 1.53 at ImageNet 256×256 after 500 epochs with PixelDiT-XL** (`2610.36192`) is a concrete reported endpoint, but the retained evidence lacks its matched comparator value and complete guidance settings. It therefore cannot support a precise improvement claim here.

## Open research directions

The following are editorial proposals grounded in explicit limitations of the supplied analyses.

1. **Measure memory’s net value under matched budgets.** Motivated by `2610.24520`, `2610.34073`, and `2610.37624`. Compare raw-history retrieval, constructed memory, and source-recoverable memory with equal reader tokens and total acquisition cost. Annotate evidence preservation, retrieval access, support–decoy balance, and final correctness separately.
2. **Test whether additional searches earn their cost.** Motivated by `2610.30435` and `2610.34714`. Branch SARL and outcome-only agent histories into repeated THINK/SEARCH continuations. Compare incremental correctness and token-priced utility under matched search and generation budgets.
3. **Separate reusable memory from weight adaptation.** Motivated by `2610.32046` and `2610.34724`. Run a factorial comparison of strategy memory, adapter updates, and replay with common trajectories and matched total compute; measure held-out transfer, forgetting, and repeated-run variability.
4. **Evaluate harness improvement after equal adaptation and preparation.** Motivated by `2610.33624`, `2610.36077`, and `2610.37255`. Compare fixed and evolved interfaces before and after equal adaptation budgets, separating prepared skills, routing, oracle selection, and executable module changes.
5. **Combine predicted execution with artifact-grounded verification cautiously.** Motivated by `2610.33245` and `2610.37175`. On task-disjoint ML workflows, compare prediction-only search, real execution, and predicted search plus rubric audits. Measure missed silent errors, held-out performance, and preparation-inclusive cost.
6. **Identify what future supervision contributes to robot actions.** Motivated by `2610.37219`, `2610.37381`, and `2610.37666`. Hold backbone, robot data, action expert, and training compute fixed while varying future-feature targets, physical tokens, and reinforcement. Evaluate physical tasks and camera/contact perturbations with multiple seeds.
7. **Test visual-control preservation outside the requested change.** Motivated by `2610.29003`, `2610.36269`, `2610.36456`, and `2610.36729`. Use controlled edits with independently annotated target attributes, untouched regions, and action windows; score requested change, collateral change, temporal coherence, and latency separately.
8. **Validate streaming metrics against closed-loop assistance.** Motivated by `2610.34283` and `2610.35439`. Preserve legal observation prefixes and equal response opportunities, include execution latency, and test whether temporally eligible answers improve interactive task completion.
9. **Establish artifact-level and human validity for video evaluation.** Motivated by `2610.35864` and `2610.37341`. Repeat generations and expand blinded human evaluation across systems, separating renderability, content retention, object trajectories, and audiovisual synchronization.
10. **Audit scientific-discovery judgments independently.** Motivated by `2610.37649` and `2610.31924`. For hidden-law exploration, compare blinded expert judgments with automated judges and require discriminating follow-up experiments. For molecular-interface reconstruction, obtain independent structural measurements and quantify alternative-fit identifiability.
