# Online Dense Reinforcement Learning with Planning-Grounding Agent for UI Navigation on Live IOS Apps

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39264) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39264)


## One-sentence takeaway
Online RL with dense progress rewards improves an 8B UI planner over SFT on live-app benchmarks, but the reported gains depend on separate grounding models and do not establish deployment cost savings or broad personalization competence (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
The authors target reliable, affordable UI navigation: frontier models are costly, while smaller SFT agents can fail when execution departs from demonstrated trajectories. They identify sparse terminal rewards and limited live interaction as obstacles to learning intermediate progress and error recovery (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Planning–grounding training framework:** Warm-start a Qwen3-VL-8B planner with demonstrations and generated reasoning, then train it through live iOS interaction while keeping grounding separate. Training uses an internal SFT grounder with Gemini handling swipes; a snapshot-based device infrastructure supports concurrent rollouts (§§3.2–3.4, 4.2).
- **Dense online reward and asymmetric returns:** Gemini judges intermediate progress and terminal outcomes. PPO uses step-level advantages, discounting dense rewards by 0.5 while preserving undiscounted terminal credit to discourage prolonging trajectories for reward (§§3.4–3.5).
- **GiGPPO:** A neighbor-conditioned critic incorporates embeddings and observed returns from visually similar states in sibling trajectories, with perceptual-hash distance indicating similarity. The authors propose this to improve value estimation (§3.5).
- **Empirical contribution:** Comparisons against SFT, terminal-only PPO, and frontier planners show benchmark improvements and some faster progress by update count (§4.3). The claimed first large-scale live-iOS RL training is an author priority claim, not independently verified here.

The evidence supports improved task success under the reported evaluation setup and suggests that dense rewards help training progress. It does not prove general-purpose recovery, statistically reliable superiority, or lower total compute cost. Android transfer is beyond the **RL** environment, rather than transfer from wholly iOS-only training. The retrieved text includes captions but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1 and 2, limiting verification of complete rankings and grounding comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated distribution limits:** Synthetic app layouts, personalization tasks, and workflows spanning more than two apps differ from training. Later checkpoints sometimes mistake synthetic app names for real apps or web services (§4.3).
- **Author-stated remaining gap:** Frontier models still outperform the RL checkpoints on AndroidWorld (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—system dependence:** Scores evaluate a planner-plus-grounder system. Gemini supplies swipe grounding during training and all grounding on iOSWorld and AndroidWorld, so results do not establish an independently deployable 8B agent (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Analyst inference—judge validity:** Model-generated rewards, simulated user responses, and VLM-based iOS evaluation introduce potential evaluation dependencies. Human agreement or reward-judge calibration results are not supplied (§§3.4, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The failed GRPO setup differs from PPO in reward magnitudes, penalties, and optimization. It does not isolate discounting as the sole cause of reduced reward hacking (§3.5; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and efficiency:** Internal datasets and grounding models are used without a stated release. Update-count gains do not establish wall-clock, monetary, or inference-cost savings; uncertainty estimates and repeated-training results are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Does GiGPPO improve learning across repeated runs and resource budgets?** Earlier comparable checkpoints do not establish lower total training cost. Run seeded PPO/GiGPPO comparisons with matched rollout budgets, reporting success, critic error, wall time, and judge cost.
- **How much does asymmetric discounting independently prevent reward hacking?** The GRPO comparison changes several factors. Hold PPO and reward values fixed while varying dense and terminal discounting; measure completion, trajectory length, and excess actions.
- **How reliable are the judges and simulated users?** No human calibration is reported. Blindly audit sampled reward decisions and benchmark outcomes, then repeat clarification tasks with human users.
- **What limits personalization and broader workflow transfer?** iOSWorld performance remains low and differs from training in several ways. Separately add synthetic-layout, personalization, and longer multi-app training tasks, then test held-out categories.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are explicit, but the supplied text lacks full numerical Tables 1–2, uncertainty estimates, training-seed variation, dataset sizes and split-overlap details, judge-validation results, measured costs and latency, and confirmed code/model/data availability.
