# OpenFlyV2: An onboard vision-language-action model for aerial perception and navigation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39287) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39287)


## One-sentence takeaway
OpenFlyV2 combines aerial visual understanding and waypoint control in one model, reporting 10 Hz onboard action generation and 48% success across 100 physical navigation trials without real flight training demonstrations (§§2.6, 4.6).

## Problem and motivation
Aerial robots need to interpret landmarks, reason about spatial relationships, and follow language instructions while responding to changing views. The authors identify a gap between systems specialized for individual capabilities and systems able to sustain this full loop within onboard computing limits (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified aerial model:** A shared 0.8B vision-language backbone supports textual answers and conditions a 0.2B flow-based action expert that predicts flight waypoints. Training combines navigation and VQA supervision, followed by reinforcement post-training of the action expert (§§2.1, 4.1, 4.5).
- **Motion-Adaptive Memory (MAM):** Compresses past actions and their start/end images into bounded event tokens, with cached event content (§4.3).
- **Motion-Structured Prior (MSP):** Selects and adjusts one of four trajectory prototypes learned from training data, then adds correlated noise to initialize flow-based action generation (§4.4).
- **Dataset and deployment:** OpenFlyV2-130K contains 72,352 navigation trajectories and 60,006 VQA pairs from shared rendered environments; a subset supplies navigation training. The system executes asynchronously onboard a physical UAV (§§2.2, 4.7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better simulated navigation than DualVLN | On 800 seen / 300 unseen episodes, SR was 62.88% / 52.67% versus 38.88% / 36.67%; success required explicit stopping within 25 m | §2.4; Fig. 4B |
| Better grounding and distance estimates than GPT-5.5 | Grounding Acc@0.5: 67.1% versus 61.6%; distance MAE: 47.32 versus 52.62 m, relative error: 0.27 versus 0.34. Evaluation used 1,000 grounding and 300 distance examples | §§2.3, 4.6 |
| MAM reduces history-processing burden | Input tokens fell from 446 to at most 218; one-step action-expert denoising on Orin NX fell from 80 to 20 ms | §2.5 |
| MSP helps principally with one-step generation | Before RL, pooled SR increased from 53.45% for MAM to 57.09% with MSP; at two steps, MAM alone scored 59.45% versus 57.73% with MSP | Table 1; §2.5 |
| Physical onboard transfer demonstrated | Aggregate SR was 48% across 100 trials, four locations, and equally many single-/two-landmark instructions; 12-waypoint chunks generated at 10 Hz on Orin NX | §§2.6, 4.6 |

These results support onboard feasibility and gains under the reported protocols. They do not establish broad operational reliability or direct equivalence between simulation and field success: field success used manual landmark-based judgment rather than the simulation distance threshold (§4.6). The reported 20 ms timing covers action-expert denoising, not the entire perception–action cycle (§2.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated directions:** Detecting task deviations, recovering from errors, dialogue, concurrent navigation and question answering, and broader operating-condition evaluation remain future work (§3).
- **Analyst inference:** Pooled physical success obscures differences between seen/unseen locations and instruction types; subgroup results are unavailable (§§2.6, 4.6).
- **Analyst inference:** “Zero-shot” excludes real flight demonstrations and site fine-tuning, but two physical locations were represented in training through rendered 3DGS reconstructions (§2.6).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied ablations do not isolate reinforcement post-training or test whether joint VQA training improves navigation. Architectural ablations precede RL, whereas headline results use the final policy (§§2.5, 4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Description quality depends on an automated judge, GPT-6.1-sol; uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variability are not reported (§4.6). Code release is promised rather than established by the text.

## Open research questions
1. **How well does transfer hold at entirely new sites?** Aggregate field results mix represented and unseen locations. Next experiment: report balanced per-location and instruction-type results across additional unseen sites.
2. **What causes unsuccessful termination?** Oracle success exceeds actual success in the supervised ablation (§2.5). Next experiment: classify failures and compare explicit stop calibration or recovery policies.
3. **Which training components drive navigation gains?** Joint VQA training and RL lack isolated comparisons. Next experiment: controlled navigation-only/joint-training and pre-/post-RL evaluations.
4. **Can understanding and control operate concurrently?** The architecture selects output pathways, while concurrent interaction remains future work (§§3, 4.1). Next experiment: ask questions during flight and measure answer quality, navigation success, and latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main architecture and reported results, but lacks exact navigation-training subset size, field subgroup outcomes, numerical field stopping/time thresholds, uncertainty estimates, detailed failure analysis, full training hyperparameters, and confirmed asset availability.
