# IntReal: A Real-World Dataset and Benchmark for Multimodal Interleaved Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38425) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38425)


## One-sentence takeaway
IntReal introduces a curated text–image interleaved dataset and diagnostic benchmark, with experiments showing that image-only reinforcement learning after joint supervised fine-tuning improves evaluated interleaved generation, although gains vary by metric and evaluator.

## Problem and motivation
Generating illustrated explanations, tutorials, plans, and stories requires coordinating text and images across a sequence. The authors identify two gaps: existing interleaved datasets often lack generation-oriented filtering and scenario organization, while benchmarks relying primarily on holistic VLM judgments provide limited capability diagnosis (§1, Tables 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset:** IntReal-100K contains 100,000 sequences and 538,452 images from seven sources across Planning, Knowledge, Tutorial, and Story. Curation combines metadata checks, visual quality filtering, deduplication, model-assisted text refinement, and prompt/reasoning annotation (§2, Table 4).
- **Benchmark:** The IntReal Evaluation Suite uses 600 curated samples, balanced across categories. It combines complete-response generation with next-image generation under fixed reference contexts. Seven metrics assess text richness, text logicality, image aesthetics, image diversity, image-count compliance, context fidelity, and subject consistency (§3).
- **Post-training finding:** Experiments with Bagel and SenseNova-U1 test whether image-generation optimization transfers to complete interleaved outputs. Importantly, U1 uses only an aesthetic RL reward; Bagel combines aesthetic, DINO, and rubric rewards (§4.3, Table 6).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Automatic judgments broadly agree with experts | Average agreement is 83.3% across 750 pairwise comparisons covering five metrics; aesthetics and quantity compliance are excluded. | §4.4, Fig. 6 |
| Evaluator rankings are stable | Gemma4 versus Qwen3.6 ranking correlations: 0.905 for Text Richness, 0.929 for Text Logicality, and 0.976 for Context Fidelity. | Fig. 4 |

| Image RL improves U1 after SFT | Aesthetic-only RL raises Image Aesthetic from 75.10 to 78.53, Image Diversity from 87.05 to 93.31, and mean score from 72.31 to 73.81—the highest evaluated open-source mean under Gemma4. | Tables 3, 6 |
| Closed-source advantage is uneven | NanoBanana2 scores 84.84 overall versus 73.81 for U1+SFT+RL, but its Context Fidelity is 76.70 versus 87.29 for base U1; all scores use Gemma4 judging where applicable. | Table 3 |

The evidence supports complementary diagnostic measurements and within-benchmark post-training gains. It does not establish general transfer to unseen domains or universal evaluator calibration. Under Qwen3.6, U1 SFT’s mean slightly decreases from 71.58 to 71.47, although SFT+RL reaches 73.09 (Table 7). The authors also caution that the equally weighted overall score is a comparison aid rather than a definitive quality measure (Appendix B.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Aggressive filtering retains only about 10% of collected samples; synthetic augmentation and mixed post-training with unimodal data remain unexplored (§§2.2, 6).
- **Scope:** Categories are closely tied to particular sources: tutorials come from a cooking platform, and stories from three animated franchises. **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond these content distributions remains uncertain (§2.1).
- **Evaluation proxies:** Subject Consistency measures DINO similarity to reference images under fixed contexts, rather than directly measuring consistency throughout freely generated sequences. Text Logicality uses model judgment without external factual verification (Appendices B.2–B.3).
- **Optimization validity:** RALI participates in filtering, evaluation, and RL rewards. **Analyst inference:** Independent human assessment of post-training gains would help distinguish broader improvements from reward-specific gains. No reward-component ablation is reported (§§2.2, 4.3, Table 6).
- **Comparison and reporting:** Emu3.5 is evaluated on 592 samples and NanoBanana2 on 598, versus 600 for most models. No uncertainty intervals or repeated-seed results are reported. Section 4.3 also assigns Subject Consistency a 6.53% SFT gain, while Table 3 reports that gain as 6.53% and is the basis used here for interpreting results; its surrounding numerical presentation requires careful reading.
- **Reuse:** Appendix A.1 describes heterogeneous licensing conditions; its animated-source descriptions do not clearly establish blanket redistribution permission despite the broader claim in §2.1.

## Open research questions
- **Do gains transfer beyond the source-linked categories?** Current evaluation uses curated samples from the same collection. Test frozen models on independently collected tutorials, stories, and planning content.
- **Which RL rewards produce transferable improvements?** Reward configurations differ between models, without component ablations. Compare aesthetic-only, rubric-only, DINO-only, and combined rewards using independent human evaluation.
- **Do fixed-context improvements persist over autonomous sequences?** Next-image evaluation supplies reference histories. Compare it with full generated-history rollouts and measure how errors accumulate.
- **Can mixed-data training reduce SFT trade-offs?** The authors explicitly leave this unexplored. Compare interleaved-only SFT against matched-compute mixtures containing unimodal generation data.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed protocols, appendices, and numerical results, but lacks uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variability, detailed human-annotator procedures and agreement, reward ablations, verified artifact availability, and independent-domain evaluation.
