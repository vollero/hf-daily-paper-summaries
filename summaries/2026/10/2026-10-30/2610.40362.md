# Your LR Schedule May Not Be Principled: Effective Learning Rate Scheduling in Weight-Normalized Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40362) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40362)


## One-sentence takeaway
AIR, an affine inverse-square-root learning-rate schedule motivated by idealized directional-alignment dynamics, improves reported MoE pretraining losses over cosine, while effective-learning-rate matching largely reproduces weight-normalized and weight-decayed training trajectories (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation
Weight-normalized (WN) training fixes parameter-block norms, whereas weight decay (WD) permits norms to evolve. Consequently, identical nominal learning-rate schedules produce different effective learning rates (ELRs), defined as nominal LR divided by block norm. WN beats WD under cosine but loses under warmup-stable-decay in the authors’ experiments, motivating an explanation based on ELR trajectories rather than normalization alone (Fig. 1; §2).

The authors identify two gaps: separating normalization effects from induced scheduling effects, and deriving a useful ELR decay shape from an explicit optimization objective (§§1, 6).

## Approach and contributions
- **ELR matching experiments:** Replay recorded WD block norms or rescale blockwise LRs to align WN and WD ELRs. These interventions largely align training-loss trajectories (§2.2; Appendix A.3).
- **Alignment-based schedule derivation:** Under fixed-norm, tangential updates with constant magnitude and expected target-direction drift, derive a time-shifted inverse-square-root schedule that maximizes expected directional alignment in the specified scalar dynamics. The discrete recurrence has the same optimum (§§3.1–3.2; Appendix B.3).
- **Practical AIR schedule:** Affinely transform the inverse-square-root curve to meet prescribed peak and terminal LRs; calibrate the peak using the complete discrete LR sum when matching budgets. This construction is inspired by the unconstrained optimum; constrained optimality is not established (§3.3; Appendices B.5–B.6).
- **Empirical schedule analysis:** Compare schedule families and intervene on training stages to investigate AIR’s gains and the importance of late scheduling (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| ELR matching largely aligns WN and WD losses | Under cosine, norm replay leaves a loss gap below \(2\times10^{-4}\); LR matching at fixed WN norms leaves several × \(10^{-3}\). Reverse matching also tracks WN under cosine and AIR. | §2.2; Fig. 2 |
| AIR improves smaller-model terminal losses | At 110M activated/4.7B total parameters and 128B tokens, AIR versus cosine: training loss **1.730 vs 1.740**, evaluation loss **2.174 vs 2.185**; matched warmup duration, terminal LR, and cumulative LR. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 |
| AIR improves larger-model terminal losses | At 790M activated/35.7B total parameters and 500B tokens, AIR versus cosine: training loss **1.421 vs 1.431**, evaluation loss **1.832 vs 1.843**, under the matched controls. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Downstream averages improve | AIR versus cosine across the listed benchmarks: **29.97 vs 29.24** at the smaller scale and **57.78 vs 56.22** at the larger scale. AIR does not win every benchmark. | Table 1 |
| Training-loss robustness is favorable within measured sweeps | AIR’s sensitivity is **0.0018**, the lowest training-loss value reported. Its evaluation sensitivity is **0.0018**, compared with Cycloid’s **0.0017**. Sweep sizes and ranges differ. | Appendix C.5; Table 5 |
| Late-stage swaps transfer most of one schedule gap | For AIR and a Cycloid comparator whose original full schedules share peak, terminal, and cumulative LR, swapping the late stage closes about **90%** of the terminal training-loss gap. Individual swaps need not preserve cumulative LR. | §5.1; Fig. 5; Appendix C.6 |
| A common AIR tail reduces—but does not erase—earlier schedule differences | Keeping each of six schedule shapes over the first **70%** of training and using a shared AIR tail closes **42%–83%** of training-loss gaps and **45%–94%** of evaluation-loss gaps to AIR. | §5.2; Table 2 |

Training losses are terminal EMA summaries; evaluation losses are final unsmoothed logged values. They summarize individual trajectories rather than independent-seed averages (Appendix C.3).

The evidence supports AIR’s advantage over cosine in these settings and a substantial role for ELR and late scheduling. It does not establish universal superiority: several smaller-model losses tie AIR at displayed precision, and the theory optimizes alignment rather than measured loss.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical scope:** Constant positive drift cannot describe physical cosine alignment indefinitely. The continuous approximation additionally requires small relative updates, slowly varying states, and approximately constant update statistics (Appendices B.1, B.4).
- **Author-stated open scope:** Evolving update statistics, a quantitative alignment-to-loss connection, additional architectures and optimizers, and continued training, SFT, and RL remain future work (§7).
- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** Individual trajectories without independent-seed confidence intervals leave uncertainty around small differences (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Shape parameters are selected during evaluation, and tuning budgets differ across families. Sensitivity normalization does not make those budgets equivalent (Table 1; Appendix C.5).
- **Analyst inference—intervention confounding:** Stage swaps can change cumulative LR; late-exponent interventions have LR-integral deviations below 1%, rather than exact matching. These qualify causal attribution to decay shape alone (Appendices C.6–C.7).
- **Analyst inference—WD transfer practicality:** Reverse matching uses recorded WD norms and replay, so it does not establish an autonomous WD scheduler that realizes the target ELR without replay (§2.2; Appendix A.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does AIR retain its advantage across seeds and optimizer families?** Current evidence uses individual trajectories and Muon-based MoE experiments. Repeat matched-budget AIR/cosine comparisons across independent seeds and additional optimizers.
2. **Can evolving update statistics predict a better schedule?** The derivation fixes drift and update magnitude, a limitation acknowledged in §7. Measure their evolution and compare a resulting schedule with AIR.
3. **How much late-stage benefit survives exact intervention-budget matching?** Existing swaps need not preserve cumulative LR. Repeat stage replacements with explicit budget correction and multiple stage boundaries.
4. **Can WD realize AIR’s ELR online without norm replay?** Transfer experiments rely on recorded norms. Test a controller using current block norms and compare realized ELRs and losses with replay-based transfer.
