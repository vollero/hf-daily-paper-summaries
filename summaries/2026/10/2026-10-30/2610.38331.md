# Multi-Token Prediction Done Right

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38331) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331)


## One-sentence takeaway
Soft-MTP recovers exact hidden-state KL self-distillation gradients from existing cross-entropy computations, improving standard MTP across the tested model scales with negligible measured training overhead (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Standard multi-token prediction (MTP) trains future-token heads against observed tokens, although speculative decoding benefits from matching the model’s own next-token prediction (NTP) distribution. Explicit KL self-distillation addresses this mismatch but can require costly vocabulary-sized distributions. The author identifies a residual component in standard MTP gradients that obscures the self-distillation signal and interferes with learning (§§1, 4, 6.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Exact gradient decomposition:** MTP’s hidden-state cross-entropy gradient splits into a forward-KL self-distillation gradient toward the position-aligned NTP teacher and a residual NTP cross-entropy gradient (§4).
- **KL gradient recovery:** Straight-through routing subtracts the residual using existing NTP gradients, avoiding additional vocabulary-sized tensors. Exactness requires a shared linear output head and correctly aligned, weighted losses (§5.1; Appendix B.5).
- **Soft-MTP:** MTP heads and their upstream trunk receive recovered KL gradients, alongside ordinary NTP training; the shared output head retains cross-entropy supervision. Thus, Soft-MTP differs from direct KL optimization at the output head (§5.2).
- **Empirical diagnosis and validation:** Gradient diagnostics, pre-training comparisons, decoding evaluations, and downstream fine-tuning support this routing choice. Code is linked in the abstract; Appendix A supplies an implementation.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Residual has weaker gradient signal | Across 167M–1.2B models, residual SNR is lower than KL-component SNR by 4.1× at 167M and 4.6× at 1.2B; residual contributes approximately 80% of the trunk MTP gradient by projection share. Diagnostics use frozen checkpoints and 256 independent batches of four sequences. | §6.1, Fig. 3; Appendix E |
| Soft-MTP improves training losses | In the main FineWeb-Edu experiments, NTP loss improves at every tested scale; MTP loss improves from 689M onward. | §6.1, Fig. 4 |
| Decoding gains are consistent but modest | Relative to standard MTP, domain-averaged acceptance length improves 1.8–2.2% and inference speedup improves 1.4–1.5% across tested scales. Evaluation uses three draft heads, five domains, and 256 prompts per domain with 512-token prompts and continuations. | §6.2, Table 2 and text |
| Zero-shot accuracy improves | Average accuracy gains over standard MTP are approximately 0.1 points at 167M/332M and 0.5 points at 689M/1.2B; Soft-MTP also exceeds NTP at the latter scales. | §6.2, Table 3 and text |
| Math and code transfer improve | After NTP-only fine-tuning of 1.2B models, gains over standard MTP reach 3.1 percentage points on HumanEval and 1.2 on MBPP at pass@64; math gains are 1.1 points on GSM8K and 1.8 on MATH-500 under zero-shot greedy decoding. Code results select the best temperature from the stated sweep. | §6.2, Table 4 and text |
| Training overhead is negligible in the measured setting | At 1.2B with three MTP heads on one H200, Soft-MTP throughput is within 0.07% of standard MTP, with identical reported FLOPs, MFU, and fused-cross-entropy peak memory. Explicit KL-MTP adds 1.5% FLOPs, reduces throughput 5.6%, and adds 21 GB peak memory versus standard MTP. Throughput uses each objective’s largest fitting micro-batch; memory uses a matched micro-batch of 19. | §6.3, Table 5 and text |
| Direct KL is not uniformly superior | At 1.2B, Soft-MTP ties or beats KL-MTP on perplexity, improves average zero-shot accuracy, and wins all reported code metrics; KL-MTP leads on math. | §6.3, Table 6 and text |

These are author-reported results from models trained on approximately 31.5B FineWeb-Edu tokens under a common main experimental recipe (§6). They support Soft-MTP within that setting, but do not establish frontier-scale gains or statistical reliability of small differences. ## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated bias:** Removing the residual biases MTP learning toward the NTP teacher rather than the data distribution; teacher error remains relevant (§5.3).
- **Author-stated exactness boundary:** Exact KL gradients apply upstream of the MTP hidden states; the shared output head receives cross-entropy gradients (§§5.2, 7).
- **Author-stated diagnostic boundary:** Hidden-state intervention results are proxies, not realized optimizer updates or proofs of final-model behavior (§6.1; Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Main experiments stop at 1.2B parameters, use one pre-training corpus and optimizer recipe, and vary width at fixed depth. Frontier-scale and broader optimizer claims remain untested here (§6; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—limited secondary validation:** Meta-style heads are tested only at 332M with 7.3B tokens; weight and horizon sweeps use that same scale and token budget (Appendix C; Fig. 5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** The supplied text gives no repeated-run uncertainty or significance estimates. The explicit-KL cost comparison also does not establish costs for a custom fused KL implementation, which §7 discusses as an alternative.

## Open research questions
- **Do benefits persist at frontier scale?** Experiments end at 1.2B, while distributed compatibility is discussed analytically. Next experiment: compare matched MTP and Soft-MTP runs at larger scale with measured communication and throughput (§7).
- **How sensitive are gains to teacher quality and training recipe?** Soft-MTP inherits teacher bias, and evaluations use a common corpus and optimizer. Next experiment: vary teacher maturity, corpus, and optimizer while tracking losses, gradient SNR, and acceptance (§5.3; §6 setup).
- **Does Soft-MTP improve later standalone drafters?** The author raises this possibility without testing it. Next experiment: train identical post-training drafters on NTP-, MTP-, and Soft-MTP-pretrained backbones and compare quality and decoding efficiency (§7).
