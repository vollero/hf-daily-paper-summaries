# Investor&#x27;s Last Exam: Can AI Agents Do Equity Investment Without Seeing the Future?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38342) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38342)


## One-sentence takeaway
Investor’s Last Exam evaluates equity-research agents with version-controlled, cutoff-constrained evidence and finds that strong research scores do not consistently translate into accurate forecasts or higher simulated investment returns (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Professional equity research requires a coherent chain from evidence through assumptions and calculations to forecasts and investment decisions. The authors argue that existing benchmarks evaluate parts of this workflow without enforcing source-version availability throughout retrieval and computation, allowing later information to contaminate historical evaluations (§1).

ILE addresses two related gaps: controlling the information available at decision time and assessing whether financially sound analysis supports subsequent judgments and outcomes.

## Approach and contributions
- **Version-controlled research environment:** Immutable document versions have availability timestamps enforced across search, reads, downloads, cached results, and structured-table operations. Local computation runs without network access; evaluation references and later outcomes remain isolated (§2.1; Appendix B).
- **Professional task suite:** The benchmark contains 86 assignments: 78 company-research and eight market-forecasting tasks. Its taxonomy organizes assignments by analyst deliverable, including forecasting, investment decisions, monitoring, and integrated research (§2.2; Appendix E.2).
- **Evidence-linked evaluation:** Experts construct financial dependency graphs connecting source facts, assumptions, calculations, and judgments. Weighted rubrics combine deterministic numerical checks with model-based semantic grading, alongside realized forecast outcomes and a stock-return simulation (§§2.3, 3.1–3.3).

The contribution is an evaluation environment and construction framework; the paper does not introduce a newly trained investment model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Overall leadership coexists with weak forecasting | Across 86 tasks, default answer-based scores are Astra 68.1/100 and Opus 66.9/100. Astra scores 78.9 on Research & Evidence but 52.5 on Forecast & Model. | §§3.1–3.2; Table 3 caption and accompanying prose |
| Predictive ranking differs from overall ranking | Flash leads task-normalized realized forecast-outcome credit at 40.3, versus Astra 37.7 and Opus 37.6; outcome criteria are evaluated separately from supporting analysis. | §3.2; Appendix G.1 |
| Simulated returns yield another ordering | Over 20 opportunities, 11 companies, and 13 disclosures during March–August 2026, Hunyuan returns 8.50%, Astra 7.18%, Kimi 7.15%, and Opus 6.39%, versus 1.18% always long. Simulation charges 10 basis points per leg and 3% annualized short borrowing. | §3.3; Table 4; Appendix I.2 |
| Similar answer scores can conceal temporal leakage | On the same 86 questions and rubrics, Astra scores 68.1 with ILE and 68.3 with public-web Codex; 49 public-web trajectories, reported as 57%, receive explicitly post-cutoff sources. | §3.4; Appendix I.4 |
| Correct extraction and reconciliation can miss financial meaning | In AEP N27, all 11 systems recover eight inputs and reconcile both cash-flow identities; only five calculate both funding shortfalls, three distinguish net fuel adjustments from gross collections, and five reach the supported funding conclusion. | §4.1; Appendix H.4 |
| Supporting analysis can be correct while forecasts fail | On Tesla O80, Astra, Qwen Max, and Opus each earn all 50 supporting-analysis points but zero of 50 forecast-outcome points for deliveries, revenue, and margin. | §4.2; Appendix G.2 |
| Submission requirements materially change scores | Requiring accepted submissions reduces Step 5 Preview from 55.1 to 34.5 and Hunyuan from 60.4 to 47.7, using the same financial criteria. | §3.1 |

These results support distinguishing research quality, predictive accuracy, investment payoff, and task completion. They do not establish prospective investment profitability or complete freedom from future knowledge. The public-web comparison demonstrates exposure to later sources; it does not quantify their causal effect on scores.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Cutoff-constrained retrieval cannot remove historical outcomes memorized during pretraining. Counterfactual or cold-start experiments remain necessary (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Realized outcomes contain exogenous noise, so forecast errors do not necessarily imply faulty reasoning (§5).
- **Acknowledged judging risk:** Semantic grading may exhibit model biases. Regrading fixed answers with GPT-OSS-120B changes some rankings, although Astra remains first (§3.1; Appendix I.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Trading evidence has limited generalizability: the sample is retrospective, assignments can share issuers or disclosures, missing forecasts hold cash, and returns exclude dividends (§3.3; Appendix I.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The environment’s temporal validity depends on timestamp verification, which sometimes uses model-assisted inference. The supplied text provides no timestamp error-rate audit (§2.1).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Reference analyses and rubrics are private, and many tables appear only as captions in the retrieved text, limiting independent inspection of detailed results (§2.3; appendices).

## Open research questions
1. **How much performance comes from memorized outcomes?** Retrieval controls leave parametric memory unresolved (§5). A next experiment could compare original assignments with synthetic statements and renamed companies preserving the financial dependencies.
2. **How stable are model rankings under expert grading?** Alternative model judges change rankings, while a human-agreement study is not supplied (Appendix I.3). Experts could independently grade a blinded, stratified sample.
3. **Do forecast-driven returns persist prospectively?** Current evidence uses a small retrospective sample (§3.3). A next experiment could preregister signals and evaluate them on newly arriving disclosures with dependence-aware uncertainty estimates.
4. **Can explicit consistency checks improve forecasts?** Cases separate accounting coherence from predictive validity (§4.2). An ablation could add accounting constraints and assumption-sensitivity checks, then measure consistency and outcome accuracy separately.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the benchmark design and headline findings, but many detailed tables are absent.

Material missing information includes complete per-model results, task-category counts and full questions, rubric weights and tolerances across tasks, timestamp validation accuracy, human–judge agreement, repeated-run variability, statistical uncertainty for comparisons and returns, and the precise accessibility of released data and code.
