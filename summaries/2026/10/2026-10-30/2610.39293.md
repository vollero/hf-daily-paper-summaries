# Unlocking Any-Order Generation in Pretrained Autoregressive Image Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39293) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39293)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors adapt pretrained raster-scan image generators into any-order models using target-position embeddings and an ordering curriculum, gaining image completion capabilities at reported adaptation costs of 13–33% of pretraining while worsening class-conditional FID (Sections 3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Fixed raster-scan autoregressive models cannot naturally condition on arbitrary observed image regions. Existing any-order approaches typically train from scratch, while bidirectional alternatives cannot directly reuse standard causal KV caching. The authors ask whether pretrained image representations can support new generation orders through relatively inexpensive adaptation rather than complete retraining (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Target-position conditioning:** Add a learned embedding identifying the next spatial position to predict. Spatial positional encodings follow image coordinates rather than sequence order, preserving the causal single-stream architecture (Section 3.1).
- **Ordering curriculum:** Gradually increase the probability of sampling a uniformly random permutation instead of raster scan. This mixes two ordering distributions; it does not progressively shuffle individual permutations (Section 3.2).
- **Checkpoint adaptation:** Continue training LlamaGen-L/XL and RAR-L/XL with these components. At inference, place observed tokens first and generate the remainder, enabling inpainting, outpainting, and region completion without task-specific training (Sections 3.3–4.3).

The principal contribution is this adaptation recipe. The explanation that it preserves pretrained knowledge while developing flexible conditional pathways is the authors’ interpretation, rather than a directly established mechanism.

These results support useful completion capabilities and a measurable quality–compute trade-off in the tested setting. They do not establish lossless conversion, universal transfer across image models, or a matched-quality inference-speed advantage. Tables 2–8 have captions but no data rows in the supplied text, limiting independent assessment of several comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation is confined to ImageNet class conditioning, principally at 256×256 evaluation resolution. Text-to-image models, higher-resolution evaluation, and non-token-based adaptation remain untested (Section 5).
- **Author-stated editing limit:** Editing means region completion. Reference-based metrics do not establish instruction following or identity preservation (Section 4.3).
- **Comparison limits:** The random-initialization control matches compute but not schedule; MaskGIT results depend partly on its scheduler. Native-throughput comparisons are explicitly not matched for FID (Sections 4.2–4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Ablations support the recipe’s utility but do not isolate catastrophic forgetting or validate the proposed gradient mechanism; the supplied text provides a conceptual argument rather than direct gradient measurements (Section 4.4; Appendix A).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Missing table entries omit per-model adaptation budgets, curriculum ramps, and detailed sampling settings. GPU-hours were not logged for models other than LlamaGen-XL (Appendices B–C).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the recipe transfer beyond ImageNet class conditioning?** The authors leave this untested. Adapt a text-conditioned causal checkpoint and evaluate completion quality alongside text alignment.
2. **Can curriculum design reduce the generation-quality loss?** All tested checkpoints worsen in FID. Compare linear and adaptive curricula under equal adaptation budgets, measuring both generation and completion.
3. **How much benefit comes from pretraining versus schedule design?** The existing control confounds them. Compare pretrained and random initializations under matched total compute and explicitly controlled ordering schedules.
4. **Does completion superiority hold across mask types and repeated runs?** Main quantitative completion settings use center masks, and uncertainty is unavailable. Evaluate irregular masks and varied observed fractions across multiple seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and class-conditional results are clearly described, but important supporting tables are absent.

Material missing information includes Tables 2–8’s numerical entries, exact per-model hyperparameters, full Halton/MAR-L comparisons, completion-result checkpoint attribution, uncertainty estimates, detailed parallel-decoding settings, and independently verified code or checkpoint availability.
