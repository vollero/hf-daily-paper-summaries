# Matched Capabilities, Different Behaviors: A Controlled Study of MCP and CLI Interfaces for Language Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34298) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34298)


## One-sentence takeaway
With executable capabilities held fixed, MCP and CLI representations produce model- and environment-dependent differences in agent decisions, outcomes, and token use; an error-triggered controller that separates semantic selection from realization improves scores in all six tested Gemini settings (§§6.1–6.4).

## Problem and motivation
Tool interfaces influence both which action an agent selects and how it expresses that action. The authors identify two attribution gaps: existing MCP–CLI comparisons can also change capabilities or execution scaffolding, while endpoint scores conceal different failure and recovery pathways. They therefore study representation under matched executable semantics and trace how local differences develop through interaction (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **MICA construction:** Generate MCP-style tools and CLI-style commands from one semantic action contract, with shared validation, native compilation, and execution. Conformance checks cover action coverage, round-trip decoding, compilation equivalence, and public contract facts (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Behavioral attribution:** Combine matched-state factorial probes of presentation, realization, and feedback with paired trajectories aligned until their first consequential divergence (§4.2).
- **DART intervention:** After an eligible failure, select and validate a canonical semantic action, then realize it through the assigned interface. Allow one correction that preserves the committed operation and every typed argument; otherwise return to ordinary retry (§4.3; Appendix D).

The evidence supports representation-sensitive behavior within these controlled implementations, rather than a universal MCP or CLI advantage. Matched-state probes isolate local component effects; post-divergence trajectories describe propagation under different histories, rather than further matched-state interventions. DART’s endpoint gains support the complete controller, but do not independently establish which controller component causes those gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences grounded in its protocol**:

- **Deployment scope:** MCP uses prompted structured response containers, and CLI uses registered commands parsed directly into arguments. Results therefore concern these controlled representations; unrestricted terminal workflows and other tool-calling integrations are not evaluated (Appendices C.2, E).
- **DART attribution:** DART selects a new semantic commitment after failure; it does not recover a demonstrated latent intent from the failed response. No reported ablation separates recommitment, extra staged calls, trigger configuration, and commitment preservation (Appendices D–E).
- **Limited intervention coverage:** The interface study evaluates six models, but DART evaluates only Gemini-2.5-Flash (§5).
- **Sparse preserving-correction evidence:** Only four interventions reach a preserving-correction opportunity, with three admitted. This limits conclusions about that particular branch (Appendix A.4).
- **Probe and uncertainty boundaries:** Probes use temperature 0.6 and eight samples per condition, whereas natural episodes use temperature zero. Numerical uncertainty intervals are not supplied for the headline Table 1 or Figure 4 effects, although the bootstrap procedure is described (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Which part of DART drives improvement?** The combined controller changes selection and realization after failure. Compare ordinary retry, canonical reselection alone, staged realization without preservation, and full DART under matched call budgets.
- **Does DART generalize across models?** Its evaluation covers one model. Repeat the paired intervention study across the remaining model suite using the same trigger rules.
- **How sensitive are results to interface implementation?** The study uses specific response containers and command grammars. Vary serialization, quoting rules, and constrained decoding while preserving the semantic contract.
- **Does preserving correction help at scale?** Only four correction opportunities are reported. Evaluate a larger, prospectively specified set of realization failures and measure admission, eventual success, and total token cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported setting-specific findings; **medium** in broader generalization and DART’s component-level explanation.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes exact task identifiers and replicate counts, the concrete probe category/hash selection rule, fully enumerated benchmark-specific trigger configurations, and numerical confidence intervals for headline endpoint effects. The repository is stated to be available, but its contents are not supplied or verified.
