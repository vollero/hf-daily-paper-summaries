# Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39302) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under idealized spectral assumptions, separately trained JEPAs align up to rotation when their positives connect the same world states, and experiments show that captions grouped by scene improve vision–text alignment substantially over next-token prediction.

## Problem and motivation
The Platonic Representation Hypothesis proposes that models trained on different modalities converge toward shared representations as they scale. The authors identify a missing sufficient condition for practical self-supervised objectives: when should separately trained encoders agree, and how can training improve that agreement? Their answer centers on positive-pair construction rather than scale alone (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Conditional alignment theory:** The authors prove agreement up to rotation for shared positive distributions and minimizers unique up to rotation. Whitened squared-error JEPAs satisfy this uniqueness condition under a spectral gap. Further results bound misalignment from mismatched positive kernels and quantify the cost of encoding modality-specific nuisance (§2; Appendix A).
- **Objective interpretation:** They express next-token loss as prediction plus a softmax log-partition term. Its proposed nuisance-based explanation for weaker alignment is explicitly heuristic, rather than a consequence of the spectral theorems (§2.4).
- **Training and scaling evidence:** LeJEPA text encoders use different captions of one image as “good views,” compared with corruptions of one caption and causal next-token prediction. Experiments vary data scale, encoder size, view mixtures, and vision backbones (§§3–4).
- **Applications:** Frozen encoders are connected through a fitted orthogonal or row-orthonormal map for captioning; an existing geometry-matching method tests alignment without paired examples (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Small encoder exceeds evaluated large language models on alignment | WIT-1024/DINOv2-B mutual 10-NN: **.184** for the 109M JEPA, **.154** for Qwen2.5-72B, **.111** for the matched next-token model, and **.088** for bad views. JEPA and next-token results average three seeds; bad views use one. | Tables 3, 14, 16 |
| Scaling law predicts held-out runs locally | For BERT-S on texts of 10.2M images, 85 prospectively predicted runs yield Flickr R@1 RMSE **.0093**, versus **.0096** between training seeds. | §4.4; Appendix C |

The evidence supports better alignment from good views in the tested settings. It does **not** establish universal convergence, general language-model superiority, or a compute-normalized efficiency advantage. “Pair-free” describes fitting the final alignment: scene grouping and vision–language-generated recaptions still supply paired information upstream (§7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ theoretical limits:** Real observations are lossy; finite-sample optimization and network inductive bias are outside the main guarantees. Uniqueness is unproved for LeJEPA’s full-distribution constraint, learned predictors are excluded, and unequal-dimensional maps fall outside the theorem (§§2.1–2.2, 7).
- **Authors’ data caveat:** Most web views come from a captioner with CLIP-based vision features. Without image-generated recaptions, the text-only-paraphrase setup performs substantially worse; identical-text objective comparisons provide stronger evidence than comparisons with independently pretrained models (Table 7; §7).
- **Selection and stability:** Hyperparameters were tuned only for good views. The largest model’s SIGReg weight was selected after observing WIT results; collapse and weight sensitivity are documented (§7; Appendix C).
- **Evaluation boundaries:** Known web-data overlaps receive removal checks, but WIT image overlap cannot be checked. Pair-free matching has substantial initialization variance; significance tests treat method runs sharing training seeds as independent (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The 42× comparison demonstrates a reported sample-to-score advantage, not an optimized efficiency frontier: token exposure and recipes differ, and intermediate next-token performance is unavailable (§4.2).
- **Scaling-law scope:** Exponents are poorly identified, and long training on repeated data breaks extrapolation (§4.4).

## Open research questions
- **Can guarantees cover lossy modalities and practical LeJEPA training?** These remain outside the proof. Next experiment: vary controlled information loss and compare whitened MSE with SIGReg.
- **How much improvement survives without multimodal distillation?** Text-only paraphrases help less than recaptions. Next experiment: compare independently authored scene descriptions and generated views at matched token and compute budgets.
- **Do good image views scale similarly?** Evidence is limited to small MSR-VTT training. Next experiment: train larger video-frame JEPAs against matched crop-based controls.
- **Can scaling predictions handle data reuse?** The law overpredicts long good-view runs. Next experiment: independently vary unique scenes and training passes, then prospectively test a repetition-aware law.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings and their conditional scope; **medium** in broader efficiency and convergence interpretations.

Material gaps include compute-matched comparisons, intermediate next-token checkpoints for the 42× claim, exhaustive contamination checks, and guarantees for lossy observations, learned predictors, and practical SIGReg optimization.
