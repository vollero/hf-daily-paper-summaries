# WebAnything: From the Wild Web to Scalable Agent Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37786) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37786)


## One-sentence takeaway
WebAnything reconstructs real websites into reusable agent environments using browser evidence and independent verification, with reported training benefits but uneven functional coverage across evaluated sites (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Web agents need diverse environments with repeatable initial states, executable workflows, and reliable evaluation. The authors identify a trade-off: human-assisted mirrors require site-specific engineering, generated websites may diverge from their intended sources, and replay systems cover only captured interactions. They target two gaps: reconstructing behavior without private backend access, and distinguishing implementation defects from missing source evidence (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evidence-grounded reconstruction:** A coupled Explorer–Builder collects page structure, styles, geometry, screenshots, resources, and before/after interaction observations, then implements local routes and shared state. Construction is bounded by an explicit site contract (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Independent verification and refinement:** A separate verifier tests frozen candidates without editing them. Supported discrepancies trigger local repair; insufficient observations trigger targeted source recapture, followed by renewed verification (§3.3).
- **Environment and interaction-data supply:** Local environments package resources, initial data, and recovery interfaces. A task pipeline generates goals and hidden success conditions, executes agents, and exports accepted trajectories for supervised fine-tuning (§3.4).

The distinctive contribution is the integration of source evidence, frozen-candidate verification, and evidence-dependent repair routing. The reported experiments do not isolate each component’s causal contribution (§4.4; Appendix B.7).

The evidence supports useful environment supply and transfer from collected trajectories under the reported harness. It does not establish uniform quality across the inventory, general functional superiority, or causal benefits from independent verification or recapture. Relaxing JSON-format judgment raises the same SFT retrieval outputs from 39/325 to 105/325 passes, but no relaxed baseline is reported; this cannot replace the strict comparison or explain the entire regression (Appendix E.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limits:** Training validation centers on Qwen3.5-9B; larger models, alternative post-training methods, and larger budgets are not systematically evaluated. Long-term mirror maintenance and source-change responsiveness are also untested (Limitations).
- **Uneven inventory quality:** Homepage availability defines inventory inclusion. Appendix A.6 identifies blank homepages and presentation anomalies, while many artifacts lack additional review. There is no uniform complete-attempt cohort supporting a construction success rate (Appendices A.1, A.6).
- **Reference comparability:** Historical references include rendering defects. Text F1 compares JavaScript-disabled source replay against earlier JavaScript-enabled mirror captures. Personalized content, language, location, and source-version differences further constrain some comparisons (Appendices A.3–A.4).
- **Observational verification evidence:** Most Builder–Verifier pairs differ in candidate, scope, or runtime conditions. Confirmed resolution records are not independent bugs, and recorded recapture does not establish closure of the targeted evidence gaps (Appendix B.7).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** SFT benefits cannot identify which reconstruction mechanism matters. The smaller data-source comparison also varies training-row counts and task pools (Appendices E.1–E.2).
- **Evaluation and release boundaries:** Overlap checks exclude neither semantic paraphrases nor all shared templates or visited hosts. Task evaluators lack uniformly enforced positive/negative calibration in earlier runners. Content authorization constrains mirror distribution (Appendices B.4, E.1; Limitations).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does independent verification improve quality per unit cost?** Historical comparisons are mostly unmatched and observational. A next experiment could randomize identical initial candidates to self-checking alone or independent verification under equal budgets, then measure held-out defects and total cost.
2. **When does targeted recapture resolve missing evidence?** Recorded recapture lacks comparable gap-closure evidence. A next experiment could define unresolved behaviors in advance and compare recapture against local repair using matched source-grounded tests.
3. **Which environment properties drive training transfer?** Existing SFT results do not separate fidelity, interaction coverage, and data diversity. A next experiment could vary these factors while matching trajectories, training tokens, and task categories.
4. **Can mirrors remain accurate economically as sources change?** Maintenance efficiency is untested. A longitudinal experiment could compare incremental updates with full reconstruction, measuring workflow fidelity, staleness, and total operating cost.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed denominators and unusually explicit validity caveats, but several referenced table bodies and supplementary artifacts are absent.

Material missing information includes complete per-site metric tables, absolute Odysseys-All scores, full training configuration tables, original prompt supplements, evaluator calibration results, controlled component ablations, uncertainty estimates, complete operating costs, and confirmed public availability of individual mirrors and datasets.
