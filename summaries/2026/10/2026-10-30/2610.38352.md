# WeVisCut: The Whole Editing Room in One Agent From Narration to Long-Form Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38352) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38352)


## One-sentence takeaway
WeVisCut trains a shared 9B editing policy on executable video-production trajectories, achieving reported scores close to Claude Opus 5 with approximately one thirty-third as many generated tokens per successful output minute on a fixed 100-task benchmark (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Narration-driven video production requires coordinated planning, asset retrieval, speech synthesis, timeline assembly, and repair: changing one shot can affect later production decisions. The authors identify a gap between general-model editing workflows and compact policies trained for this task. Finished videos also lack the tool calls, intermediate failures, and repairs needed to supervise such policies (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Stateful production harness:** One policy serves orchestrator, generator, and critic roles. Typed tool contracts, persistent artifacts, prerequisite checks, and bounded repairs connect decisions to actual execution (§3).
- **Executable supervision:** Editing inversion recovers narration–shot intent; verified replay executes it; inspection and repair correct defects; trajectory materialization creates role-specific examples. The pipeline produces 134,771 examples from 3,750 Bilibili video projects (§4).
- **Task-adapted policy training:** Qwen3.5-9B undergoes supervised fine-tuning, runtime-verified rejection sampling, and Guided-OPD. The proposed RCF-DAPO adaptation compares candidate decisions within matched roles and production contexts (§5).
- **Evaluation framework:** A 100-task benchmark links scripts, plans, traces, and rendered videos, with five metrics covering fidelity, visual alignment, plan realization, consistency, and delivery (§6.1; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

All automatic comparisons below use 100 held-out tasks and five independent runs; Overall is a descriptive mean of five component means (§6.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Training improves the base policy | Shared tools: Overall 74.5 → 90.5; script fidelity 69.8 → 88.4; visual–narration match 59.2 → 74.6; rendering yield 70.4 → 96.7. | Table 1 |
| Scores approach Claude at lower token usage | WeVisCut versus Claude Opus 5: Overall 90.5 versus 89.9; visual–narration match 74.6 ± 2.3 versus 74.8 ± 1.1. Generated tokens per successful output minute: 2,377 versus 78,763, including failed attempts. | Table 1 |
| Native-system comparison favors WeVisCut | Overall: 90.5 versus Crayotter’s 84.9 and VideoAgent’s 82.5. Wall-clock minutes per successful output minute: 2.1 versus 8.6 and 10.4; estimated USD: 0.09 versus 0.97 and 0.58. | Table 2 |
| Successive training stages improve aggregate scores | Overall progresses from 74.5 base to 79.5 SFT, 83.5 after RFT, 84.9 after Guided-OPD, and 90.5 after RCF-DAPO, with backbone and environment fixed. | Table 3 |
| Human ratings favor WeVisCut | Three blinded raters on 100 tasks: mean overall-preference rating, on a 1–5 scale, is 4.56 versus 3.48 for Crayotter and 3.46 for VideoAgent. | Table 4; §6.5 |

These results support strong performance within the registered task contract. They do **not** establish statistical equivalence to Claude, independent causal effects of each training mechanism, or general video-editing superiority. Token efficiency measures policy generation rather than total production expense; native-workflow costs are reported separately. Overall combines partly dependent metrics with different eligibility populations (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Template coverage restricts visual styles; source projects cannot cover all domains or editing conventions; verified replay guarantees execution only within the registered runtime; retrieval stability limits quality (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** VLM evaluation remains vulnerable to judge bias and service drift. Human means do not establish statistical significance (§6.5; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Cumulative checkpoints cannot isolate RCF-DAPO’s context matching, role rewards, or recovery mechanisms. The authors explicitly acknowledge this attribution limit (§6.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Metric validation is described, but actual corruption-test results, judge agreement, human correlations, and eligibility counts are absent from the supplied text. Thus the stated VNSM agreement gate cannot be independently checked (Appendices B.1–B.3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix A.1 specifies intervention decay over 70% of training, whereas Table 5 specifies 70 steps out of 90; the intended schedule is unclear.

## Open research questions
1. **Which training mechanisms drive improvement?** Cumulative results leave component effects unresolved (§6.4). Run compute-matched ablations of context matching, role rewards, and Guided-OPD.
2. **Does learned repair improve recovery at depth?** Aggregate scores do not isolate recovery, and Appendix C.3 supplies a diagnostic protocol without numerical results. Inject matched late-stage faults and measure repair success and cost by depth.
3. **How well does the system transfer beyond its templates and source distribution?** Appendix F identifies both boundaries. Evaluate held-out domains, longer duration strata, and unfamiliar visual styles under controlled retrieval conditions.
4. **How robust are semantic rankings to evaluator choice?** VLM bias remains acknowledged and audit outcomes are unavailable. Repeat blinded human and cross-family judging on identical rendered outputs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline comparisons are clearly described, but their broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes actual metric-validation outcomes, per-metric eligibility and missingness counts, benchmark duration and language distributions, detailed cost assumptions, complete SFT/RFT settings, and numerical depth-recovery diagnostics. Code and weight availability are linked but not verified here.
