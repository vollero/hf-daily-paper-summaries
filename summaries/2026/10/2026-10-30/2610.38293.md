# EPSBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Earnings Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38293) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38293)


## One-sentence takeaway

EPSBench makes earnings forecasts traceable through accounting drivers, finding no detectable point-error difference between three leading agents and analyst consensus, but substantial undercoverage of every model’s stated uncertainty (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation

Forecasting earnings requires translating evidence about sales, costs, and exceptional events into a numerical outcome. The authors argue that existing EPS forecasting benchmarks mainly evaluate the final number, leaving intermediate assumptions difficult to compare. EPSBench records company-specific income-statement estimates and their supporting evidence so errors can be located within the accounting calculation (§1).

The target is **unreported GAAP diluted EPS after the quarter has ended**, using information available the day before the earnings release (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Accounting schemas:** Each firm-quarter has a year-ago baseline, forecast drivers, and a directed acyclic graph of accounting identities. A shared calculator converts submitted drivers into EPS (§3.2).
- **Controlled evaluation environment:** Agents retrieve dated filings, transcripts, and news through restricted tools, cite evidence for their estimates, and submit a point forecast plus an 80% interval. Evaluation combines forecast accuracy, interval quality, resource use, and compliance (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Benchmark and diagnostic assets:** The benchmark covers 149 firm-quarters from 86 US companies. The authors report releasing schemas, tools, prompts, forecasts, scores, and trajectories, subject to licensing restrictions (§1; Reproducibility statement).
- **Empirical analysis:** Six models are compared under a common harness, with additional source, harness, and reasoning-effort experiments. Driver records support descriptive analysis of where model errors differ (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results

PDE is absolute EPS error divided by the pre-release share price. Main point results aggregate each instance’s median PDE across three runs, then take the median across 149 instances; interval results pool runs returning intervals (§3.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Leading models have no resolved point-error difference from consensus | Under OpenCode with full information, Opus 4.6, GPT-5.5, and GPT-5.4 have median PDEs of 0.113%, 0.131%, and 0.126%, versus consensus at 0.111%. Each paired 90% bootstrap interval includes zero; each wins on 50% of instances. | §5.1, Table 1, Figure 2 |
| Other models have higher error | GPT-5-mini, Qwen 3.5, and GLM 4.5 Air have median PDEs of 0.191%, 0.190%, and 0.215%; paired comparisons indicate higher error than consensus. | §5.1, Table 1, Figure 2 |
| All models understate uncertainty | Nominal 80% intervals achieve 37–70% coverage among returned intervals. Outcomes exceed upper bounds in 20–38% of those runs, versus the intended 10%. | §5.2, Table 10 |
| Model separation increases on large earnings changes | On the 50 instances with the largest price-scaled departures from year-ago EPS, leading-model median PDE is 0.392–0.398%, versus 0.599–0.716% for trailing models and 0.409% for consensus. Groups are defined using realized outcomes. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Operating income accounts for most group differences | On 125 instances with an operating-income subtotal, median operating error on the PDE scale is 0.089–0.098 pp for leading models versus 0.149–0.166 pp for trailing models. | §5.3; Appendix G decomposition |
| Source removal has uneven effects | For GPT-5.5, keeping only news changes paired PDE by approximately 0.000 pp, with 90% CI [−0.010, +0.009]; removing both source groups raises it by 0.033 pp [0.019, 0.047]. Schemas and calculator remain available. | §5.4 |
| Harness and effort affect resources more than point accuracy | Across tested Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.5 alternatives, paired PDE changes range from −0.004 to +0.001 pp, with all five 90% intervals including zero; interval coverage and token use vary. | §5.5 |

These results support **unresolved differences**, not equivalence, between leading models and consensus. They establish undercoverage in this evaluation and locate forecast discrepancies within submitted accounting estimates. They do not establish causal explanations for model superiority, faithful internal reasoning, or investment usefulness (§3.4; Appendix A; Ethics statement).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors acknowledge unequal information access:** Analysts can use sources outside EPSBench, while agents may encounter consensus figures in news. The consensus comparison is therefore not a controlled information comparison (§3.4).
- **Temporal validity remains imperfect:** News effective dates approximate historical availability. Qwen’s recall probe finds no benchmark hits under its tested protocol, which does not prove absence of exposure (Appendices B.2, F).
- **Restricted reproducibility:** Transcript text and IBES consensus values are withheld. The original schema-generation model is no longer served, so extending the benchmark requires a replacement (Reproducibility statement).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Selection favors large, widely followed US companies, and source ablations use only GPT-5.5. Results need replication across other company populations and models (§§3.1, 5.4; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—scaffold benefit is unisolated:** The reported experiments retain schemas and calculator; they do not compare structured forecasting against direct EPS prediction under matched conditions (§§4–5).
- **Conditional interval reporting:** Coverage excludes missing intervals. GLM returns 409 of 447 intervals, with missing intervals attributed to context compaction (Table 10).

## Open research questions

- **Does accounting structure improve accuracy?** Its benefit is not isolated here. Compare direct EPS prediction against schema-based forecasting with matched models, documents, and budgets.
- **Can calibration improve without sacrificing accuracy?** Every model undercovers. Test held-out-quarter interval recalibration and driver-level uncertainty propagation, measuring both coverage and interval score.
- **Do source findings generalize?** Ablations cover one model. Repeat them across models and additional company populations, reporting instance-level effects.
- **Do numerical citations improve forecasts causally?** Their association with better adjustments is descriptive (§5.3). Randomize evidence requirements and audit citation relevance and correctness.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in the main reported findings; **medium** in broader interpretation.
