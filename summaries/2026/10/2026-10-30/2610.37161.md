# WorkFactory: Turning Professional Intents into Executable Work for Scalable Agent Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37161) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37161)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorkFactory combines backward information-dependency planning with execution-based environment repair to generate professional-task demonstrations that improve three Qwen models across four work benchmarks, although task correctness and frontier-model comparisons remain qualified.

## Problem and motivation
Professional-agent training needs diverse tasks whose supporting materials let teachers complete meaningful work. Missing premises or inconsistent files can produce unsupported assumptions and incomplete demonstrations; supplying derived answers instead can remove the intended reasoning. The authors identify a gap between assembling plausible artifacts and ensuring that every requested output has sufficient, consistent, solver-accessible support (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Information-level task construction:** Professional intents come from occupational hierarchies, documented skills, and real deliverables. An information support graph works backward from outputs, records dependencies and information locations, and separates supplied premises from results the solver must derive (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Execution-based repair:** An isolated probe attempts each task from visible inputs. Blocking diagnoses trigger constrained edits, construction checks, work-preservation review, and fresh execution; unresolved tasks are discarded (§3.4; Appendix C).
- **Training assets:** Independent GLM-5.3 trajectories from 112,686 tasks spanning 612 occupations train WorkPilot models at 4B, 27B, and 35B-A3B scales. The authors report releasing model weights and a task-data subset (§§1, 3.5).
- **Empirical analysis:** Matched-base comparisons, data-scaling studies, component analyses, and a task-matched teacher comparison examine the utility of coherent environments and resulting demonstrations (§§4.2–4.5).

The distinctive methodological contribution is planning information dependencies and their locations before materialization, then repairing execution blockers while preserving substantive solver work (§2).

The matched-base results support the usefulness of the complete training pipeline for the tested models and benchmarks. The component study supports incremental benefits from information planning and repair, but does not establish universal superiority over other synthesis frameworks.

“Frontier-level” refers to the range of compared GDPval-AA scores. WorkPilot ratings are locally calibrated estimates, while several comparator ratings are official. Bootstrap intervals exclude judge, anchor, calibration, and method-form uncertainty (Appendix E.1). The supplied text does not define the non-Elo metrics beyond calling them official benchmark metrics.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Training and task solving are text-only, leaving direct learning from visual evidence untested. Training uses SFT rather than reinforcement learning (Limitations).
- **Residual errors are explicitly possible:** Only blockers initiate repair; non-blocking correctness errors can remain. Most teacher rollouts receive lightweight validity filters rather than task-specific correctness judgments (Appendices C.1, D.3).
- **Analyst inference—probe dependence:** Defect reductions measure findings from a common model-based probe, not independently verified ground-truth defect rates. Fresh sandboxes prevent execution-history leakage, but do not establish diagnostic accuracy (§4.4; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited component generalization:** Component utility is tested with one student model on GDPval-AA. Table 3 supplies point estimates without uncertainty intervals or repeated-training variability (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Matched bases are the strongest controls. The main table does not provide a matched-data, matched-compute comparison against competing task-construction frameworks (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Cost claims are conditional:** The reported inference-cost frontier uses assumed cache hits and price proxies, excludes construction/training and operational overhead, and omits trials with execution exceptions. It does not establish deployment costs or total economic efficiency (§4.6; Appendix E.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Do probe-reported improvements match independent professional judgments?** Environment assessment relies on model diagnoses, and retained tasks may contain correctness errors. Next experiment: blind domain experts to construction condition and audit matched task environments and teacher deliverables (Appendices C.1, D.3).
2. **How much benefit comes from repair versus additional generation and selection effort?** The component study reports incremental gains without a matched-cost alternative. Next experiment: compare repair with extra candidate generation and filtering under equal token budgets (§4.4; Appendix D.2).
3. **What drives the teacher-dependent performance gap?** Teachers differ in interaction behavior, but the comparison does not isolate correctness, trajectory length, and tool-use style. Next experiment: train students on length- and quality-matched trajectories from both teachers (§4.5; Appendix D.4).
4. **Does the approach transfer to visual professional work?** Direct visual-input learning is outside the study. Next experiment: construct matched text-only and multimodal tasks, then compare both environment defects and student deliverable quality (Limitations).
