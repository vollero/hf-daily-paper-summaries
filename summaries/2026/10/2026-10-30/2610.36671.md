# Look Only When Needed: Certificate-Guided Visual Routing for Efficient Diffusion VLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36671) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36671)


## One-sentence takeaway
LOWN uses agreement between text-only predictions and the latest image-conditioned predictions to skip visual processing, retaining 99.6% of LLaDA-V’s normalized benchmark score at 35.3% of its decoding FLOPs across ten benchmarks (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Diffusion vision-language models repeatedly process an unchanged image during iterative decoding. Visual tokens account for 89–97% of LLaDA-V’s input on the evaluated benchmarks, making this repetition expensive (Table 6).

The authors identify an overlooked decision: whether a decoding step needs image access at all. Pruning reduces visual tokens, caching reuses computation, and faster schedules reduce steps; these approaches do not directly test that need. Their paired-forward measurements find substantial redundancy, but image dependence varies across tasks and decoding states, motivating adaptive routing (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Empirical motivation:** Compare image-conditioned FULL and TEXT-ONLY predictions along identical baseline trajectories, measuring when removing the image changes an unmasked decision (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Certificate-guided routing:** Store top-1 predictions and confidences from the latest FULL forward as an anchor. A TEXT-ONLY forward tests agreement in descending anchor-confidence order. A sufficiently long agreeing prefix permits image-free unmasking; otherwise, a FULL forward refreshes the anchor (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Adaptive decoding:** Accept the entire certified prefix on TEXT-ONLY steps; prioritize positions with FULL/TEXT-ONLY disagreement on FULL steps. A repetition guard stops acceptance before adjacent duplicate tokens. The method requires no training or tuned confidence threshold (§4.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Composition and transfer:** Evaluate the same decision rule with visual pruning, approximate key-value caching, and three DVLM backbones (§5.2).

“Certificate” denotes an empirical agreement test, not a guarantee that a fresh FULL forward would produce the same token.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline trajectories contain redundant visual processing | On LLaDA-V, FULL and TEXT-ONLY agree on 83.4% of unmasked decisions; image-dependent steps average 22.8%, ranging from 7.9% on MMBench to 43.8% on DocVQA. These are unweighted benchmark means. | Table 6; §3.2 |
| Strong aggregate quality–compute trade-off | Across ten LLaDA-V benchmarks, normalized quality is 99.6% at 0.353× decoding FLOPs. Capping each benchmark’s quality at baseline before averaging yields 98.6%. LOWN leads accelerated comparators on eight benchmarks. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Measured latency also improves | Mean relative generation time is 0.426× across ten LLaDA-V benchmarks: a 57.4% reduction. Measurements use H200 GPUs, batch size one, and one recorded full-set execution. | Table 10; Appendix D.1 |
| Pruning composition reduces cost further | LOWN plus RedVTP at 50% retention achieves 98.3% normalized quality at 0.223× LLaDA-V decoding FLOPs. | Table 1 |
| Caching composition trades some quality for speed | Adding LOWN to Fast-dLLM reduces generation time by 38.1–50.1% across three tasks, but lowers scores on all three; LLaVA-Bench falls from 63.00 to 58.00. | Table 2 |
| Transfer extends beyond LLaDA-V | LaViDa retains 99.7% normalized quality at 0.174× FLOPs across ten benchmarks. Under the controlled VidLaDA protocol, EgoSchema accuracy is 60.2 versus 60.6 at 0.255× FLOPs; MLVU M-Avg is 43.3 versus 41.8 at 0.272×. | Tables 13, 3; Appendix C.4 |
| Routing and acceptance both contribute | Routing alone reduces FLOPs by approximately 27–68%; adaptive acceptance removes another 21–43% of the remaining cost across ten LLaDA-V benchmarks. | Table 5 |
| Agreement is imperfect | On LOWN trajectories, 2.2–4.7% of tokens accepted on TEXT-ONLY steps disagree with a fresh FULL prediction; 94.8% use a one-step-old anchor. | Tables 15–16 |

The evidence supports substantial decoding savings with small aggregate quality changes in the tested settings. The normalized average combines different metrics, including a special additive normalization for CapArena; it is not pooled accuracy (Appendix C.1). Comparator budgets are selected from discrete grids at or above LOWN’s cost, rather than matched exactly. Token agreement also does not establish correctness against ground truth.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reused predictions can differ from fresh FULL predictions, with disagreement generally increasing as anchors age. Chart and document tasks save less, and decoding cost cannot be fixed in advance (§6; Appendix F.2).
- **Author-stated scope:** Video evaluation uses a controlled protocol differing from VidLaDA’s released evaluation, including disabled baseline parallel decoding (Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer across the tested LLaDA-based models does not establish applicability to other DVLM families or stochastic decoding.
- **Analyst inference:** Latency generalization is uncertain because timing covers batch size one on H200 GPUs, with no repeated-run variability reported (Appendix D.1).
- **Accounting boundary:** FLOPs exclude vision encoding and non-matrix operations, whereas measured generation time includes preparation and vision encoding (Appendices B.2, D.1).
- **Statistical uncertainty:** Main quality comparisons lack reported confidence intervals; some tasks use model judges, and LLaVA-Bench contains only 60 questions (Appendices B–D).

## Open research questions
- **Can acceptance disagreement be reduced efficiently?** Fresh FULL predictions overturn some accepted tokens. Test an anchor-age cap or selective refresh, measuring quality and FLOPs against unchanged LOWN (Table 15).
- **Does routing transfer beyond the tested model family?** Evaluate the unchanged rule on other DVLM architectures and stochastic decoding; current transfer evidence covers LLaDA-based backbones.
- **Does the latency benefit persist under serving workloads?** Measure throughput and latency across batch sizes and hardware, since current timing is limited to batch-one H200 runs (Appendix D.1).
- **How much video acceleration survives the released protocol?** Compare against VidLaDA with its released frame count, response budget, and parallel decoding enabled; the reported controlled protocol differs materially (Appendix C.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the method and reported results; broader applicability remains unestablished.

Material missing information includes repeated-run timing uncertainty, confidence intervals for main quality differences, serving-scale throughput, results outside the tested backbone family, and independently verified code contents.
