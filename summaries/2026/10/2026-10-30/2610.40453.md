# PhysInsert: Physically Plausible Object Insertion with Geometric Priors and Bidirectional Object-Scene Interactions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40453) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40453)


## One-sentence takeaway
PhysInsert combines controlled rendered training pairs, supervised geometry features, and spatial supervision to improve reference-based object insertion, although its evaluations establish visual plausibility rather than verified physical correctness.

## Problem and motivation
Reference-based insertion must preserve an object’s identity while adapting its pose, perspective, illumination, and effects on the surrounding scene. The authors argue that masked inputs, imperfect generated training pairs, and limited reference-view variation inadequately supervise these coupled changes—especially shadows, reflections, occlusion, and text-directed placement (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **InsertWorld dataset:** Aligned Blender-rendered scenes differing only in object presence capture both object appearance and environmental changes. Training includes 12,951 counterfactual pairs and 275,310 references for 1,710 objects, with varied viewpoints, lighting, and generated spatial instructions (§3).
- **Geometry-Guided Adaptation (GGA):** A geometry stream initialized from frozen VGGT background features interacts with image and text streams in Qwen-Image-Edit-2511. Training readouts predict VGGT features of the completed scene; target features provide supervision only (§4.1.1).
- **2D Object–Scene Spatial Supervision:** COSS supervises object occupancy and external image changes; SSG aligns spatial-text attention with object regions. Difference-derived effect labels are proxies, not separate semantic labels for shadows or reflections (§4.1.2).
- **Adaptation benchmarks:** PhysInsert-Bench tests controlled optical and structural adaptation; PhysInsert-Bench-Wild adds real-world cases with differing reference viewpoints (§4.2; Appendix I).

These results support improved image fidelity, placement, and perceived integration in the reported settings. Component ablations support benefits beyond training-data changes alone. They do not establish physical correctness: geometry scores compare estimated depths, Phy-VLM uses a learned judge, and human ratings assess visible plausibility. The overlapping Phy-VLM intervals do not establish a clear advantage over Nano Banana Pro.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Identity preservation is not explicitly optimized and remains limited under substantial reference–target viewpoint changes (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** External-effect targets identify visible changes rather than decomposing individual optical effects. Automatic foreground extraction can omit parts or select background regions, and estimated-depth metrics inherit estimator and paired-context errors (§4.1.2; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Full-image fidelity can reflect background preservation as well as insertion quality. DINO-Obj. also retains crop context, so it is not an isolated identity measurement (Appendices D.1, D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence remains bounded by the reported real-world sets. DreamBooth backgrounds involve generated removal; some Wild references come from reconstructed objects, introducing additional construction dependencies (Appendix I.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied ablations remove whole components but do not separately isolate geometry-stream architecture from target-feature supervision, or COSS occupancy from effect supervision (Table 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Human-study confidence intervals resample participants while holding cases fixed, limiting claims about uncertainty across new cases. Phy-VLM explicitly excludes size errors and retries unclear judgments; retry frequencies are unreported (Appendices D.5, K).

## Open research questions
1. **Can multiple references preserve distinctive identity across large viewpoint changes?** Appendix A identifies this unresolved failure. Test controlled viewpoint-gap bins with matched single-view and multiview inputs, reporting identity and placement separately.
2. **Which geometric mechanism supplies the gains?** Whole-GGA removal does not isolate its ingredients. Compare a background-only geometry stream, the dynamic stream without alignment loss, and full target-feature supervision under matched budgets.
3. **Does effect supervision improve specific physical interactions?** COSS combines occupancy and aggregate external-change targets. Ablate its two channels separately and report shadow, reflection, and contact performance by benchmark tag.
4. **How well do gains survive independently captured real scenes?** Current real-world construction includes edited backgrounds and reconstructed references. Evaluate a larger captured with/without dataset using real multiview references and case-level uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported comparisons; **medium** in broader physical-validity and generalization claims.

Material information unavailable includes confirmed code/data/checkpoint release status, training wall-clock cost, independent training-seed variability, complete per-tag performance, Phy-VLM null/retry frequencies, and universal dataset-retention thresholds with historical annotation configurations (Appendix J.1).
