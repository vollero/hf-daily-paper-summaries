# Glance: The Generator Is Its Own Reranker

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40454) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40454)


## One-sentence takeaway
Glance uses lightweight adapters and passage-specific probes to make one generator backbone rerank retrieved evidence, improving QA in the tested setting while remaining slower per query than a dedicated cross-encoder.

## Problem and motivation
RAG commonly serves separate reranking and generation models, duplicating model residency and passage processing. The authors ask whether the generator can judge candidate passages using shared computation, reducing the need for a separate reranker backbone (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared-prefill reranking:** Candidate passages are encoded together once; short, isolated probe branches produce passage relevance scores through a rank-16 LoRA adapter and a 256-dimensional pointer head. The backbone remains frozen (§§3.2–3.4).
- **One-backbone serving:** Separate adapters support general decisions and reranking alongside base generation. The shared-state machinery comes from Kev; the contribution is its application and training for generator-based RAG reranking (§§3.5, 4.6, 5).
- **Two answer pipelines:** Main Glance re-prefills selected passages with the adapter off. Glance-Activated applies adaptation only to probes, enabling generation from the original cache, which retains **all** candidates (§§3.1, 4.7).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Retrieval, downstream QA, candidate-count scaling, adapter switching, and prototype vLLM serving are evaluated (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Trained reranking exceeds the tested cross-encoder | BM25 top-20: SciFact nDCG@10 **0.741 vs. 0.721** over 300 queries; NFCorpus **0.345 vs. 0.326** over 323 queries. Adapter trained on MS MARCO for 39 minutes; differences not significance-tested. | §§3.6, 4.1, 4.3; Table 3 |
| Sharing computation speeds up same-model scoring, but cross-encoder is faster | Zero-shot SciFact top-20: shared probes **614 ms/query**, sequential separate calls **2,210 ms**, cross-encoder **31 ms**; respective nDCG@10 **0.715, 0.729, 0.721**. Shared RTX PRO 6000. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Trained rankings improve downstream QA | First 300 HotpotQA distractor validation questions; top-3 context, same adapter-off generator: trained Glance F1 **0.729 vs. 0.684**, difference **+0.045**, paired-bootstrap 95% CI **[0.014, 0.076]**. | §4.5; Table 5 |
| Longer-context training supports more candidates | First 100 SciFact queries, 100-word passages: trained-long at 200 candidates/43.4k tokens achieves nDCG@10 **0.717 vs. 0.659** for cross-encoder; chunked probes take **15.79 s/query** on B200. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Activated cache continuation preserves similar QA scores in a pilot | 100 HotpotQA questions, H100: continued full cache F1 **0.750**, versus **0.754** after re-prefilling top-3; median answer latency **138 vs. 181 ms**. Single seed. | §4.7 |
| Multiple adapters coexist with small residency overhead | Three-mode switching preserves standalone outputs in the tested loop; reported residency **16.85 GiB**, with roughly **0.1 GiB** per additional decision skill. | §4.6; Table 6 |

The results support generator-based reranking and a statistically supported QA improvement under the same base generator in this sample. They do not establish broad retrieval superiority, end-to-end latency superiority, or learned answer utility: training uses relevance labels, and BEIR comparisons lack significance tests. Activated continuation demonstrates cache reuse but does not filter the generator’s context (§§4.5, 4.7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors:** Evaluation covers two retrieval datasets, one QA dataset, and one backbone family; most samples contain only hundreds of queries (§5).
- **Authors:** The trained adapter was not compared against trained separate-passage calls. BEIR differences are not significance-tested (§5).
- **Authors:** Timing uses different, sometimes shared GPUs and an unoptimized path. Current cache copying increases memory with candidate count × context length; cross-query batching reduces throughput (§§4.1, 4.4).
- **Authors:** Passage scores depend on neighbors and order. Activated results are single-seed, and the exact cache check used a smaller model (§§4.7–4.8).
- **Authors:** General chat checks are limited; reranking specialization reduces general-decision performance. The vLLM prototype differs numerically from Kev (§§4.5–4.6, 4.9).
- **Analyst inference:** Keeping all candidates during activated generation prevents attributing its QA outcome to context selection alone (§4.7).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the benefit generalize across backbones and domains?** Only one family and limited datasets are tested. Evaluate multiple backbone families on broader retrieval and QA suites with repeated seeds and confidence intervals.
2. **How much comes from shared passage comparison?** Trained separate-passage scoring is missing. Compare shared and isolated scoring using the same trained adapter, candidates, and hardware.
3. **Does activated reranking help answers when all candidates remain visible?** Its cache retains every passage. Compare continuation with versus without probes and with explicit score guidance across larger candidate pools.
4. **Can optimized shared-cache serving become competitive in latency?** Cache copying and batching remain bottlenecks. Implement shared branch access and benchmark quality, memory, throughput, and full RAG latency against the cross-encoder pipeline.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this account of the supplied evidence; broader effectiveness remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes retrieval significance tests, trained isolated-scoring results, broad multi-seed evaluation, cross-backbone replication, comprehensive chat assessment, and optimized full-pipeline serving comparisons. The reported asset releases were not independently verified.
