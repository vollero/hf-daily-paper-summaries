# AskStream: When and What to Ask Proactively under Uncertainty in Streaming Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38678) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38678)


## One-sentence takeaway
AskStream benchmarks when and what streaming video assistants should ask under missing information, finding substantial failures in asking decisions even among its strongest evaluated models.

## Problem and motivation
Streaming assistants may lack visual evidence, personalized concepts, clear user intent, or current preferences. Waiting cannot necessarily resolve these gaps, while answering prematurely risks unsupported responses. The authors argue that existing proactive video benchmarks emphasize when to answer and insufficiently evaluate active information acquisition (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interaction formulation:** A WAIT–ASK–ANSWER action space, modeled as a partially observable decision process, distinguishes situations requiring observation before asking from those allowing immediate clarification (§3.1).
- **AskStream benchmark:** Four uncertainty categories and eight subtasks cover missing visual evidence, viewpoints, personalized entities/actions, ambiguous or incorrect questions, and missing or outdated preferences. It contains 438 videos and 1,401 questions, with manually annotated action windows (§3.3–3.5).
- **Ask-F1 metric:** A harmonic aggregation of timing-aware asking and answering scores. Answering is scored conditionally on a correct, timely ASK; within-window credit decreases with delay. This is an F1-style score, rather than conventional precision–recall F1 (§3.2; Appendix A.2).
- **StreamHarness and diagnostic evaluation:** Hierarchical memory combines recent frames with compressed history for offline models evaluated causally on streams. Experiments compare multiple-choice and open-ended interaction and examine oracle decisions, memory, and asking budgets (§4; Appendix A.6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant revealed evidence improves answer accuracy | Qwen3.5 9B on 130 five-choice events from 78 videos: blurred input **42.3%**, ask-and-reveal **62.3%**, fully visible **63.1%** accuracy. | Figure 2; Appendix A.3 |
| Best multiple-choice scores remain limited | Gemini-3.8 Flash achieves **52.18% Ask-F1** overall; best streaming proactive model, AURA, achieves **7.22%**. Gemini’s asking/answering subscores are **44.63%/62.80%**. | Table 2 |
| Open-ended interaction is harder overall | Gemini achieves **29.19% Ask-F1**, with **23.34%/38.97%** asking/answering subscores; AURA achieves **4.01% Ask-F1**. | Table 6; Appendix A.4 |
| Asking timing is a major recoverable bottleneck | Gemini, multiple-choice KVEM: Ask-F1 rises from **37.17% to 58.30%** with oracle ASK timing, and to **81.0%** with oracle timing and ASK content: **+21.13** and **+43.83 percentage points**, respectively. | Figure 6 |
| Hierarchical context helps in the tested comparison | Gemini, multiple-choice overall Ask-F1: StreamHarness **52.18%**, sliding window **41.70%**, uniform 128-frame sampling **26.72%**, uniform 64-frame sampling **16.99%**. | Table 5 |
| Additional asking opportunities help but saturate | Gemini, open-ended KVEM: Ask-F1 increases from **27.61%** with budget 1 to **52.35%** with budget 16. | Figure 7(b); Appendix A.2 |
| Evaluation checks support consistency | Open-ended judge agrees with human consensus on **93.50%** of ASK/ANSWER correctness judgments across 300 sampled turns; alternative temporal weights preserve all seven offline-model rankings. | Tables 3–4 |

These results support the authors’ claim that asking is a substantial bottleneck under this benchmark’s rules. The oracle result is specific to KVEM and one model, rather than an established benchmark-wide improvement. Offline and native streaming models use different memory mechanisms, so their score gap does not isolate architecture or training effects (§4.1.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Main evaluation permits one ASK per turn, requires an explicit user query, excludes coordinated physical actions, and simplifies potentially overlapping or changing action opportunities (Appendix A.1). The budget experiment partially probes repeated asking.
- **Analyst inference—asking necessity is prescribed:** Benchmark prompts require a correct ASK before answering. Results therefore do not establish whether models can decide when asking is unnecessary or balance clarification against user burden (Appendix A.8.1).
- **Analyst inference—constructed uncertainty:** Blurring, synthetic action concepts, corrupted questions, and constructed preferences enable controlled tests, but transfer to naturally occurring uncertainty remains untested in the reported experiments (Appendix A.7).
- **Analyst inference—setting comparisons have confounds:** ICM instructions differ across multiple-choice and open-ended settings, and AUQ requires exact answer reproduction. Thus, setting differences cannot be attributed solely to free-form generation difficulty (Appendix A.8.2–A.8.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited diagnostic coverage:** Oracle and asking-budget experiments use one model on KVEM; StreamHarness ablation also uses one model. Broader generalization of these findings is unresolved (§4.3; Appendix A.6).
- Judge validation and temporal annotation consistency checks provide useful evidence, but confidence intervals and repeated-run variability are not reported.

## Open research questions
- **Can models learn when asking is unnecessary?** All main tasks require ASK. Add answerable-without-clarification controls and measure unnecessary asks alongside successful answers.
- **Does timing remain the main bottleneck across uncertainty types?** Oracle analysis covers KVEM only. Repeat the stage-wise oracle experiment across all subtasks and multiple models.
- **Can repeated clarification resolve genuinely cascading uncertainty?** The budget protocol permits recovery, while the authors identify newly revealed information gaps as a limitation. Construct chained clarifications and measure success, interaction cost, and latency.
- **How well does performance transfer to natural interactions?** Much uncertainty is deliberately constructed. Evaluate real user sessions with naturally missing evidence and independent human judgments.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and descriptive results; **medium** in broader generalization.

Material missing information includes confirmed code/data release locations and licenses, repeated-run uncertainty, detailed inference resource costs, and evaluation on natural live interactions. The annotation total is also described inconsistently as 2,714 “time windows” in the abstract and “timestamps” in Figure 4.
