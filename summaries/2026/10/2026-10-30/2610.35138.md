# Co-Design Bench: Multimodal Collaborative Coding Evaluation for Agentic Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35138) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35138)


## One-sentence takeaway
Co-Design Bench evaluates real-user application revisions with hidden acceptance criteria and browser-backed verification, finding that its strongest tested endpoint satisfies 69.9% of criteria per task on average but completes only 26.4% of tasks (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Coding agents must translate requests, attachments, and existing application context into revisions that work across interface, interaction, and state. The authors identify limited benchmark coverage of compound requests recovered from real coding sessions, especially across both localized multimodal edits and coordinated repository changes (§1).

Average requirement coverage can also obscure incomplete delivery, while execution conditions materially affect measured performance; the benchmark therefore evaluates iterative tool use and reports complete-task success alongside criterion completion (§§1, 4.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Session-derived benchmark:** 273 revisions with runnable starting applications and 2,331 expert-authored hidden criteria, curated from 37,463 deduplicated queries across 26,314 sessions. Multimodal Editing (ME) covers localized revisions; Repository Editing (RE) covers coordinated changes (§3).
- **Reference-free acceptance protocol:** A primary evaluator grades source and browser evidence; a separate verifier challenges positive verdicts. Audits require screenshots for visual claims and recorded actions for interaction claims, without requiring gold code or target screenshots (§4.1).
- **Evaluation and diagnostic assets:** Ten endpoints share a solver harness, with strict, graded, and full-pass metrics plus modality, edit-breadth, evaluator, and harness analyses (§§4.2–5; Appendices C, H). This is an evaluation contribution rather than a new solver.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial criterion coverage leaves many tasks unfinished | Across 273 equally weighted tasks, gpt-6-astra achieves Strict Completion 0.699 and Full-Pass Rate 26.4%, leading ten endpoints. | §5.2 |
| Complete delivery is lower on RE for the leading endpoint | gpt-6-astra: ME strict 0.754 and full-pass 41.0% over 100 tasks; RE strict 0.667 and full-pass 17.9% over 173 tasks. | §5.2 prose |
| Broader repository edits have lower criterion coverage | Pooled across ten endpoints, RE tasks with 1–2 edit types score 0.654 strict versus 0.591 for 3–4 types; every endpoint declines. Full-pass rates remain similar: 9.1% versus 9.7%. | §5.3; Fig. 4b; Table 6 discussion |
| Iterative execution changes measured completion | On the same 150 WebCompass-edit tasks, qwen3.7-plus improves from 73.6% to 95.1% graded completion and from 32.7% to 69.3% full-pass, holding rubric, judge, and rendering fixed. | Appendix A; Fig. 1a |
| Limited evaluator sensitivity check preserves a track-specific ordering | For stored kimi-k3 and Qwen3.8-27B outputs, four evaluator configurations shift graded scores by at most 0.053 while preserving their opposite ordering across ME and RE. | Appendix C |
| Model comparisons depend on the harness | Across three harnesses and all 273 tasks, the kimi-k3 minus Qwen3.8-27B score gap spans 10.0 percentage points on ME and 16.2 on RE; their RE ordering changes. | Appendix H |

The evidence supports a gap between requirement coverage and complete delivery under the tested conditions. Modality and edit-breadth comparisons are descriptive, since task content differs between groups. The paired WebCompass experiment supports an execution-regime effect for that endpoint and split; it does not establish a controlled difficulty comparison between benchmarks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Requests are consolidated into fixed tasks without subsequent user feedback, limiting evaluation of ongoing collaboration (§3.2).
- **Selection boundaries:** Curation prioritizes difficulty, verifiability, and historical failures, while excluding inaccessible services. Results therefore describe a selected, executable task pool (§3.2; Appendix I).
- **Confounded subgroup comparisons:** Capability labels strongly overlap with tracks, and modality groups differ in task content. Image tasks and some capability categories are particularly small (§5.3; Appendix F).
- **Evaluator uncertainty:** The authors acknowledge residual judgment error. Two experts reviewed 200 outputs, but the supplied text provides no agreement rate or measured evaluator error (§4.1; Appendix J).
- **Limited robustness scope:** Evaluator and alternative-harness checks cover two solvers. Judge-only rescoring may differ in sampling and serving; whole-harness swaps cannot isolate individual practices (Appendices C, H).
- **Analyst inference:** The evidence-audit protocol is plausible, but its incremental benefit remains unquantified because no isolated refutation/audit ablation is reported. Several numerical tables are incompletely rendered in the supplied text, further limiting independent checking.

## Open research questions
- **Does refutation improve judgment accuracy?** Its error reduction is unmeasured. Compare primary-only and judge-then-refute verdicts against independently adjudicated criteria.
- **What closes the criterion-to-task completion gap?** The benchmark documents unfinished requirements without isolating remedies. Test explicit requirement tracking and final browser verification under matched execution budgets.
- **Does edit breadth independently reduce performance?** Existing groups differ in content and context. Construct matched revisions with increasing change types while controlling rubric size.
- **How does live clarification affect delivery?** Fixed requests omit further feedback. Compare fixed-request runs with a controlled user-feedback condition on ambiguous tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central findings are explicit and repeated, but some table bodies or columns are missing from the retrieved text.

Material gaps include complete numerical leaderboards and robustness tables, exact production judge/verifier identities and prompts, quantified human agreement, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variability, isolated refutation/audit ablations, and precise asset access and licensing details.
