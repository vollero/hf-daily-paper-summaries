# Broken on Arrival: Non-Functional Quantized LLM Artifacts in Public Model Registries and How to Catch Them

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05881) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05881)


## One-sentence takeaway
Seven of 305 eligible Ollama artifacts nearly failed Python code generation, yet five rebuilt failures exactly matched their source-derived quantizations, demonstrating that reproducibility alone cannot establish functionality (§§IV-A–B).

## Problem and motivation
Public registries distribute quantized LLM files without documented functional testing in the pages and metadata examined. A downloadable artifact can generate fluent text while failing its intended task, and provenance checks cannot establish whether it works. The paper addresses two gaps: measuring such failures across an eligible registry population and separating file defects from quantization-recipe, model, and backend effects (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Execution census and audit data:** Test every eligible code-capable instruct tag within selected Ollama model lines, plus a popularity-selected Hugging Face community sample. Screen artifacts on easy Python tasks and confirm suspects with HumanEval+ (§§III-A–B).
- **Recipe-matched attribution:** Rebuild suspect artifacts from original checkpoints using matching quantization recipes, compare tensors independently of metadata and ordering, and test calibration while controlling weights, toolchain, and tensor mix (§III-C).
- **Measured acceptance screen:** Release the 15-task screen, scorer, rebuild and comparison scripts as **quantcheck**, together with transcripts, scores, hashes, and run logs. The contribution is an audit and attribution workflow, rather than a new quantization algorithm (§V; Data Availability).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Near-total failures occur in the eligible census | Of 305 official artifacts across 35 variants, seven tags (2.3%) in three variants (8.6%) were suspects: six solved 0/164 HumanEval+ tasks and one solved 6/164. | §IV-A |
| Exact rebuilding reproduced failure | All five rebuilt suspects matched registry tensors: 434 tensors per Qwen artifact and 197 for phi3.5. Their matching plain rebuilds also failed. | §IV-B |
| Calibration rescued three Qwen levels substantially | With weights, toolchain, and tensor mix fixed, imatrix raised Qwen2.5-Coder-3B from 0/164 to 106–113 at q2_K, 76–89 at q3_K_M, and 80–86 at q3_K_L; its registry 4-bit tag solved 132/164. | §IV-B; Fig. 3 |
| Rescue was incomplete and configuration-dependent | Qwen q3_K_S remained at 13–30/164 across calibrated builds. For phi3.5, changing the tensor mix raised 0/164 to 21/164; adding imatrix at that current mix raised 21 to 37/164. | §IV-B; Fig. 3 |
| Recipe alone did not predict collapse | Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B registry artifacts and matching uncalibrated rebuilds scored 13–15/15 at q2_K through q3_K_L, whereas the corresponding 3B artifacts failed. | §IV-B; Table VI |
| The screen detected confirmed suspects, but misses remain bounded loosely | All seven suspects solved at most 6/164. None of 66 audited unflagged tags was non-functional; the seeded sample supported a conservative 95% upper bound of 22 failures among 223 other tags (9.9%). | §IV-D |
| Cheap indicators had limited validation | A repetition-or-truncation rule at 0.3 caught four suspects and six other tags. A post hoc threshold of 10–25× fp16 perplexity flagged all five measured non-functional builds; population false-positive rate was unknown. | §IV-C; Table VII |
| Screening was inexpensive relative to full evaluation | Generation took 12–105 seconds per artifact, median 30 seconds, on one consumer GPU, excluding downloads. | §IV-D |

These results establish that the five rebuilt failures were reproducible uncalibrated quantizations, rather than corrupted tensors under the paper’s attribution rule. Controlled comparisons support an imatrix benefit in the tested builds. They do not establish registry-wide failure prevalence beyond the eligibility frame, universal screen sensitivity, or a general explanation for model susceptibility.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The screen measures Python code generation and targets near-total failure, not ordinary degradation or other capabilities. Its tasks were selected using Qwen configurations, the family containing most confirmed failures (§VI).
- **Incomplete attribution:** The two llama3.2-1B failures were neither rebuilt nor replicated on Metal. Community backend-dependent failures and the uniformly weak deepseek-coder-1.3B group remain unattributed (§§IV-A–B; VI).
- **Calibration mechanism remains mixed:** Supplying imatrix also changes weighted scale search; the experiment isolates the whole imatrix intervention, not calibration text alone. Third-party AWQ comparisons additionally change the output head (§IV-B).
- **Evaluation and planning risks:** A scoring timeout fault required serial correction. The rescue rule followed initial rebuild results, and the run plan was not publicly preregistered (§§III-B–C; VI).
- **Analyst inference:** Zero observed failures in the audited unflagged sample offers limited assurance about unseen tags; the paper’s conservative population bound remains substantial (§IV-D).

## Open research questions
- **What predicts collapse across models?** The same recipe works for the tested smaller sibling but fails for the larger one. Next experiment: apply matched plain/calibrated quantization ladders across additional model families and sizes (§IV-B).
- **Why does calibrated Qwen Q3_K remain weak?** The authors leave its residual perplexity and correctness gap unexplained. Next experiment: separately vary tensor allocation, scale search, and calibration inputs while holding source weights fixed (§IV-B).
- **Can screening generalize beyond Python?** Other capabilities are untested. Next experiment: construct independent screens for additional tasks and validate against full evaluations on model families excluded from tuning (§VI).
- **Can perplexity provide a reliable pre-check?** Thresholds were chosen after inspecting a small measured set. Next experiment: preregister thresholds and measure sensitivity and false-positive rates across a broader artifact census (§IV-C).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported census outcomes and controlled rebuild findings; **medium** for broader acceptance-gate effectiveness.
