# ALPS: Visual State Encoders for World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30120) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30120)


## One-sentence takeaway
ALPS combines state-sensitive visual tokens, native language alignment, and pixel decoding through still-image pretraining and cross-view refinement, achieving strong simulated control and planning results without leading every task.

## Problem and motivation
Frozen visual encoders determine which scene properties downstream world models can predict and use for planning. The authors identify a gap: existing encoders typically emphasize physical-state information, language alignment, or reconstruction, while a shared representation needs all three to support prediction, instructions, and visual inspection. Improving spatial features can also damage an encoder’s existing language interface (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint visual pretraining:** Train a ViT-B/16 from scratch on DataComp images, web captions, and synthetic captions. Contrastive alignment, caption generation, pixel reconstruction, and dense cross-view correspondence supervise the same unnormalized patch tokens. Language embeddings use a linear projection and mean pooling (§3.1).
- **Geometry-conditioned refinement:** Initialize a student and frozen teacher from Stage 1. The student predicts standardized teacher features across crops using position and relative-scale queries, supervising only overlapping regions. Refinement uses individual images without edit-pair, video, or interaction supervision (§3.2).
- **Unified evaluation and interfaces:** Evaluate frozen representations for state probing, language alignment, reconstruction, control, and world-model prediction/planning. The resulting tokens support a native text interface and separately trained pixel decoders, including decoding predicted future tokens (§§4–5).

The contribution is the combined recipe and demonstrated balance of capabilities; the individual loss families build on prior methods (§2).

These results support a useful balance across interfaces under the supplied protocols. They do not establish universal encoder superiority, statistically decisive policy gains over the closest competitor, or successful integration of language-guided planning and pixel verification into one system.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation focuses on ViT-B/16 and simulation; larger models and real robots remain untested. The recipe requires two stages, has limited fine-grained temporal sensitivity, and evaluates language-conditioned control separately from world-model planning (§6).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism remains uncertain:** Linear mean pooling is a plausible explanation for language retention, but comparisons also differ in architecture and pretraining. The supplied experiments do not isolate pooling as the causal factor (§5.2; §S5).
- **Analyst inference—ablation scope:** Objective ablations use reduced pretraining budgets and always retain contrastive alignment; their conclusions may not transfer unchanged to the full recipe (§5.4; §S4).
- **Comparison boundaries:** Some baselines run below native resolution; the paper tests higher-resolution frames and selected intermediate layers, but the main ranking remains protocol-dependent (§S2).
- **Decoder/data confounds:** The stronger decoding experiment changes capacity, training duration, and data together. The Stage 2 image-source comparison changes both scene composition and native resolution (§S2; §S3).

## Open research questions
- **Does the balance transfer to real robots?** Simulation-only downstream evidence leaves deployment validity open. Next experiment: matched frozen-encoder policies on real manipulation tasks with visual shifts (§6).
- **What causes language retention?** Architecture and pretraining differences remain entangled. Next experiment: vary pooling and token normalization while matching data, objectives, and refinement (§S5).
- **Can temporal refinement add action sensitivity without losing other interfaces?** Static refinement improves correspondence but leaves action recognition largely unchanged. Next experiment: add video or interaction supervision and reevaluate all three interfaces (§S2; §6).
- **Can all interfaces improve one planner?** Language control and inspectable planning are demonstrated separately. Next experiment: evaluate instruction-conditioned planning with decoded rollout verification (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings and scope; **medium** in the proposed explanation for language retention.

Material gaps in the supplied text include missing bodies for several core and supplementary tables, the detailed Stage 2 source-mixture table, full uncertainty estimates for most encoder rankings, hardware/runtime costs, and verified release contents. The paper says complete reproduction configurations will be released; their availability is not established here.
