# CompactSplat: Spatially Adaptive Gaussian Distribution for Feedforward Scene Reconstruction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13177) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13177)


## One-sentence takeaway

CompactSplat uses texture-driven quadtree partitioning and scale-aware Gaussian sampling to reduce sparse-view scene representation size with inference-time sparsity control, although rendering-quality gains vary by dataset and metric (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Feedforward 3D Gaussian reconstruction avoids per-scene optimization, but assigning one or more primitives to every input pixel wastes representation capacity in homogeneous regions. The authors seek to preserve detailed reconstruction while reducing Gaussian counts and allowing users to adjust representation size without retraining (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Texture-Aware Partitioner (TAP):** Recursively subdivides image blocks when their maximum channel-wise intensity range exceeds a threshold. Homogeneous regions receive larger blocks; textured regions receive finer partitions (§3.2).
- **Hierarchical Pyramid Sampler (HPS):** Samples Gaussian attributes at the partition’s corresponding pyramid level and multiplies predicted relative scales by block dimensions, allowing fewer primitives to cover broader regions. The detailed formulation constructs the pyramid from a full-resolution attribute field (§3.3).
- **Controllable sparsity with one checkpoint:** Training first uses a fixed zero threshold, then randomly sampled thresholds to learn multiple sparsity settings. At inference, changing the threshold adjusts primitive counts without retraining (§3.4).

The dual-branch encoder and Gaussian processing incorporate prior architectures and SurfSplat components; the proposed contributions center on allocation, sampling, and sparsity control (Appendix A.2).

The tables support reduced primitive counts and competitive rendering at selected operating points. They do **not** support the blanket claim that CompactSplat consistently matches or exceeds every baseline across datasets and metrics. Primitive counts also do not directly establish measured memory savings, rendering speed, or geometric watertightness; the supplied evidence for geometry is primarily rendering-based and qualitative (§4.1; Figure 3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Orthogonal quadtree grids constrain allocation flexibility, and redundancy across overlapping source views remains largely untreated (Appendix A.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Texture is an imperfect geometry proxy. The authors acknowledge that detailed texture can occur on flat surfaces, but provide no targeted test establishing their stronger claim that texture reliably captures all complex geometry (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Component attribution is uncertain: the supplied text provides no isolated ablations for TAP, pyramid pooling, scale modulation, curriculum training, or inherited SurfSplat processing (§3; Appendix A.2).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §3.1 and Figure 2 describe sampling across decoder features, whereas §3.3 specifies a pooled pyramid of full-resolution attributes.
- **Comparison limits:** YonoSplat uses a different evaluation resolution (Table 1 note). Non-pixel-aligned comparisons differ in training and camera handling, and their numerical results are missing (Appendix A.5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does texture-based allocation preserve low-contrast geometric boundaries?** The proxy’s claimed coverage is not directly tested. A next experiment could vary texture contrast independently of geometry and measure boundary reconstruction error (§3.1).
2. **Which components produce the quality–compactness gains?** Their individual effects remain unresolved. Ablate partitioning, pooling, scale modulation, and curriculum training under matched Gaussian budgets (§3; Appendix A.2).
3. **Can cross-view fusion further reduce representation size?** Current allocation mainly removes within-view redundancy. Compare cross-view primitive merging against the current method at matched rendering quality (Appendix A.7).
4. **Do fewer Gaussians improve deployment efficiency?** Direct resource measurements are absent. Measure peak memory, representation bytes, reconstruction latency, and rendering latency across inference thresholds (§4.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The main algorithm and benchmark tables are explicit, but broader performance and efficiency claims have incomplete support.

Material missing information includes numerical Tables 3–4, readable sparsity-curve values, component ablations, direct runtime/memory measurements, uncertainty estimates, training-stage durations, main-table inference thresholds, the ACID model variant, and code/checkpoint availability.
