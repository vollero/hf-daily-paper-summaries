# WorldStream: Streaming Interactive 3D Worlds with Hierarchical Planning and Persistent State

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28827) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28827)


## One-sentence takeaway
WorldStream combines hierarchical semantic planning, deterministic 3D compilation, and persistent tile records to stream editable worlds, with controlled mechanism evaluations but limited direct evidence for end-to-end revisit fidelity.

## Problem and motivation
Interactive worlds must expand during exploration, interpret language against existing objects, and preserve places and edits after geometry is unloaded. The authors identify a gap between view-generating models without explicit persistent identities and procedural scene systems that prepare a fixed scene before play (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The contributions center on integrating three mechanisms:

- **Hierarchical executable planning:** Macro plans organize regional settlements, ecology, transport, and water; Micro plans specialize these under local terrain and accepted neighbor commitments. A joint resolver selects feasible assignments from typed model probabilities before deterministic compilation (§3).
- **Dual-frontier streaming:** Semantic planning runs farther ahead than geometry construction. Direction-aware scheduling admits each stage under separate budgets and evicts geometry behind the player (§4).
- **Revisioned persistent state:** Tile records retain plans, seeds, boundaries, identities, mutable state, edits, and compatible versions. Language operations update these records; revision checks reject stale work, and revisits reconstruct geometry from retained state (§§2, 4–5).

The claimed novelty is this combined runtime architecture; the paper explicitly builds on existing procedural placement and concurrency techniques.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy and neighbor context address different failures | Same city-to-rural request, geography, and generators: removing Macro context fails to form the requested city; removing Micro-neighbor context causes overbuilding and abrupt transitions. One run per variant. | §6 setup; §6.1; Fig. 7 |
| Joint resolution improves executability | On the same 100 model-produced probability distributions, independent highest-probability selection leaves 63% invalid; joint resolution makes all 100 executable under the tested hard constraints. | §6.1; Table 3 discussion |
| Directional construction improves arrival readiness | Across 100 route replays with matched preparation times and worker limits, directional prefetch reduces cumulative waiting to 3.52 s over the complete 4 km benchmark route, with a required 3 × 3 neighborhood. | Language interpretation succeeds on most benchmark requests | Intended operation or safe non-execution recovered for 76/80 bilingual requests (95.0%), with world facts and capabilities fixed. Errors concentrate on current-versus-future scope. | §6.3; Table 4 discussion |
| Compiler optimization preserves published output while accelerating compilation | Before/after speedups: dense forest 3.24×, forest–wetland 1.89×, light village 1.67×, park 1.27×. Authors report identical object records, assets, transforms, and counts. | §6.4; Table 5 discussion |

Replacing two compile threads with two persistent processes raises warm throughput from 0.234 to 0.464 tiles/s under the same workload and configuration. | §6.4 |

These results support the tested mechanisms within the prototype. They do not establish superiority over external world-generation systems, long-duration storage scalability, or complete user-visible streaming latency. Persistent reconstruction is specified architecturally, but the interaction benchmark explicitly does not isolate an edit–evict–revisit cycle (§6 setup).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The prototype uses a bounded generation vocabulary, local controllers, relational edit geometry, and curated geographic rules. Reconstruction requires retained records and compatible generator and asset versions (§7.1).
- **Author-stated:** Planning ablations use one run per variant, so they do not estimate model-sampling variance. Readiness excludes browser installation, and interaction evaluation holds world facts fixed (§6 setup).
- **Author-stated:** Accumulated-record storage and simulation of actors outside the active neighborhood remain unresolved policies (§7.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization of regional planning remains uncertain because the controlled hierarchy ablation covers one city-to-rural request (§6.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied evidence does not directly quantify reconstruction fidelity, stale-update rejection under stress, or memory growth during prolonged exploration.
- **Reporting gap:** Tables 1–5 have captions but no visible cell contents in the supplied text; numerical analysis therefore relies on prose. Hardware, detailed model configuration, and release availability are not established.

## Open research questions

- **Does persistence remain exact under concurrent edits and revisits?** The interaction benchmark excludes the full cycle. Next experiment: edit objects and actors, force eviction, revisit, and compare retained and reconstructed state while injecting delayed background jobs.
- **How robust is hierarchical planning across requests and sampling variation?** The ablation uses one request and one run per variant. Next experiment: repeat hierarchy variants across multiple terrains, regional intents, and model samples, measuring constraint success and boundary continuity.
- **Does backend readiness translate into smooth visible traversal?** Browser installation is excluded. Next experiment: include installation, rendering, abrupt turns, and changing preparation latency when measuring arrival coverage and user-visible stalls.
- **How does retained state scale over long exploration?** Storage and inactive-actor policies remain open. Next experiment: run extended traversals with repeated edits and revisits, measuring record growth and actor-state consistency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and prose-reported mechanism results are clear, but full table values, detailed hardware and planner configuration, public artifact availability, repeated-run uncertainty, and direct end-to-end persistence measurements are unavailable in the supplied text.
