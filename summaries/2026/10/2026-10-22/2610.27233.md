# MMedAgent-Plus: Scaling Multimodal Medical Agents across Modalities, Tasks and Workflows

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27233) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27233)


## One-sentence takeaway
An instruction-tuned vision-language planner coordinates medical specialists effectively on benchmark-derived workflows, but specialist performance is preserved unevenly and fully adaptive clinical workflows remain untested (§§2–3.4).

## Problem and motivation
Medical workflows require selecting analyses, executing them in sequence, and carrying intermediate findings forward. The authors argue that isolated specialists lack this coordination, while broad generalist models do not automatically acquire reliable tool orchestration. Their identified gap is evaluation across heterogeneous modalities, tasks, and interactions rather than a single clinical domain (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Expanded specialist orchestration:** Building on MMedAgent, the system combines Qwen2.5-VL-7B with 18 specialist models covering 12 tasks and eight modalities. Standardized interfaces describe inputs, parameters, and outputs, enabling compatible tools to exchange artifacts (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Workflow instruction corpus:** The authors report 208,928 samples derived from 32 medical benchmarks, organized into single-tool multi-round, multi-tool multi-round, and multi-tool single-round settings. Newly constructed dialogues use fixed templates and structural, modality, and interface checks (§4.3).
- **Learned planning and synthesis:** Supervised tuning updates the language decoder and vision-language projector while freezing the vision encoder; targets include structured calls and final responses (§4.2).
- **Separated evaluation and assets:** Tool orchestration, synthesis with correct supplied outputs, and autonomous single-tool execution are evaluated separately. The authors report releasing weights, annotations, splits, and training/evaluation code, with some reproduction components deferred until publication (§§2, 5–6).

The contribution is the expanded integration, training corpus, and evaluation framework; the specialist models themselves are existing methods.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Routing is strongest across multiple dialogue rounds | On 500 held-out dialogues per setting, tool-call / whole-case accuracy: single-tool multi-round **91.0% / 91.0%**; multi-tool multi-round **98.1% / 95.8%**; multi-tool single-round **80.7% / 77.1%**. Whole-case accuracy requires the exact ordered sequence. | §2.1; Fig. 3 |
| Aggregate results conceal weak specialists | Across 1,500 routing dialogues, recall is **0.36** for UniGradICON, **0.64** for CONCH, and **0.76** for MedSAM. | §2.1; Fig. 4 |
| Synthesis scores exceed direct-answer baselines when correct tool outputs are supplied | On 600 cases, 200 per setting, combined judge scores out of 15 are **14.52 vs 13.27**, **14.36 vs 12.86**, and **14.77 vs 10.89** against Qwen3-VL, respectively. Baselines receive original inputs without specialist outputs. | §2.2; Table 1 |
| Autonomous specialist invocation is usually successful | **317/350 cases, 90.6%**, 95% Wilson CI **87.1–93.2%**; seven single-tool tasks, 50 cases each. | §2.3.1; Fig. 5b |
| Specialist-level accuracy is retained on the evaluated QA task | On 50 QA/rationale cases, final-answer accuracy is **0.98**, matching PMC-LLaMA versus **0.28** for the strongest direct-answer baseline. This is a task-specific result, not overall system accuracy. | §§2.3, 2.3.3; Fig. 5d |
| Pathology loses performance after routing and synthesis | Across 50 pathology cases, micro-F1 falls from specialist **0.72** to agent **0.38**. Within 35 correctly routed cases, specialist versus agent micro-F1 is **0.69 vs 0.51**. | §2.3.3; Fig. 5d |

These results support reliable routing in several constructed settings and preservation of specialist capabilities on selected tasks. They do **not** establish autonomous multi-tool clinical effectiveness: the executed end-to-end evaluation uses single-tool cases, and synthesis scores measure communication of specialist outputs rather than independent clinical correctness. The authors also acknowledge that baseline differences cannot be attributed solely to tool access (§§2.2–2.3, 4.5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Specialist errors can propagate; longer sequences, output-dependent branching, multiple valid execution paths, and prospective clinical workflows are not evaluated. Adding capabilities requires further tuning, whose retention effects remain unverified for this library (§3.4).
- **Language generalization:** In the audited multi-tool multi-round split, **67.0% of 3,657 distinct held-out requests** also occur verbatim in training. The authors characterize this as unseen-image evaluation rather than unseen-language generalization (§S2.6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** The agent receives specialist evidence while direct-answer baselines do not, and faithfulness uses different reference evidence. Brief acknowledgments can receive maximum scores for non-text outputs; these scores therefore cannot establish artifact quality (§§4.5.3, S4.1).
- **Analyst inference—measurement sensitivity:** Exact label/entity matching rewards schema conformity. Figure S11 explicitly describes correct baseline yes/no conclusions failing reference matching. “CheXpert F1” uses a keyword-and-negation approximation, and segmentation HD95 excludes cases without generated masks (§S4.2; Fig. S11).
- **Reporting and reproducibility:** Corpus/split counts differ across passages without a clear reconciliation; §3.4 describes shorter workflows than the four-invocation independent sequences documented in §S2.4. Table S3 labels the OCT source non-public despite broader public-data claims. Deployment code, some metric implementations/scripts, and evaluation indices are deferred (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Can the planner execute adaptive workflows?** Output-dependent tool choice remains untested (§3.4). A next experiment could use clinician-designed branching cases with multiple acceptable paths and measure final task success.
- **How much routing accuracy survives new wording?** Fixed-template overlap limits linguistic generalization evidence (§S2.6). Evaluate unchanged tools on independently written, ambiguous requests excluded from template construction.
- **Can synthesis preserve structured specialist predictions?** Pathology performance declines even after correct invocation (§2.3.3). Compare free-form synthesis with constrained label preservation on the same cases.
- **How much benefit comes from tools versus tuning?** Existing baselines differ in architecture, training, and evidence (§4.5.3). Compare the same backbone before and after tuning, with matched tool access, using task metrics and blinded clinician assessment.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central architecture and reported results, but the source was truncated; coverage is incomplete.

Material gaps include omitted specialist/interface documentation, numerical figure contents unavailable in the supplied Markdown, reconciliation of dataset counts and provenance, deferred reproduction components, and evidence for adaptive multi-tool execution or prospective clinical validity.
