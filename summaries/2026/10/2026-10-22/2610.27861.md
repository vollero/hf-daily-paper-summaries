# RoboCasa-Shift: Stage-Aware Robustness Evaluation of Vision-Language-Action Policies in Long-Horizon Household Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27861) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27861)


## One-sentence takeaway
On four clean-qualified simulated household tasks, RoboCasa-Shift shows that matched episode outcomes and semantic progress reveal GR00T N1.5 robustness failures hidden by aggregate success rates (§IV-B–D).

## Problem and motivation
Aggregate task success obscures both which executions change outcome under a distribution shift and how far failed executions progress. Unmatched clean and perturbed episodes can also confound shift effects with differences in episode difficulty. The authors address this diagnostic gap for long-horizon manipulation through controlled episode pairing and semantic milestone tracking (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark protocol:** RoboCasa-Shift extends RoboCasa365 with single-factor illumination, camera-orientation, base-initialization, and Gaussian-blur shifts. Each has three severity levels validated independently of policy success and frozen before formal evaluation (§III-B).
- **Matched comparisons:** Clean and perturbed rollouts share task conditions, pre-shift simulator state, horizon, checkpoint, and episode-level policy seed. State fingerprints and repeatability checks audit pairing (§III-C; Tables II, V).
- **StageTracker diagnostics:** Ordered simulator-state predicates track completed semantic milestones, normalized progress, milestone reach, and the first incomplete milestone. Completed milestones remain latched; official task success remains separate (§III-D–E).
- **Empirical study:** The authors evaluate GR00T N1.5 on four tasks passing a predefined clean-competence gate, using 1,040 formal rollouts. This is an evaluation contribution, not a new policy or robustness-training method (§IV-A).

## Evidence and main results

Here, ΔSR means clean success rate minus perturbed success rate; positive values indicate degradation. Macro results weight the four tasks equally.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Formal coverage is controlled but narrow | GR00T N1.5; 20 base episodes per task; four tasks; clean plus 12 shifted conditions; 1,040 rollouts. Clean macro SR: 0.738. | §IV-A–B; Table VIII |
| Zero net degradation can conceal outcome changes | Camera L1: ΔSR = 0.000, but 8/80 matched pairs change outcome—four success→failure and four failure→success. | §IV-C; Table III |
| Equal endpoint degradation can hide different execution depths | Camera L2 and blur L1 both have macro ΔSR = 0.188. Among their respective success→failure cohorts, mean progress is 0.437 versus 0.356, and first-milestone failure fractions are 19.0% versus 36.4%. | §IV-D; Table XI |
| Blur produces severe, increasingly early failures | Blur L1/L2/L3 macro SR: 0.550/0.225/0.000; ΔSR: 0.188/0.513/0.738. Among success→failure cohorts, mean progress falls from 0.356 to 0.223 to 0.000; all 59 L3 transitions fail before the first milestone. | Tables VIII, XI |
| Severity responses are not uniformly monotonic | Camera L2 ΔSR = 0.188, 95% paired-bootstrap CI [0.063, 0.313]; L3 ΔSR = 0.138, CI [0.025, 0.250]. Illumination’s three degradation CIs all include zero. | §IV-B; Table VIII |
| Additional policies do not support robustness comparisons | SmolVLA and π0 achieve 1/40 and 2/40 clean successes, respectively, on the frozen suite; neither qualifies for perturbation evaluation. | §IV-A; Table XII |

The evidence establishes that endpoint degradation, paired outcome sensitivity, and semantic execution depth capture different properties in this evaluated setting. It does not establish cross-model or real-world generality. Failure→success transitions are outcome changes, not evidence of improved robustness (§IV-C–E). The reported camera results also do not establish a statistically significant difference between L2 and L3; no direct test of that contrast is supplied.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Formal shift evaluation covers one policy and four competence-selected tasks in simulation. The authors explicitly avoid cross-model, broader task-structure, mobility, and real-robot generalization claims (§IV-E).
- **Diagnostic validity:** Predicates were audited on successful demonstrations, not against ground-truth per-frame stage labels. Latched progress measures milestones reached, not conditions maintained or satisfied at termination (§III-D; §VI-D).
- **Localization is not causation:** The first incomplete milestone does not identify when or why a failure began. Some official failures complete every tracked milestone (§III-E; §VI-H).
- **Analyst inference—cohort composition:** Stage-depth comparisons use condition-specific success→failure cohorts, whose episode and task composition can differ. Their aggregate differences therefore do not isolate within-episode changes in failure depth (§IV-D; Table XI).
- **Analyst inference—sampling and shift coverage:** Twenty base episodes per task and single-factor perturbations leave broader sampling stability and interacting shifts unresolved. Stage-depth uncertainty intervals are not supplied (Tables VIII, XI).

## Open research questions
1. **Do these diagnostic patterns persist across policies?** No additional screened policy qualified. Evaluate several policies with adequate clean competence on a shared task suite (§VI-I).
2. **How sensitive are conclusions to milestone design?** Predicate validation establishes consistency on demonstrations, not annotation agreement. Compare alternative milestone definitions with independent rollout annotations (§VI-D).
3. **Do combined shifts change failure progression?** Only individual factors are evaluated. Run matched factorial experiments combining visual and base-initialization shifts (§III-B).
4. **Do findings transfer to physical robots?** All results are simulated. Repeat matched perturbation trials on physical household tasks with independently measured milestones (§IV-E).
