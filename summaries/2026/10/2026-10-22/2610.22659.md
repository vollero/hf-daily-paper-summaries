# Exploring The Empirical Privacy Landscape of Synthetic Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22659) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22659)


## One-sentence takeaway
Privacy-aware rewriting improves the measured privacy–capability balance of language-model finetuning on synthetic medical records, but provides no formal privacy guarantee and occasionally leaves individual canaries recoverable.

## Problem and motivation
Finetuning on sensitive records can improve domain performance while exposing memorized information. The authors identify a lack of controlled comparisons between data preprocessing and training defenses that measure both privacy leakage and capability (§1). Their study tests whether rewriting can preserve useful clinical patterns while removing distinctive patient details.

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled evaluation:** Synthea-generated medical records with planted fictional medications support comparisons of direct finetuning, PII scrubbing, DP-SGD, Goldfish, ordinary rewriting, and privacy-aware rewriting. Targets are Qwen3.5-4B-Base and gpt-oss-20b, trained with LoRA (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Uniqueness-aware rewriting:** An additional instruction asks rewriters to replace rare, identifying details with plausible alternatives while retaining common clinical information. The contribution is its empirical evaluation against ordinary rewriting and other defenses; the authors acknowledge related contextual anonymization work (§§2, 3.2).
- **Rewriting and evaluation studies:** Experiments vary rewriter capability, reasoning effort, summarization, and independent rewrites, and test whether judgments of rewritten data predict downstream outcomes (§§4.2–4.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy-aware rewriting improves on ordinary rewriting. | Privacy rank improves for every rewriter comparison shown across both targets; capability improves in most comparisons. Ranks aggregate two privacy and two capability metrics. | §4.2, Fig. 3; Fig. 1 |
| Other defenses show distinct trade-offs. | Direct finetuning gives the strongest capability improvements; DP-SGD preserves privacy with lower capability; PII scrubbing and Goldfish offer limited canary protection in this setting. | §4, Figs. 1, 8 |
| Direct finetuning can make planted secrets highly likely. | For Qwen, prefix-conditioned whole-medication generation probability rises from 16% at one exposure to 99% at 64 exposures; PII scrubbing gives 7% and 94%, respectively. Each duplication group contains different canaries. | §A.10, Fig. 22 |
| Privacy-aware rewriting substantially reduces candidate-name exposure. | Across 100 canaries, ranking each secret against 6,300 alternatives, mean exposure for Qwen/gpt-oss is 12.22/10.98 bits after direct finetuning, 2.21/1.71 with GPT-5.5 privacy-aware rewriting, and 1.74/1.56 with DP-SGD. Lower is better. | §A.11, Table 3 |
| Strong aggregate privacy does not eliminate individual leakage. | Under GPT-5.5 privacy-aware rewriting, one Qwen canary in the 16-copy group has 0.5% prefix-conditioned generation probability; the next-highest is approximately \(10^{-11}\). | §A.10, Fig. 22 |
| Stronger rewriters help privacy more consistently than capability. | Larger models and later GPT variants generally improve privacy; capability and gains from greater reasoning effort are nonmonotonic. Summarization gives no consistent overall improvement. | §4.2, Figs. 3–5; §§A.3–A.5 |
| More independent rewrites offer no clear benefit. | Five independently generated rewrites trained for one epoch perform similarly to, or worse than, one rewrite trained for five epochs, despite additional generation cost. | §4.2, Fig. 6; §A.6 |

These results support the authors’ claim that privacy-aware rewriting offers a favorable empirical balance in this setup. Privacy perplexity measures secret likelihood under specified probes; capability perplexity measures record and medication prediction. Neither establishes comprehensive resistance to privacy attacks or clinical usefulness. Most plotted absolute values are unavailable in the supplied text, limiting assessment of effect sizes and uncertainty.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated boundary:** Rewriting has no formal differential-privacy guarantee (§2). It also requires additional generation compute (§4).
- **Authors’ stated confound:** Duplication levels use different canary groups, so differences combine exposure frequency with intrinsic secret difficulty (§4.1).
- **Authors’ stated metric concern:** Summary expansion may improve held-out record fit partly through formatting guidance rather than useful clinical learning (§A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Synthetic records, coined medication secrets, two target models, and a single training seed limit generalization to real patient information, broader model families, and training variability (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Example-level DP-SGD is evaluated on duplicated records; the reported privacy budget should not be interpreted as an established patient-level guarantee across all copies (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate scores can obscure rare failures, as the individual leaked canary demonstrates (§A.10). The judge analysis is also restricted to GPT-series rewriters (§4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does rewriting preserve useful rare clinical information?** Perplexity does not establish clinical fidelity. A next experiment could measure expert-reviewed factual retention and downstream task performance alongside leakage.
2. **How robust are the findings to canary construction and training randomness?** Different secrets are assigned to each duplication level, and training uses one seed. Repeat experiments across seeds while testing the same canaries at each multiplicity.
3. **Can rare rewriting failures be detected before training?** Individual leakage persists despite favorable averages. Test an independent screening-and-regeneration stage, measuring residual leakage and utility loss.
4. **How does rewriting perform under matched total compute?** Generation adds cost, but quantitative cost comparisons are absent. Compare defenses using matched preprocessing-plus-training budgets.
