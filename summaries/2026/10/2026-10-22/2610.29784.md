# Evolving with Open Worlds: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Future Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29784)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeepEvolve combines live forecasting tasks, training on cached evidence, reusable skills, and weakness-targeted task generation to demonstrate short-run forecasting improvements, although inconsistent reporting and limited evaluation leave sustained recursive improvement unproven.

## Problem and motivation
The authors study how forecasting agents can improve from their own experience as the world changes. Static benchmarks cannot continually supply fresh challenges and independently arriving outcomes; live forecasting can, but events become contaminated training examples once their answers are publicly searchable. Fresh trajectories are also scarce because predictions must precede resolution (§§1, 2.3).

The identified gap is a closed loop that renews experience while improving model parameters, procedural skills, and the task distribution together.

## Approach and contributions
- **Living task environment—Prometheus:** Generates questions from structured sources and open-ended news discovery, then applies temporal checks, deduplication, conflict filtering, and diversity selection. Outcomes supply delayed supervision (§4.2).
- **Search–prediction separation—Branchus:** A scheduler, searcher, and critic gather evidence before resolution. A separate predict team reasons over cached trajectories, allowing subsequent training without fresh searches that could reveal outcomes (§4.3).
- **Three evolution operators—Athena:** Updates predict-team parameters through RLVR/GRPO or successful-trajectory fine-tuning; distills four-level procedural skills; and converts category-level errors into guidance for generating harder tasks (§§2.3, 4.4).

The central methodological contribution is their integration around shared forecasting trajectories. The authors announce code and data availability, but the supplied text does not establish the repository’s contents or reproducibility (Abstract).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong overall forecasting, with a reporting contradiction | On Prometheus evaluation, Qwen3.8-Max Branchus scores **62.2 BT versus 51.5** for the best same-model baseline, MAS. Branchus configurations rank **1, 2, 3, and 9** among 23 systems; the prose incorrectly describes all four as occupying the top three. | Table 1; §2.2 |
| External leaderboard success | Authors report the highest overall FutureX rating in the first **three weekly evaluations**, with a fourth evaluation remaining competitive. The abstract’s “up to 195%” rating improvement lacks a specified comparator in the supplied text. | Abstract; Appendix A; Figs. 2, 11 |
| Parameter updates improve forecasts | For Qwen3-4B-Thinking and Qwen3-8B, RLVR reportedly increases choice accuracy by **1.3 percentage points each** and numeric score \(1-\mathrm{bWIS}\) by **0.31 and 0.38**. | §2.3; Fig. 5 |
| Repeated RFT yields incremental gains | GLM-4.7-Flash: Brier **0.651 → 0.636 → 0.630** and relative WIS **1.02 → 0.99 → 0.98**, with training pools growing from **600 to 1,000 examples** across two evolution rounds. | §2.3; Fig. 3 |
| Skills improve forecasts without weight changes | Across nested pools of **88, 186, and 262 trajectories**, Brier ultimately falls **0.135 → 0.122** and numeric MAPE **2.80% → 2.28%** versus skill-free Branchus. The associated base model is unspecified here. | §2.4; Fig. 6 |
| Skill effects depend on task structure | Structured tasks: tool calls **32 → 18** per question with MAPE maintained or improved. Open tasks: calls **30 → 34**, while MAPE improves **3.40% → 2.31%**. | §2.5; Fig. 8(a) |
| Evolved tasks are harder for the same checkpoint | Selection accuracy **0.60 → 0.53**, Brier **0.52 → 0.58**, and AUROC **0.678 → 0.680**, comparing original versus evolved generation skills. | §2.6; Fig. 9(a) |
| Hard-task training improves over plain RFT | Authors report relative improvements of **1.6% in Brier**, **2.7% in AUROC**, **1.0% in relative WIS**, and **4.0% in Md-sAPE**; absolute values are unavailable in the text. | §2.7; Fig. 10 |

These results support useful component improvements over a small number of rounds. They do not establish indefinite compounding, generalization beyond forecasting, or that an evolving environment is necessary for all self-improvement. Stable AUROC shows retained confidence discrimination; it does not independently prove question quality or answerability.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Improvement depends on available experience; long-resolution events slow learning; non-stationary drift can invalidate skills; category-error targeting can miss silent weaknesses such as overconfidence (§§1, 3).
- **Analyst inference—limited duration:** The reported short evolution sequences cannot establish long-run stability or distinguish sustained improvement from eventual saturation (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Shared questions and tools improve comparability, but equal tools do not establish equal inference budgets. Full per-condition compute and statistical uncertainty are unavailable (§4.5; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—contamination and scoring:** Caching prevents later web retrieval, but observed gains alone do not prove contamination absence. Codex retrieves and scores outcomes; no independent outcome-audit results are supplied (§§2.1, 2.3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Beyond the rank contradiction, Figure 3 mixes MAPE and relative-WIS labels. Appendix C does not clearly define the reported bWIS transformation, relative-WIS normalization, or MAPE calculation. The numeric RLVR reward is also unclear because the formal reward describes categorical correctness (§4.4; Appendix C).

## Open research questions
1. **Does improvement persist through substantial distribution drift?** Short sequences leave durability unresolved. Run a longer prospective evaluation comparing accumulating skills with time-weighted and forgetting-based libraries.
2. **How much does joint evolution add beyond its components?** Component experiments do not fully isolate interactions. Compare fixed, parameter-only, skill-only, environment-only, and combined systems under matched experience and compute budgets.
3. **Does error-targeted generation outperform equally difficult untargeted generation?** Harder tasks and targeting change together. Compare targeted tasks with difficulty-matched random tasks on an independently generated future test stream.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and several numerical results are explicit, but broad claims exceed the demonstrated duration and some reporting conflicts remain.

Material missing information includes exact evaluation dates and question counts by experiment, uncertainty estimates, full training hyperparameters, numeric reward details, complete runtime prompts, independently audited outcome labels, matched total compute budgets, and numerical values contained only in linked figure images.
