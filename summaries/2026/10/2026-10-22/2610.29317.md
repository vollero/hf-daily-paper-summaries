# SpatialBrain: Spatial Intelligence Growing from Experience

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29317) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29317)


## One-sentence takeaway
SpatialBrain combines scene memory, reusable spatial procedures, and experience-guided tool selection to improve a fixed vision-language backbone on four of five benchmarks, but does not establish that accumulated experience itself improves accuracy (Table 1; §5.3; Appendix C).

## Problem and motivation
Spatial questions require different computations: direct observation, reference-frame changes, multi-view geometry, or sequential movement tracking. The authors argue that access to tools alone does not tell a reasoner when to use them; unnecessary reconstruction can introduce cost and noise, while direct answers can miss required evidence (§1).

The identified gap is retaining reusable decision experience without transferring scene-specific coordinates or answers between tasks (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
The proposed contribution is a separation of three kinds of state around a fixed vision-language backbone:

- **3DFusionMemory:** Builds a task-specific scene graph containing objects, spatial relations, camera states, route history, supporting views, and uncertainty (§3.2).
- **Spatial Skill Library:** Stores parameterized procedures with applicability conditions and typed interfaces. The executable configuration can compile and validate procedures; coverage curves primarily track references to existing seed skills (§3.3; §5.3).
- **Spatial Cognition:** Uses task signatures and prior process feedback to choose reasoning routes, memory access, skills, and verification. Updates occur between blocks, exclude answers and literal scene coordinates, and leave model weights fixed (§3.4; Appendix D).

These are the authors’ proposed architectural contributions; their individual causal benefits are only partially isolated by the evaluations.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher aggregate score | Same-backbone direct answers → SpatialBrain: unweighted five-benchmark mean **67.5% → 72.6%**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Largest gain on MindCube | Accuracy **67.2% → 87.6%** on **1,050 items**, a **20.4 percentage-point** gain. | Table 1; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Smaller gains elsewhere; SAT tie | MMSI **53.8% → 57.1%** on **1,000** items; OmniSpatial **68.3% → 69.1%** on **1,533**; SpaceDG **56.9% → 57.9%** on a fixed **1,000-item** subset, using MRA for numeric questions; SAT **91.3% → 91.3%** on **150**, with fixed option order. | Table 1; Table A1 |
| Matched memory comparison is modest | MindCube matched Full/no-Memory scores are **75.9%/75.2%**, a reported **0.67-point** difference; the larger headline removal gap cannot be assigned to memory alone. | Appendix A |
| Skill-removal gap depends on answer availability | On **908 jointly answered** MindCube Full/no-Skill items, correct counts are **799/805**; overall Full/no-Skill runs contain **0/142 blank answers**, counted wrong. | Appendix A |
| Experience reaches later tasks | Earlier committed experience is delivered to **231/1,000 MMSI** and **212/1,050 MindCube** tasks in the executable configuration; this measures exposure, not benefit. | Appendix B.2 |
| Adaptive experience does not show an aggregate accuracy advantage over the reported control | MMSI accuracy is **57.4%** for the fixed-policy control versus **57.1%** for SpatialBrain, compared with **53.8%** direct. | Appendix C |

The evidence supports improved performance for the complete framework relative to direct answers under the reported shared-backbone settings. It also establishes experience accumulation and subsequent delivery. It does **not** establish a general accuracy or cost benefit from self-evolution, isolated component gains, or uniformly better performance across question types (§5.3; Appendices A–C).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated limitations:** Cross-method comparisons mix protocols, backbones, and serving precision; SAT fixes option order; memory removal also removes answer checks; geometric uncertainty remains imperfectly calibrated (§5.3).
- **Component attribution:** Answer availability and implementation changes affect removal comparisons. Earlier MMSI removals were not verified as complete; later MMSI runtime audits remain unverified (Appendices A, B.3–B.4).
- **Nonuniform benefits:** OmniSpatial egocentric questions and SpaceDG distance/counting questions regress; MMSI multi-step errors also increase (§4.2; Appendix B.5).
- **Analyst inference—adaptation remains unproven:** Coverage curves and selected traces cannot substitute for a matched initial-frozen experience control. The reported fixed-policy MMSI score is slightly higher (Appendix C).
- **Cost evidence is limited:** Qualitative cases were selected after scoring, timing lacks controlled concurrency/cache conditions, and image-byte identity was not checked through recorded hashes (Appendix C).

## Open research questions

1. **Does updating experience improve accuracy or total cost?** Accumulation and exposure are demonstrated, but general benefit is not. Compare updating and initial-frozen systems with identical resources, task orders, and starting experience, charging experience-acquisition costs (§5.3).
2. **Which component improves reasoning beyond answer completion?** Removal gaps partly reflect blank answers and configuration differences. Run matched removals with equal budgets and report completion rates alongside paired correctness (Appendix A).
3. **Can process feedback safely guide resource choices?** Admission rules constrain execution risk and estimated cost, rather than answer correctness. Test whether admitted choices preserve accuracy across task signatures and perception-error levels (Appendix D.2).
4. **Does experience transfer across settings?** Reported coverage streams are independent, and cross-benchmark transfer is not established. Build experience on one benchmark, freeze it, and evaluate another against the same system without that experience (§5.3; Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main scores and major qualifications are explicit, but causal attribution is limited.

Material missing information includes confidence intervals or repeated-run variability, a reported matched initial-frozen adaptation experiment, full end-to-end cost totals, exact tool/model and decoding specifications, numerical admission thresholds, and code/model/experience-store release availability. Several qualitative trajectory details are also absent from the supplied text.
