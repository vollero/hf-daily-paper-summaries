# PACE: Periodic and Channel-Aligned Encoding of Four-Bit Recurrent States

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29545) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29545)


## One-sentence takeaway

PACE combines periodic rounding, column-aligned quantization groups, and optional mixed-precision heads to approach DAMP INT8’s average accuracy on three larger Qwen hybrid models at 0.69 times its resident state-and-buffer bits, according to the authors (§7.2, Table 1 caption).

## Problem and motivation

Linear-attention language models store a recurrent state for each concurrent sequence, making state memory a constraint on serving capacity. Repeatedly rounding that state during decoding accumulates error; prior four-bit approaches reportedly lose accuracy or require fine-tuning (§1).

The authors identify an overlooked variable: **how often the state is rounded**. Prefill already rounds only at chunk boundaries, whereas conventional decoding rounds after every token. Existing decode buffers enable less frequent state updates, but their interaction with sub-eight-bit quantization—and their memory cost—had not been evaluated in the cited work (§§2.3–2.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Periodic rounding with explicit memory accounting.** PACE buffers recent decoding inputs and rounds the state only when the buffer is flushed. Its resident-bits metric includes both quantized state and buffer. The authors derive an error-accumulation expression linking rounding period and head decay (§§3, 5.1).
- **Channel-aligned scale groups.** State-tail measurements and channel-permutation experiments suggest that persistently large delta-value channels create columns of large state entries. PACE groups entries within columns, along the key axis, to avoid sharing scales across columns of different magnitudes (§§4, 5.2).
- **Optional head-level precision allocation.** PACE-4 uses four-bit heads throughout. PACE-4/8 assigns eight bits to the quarter of heads per layer with the highest estimated retained rounding error, adapting DAMP’s allocation criterion from channels to heads. Ranking uses held-out windows without model training (§5.3).
- **Packed-state decoding kernels.** The authors implement Triton kernels and demonstrate periodic uniform-INT4 decoding that preserves the speed benefit of buffering; the full PACE grouping and head allocation are not timed (§7.5; Limitations).

These results support a memory–accuracy tradeoff for the evaluated hybrids. They do not establish accuracy equivalence, measured gains in maximum serving concurrency, or throughput for the complete PACE-4/8 configuration. The supplied text includes table captions and narrative conclusions but omits numerical table bodies and plotted values, preventing independent assessment of effect sizes and uncertainty.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** All evaluated models are Qwen gated-delta hybrids. Column structure, decay behavior, and head ranking are not validated on other families or fully recurrent models (Limitations).
- **Author-stated coverage:** Prefill period is fixed at 256; only part of the configuration space is explored. Static scales, stochastic rounding, and state rotations are not tested under periodic rounding (Limitations).
- **Comparison boundary:** Q-Mamba DSQ is reimplemented without its published fine-tuning, so the results compare quantizers under a post-hoc setting rather than complete published systems (§6).
- **Analyst inference—analytical validity:** Equation 5 assumes independent rounding errors and substitutes a mean head decay for token-varying decays. The supplied derivation does not establish when that substitution yields a rigorous bound (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—reporting ambiguity:** The drift experiment’s Figure 5 caption describes DAMP with fp16 protected channels, whereas the main DAMP INT4 baseline uses eight-bit protected channels. Those settings should not be treated as identical (§§6, 7.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does prefill rounding explain the remaining long-context loss?** Similar RULER deficits across decode periods suggest this, but do not establish it (§7.1). Sweep prefill period while holding decoding and quantization fixed.
2. **Do column alignment and head ranking transfer beyond Qwen hybrids?** The authors explicitly leave transfer unvalidated. Repeat state statistics, permutation tests, and equal-memory comparisons on another recurrent family.
3. **What is the runtime of full PACE-4/8?** Only uniform INT4 is timed. Benchmark channel-aligned mixed-precision kernels against buffered bf16 and DAMP at matched workloads.
4. **How predictive is the retained-error expression?** Its assumptions are not empirically validated in the supplied text. Compare predicted per-head error with measured error under varying decays and rounding-error correlations.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported directional findings are clear, but quantitative verification is limited.

Missing information includes numerical table bodies, plotted effect sizes, reported confidence-interval values, calibration-window provenance, and a code-release location. The supplied text also provides no timing for full PACE-4/8 or measurements of maximum concurrent serving capacity.
