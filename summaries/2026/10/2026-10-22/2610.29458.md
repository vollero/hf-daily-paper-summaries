# Harness Scaling for Generalizable Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29458) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29458)


## One-sentence takeaway
Structured training across diverse agent harnesses improves held-out configuration performance and transfer to external harnesses over single-harness RL across the tested Qwen3 models and optimization algorithms (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
An agent harness controls instructions, tools, observations, context management, and execution procedures. The authors argue that RL through one fixed harness can encourage specialization, limiting transfer when these execution conditions change. They propose varying **how agents execute the same tasks**, complementing task and environment diversity (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Factorized execution space:** Represent harnesses through five components: system prompt, tool interface, observation, context management, and workflow. Three realizations per component produce 243 configurations; alternatives are generated through constrained model-driven transformations (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Structured selection:** Choose 18 training configurations using a strength-2 orthogonal design that balances individual realizations and every cross-component pair (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Local cross-harness training:** Assign each task a random anchor and nearby configurations under Hamming distance, using two harnesses over successive training steps by default. This changes rollout assignment while retaining the underlying PPO or GRPO objective (§3.3).
- **Transfer evaluation:** Test unseen combinations and external harnesses, with ablations separating configuration selection, task repetition, and per-task harness variation (§§4.2–4.3).

## Evidence and main results

Scores below are reported Pass@1; evaluations average three independent runs. In-space results additionally average 18 fixed held-out configurations (§4.1; Appendix C.2).

These results support cross-harness benefits under the tested budgets and configurations. The ablations support a role for both structured selection and within-task variation. They do not establish a general scaling law or universal transfer to arbitrary harnesses. Behavioral improvements are judge-based associations rather than demonstrated causal mechanisms (§5; Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated qualification:** Exhaustive training includes the white-box evaluation configurations, so that comparison is a fixed-budget stress test rather than held-out generalization (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evidence covers two Qwen3 backbones, one training collection, three workspace benchmarks, and two external harnesses. Generalization beyond this scope remains untested.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Three evaluation runs are reported, but the supplied text gives no confidence intervals or variation across independent training seeds.
- **Analyst inference—design coverage:** In-space evaluation tests new combinations of known component realizations, not entirely new component realizations. Per-component contributions and higher-order interactions are not isolated.
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** GPT-5.4 judges rubric-based outcomes and behavioral capabilities; human agreement or alternative-judge validation is not reported (Appendices C.2 and D).
- **Analyst inference—resource comparison:** Matched updates and rollout counts do not establish equal token consumption, runtime, or total compute across harnesses (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer survive unseen component realizations?** Current in-space tests hold out combinations. Generate new tool schemas, compaction policies, or workflows and evaluate without retraining.
- **How should selection change with budget?** Exhaustive coverage underperforms at the tested fixed budget. Sweep training budgets and configuration counts while measuring tokens, compute, and transfer.
- **Which variations drive the gains?** Combined-component training leaves attribution unresolved. Run component-removal ablations and test whether weighted structural distance improves assignment.
- **How stable are the improvements?** Training-seed uncertainty and judge sensitivity are unavailable. Repeat training across seeds and validate a blinded outcome sample with human assessors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes methods, appendices, and numerical comparisons, but lacks training-seed uncertainty, statistical intervals, exact figure values, full executable harness artifacts, judge prompts, and measured runtime or token costs. Public code, checkpoint, and dataset releases are not established.
