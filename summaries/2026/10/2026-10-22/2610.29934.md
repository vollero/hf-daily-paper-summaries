# ToM2Vec: Continuous Theory-of-Mind Tokens for Multi-Agent Coordination

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29934) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29934)


## One-sentence takeaway
ToM2Vec trains Qwen3-8B to predict continuous representations of a partner’s reasoning, improving reported multi-agent task performance and partner-state probes, although missing table contents and inconsistent significance claims limit verification (§§2–4; Appendix C).

## Problem and motivation
LLM agents must anticipate partners’ beliefs, goals, and actions under partial observability. The authors argue that verbalized partner models tend to commit to one interpretation, whereas coordination may require maintaining several plausible intentions. They propose latent partner representations as a more suitable abstraction; representing multiple hypotheses is a motivation, rather than directly established by the reported experiments (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Latent partner modeling:** From its local context, an agent generates 10 continuous tokens by feeding each final-layer hidden state back as the next input embedding. Subsequent reasoning and actions condition on these tokens (§§2.2–2.4).
- **Partner-reasoning supervision:** During self-play, partner reasoning is segmented into sentence chunks. Start, end, and chunk-center hidden states provide targets for position-wise cosine alignment. This is the only training objective; task rewards are not directly optimized. Training agents share a base model and representation space (§2.3).
- **Evaluation and diagnostic evidence:** The authors evaluate cooperative, competitive, and mixed-motive environments, unseen partner families, partner-state questions, context corruption, false beliefs, and belief revision. The claimed novelty is applying continuous latent reasoning to modeling other LLM agents, building on existing latent-reasoning mechanisms (§§3–5; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; the supplied text includes table captions and narrative summaries but omits table bodies.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better Overcooked coordination | Mean episode score improves by **12.0 points** over base Qwen3-8B across three layouts, each evaluated on 30 seeds. Language ToM trails ToM2Vec by **6.0, 9.0, and 12.3 points** across those layouts. | §3.4; Table 1 |
| Higher negotiation agreement rate | Deal success rises from **62.0% to 71.0%** over 100 negotiations; agreement requires at least 5/6 parties. Own and collective scores reportedly improve, but values are unavailable. | §§3.1–3.4; Table 2 |
| Better Decrypto performance | In the base-model comparison context, decoder and interceptor win rates improve by **4.4 and 6.1 percentage points**, respectively, with 1,000 samples per role and Qwen3-8B counterparts. Absolute rates are unavailable. | §3.4; Table 3 |
| Higher Hanabi score | **11.3/25**, exceeding the untrained baseline by **2.4 points** and Language ToM by **0.7 points**, over 100 games. | §3.4; Table 4; Appendix B |
| Better capture and escape outcomes | Capture success rises from **0.67 to 1.00** on six seeds; Prompted ToM also reaches 1.00, with average turns **4.76 versus 4.32** for ToM2Vec. Escape success rises from **0.76 to 0.92** on 12 seeds, tying the highest rate. | §3.4; Table 5 |
| Transfer to unseen partners | With GPT-5.4 partners, Overcooked mean score improves by **17.3 points** over the base agent, averaged across layouts on 20 seeds. Smaller Gemma-4 gains are reported without exact values. | §4; Table 6 |
| More accurate partner-state predictions | On six-choice Overcooked probes with 300 samples per question type, accuracy exceeds the next-best baseline by **5.6, 2.0, and 2.7 percentage points** for beliefs, desires, and actions. Absolute accuracies are unavailable. | §4; Table 7 |
| Context-sensitive latent representations | Perplexity of true partner reasoning is **1.412** with normal-context latents, versus **1.702** with altered-layout and **1.613** with adversarial-intent latents. Sample count is unspecified. | §4; Table 8 |
| False-belief performance | On 50 seeds of a custom cupboard task, accuracy is **0.88**, **0.14** above the untrained agent and tied with Self-Consistency. | Appendix C; Table 9 |
| Added inference cost | Ten latent tokens add **1,150.1 ms/query** using HuggingFace generation on A6000 GPUs, tested over 20 generation steps; reported inference peak memory remains **16.3 GB** for both models. | Appendix C; Table 14 |

The evidence supports useful latent partner conditioning across the tested tasks and some transfer across partner families. It does not establish that the tokens explicitly maintain multiple competing hypotheses or constitute a general theory of mind.

Statistical support is mixed: Appendix C reports significant gains in **16/18 Overcooked comparisons** and **7/10 Decrypto comparisons** at \(p<0.05\), but **none of five negotiation deal-success comparisons** is significant. That directly conflicts with §3.4’s claim of significant negotiation superiority.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Training requires partner chain-of-thought access, a shared representation space, and an additional training phase. Experiments are English-only (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—limited transfer scope:** Cross-family transfer is demonstrated in Overcooked with two unseen partner families; broader partner or domain generalization remains unestablished (§4).
- **Analyst inference—proxy supervision:** Belief and desire labels come from partner self-reports, while action labels come from generated actions. Probe gains therefore establish agreement with those operational labels, rather than independently verified mental states (§4).
- **Analyst inference—small evaluations:** Capture and Escape use only six and 12 seeds, respectively, limiting confidence in success-rate differences (§3.1).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Hanabi significance discussion mentions both 20 and 100 matched seeds, and its final baseline-comparison sentence is incomplete. Negotiation significance claims conflict across sections (Appendix C).
- **Missing controls:** The supplied text reports no latent-budget sweep or matched generic-latent control, leaving the effects of token count and partner-specific supervision incompletely separated (§2.3; §§3–4).
- **Potential split risk:** Negotiation samples are split after extracting discussion turns; whether complete negotiations remain separated is unclear. Related turns could cross splits if splitting occurs at turn level (Appendix B; analyst inference).

## Open research questions
- **Do the latents preserve multiple hypotheses?** Context sensitivity and directional belief revision do not directly measure this. Test ambiguous partner states with multiple valid intentions and evaluate calibrated predictions before and after disambiguating observations.
- **How much benefit comes specifically from partner supervision?** Language ToM tests representation format, but a generic-latent control is absent. Compare partner-aligned, shuffled-target, and unsupervised latent tokens under matched training and inference budgets.
- **How broadly does partner transfer hold?** Unseen-family tests cover Overcooked only. Evaluate fixed trained agents with diverse partners across negotiation, Hanabi, and Decrypto.
- **Can training avoid shared-space reasoning access?** Current supervision requires both. Test a learned cross-model projection or observable-action targets against the original method.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported directional findings are clear, but the supplied text lacks table bodies, most absolute baseline scores, standard errors, exact p-values, training duration and epoch counts, checkpoint availability, and a clear negotiation split unit. It also does not establish cross-task transfer of a single checkpoint or directly verify multiple-hypothesis encoding.
