# StreamEval: Evaluating Interactive Streaming Video Generation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28402) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28402)


## One-sentence takeaway
StreamEval benchmarks five interactive video tasks and finds that strong rendering or identity retention can coexist with weak execution of timed requests and incomplete edits across the evaluated systems (§3.3).

## Problem and motivation
Interactive video systems must respond to new inputs while preserving relevant identities, scene state, and source motion. The authors identify a gap in unified evaluation across avatar interaction, world generation, and live editing with explicit timing, modification, and preservation requirements (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified benchmark:** 300 scripted sessions across dialogue, avatar performance, world generation, instruction editing, and reference editing, balanced across durations of 15–300 seconds. A scheduler releases inputs incrementally; authored oracles specify what must change, persist, or be restored (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Evaluation framework:** Ten task-specific metrics cover execution, state consistency, visual fidelity, and audiovisual coordination. Structured automated judgments feed fixed scoring rules; aggregation equally weights duration strata (§3.2).
- **Comparative study:** The reported evaluation covers 37 models in 47 task configurations, with three runs per case and model. Its contribution is benchmark design and comparative evidence rather than a new generation model (§3.1).

## Evidence and main results

All content scores below use a 0–100 scale. Results average three repeats per case, cases within duration strata, and then equally weighted duration strata; each task contains 60 cases (§§3.1–3.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue execution trails visual quality even for the leading custom-avatar system | Vidu S2 Avatar: Dialogue **75.29**, Quality **96.78**, Identity **90.12** in digital-human dialogue. | Table 2 |
| Prescribed avatar actions remain difficult | Across seven performance models, Following ranges from **6.37** to **36.90**; leader Vidu S2 Avatar also achieves Stability **97.25** and Identity **86.12**. | Table 3 |
| World quality does not ensure control execution | HappyOyster: Quality **58.34**, Following **5.56**, Continuity **2.78**. Krea Realtime has the highest reported Following, **25.93**; MemFlow leads Overall at **33.36**. Text- and image-conditioned configurations are ranked together. | Table 4; §3.3.3 |
| Full instruction edits are harder than operation following | Vidu S2 Editing: Following **72.10**, Edit Success **57.20**. JoyAI: **63.89** and **43.52**, respectively, while leading Preservation at **83.33** among five reported editors. | Table 5 |
| Reference-edit completion is particularly weak | Among three reported editors, Vidu S2 Editing leads Edit Success at **15.00**, versus JoyAI **8.89** and XMax **1.11**; its Preservation is **64.44**. | Table 6 |

These results support the authors’ claim that execution, preservation, and rendering measure distinct capabilities. They do not establish why models fail, whether small ranking differences are statistically reliable, or whether scores predict human preferences. Overall scores also use different metric profiles across tasks; avatar rankings additionally include a customization bonus (§3.2.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ scope boundaries:** Live-user-video perception is excluded. Broader identities and environments, global resets, and selective restoration with concurrent edits remain future extensions (§§4.2, 5.2).
- **Authors’ measurement caveats:** Stability explicitly overlaps with Quality. Sparse frame sampling limits A–V Sync temporal resolution (§3.2.1).
- **Human validity remains open:** Human pairwise agreement with the rubric is proposed as future work. Judge calibration describes acceptance thresholds but provides no measured calibration outcomes or sample counts (§§2.5, 6).
- **Analyst inference—selection and comparability:** Editing tables retain only configurations with Overall at least 5, selected after evaluation. Mixed conditioning routes, adapter components, and different identity references for fixed versus custom avatars limit direct interpretation of rankings (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and attribution:** Aggregate tables lack score uncertainty and duration-specific breakdowns. Unsupported operations and controls beyond provider session limits receive zero, so low scores can reflect interface constraints as well as generation failures (§3.2.2).

## Open research questions

- **Do automated scores match human preferences?** Agreement is untested (§6). Collect blinded pairwise judgments and compare them with individual metrics and Overall rankings.
- **How much failure comes from duration versus interaction load?** Published aggregates combine duration strata (§3.2.2). Report results by duration and vary control density while holding session content fixed.
- **Which editing constraints cause failed completion?** Edit Success combines timing, scope, persistence, and protection checks (§3.2.1). Score these components separately and test matched cases that add one constraint at a time.
- **How robust are rankings to interface and aggregation choices?** Routes differ and avatar rankings include a capability bonus (§§3.1–3.2). Compare matched input routes and perform metric-weight sensitivity analyses.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the benchmark and aggregate findings, but leaves material validation and reproducibility details unavailable: measured judge-calibration results, human agreement, score confidence intervals, duration-level results, detailed adapter/runtime settings, latency and resource measurements, and the full excluded editing roster. Benchmark assets are promised for release; their availability is not established by the supplied text.
