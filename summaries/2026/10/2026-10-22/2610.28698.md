# Scaling in Games: Continued Pre-Training For Embodied Agents In Diverse Virtual Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28698) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28698)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across a controlled gameplay continued-pre-training grid, more data and broader mixtures improve familiar and related-world performance, but sustained transfer to distant worlds remains limited, while real-time rankings depend strongly on inference latency (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation

Virtual worlds differ in visuals, physics, controls, and objectives, making experience transfer uncertain. The authors identify a gap in prior studies: model size, data volume, and data composition often change together, obscuring their individual effects. They investigate whether heterogeneous gameplay experience supports general embodied control or introduces interference (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled scaling study:** Evaluate 120 conditions combining four Qwen3.5 sizes (0.8B–27B), six gameplay budgets (50–1,600 hours), and five mixtures. Training uses next-token imitation of keyboard–mouse actions, with a fixed 15% vision–language token anchor and sparse retrospective reasoning annotations (§3; Appendices A.1–A.4).
- **Learning dynamics and empirical modeling:** Identify an early “interface phase” with broad action-loss improvements, followed by a “world phase” whose gains depend on relevant training exposure. Adapt the existing D-CPT scaling-law form to model size, gameplay hours, and mixture share; the contribution is its application and validation here, rather than an entirely new functional form (§§4.1, 4.4; Appendix C.6).
- **Evaluation across transfer distances and timing settings:** Compare in-corpus Minecraft/ViZDoom, related unseen OmniGameArena worlds, and distant GameWorld tasks, including time-paused and real-time interaction (§§3.2, 5).

“Diversity” primarily means redistributing experience across a fixed game pool: S2–S4 contain the same world inventory, and S5 removes Minecraft. It does not independently test increasing the number of worlds (Appendix A.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Early adaptation transfers broadly | At 50 gameplay hours, action validation losses fall below the corresponding backbone on all three held-out sets, including unseen titles. This is loss evidence, not universal gameplay success. | §4.1; Figure 3 |
| Relevant exposure supports later gains | From 50 to 1,600 hours, Minecraft validation loss falls about 10.4%, averaged across four sizes under Minecraft-heavy S1–S3. | §4.1 |
| Mixed training improves related-world transfer | At 1,600 hours, mean OGA score across S2–S5 is roughly 2.8× S1; all 16 mixed conditions exceed matched-size S1. | §4.2; Figure 4 |
| Unseen-title loss deteriorates after early gains | All 20 size–mixture curves end with OOD action loss 0.007–0.019 higher at 1,600 than at 50 hours. | Appendix C.6 |
| Loss laws fit familiar distributions well | Across 120 conditions, Minecraft/in-corpus fits achieve R² 0.982/0.995 and RMSE 0.0020/0.0012; within-mixture R² is 0.912/0.959. Held-out 27B prediction RMSE is 0.0026/0.0016. | Tables 10, 13 |
| Minecraft performance is competitive | On MCU’s 30-task evaluation, 27B-S1 achieves 63.3% average success versus the quoted GameWAM result of 50.7%. These are not controlled baseline comparisons. | Table 1; Appendix B.2 |
| Related and distant transfer diverge | In time-paused evaluation, 27B-S3 improves OGA from its backbone’s 9.7 to 25.7, while GameWorld falls from 29.3 to 25.3. | Table 2 |
| Real-time deployment changes rankings | 9B-S3 averages 25.3 across four real-time benchmarks versus 19.2 for the best-tested frontier model, Gemini-3.8-Flash. Mean request latency is 0.31 versus 7.75 seconds per step under their respective deployments. | Tables 2, 9; Appendix B.4 |

The evidence supports distribution-dependent scaling within this training recipe and benchmark suite. It does not establish that CPT alone produces general gameplay competence, that the proposed phases are causally distinct mechanisms, or that fitted loss improvements reliably predict distant-world task success. The OOD action-loss set and GameWorld are separate evaluation assets with different compositions (Appendices A.1, B.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Training uses one seed; repeated-seed experiments and independently varying world count remain future work. Bootstrap intervals describe fit sensitivity, not training-seed variability (Appendices A.1, C.6).
- **Author-stated:** The empirical law is scoped to the studied grid. Several parameters trade off, asymptotic loss floors are not identifiable, and distant-world loss violates the decreasing-data-term assumption (Appendix C.6).
- **Author-stated:** Imitation lacks environmental success/failure feedback. The paper cannot distinguish insufficient world coverage from objective limitations as the main transfer bottleneck (§6.3).
- **Comparison risk:** Table 1 imports prior results with different interfaces and harnesses. Table 2 uses a common evaluation interface, but real-time results combine model behavior, reasoning settings, serving hardware, and provider latency (Appendices B.2, B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Mixture balancing changes per-world exposure alongside diversity, limiting causal attribution to diversity itself. The supplied experiments also do not isolate the contributions of retrospective reasoning or the fixed vision–language anchor (Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Evaluation uncertainty:** GameWorld runs each task once, and Table 2 lacks uncertainty intervals, limiting assessment of small score differences (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does adding worlds help beyond rebalancing existing ones?** The inventory is largely fixed. Next experiment: vary world count while matching total hours, target-world exposure, and mixture evenness (Appendix A.1).
2. **Can feedback overcome distant-world stagnation?** Coverage and imitation-objective explanations remain unresolved. Next experiment: compare additional offline CPT with online feedback training under matched interaction budgets (§6.3).
3. **How much real-time advantage comes from reasoning policy versus serving?** Default reasoning settings and deployments differ. Next experiment: sweep reasoning budgets and imposed latency under a common action interface, reporting both task scores and response times (Appendices B.4, C.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods and supporting appendices, but key findings lack training-seed replication.

Material gaps include repeated-seed variability, uncertainty for Table 2, ablations of reasoning annotations and the vision–language anchor, independent world-count experiments, exact provider serving configurations, and verified contents of the linked model/code artifacts.
