# Sparsity Should Be ALLOTted by Output: Training-Free Allocation of Block-Sparse Attention for Video Diffusion Transformers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29441) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29441)


## One-sentence takeaway
ALLOT uses offline measurements of final-video error to allocate sparse-attention density across layers and denoising steps, improving fidelity to dense generation at comparable runtime in the tested video diffusion pipelines (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Block-sparse attention reduces video Diffusion Transformer computation, but deciding how much attention to retain in each layer and step also affects fidelity. The authors argue that fixed warm-up rules transfer poorly across models, while attention-map metrics and one-step errors miss how perturbations propagate into the final video (§§1, 3).

The central opportunity is that sensitivity varies strongly across layers and steps but its ranking remains relatively stable across tested inputs, making reusable offline allocation possible (§3; Appendix D).

## Approach and contributions
- **Output-based sensitivity analysis.** The authors compare layer and step sensitivity against local proxies and document input-stable rankings (§3; Appendices B–D).
- **Factorized error estimation.** Isolated layer probes measure velocity-prediction MSE at different noise levels and densities. Sparse-window generations calibrate a step-dependent gain using final-video pixel MSE. Their product estimates each layer–step cell’s contribution (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Joint latency allocation.** A discrete Lagrangian solver chooses cell densities under a measured attention-latency budget. Dense layers and warm-up behavior emerge from the solution. Inference reuses a fixed density table while retaining the underlying block ranking and kernel (§§4.4–4.5).

The contribution is an allocation method and its empirical motivation; it does not introduce a new attention kernel or block-ranking strategy.

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large HunyuanVideo fidelity gain | XAttention, 720×480×129 frames, 50 steps, 32 prompts: PSNR gain over default **11.84 dB**, paired 95% interval **[10.85, 12.83]**; ALLOT/default runtime **481.4/481.3 s**. SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS improve on every prompt. | Appendix G.3 |
| Broad integration succeeds under the paper’s verdict rule | Across SVOO, SVG2, SpargeAttn, and Radial on Wan/HunyuanVideo, **six wins and three ties** over nine settings, with PSNR gains up to **5.03 dB** at matched or shorter runtime; 32 prompts per setting. | §5.3; Table 2 caption |
| Fidelity and runtime can improve together | SVOO, HunyuanVideo 720p×129, 32 prompts: **+3.38 dB PSNR [2.51, 4.25]**; speedup relative to dense rises from **1.75× to 1.90×**. | §5.3; Appendix H.1 |
| Trajectory calibration is the dominant ablated component | XAttention, Wan2.1-1.3B 480p×81, 32 prompts, matched ALLOT cost: replacing trajectory gains with a constant lowers PSNR by **7.05 dB**. | §5.5; Table 4 caption |
| Surrogate ranks allocations well in one diagnostic setting | **80 tables**, three budgets, Wan2.1-1.3B 480p×33, four held-out prompts per table: pooled predicted-versus-measured error ranking **Kendall τ=0.83**. | §5.6; Appendix E.5 |
| Calibration creates substantial upfront cost | Reported measurement cost is roughly **6–23 GPU-hours per model**, over **30** for Wan2.2’s experts; switching to Radial on HunyuanVideo requires **57 GPU-hours** for probes. Re-solving takes about **4 CPU seconds**. | §5.6; Appendix F.6 |

These results support improved **similarity to dense outputs**, rather than a general claim of better absolute video quality. A win requires both a positive paired PSNR interval and lower mean LPIPS; VBench does not determine the verdict (§5.1). “Matched” runtime permits ALLOT to take up to 1.01 times baseline runtime (Appendix A.2).

The aggregate verdict also conceals metric disagreements: SVOO on Wan2.1-1.3B and SVG2 improve PSNR while perceptual metrics favor the default in some comparisons (Appendices H.1, H.4). HunyuanVideo’s mean-pooled comparison uses equal density because its FlexAttention path offers little or no speedup; it is not evidence of equal-latency acceleration (Appendix J.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Offline calibration is required, and changing selection strategies generally requires new probes. The additive, layer-separable estimate predicts rankings more reliably than error magnitudes (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Density cutoffs replace native input/head threshold adaptation. Their isolated effect differs by strategy, helping XAttention but hurting SVOO in tested controls (Appendix F.5).
- **Measured approximation failures:** Isolated probes can differ substantially from their contribution when other layers are sparse. A proposed correction helps HunyuanVideo PSNR but harms Wan settings, so the paper leaves it unused (Appendix F.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains bounded by the tested models and prompt sets. The arbitrary-table ranking diagnostic covers only Wan2.1-1.3B, while oracle perturbation tests exclude Wan2.2 and HunyuanVideo (Appendices E.5, E.7).
- **Comparison scope:** Several allocation baselines are reimplementations or adapted rules. FAST uses XAttention masks rather than its original topology and kernel, so that comparison isolates an allocation adaptation rather than the complete original system (Appendix G.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can cell interactions improve allocation reliably?** In-context corrections transfer poorly across models. Next experiment: fit a small interaction-aware surrogate and compare held-out fidelity at fixed runtime and calibration cost (Appendix F.2).
- **Can ALLOT preserve adaptive thresholds?** Its common density interface removes native adaptation. Next experiment: optimize cell-specific threshold policies against fixed densities using the same ranking, kernel, and runtime budget (§6).
- **How stable are rankings under input distribution shifts?** Reported stability covers the sampled videos and prompts. Next experiment: evaluate fixed tables on distinct prompt groups and compare with recalibrated tables (Appendix D).
- **Can calibration cost be reduced further?** Small input sets work well, but strategy-specific probing remains expensive. Next experiment: adaptively select probe densities and noise levels, then measure cost versus held-out allocation quality (§5.6; Appendix F.6).
