# Hidden Messages Between Diffusion Language Model Agents: Causal Evidence for Latent Communication

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28905) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28905)


## One-sentence takeaway
Receiver-side message substitutions support example-specific communication through supervised latent adapters between independent diffusion agents, while timing experiments favor sequential exchange for dependent lookups and late fusion for distributed QASC (§§5.1–5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Diffusion language models maintain revisable continuous states, but agent systems typically communicate decoded text. The authors ask whether these states can transmit useful information between independent agents—and whether receivers use the message’s specific content rather than merely benefiting from an additional activation or computation (§1).

The identified gap is causal evidence for communication across separate diffusion trajectories, including when messages should arrive.

## Approach and contributions
- **Continuous communication interface:** Compress sender hidden states into four 128-dimensional signed BF16 vectors. Receivers consume them through gated residual fusion on Tiny-A2D or virtual prefix embeddings on Dream-7B, retaining separate contexts, canvases, and trajectories (§3.1).
- **Supervised adapter training:** Freeze backbones and language-model heads; train task-specific encoders and receiver modules using teacher representations or answer-oriented supervision. This establishes learned communication, not spontaneous language emergence (§3.2).
- **Content and timing interventions:** Compare matched messages with absent, wrong-example, zero, random, and self messages; substitute specific facts; and compare recurrent versus late delivery (§3.3; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Tiny-A2D uses example-specific content | HM-Synth, 2,000 examples: accuracy is 72.9% matched, 8.6% without communication, and 9.3% deranged. Matched-minus-deranged: +63.6 percentage points, 95% paired bootstrap CI [61.5, 65.8]. | §§4.1, 5.1 |
| Synthetic fact substitutions redirect answers | On HM-Synth, sender-fact replacement changes receiver answers in 89.4% of examples; 72.9% select the substituted target. | §5.1 |
| Sequential lookup depends strongly on message content | Dream-7B dependency task, 1,024 examples: matched two-stage accuracy 100.0%, no-message 9.8%, deranged 0.0%. Table-fact substitutions produce the replacement target in 100.0% of examples. | §§4.1, 5.1 |
| Recurrent communication improves distributed QASC | All 1,000 derived development examples: 80.8% matched versus 72.6% without communication; gain +8.2 points, 95% paired CI [5.7, 10.7]. | §§4.1, 5.1 |
| Wrong QASC messages are harmful | Deranged-message accuracy is 46.8%; matched-minus-deranged is +34.0 points, 95% paired CI [30.7, 37.2]. Derangement requires a different answer label. | §5.1; Appendix B |
| Late delivery fails for the sequential dependency | Dream-7B dependency task: one late message yields 10.8% accuracy versus 100.0% with recurrent two-stage communication. | §5.2 |
| Late delivery improves QASC with fewer passes | Late accuracy is 87.8% versus 80.8% recurrent: +7.0 points, 95% paired CI [4.4, 9.6]. Late uses one transmission and three agent forward passes; recurrent uses four transmissions and nine passes. | §5.2 |

The replacements support a causal role for message identity within these trained systems: changing the sender-derived input changes a fixed receiver’s answer, sometimes toward a specified replacement fact. They do not establish human-readable semantics, an emergent language, task-independent transfer, or superiority over text communication.

The authors suggest that repeated provisional messages disrupt QASC evidence formation (§5.2). **Analyst inference:** the schedule comparison supports a timing effect, but does not directly establish that internal mechanism or a general rule covering other tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Natural-question evidence covers only Dream-7B on QASC; larger teams, other backbones, and long-form generation remain untested (§7).
- **Author-stated transfer limitation:** Adapters require task-specific supervision. Initial heterogeneous zero-shot reuse showed no detectable benefit (§7).
- **Missing comparisons:** Matched text-channel and wall-clock comparisons are explicitly deferred. Forward-pass savings therefore do not establish latency superiority (§7).
- **Analyst inference—controlled evidence access:** QASC supplies gold supporting facts, shared questions/options, and filtered distractors. Results do not establish performance with retrieved or missing evidence (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—semantic specificity:** Different-label QASC derangement establishes answer-relevant content sensitivity, but does not alone distinguish detailed fact transfer from answer-label information; Dream training explicitly includes answer-label supervision (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §4.1 specifies 5,000 bootstrap resamples for HM-Synth, whereas Appendix B describes 10,000. Dream’s aggregate over “locked evaluations” is not fully explained, and corresponding uncertainty intervals are absent (§4.1).
- Dense messages are explicitly not claimed to provide privacy (§7).

## Open research questions
- **Can adapters transfer across tasks?** Initial zero-shot reuse lacked detectable benefit (§7). Train on several tasks, then evaluate matched and deranged messages on an unseen task.
- **Can delivery timing adapt to dependencies?** The paper compares fixed schedules with opposing outcomes (§5.2). Test a learned delivery policy against fixed schedules under equal communication and compute budgets.
- **How much QASC information exceeds answer-label signaling?** Training uses label supervision and derangement changes labels (§3.2; Appendix B). Compare same-label, different-fact replacements and a matched label-only channel.
- **How does latent communication compare with text?** Matched text-channel evaluation remains future work (§7). Compare accuracy, message budget, forward passes, and measured latency under the same evidence partition.
