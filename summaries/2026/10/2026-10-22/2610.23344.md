# Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23344) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344)


## One-sentence takeaway
LM2LM improves language-model research proposals by training a proposer against an LLM surrogate periodically calibrated with real experiments, achieving stronger aggregate held-out gains than the tested baselines with fewer data-generation runs.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target **research judgment**: choosing architecture, objective, or optimization changes that improve a target language model. Executing every proposal during reinforcement learning is expensive, but optimizing against a surrogate can exploit its prediction errors as proposals change during training (§§1–2).

The identified gap is how to learn transferable proposal-generation ability from inexpensive experimental feedback while keeping that feedback grounded in current-policy outcomes.

## Approach and contributions
- **Surrogate-guided proposer training:** LM2LM trains Qwen3.8-27B with DAPO using gains predicted by a frozen GPT-6-sol research world model (RWM), grounded in real experimental records supplied in context. Rewards normalize and clip predicted gains and incorporate validity, repetition, and diversity considerations (§3.2).
- **On-policy calibration:** It periodically executes highly rated current-policy proposals and adds measured outcomes to the RWM’s context. The default configuration uses initial records plus three calibration rounds, totaling 200 real runs; the RWM’s parameters remain frozen (§3.3; §4.1; Appendix E.3).
- **Synthetic research histories and empirical diagnosis:** Predicted outcomes expand training histories, while held-out real executions evaluate proposal quality and diagnose surrogate overoptimization. The distinctive contribution is the combined training-and-calibration framework; the paper attributes the underlying RWM concept to prior work (§3.2; Appendix A.1; Appendix B.2).

## Evidence and main results
Gains are reference loss minus proposal loss. Avg@4 and Best@4 average, across research states, the mean and maximum gain among four proposals.

These real-execution evaluations support improved **aggregate proposal quality** and the value of updating surrogate evidence. The identical-proposal comparison specifically supports an effect of the updated records on ranking accuracy. They do not establish universal family-level superiority, successful long-running research campaigns, or recursive self-improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation concerns individual proposals rather than end-to-end research campaigns. Surrogate predictions remain imperfect, calibration still requires training runs, and post-training environments remain future work (§6).
- **Restricted search and scale:** Proposals operate within a curated recipe action space while model size, data, budget, tokenizer, evaluation, seeds, and hardware remain fixed. Held-out target models contain 115M–486M total parameters. **Analyst inference:** transfer to unrestricted model development or larger targets remains untested (Appendix C.2; Table 4).
- **Uneven coverage:** States are averaged equally despite uneven family representation; Marin has only two held-out states, and MDM has no held-out task. Entire-family transfer is tested only on Qwen3 (Table 3; §4.3; Appendix D.1).
- **Uncertainty and ablation gaps:** Reference losses use three runs, and feedback diagnostics include bootstrap intervals. The supplied text does not report repeated independent proposer-training runs or confidence intervals for the main comparison, nor isolated ablations of synthetic histories and reward bonuses (Table 4; Appendix I.1).
- **Cost-accounting limits:** Reported costs exclude proposer/builder inference and shared research-state construction. Tables 10–11 assign SFT zero data-generation cost despite its use of collected records; the rationale is unclear. Thus, the “total cost” comparison is not exhaustive (Appendix D.2; Tables 10–11).
- **Outcome scope:** The authors caution that predicted rewards and validation gains do not guarantee safety or broader usefulness (§7).

## Open research questions
- **Do proposal gains compound over long campaigns?** The paper evaluates fixed research states rather than complete campaigns (§6). A next experiment could compare cumulative best validation loss under equal execution budgets across sequential campaigns.
- **How broadly does transfer hold?** Only Qwen3 is evaluated as an entirely unseen family (§4.3). Repeat leave-one-family-out training across the other families and test larger targets.
- **Which auxiliary components are necessary?** Synthetic histories and reward bonuses accompany calibration without isolated ablations. Compare otherwise matched runs removing each component, measuring real gains and surrogate reliability.
- **Can calibration use fewer executions?** Top-rated selection beats random selection, but alternatives remain untested (§4.4). Compare top-rated, uncertainty-based, and hybrid selection under matched execution budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive summary of the supplied results; **medium** in broader generalization and cost conclusions.

Material missing information includes public code/model/record availability, the complete proposer prompt and execution harness, repeated-training uncertainty for main results, isolated component ablations, exhaustive inference costs, and the rationale for zero SFT data-generation cost.
