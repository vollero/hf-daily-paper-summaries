# Building AI Research Teams for Autonomous Discovery of Linear Attention Architectures

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28887) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28887)


## One-sentence takeaway
A team combining disciplinary profiles, private memory, shared evidence, and adaptive researcher selection discovered a linear-attention architecture that the authors report outperforms matched-compute baselines at 1.3B scale, although missing table contents limit verification of the gains.

## Problem and motivation
Costly experiments constrain how autonomous research teams explore ideas and refine promising directions. The authors identify an unresolved organizational question: what do disciplinary backgrounds and private accumulated experience add beyond shared results, and how should limited experimental opportunities be allocated? Linear-attention architecture search provides a setting where proposals require implementation and training to establish their value (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Research-team organization:** Sixteen instances of the same agent model receive different scientific profiles, retain private histories, and share proposals, reviews, results, and lessons. Discounted upper-confidence-bound selection allocates opportunities based on recent improvements and exploration (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Experiment workflow:** Each proposal undergoes technical and contextual review, refinement, separate implementation, efficiency checks, training, evaluation, and reflection. Search utility combines language-modeling loss, common-sense accuracy, and synthetic retrieval (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Discovered architecture:** Context-First DeltaNet (CFDN) adds a recurrent context vector to GDN2’s matrix memory. It updates shared context first, then uses the remaining prediction error to update the matrix; learned address translation and normalization complete the mechanism (§3.2; Appendix D.1).
- **Organizational evidence:** Ablations compare disciplinary profiles, private memory, and adaptive selection; a separate experiment compares multi-target and language-modeling-only objectives (§§3.3–3.5). The contribution is their combination and evaluation, rather than invention of adaptive allocation itself (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| CFDN improves all search-feedback targets over GDN2 | At approximately 150M non-embedding parameters under matched reference FLOPs: aggregate LM perplexity **12.21 → 11.90**, eight-task reduced-suite CSR accuracy **44.48% → 44.63%**, teacher-forced NIAH proxy **28.91% → 50.00%**. | §3.2, discussion of Table 1; Appendix B defines scoring. |
| Search-feedback gains do not imply uniform full-evaluation gains | At 150M, authors report better LM and generated-answer NIAH results than all baselines, but slightly worse full CSR than GDN2. Numerical table cells are absent. | §3.2; Tables 1, 8–9 referenced. |
| Larger-scale validation favors CFDN | At 1.3B under the GDN-100B reference compute budget, authors report lowest perplexity on both LM datasets and highest mean CSR, NIAH, and real-world retrieval among CFDN, GDN, KDA, and GDN2. Magnitudes are unavailable. | §3.2; Table 2 referenced. |
| Full organization finds higher-utility candidates | Across **80 iterations** per condition, best utility is **1.04** for the full system versus **0.54** without backgrounds, **0.56** with fresh memory, and **0.76** with uniform selection. Main run evaluates **63** candidates versus **65–68** in variants. | §3.3; Figure 3 discussion. |
| Objective choice changes the discovery tradeoff | At 150M, LM-only search produces lower LAMBADA perplexity; three-target search performs better on the other reported metrics. Numerical differences are unavailable. | §3.4; Table 3 referenced. |

These results support the authors’ claim that organization and evaluator choice matter in this search setting. They do not establish statistical reliability across repeated searches or generality across research domains. Search retrieval uses teacher forcing with the first answer token revealed; full evaluation uses generated answers, so those scores measure different settings (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated caveats:** Review and implementation safeguards do not guarantee correctness or novelty. Analytical FLOPs are an architectural proxy, and parameter checks plus successful training do not prove implementation fidelity (Ethics statement; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain reliability:** The supplied text reports no repeated-run variation, confidence intervals, or significance tests for organizational comparisons.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evidence covers one architecture-search problem, one agent model, and a constrained backbone. Fresh-memory and uniform-selection comparisons share the main run’s initial investigations, whereas no-background starts independently (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Shared evidence remains present in every organizational variant; its independent contribution is not isolated. The supplied text also lacks a controlled architectural ablation isolating context-first ordering from CFDN’s other changes (§3.3; Appendix D.1).
- **Evidence availability:** Table captions are present, but numerical table bodies, the promised complete profile, and several configuration details are missing from the retrieved text.

## Open research questions
- **Are organizational gains reproducible?** Run-to-run variability is unreported. Repeat each condition with independent search seeds and compare best utility and discovery trajectories.
- **Which CFDN changes cause its gains?** The trajectory combines several modifications without isolating them. Compare update order, context vector, address translation, and normalization under matched training budgets.
- **Does the organization transfer beyond this setting?** Only one agent model and architecture family are tested. Repeat the organizational comparisons with another agent model and another costly research task.
- **How much does retrieval-feedback design affect discovery?** Search uses a teacher-forced proxy. Compare searches using that proxy versus generated-answer retrieval under matched total evaluation and training budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The workflow and reported prose results are clear, but material gaps include numerical table contents, uncertainty estimates, repeated searches, utility normalization scales, complete model/training configurations, total agent and hardware costs, and confirmed availability of code, checkpoints, profiles, and research logs.
