# AgenticRoboRL: Bringing Coding-Agent Post-Training to Agentic Robotics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28589) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28589)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgenticRoboRL improves a 9B robot planner through supervised fine-tuning and multi-turn reinforcement learning, raising LIBERO-PRO success from 18.1% to 71.0% with frozen execution components and no external memory at evaluation (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Robot agents combine a planner with a harness that executes skills or generated control code. The authors identify a gap in approaches that improve harnesses and external memory while leaving planner capabilities unchanged. Applying planner post-training across harnesses is difficult because their commands, feedback, and success checks differ (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared training interface:** A proxy records actual planner requests, responses, token IDs, and rollout probabilities. Harness-specific adapters connect these calls to episode outcomes, enabling shared optimization without a common robot-action representation (§3.2).
- **Planner-only post-training:** Successful teacher trajectories initialize the planner through SFT; multi-turn RL then uses terminal task success, leave-one-out advantages, and dual-clipped PPO. Execution components remain fixed (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Teacher–student context adaptation:** For RPent, memory-dependent teacher responses are rewritten and judge-filtered to supervise a student without memory. CaP-X uses teacher outputs directly under the student chat template (§3.3.1).
- **Two execution mechanisms demonstrated:** Separate training runs improve VLA skill invocation in RPent and control-code generation in CaP-X. The authors claim a first general framework of this kind; that priority claim is not independently established here (§§1, 4.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large RPent improvement, mostly from SFT | LIBERO-PRO success: base 18.1%, SFT 65.1%, RL 71.0%; 800 episodes across eight suites; student uses no external memory and the VLA is frozen. | §4.2, Table 1 |
| RL adds a statistically supported paired gain | Relative to SFT, RL solves 121 previously failed episodes and loses 74 previously successful episodes; exact sign test reported as p=0.001 on the same 800 episodes. | §4.2 |
| Near a stronger memory-enabled reference | RL student reaches 71.0%, versus 72.1% reported for Codex (GPT-5.5) with memory; this is a system-level comparison, not a matched memory ablation. | §1, Figure 1; qualification in §4.2 |
| Gains persist under additional perturbations | LIBERO-Plus success: base 19.2%, SFT 66.7%, RL 72.2%; one episode per 10,030 instances, using unchanged checkpoints and no additional training. | §4.3, Table 2 |
| Control-code harness also benefits | CaP-X LIBERO-PRO success: base 2.7%, SFT 18.7%, RL 28.5%, versus teacher 20.3%; 60 tasks, ten evaluation states each, privileged-state access, no external task memory. | §4.4, Table 3 |
| Memory-response processing helps, with confounding | RPent rewrite-and-filter reaches 60.2% at step 438 versus mask-only 55.2% at step 424, evaluated on 800 LIBERO-PRO episodes; content and supervision coverage both differ. | §4.5, Figure 3, Table 4 |

The reported comparisons support improved planner performance within both tested harnesses and robustness to LIBERO-Plus perturbations. They do not establish transfer between harnesses: CaP-X uses separately collected trajectories and separately trained checkpoints. LIBERO-Plus also retains the underlying LIBERO tasks, so its results establish perturbation robustness rather than generalization to entirely new tasks (§§4.1, 4.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Memory-enabled teachers supply knowledge through supervision; comparisons against planners retrieving memory do not isolate memory’s contribution (§4.2). The rewriting ablation also confounds response content with supervision coverage (§4.5).
- **Narrow task generalization — analyst inference:** Training and evaluation separate initial states but retain underlying tasks. No physical-robot or wholly unseen-task evaluation is reported in the supplied text (§4.1).
- **Uneven CaP-X coverage:** Teacher demonstrations cover only 26 of 60 tasks, and RL samples a validation-selected pool of 35 tasks. Goal and Spatial performance remains low; the authors attribute this partly to sparse successful supervision (§4.4; Appendix C.3).
- **Unresolved training robustness — analyst inference:** No repeated training seeds, confidence intervals, RL-from-base ablation, or matched alternative RL objective is reported. The paired sign test supports the reported RPent checkpoint comparison but does not measure training-run variability.
- **Incomplete presentation:** Tables 1–5 have captions but no cell contents in the supplied text. Numerical analysis therefore relies on accompanying prose; the announced release is not verified.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the planner generalize to unseen tasks?** Current evaluation retains underlying training tasks. Train with entire tasks held out, then evaluate both standard and perturbed initial states.
2. **What does memory-derived supervision contribute?** Existing comparisons do not isolate it. Collect matched teacher trajectories with and without memory and control supervision volume and update count.
3. **Can planners transfer across harnesses?** The framework shares infrastructure, but checkpoints are trained separately. Compare zero-shot transfer and limited-data adaptation between RPent and CaP-X.
4. **How much do successful demonstrations constrain RL?** CaP-X gains concentrate where supervision is stronger. Compare broader demonstration collection against the current data at matched RL budgets, especially on Goal and Spatial tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly supports the main improvements and protocol, but omits full table contents, repeated-run uncertainty, hardware and total compute costs, detailed rewrite/judge prompts, and confirmed release availability. Evidence for unseen-task, physical-robot, and cross-harness checkpoint transfer is unavailable.
