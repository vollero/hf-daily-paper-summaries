# Design Anything, Design at Scale

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24771) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24771)


## One-sentence takeaway
Design Anything combines persistent design requirements, explicit artifact dependencies, and deterministic delivery checks to improve factual constraint satisfaction over same-LLM systems on multi-artifact design projects, although the comparisons do not isolate individual mechanisms (§§2–3; Appendix F).

## Problem and motivation
Design agents must deliver complete sets of artifacts, satisfy requirements wherever they apply, and preserve earlier design decisions across outputs. The authors identify a gap between generating individual artifacts and maintaining project-wide intent as deliverables multiply: existing approaches often depend on model judgments of completion or inspect artifacts individually (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Intent–execution graph:** A persistent task contract stores deliverables, constraints, and decisions; a typed workflow graph passes selected designs directly into later generations and binds deliverables to producing nodes (§2.2).
- **Two execution loops:** The agent plans, builds, executes, and audits through validated tools. A deterministic delivery controller checks execution status, output types, and quantities, then names outstanding work in subsequent turns. These checks do not establish semantic correctness (§§2.3–2.4; Appendix A).
- **Benchmark and evaluation:** DesignAnything-Bench contains 228 tasks across 19 domains and 152 distinct projects, including 38 matched project families requesting 6, 14, and 24 artifacts. Evaluation combines artifact-based metrics with blind designer preferences (§3.1; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher overall constraint satisfaction | Across 228 tasks, Design Anything scores 85.0% with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash High and 92.5% with GPT-5.6 Sol High; reported leads over the strongest same-model baselines are 15.4 and 14.4 percentage points. | §1; §3.3.1; Fig. 1 |
| Advantage persists at larger project scale | On 38 matched families requesting 24 artifacts, scores are 85.7% with DeepSeek versus OpenDesign, a 25.1-point lead; GPT scores 91.9% versus Codex’s 68.9%, a 23.0-point lead. | §3.3.2; Fig. 7 |
| Broad domain coverage | Leads or ties in constraint satisfaction in 18 of 19 domains under each LLM; each domain contains 12 tasks. These are not significance claims for individual domains. | §3.3.1; Fig. 5; Appendix C.3 |
| Full harness improves over stopping unaided | On 19 paired 24-artifact DeepSeek tasks, delivery rises from 73.9% to 96.1%, and constraint satisfaction from 57.5% to 90.4%, versus Stop at model end. | §3.4; Table 2 |
| Lower LLM cost than generic continuation | Complete versus Generic prompt uses 24.1 versus 36.1 generation requests per run across 19 tasks. Mean list-price LLM cost is $0.43 versus $1.30 on 17 paired tasks with complete telemetry; delivery and constraint differences are not statistically significant. | §3.4; Appendix D.1; Tables 2, 15 |
| Designers favor complete output sets | Twelve designers provide 1,019 blind judgments over 185 tasks. Same-model head-to-head choices favor Design Anything in 82% against OpenDesign, 70% against Codex, and 51% against JarvisHub; the latter receives 37%, with 12% ties. | §3.5; Fig. 9 |
| Scale-specific gaps have statistical support | Family-clustered bootstrap intervals for same-model constraint-satisfaction gaps exclude zero at every tested scale. Evidence that the gap *grows* with scale is significant only against JarvisHub. | Appendix C.4; Table 8 |

Constraint satisfaction pools passed factual constraints, requiring each to hold in every eligible delivered target; it is **not** the percentage of fully successful projects. Checks use normalized, case-sensitive text matching, with exclusions for unavailable evidence (Appendix B.3).

The results support better factual compliance for these complete system configurations. They do not establish universal design superiority: OpenDesign and Codex score higher on set consistency, and Codex has higher delivery point estimates (§3.3.1). Nor do they isolate the graph’s causal contribution.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One run per configuration per task leaves run variability unresolved; the design-quality judge lacks calibration against designers on these artifacts (Appendices B.3, F).
- **Author-stated:** Main comparisons vary tools and execution policies despite fixing the LLM. The delivery study changes several mechanisms together, including verifier availability (Appendices C.1, D.1, F).
- **Author-stated:** Runtime checks trust model-selected bindings and declared capabilities; they cannot verify requested content or factual compliance. Some page requirements are checked only through capability declarations (Appendices A.3, F).
- **Author-stated scope:** English briefs, scripted client responses, and mutually supported modalities exclude live client interaction, other languages, audio, and 3D evaluation (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark independence is imperfect: development used small final-corpus subsets, and one book family has different briefs across systems. These warrant clean holdout replication (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Missing-category and transcription exclusions complicate constraint-score interpretation. Reported sensitivity checks address some missing-output effects, but do not remove every eligibility concern (Appendices B.3, C.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Which mechanisms cause the gains?** Existing comparisons bundle components. Run factorial ablations of the contract, dependency edges, controller, and verifier with tools and budgets fixed.
2. **How stable are results across runs and larger projects?** Evaluation uses one run per task and matched scales up to 24 artifacts. Repeat runs and extend matched families to larger sets.
3. **Can delivery checks verify meaning?** Current checks cannot establish content compliance. Test artifact-level semantic verification against capability-only checks, measuring correction accuracy and added cost.
4. **Does the harness handle evolving client intent?** The scripted client does not test substantive revisions. Introduce controlled mid-project requirement changes and measure propagation across dependent artifacts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings and their stated scope; **medium** in attribution to particular mechanisms.

The supplied text omits many referenced tables’ numerical bodies, including complete aggregate scores, bootstrap endpoints, and configuration matrices. It also leaves the video backend unnamed, provides no repeated-run results, and does not establish full harness source availability or end-to-end production cost.
