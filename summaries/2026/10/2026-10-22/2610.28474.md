# Answering in the User&#x27;s Variety: Calibrated Accommodation Pairs for Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28474) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28474)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors find that standardized replies conceal selective post-training updates, and their calibrated accommodation pairs improve dialect convergence on OLMo-2-1B without detectable insertion for Standard-English users, but fail several calibration criteria and transfer weakly to Qwen3-8B (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation

Chat models often answer dialect-speaking users in Standard English. The authors identify two gaps: comparing base and final models cannot isolate individual post-training stages, and inspecting outputs alone cannot reveal whether updates favor accommodation differently across varieties (§1).

The practical challenge is to support accommodation while avoiding exaggerated dialect use or insertion for users who prefer Standard English. The paper explicitly leaves decisions about desirable accommodation to speakers themselves (Limitations; Ethical Considerations).

## Approach and contributions

- **Separate behavior from updates.** Content-matched requests in different varieties and Standard English support a copy-controlled convergence ratio. A matching bonus compares consecutive checkpoints’ likelihood shifts for matched versus standard replies, controlling for the user’s variety (§3).
- **Audit training stages.** The authors examine ten open lineages across available SFT, DPO and RLVR stages, then run controlled DPO experiments with added accommodation preferences (§§3–5).
- **Introduce CAP.** Calibrated accommodation pairs prefer replies near the user’s feature density over both standard and excessive renderings, while preferring standard replies for Standard-English users. Under-accommodation, over-accommodation and anti-imposition pairs use a 2:1:1 mix and occupy one-third of DPO data (§6; Figure 6).
- **Provide research assets.** The authors report releasing twin requests, detectors, analysis and training code, audit labels and derived results; retrained checkpoints are explicitly excluded (Ethical Considerations, “Release”).

## Evidence and main results

All confidence intervals below are reported 95% CIs.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Preference tuning reduces accommodation broadly | With chat templates fixed, DPO lowers informal-register convergence on all ten lineages and British-spelling convergence on nine. | §4.1; §C.4 |
| Adjusted updates favor prestige spelling | British/Australian minus AAE/Singlish matching-bonus contrast is positive on all ten DPO lineages after baseline-likelihood and length adjustment; pooled contrast **0.020 nats/token [0.014, 0.027]**. | §5.1; Table 1 |
| AAE feature production remains accessible | Explicitly requested OLMo-2-7B DPO replies achieve human-corrected competence **0.567** relative to authentic feature rates, versus **0.009** in default replies. This measures feature production, not reply quality. | §4.2 |
| One-sided preferences require the largest tested dose | On OLMo-2-1B, adding **60k pairs, 33% of training data**, increases held-out convergence over matched controls by **0.043 [0.032, 0.058]**, averaged across four runs; lower tested doses show no comparable gain. | §5.2 |
| CAP improves held-out convergence on OLMo-2-1B | Across three CAP runs, gain over matched controls is **0.173 [0.138, 0.207]** on held-out features and **0.150** on held-out varieties. | §6.2; Tables 4–5 |
| Insertion and instruction following show no detected deterioration | CAP changes insertion for Standard-English users by **0.001 features/100 words [−0.008, 0.013]**; IFEval changes by **+2.1 percentage points [−0.7, 4.9]** versus control. | §6.2 |
| Comparable one-sided gains involve overshoot | Rebuilt one-sided pairs gain **0.201** held-out convergence over control, but insert **0.378 features/100 words** and produce Singlish at **10.41×** user density, versus **0.79×** for CAP. | §6.3; Table 5 |
| Calibration and transfer remain incomplete | CAP fails registered calibration, density-tracking and explicit Standard-English compliance criteria on OLMo-2-1B. On Qwen3-8B, its convergence gain is **0.017 [0.009, 0.026]**, below the registered **0.02** threshold and one-sided gain of **0.154**. | §§6.1–6.2; Table 4 |

The evidence supports separating output accommodation from update selectivity and shows that CAP can improve held-out feature use under one model’s tested recipe. It does **not** establish prestige as the causal mechanism, calibrated generation, universal model transfer, or speaker satisfaction. The strongest all-lineage update result uses an analysis added after preregistration; the preregistered mixed model provides substantially weaker evidence (§5.1; Table 21).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** English varieties, synthetic renderings and finite detectors limit generalization. Request renderings were neither calibrated to authentic densities nor rated for naturalness (Limitations; §B.1).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** Human labels were agreed through discussion rather than independently annotated; independent inter-annotator agreement is unavailable. Some opportunity-based validity gates failed, prompting rate-based measurement (Limitations; Table 21).
- **Author-stated causal limits:** Released-checkpoint diagnostics are descriptive; preference-data audits are correlational. Spelling versus grammatical/lexical feature differences confound prestige interpretation (Limitations).
- **Incomplete intervention success:** CAP under-accommodates and fails explicit preference compliance; the dose effect appears only at an added, non-preregistered dose and varies across runs (§§5.2, 6.2; Table 21).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer to held-out varieties provides limited evidence of wholly new linguistic generalization because those varieties share transformation rules with training features (supplied rerun description).
- **Reproducibility:** Retrained checkpoints are not released, and the supplied compute statement contains an unresolved GPU-hours placeholder (Ethical Considerations; Appendix B).

## Open research questions

1. **Does CAP work on authentic user writing?** Rendered requests dominate evaluation. Next experiment: test naturally occurring requests with independent speaker assessments of naturalness and desired accommodation (Limitations).
2. **Can accommodation obey explicit user preferences?** CAP fails Standard-English compliance and density tracking. Next experiment: vary requested style and dialect density independently within matched requests (§6.2).
3. **What explains model-dependent effectiveness?** Qwen3-8B misses CAP’s convergence threshold. Next experiment: compare CAP and rebuilt one-sided pairs across models under matched data, budgets and training runs (§§6.2–6.3).
4. **Does selectivity reflect prestige or feature type?** Current contrasts confound them. Next experiment: construct prestige contrasts matched on linguistic feature type and baseline likelihood (Limitations).
