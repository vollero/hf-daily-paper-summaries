# From RL Papers to Framework Code: Inclusion and Review in Seven Open-Source Training Frameworks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28027) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28027)


## One-sentence takeaway
Only 37 of 1,598 screened general RL methods reached seven language-model training frameworks, with inclusion associated with larger-policy experiments and compact loss changes, while most unincluded methods had no public proposal record (§§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
Publishing a method, releasing code, and integrating it into a training framework are separate outcomes. Framework inclusion makes methods readily accessible to practitioners, but prior work had not measured this transition systematically. The paper studies which methods reach framework code and what public contribution records reveal about the process (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Inclusion measurement:** Trace main-branch histories of verl, TRL, OpenRLHF, NeMo-RL, ROLL, slime, and AReaL; map configuration options to methods; audit paper citations for implementations missed by parsing; distinguish defaults, selectable options, switches, experimental code, and recipes (§3).
- **Supply and comparison study:** Screen arXiv papers from January 2025–June 2026, identify general training methods, measure inclusion with exposure windows, and compare included papers with quarter-matched controls (§§4–6).
- **Public-record triangulation:** Code proposals and reviews, user-reported failures, and large-model technical reports to contextualize inclusion (§§7–9).
- **Reusable tracker:** Release implementation mappings, histories, labels, coded records, fixed snapshots, and an offline report-reproduction command (§12). This is an empirical measurement contribution, rather than a new RL algorithm.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inclusion is rare | Of 1,598 general methods among 6,939 screened papers, 37 reached at least one tracked framework: 2.3%. Seven were currently in at least three frameworks. | §5; Fig. 1a |
| Cohort intake lagged supply growth | Across five quarterly cohorts with complete 180-day exposure, cohort size grew from 72 to 386 papers, while 180-day inclusions stayed between 5 and 7; inclusion share fell from 6.9% to 1.3%. | §§1, 5; Fig. 1b |
| Included papers differed in scale and change form | Comparing 31 included general-method papers from 2025 with 150 quarter-matched unincluded controls, reported ≥30B policies appeared in 41% versus 10%; coefficient/clip/mask/weight changes in 29% versus 9%. Missing fields were excluded per row. | §6; Fig. 3; App. C |
| Code release did not distinguish groups | First-version training-code links appeared in 44% of included papers and 44% of controls in the same comparison. | §6 |
| Most unincluded methods lacked a public record | Of 1,561 unincluded general methods, 1,500 had no link/identifier/title match in framework threads. Verified method-name matches reduced the missing-record share from 96% to 95%. | §7; Fig. 1c |
| Many implementation proposals remained unanswered | Of 28 unmerged implementation PRs, 17 remained open after a median 196 days; 13 of those had no prior-merger reply. | §7; Table 9 |
| Reviews accompanied successful inclusion | Of 88 merged landing PRs, 60 drew explicit concerns and 49 underwent proposer revisions; demand/maintenance concerns appeared in 6 of 88 merged versus 5 of 28 unmerged PRs. | §7; Table 3 |
| Public failure reports were sparse | Keyword screening of 7,075 issues/discussions yielded 48 observed-failure reports: 23 unspecified collapse/divergence and 12 rollout–training mismatch reports. | §8; Table 4 |
| Large-model reports partly overlapped framework offerings | The 13 reports for open-weight models with ≥100B parameters collectively stated 8 of 13 widely included methods; 6 reports stated at least one. | Abstract; §9; Table 5 |

These observations establish patterns in shipped code and public documentation. They do not establish actual usage, algorithm quality, causes of inclusion, or that unanswered proposals were substantively rejected. The feature gaps persisted under several exclusions, including removing framework-maintaining companies’ papers, but remain observational (§§6, 11).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coverage limits:** Inclusion is a lower bound based on parsed options and cited implementations. Sampling and reward logic can reside in user code. The screened paper pool and selected frameworks exclude parts of the ecosystem (§11).
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** The feature comparison is small and observational; scale, resources, affiliations, and experimental breadth can vary together. Missing-field handling matters, and the authors decline to interpret the unstable hyperparameter-count gap (§6).
- **Author-stated public-record limits:** Private discussions are invisible; bulk releases obscure review histories; prior mergers imperfectly proxy maintainers; thread text can change. One reader coded review fields and failure reports (§11).
- **Author-stated report limits:** Technical reports disclose selectively, and latest-version readings cannot establish direction of influence between reports and framework code (§§9, 11).
- **Analyst inference:** Component-level inclusion differences may partly reflect where components are implemented and detected, given the acknowledged omission of user-side logic (§11).

## Open research questions
- **Does framework inclusion produce sustained use?** Main-branch presence does not measure adoption (§11). A prospective study could collect opt-in configuration telemetry and follow method usage after landing.
- **What causally improves proposal outcomes?** Scale and compact loss changes are associated with inclusion, while recommended author actions remain untested (§§6, 10). Randomizing proposal templates or reproducible-example support could test effects on replies and merges.
- **How much does detection miss outside framework options?** User-side sampling and reward code are outside much of the measurement (§11). A full-code audit of sampled training projects could estimate this missing coverage.
- **Do public failure reports represent training needs?** Sparse reports and unspecified collapses prevent that conclusion (§8). Structured run diaries paired with issue reports could compare observed failures with publicly reported ones.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive summary; causal interpretation is unsupported.
