# DeLoop: Repeated Actions as Self-Supervision for Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19376) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19376)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeLoop trains agents to prefer their own later non-looping actions over repeated tool calls, sharply reducing exact loops and interaction steps while preserving or improving task success in the reported evaluations (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents sometimes repeat a tool call with identical arguments and receive an identical observation, spending interaction budget without obtaining new information. Context compression increases this behavior on the principal CodeGym evaluations, but loops also occur with full history and when the earlier action remains visible (§§3.1–3.3).

The authors identify a gap between retaining useful history and training a policy to use that history. Their question is whether an agent’s failures can supply supervision without task rewards, expert actions, or external judges (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Operational loop definition:** Detect repeated canonicalized tool calls with byte-identical observations; calls returning new observations remain permissible (§3.1; Appendix D).
- **Self-mined preferences:** For each looping action, reject that action and prefer the first later different, non-looping action from the same rollout, using the context before the loop. Discard cases without later recovery; successful task completion is unnecessary (§4.1).
- **Policy training:** Apply DPO against the frozen base policy. Qwen3-8B uses one epoch of LoRA training on 8,000 CodeGym pairs; DeLoop-Mix adds cross-domain pairs. The new contribution is the structural supervision and pair construction, rather than DPO itself (§4.2; Appendix G.1).
- **Empirical characterization and transfer:** Test loop behavior, alternative objectives, preference controls, memory policies, model sizes, and additional agent environments (§§5–6; Appendices F–J).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Compression increases exact loops | Qwen3-8B CodeGym loop rate: 29.0% with full history, 57.3% with summarization at 4,096 tokens, 64.7% at 2,048 tokens. | §3.3; Fig. 3a |
| Main efficiency gain with similar success | Qwen3-8B, 300 held-out CodeGym tasks, 4,096-token summarization: loop rate 57.3% → 6.1%; steps/task 15.00 → 4.67; success 25.1% → 25.9%; estimated tokens/task fall 47%. Main policies average three evaluation runs. | §5.2; Appendices A, G.4 |
| Larger models improve success | Same compressed CodeGym setting: Qwen3-14B success 45.1% → 52.3%, loops 42.9% → 12.3%; Qwen3-32B success 37.7% → 44.8%, loops 38.5% → 12.9%. | §1; Appendix G.17 |
| Both preference sides matter | Qwen3-8B compressed CodeGym: chosen-only SFT leaves 37.7% loop rate versus 6.1% for DPO on the same 8,000 pairs. Recomputed chosen-action controls give 41.9%/36.1% for random alternatives versus 5.7% for actual later recovery. | §5.3; Appendix I.4, Table 28 |
| Cross-domain data improves success | Qwen3-8B compressed CodeGym: DeLoop-Mix raises success 25.1% → 30.3%, with loop rate 57.3% → 10.1%. | §5.4 |

These author-reported results support substantial reductions in exact repetition, with controls showing that arbitrary preferences or recovery imitation alone do not reproduce the gain. They do not establish that loops cause task failure: looping-versus-loop-free comparisons are observational. Token estimates count logged transcript content once, so they do not directly measure total inference billing, latency, or training-adjusted savings (Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described scope:** Mining requires eventual recovery and excludes unrecovered looping actions. A non-looping positive need not solve the task (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Exact equality can miss semantically redundant calls with different arguments or observations. Broader-signature analyses partly address this on CodeGym, without establishing coverage in every environment (Appendix I.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Action generation and summarization share updated weights. Without a reported frozen-summarizer comparison, their respective contributions are not fully isolated (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Shorter episodes and increased `Done()` calls make premature termination a relevant concern. Preserved success and length-conditioned analyses mitigate this concern, but success improves under only four of seven alternative memory policies (Appendices F.1, G.16, J.4).
- **Comparability and uncertainty:** Additional environments use native repeat detectors; many comparisons use one evaluation run, and repeated runs generally reuse one trained policy. The duplicate blocker also requires separating attempted repeats from executed loops (Appendices A, J.2).
- **Supplied-text gap:** Most table bodies are absent despite their captions and prose summaries. Code/data release is promised, not demonstrated.

## Open research questions
1. **Can unrecovered loops provide useful supervision?** They are discarded here (§4.1). Compare standard mining with externally generated recoveries on matched unrecovered prefixes.
2. **How much gain comes from altered summaries?** Shared weights leave this unresolved (§4.2). Cross base/trained action policies with base/trained summarizers.
3. **Does useful retry behavior remain reliable in changing environments?** CodeGym probes support selectivity, but native detectors differ elsewhere (Appendix I.1; Appendix A). Test controlled tasks where polling or repeated verification is necessary.
4. **How stable are success gains across training seeds and budgets?** Evaluation repetitions mostly reuse trained policies (Appendix A). Repeat training independently and evaluate across longer interaction budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, controls, and consistent main findings. Material gaps include most numerical table bodies, broad uncertainty estimates, independent training-seed variation, measured latency and total compute costs, a frozen-summarizer ablation, and confirmed access to released code/data.
