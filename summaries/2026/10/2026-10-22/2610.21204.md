# Entertain, then Cancel: How Thinking Makes Reasoning Models Read Some and Or Literally

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21204) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21204)


## One-sentence takeaway
Additional thinking shifts many tested reasoning models toward literal interpretations of *some* and *or*, while improving several context-dependent inference tasks; early trace answers partly recover scalar implicatures in a narrow exploratory evaluation (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
The paper asks whether additional deliberation changes how models interpret speakers, beyond changing answer accuracy. “Some” can literally include “all” while pragmatically suggesting “not all”; both readings are possible, so accuracy alone can obscure an interpretation shift.

The authors identify a gap in prior evaluations: fixed-setting accuracy comparisons do not characterize how interpretation changes with thinking budget on unchanged model weights, or where that change appears along generated reasoning (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Fixed-weight interpretation evaluation:** Vary thinking modes, token budgets, or effort levels and measure pragmatic-reading rates and answer log-odds. Separate logical-word implicatures, lexical scales, presuppositions, and contextual inferences; test stopping procedures, polarity, and answer formats (§3).
- **Trace-prefix diagnostic:** Truncate a model’s own reasoning at successive prefixes and elicit answers. This measures answers conditional on an intervention, rather than directly observing internal interpretation (§§3, 4.3).
- **Empirical characterization:** Document literal shifts across model families, contrasting improvements on contextual tasks, and recipe-dependent changes across training checkpoints (§4).
- **Exploratory remedy and resources:** Route selected scalar questions to answers extracted after 128 trace tokens. The authors report releasing code, prompts, traces, new sentences and speaker-knowledge scenarios, IMPPRES variants, and translated slices (§5; Ethical considerations).

## Evidence and main results

Here, **pp** denotes percentage points; pragmatic-reading rate is distinct from accuracy unless the task supplies a uniquely intended answer.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Literal shifts are common, but not universal | Authors count significant decreases for *some* or *or* in **30/40 open models**, using uncorrected item-bootstrap intervals plus declared robustness checks; **21/24** models shift literal in the three-way evaluation. | §4.1; §C.1; Table 12 |
| Core and larger-set evaluations agree | Qwen3-8B natural versus disabled thinking: *some* **−28.2 pp**, 95% CI **[−41.0, −16.7]**; *or* **−36.4 pp**, **[−49.5, −24.2]**. On full held-out IMPPRES sets, **300 items per trigger**, both shifts are **−37.7 pp**. | Tables 1 and 9 |
| GPT-5 also shifts literal | High versus minimal effort: *some* **−20.6 pp**, 95% CI **[−31.2, −11.2]**; *or* **−18.8 pp**, **[−27.5, −10.0]**. A second format supports the direction. | Table 1; §B.3 |
| Short thinking can initially increase enrichment | Qwen3-8B independently capped generations: *some* changes **+37.2 pp** at **128 tokens**, versus **−21.8 pp** at **1,024 tokens**, relative to disabled thinking using the stop-phrase procedure. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| Prefix answers show early endorsement followed by rejection | On **300 held-out some items, three seeds**, Qwen3-8B rises from **35.8%** with an empty trace to **52.0%** after **128 tokens**: **+16.2 pp**, CI **[12.8, 19.8]**; it ends at **1.1%**. | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Contextual inference improves on additional datasets | Qwen3-8B natural versus disabled thinking improves Circa accuracy **+17.5 pp**, CI **[12.4, 22.7]**, and NOPE accuracy **+9.9 pp**, **[4.5, 15.5]**. | Table 10 |
| Default interpretation changes at different training stages | Without deliberation, OLMo-3 *some* pragmatic rate falls **75.6% → 39.7%** from base to thinking-SFT; Tülu-3 falls **46.2% → 14.1%** from SFT to DPO, under fixed four-shot scoring. | §4.4; Table 3 |
| Early extraction gives limited recovery | Held-out scalar felicity accuracy: Qwen3-8B **1.2% → 20.5%**; DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B **2.2% → 8.1%** versus natural thinking. Pooled equal-family score improves **1.4 pp**, CI **[1.2, 1.6]**; literal accuracy is unchanged because those strata are not routed. | §5; Table 4 |

The evidence establishes a recurring, task-dependent change in elicited interpretation. It supports the authors’ operational “entertain, then cancel” description, but does not establish a cognitive mechanism or universal decline in pragmatic competence. Training-stage associations are correlational, and contextual tasks often improve.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated measurement limits:** Forced-choice framing matters; Claude results reverse direction across formats. The three-way check also changes speaker-belief questions into entailment questions, so it is not a matched replication (§4.2; Limitations).
- **Authors’ stated causal limits:** Forced-empty baselines are unfamiliar to some models; mode and effort contrasts can change templates or system prompts. Prefix probes intervene on generated text, and checkpoint comparisons cannot isolate training mechanisms (§B.1; Limitations).
- **Selective trace validity:** Some truncated traces fail to produce valid answers. Qwen3.5 trajectory summaries describe a surviving subset, limiting generalization (§4.3; §C.1).
- **Incomplete preregistered support:** The aggregate default-and-cancel gate partly failed; the framing-by-class arm and human-scale ordering test were not run. All preregistered remedies failed their development gates (Table 13; §D.2).
- **Narrow remedy and language evidence:** The remedy covers two shared-base models. Literal preservation follows from routing exclusions. Translation errors weaken multilingual evidence; human labels use discussion-based consensus without independent agreement estimates (Limitations; §§5, B.2, C.1).
- **Analyst inference—reporting ambiguity:** Some summaries differ across sections: §4.3 reports a **+16.2 pp** early-prefix rise, while Table 13 cites **+23.1 pp**; model counts also vary across roster summaries. These discrepancies warrant checking run-level records before reusing aggregates.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the shift persist in natural conversation?** Headline results rely on forced choices because automated free-answer coding failed validation. Next experiment: independently human-annotate free-form answers under randomized thinking budgets.
2. **What causes cancellation?** Prefix interventions reveal answer trajectories without identifying internal mechanisms. Next experiment: compare matched-length traces that differ only in intent-focused versus entailment-focused content.
3. **Can early extraction generalize while respecting context?** The remedy covers shared-base models and excludes literal-gold strata. Next experiment: test unrelated model families on unseen templates, including contexts that explicitly cancel implicatures.
4. **Does the effect hold across languages?** Existing translated slices contain documented errors. Next experiment: use native-authored, independently annotated multilingual scalar and contextual items.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Multiple settings support the central interpretation shift, but mechanism, natural-conversation validity, and remedy generality remain uncertain.

Material unavailable evidence includes the actual released run records and checkpoint hashes, executable routing details, complete development-gate results for failed remedies, independent human annotations, and a reconciled explanation of inconsistent numerical summaries.
