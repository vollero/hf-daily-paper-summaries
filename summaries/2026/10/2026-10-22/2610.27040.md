# ACE-Brain-0.6: Scaling Spatial Grounding for Physical Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27040) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27040)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that fine-tuning Qwen3.5-9B on diverse, annotation-verifiable grounding data raises its seven-benchmark average from 65.72 to 75.41, above Astra’s 73.68, while leaving gaps in counting and spatially constrained placement (Table 1; §5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Grounding connects language instructions to objects, parts, and locations in a scene; localization errors can therefore misdirect physical actions despite sound high-level reasoning. The authors ask whether expanding grounding supervision can bring an open vision-language model toward Astra’s spatial perception while keeping the backbone and training recipe fixed. Robot recordings offer relevant scenes but generally lack object annotations and comprehensive supervision for counting, parts, and spatial constraints (§§1, 4.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified supervision pipeline:** Combine robot recordings, egocentric interactions, object/part annotations, and spatial data. Robot annotation uses action-based keyframes, VLM-generated vocabularies, Grounding DINO detection, SAM 3 refinement and relabeling, and VLM box verification. Template-generated answers are checked against annotations, with controls for completeness, absent targets, and answer-format shortcuts (§4.2).
- **ACE-Brain-0.6:** Train on approximately 1.54 million samples using answer-token cross-entropy for one epoch. The vision encoder stays frozen while language-model parameters are fine-tuned. Grounding becomes text generation of coordinates, point lists, boxes, counts, or categorical answers (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Scaling study and evaluation asset:** Compare successive data releases under a fixed recipe and introduce RobotGrounding, a human-annotated evaluation set covering robot-scene grounding and cross-view correspondence. Public availability of the model, data, and benchmark is not established in the supplied text (§5.4; Appendix A.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher public-benchmark aggregate | Avg-7, the mean of seven official benchmark scores: ACE 75.41, Qwen3.5-9B 65.72, Astra 73.68. ACE improves over its base on six benchmarks and exceeds Astra on four. | Table 1; §§5.1–5.2 |
| Gains coexist with regressions and gaps | PixMo-Count: ACE 86.96 versus base 90.35 and Astra 88.10. RefSpatial: ACE 71.84 versus Astra 80.14. | Table 1 |
| Strong robot-scene evaluation | RobotGrounding macro accuracy: ACE 93.42 versus base 86.70 and Astra 91.10, across 2,591 questions and 14 scoring groups on held-out robot frames. | Table 1; Appendix A.2 |
| Placement remains weaker | RefSpatial placement subset: ACE 75.00 versus Astra 84.0. Adding several thousand placement questions derived from 2D masks reportedly left placement performance unchanged. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| Performance increases across expanding releases | With the same initialization and recipe, releases grow from 0.26M to 1.55M samples; Avg-7 rises from 69.3 to 75.4. Reported log-linear fit: about 2.4 points per doubling, correlation r=0.99. | §5.4; Figure 6 |
| Evidence for coverage over repetition is limited | One control doubles samples from existing sources without adding tasks/domains, gaining less than one Avg-7 point and finishing within one point of the release adding new sources. | §5.4 |

These results support improved benchmark grounding under the reported protocol. They do not establish improved physical execution by ACE: the manipulation demonstrations concern Astra (§3.1). The scaling series also changes sample count and supervision coverage together, so it does not isolate a pure data-volume effect.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Complete instance enumeration and spatially constrained placement remain weaknesses. Unoccupied 2D regions do not certify support surfaces, clearance, or 3D feasibility (§§5.3, 6).
- **Author-stated:** RobotGrounding’s box-based point scoring is lenient for thin or hollow objects. An absent-target response passes whenever it contains no point, rather than requiring an explicit correct rejection (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that supervision coverage drives scaling is suggestive rather than fully isolated: releases change multiple data properties, and only one volume control is described (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Annotation-verifiable answers can still inherit annotation errors. Automated checks are described, but annotation error rates are not reported (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty estimates or repeated-run results accompany the reported aggregate lead. Its robustness to training or evaluation variation is therefore unclear (§5).

## Open research questions
- **Does 3D-verified supervision improve placement?** The 2D-mask addition did not help (§5.3). Compare matched-size 2D and 3D-feasibility training sets on constrained placement and physical execution.
- **What fixes exhaustive enumeration?** More long point-list examples reportedly failed to improve many-instance questions (§5.4). Compare verified-complete targets and alternative output representations while holding images and sample counts fixed.
- **How much does coverage independently contribute?** Scale and composition remain confounded (§5.4). Run a factorial study varying sample count and task/domain diversity independently.
- **Do grounding gains transfer to manipulation success?** ACE is evaluated on grounding, while action case studies use Astra (§§3, 5). Substitute ACE into a fixed manipulation pipeline and measure task success and localization-related failures.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark comparisons and training outline, but causal scaling and physical-transfer conclusions remain limited.

Material missing information includes exact training-source proportions and release compositions, annotation error rates, repeated-run uncertainty, detailed public-benchmark metric definitions, compute requirements, and confirmed access to checkpoints, training data, benchmark files, and evaluation code. Training size is also reported approximately as 1.54M in §4.2 and 1.55M in §5.4.
