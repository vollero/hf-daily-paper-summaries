# Retrieval Augmented Generation Compositionality

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29722) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29722)


## One-sentence takeaway

The study finds that isolated RAG retrieval gains can substantially shrink when combined and profile rankings can change with corpus size, although its original large-interaction faithfulness hypothesis was not confirmed (§5; Appendix A.6).

## Problem and motivation

RAG pipelines combine embedding, chunking, retrieval, reranking, query augmentation, compression, and verification, but techniques are often evaluated individually. The authors ask whether those gains survive composition, whether retrieval quality and answer faithfulness favor the same choices, and whether small-corpus rankings transfer to larger corpora (§1).

The gap is controlled measurement of component interactions, rather than simply searching for a winning configuration (§2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled compositional study:** An executed D-optimal subset spanning eight pipeline dimensions, supplemented by targeted ablations and corpus scaling. Retrieval and generation receive separate evaluation tracks (§§4–5).
- **lasagna framework:** Typed stage interfaces, component registration, configuration sweeps, and isolation tests support comparisons within a fixed surrounding pipeline (§3).
- **Empirical findings and candidate profiles:** The authors characterize retrieval composition shortfalls, metric-dependent effects, and scale-dependent rankings, and derive four operational profiles. The contribution is evaluation methodology and evidence, rather than a new retrieval algorithm (§5; Appendix L.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong isolated retrieval gains substantially overlap | Cross-encoder reranking, disabling MMR, and document-awareness prefixes predict **+46.6% NDCG@10** over default_standard (**0.412**); their balanced composition realizes **+17.2%**, a **63% shortfall**. Retrieval factorial uses 93 queries. | §5; Appendix A.1, Table 3 |
| Retrieval and faithfulness are weakly related in tested configurations | Matched configurations yield pooled Pearson **r = −0.01, p = 0.91**; the authors report similarly weak relationships within benchmarks. | §5; cited Appendix D.2 |
| Original faithfulness interaction claim was not confirmed | The planned joint test was unidentifiable at configuration level. Focused query-augmentation × gating experiment: **partial η² = 0.033**, using **43 unique queries × 3 seeds**, below the planned **0.20** threshold. | Appendix A.6, Table 12; Limitations |
| No profile wins every headline metric | On the SQuAD v2/MuSiQue/HotpotQA mixture over three seeds, standard achieves NDCG@10 **0.860 ± 0.003**, faithfulness **0.739 ± 0.016**, mean latency **5.30 ± 0.05 s**; balanced achieves **0.840 ± 0.003**, **0.911 ± 0.014**, **12.25 ± 3.55 s**, respectively. | Table 2; Appendix A.1 |
| Retrieval rankings change with corpus scale | Standard leads at small sizes; balanced leads from **25K** documents and exceeds standard by **13.7% relative NDCG@10 at 200K**. §5 reports **p = 0.003**; Appendix A.6 reports **p = 0.004** under a differently named test. | §5; Figure 4(a); Appendix A.6 |
| Faithfulness additivity evidence is exploratory | Separate component gains predict **+28% faithfulness**, versus **+32%** for the best full pipeline over the same reference; the reference’s detailed specification is unavailable here. | §5; cited Table 47 |

These results support evaluating complete pipelines on both metrics and at the intended scale. They do not establish universally large interactions or a universally best profile. The retrieval shortfall is separate from the unsuccessful original faithfulness hypothesis.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** One constructed English test bed, focused domains, limited query counts, proprietary generators, and no enterprise/private or multilingual evaluation (Limitations).
- **Design and inference:** The factorial was partially executed; the original joint interaction analysis was unidentifiable, and a per-sample estimate was retracted as pseudoreplication. H2’s original cross-benchmark test was not executed (Appendix A.6).
- **Faithfulness validity:** Scores depend on judge calibration and answer length. Generator substitution changes the ordering of the closest profiles; headline profiles use deployment-native prompts (Limitations; Table 2).
- **Scaling validity:** Larger-corpus repeats vary execution stochasticity rather than corpus composition; distractors come from the same source domains. Beyond-range curves are projections (§5; Figure 4).
- **Reproducibility:** Framework code is released, but design matrices, raw scores, profiles, and analysis scripts are available only on request (Reproducibility statement).
- **Analyst inference—internal inconsistencies:** Appendix J sometimes describes balanced and max-reliable as differing in agentic components despite also saying they share verification depth and gates. Its attribution of retrieval scaling to generation-side defenses conflicts with §5’s retrieval-stage explanation. These passages weaken mechanistic conclusions.
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated; substantial supporting appendices cannot be inspected.

## Open research questions

1. **Do scale-dependent rankings survive different corpus samples?** Current repeats hold larger-corpus composition fixed. Repeat the scaling sweep across independently sampled distractor corpora and report rank uncertainty.
2. **How large are interactions under an identifiable design?** The original joint faithfulness test could not be estimated as planned. Run a sufficiently replicated factorial with independent configuration-level observations and the original decision rules.
3. **Can verification preserve bridging evidence?** Appendix J describes aggressive filtering removing multi-hop support. Compare strict relevance filtering with partial-relevance acceptance and joint cross-chunk assessment, measuring faithfulness, completeness, and latency.
4. **Do profile rankings survive standardized generation conditions?** Prompt length and generator choice affect faithfulness. Compare profiles under common prompts, controlled answer lengths, and additional generators.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main descriptive findings but contains statistical corrections and inconsistent mechanistic accounts.

Missing information includes the truncated analyses, complete profile specifications, raw per-query results, full uncertainty estimates, reconciliation of conflicting profile descriptions and scaling tests, and a clear accounting of the headline configuration total. Coverage is therefore incomplete.
