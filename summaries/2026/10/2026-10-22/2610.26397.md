# Bridged Self-Distillation for On-Policy Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26397) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26397)


## One-sentence takeaway
BSD converts external-teacher text into verifier-guided token penalties through a privileged copy of the student, improving mean Avg@4 over every base model across twelve tested teacher–student pairs (§§3, 5.1).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation usually requires teacher token probabilities aligned with the student’s tokenizer. Different tokenizers complicate alignment, while closed APIs may expose only text. Direct supervised imitation avoids these requirements but can make smaller students reproduce unsuitable reasoning or response lengths. The authors seek one training procedure that handles all three access settings while learning from student-generated rollouts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Text-to-token guidance bridge:** An external teacher supplies a precomputed solution or final response. A frozen student copy receives this text as privileged context and scores the unconditioned student’s own rollout. Their token log-probability difference supplies guidance entirely within the student’s vocabulary (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Outcome-preserving penalty adjustment:** A verifier supplies GRPO advantages. BSD applies clipped, decaying token weights only to unsuccessful rollouts with negative advantage; successful rollouts retain ordinary GRPO updates. Guidance changes penalty magnitude while preserving update direction (§3.3).
- **Unified evaluation and analysis:** The authors test shared-tokenizer, cross-tokenizer, and closed-API teachers, and examine context completeness, verifier dependence, successful-rollout weighting, and token uncertainty (§§4–6). BSD builds on privileged self-distillation; its distinctive contribution is using external-teacher text through this bridge and update rule (§7).

## Evidence and main results

Avg@4 measures average correctness across four samples per problem; the main “Avg” averages this metric across three mathematics benchmarks (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Consistent gains across teacher access settings | Authors report mean Avg@4 improvements of **1.2–4.7 percentage points** over base models across twelve pairs; BSD is best or tied among distillation methods in eleven pairs. | §5.1; Table 1 caption; §8 |
| Shared-tokenizer performance matches or exceeds OPD | With Qwen3-32B as teacher, BSD ties vanilla OPD for Qwen3-1.7B and exceeds it by up to **3.6 points** for the other students, using mean Avg@4 across the three benchmarks. | §5.1 |
| Cross-tokenizer gains have an exception | With Nemotron-3-30B-A3B, BSD improves base mean Avg@4 by **1.2–4.4 points**, beating xOPD for three students; xOPD wins for Qwen3-0.6B. | §5.1 |
| Closed-API distillation beats tested alternatives | With GPT-5.6 Luna, BSD exceeds SFT by **3.1–9.7 points** and ROPD by **0.6–3.6 points** in mean Avg@4 across the three benchmarks. | §5.1 |
| Full traces improve over answer-only guidance | Qwen3-4B/Qwen3-32B ablation: mean Avg@4 is **59.2** base, **60.6** GRPO, **61.7** answer-conditioned RLSD, and **63.9** BSD-trace. Hint and guide variants score **61.7** and **62.5**. | §6.1; Table 11 |
| Restricting guidance to failed rollouts helps in the ablation | Same setting: BSD-trace scores **63.9** mean Avg@4 versus **61.4** when successful rollouts are also weighted and **59.7** for verifier-free OPSD. | §6.1; Table 11 |
| Guidance concentrates on uncertain tokens | Before training, Spearman correlations between absolute token guidance and student entropy range **0.83–0.90** on unsuccessful rollouts. Measurement uses six rollouts for each of 24 shared hard problems, scored under each teacher’s verified trace. | §6.2; Appendix A |
| Training cost depends on the comparison | For Qwen3-4B over 150 updates: BSD/OPD costs **31.4/29.4 GPU-hours**, BSD/xOPD **48.7/70.8**, and BSD/ROPD **33.3/83.0**. Trace generation and evaluation are excluded; ROPD API compute is uncounted. | Appendix A, Table 6 |

These results support BSD’s effectiveness in the tested settings, including when only short teacher responses are available. They do not establish universal teacher compatibility, causal token-level credit assignment, or statistically reliable superiority for every reported margin. Several main table bodies are absent from the supplied extraction, so aggregate claims above rely on the authors’ explicit prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Students come from one family, reach at most 4B parameters, and are trained on English competition mathematics. Larger models, other families, and other training domains remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated verifier dependence:** BSD requires automatically checkable answers; open-ended tasks are outside the demonstrated scope (Limitations).
- **Author-stated mechanism uncertainty:** Guidance–gain relationships are correlational. The small-gap decomposition provides an interpretation, not a stability or credit-assignment guarantee (Limitations; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—limited precision:** Each main benchmark has only 30 problems. The supplied text provides no main-result confidence intervals or repeated-training-seed analysis, limiting interpretation of small differences (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Baselines use different rollout counts, response limits, and learning rates. These follow stated implementations but do not isolate performance under equal compute budgets (Table 4).
- **Analyst inference—narrow ablations and reproducibility:** Core design ablations cover one teacher–student pair. Code is announced, but weights and teacher traces are not released, and several configuration tables lack their bodies in the supplied text (§6.1; Ethics Statement; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
1. **Does BSD transfer across student families and scales?** Current students share one family and stop at 4B. Repeat matched BSD, GRPO, and OPD comparisons with other families and larger students (Limitations).
2. **Which tokens causally benefit from guidance?** Entropy correlations do not identify useful interventions. Compare full guidance with entropy-matched random-token guidance and guidance restricted to verified reasoning errors (§6.2; Limitations).
3. **How robust is BSD to incorrect teacher text?** Trace-error sensitivity is not reported. Inject controlled answer and intermediate-step errors, then measure downstream accuracy and verifier-conditioned updates (§3; §6).
4. **How much of the advantage survives equal budgets?** Existing methods use different training configurations. Compare accuracy against total compute and API cost, including offline trace generation (Appendix A).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and ablation evidence are explicit, but the supplied text omits bodies of several important tables, including full main results, Pass@4 results, teacher scores, and BSD-specific settings. Main-result uncertainty estimates, repeated-training-seed variability, trace-error sensitivity, and complete end-to-end cost comparisons are unavailable.
