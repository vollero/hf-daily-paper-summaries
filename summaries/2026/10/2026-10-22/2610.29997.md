# DONIC: Generalist Dexterous Bimanual Tracking Control from Human Demonstrations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29997) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29997)


## One-sentence takeaway
DONIC combines reference-conditioned PPO, verified demonstrations, per-hand contact rewards, and clip-centered advantages to substantially outperform two adapted baselines on simulated bimanual tracking, while physical transfer remains untested (Table 2; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Human motion references are difficult to execute with robot hands because kinematics and physical contacts differ. Bimanual tracking adds changing hand roles and asynchronous contact: accurate hand poses alone need not produce correct object motion. The authors identify scarce sustained-contact demonstrations and uneven learning signals across heterogeneous clips as barriers to training one shared controller (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared bimanual tracker:** One policy predicts bounded corrections around upcoming robot references. PPO and behavioral cloning alternate with collection of replay-verified specialist and generalist demonstrations. The authors claim novelty in joint training across operation categories and human-motion sources; the control interface and reinforcement–imitation loop build on prior trackers (§§4.1–4.4; Appendix A).
- **Object-centered per-hand reward:** Object pose and velocity remain supervised throughout a clip, while reference-required fingertips receive contact feedback separately for each engaged hand (§4.3).
- **Clip-Centered Advantages (CCA):** Subtracting each reference clip’s mean GAE advantage removes empirical clip offsets while preserving within-clip differences; critic targets remain unchanged (§4.4).
- **BiHOI dataset:** Calibrated multi-view recordings are reconstructed and retargeted into robot references, emphasizing sustained two-hand interaction (§3).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger tracking on unseen trajectories | On 53 new trajectories of training objects, public-only DONIC achieves 86.79% success rate (SR) and 67.62% completion, versus Dexplore-bi’s 9.43% and 27.87%; one checkpoint, no fine-tuning. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Generalization to held-out objects | On 164 held-out-object clips, DONIC achieves 57.32% SR and 54.69% completion, versus Dexplore-bi’s 11.59% and 26.37%. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| CCA improves joint success | Removing CCA lowers SR from 86.79% to 81.13% on unseen trajectories and from 57.32% to 48.17% on unseen objects, with training settings otherwise retained. | Table 2; §D.4 |
| Proximity-based contact attribution helps | Replacing force-plus-distance contact detection with force alone lowers SR to 81.13% and 48.78%, respectively; this ablation retains object-motion rewards. | Table 2; §D.4 |
| Additional BiHOI training helps | Expanding training from 1,845 to 3,420 clips raises SR to 88.68% and 59.76% on the same trajectory/object splits; all seven reported metrics improve. | Table 2; §§5.1, D.4 |
| BiHOI emphasizes simultaneous contact | Its 1,575 sequences cover 229 objects and 14.77 hours. Both-hand contact occupies 65.91% of contact-active frames, versus 38.57% for the analyzed H2O subset. These are pooled subset measurements. | Table 1; §B.1 |

SR tests trajectory-averaged errors for both hands: mean object/wrist translation must be at most 10 cm, mean object/wrist rotation at most π/3, and finger error at most 2π/9. Completion measures the fraction of frames passing these tests; valid later frames still count after earlier failures (§D.2).

The evidence supports improved reference fidelity under this simulation protocol. It does not establish uninterrupted task completion, physical deployment, arbitrary goal execution, or held-out operation-category generalization. DONIC also does not beat Dexplore-bi on unseen-trajectory object rotation error (Table 2; Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers simulated, reference-conditioned, single-rigid-object tracking. Multi-object control, explicit articulation/deformation, independent benchmarking, and physical transfer remain outside the demonstrated scope (§6; §§B.1, D.6).
- **Author-stated supervision limits:** Net force plus proximity cannot identify the actual collision partner. Reconstruction refinement has no explicit contact or collision loss (§C.2; §D.6).
- **Analyst inference—selection and comparison:** Replay screening excludes immediate object loss and ejection, limiting evidence on difficult unscreened references. Baselines share data and hardware simulation settings, but retain different learning pipelines and rewards; the main comparison therefore does not isolate individual innovations (§B.3; §D.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and attribution:** No multi-seed variability or confidence intervals are supplied. There is no matched-size data experiment separating BiHOI’s contact composition from the benefit of more training clips.
- **Internal reporting inconsistency:** The text says removing CCA increases all five tracking errors, but Table 2 shows lower unseen-object finger error without CCA: 0.0378 versus 0.0373 radians for DONIC. The table supports the SR/completion claim, but not that blanket error claim.

## Open research questions
1. **Does tracking transfer to physical hands?** Simulation is the only quantitative setting. A next experiment could replay held-out references on physical Wuji hands while measuring tracking errors and contact failures (§6).
2. **What drives BiHOI’s benefit?** Added data improves results, but quantity and contact composition change together. Compare equal-sized training pools with different simultaneous-contact proportions (Table 2; §B.1).
3. **Does the policy generalize to new operation categories?** The authors explicitly distinguish object/reference generalization from category generalization. Hold out complete operation categories and evaluate the frozen checkpoint (Appendix A).
4. **How robust is CCA across sampling conditions?** Empirical gains do not establish an unbiased gradient estimator. Vary per-clip rollout counts across multiple seeds and compare CCA with batch-wide centering (§C.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported simulation results and method summary; broader generalization remains unestablished.

Material missing information includes public code/data/checkpoint availability, training duration and hardware cost, multi-seed uncertainty, reconstruction accuracy against ground truth, detailed object/category split inventories, and matched-data-size or full reward-component ablations.
