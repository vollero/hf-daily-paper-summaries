# From VLM Planners to Robotic Agents: Self-Evolving Harnesses for History-Dependent Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29560) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29560)


## One-sentence takeaway
SERA revises an external planning harness around a fixed VLA executor using development rollouts, increasing reported RoboMME success from 44.38% to 51.38% with the selected harness fixed before testing (§4.3).

## Problem and motivation
History-dependent manipulation requires remembering earlier cues and distinguishing attempted actions from successful ones. Current observations alone may not reveal repetition counts, demonstration order, or unfinished prerequisites. The authors identify two linked needs: maintaining reliable task context within episodes and converting execution experience into reusable planning improvements (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Context-managing planning harness:** A VLM jointly assesses the previous command, updates a compact task summary, and selects a supported command for a fixed VLA executor. Harness rules check command formatting and respond to stagnation (§3.2; Appendices B.1–B.2).
- **Development-time harness revision:** Analysis and revision agents diagnose failures and modify planning guidance, assessment criteria, and registered control rules. Rollout verification guides further revision and selection without additional planner supervised fine-tuning (§3.3).
- **Empirical evaluation:** RoboMME experiments assess the combined framework and aggregate benefit of harness revision. The contribution is this particular integration and evaluation; the paper acknowledges prior memory, hierarchical planning, and harness-evolution systems (§2.3; §4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Harness revision improves aggregate success | Autonomous SERA: **51.38%**, versus **44.38%** without self-evolution; **+7.00 percentage points**. Each configuration uses 50 episodes per task across 16 RoboMME tasks, totaling 800 episodes; scores equally average task success. | §§4.1, 4.3; Fig. 3a; Appendix A.1 |
| SERA exceeds two reported leaderboard comparators | RoboMME overall task-averaged success: SERA **51.38%**, MemER **42.38%**, FrameSamp+Modul **44.51%**. Baseline values come from the official leaderboard rather than matched local reruns. | §4.2; Table 1 caption |
| Iterative repair improves a development panel | PatternLock successes increase from **2/6** for the base harness to **3/6** after direction guidance revision and **5/6** after also exposing all eight existing directional commands. These are the same pre-test revision episodes. | §4.3; Fig. 3c |
| Retained context can support demonstration-dependent execution | A VideoPlaceOrder test example retains the intended target through grasp attempts and combines it with current observations to complete placement, without resupplying the full demonstration each round. | §4.2; Fig. 3b |
| Planner context does not guarantee physical recovery | Exploratory RoboMemArena cases retain an unfinished pouring requirement despite executor deviation; gripper interventions yield mixed development outcomes without aggregate rates. | §4.4 |

The evidence supports an aggregate benefit from the selected harness revisions in this evaluation. It does not isolate individual revision components, establish statistical significance, or show that the gains arise specifically from memory rather than other harness changes. The PatternLock sequence is explicitly a repair illustration, not a component ablation (§4.3). Selection precedes testing and retains test regressions, but includes development fallbacks that need not pass fresh validation (Appendices A.1, B.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Repeated VLM reasoning introduces latency; the trade-off between decision quality and responsiveness has not been systematically evaluated (§5).
- **Author-observed:** Correctly retained task intent can coexist with unrecoverable executor errors; execution-level interventions remain exploratory (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons against leaderboard systems cannot isolate the harness’s contribution from differences in planner, executor, or evaluation configuration. SERA uses gpt-5.6-sol through Codex with high reasoning effort (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Task-specific priors, known-task candidate retrieval, and potentially privileged offline diagnostic materials constrain conclusions about unfamiliar tasks or deployment without such support (Appendices B.1–B.3).
- **Evidence limitation:** No supplied component ablation separates guidance, assessment, candidate availability, and control rules. Failure-only development panels cannot establish preservation of prior successes (Appendix B.3).
- **Measurement limitation:** Task-state fields are model estimates, and visual assessment has no independent Python-side veto (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which harness components drive the improvement?** The aggregate comparison and sequential PatternLock repairs do not isolate mechanisms. Next experiment: independently ablate context updates, guidance revisions, assessment overlays, and control rules on matched episodes.
- **Do revisions transfer beyond known tasks?** Current retrieval and activation conditions can use task identity. Next experiment: select revisions on one task subset and test unchanged revisions on unseen tasks with reduced task-specific priors.
- **What is the success–latency trade-off?** The paper identifies overhead without measuring it systematically. Next experiment: vary reasoning effort and planning frequency while recording success, wall-clock latency, and response to dynamic changes.
- **Can execution-level recovery reliably complement planning?** Exploratory interventions have mixed outcomes. Next experiment: compare planner-only recovery with registered gripper interventions on matched executor-deviation episodes.
