# TraceForge: Distilling with Your Own Thinking

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29680) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29680)


## One-sentence takeaway
TraceForge synthesizes reasoning in a student’s model-family style and reports better coding distillation than original teacher traces with answers held fixed, although single-run experiments do not establish why it works.

## Problem and motivation
Strong teachers may expose answers without detailed reasoning, limiting reasoning-based distillation. The authors ask whether generating replacement traces in the student family’s style can provide useful supervision without recovering hidden teacher reasoning (§§1–2). Their proposed gap concerns the **source and form of reasoning supervision**, rather than answer generation alone (§5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Answer-conditioned trace synthesis:** Train a Qwen-family generator on roughly 10,000 Qwen3.8-Max conversations to reconstruct removed reasoning from the remaining context, answer, and tool interactions (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Authenticity-based refinement:** Train a critic on real/generated trace pairs, then alternate generator and critic updates using online preference data and DPO. The critic also selects among four generated candidates (§§2.3–2.4, 3.1).
- **Controlled downstream comparisons:** Compare original versus synthesized traces with Fable 5 answers fixed, and TraceInversion versus TraceForge with GPT-6.1-SOL answers fixed (§3.1). The contribution is the family-trained synthesis pipeline and its evaluation; answer-conditioned trace synthesis itself has precedents acknowledged in §5.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Synthesized traces improve over original Fable 5 traces | Same answers and prompts, Qwen3.5-9B-Base student, final checkpoint at approximately 1,600 updates: HumanEval+ pass@1 **68.29 vs 64.63**; MBPP+ **59.52 vs 52.38**. Authors round gains to 3.7 and 7.1 percentage points. | §3.1; Table 1 |
| Advantage persists more consistently on MBPP+ | S2 exceeds S1 at all five reported MBPP+ checkpoints; on HumanEval+, S2 trails S1 at step 600. | Table 2 |
| TraceForge outperforms TraceInversion at the final checkpoint | Identical GPT-6.1-SOL answers: HumanEval+ pass@1 **59.15 vs 3.66**; MBPP+ **60.05 vs 6.61**. TraceInversion declines from **21.95 / 42.59** at step 900. | Tables 1–2 |
| General-task scores remain close | Across five corpora, ARC-Easy acc-norm spans **80.98–81.52**, ARC-Challenge acc-norm **58.19–59.73**, and BoolQ accuracy **88.35–89.63**. | Table 1 |
| Refinement improves judged authenticity | GPT-6.1-SOL’s mean assigned probability of being real: supervised generator **0.54**, real traces **0.66**, refined generator **0.68**, critic-selected best-of-four **0.82**. | §§2.2, 2.4 |

These results support a downstream advantage for the complete TraceForge pipeline in the reported settings. They do not isolate family style as the cause, establish reasoning correctness or faithfulness, or demonstrate statistical reliability across training runs. Authenticity scores measure resemblance, not task-solving quality (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged confounding:** The comparisons do not isolate family style from other pipeline differences. S3 also changes answers, so it is not a controlled style ablation (§5).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** One student family, two coding benchmarks, and one run per cell leave cross-family transfer and run-to-run variability unresolved (§3; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—missing component ablations:** Downstream results do not separately quantify supervised synthesis, adversarial refinement, and critic selection (§§2–3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The trajectory source is confidential; exact generator/critic checkpoints, judge protocol, and several synthesis settings are unspecified (§§2–3).
- **Internal inconsistencies:** The abstract cites 3,500 questions, while §3.1 specifies 5,000 trajectories. Table 1’s caption calls S4 strongest at step 900, but Table 2 contradicts that. Section 3.3 describes S2 as steadily rising despite reported declines.
- **Unverified explanations:** Reduced forgetting, answer-style adaptation, and causes of repetitive loops are author interpretations without direct mechanistic measurements (§§1, 3.3). Legal-compliance claims and the alternative answer-rewriting variant are asserted without sufficient supporting documentation or quantitative results (§4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does family style cause the gain?** Existing comparisons leave pipeline differences entangled (§5). Next experiment: vary trace style while matching answers, semantic content, length, and selection procedure.
2. **Which refinement components improve student performance?** Judge scores improve, but downstream component ablations are absent (§§2–3). Next experiment: compare supervised-only, adversarially refined, and critic-selected traces under matched budgets.
3. **Does the result generalize reliably?** Evaluation uses one student family and single runs (§3; Table 1). Next experiment: repeat across seeds and student families using a releasable corpus.
4. **Are authentic-looking traces correct and useful?** Authenticity is explicitly separate from correctness (§5). Next experiment: independently audit derivation validity and test whether authenticity predicts downstream gains.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied tables support the reported comparisons, but causal explanations and broader claims remain weakly established.

Material missing information includes exact generator/critic identities, complete training and judging protocols, trace-length and compute comparisons, uncertainty estimates, contamination checks, releasable data or weights, quantitative results for the §4 variant, and resolution of the sample-count discrepancy.
