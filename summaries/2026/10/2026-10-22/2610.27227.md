# Which Agent&#x27;s Skill Should Change? Group-Relative Credit Assignment for Evolving Multi-Agent Teams

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27227) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27227)


## One-sentence takeaway
GRSE compares repeated executions of the same question to guide agent-specific skill edits, improving reported QA results over MASkills in most evaluated settings, although its attribution remains observational (§§3, 5).

## Problem and motivation
A shared team reward does not reveal which agent or reusable skill caused success or failure: an answering agent may fail because its retriever supplied incomplete evidence. The authors argue that crediting skills from individual trajectories confuses skill presence with responsibility, allowing harmful skills to benefit from unrelated successes (§1).

The target setting keeps model weights and agent roles fixed, making natural-language skills the editable units of improvement (§3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Group-relative behavioral credit:** Run each question repeatedly under identical skill libraries, then compare rewards, agent actions, and handoffs to assess agent–skill pairs. Experiments use four executions per question (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Checked, actionable diagnoses:** Associate failures with a failure type, execution references, and a proposed action; check reference ownership and action consistency. These checks validate references, not causal explanations (§3.2).
- **Cross-question editing evidence:** Aggregate support by distinct questions, then refine, induce, consolidate, or prune skills. Candidate updates are validated separately for each agent against the pre-update team (§§3.3–3.4).

The skill representation, aggregation operator, editing operators, and validation mechanism build on MASkills; the principal change is how credit and diagnosis are formed (§§2.2, 3).

These results support better skill evolution in the tested teams, including a controlled corruption case. They do not establish causal attribution accuracy generally, universal superiority to single-model prompting, or robustness across many corrupt libraries. Citation presence is an evidence-grounding proxy; it does not itself verify that cited content supports the answer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers two sequential agents, two QA benchmarks, two actors, and one critic. Larger teams, other communication structures, longer tool-heavy tasks, and different critics remain untested (Limitations).
- **Unequal sampling and decoding:** GRSE uses more rollouts. Appendix A also states that MASkills uses greedy decoding throughout LoCoMo training, whereas GRSE samples. **Analyst inference:** the main comparison therefore does not isolate attribution quality from sampling diversity or compute.
- **Limited replication of diagnostic studies:** Robustness, mixed-task ablations, and answer-transition analyses each use one run (§§5.2–5.5). Robustness seeds also reuse helpful skills from a prior GRSE library (Appendix C).
- **Observational checks:** The authors explicitly distinguish reference consistency from explanation correctness (§3.2). **Analyst inference:** cited contrasts can still support an incorrect responsibility judgment.
- **Internal inconsistencies:** The conclusion names multi-hop as the LoCoMo exception, contradicting the abstract and §5.1, which identify open-domain. Runtime ranges also disagree: Limitations reports **1.6–2.9× actor-token cost / 1.3–1.8× wall time**, while Appendix E reports **1.7–3.0× overall actor tokens / 1.2–1.5× wall time** relative to MASkills.
- **Incomplete rendered evidence:** Several table bodies and prompt figures are absent from the supplied extraction despite their captions being present, limiting independent checking.

## Open research questions
1. **Does contrast improve credit beyond extra sampling?** The main comparison changes rollout count and LoCoMo decoding. Next experiment: match sampling, actor-token budgets, and optimizer calls while varying only joint versus isolated trajectory credit.
2. **How accurate are the responsibility judgments?** Reference checks do not establish causality (§3.2). Next experiment: intervene on individual skills or handoffs and compare measured effects with critic judgments.
3. **Does the method scale to interacting teams?** Only sequential pairs are evaluated. Next experiment: test larger teams and branching communication, including whether separately accepted updates remain beneficial when combined.
4. **How stable are robustness and diagnosis gains?** Relevant studies use single runs. Next experiment: repeat across seeds, independently constructed corrupt libraries, and alternative critics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and selected results are explicit, but complete numerical table bodies, actual prompt templates, several hyperparameter values, statistical significance tests, and critic/optimizer token costs are unavailable in the supplied text. The decoding difference and contradictory summary/runtime statements further limit interpretation.
