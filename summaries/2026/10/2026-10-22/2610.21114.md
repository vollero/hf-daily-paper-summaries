# Spectral-Spatial Interpretation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21114) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21114)


## One-sentence takeaway
SSI applies Shapley allocation to channels within each spatial cell while fixing the surrounding image, yielding localized channel explanations with exact spatial aggregation and reported faithfulness gains across three image datasets (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Spatial heatmaps collapse channel information, while global channel rankings collapse spatial variation. Multiplying these summaries assumes the same channel profile everywhere—a rank-1 restriction that can hide localized spectral evidence. The authors address this gap for RGB and multispectral models, where different regions may rely on different physically meaningful channels (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Observed-context attribution:** For each spatial cell, SSI holds all other cells at their observed values and varies only that cell’s channels against a masking baseline. Standard Shapley values allocate the resulting local logit contrast among channels. The contribution is this game formulation, not a new allocation rule (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Local guarantees:** Channel attributions sum exactly to the logit change from replacing the entire cell with baseline values. SSI satisfies local Shapley axioms but does not guarantee that attributions across all cells sum to the prediction’s total baseline-relative value (§3.4).
- **Full-grid computation and evaluation:** SSI enumerates channel subsets for RGB and samples channel permutations for larger channel counts. The authors compare joint, spatial, and spectral explanations against dense attribution methods, flat KernelSHAP, a RISE-based rank-1 control, and MC-Owen (§§3.5–4.6).

The supplied text supports the mathematical aggregation identity and reports consistent empirical advantages. However, most tables contain captions without their numerical bodies, preventing assessment of effect sizes, variability, or statistical separation. Grid-sum insertion evaluates spatial summaries rather than joint channel-position insertion (§4.1). Agreement with physical priors is a separate form of evidence from faithfulness to model behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Hundreds of correlated hyperspectral bands remain unvalidated; channel screening or adaptive grouping is proposed as future work (§5).
- **Author-stated semantics:** Results depend on the masking baseline, and SSI provides local contextual explanations rather than globally efficient prediction accounting (§§3.4, 4.1).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** ROAD mitigates some removal artifacts without eliminating all distribution shift; EuroSAT lacks a useful spatial-localization proxy (§§C.2, C.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison ambiguity:** SSI and MC-Owen estimate different quantities. Their comparison combines attribution-objective differences with estimator variance; it does not isolate either factor (§4.5, A.7).
- **Analyst inference—sampling assurance:** The formal concentration bound requires a range valid over all permutations, whereas A.5 substitutes an observed empirical range without explaining how its coverage is guaranteed. The resulting error claims are therefore less secure than the conditional theorem.
- **Analyst inference—restricted validation:** Spectral hit rates exclude classes without positive priors and evaluate prior agreement, not exhaustive channel relevance. HLS attribution also uses ground-truth mask locations to define its scalar response (§4.1, C.3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Actual training hyperparameters, evaluation sample counts, model performance, most numerical results, and the implementation-specific FullGrad conversion are unavailable or insufficiently specified (B.1–B.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How much of the MC-Owen gap comes from sampling noise?** The reported comparison changes both target quantity and estimator burden. Next experiment: sweep budgets and seeds for both methods, using exact attribution on small controlled games (§4.5).
2. **Does fixed-budget sampling remain reliable with many correlated bands?** Evaluation stops at the reported multispectral settings, while hyperspectral scalability is projected. Next experiment: measure convergence and faithfulness across channel counts, correlation levels, and permutation budgets (§5; B.4).
3. **How sensitive are explanations to the replacement mechanism?** Only mean and zero baselines are evaluated. Next experiment: compare attribution stability and removal metrics under additional baseline or reconstruction schemes (§4.1).
4. **Do HLS gains persist without ground-truth-defined attribution targets?** The current scalar response uses annotated burn locations. Next experiment: repeat evaluation using prediction-defined regions or a fixed image-level response (§4.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The formulation and local guarantees are explicit; empirical assessment is constrained by missing numerical table bodies and figure data.
