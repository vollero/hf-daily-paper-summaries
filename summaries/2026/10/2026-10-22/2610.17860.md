# Socratic Skill Learning: Interleaving Teaching and Practice for Transferable Agent Skills

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-22.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17860) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17860)


## One-sentence takeaway
Interleaving expert guidance with Student-owned practice improves within-family Skill transfer, while issue-guided review achieves similar measured utility with substantially fewer Educator tokens on the main evaluation cohort.

## Problem and motivation
A successful task trajectory may leave a procedure’s assumptions and applicability untested, making it an unreliable basis for reusable agent Skills. The authors identify a gap in trajectory distillation and post-attempt feedback: advice arrives after the learner has already chosen what to investigate. SSL instead lets external expertise redirect evidence gathering during practice (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interleaved Skill acquisition:** A read-only Educator reviews ongoing work and proposes explanations, challenges, or checks. The Student alone operates the environment, tests or rejects advice, and writes the Skill package. Fresh solvers evaluate anchor-task reuse between acquisition rounds (§4).
- **Issue-Guided SSL:** A persistent Learning-Issue Tree records questions, acceptance criteria, evidence, and revisions. Unresolved transitions trigger focused review; contradictory anchor feedback can reopen issues (§4.3; Appendix C.2).
- **SkillTransferBench:** The benchmark contains 35 families and 408 executable cases across six domains. Public specifications define permitted variation; frozen packages transfer to held-out cases in fresh sessions (§3).
- **Comparative and process evidence:** Experiments compare acquisition protocols across three model pairs, supplemented by review-behavior analysis and traced examples of advice being tested and revised (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SSL improves held-out utility | Qwen main cohort: 22 families, 155 cases, three acquisition repeats; family-macro Avg@3 utility is 0.801 for Fixed-Window SSL, versus 0.723 No Skill, 0.761 adapted Trace2Skill, and 0.751 adapted SKILL-KD. | Abstract; §5.1 |
| Gains exceed uncertainty for reported baseline contrasts | Fixed-Window SSL gains 3.99 percentage points over adapted Trace2Skill, paired family-bootstrap 95% CI [1.45, 6.49], and 4.99 points over adapted SKILL-KD, CI [1.79, 8.48], on the Qwen main cohort. | §5.1; Appendix D.1 |
| More cases pass every private check | Qwen main-cohort family-macro full-pass rate: 32.50% No Skill, 49.49% Fixed-Window SSL, 50.82% Issue-Guided SSL. | §5.1 |
| Interleaving beats post-hoc guidance across model pairs | On the shared 10-family, 77-case subset, Fixed-Window SSL exceeds Post-hoc Teaching by 4.88, 2.54, and 4.09 utility points for Qwen, DeepSeek, and Kimi. Qwen uses three repeats; the others use one. | Appendix D.3 |
| Issue-guided review reduces supervision cost | Qwen main cohort: utility 0.805 versus 0.801 for Fixed-Window SSL at g=10; 61.7% fewer Educator tokens and 16.9% fewer total acquisition tokens, with totals falling from 6.89M to 5.72M per run. | §5.4; Appendix F.1 |
| Teaching precedes more writing and revision | Qwen Fixed-Window SSL: round-standardized teaching-versus-silence differences are +23.9 percentage points for next-call writing/editing, CI [13.0, 36.1], and +38.6 points for package change over ten calls, CI [10.7, 57.8]. | Appendix E.2 |

These results support reuse across held-out members of specified task families, beyond the acquisition session. They do not establish transfer to unrelated families. The process analysis explicitly measures associations rather than causal teaching effects. The small utility difference between the two SSL schedules does not establish Issue-Guided SSL’s superiority or formal equivalence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Complete protocols are compared under a shared single-anchor setting; the separate contributions of Educator involvement, review timing, and issue structure remain unresolved (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference—bundled intervention:** Issue-Guided SSL changes triggers, context, and follow-up policy together, preventing attribution of savings to issue tracking alone (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Main-cohort selection depends partly on No-Skill screening. Additional model pairs use a smaller subset and one repeat, limiting generalization and repeatability evidence (§3.2; Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference—process validity:** Review decisions respond to ongoing work, and some revision windows lack comparable snapshots; round adjustment does not remove all potential confounding (Appendix E.2).
- **Reported cost boundary:** Educator savings do not guarantee total savings: additional Student work raises DeepSeek’s total. Accounting scopes also differ across model pairs, and adapted baseline costs lack a common scope (Appendices C.3, F.1).

## Open research questions
- **Which protocol component causes the gains?** Appendix H leaves component effects unresolved. A factorial experiment could independently vary Educator presence, review timing, issue tracking, and context size.
- **How far does transfer extend?** Evaluation stays within declared families. A next experiment could test frozen packages on variations outside those specifications and on related unseen families.
- **When do supervision savings reduce total cost?** DeepSeek requires more Student work under issue-guided review. Repeated experiments with harmonized accounting could compare total tokens and utility across model pairs.
- **Do teaching-triggered revisions improve transfer?** The process evidence links lessons to edits, not causally to utility. Randomizing eligible review opportunities and evaluating resulting package snapshots could test that link.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol design and headline results; **medium** in broader generalization and mechanism attribution.

The supplied text omits the bodies of many referenced results tables, including complete per-family scores, absolute cross-model utilities, and some uncertainty estimates. It also does not provide component-isolating ablations or evidence of transfer beyond specified families. Code and artifact availability are author-reported and were not independently verified.
