# Long-Horizon RL Needs Muon

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20256) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20256)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that a Muon-based asynchronous RL recipe improves repository issue-resolution training success over SPO baselines in selected runs across two model families, without establishing held-out generalization or Muon’s necessity.

## Problem and motivation
Software repair agents must coordinate repository inspection, edits, and tests across long interactions with sparse final rewards. Variable trajectory lengths affect loss weighting, while asynchronous collection creates differences between rollout and learner policies. The authors investigate optimizer choice and its interaction with reward shaping, importance correction, and loss aggregation in this setting (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated training recipe:** One asynchronous rollout per issue, Muon for hidden weight matrices, AdamW for other parameters, SPO++ token-based advantage standardization, token-averaged policy loss, and truncated importance sampling. These components build on existing methods; the contribution is their combination and evaluation for issue resolution (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Auxiliary reward shaping:** Turn-level length and tool-error penalties modify advantages before standardization; final patch correctness remains the reported success criterion (§3.2).
- **Empirical comparisons:** Adam versus Muon within SPO, full-recipe comparisons across two model families, and ablations of penalties, importance correction, and loss aggregation (§4).

The evidence supports a directional training-success advantage for the combined recipe in the reported runs. It does not quantify effect sizes in the supplied text, isolate every component’s contribution, demonstrate held-out performance, or establish that long-horizon RL generally requires Muon. The retrieved text includes figure captions but no readable plotted values.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. It explicitly discloses selected-run plotting, joint recipe changes, independently collected asynchronous trajectories, and seed-dependent aggregation results (§§4.1–4.2).

**Analyst inferences from those disclosures:**

- **Selection and uncertainty:** Curves use the longest available run per configuration, with a separately selected MiniCPM5-2B SPO + Muon run. Without all-run summaries or uncertainty estimates, robustness across runs is unclear (§4.1).
- **Causal attribution:** Full-recipe comparisons jointly change advantage normalization, outcome estimation, clipping, and penalties. Their gains cannot be assigned to one component (§4.1).
- **Limited generalization evidence:** Evaluation covers training success on R2E-Gym with two model families; held-out issue performance and other long-horizon domains are not reported (§4).
- **Incomplete reproducibility detail:** Exact budgets, optimizer hyperparameters, task counts, seed counts, and several penalty implementation details are unavailable. Infrastructure failures remain unscored, but their frequency is not given (§§3.2, 4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does Muon improve held-out repair performance?** The paper reports training success only. Evaluate matched checkpoints on unseen issues using the same test-based success criterion.
- **Which components drive the combined gain?** Joint recipe changes leave attribution unresolved. Run a factorial comparison of optimizer, normalization, clipping, and penalties under matched budgets across seeds.
- **When does token averaging help?** Its ranking against sequence averaging varies across seeds without length penalties. Cross aggregation choice with penalty settings and report trajectory-length distributions and success variability.
- **How does policy lag affect the recipe?** TIS addresses lag, but no lag-controlled results are reported. Vary collection staleness and compare training success and collapse frequency with and without TIS.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method descriptions and directional author claims are clear, but quantitative assessment is limited.

Missing material information includes numerical success rates and effect sizes, readable plots, complete seed-level results, uncertainty estimates, exact training and compute budgets, full hyperparameters, held-out evaluations, infrastructure-failure rates, and code or checkpoint availability.
