# More Methods, Better Agents? A Reality Check on Self-Distillation in Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19789) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19789)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the evaluated text-agent settings, self-distillation gains over GRPO are setting-dependent, and favorable final scores can conceal training instability and computational overhead (§§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
Sparse outcome rewards give multi-turn agents limited feedback about individual decisions. Self-distillation adds denser guidance from teachers with training-only privileged context, but the authors argue that reused baseline scores, inconsistent experimental conditions, and sparse stability and cost reporting obscure methodological progress (§§1–3).

The paper addresses this evaluation gap through direct reproduction and comparison, rather than proposing another training algorithm.

## Approach and contributions
- **Common comparative benchmark:** Ten self-distillation methods and GRPO are evaluated on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, Search-QA, and WebShop using Qwen3.5-2B, 4B, and 9B. Shared environments and evaluation procedures coexist with source-informed, method-specific hyperparameters (§4; Appendix C).
- **Evaluation beyond endpoints:** The study combines repeated final evaluation, validation-curve AUC, intermediate collapse and late-regression analysis, and measured training cost, including comparisons within GRPO’s time budget (§§5–7).
- **Documented implementations and records:** Appendices specify adaptations, objectives, hyperparameters, and per-run profiles; the authors describe retained configurations, logs, evaluation summaries, and analysis scripts. A project page and repository are listed, but their contents are not independently verified here (p. 1; Appendices B–G).

## Evidence and main results

Final evaluations below use eight rollout seeds at temperature 0.4 after 100 training steps. Cross-setting means equally weight twelve settings, combining ALFWorld/WebShop success percentages, Search-QA exact-match percentages, and ScienceWorld task scores.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Only two alternatives improve the aggregate endpoint | PCSD averages **54.26**, AgentOPSD **51.34**, and GRPO **49.68**: gains of **4.58** and **1.66** points. PCSD beats GRPO in six settings, loses five, and ties one; it leads no individual setting. | §5; Table S5 |
| Win counts can hide severe losses | ICSD wins seven settings but averages **44.75**, below GRPO’s **49.68**. On 4B WebShop, ICSD obtains **0.00 ± 0.00%** success versus GRPO’s **66.02 ± 1.56%**. | §5; Table 3; Table S5 |
| Endpoint and sustained-performance rankings differ | AgentOPSD ranks second by final mean (**51.34**) but ninth by horizon-normalized validation AUC (**34.41**), below GRPO’s **36.89**. OCSD leads AUC at **39.88**. | §6; Table S6 |
| Recovery can conceal prolonged collapse | On 4B ALFWorld, AgentOPSD records validation success at or below **4.3%** at six consecutive evaluations during steps 45–70, then reaches **79.3%** at step 100. | §6; Figure S2; Appendix F.2 |
| Failure depends on decoding | For 2B OVCSD on Search-QA, greedy validation accuracy falls from a **38.02%** peak to **1.34%** at step 100; the same final checkpoint obtains **33.21 ± 0.33%** under sampled evaluation, versus GRPO’s **32.38 ± 0.28%**. | Appendix F.2; Tables 3 and S10 |
| More computation frequently fails to improve endpoints | Of 120 alternative-versus-GRPO comparisons, **112** take longer; among these, **58** perform worse and **seven** tie. PCSD and AgentOPSD have mean paired net-training-time ratios of **1.167×** and **1.187×**. | §7; Table S14 |
| A fixed-step advantage can reverse under matched time | Averaging validation success across model sizes, AgentOPSD on ALFWorld drops from **68.10%** at step 100 to **37.63%** within GRPO’s time budget, below GRPO’s **58.10%**. PCSD on WebShop drops from **73.17%** to **63.83%**, below GRPO’s **68.20%**. | Table S15; Appendix G.1 |

These observations support the authors’ conclusions within the tested implementations and settings. They do not establish that self-distillation is generally ineffective or that the observed rankings are reproducible across independent training runs. The aggregate mean combines different benchmark metrics; it is not a pooled success probability. Time-budget results use logged validation checkpoints, whereas endpoint comparisons use repeated sampled evaluation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coverage limit:** Computational resources and researcher time restrict evaluation to ten representative methods; additional methods could change how broadly the patterns hold (Limitations).
- **Training randomness remains unresolved:** Repeated rollout evaluation measures variability for fixed checkpoints. The authors explicitly caution that these standard deviations do not establish variability across independent training runs (Appendix E.1).
- **Implementation dependence:** Skill-SD and RLSD use skill-bank adaptations; GRPO+OPSD’s evaluated auxiliary gradient is independent of teacher scores; OVCSD includes study-specific continuation and distribution-approximation choices. Findings therefore apply to these implementations (Appendices B–D).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generalization:** One backbone family, text environments, fixed task sets, and the specified training horizon leave transfer to other models, modalities, and longer training uncertain (§4; Tables S2–S3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confounds:** Method-specific optimization overrides preserve source configurations but prevent attributing differences solely to distillation mechanisms. No controlled ablation separating these effects is reported (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—decoder sensitivity:** Search-QA’s greedy validation and sampled final evaluation limit direct interpretation of AUC and endpoint rankings as measurements of identical behavior (Appendix F.2).

## Open research questions
- **Do rankings survive independent training seeds?** Checkpoint-level rollout repeats leave training variability unresolved. Repeat training for each method-setting and estimate uncertainty in paired performance and cost differences.
- **Which components cause collapse or recovery?** Trajectories and gradient diagnostics identify failures without establishing their causes. Ablate teacher context, auxiliary weighting, and optimization overrides in the documented failure settings.
- **Do advantages persist under longer, equal-compute training?** The fixed horizon and logged-checkpoint budget rule leave longer-term efficiency uncertain. Train methods to matched elapsed-time budgets and repeatedly evaluate their budget endpoints.
- **Why does Search-QA performance diverge across decoders?** The paper documents decoding-specific regression without resolving its mechanism. Evaluate intermediate checkpoints under both greedy and sampled decoding while analyzing their generated trajectories.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive findings for the reported runs; **medium** in broader method-selection conclusions.

Material missing evidence includes independent training-seed replications, controlled attribution ablations, results beyond the tested backbone family and horizon, and independently verified repository contents. The supplied text provides extensive configurations and records descriptions, but not the underlying logs or executable artifacts themselves.
