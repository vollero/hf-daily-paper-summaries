# What Makes a Social Agent&#x27;s Interactions Effective? Learning from Verifiable Outcomes in Paired Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20195) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20195)


## One-sentence takeaway
HICO-Bench exposes failures hidden by high conversation ratings, while PaCPO’s paired-world credit assignment improves Qwen3-8B’s Verified Success from 20.42% to 28.33% on 240 test tasks (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Everyday coordination depends on private constraints such as availability, budgets, and authority. Fluent dialogue can therefore produce an infeasible or unsupported plan. The authors identify a gap between conversation-focused social evaluations and benchmarks that verify outcomes in narrower negotiation or tool-use settings (§§1–2).

The paper addresses both outcome evaluation and the difficulty of assigning training credit to individual turns when success becomes known only after a conversation.

## Approach and contributions
- **HICO-Bench:** 180 scenarios across six coordination families, each with two worlds differing in one private condition and admitting disjoint valid plans. Agents converse and independently submit structured records. **Feasibility** checks agreement between records and satisfaction of constraints; an LLM judges **Integrity**, including factual errors and contradictions between dialogue and records. Both must pass for **Verified Success** (§3).
- **PaCPO:** After each tested-agent turn, the policy generates a tentative plan outside the dialogue. Training checks plans against both worlds and assigns paired credit once the differing condition is available. Potential-based redistribution preserves each conversation’s total terminal reward with discount factor γ = 1; PPO performs the update (§4; Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical analysis:** The authors compare thirteen models, outcome-trained RL methods, credit-assignment controls, self-play, direct SOTOPIA transfer, and separate task-specific training on MultiWOZ and CaSiNo (§5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| High Goal ratings do not certify verified outcomes | Across thirteen models, 1,010/2,806 observations with Goal ≥ 8/10 fail Verified Success: **35.99%**. These are repeated model–task observations, not distinct scenarios. | §5.2; Appendix G, Fig. 8a |
| Conversation ratings can reverse model rankings | With a fixed Qwen3-8B partner and 240 tasks per model, GPT-5.6 Luna scores higher than Gemini 3.7 Flash on Goal (**9.61 vs. 9.54**) but lower on Verified Success (**52.08% vs. 82.50%**). | §5.2; Table 2 description |
| PaCPO improves the main outcome | On 240 test tasks, Base obtains **20.42%**, PPO **25.0%**, GRPO **26.3%**, and PaCPO **28.33%** Verified Success. RL methods sample 384 training dialogues, but scenario coverage and compute differ. | §1; §§5.1–5.3; Appendix E |
| Credit controls support the paired design | On 240 tasks per condition, same-world comparison and shuffled timing each reach **26.25%** Verified Success; removing disclosure gating reaches **25.83%**, versus **28.33%** for full PaCPO. | §5.5; Table 5 description |
| Gains vary substantially by family | On 40 tasks per family, bounded-quantity Verified Success rises **30.0% → 52.5%** from Base to PaCPO; resource allocation remains **17.5%** for Base, PPO, and PaCPO. | Appendix J; Fig. 9 |
| Self-play retains gains with a smaller PPO margin | Across three generation seeds, each with 120 dialogues and 240 scored role views, PaCPO averages **28.33%** Verified Success: **6.39 percentage points** above Base and **1.11 points** above PPO. | §5.4; Appendix H |
| Judge validation provides qualified support | Two LLM judges agree on **216/240** Integrity decisions, κ = **0.783**; Kimi-K3 accepts 24 additional cases and rejects none accepted by Qwen3.8-Max. The human pilot reports **100%** Feasibility and **82.22%** Integrity agreement across a review involving 48 dialogues. | Appendix K.1–K.2 |

These results establish a mismatch between conversation ratings and the paper’s outcome criteria, and improvements under its tested training protocol. They do not establish general social competence or equal-compute superiority. MultiWOZ and CaSiNo results involve task-specific training; only SOTOPIA tests direct transfer without further training (§5.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Structured coordination excludes interactions such as emotional support and relationships without a clear final outcome. Feasibility under formal constraints is not general social appropriateness (Appendix L).
- **Judge dependence:** Integrity remains LLM-assessed. Cross-judge disagreements are directional, and human validation is a small pilot (Appendix K).
- **Comparison limits:** Dialogue budgets match, but group-based methods cover fewer training scenarios, and PaCPO incurs extra generation and annotation costs. Its generated output is 12.51% higher than PPO’s (Appendix E.2).
- **Analyst inference:** One main training seed and one main test-generation seed leave uncertainty about the stability of small method differences. Self-play repeats generation, not training (Appendices E, H).
- **Mechanism boundary:** Disclosure annotation approximates when information becomes available. Scheduling frequently yields infeasible tentative plans in both worlds, producing zero paired contrast (Appendices E, J).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The human Integrity agreement percentage lacks an explicit aggregation denominator in the supplied narrative. Many result tables appear only as captions, limiting independent checking.

## Open research questions
- **Does PaCPO retain its advantage under matched compute and coverage?** Current comparisons match dialogue counts only. Repeat training across seeds while matching scenario coverage and total generation, annotation, and optimization budgets.
- **Can paired credit help when both tentative plans are infeasible?** Scheduling often produces zero contrast and remains difficult (Appendix J). Test partial-constraint credit against binary feasibility while keeping the terminal objective fixed.
- **Do gains hold with human partners and human outcome assessment?** The paper evaluates model partners and a small human review pilot. Run blinded human–agent coordination trials with independent plan and helpfulness judgments.
- **How far does the method extend beyond disjoint outcomes?** HICO’s paired worlds deliberately exclude shared valid plans (§3.2). Evaluate overlapping valid-plan sets with a revised contrast signal.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central method and reported results, but does not expose most table cells or numerical figure contents. Material missing information includes complete baseline scores, exact external-task gains, main-comparison uncertainty across training seeds, some configuration-table details, the human-agreement aggregation denominator, and confirmation that the intended artifacts were released.
