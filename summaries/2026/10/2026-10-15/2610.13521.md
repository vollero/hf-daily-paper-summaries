# Beyond Domain-Routed Supervision: Cross-Teacher Guarding for Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13521) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13521)


## One-sentence takeaway
Road MOPD uses auxiliary teachers to assess the same student trajectories, combining agreement-guided token supervision with constrained gradient projection to improve reported multi-domain accuracy, especially medical capability under conflicting updates (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Domain-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation assigns each domain its specialist teacher, but every domain still updates shared student parameters. The authors find that medicine paired with mathematics or code exhibits persistent gradient conflict and medical degradation, whereas mathematics–code is more compatible. Their identified gap is that routing supervision does not coordinate its effects on shared parameters (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure-mode analysis:** The authors associate incompatible domain updates with asymmetric capability degradation and show that teacher disagreement predicts higher gradient-conflict rates (§§1, 4.3).
- **Agreement-guided credit assignment:** Routed and auxiliary teachers score identical student-generated tokens relative to a frozen student-initialization reference. Agreement amplifies the routed teacher’s relative reward; disagreement attenuates it. This scales the relative reward rather than the entire loss (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Preventive gradient projection:** Auxiliary-teacher gradients on those same trajectories constrain conflicting target gradients, with an adaptive floor preserving target-gradient norm. Only corrected target gradients enter the optimizer update. Multiple teachers use averaged agreement and sequential projection (§§3.3–3.4; Appendix A.2).

These are author-reported results from the supplied prose: most numerical table bodies are absent. They support improved benchmark trade-offs in the tested configurations, but do not establish that gradient conflict causes capability degradation. The authors explicitly distinguish local projection properties from guarantees about domain performance after an optimizer step (§3.3; Appendix A.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Auxiliary scoring and gradient processing add training cost; evaluation covers only mathematics, code, and medicine, with at most three domains (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated mathematical boundary:** Partial projection can retain conflict, later peer projections can reintroduce earlier conflicts, and norm retention applies before aggregation, clipping, and AdamW—not to the final optimizer displacement (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Baselines differ in optimizer-update counts and domain sampling. Single OPD references also use different training schedules, so these comparisons do not isolate performance under identical optimization budgets (Appendices B.3–B.4).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Main-result seed variability, confidence intervals, quantitative runtime overhead, and hyperparameter sensitivity are unavailable. The disagreement probe is explicitly exploratory (Appendix B.5).
- **Evidence quality:** Several cross-references are inconsistent, and many tables contain captions without score bodies in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does the benefit survive larger teacher pools?** Evaluation stops at three domains, while sequential projection cannot ensure simultaneous compatibility. Next experiment: increase teacher count and compare all-peer projection with selective teacher scoring.
- **How much improvement remains under matched compute?** Runtime overhead is unquantified and update schedules differ. Next experiment: compare accuracy at matched GPU-hours, rollout budgets, and optimizer-update counts.
- **Does same-trajectory compatibility predict future capability preservation?** The probe measures local gradient association rather than causal protection. Next experiment: measure held-out domain accuracy after controlled updates spanning different disagreement levels.
- **How dependent is the method on tokenizer and teacher compatibility?** Tested models share compatible Qwen3 tokenizers. Next experiment: evaluate heterogeneous teacher families with an explicit scoring-alignment mechanism.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method is described in detail, but empirical assessment depends heavily on narrative summaries. Missing material includes most numerical table bodies, main-result uncertainty across runs, complete shared optimization settings from Table 6, quantified compute overhead, sensitivity studies, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
