# Lluna: Collaborative Unimodal Models for Missing-Modality Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20526) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20526)


## One-sentence takeaway
Lluna combines direct predictions from frozen unimodal features with graph-based residual collaboration and progressive consistency, achieving the best evaluated Mean-Missing performance on eight datasets with a reported average relative improvement of 4.26% (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Collaboration among pretrained unimodal models can become worse than a surviving unimodal model when other modalities disappear, even though the surviving model’s input is unchanged. The authors identify two accompanying patterns: reduced linear recoverability of modality-specific information after aggregation and representation changes across availability states (§1; Appendix E).

The gap is collaboration among independently pretrained, frozen models under missing inputs, rather than missing-modality handling within a single multimodal predictor (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic framework:** Fixed surviving-model anchors measure whether collaboration helps as modalities disappear; feature probes examine private-signal recoverability (Appendix E).
- **Private prediction plus common correction:** Model-specific adapters feed a direct prediction head. A second branch performs gated message passing over a fixed graph estimated from training-set prediction agreement, then supplies a learned residual correction (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Progressive Collaboration Consistency (PCC):** Along sampled single-modality removals, richer states guide poorer states’ normalized common representations using stop-gradient targets. Task supervision remains state-specific; inference processes only the observed state (§3.4).
- **Stability analysis:** A pathwise bound relates distant normalized-representation drift to accumulated adjacent cosine discrepancies. This is a geometric guarantee under stated assumptions, not an accuracy guarantee (§3.5; Appendix G).

MM equally averages all legal nonempty incomplete states on the same test examples; one validation-MM-selected checkpoint serves all states. These results support effectiveness within the evaluated frozen-feature protocols. They do not establish superiority under naturally occurring missingness, end-to-end backbone training, or every individual availability state. Main Omni comparisons use frozen backbones with heads trained on complete inputs; additional missing-view head training covers selected models on three datasets (Appendices B.2, B.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation removes entire inputs with known availability. Partial corruption, temporal misalignment, and sample-dependent natural missingness require further evaluation (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Pool changes require training corresponding collaboration modules; replacement experiments also refit graphs and retune hyperparameters (Appendices D.1, H).
- **Analyst inference:** Equal-state MM may differ from deployment performance when availability states occur at unequal frequencies (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The graph comparison changes both weights and connectivity, so it does not isolate prediction-agreement weighting alone (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Parameter-count comparisons exclude frozen backbones and feature extraction; PCC comparison counts do not establish wall-clock savings (Appendices D.1, D.3).
- **Evidence boundary:** Positive anchor gains are dataset averages. The authors explicitly allow negative individual comparisons, and rank markings do not indicate statistical significance (Appendices A.2, E.1).
- **Author caution:** Missing-modality robustness does not establish calibration, fairness, or safety (Appendix I).

## Open research questions
1. **Does PCC help under natural or corrupted missingness?** Controlled whole-input removal leaves this unresolved. Evaluate sample-dependent missingness, partial corruption, and temporal offsets against matched controls (Appendix H).
2. **Can model pools change without full retraining?** Existing replacement experiments retrain and retune. Test adapter-only or graph-only updates after adding or replacing a frozen model (Appendices D.1, H).
3. **Does lower representation drift improve reliability?** The theorem controls geometry, while calibration remains unestablished. Sweep PCC strength and measure task performance, calibration, and subgroup errors by availability state (§3.5; Appendix I).
4. **Does chain sampling reduce actual resource use?** Reported savings count PCC comparisons. Measure training time and peak memory under matched configurations as the number of maskable inputs grows (Appendix D.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture, main results, and stated limitations, but many supplementary tables contain captions without numerical bodies, and figures lack readable plotted values. Consequently, complete per-state results, most ablation scores, several split counts, and full optimization settings cannot be checked. Actual repository contents and reproducibility are also unverified; Appendix F describes some release materials prospectively.
