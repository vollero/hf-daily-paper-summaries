# Player-1: Learning to Play from Synthetic Experience

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20584) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20584)


## One-sentence takeaway
Player-1 shows that reinforcement learning in synthetic game environments can improve screenshot-based game control beyond supervised initialization, raising its 9B model’s MineDojo-300 success from 10.54% to 38.33% with additional gains outside the RL training suite (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Gameplay requires precise, sustained, concurrent keyboard–mouse control under changing visual feedback. The authors identify two bottlenecks: demonstrations cover limited gameplay states, and interactive training with programmatically verifiable rewards is difficult to scale across commercial games. They investigate whether synthetic environments built around recurring control demands can teach skills that transfer to other games (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared control interface and supervised initialization:** Extend computer-use actions with relative mouse motion, sustained inputs, and concurrent composite actions. Initialize policies using 6,044 curated trajectories containing 295,097 turns, annotated with instructions and visual reasoning (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Player-1-Gym:** Construct sixteen synthetic 2D/3D environments covering aiming, combat, driving, navigation, and UI interaction. Game-state score increments provide verifiable rewards; appearance, camera, scene, and control-response randomization diversify experience (§3.3).
- **Multi-environment post-training:** Apply PPO to SFT-initialized Qwen3.5-based policies, using per-environment advantage normalization. Evaluate transfer through Player-1-9B and Player-1-35B-A3B, alongside analyses of environment coverage and action composition (§§3.3–5).

The contribution is the combined game-control training framework and synthetic suite; PPO itself is an adopted algorithm.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large Minecraft improvement over SFT | MineDojo-300, 8 trials/task: 9B success rises from **10.54% to 38.33%**; final 95% task-bootstrap CI **[35.13, 41.58]**. GameWAM, the strongest evaluated external baseline, scores **12.63%**. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Larger model also benefits | Same panel: 35B-A3B success rises from **23.38% to 41.04%**, with final CI **[37.83, 44.29]**. | Table 2 |
| Broader repeatable task coverage | Same panel: 9B tasks completed in at least 4/8 trials increase from **20/300 after SFT to 136/300 after RL**. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Minecraft gains concentrate in combat | 9B combat success rises from **12.50% to 52.31%**, while harvest success rises from **6.63% to 10.38%**; same 8-trial protocol. | Table 2 |
| Transfer to first-person shooting | ViZDoom-10, 50 trials/map: 9B mean scaled return rises from **0.4361 to 0.6922** after RL. Final 35B-A3B scores **0.7236**, versus GameWAM’s **0.3938**. Returns use fixed map-specific scales and equal map averaging. | §4.2; Appendix E.4 |
| Smaller browser-game progress gain | GameWorld, 170 tasks across 34 games, 3 trials/task: reported progress rises from **31.43% to 34.45%**. The prose does not explicitly identify the model variant for this pair. | §4.2; Appendix E.5 |
| More concurrent control emerges during RL | In-suite 9B validation: composite-action turns rise from **64.7% initially to 93.2% after 100 RL steps**, without an explicit composition reward. | §5.3, Figure 8 |

These results support transfer beyond the synthetic RL environments under the authors’ protocols. They do not establish that all evaluation games were absent from SFT: that stronger exclusion is explicitly stated only for the qualitative Watch Dogs 2 example (§4.2, Figure 4). Increased composite-action use is an association, not evidence that it causes transfer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Proprietary API models are largely excluded because of estimated evaluation cost; comparisons focus on representative released policies (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—comparison sensitivity:** Baselines retain different resolutions, histories, action adapters, and replanning schedules. Shared tasks, seeds, and decision budgets help comparison, but do not isolate training quality from interface differences (Appendix E.1).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The supplied text reports no isolated ablations for randomization dimensions, action-space extensions, reasoning supervision, or advantage normalization. Their individual contributions remain unresolved.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Minecraft confidence intervals quantify within-panel task-sampling uncertainty, not training-run variability. Comparable uncertainty is unavailable for the quoted ViZDoom and GameWorld results (Appendix E.3; §4.2).
- **Evidence availability:** Several tables and plots appear only as captions. Environment-scaling improvements are claimed, but their numerical outcomes are unavailable (§5.2, Figure 7). Commercial-game evidence is a qualitative sequence rather than a systematic evaluation.

## Open research questions
1. **Which randomizations drive transfer?** All supported dimensions are jointly randomized, without isolated effects reported. Next experiment: remove each dimension separately under matched training budgets and evaluate external-game performance.
2. **Does concurrent action capability cause the gains?** Composite-action frequency increases, but its causal role is untested. Next experiment: train matched policies with and without composite actions.
3. **Does environment diversity help beyond specific subset choices?** The scaling study fixes episode count, while interaction lengths and compute can vary; numerical outcomes are missing here. Next experiment: repeat multiple environment subsets with matched interaction budgets and report uncertainty (§5.2; Appendix D.4).
4. **How robust is transfer to games excluded from all post-training data?** External RL-suite transfer is demonstrated, but SFT overlap is not fully specified. Next experiment: audit game overlap and evaluate a preregistered, SFT-disjoint panel.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the framework and main Minecraft results. Material gaps include full contents of several result/configuration tables, numerical scaling results, training-seed variability, complete SFT–evaluation overlap, systematic commercial-game evaluation, and confirmed code/model/data release availability.
