# HumanoidToolBench: Benchmarking Humanoid Tool Use from Selection to Mobile Execution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02089) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02089)


## One-sentence takeaway
HumanoidToolBench provides a structured benchmark and demonstration dataset for separating humanoid tool selection from stationary and mobile execution, but the supplied text omits the numerical policy results needed to assess performance gaps.

## Problem and motivation
Humanoid tool use requires choosing a functionally suitable object, grasping it appropriately, and coordinating manipulation with locomotion. The authors identify a gap in benchmarks that jointly evaluate these capabilities: existing evaluations cover humanoid control, tool understanding, or fixed-base manipulation without combining functional selection and progressive execution demands (Introduction; Table I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 18 tasks combine three scenarios—BallMove, BallRetrieve, and IceBreak—with selection/pickup, stationary use, and mobile use. Standard and decoy modes vary tool alternatives under the same instruction (Sections III-A–B; Fig. 2).
- **Diagnostic structure:** Candidate contact, correct first contact, suitable-tool lift, tool-target contact, and success distinguish selection and execution behavior (Section III-B).
- **ToolBook dataset:** 3,094 demonstration trajectories comprise 3,003 simulation trajectories and 91 real-robot demonstrations collected through teleoperation (Section III-D).
- **Evaluation and probes:** The authors report evaluating seven policies in simulation and three on a real G1, plus focused GR00T N1.7 tool and instruction probes. Their numerical outcomes are absent from the supplied text (Abstract; Introduction).

The contribution is primarily an evaluation framework and dataset, rather than a new policy-learning algorithm.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described scope:** Real evaluation covers only stationary BallMove and BallRetrieve; mobile execution and IceBreak are evaluated only in simulation in the stated protocol (Section III-E).
- **Analyst inference:** Three scenarios on one embodiment and control stack provide limited evidence for generalization to other robots or tool functions (Sections III-B–C).
- **Analyst inference:** L0 measures selection **and grasping**, so L0 success alone cannot isolate functional reasoning. Interaction events provide additional diagnostics, but their results are unavailable (Section III-B).
- **Analyst inference:** L0 prefixes are extracted from L1/L2 attempts, so trajectory counts do not represent independent collected episodes across levels (Section III-D).
- **Missing evidence:** The supplied text gives no numerical experimental results, evaluation episode counts, uncertainty estimates, detailed training configurations, or ablations. Their presence elsewhere in the paper cannot be determined.

## Open research questions
- **What most limits completion after suitable-tool acquisition?** The authors report a gap, but its numerical breakdown is missing. Compare normal execution with suitable tools placed in a standardized grasp, recording target contact and completion.
- **Do policies learn functional properties beyond familiar appearance?** The reported unseen-tool probe lacks results here. Evaluate held-out appearances and functional properties separately using controlled decoy pairs.
- **How well does mobile tool use transfer to hardware?** Real evaluation excludes L2. Extend the real-robot protocol to matched stationary/mobile tasks and report success and safety stops.
- **How strongly do instructions control action?** Continued execution under unrelated instructions is reported without quantitative evidence. Test matched paraphrases, conflicting goals, and unrelated instructions in fixed scenes, measuring action changes and success.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium overall**—high for benchmark design and dataset counts, low for empirical performance conclusions.

Material missing information includes the Table III values, policy identities beyond GR00T N1.7, real-robot results, probe outcomes, evaluation sample sizes, uncertainty estimates, training details, and task-level demonstration counts from Table II. Code and data availability is asserted by the authors but was not independently verified.
