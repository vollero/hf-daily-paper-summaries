# KV4-RL: Unlocking Faster Asynchronous Rollouts for RLVR with INT4 KV Cache Quantization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14448) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14448)


## One-sentence takeaway
KV4-RL combines INT4 KV caches with bounded INT8 protection to achieve 1.80× rollout and 1.27× full-iteration speedups in Qwen3-8B mathematical RLVR training while keeping final six-task mean accuracy close to BF16 rollout (§6, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Asynchronous rollout sustains generation concurrency, but concentrates context processing into fewer, more expensive decode steps—a phenomenon the authors call **KV concentration**. This increases the importance of KV storage and attention processing relative to amortized weight reads (§4).

Aggressive cache quantization can accelerate decoding while changing the trajectories used for learning. The authors identify a gap between inference-oriented KV compression and repeated RLVR optimization: token-level truncated importance sampling (TIS) corrects probabilities at sampled prefixes but cannot restore unvisited prefixes or unchanged group advantages (§§3, 5.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Workload characterization:** A scheduling analysis and frozen-checkpoint traces establish KV concentration; matched decode replay compares KV quantization with specific weight-only and weight–activation implementations (§4).
- **Selective precision for RL rollout:** Calibration-free affine INT4 quantization uses one group per token-head vector, with Hadamard rotation of keys and queries. An INT8 side cache protects the first 16 and most recent 128 positions per response (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Efficient attention execution:** Separate INT4 and protected attention regions use integer operations and higher-fidelity protected probability–value arithmetic, then merge regional softmax outputs. Additional protection overhead stays bounded per response as context grows (§5.3, Appendix A.3).
- **Training validation:** Full-parameter GRPO experiments isolate rollout precision by keeping learner computation and policy evaluation in BF16 (§6.1). The authors claim the first study of sub-8-bit KV quantization for RLVR rollout; priority is not independently established here (§1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Asynchrony concentrates KV processing | Qwen3-4B checkpoint 300, matched budgets and limits: asynchronous versus synchronous rollout has 7.82× mean nominal resident KV volume, 4.52× decode-step latency, and 1.83× decode-service throughput; excludes prefill and collection work. | §4.1, Fig. 2 |
| KV quantization leads tested decode alternatives | Qwen3-4B fixed-state replay on H200 TP2: unprotected KV4 gives 1.53×, 1.71×, and 1.86× BF16 decode-forward speedups at early/middle/late checkpoints; tested W4A16 and FP8 W8A8 remain near or below BF16 speed. | §4.2, Fig. 3 |
| Protection improves conditional fidelity with modest overhead | At 4B checkpoint 300, sink-16/recent-128 protection reduces shared-prefix KL 7.0× versus KV4; heterogeneous-state replay gives 1.90× BF16 decode-forward speedup and approximately 94% of KV4 throughput. | §5.2, Fig. 4 |
| End-to-end acceleration at 8B | Over 400 GRPO updates on four H200 GPUs: 1.80× cumulative rollout and 1.27× full-iteration speedups versus BF16; run-wide mean response length differs by less than 1%. Final 50 updates reach 1.97× rollout speedup. | §6.2, Fig. 6 |
| End-to-end acceleration at 4B | Over 600 updates: rollout time falls from 8.65 to 5.50 hours (1.57×); full-iteration time from 19.80 to 16.87 hours (1.17×), with closely matched response lengths. | Appendix D.6, Fig. A6 |
| Aggregate accuracy remains close to BF16 | Common BF16 evaluation: six-task mean is 52.8% versus 52.4% at 8B after 400 updates, and 46.6% versus 47.0% at 4B after 600. Tasks use avg@8 or avg@32 as specified in Table 1. | §6.3, Table 1 |
| Aggregate similarity masks task differences | At 4B, AIME 2025 avg@32 is 13.6% for KV4-RL versus 16.5% for BF16 rollout. Unprotected KV4 has six-task means of 47.8% at 8B and 46.2% at 4B. | Table 1 |

The evidence supports an efficiency–quality trade-off in the tested setting. Common BF16 evaluation separates learned-policy differences from evaluation-time quantization. End-to-end timing combines execution, cache-capacity, scheduling, and policy-generated workload effects; it does not separately identify their contributions. The accuracy averages do not establish statistical equivalence or consistent per-task preservation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers two dense Qwen3 models, mathematics, and colocated asynchronous GRPO. MoE, hybrid attention, disaggregated systems, and multi-turn or agentic RLVR remain unvalidated (§7.2).
- **Analyst inference—training uncertainty:** Each configuration has one training run. Three evaluation seeds measure sampling variability at fixed checkpoints, leaving variation across independent training runs unknown (§6.1).
- **Diagnostic boundary:** Shared-prefix KL uses BF16-generated histories and does not measure divergence in visited trajectories (§5.2, Appendix B.5).
- **Comparison boundary:** Quantization comparisons change kernels and arithmetic as well as storage precision. Small-group and FP4 fidelity probes do not establish optimized-kernel latency rankings (§4.2, Appendices D.2–D.4).
- **Timing boundary:** Reported totals exclude initialization, restart downtime, evaluation, and checkpoint saving (§6.1).
- **Coverage:** The supplied source was truncated, including an omission within Appendix B.4; this analysis does not imply complete coverage.

## Open research questions
- **Does accuracy preservation survive training randomness?** Evaluation repeats do not cover independent training runs. Repeat each precision configuration across training seeds and compare task-level accuracy and time to a fixed quality target.
- **How portable is the protection budget?** Broader architectures and workloads are untested (§7.2). Sweep sink/recent widths on MoE, hybrid-attention, and multi-turn workloads while measuring fidelity, learning, and latency.
- **How much speedup comes from execution versus capacity?** End-to-end results combine both effects. Run controlled comparisons with equal resident-context capacity and equal memory budgets, recording admission and throughput.
- **Can cheaper fidelity diagnostics predict learning degradation?** Conditional KL excludes trajectory changes. Compare checkpoint KL with subsequent learning across protection budgets and independently sampled rollout histories.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, protocols, and consistent reported results, but coverage is truncated and training replication is limited.
