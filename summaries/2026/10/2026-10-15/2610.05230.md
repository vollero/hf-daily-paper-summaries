# Vela: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models with Adaptive Action Curve Parametrization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05230) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05230)


## One-sentence takeaway
Vela predicts continuous spline trajectories with motion-dependent horizons, improving reported manipulation success over π0.5 on two simulation benchmarks and mean subtask success on two physical-robot tasks, with ablations supporting both trajectory-space learning and horizon adaptation.

## Problem and motivation
Fixed-rate action chunks couple prediction horizon and temporal resolution to the output budget. The authors argue that this spends capacity on correlated actions and forces a compromise between covering long motions and preserving contact-sensitive detail (§1).

Prior work already uses structured trajectories and splines. The identified gap is making trajectory-space prediction work for foundation-scale VLA pretraining across heterogeneous robot datasets, rather than individual platforms or downstream execution interfaces (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory-space VLA:** A π0.5-style vision-language backbone and flow-matching action expert jointly predict cubic B-spline control points and a scalar horizon. The implementation uses 12 control points and candidate horizons of 16–40 action steps; decoded trajectories support arbitrary query grids (§§3.1–3.2, 4.1).
- **Motion-dependent supervision:** For each demonstration window, select the longest horizon whose spline residual meets a source-specific, noise-normalized criterion, with separate gripper-transition checks. Targets are constructed through batched fitting during training; inference requires no fitting or search (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Cross-embodiment training:** A shared 26-dimensional action interface, validity masks, and source-specific normalization accommodate heterogeneous data. Pretraining uses approximately 20,000 hours of public data and approximately 20,000 hours of private data (§§3.4, 4.1).
- **Decoder-aware objective:** Decoded-trajectory flow matching weights control-point errors by their effect on the resulting action trajectory. Its empirical benefit is modest relative to the broader representation change (§3.4; Table 5).

The claimed novelty is the combination enabling large-scale trajectory-space VLA pretraining; the paper does not introduce spline-based robot policies generally (§2).

The strongest controlled evidence comes from π0.5-Vela, which shares π0.5’s starting checkpoint and benchmark optimization settings, plus the horizon and loss ablations. Full Vela’s improvement over π0.5-Vela is consistent with benefits from its pretraining, but does not isolate trajectory-space pretraining from differences in pretraining data and initialization. Results establish gains in the reported settings, without establishing statistical significance, deployment efficiency, or end-to-end physical-task completion rates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Model and action-expert scaling remain unexplored. The representation adapts horizon while retaining a fixed control-point count; variable capacity is proposed as future work (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—noise calibration:** Horizon selection relies on an idealized zero-mean, temporally uncorrelated perturbation model and second-difference noise estimates. Robustness to violations is not evaluated (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—adequacy exceptions:** If no horizon passes the criteria, training uses the shortest candidate anyway. The paper explicitly notes that this fallback does not guarantee adequacy, but reports no fallback frequency (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and scope:** No confidence intervals or variation across training seeds are reported. Physical evaluation covers two tasks on one platform, with ten trials per subtask rather than reported full-task completion (§4.3; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and attribution:** Private data form a substantial part of pretraining. The text does not establish artifact availability or provide a data-matched pointwise pretraining comparison (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—continuity scope:** Mathematical smoothness is specified within predicted spline segments; continuity across replanning boundaries and benefits from changing control rates are not demonstrated (§3.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does trajectory-space pretraining outperform pointwise pretraining on identical data and compute?** Current full-model comparisons do not isolate that factor. Train both representations with matched initialization, mixtures, compute, and execution settings.
- **How reliable is noise-aware horizon selection across demonstration sources?** Calibration assumptions and fallback frequency are not empirically characterized. Vary temporal noise correlation and magnitude, then measure selected horizons, fallback rates, reconstruction fidelity, and task success.
- **Would variable control-point counts help complex motions?** Only temporal support adapts in this study (Appendix E). Compare joint horizon-and-capacity prediction against fixed-budget Vela under matched inference budgets.
- **Do subtask improvements translate into reliable complete physical tasks?** Reported physical metrics average separate stage outcomes. Evaluate uninterrupted cooking and shredding episodes with more trials, reporting completion, recovery, and uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported scores, but causal attribution and generalization remain less certain.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates, training-seed counts, full-task physical success rates, real-world training details, private-data composition, artifact availability, measured latency and fitting overhead, horizon/fallback distributions, the fixed-horizon ablation’s chosen horizon, and a data-matched pointwise pretraining control.
