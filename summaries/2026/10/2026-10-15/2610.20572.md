# Recovering Reliable Supervision via Critique-Guided On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20572) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20572)


## One-sentence takeaway
CriticOPD uses teacher critiques to guide student-written corrections and teacher scoring during training, improving six-benchmark mathematical accuracy over standard on-policy distillation while requiring no feedback at inference (§§3–4, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) supervises a student’s own generated prefixes with teacher token probabilities. Its corrective value depends on **teacher recoverability**: the probability that the teacher reaches a correct answer when continuing from a student prefix (§2).

The authors find that erroneous prefixes can derail an otherwise capable teacher even when it can still identify errors. They argue that existing truncation and teacher-takeover approaches avoid unreliable supervision at the cost of increasing the mismatch between training trajectories and student inference trajectories. Their goal is to improve corrective supervision while keeping supervised text student-generated (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Recoverability diagnosis:** The paper measures continuation accuracy along correct and incorrect student rollouts and distinguishes declining continuation reliability from relatively stable precision of teacher error flags (§2).
- **Critique-guided trajectory repair:** For incorrect or truncated rollouts, the teacher receives the reference answer and identifies errors. The student retains the prefix through the last critiqued paragraph and generates a replacement suffix with the critique available (§3.1).
- **Privileged-feedback distillation:** The teacher scores the rewritten suffix with the critique; student training probabilities exclude it. Only the rewritten suffix receives supervision, with standard OPD used for correct rollouts or those lacking a usable critique. The update is explicitly a surrogate because generation and student scoring use different contexts (§3.2).
- **Mechanistic analysis:** The authors decompose the feedback-conditioned advantage into an ordinary OPD term plus a critique-dependent token reward, then test whether this improves credit for corrections and student recovery (§4.4).

## Evidence and main results

Main mathematical results report macro-average accuracy across six benchmarks, using 32 samples per problem for AIME/AMC and eight elsewhere, with a 32,768-token evaluation budget. Qwen3 results are means ± sample standard deviations across three training seeds (§4.1; Appendix C.5).

The evidence supports improved mathematical accuracy and stronger recovery from erroneous prefixes in the tested settings. Ablations support the value of both critique-guided generation and critique-conditioned scoring. They do not establish universal recoverability, a causal explanation based solely on occupancy mismatch, or gains under every inference budget.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Training requires reference answers and verifiable correctness. Training on code, tasks without verifiable answers, thinking-mode students, and broader model scales remains untested (Limitations).
- **Critique reliability:** The diagnostic error judgments come from a model judge, which may disagree with humans. Reported flag precision does not establish detection recall or directly measure answer-conditioned training-critique quality (§2.2; Limitations).
- **Limited replication of components:** Main Qwen3 comparisons use multiple seeds, but component ablations and the OLMo extension use one seed. Writer/scoring ablations use the answer-free variant as their reference (Table 2; Appendices A.2, E.1).
- **Budget sensitivity:** CriticOPD produces longer responses and falls below OPD under the paper’s retrospective 4k and 8k token cutoffs. Those evaluations count over-budget completed responses as incorrect rather than regenerating under shorter limits (Appendix E.6).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic evidence is qualified:** Position and hidden-state occupancy measurements are lower bounds on distributional mismatch, while the rewrite experiment changes both trajectories and teacher context. Neither isolates a complete causal account (§3.1; Appendix B.6).
- **Optimization caveat:** The authors acknowledge that the feedback update is a surrogate rather than an unbiased gradient of the stated KL objective (§3.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Can CriticOPD work without reference answers?** The paper leaves autonomous correctness assessment unresolved. A next experiment could replace reference answers with a separately validated correctness critic and measure training accuracy and critique errors.
2. **How much does critique quality control the gain?** The paper uses one critique per rollout and lacks human-validated critique-quality comparisons. Test single versus multiple critiques with human-audited error precision and recall under matched compute.
3. **Can the benefit survive tight inference budgets?** The reported advantage reverses under shorter retrospective cutoffs. Train with explicit length constraints and evaluate freshly generated responses at matched token budgets.
4. **Does correcting the sampling mismatch improve training?** The surrogate update’s bias is acknowledged but not experimentally isolated. Compare the current update with an importance-corrected alternative while measuring stability and held-out accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported main results and method description; **medium** in the proposed mechanism’s generality.
