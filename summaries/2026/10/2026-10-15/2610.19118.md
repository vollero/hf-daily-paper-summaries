# DecisionRobust: Benchmarking Robustness for JEV Style Decision Models Across Text and Vision

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19118) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19118)


## One-sentence takeaway
DecisionRobust finds that validated text and image attacks affect decision models beyond those used to select attacks, while robustness varies by task and threat type and apparent attack success often decreases after label-consistency checks (§§3.2–3.3).

## Problem and motivation
Decision models return structured choices, judgments, or scores that can control automated workflows. Attacker-controlled observations or images can induce incorrect decisions while preserving the required output format, so clean accuracy alone is insufficient (§1).

The authors identify a gap in application-grounded evaluation across modalities, attack strategies, and models, including whether attacks transfer and whether apparent failures preserve the original task’s answer (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Application-grounded benchmark:** Tasks retain native Choice, Noul, and Score interfaces, covering textual observations and image-based decisions (§2.1).
- **Validated attack curation:** Existing text attacks, sensor edits, image corruptions, surrogate-optimized image attacks, and prompt injections are combined with applicable label checks, semantic review, readability review, and cross-model selection (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Cross-model evaluation:** Curated sets are tested on models excluded from candidate selection, with separate metrics for incorrect decisions and attacker-targeted decisions (§3).

The contribution is the benchmark, curation protocol, and comparative evidence; the paper largely adapts existing attack methods rather than introducing a new attack algorithm.

## Evidence and main results

ASR uses each model’s eligible clean-correct samples with valid attack responses; candidate-level ASR counts individual retained variants (§3.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Curated text attacks generalize beyond selection models | Evaluation covers 30 models, including 24 held out, using 141 perturbations and 374 injections; all held-out models incur errors. | §3.2.3; Fig. 2 |
| Text perturbation resistance does not imply injection resistance | GLiNER2.5-Decide-340M has the lowest curated candidate-level perturbation ASR, 11.4%, but injection target ASR of 55.3%. | §3.2.3; Fig. 2 |
| Curated image attacks transfer, with substantial threat-specific differences | Across 17 models, including 12 held out, Strands Decider-2B has the highest held-out candidate-level perturbation ASR, 31.8%, and in-image target ASR, 81.2%. | §3.3.3; Fig. 3 |
| A model resistant to image perturbations remains vulnerable to instruction injection | Perplexity Decider v1.1-27B records 3.2% candidate-level perturbation ASR versus 45.7% instruction-injection target ASR on the respective curated sets. | §3.3.3; Fig. 3 |
| Optimized image transfer is limited under the tested setting | With three-surrogate optimization, an \(L_\infty\) budget of 8/255, and 160 Choice image pairs, label-consistent ASR remains below 10% on seven of ten nonsurrogate targets. | §2.3.2; Appendix A.5 |
| Validity checks exclude many apparent successes | Checks reduce common-image ASR on every evaluated dataset/model and in-image injection ASR on all five selection models; most raw text successes on NevIR and Siim fail label checks. Exact reductions are unavailable in the supplied table bodies. | §§3.2.1, 3.3.1–3.3.2 |
| Attacks exceed ordinary variation on the construction model | Across all 16 text source groups, combined attacks exceed unchanged reruns in raw sample-level ASR and paraphrasing in label-consistent ASR. | §3.2.1; Appendix A.1 |

These results establish vulnerabilities under the tested attacks and curated distributions. They do not establish deployment-wide failure rates or a universal robustness ranking: candidate selection favors difficult examples, eligible denominators differ by model, and threat sets differ (§§2.3.3, 3.1). Low transfer ASR also does not demonstrate resistance to target-adaptive attacks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation concerns individual decisions, not downstream workflow effects. Computational constraints restrict attack methods, configurations, and task coverage; multiple image surrogates do not ensure broad transfer (Limitations).
- **Author-stated comparison caveats:** Text attack comparisons do not equalize query budgets. Cross-format experiments have differing candidate pools or denominators, and text destination-format validity is not independently checked (Appendices A.1 and B).
- **Analyst inference:** LLM-based label checks may introduce validator-dependent filtering; the supplied text provides no independent estimate of validator error (§2.3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Injection-location comparisons involve different permissions and validation: instruction injection assumes task-prompt access, while only in-image injection receives label checks (§§2.2–2.3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Clean performance should accompany ASR rankings. GLiNER’s low perturbation ASR occurs alongside 49.6% clean accuracy on its curated perturbation pairs (Table A9).

## Open research questions
- **Do individual errors cause consequential workflow failures?** Workflow effects are outside scope. A next experiment could embed the decision tasks in complete workflows and measure final task outcomes.
- **Can image attacks transfer more broadly at comparable cost?** Tested ensemble optimization transfers unevenly. Compare alternative surrogate ensembles and objectives using matched perturbation and computation budgets (Appendix A.5).
- **How sensitive are conclusions to validity filtering?** Checks materially change measured success, but validator accuracy is unquantified. Independently adjudicate accepted and rejected candidates and recompute ASR.
- **Which text methods remain strongest under equal query budgets?** Reported method comparisons do not control search cost. Repeat the shared-task comparison with matched budgets and validity checks (Appendix A.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose and Tables A9–A11 support the central findings, but most results tables contain captions without numerical bodies, and Figures 2–3 lack readable plotted values.

Material missing information includes exact per-task ASRs and denominators for those tables, uncertainty estimates, validator error rates, complete task-source mappings from Table 1, and several search hyperparameters. The text announces benchmark availability but does not establish that the promised full preselection candidate release is available.
