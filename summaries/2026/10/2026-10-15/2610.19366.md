# Seeing, Maintaining, and Learning: The Evolution of Multimodal Agent Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19366) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19366)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey organizes multimodal agent memory by representation and deployment-time adaptation, highlighting how retained evidence constrains later retrieval, correction, and policy improvement (§§1–2.3).

## Problem and motivation
Long-running multimodal agents must preserve useful experience across interactions while users, tasks, and environments change. Images, audio, video, and spatial observations are costly to retain and difficult to summarize without losing information needed later (§1).

The authors identify a gap in component-centered taxonomies: they do not sufficiently connect how memory is formed with what can subsequently be retrieved, verified, revised, or forgotten. Their framework instead follows the operational memory lifecycle (§1).

## Approach and contributions
This is a survey and analytical taxonomy; the supplied text does not present a new agent implementation or controlled performance evaluation.

- **Two-axis framework:** Axis I classifies the representation supplied to downstream computation: source/modality-specific, explicit textual/structured, or latent/parametric. Hybrid systems combine these forms (§3; Figure 6).
- **Adaptation tiers:** Axis II distinguishes fixed memory (T1), changing memory under a stable policy (T2), and feedback-driven evolution of the management policy itself (T3). Updating retrieval metadata alone does not establish T3 (§§2.3, 4).
- **Corpus synthesis:** The authors report organizing 297 memory architectures and 82 benchmark records, and identify qualitative patterns involving dynamic updates, hybrid representations, and scarce deployment-time policy evolution (§1).
- **Design principles:** The synthesis emphasizes recoverable evidence, provenance, temporal validity, consistency across representations, and controlled forgetting (Abstract; §§1, 3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated during §4.2.1 and resumes in the bibliography. Most management analysis, cross-axis analysis, infrastructure, evaluation, and outlook content is unavailable.
- **Author-identified system risks:** Compression can irreversibly remove evidence; textualization can introduce unsupported interpretations; structured extraction errors can propagate; parameter updates can cause interference or forgetting (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Author-identified adaptation risk:** Changing memory or its management policy can introduce instability and make updates harder to control (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** The corpus-level patterns cannot be independently assessed from this excerpt because search procedures, selection criteria, annotation reliability, and detailed category counts are unavailable.
- **Analyst inference:** Different persistence horizons complicate comparison: memory retained within a reasoning trajectory and memory reused across sessions need separate evaluation (§3.3.1).

## Open research questions
- **When should an agent reopen source evidence?** Hybrid systems need to decide when abstractions are sufficient, but the excerpt supplies no validated decision rule (§3.4). A next experiment could compare fixed and learned reopening policies under matched storage and inference budgets.
- **Does evolving the management policy outperform updating memory alone?** The taxonomy distinguishes T2 from T3 without visible controlled evidence of their relative benefit (§4). A next experiment could hold the representation and backbone fixed while comparing stable and feedback-adapted policies under environmental change.
- **How can linked memories remain consistent after revision or deletion?** The authors identify cross-representation consistency as unresolved (§3.4). A next experiment could inject conflicting observations and deletion requests, then measure stale answers and broken evidence links.
- **What is lost when audio memory preserves transcripts instead of acoustic experience?** Persistent acoustic and latent audio memory is described as underexplored (§§3.1.2, 3.3.1). A next experiment could compare transcript-only and acoustic-preserving memory on delayed questions requiring speaker or prosodic evidence.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The framework and scope are explicit, but truncation prevents complete coverage or verification of the broader synthesis.

Material missing information includes corpus collection and annotation procedures, detailed distributions, benchmark protocols and metrics, controlled comparisons, full T3 analysis, infrastructure findings, and availability of reusable corpus assets.
