# τ-Emotion-Bench: Exposing the Interaction Fragility of LLM Agents through Real-World User Emotions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19560) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19560)


## One-sentence takeaway

τ-Emotion-Bench exposes inconsistent tool-agent performance with emotion-conditioned simulated users, while Tracer supervision improves small Qwen3 agents, although the specific causal benefit of emotion-aware filtering remains unisolated (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation

Task completion alone can reward agents that repeatedly request information available through tools or burden users with unnecessary clarification. The authors identify a gap in evaluating how emotional variation affects executable task completion while holding task objectives fixed, and in filtering training trajectories for interaction quality (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 580 manually reviewed tasks across ten executable domains, with emotion-conditioned users and corresponding versions without emotion conditioning. The taxonomy combines five emotion families, fine-grained emotions, politeness, urgency, and trust (§§3.3–3.4; Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Tracer pipeline:** Samples policy-valid tool workflows, grounds them in databases, generates user requests and emotional context, and verifies executability. It reuses existing environment logic rather than introducing new underlying environments (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Interaction-aware supervision:** Users may terminate unsatisfactory interactions; training retains trajectories that achieve the reference database state without dissatisfied termination. The resulting data supports supervised fine-tuning and transfer evaluations (§3.1; Appendix A.3; §4).

## Evidence and main results

Unless stated otherwise, results use the Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 user simulator. Evaluation uses three trials per task, simulator temperature 0, and a maximum of 200 interaction turns (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best aggregate performance still leaves substantial inconsistency | DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: 55.9% reported Avg@3 and 32.2% average Pass³ across ten domains. | §4.2; Table 3 |
| Simulator choice materially changes scores | With GLM-5.2 users, GLM-5.3 achieves 78.6% reported Avg@3 and 66.5% average Pass³. | §4.2; Table 14 |
| Tracer improves repeated success across model scales | Qwen3 base → Tracer-High average Pass³: 1.2% → 10.4% at 1.7B; 3.9% → 17.2% at 4B; 8.4% → 19.8% at 8B. | Table 3 |
| Unseen-domain gains persist | On six excluded domains, Tracer-Low-8B reaches 8.3% average Pass³, versus base 5.0% and EnvScaler 7.0%, using 1,498 Tracer trajectories. | Table 4; §4.2 |
| Transfer extends beyond the proposed benchmark | Authors report improvements over corresponding Qwen3 bases on τ-Bench Airline/Retail and τ-Trait Telehealth/Telecom; numerical figure values are absent from supplied text. | §4.2; Figure 3 |
| Most identified faults concern execution | Across analyzed failed trajectories, misread intent accounts for 15.9% of faults; emotional neglect remains below 1.7%. These are fault shares, not failure probabilities. | Appendix C.1 |
| Proposed training mechanism has correlational support | Tool calls exceed follow-ups in 76.2%/78.9% of Tracer-Low/High trajectories, versus 55.6% for AReaL and 22.0% for Simia; datasets differ in other construction details. | §5; Figure 8 |

The evidence supports training gains and persistent inconsistency under both simulators. It does **not** isolate how much failure is caused by emotion: paired unconditioned tasks are supplied, but corresponding comparative results are not reported in this text. Likewise, the behavioral associations do not establish that reducing follow-ups causes better performance.

**Metric ambiguity:** §4.1 defines Passᵏ as success in at least *k* of three trials, but Appendix C.4 and Tables 12–14 label average-success-like values as Pass¹. Consequently, Avg@3 and reported Pass¹ should not be treated as clearly distinct, consistently implemented metrics. Pass³ has a clearer interpretation: success in all three trials.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Tracer is tested only through supervised fine-tuning; reinforcement-learning use and extension to coding or web agents remain unexplored (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No matched ablation removes dissatisfied termination while holding generation and training data construction constant. The authors explicitly describe the training-pattern evidence as correlational (§5).
- **Analyst inference—human validity:** Two LLM simulators support robustness checks, but absolute scores differ substantially. The human trials test humans acting as tool agents with simulated users; they do not establish fidelity to real emotional users (Appendices C.3–C.4, E).
- **Analyst inference—uneven coverage:** Emotion coverage varies by domain; Basketball contains only one emotion family. Domain and emotion effects therefore cannot be assumed independently balanced (Table 2).
- **Reporting risks:** Appendix B.1 ambiguously describes fine-tuning with Tracer data “together with” AReaL data, leaving the training mixture unclear. Simulator-error reporting also mixes confirmed user-error counts with an insufficiently explained failed-trial percentage (§5).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does emotion itself change success?** Paired unconditioned tasks exist, but comparative results are missing. Run matched task-level evaluations with and without emotion, holding decoding and termination rules fixed.
2. **Does dissatisfaction filtering improve training beyond ordinary success filtering?** Existing comparisons change multiple dataset properties. Generate matched trajectory pools with termination enabled or disabled, then fine-tune on equal-sized successful subsets.
3. **Do simulator results predict real-user outcomes?** Human-agent trials establish selected-task solvability, not user-simulation fidelity. Evaluate the same tasks with human users and compare abandonment, clarification burden, and completion.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Benchmark construction and Pass³ improvements are well documented, but causal and real-user conclusions remain limited.

Material unavailable or unclear information includes exact prompt and policy bodies despite appendix headings, numerical cross-benchmark figure results, paired emotion/no-emotion outcomes, uncertainty estimates, complete domain-specific decoding assignments, the precise training-data mixture, and consistent definitions for reported Pass¹ values.
