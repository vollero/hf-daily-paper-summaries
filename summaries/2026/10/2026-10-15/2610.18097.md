# APT: Feedback-Driven Agentic Post-Training for Text-to-Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18097) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18097)


## One-sentence takeaway

APT automates text-to-image post-training through repeated diagnosis and adaptive RL prompt generation, with reported improvements across models but incomplete numerical evidence and observable diversity trade-offs.

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that manually curated, fixed post-training datasets are labor-intensive and poorly matched to models with different—and changing—weaknesses. APT addresses the resulting curriculum-selection problem: deciding what the current generator should learn next, alongside optimizing its parameters (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Feedback-driven post-training:** Three agents construct an evaluation suite, diagnose current failures, and generate targeted training prompts; diagnosis repeats after each RL round (§§2–2.3).
- **General-purpose evaluation harness:** A hierarchical scenario taxonomy produces a fixed suite of 2,609 prompt–rubric pairs when users provide no specific evaluation scope (§2.1).
- **Model-specific curriculum generation:** GPT-5.4 generates prompts primarily targeting weak capabilities while retaining moderate and strong capabilities. GPT-4.1-mini generates rubrics and supplies binary criterion scores, averaged into the RL reward (§§2–2.3).
- **Cross-model implementation:** The framework uses DiffusionNFT for diffusion generators and GRPO for Janus-Pro. The contribution is the adaptive task-construction workflow; these optimization algorithms are existing components (§§3.1, 3.3).

The visible results support useful capability improvements and some benefits from adaptive feedback. They also show that advantages depend on the metric. Most aggregate tables contain captions without values, so the reported overall rankings and improvement magnitudes cannot be independently checked from this text. The evidence supports an adaptive curriculum mechanism; it does not establish indefinite recursive improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated trade-off:** RL post-training can substantially reduce generation diversity, sometimes offsetting gains in other OneIG-EN dimensions (Appendix C).
- **Incomplete retrieved evidence:** Main comparison tables, several ablations, and referenced instruction prompts lack substantive contents. This prevents checking aggregate rankings and fully reconstructing agent behavior.
- **Analyst inference—correlated evaluator errors:** Diagnosis and training rewards share GPT-4.1-mini. Its errors could influence both curriculum selection and optimization; evaluator agreement with humans is not reported (§2).
- **Analyst inference—limited generality evidence:** Architecture transfer covers Janus-Pro, while feedback and sampling ablations use Lens. Broad architecture independence is therefore more expansive than the demonstrated coverage (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and cost:** The supplied text provides no repeated-run uncertainty estimates or end-to-end compute/API costs. Matching training prompt counts does not establish matching total resource expenditure (§3.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Do improvements survive independent human evaluation?** Shared diagnosis and reward scoring leave evaluator bias unresolved. Compare held-out human judgments with rubric rewards and an independent evaluator.
2. **Can adaptive training preserve diversity?** Appendix C identifies diversity degradation. Test diversity-aware rewards or curriculum mixtures while measuring both capability scores and OneIG-EN diversity.
3. **How much diagnostic coverage is necessary?** The suite is fixed and sampled each round (§§2.1–2.2). Vary suite coverage and diagnostic batch size under matched total budgets.
4. **Does feedback remain useful over longer training?** Reported experiments use finite budgets (§3.1). Extend training and track held-out gains, diversity, and failure recurrence against a frozen-diagnosis baseline.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The mechanism is clearly described, and several detailed tables support qualified findings. Missing information includes most aggregate numerical results, uncertainty estimates, full agent prompts, human evaluator validation, resource costs, tested alternative LLM identities/results, detailed Janus-Pro settings, and code/data/model release availability.
