# StreamErase: Towards Ultra-Fast Streaming Video Erasing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20137) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20137)


## One-sentence takeaway
StreamErase distills a bidirectional video eraser into a one-step causal model, reporting 26.06 FPS and 0.87-second latency on one H200 GPU, with long-video stability evaluated through 30 seconds (§4; Tables 2, 5).

## Problem and motivation
Video object removal must reconstruct backgrounds while removing associated effects such as shadows and reflections. The authors identify three barriers to interactive use: slow diffusion inference, bidirectional processing that requires future frames, and limited sequence lengths (§1). Their goal is continuous erasing with low latency and stable quality as videos extend beyond the training horizon.

## Approach and contributions
- **Effect-aware erasing model:** Adapt Wan2.1 with video and mask conditioning. Multimodal Region Guidance maps foreground image features into prompt-token space, while an auxiliary loss supervises cross-attention using differences between paired object-containing and background videos (§3.1).
- **Progressive streaming distillation:** Train a bidirectional teacher, initialize a four-step causal student through teacher forcing and ODE distillation, then apply Distribution Matching Distillation for one-step generation. The final model conditions on source video, masks, and noise rather than explicitly feeding previous generated latents back as inputs; temporal information still passes through its KV cache (§3.2).
- **Bounded streaming context:** Preserve initial sink tokens alongside a rolling local KV window, simulating this mechanism during training. The default uses three latent frames per chunk, one sink chunk, and one local-window chunk (§§3.3, 4.3; Appendix A).
- **LongErase benchmark:** Introduce 80 real-world, 30-second video sequences with ground-truth counterparts for long-video removal evaluation (§4). Public release of the benchmark, weights, or code is not established by the supplied text.

These results support a strong measured efficiency–quality tradeoff and improved stability within the evaluated duration. They do not establish unlimited-duration quality, uniform superiority on real-world fidelity, or superiority over competing methods on full-length LongErase videos.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fixed historical context can impede adaptation to substantial scene changes; training-data diversity and duration remain limited; explicit masks add interaction cost (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference — duration:** Quantitative long-video evaluation reaches 30 seconds. Claims of indefinite streaming describe architectural capability beyond the demonstrated quality horizon (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference — comparison:** Table 2 lacks competing 30-second results. Baseline timing procedures and the scope of latency measurement are insufficiently specified to assess complete end-to-end interaction costs.
- **Analyst inference — mechanism attribution:** Stage 3 changes both distillation and dependence on previous generated latents; Table 3 does not isolate their individual contributions.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Chunks are sometimes described as frames and elsewhere explicitly as latent frames. Appendix B also describes four-step training despite Appendix A specifying one-step Stage 3 training.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text does not establish the loss weight λ, LongErase construction protocol, exact training-data filtering procedure, or artifact availability.

## Open research questions
- **How long does stability persist?** Evaluation stops at 30 seconds. Test substantially longer streams with time-resolved reconstruction, drift, memory, and throughput measurements.
- **Can context adapt to scene changes?** Appendix F identifies stale historical information as a limitation. Compare fixed sinks with scene-change-triggered cache resets or adaptive sink replacement.
- **Which Stage 3 change reduces drift most?** Existing stage comparisons combine several changes. Run a controlled ablation separating DMD from removal of explicit generated-latent conditioning.
- **How robust is interactive masking?** Explicit masks are required, but mask-error robustness is unreported. Evaluate delayed, noisy, and changing masks while measuring removal quality and response latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported tables support the central measured findings, but indefinite-duration and broad deployment claims exceed the supplied evaluation.

Material missing information includes artifact release status, benchmark construction details, complete timing methodology, mask preparation costs, several reproducibility settings, and quantitative performance beyond 30 seconds or on major scene transitions.
