# Towards Agentic Robots: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20442) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20442)


## One-sentence takeaway

This survey organizes agentic robotics into four functional domains and seventeen overlapping categories, emphasizing how agents coordinate robot capabilities through observations and execution feedback rather than establishing a new controller’s performance (§3; Table 1).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that better action prediction alone does not ensure reliable long-horizon execution: robots must track progress, revise plans, and recover from failures. Research addressing these functions is fragmented across learning, data acquisition, environment construction, and runtime orchestration, with inconsistent terminology obscuring how components interact (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Definition and taxonomy:** Agentic robots autonomously organize and revise goal-directed execution using observations and feedback. The taxonomy classifies functional contributions, allowing a system to occupy multiple categories (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Literature synthesis:** Four domains cover Model—learning and decisions; Data—collection and curation; Environment—worlds, tasks, and tests; and Harness—capability orchestration and reuse. Table 1 defines seventeen categories.
- **Research agenda:** The authors propose attention to fair evaluation, practical deployment, efficiency, and generalization across robots (§1, Contributions). The detailed outlook is missing from the truncated source.
- **Companion resource:** The opening material links an “Awesome-Agentic-Robots” repository; its contents and availability are not established by the supplied text.

The visible contribution is a survey framework and synthesis; no new trained model, dataset, or experimental benchmark is established.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Truncated coverage:** The source stops during Table 8 and resumes in the bibliography. Detailed treatment of refinement, Environment, Harness, and the outlook is unavailable; this analysis cannot represent complete coverage.
- **Authors’ cautions about reviewed methods:** Some evaluations use oracle instance masks and IDs, replay rather than closed-loop physical trials, or ground-truth simulator states (§4.2; §4.4). These limit what the corresponding examples establish about deployment.
- **Authors’ evaluation concern:** Within-episode recovery, cross-task generalization, and improvement after retraining should be distinguished, including whether previous capabilities are retained (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—survey reproducibility:** The visible text does not establish search procedures, inclusion criteria, or independent taxonomy-labeling agreement. Literature completeness and classification reproducibility therefore remain uncertain.
- **Analyst observation—citation integrity:** Some introductory references conflict with the visible bibliography: reference [465] is attached to GPT-6 Astra but lists HoloAgent-0, and [466] similarly points to a different work. This weakens traceability of the introductory claims.

## Open research questions

- **How much improvement comes from orchestration itself?** The excerpt emphasizes dependencies on models, tools, and executors without controlled attribution (§1; §4.4). A next experiment could hold the policy and tool access fixed while varying planning, monitoring, and recovery.
- **When should robots refresh spatial evidence?** The survey identifies stale grounding as a shared vulnerability (§4.2). Compare fixed observation schedules with uncertainty-triggered re-observation under controlled object movement, measuring success and observation cost.
- **Does adaptation produce durable improvement?** Context retention and parameter learning can yield different forms of improvement (§4.5). Evaluate both on held-out tasks after clearing episode context, then test retention of earlier skills.

## Confidence and missing information
