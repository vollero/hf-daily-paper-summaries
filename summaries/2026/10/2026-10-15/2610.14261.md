# BigCell: Generating Gigapixel Whole-Slide Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14261) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14261)


## One-sentence takeaway
BigCell combines embedding-conditioned flow matching with sliding-window inference to synthesize reference-conditioned histopathology images up to 32,768 × 32,768 pixels and text-conditioned images at 8192 × 8192, with improved reference fidelity and reported few-shot augmentation gains (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Histopathology generation typically produces small tiles, whereas diagnoses depend on tissue organization across large regions and available supervision often consists of slide- or patient-level reports. Independently realistic tiles can therefore miss diagnostic structure, consistent staining, and relationships between distant regions. The authors target this mismatch between patch-scale generation and whole-slide supervision (§1; Appendix A).

The novelty claim needs qualification: the paper acknowledges prior gigapixel synthesis through thumbnail generation and successive super-resolution. BigCell’s claimed distinction is generation at native resolution through a single denoising stage over the full latent canvas, coordinated by dense embeddings (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Whole-slide synthesis pipeline.** An SiT flow-matching tile generator, progressively trained through 2048 × 2048 resolution on 120,703 public WSIs, uses dense UNI-2 embeddings as a spatial blueprint. Overlapping window velocities are averaged to update a shared latent canvas, enabling reference-conditioned generation beyond training resolution (§§3.1–3.2, 4.1; Appendix C).
- **Adaptive inference.** Conditional–unconditional velocity differences rank windows by importance. Each step recomputes selected windows and reuses cached velocities elsewhere, reducing generation cost without retraining (§3.3).
- **Report-conditioned embedding generation.** A separate conditioning generation model (CGM) jointly samples an embedding grid from TITAN-encoded pathology text; BigCell renders that grid into an 8k image (§3.4).
- **Synthetic augmentation.** CGM partially noises and regenerates real training embeddings under class-specific reports, producing variations evaluated in few-shot classification (§3.5).

Sliding-window generation and representation conditioning build on prior work; the contributions concern their scaling, flow-matching implementation, adaptive scheduling, and report-to-grid pipeline (§§2–3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Higher reference fidelity at 8k | On 100 held-out ROIs, BigCell versus PixCell: TITAN cosine similarity **0.956 vs 0.941**, MOOZY **0.973 vs 0.900**, and Virchow-2 bottom-5th-percentile patch similarity **0.737 vs 0.632**. | Gains persist in matched comparisons | At identical 1024 native resolution and inference, BigCell versus PixCell: TITAN **0.952 vs 0.941**, MOOZY **0.971 vs 0.900**. | Stronger automated report agreement | On the same 100 8k ROIs, MedGemma-generated reports agree between synthetic/reference pairs on organ in **69% vs 63%** and diagnosis in **46% vs 42%**, BigCell versus PixCell. | §5.1; Table 2 referenced |
| Faster 32k synthesis | Across 256 held-out slides, adaptive scheduling achieves TITAN similarity **0.951** at approximately **11,000 NFEs**, versus **0.957** at **38,440 NFEs**. At **r = 0.25**, runtime falls from **15.6 h to 4.97 h**, a reported **3.1×** speedup on one Intel GPU Max 1550. | §5.2; Figure 3 |
| Text alignment approaches paired real data | On 150 held-out reports, generated versus real-paired similarity: TITAN **0.487 vs 0.551**, PRISM **0.454 vs 0.499**. TITAN report-to-image recall@5 is **0.655** for both; recall@1 is **0.169 vs 0.211**. | §5.3; Table 3 referenced |
| Limited expert study supports report matching and visual realism | Three pathologists each completed 100 trials, split evenly between real and synthetic 8k images. Report-matching accuracy was **0.57** for both. Real/synthetic detection accuracy was **55.3%**, with **95% CI 50–61%**; the reported permutation test was nonsignificant. | §§4.3, 5.3; Figure 4 |
| Few-shot augmentation reportedly improves performance | With **k ∈ {1, 2, 4}**, adding **k synthetic** examples per class to **k real** examples improves both datasets across shot counts, with reported maximum relative gains of **18% on PANDA** and **7% on TCGA-UT-8K**. Evaluation uses frozen TITAN features and logistic regression over ten seeds; exact Table 4 scores are unavailable. | §§4.3, 5.3; Table 4 caption |

These results support improved **conditioning fidelity**, useful compute allocation, and promising augmentation within the evaluated settings. They do not establish clinical equivalence or universal superiority: LRDM and ZoomLDM obtain better Fréchet distances, which the authors distinguish from paired conditioning agreement (§5.1). The expert study’s nonsignificant detection result does not prove indistinguishability, and comparable report matching does not establish preservation of every diagnostic feature.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Text generation stops at 8k because larger paired regions are scarce; 32k synthesis remains too slow for high-throughput use; outputs are intended for research rather than diagnostic substitution (§6). Public training-data biases may propagate into generated images (Appendix H).
- **Comparison scope:** Main baseline results combine differences in architecture, training data, and native resolution. Appendix E.2 supplies useful matched controls, but the proposed conditioning bottleneck explanation remains an author hypothesis.
- **Analyst inference—clinical validity:** Foundation-model similarity and MedGemma agreement are proxies. Expert evaluation involves only three research-team pathologists and report matching with randomly sampled distractors, limiting evidence for subtle diagnostic distinctions (§§4.3, 5.3; checklist item 15).
- **Analyst inference—text fidelity:** Report summarization explicitly excludes negative findings. This creates a plausible loss of clinically relevant conditioning information; the paper does not isolate its effect (Appendix G.1).
- **Analyst inference—downstream scope:** Augmentation evidence covers two datasets and frozen-feature logistic regression. It does not establish gains for broader clinical tasks or end-to-end model training (§4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can report conditioning scale to 32k?** The current CGM is limited by paired-data availability (§§4.1, 6). A next experiment could compare hierarchical and jointly generated conditioning grids under a fixed paired-data budget.
2. **Does adaptive scheduling preserve small diagnostic findings?** Aggregate fidelity improves, but lesion-specific preservation is not reported (§5.2; Appendix E.3). Test matched-compute schedules on expert-annotated focal lesions and tissue interfaces.
3. **Can text control subtle diagnostic alternatives reliably?** Report matching uses random distractors rather than systematically chosen near-diagnosis alternatives (§4.3). Evaluate minimally different prompts with blinded expert assessment of the requested feature.
4. **How much does report preprocessing affect fidelity?** Summaries omit negative findings without a reported ablation (Appendix G.1). Compare full reports, current summaries, and summaries retaining negatives on expert-scored feature preservation.
