# CoTranslator: Reverse to Forward Chain-of-Thought Translator for Reasoning Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20828) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20828)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoTranslator learns to rewrite answer-conditioned reasoning into verified forward-style reasoning, with reported student accuracy gains over semantic suppression and lower estimated data-construction compute than rejection sampling (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Reasoning distillation needs useful, correct teacher explanations. Question-only forward generation followed by rejection sampling (RS) can require repeated teacher calls; reverse generation uses known answers to reduce sampling cost but risks explanations that justify the answer through shortcuts. The authors identify a gap in methods that suppress answer mentions or guide response structure without explicitly learning the mapping to verified forward responses (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Paired trajectory translation:** Train a translator on reverse responses and verified forward responses for the same question. Its only input is the reverse response; the question and answer are excluded as separate inputs (§2.2).
- **Scalable distillation pipeline:** The main translator, initialized from Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, learns from approximately 35.7k pairs and translates reverse responses for approximately 285.5k student-training questions. At generation time, each question uses one reverse teacher call and one translator call (§§2.2, 3.1).
- **Conditional theoretical analysis:** The recovery bound separates model-fitting error from task information missing in the source response. Further bounds account for decoding and question coverage, under stated population assumptions (Appendix C).
- **Empirical transfer and diagnostics:** The authors test transfer without translator retraining and examine response similarity, answer-dependent shortcuts, translator size, and estimated compute (§§3.3–4.4).

| New-dataset transfer | With the translator unchanged, DeepSeek-V4-Pro teaching on ODA-Math-460k yields average math accuracy gains over SUP of **4.76/1.66 points** for Qwen3-4B-Base/8B-Base. | §3.3; Table 2 referenced |

| STEM transfer | Training students on Nemotron-SFT-Science-v2 responses yields average accuracy gains over SUP of **4.88/4.31 points** for 4B/8B students across OlympiadBench-STEM, SciBench, and AA-Omniscience. | §3.3; Table 3 referenced |

| Responses become more RS-like | Authors report higher MAUVE/cosine similarity, lower MMD, and the lowest median question-conditioned teacher NLL across difficulty groups. | §4.1, Figures 3/8; Appendix D.2 |

The evidence supports improved downstream learning relative to SUP in the tested settings and a favorable **estimated** compute tradeoff. It does not establish exact distribution recovery, causal faithfulness, or measured runtime savings. The 95.0% figure is relative accuracy retention, not absolute benchmark accuracy. Missing table bodies prevent checking absolute scores and benchmark-level variation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments cover students up to 8B and supervised fine-tuning; larger models, reinforcement learning, and on-policy distillation remain untested (Limitations).
- **Theoretical conditions:** Recovery depends on source information and population fitting. Small training loss alone does not establish unseen-pair recovery. The student-risk bound applies to bounded losses, not the raw unbounded training NLL (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—faithfulness:** Similarity metrics, automated annotations, and later answer mentions provide indirect evidence; they cannot establish that explanations causally drive answers. The authors also explicitly distinguish verified answers from causal faithfulness (§4.2; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—size confound:** The translator-size comparison also changes model variants and thinking-mode support, so it does not isolate parameter count (§4.3).
- **Reporting limits:** Full scores, uncertainty estimates, most training hyperparameters, prompts, and compute-budget tables are unavailable in the supplied rendering. Compute excludes Math-Verify verification (Appendices A, D–E).

## Open research questions

1. **Does translation remove dependence on supplied answers?** Current diagnostics are observational. A next experiment could perturb supplied answers and use blinded human assessment to test whether translated derivations preserve valid reasoning (§4.2).
2. **How much question information must survive in the reverse response?** The theoretical bound includes missing-source information, but it is not empirically quantified. Test controlled removal or corruption of task details before translation (Appendix C.2).
3. **Does the advantage extend beyond the evaluated training regime?** Larger students and RL/on-policy distillation remain open. Compare translation, SUP, and RS under matched construction budgets in those settings (Limitations).
4. **What are practical end-to-end savings?** Reported costs are estimates excluding verification. Measure hardware time, verification overhead, and cost per accepted example across problem difficulty (§4.4; Appendix D.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported aggregate findings, but omits numerical table bodies, detailed figure values, most training settings, full prompts, uncertainty estimates, and detailed compute budgets. Post-translation filtering and retained-example counts are also insufficiently specified to reconcile the practical pipeline with the filtered theoretical analysis.
