# Training LLMs for Multi-Agent Collaboration with Verifiable Information Rewards

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17893) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17893)


## One-sentence takeaway
CoSIR trains information sharing, requesting, and use through verifiable synthetic rewards, achieving the highest aggregate transfer score across seven collaboration tasks for three model backbones, without winning every individual comparison (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Agents with distributed evidence can fail to share useful facts, request clarification, or incorporate teammates’ input. Final task outcomes provide weak feedback about which messages helped. The authors address this credit-assignment gap by training in an environment where information ownership, truth, and decision relevance are computable (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Verifiable training environment:** Teams receive partial rating tables and discuss which option has the highest total. Varying information allocations creates opportunities to share, request, and combine evidence (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Information-value rewards:** An exact Bayesian evaluator scores communication by reductions in team surprisal about the true winning option. Requests receive credit for hypothetical truthful replies; statement rewards also check support and truth. Separate private-answer rewards assess evidence-conditioned choices and known-rating calculations (§§3.2–3.3; Appendices C–E).
- **Self-play training and transfer evaluation:** A shared policy learns through token-level PPO-style updates, supplemented by request demonstrations and capability-retention objectives. The authors also introduce SplitQA, which distributes supporting paragraphs across agents and requires free-form answers (§3.4; Appendices F–G).

The evidence supports transfer within the tested suite and favors the complete CoSIR reward package over outcome-only feedback. It does not establish statistically significant gains for every task, identify which reward component causes the gains, or demonstrate that reduced evaluator surprisal necessarily improves actual agent decisions. Appendix C explicitly distinguishes evaluator probability changes from LLM accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers three backbones and seven tasks; competing methods remain stronger on some model–task combinations. Repetition and several other communication failures persist (Limitations; §4.3).
- **Author-stated deployment gap:** Rewards require known facts and a computable evaluator. Applying them directly to open-ended tasks with unavailable complete state remains unresolved (Limitations).
- **Evaluator qualifications:** Its prior omits generator-specific filtering, so its posterior is exact for the simplified evaluation model. Request scores assume truthful replies and universal receipt rather than realized communication; incorrect reports can affect private-answer targets (Appendices C–E).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No component-removal ablations are supplied for sharing, requesting, private-answer rewards, demonstrations, or retention objectives. Outcome-RL compares reward packages and credit assignment rather than isolating each component.
- **Analyst inference—measurement uncertainty:** MAST results use an LLM judge; disclosure uses a content-word overlap heuristic, and correction language does not establish successful correction. No human validation or uncertainty estimates for these behavioral measures are supplied (Appendices J–K).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** SUM weights heterogeneous tasks equally. ToM and TalkHier match public speaking turns but use extra model calls, so comparisons are not compute-matched (Appendix H).

## Open research questions
- **Which reward components drive transfer?** The supplied comparison does not separate their effects. Run matched training ablations removing sharing, requesting, and private-answer rewards individually.
- **Can information rewards work without complete ground truth?** The authors identify this as unresolved. Test an approximate evaluator on tasks with partially verifiable evidence, checking reward rankings against a fully known subset.
- **Does collaboration transfer to heterogeneous teammates?** Training uses one shared policy for all agents. Evaluate trained agents paired with different checkpoints or untrained teammates.
- **How robust is the method to unreliable reports?** The evaluator accepts reported values, and private-answer targets can retain false information. Introduce controlled false reports and measure recovery, truthfulness, and final accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and headline comparisons, but several tables appear only as captions.

Material missing information includes numerical contents of Tables 2–6 and 8, learning rates and full configuration tables, most benchmark sample counts, behavioral-analysis sample sizes and validation, component ablations, statistical significance tests, parser accuracy, and code/model/data release availability.
