# CLASHarness: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Conflict in Computer Use via a Controllable and Verifiable Generation Pipeline

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19128) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19128)


## One-sentence takeaway
CLASHarness generates executable computer-use conflict benchmarks whose reported results suggest that verifying contradictory evidence is easier for current agents than completing the operational actions and coordination protocols needed to resolve it (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Agents collaborating through shared files, permissions, and messages may encounter stale reports or contradictory instructions. The authors argue that existing evaluations largely assume consistent environments or restrict multi-agent interaction to text, leaving grounded conflict resolution poorly measured. They also identify benchmark-generation problems: manual authoring scales poorly, while unconstrained generation can leak answers or produce impossible grading requirements (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled scenario generation:** Structured seeds and modular plugins define roles, permissions, tools, and conflict mechanics. Hierarchical planning generates evolving workspaces through verified round-level steps, with deterministic repairs and bounded retries (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Grounded verification:** Decisive facts are committed before image rendering; leakage scans enforce visual exclusivity where required. Executed reference trajectories ground deterministic checks and scoped semantic criteria. The authors claim this ensures solvability by construction (§3.4).
- **Benchmark and diagnostics:** The benchmark contains 600 rounds in 60 packages across 20 scenario seeds, with visual evidence and hybrid graders. Evaluations separate evidence inspection, content correctness, operational completion, and timing compliance (§§4–5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| End-to-end completion remains low | At high effort over 600 rounds, GPT-6 Astra achieves 28.7% ± 1.9 SE pass rate and 66.8% ± 1.2 SE mean check score, using a 40-step budget. Claude Opus 5 achieves 25.2% ± 1.8 and 64.2% ± 1.2 at 20 steps. | Table 1; Table D1; §E.3 |

These results support operational completion and coordination as weaknesses within this benchmark. They do not establish equivalent failure rates in live teams or isolate the overall cost of introducing conflict, because no conflict-free baseline is included. The resolution-gate estimate is suggestive, but its exact-test p-value exceeds .05 despite the bootstrap interval excluding zero.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Semantic grading lacks human validation; peer interaction uses asynchronous logs and workspace traces; conflict-free baselines are absent (§7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** The main leaderboard combines different action budgets and includes text-only models receiving OCR transcripts. Scores therefore combine capability with interface and resource differences (Table D1).
- **Analyst inference—measurement scope:** A mandatory image-open predicate measures access, rather than proving comprehension; the separate transcription and semantic checks provide additional evidence (§B.7; Table G11).
- **Analyst inference—strength of conclusions:** Near-zero correlation between visual localization and semantic resolution does not alone establish independent capabilities (§C.3). Similarly, stable rankings across two generators support limited robustness rather than generator independence (§F.1).
- **Source limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, including part of the extended results, so this analysis cannot provide complete coverage.

## Open research questions

- **Do failures persist with live peers?** The evaluation uses traces rather than live conversation (§7). A next experiment could replay matched scenarios with interactive peers and controlled response delays.
- **How much difficulty comes specifically from conflict?** Conflict-free baselines are absent (§7). Compare matched workspaces with consistent versus contradictory peer reports while keeping required actions fixed.
- **Can explicit completion tracking improve coordination?** Closing actions remain frequent failure points (§5.2). Test a persistent obligation checklist against the same agent under matched action budgets.
- **Does semantic grading match human judgment?** Judge agreement is measured without human validation (§7). Have blinded human reviewers adjudicate sampled agreements and disagreements.
