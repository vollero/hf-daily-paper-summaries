# FreeSolo: Linear-time Sparse Attention with Zero-Shot Length Extrapolation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17191) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17191)


## One-sentence takeaway
FreeSolo combines local attention with sparse access to original token memory, demonstrating retrieval beyond its training length through either bounded-memory linear attention or a growing, subquadratic tree-search archive (Table 1; §3.5).

## Problem and motivation
Long-context models face a tradeoff between fixed-state memory capacity and the growing cost of retaining directly accessible tokens. The authors argue that existing sparse attention also struggles to generalize beyond training lengths, while longer-context continued pretraining adds cost (§1). Their goal is efficient distant retrieval using only short-context training.

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared memory representation:** FreeSolo combines local RoPE attention with long-range attention using unrotated queries and keys. Mean token keys route queries to cells; attention then reads those cells’ original tokens. The contribution combines existing mean-key routing with norm priorities and local-only RoPE (§§3.1–3.3, 6).
- **FS-L:** Each head retains recent cells and cells containing the highest-norm keys before query-dependent selection. Short continued pretraining (CPT) adapts the model to pruning, yielding linear attention work and bounded token state at fixed budgets (§§3.3, 3.5).
- **FS-B:** Two content forests preserve access to a growing archive, using approximate beam search and token-level reranking. Algorithmic sequence cost is \(O(N\log^2N)\), including construction; the reference implementation has additional copying overhead (§3.4; Appendix E.2).
- **Short-context adaptation:** All training remains at 4096 tokens. At the larger scale, FS-B uses a retrieval-weighted next-token objective derived from repeated spans in natural text; forests operate only at inference (§3.4; Appendix E.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Stronger suite retrieval at extrapolated length | At approximately 800 M scale and 16 k context, RULER means are FS-B **30.4**, FS-L **22.3**, FS **23.1**, Raven **12.8**, and NAtS-L **2.8**. | Gains on harder single-needle tasks | At approximately 800 M/16 k, S-NIAH-2/3 accuracy is **88/84** for FS-B, **81/40** for FS-L, and **26/0** for Raven, under the same RULER protocol. | Table 1 |

| Short-context quality remains broadly comparable | At approximately 800 M, the eight-task zero-shot average is **56.5** for FS, **55.2** for FS-L, and **56.8** for TF++; task-specific accuracy metrics are averaged arithmetically. | §4.3 |
| Bounded FS-L storage is demonstrated | Across 4 k–256 k on one A100-40GB with bf16 parameters, attention buffers remain **766 MiB** at 340 M and **1149 MiB** at 800 M; CUDA-graph decode is about **89/75 tokens/s**, respectively. | §4.4; Appendix C |
| Linear scaling carries substantial implementation cost | At 340 M/4 k on eight A100-40GB GPUs, training throughput is **98.4 K tokens/s** for FreeSolo versus **303 K** for full attention. | §4.4; Appendix C |
| Dense fact retrieval remains weak | In the synthetic multiple-question test, FS, FS-L, and FS-B exceed the authors’ **90%** threshold for one fact at both scales, but no tested model exceeds it with two or more facts; each condition uses 50 prefixes. | Appendix D.5; Table 16 |

The evidence supports retrieval extrapolation under the reported benchmark settings and bounded storage for streaming FS-L. It does not establish broadly reliable long-context reasoning or dense factual recall. Comparisons across lengths also change task composition: suite means include QA-2 only at longer lengths (§4.1). Main-table baselines lack FreeSolo’s additional CPT, although selected continuation controls partially address this confound (Appendix D.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Experiments stop at the approximately 800 M scale. FS-L can permanently discard evidence, and its suite mean remains below base FS in the reported continuation comparison (§7; Appendix D.4).
- **Author-stated:** FS-B retains growing memory and trails Raven-full on larger-scale multi-query and multi-value retrieval (§7). Search is approximate, and reference cache copying can accumulate quadratic memory traffic despite the subquadratic algorithmic bound (Appendix E.2).
- **Author-stated:** Harder-task changes vary across CPT seeds and also occur without pruning. The local-position ablation changes both rotation and bias, preventing clean attribution to RoPE alone (Appendices D.1, D.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Component causality remains only partly isolated: larger-scale FS-B combines weighted CPT with inference changes, and two forests do not consistently beat a candidate-budget-matched single forest across scales (Appendices E.3–E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Precision and execution differences complicate direct quality–speed comparisons: main FS-L accuracy uses FP32 parameters with bf16 autocast, whereas bounded-cache timings use bf16 parameters (Appendices A, C).

## Open research questions
- **Can bounded-memory CPT improve overall retrieval rather than mainly recover pruning losses?** FS-L remains below base FS on suite means. A next experiment could compare plain and retrieval-weighted CPT under identical retention budgets, token budgets, and multiple seeds (§7).
- **Can learned search improve dense fact retrieval?** FS-B uses fixed inference search and still struggles with multi-query/value tasks. Compare learned routing against fixed forests on shared checkpoints and matched search budgets (§7; Appendix D.5).
- **Do the benefits persist at larger scales and on natural long-document tasks?** Experiments stop below one billion parameters and emphasize RULER. Train larger matched models and evaluate document question answering with controlled evidence density (§§4.1, 7).
- **Does an optimized FS-B implementation realize its algorithmic scaling?** Reference copying introduces work outside the stated bound. Implement persistent chunk storage and geometric buffer growth, then measure construction, prefill, and decode separately (Appendix E.2).
