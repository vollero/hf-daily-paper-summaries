# LensErase: A Foundation Model for Erase Anything

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18072) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18072)


## One-sentence takeaway
LensErase combines large-scale removal supervision, mask-aware generative modeling, contract-guided reinforcement learning, and selective source restoration to improve image erasing across diverse benchmarks, although several comparison tables are missing from the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation
Mask-based image erasing must remove selected targets and their associated shadows or reflections, reconstruct plausible backgrounds, and preserve unrelated content. The authors identify a coverage gap across target sizes, text, translucent watermarks, multiple objects, and layered designs; they aim to handle these through one source-image–mask interface (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions
- **Data construction:** LensErase-100M contains 101.07 million source–mask–target triplets across general objects, watermarks, large masks, visual text, and posters. Pipelines reverse object additions, composite overlays and foregrounds, verify teacher text edits, and render layer deletions (§2.1; Appendix B).
- **Mask-conditioned model:** Both MMDiT branches initialize from Lens’s image branch. The intact source supplies visual context; proposed **Mask-Aware RoPE** encodes mask membership as an additional positional coordinate without separate mask tokens. Region-weighted flow matching emphasizes edited regions (§3.1; Figure 3).
- **Post-training and preservation:** LoRA SFT precedes DiffusionNFT optimization using candidate-independent contracts defining targets, associated effects, and protected content. Removal failures receive multiplicative reward penalties. **TraceFusion** estimates edit support from masks and residuals, then restores source pixels elsewhere (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Evaluation asset:** LensErase-Bench introduces 500 reference-free cases across four domains, judged for removal, inpainting, preservation, and cleanliness (§2.3; Appendix C). Public release of the proposed assets is not established.

HiFi Removal requires a perfect removal rating and at least minor-defect-level ratings on the other dimensions (§2.3). These results support improved reference agreement and VLM-assessed editing success under the reported protocols. They do not establish universal superiority: AnimeEraseBench DINO favors ObjectClear over LensErase, and the supplied text omits numerical baseline results for LensErase-Bench. The authors’ broader state-of-the-art claim therefore exceeds what can be independently checked here.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reliable large-scale 4K training data remain difficult to construct because editing teachers struggle at ultra-high resolutions (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** GPT-5.6 Sol supports data screening, RL rewards, and benchmark judging. Shared evaluator preferences could influence reported gains; independent human or alternative-judge validation is not supplied (§§2.2–2.3, 3.2).
- **Analyst inference—confounded ablation:** Removal scoring and its gate are disabled together, preventing attribution of their separate effects (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—reliability evidence:** Evaluation uses one fixed-seed output per case, so it does not directly quantify variability across stochastic samples, despite that motivating RL (Introduction; Appendix E.4).
- **Evidence availability:** Many tables retain only captions, including full baseline comparisons and reward weights; most baseline-configuration entries are empty. These omissions limit reproducibility assessment.

## Open research questions
1. **Do RL gains transfer to independent judgments?** Training and evaluation share a VLM family. Next experiment: blinded human assessment and alternative VLM judging of identical stage-wise outputs.
2. **What does the removal-aware gate contribute separately?** Its ablation also removes Removal scoring. Next experiment: independently toggle scoring and gating while holding training conditions fixed.
3. **How robust are erasing and TraceFusion to mask errors?** Hand-drawn-mask evidence is qualitative (Appendix J). Next experiment: controlled mask erosion, dilation, and instance omissions, measuring removal and preservation.
4. **Does performance persist at 4K?** The authors identify a data-construction bottleneck, while LensErase-Bench stays below that resolution. Next experiment: evaluate native 4K cases with detailed human inspection of reconstruction and effect removal.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and selected numerical findings, but lacks many table bodies, complete baseline settings, independent judge validation, uncertainty estimates, multi-seed reliability results, inference latency, and explicit model/data/code release details.
