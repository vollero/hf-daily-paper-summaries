# From Failures to Learnable Worlds: Policy-Conditioned Environment Generation for Navigation Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19420) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19420)


## One-sentence takeaway
Wedge turns validated, policy-specific failure patterns into targeted navigation training environments, improving reported ObjectNav performance across three agents and transferring some gains from ProcTHOR to HM3D (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Generic environment generation emphasizes realism, diversity, or predefined tasks, but agents solving the same task can struggle under different conditions. The authors argue that continued training should target the current policy’s weaknesses while avoiding environments that are already mastered or too difficult to learn from (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable weakness discovery:** An LLM contrasts successful and failed rollouts represented through simulator measurements, proposes Python feature definitions, and validates them for coverage, non-redundancy, predictive power, and incremental value (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Weakness-conditioned construction:** The strongest validated weakness determines target placement, starting-state sampling, or layout generation. Candidates must fall within a diagnosed failure regime; rollout calibration then selects intermediate difficulty (§3.3).
- **Adaptation across learning mechanisms:** Selected experience supports continued RL for DD-PPO, successful-trajectory fine-tuning for Uni-NaVid, and in-context demonstrations for Gemini. Re-diagnosis can update the generation target after adaptation (§3.4).

The distinguishing contribution is the loop from discovered environmental failure conditions to controllable training environments, rather than a new policy-learning objective (§§2–3).

## Evidence and main results

Numbers below are author-reported; SR denotes success rate and SPL denotes success weighted by path length.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Different policies have different failure profiles | On ProcTHOR diagnostics, DD-PPO’s top feature is target visibility, with a 28.5-point SR gap; Uni-NaVid and Gemini have blind-path-ratio gaps of 37.3 and 37.6 points. | §4.2; Table 4; gap definition in Appendix A.1 |
| DD-PPO improves after targeted continued RL | On 1,550 ProcTHOR evaluation episodes, pretrained → Wedge: SR 82.1 → 85.6; SPL 58.2 → 59.8. Adaptation uses 668 selected environments and 10M environment steps; random/hard controls are described as yielding little improvement. | §§4.1–4.3 |
| Uni-NaVid benefits despite adaptation-domain shift | On 655 ProcTHOR episodes, pretrained → Wedge: SR 51.6 → 55.7; SPL 35.3 → 38.5. Matched random self-rollout adaptation reaches 46.6 SR; training uses 2,480 successful trajectories. | §§4.1–4.3 |
| Gemini benefits from targeted in-context experience | On 300 ProcTHOR episodes, SR is 41.0 pretrained, 40.7 with random examples, 41.8 with hard examples, and 43.7 with Wedge; matched adaptation uses one worked example. | §§4.1–4.3 |
| Uni-NaVid transfers gains to HM3D | After ProcTHOR adaptation, on 1,000 HM3D episodes: Wedge SR 73.4 versus pretrained 69.2, random 67.1, and hard-mined 69.0. Wedge/pretrained SPL is 28.6/28.5. | §§4.1, 4.3 |
| Gemini also improves on HM3D | On 300 HM3D episodes after ProcTHOR adaptation, Wedge achieves 63.5 SR and 29.1 SPL; random and hard-example SR are 58.0 and 60.9. | §§4.1, 4.3 |
| Calibration helps beyond weakness matching | DD-PPO’s calibrated subset contains 16% of weakness-matched environments and achieves 85.4 ProcTHOR SR versus 84.4 using the full set. | §4.4; Table 2 discussion |
| Guided search reduces candidate evaluation in the reported comparison | Obtaining the same number of hard target placements requires approximately 4.2k candidates/21k rollouts with guidance versus 8.4k/42k for unguided hard mining. | §4.4; Appendix A.2.1 |

These results support targeted adaptation for the tested agents and settings. HM3D evidence demonstrates simulator-domain transfer, not real-world transfer. Uni-NaVid’s higher HM3D SR accompanies essentially unchanged SPL, so improved success does not establish better path efficiency (§4.3). Complete numerical comparisons cannot be checked because Tables 1–3 have captions but no bodies in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers indoor ObjectNav, with ProcTHOR generation and HM3D transfer; additional tasks, simulators, and real-world settings remain untested (Appendix A.4).
- **Author-stated requirements:** The method assumes measurable simulator primitives and controllable environmental factors, and targets only one weakness per round (Appendix A.4).
- **Author-stated comparison constraint:** Uni-NaVid’s original HM3D training data are unavailable; its pretrained model serves as a reference rather than a matched continued-training control (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Validated success associations need not identify causal failure mechanisms. Environment interventions support practical usefulness, but do not isolate every correlated factor (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** The rollout-selection rule requires two or three successes in five trials, while Gemini’s appendix describes one interaction per practice episode; its application of calibration is unclear (§3.3; Appendix A.2.2).
- **Reporting limits:** Aggregate results lack reported uncertainty or repeated-run variation in the supplied text. The feature-proposal prompt section is empty, and the diagnostic-set size and discovery stopping criterion are unspecified.

## Open research questions
- **Would joint weakness targeting improve adaptation?** Only the top weakness is targeted per round. Compare single-feature generation with joint targeting under matched data and rollout budgets (Appendix A.4).
- **How much does automated feature discovery contribute?** The supplied ablations address generation and calibration, not LLM discovery versus fixed features. Compare discovered features with individual primitives and predefined combinations using identical downstream selection.
- **Does the method transfer beyond simulator instrumentation?** Simulator measurements and controls are explicit requirements. Test diagnosis using estimated geometry and visibility, then evaluate adaptation in another simulator or real-world navigation (Appendix A.4).
- **How reliable is calibration from few trials?** Selection relies on five rollouts per candidate. Vary the verification budget and measure selection stability, adaptation performance, and total cost (§3.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and main numerical claims are explicit, but complete Tables 1–3, plotted values, proposal prompts, diagnostic-set sizes, stopping rules, repeated-run uncertainty, and release information are unavailable. The relationship between main results and individual adaptation rounds, and Gemini’s calibration procedure, also remains partly unclear.
