# Looking Complete Is Not Being Grounded: Evaluating LLM Sitreps Against Human-Authored Situation Reports

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18762) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18762)


## One-sentence takeaway
Social-media-derived LLM situation reports can match professional reports in topic coverage under one judge while recovering little of their official numerical record, showing why completeness, grounding, and source availability need separate evaluation.

## Problem and motivation
Humanitarian situation reports must convey current needs, response actions, figures, and issuing authority. Fluent text or populated headings can conceal missing operational evidence. The authors identify a gap in evaluations based on reference similarity or generic quality ratings: these do not establish whether reports contain practice-relevant information or whether their inputs could supply it (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
1. **Practice-derived schema:** LLM open coding, embedding-based clustering, and author adjudication produce 15 information slots from professional Idai reports. The contribution is an auditable, practice-oriented schema, with acknowledged model and prompt influence (§4).
2. **Human-relative completeness and source audit:** Reports receive absent/partial/present slot scores, compared with an empirical professional-report mean. Separate numeric matching and manual audits examine official-figure recovery and input visibility (§5).
3. **Metric meta-evaluation:** ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and a schema-blind LLM quality judge are compared with operational completeness across three primary disasters (§§5–6).

The evaluation covers 121 professional and 261 generated reports across five disasters. Generation crosses two DeepSeek models with generic and schema-guided prompts; four judge families evaluate Idai, and two evaluate the other events (Abstract; §§3, 5).

The evidence supports a distinction between measured slot coverage and quantitative grounding in this corpus. It does **not** establish machine superiority, operational usefulness, or a ceiling for social media generally. Numeric absence from an official report also does not establish that a machine claim is false. The “human ceiling” is an empirical mean, not a maximum or comprehensive quality standard (§5; Limitations).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated schema bias:** Idai supplies both schema induction and its human comparison set. OCHA dominates induction; seeded examples, truncated coding inputs, and candidate filtering shape the categories. Protection, logistics, and hedging are incompletely represented (§4; Limitations).
- **Author-stated judge limits:** One annotator, also the schema approver, validates only Idai. Cross-event correctness remains unvalidated, missing verdicts are uneven, and the acceptance statistic was inconsistently documented (§5; Limitations).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** English sampled tweets, one generator family, small supplementary corpora, and mixed professional document genres restrict generalization. No deployment, practitioner-use, or early-arrival benefit study was conducted (§7; Limitations).
- **Author-stated measurement and reproducibility limits:** Regex collisions are substantial for official matches; long-reference truncation depresses scores. Mutable model aliases and withheld output text prevent guaranteed regeneration, although text-free artifacts support numerical auditing (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Completeness is a useful coverage diagnostic, but its equal-weight slot average cannot by itself determine operational adequacy; the study does not validate slot weights against practitioner decisions (§5; §7).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the schema transfer to independent reporting practice?** It is Idai-derived and lacks independent human coding. Next experiment: have multiple practitioners code held-out events and compare categories, agreement, and resulting system rankings.
2. **Can additional official inputs improve grounding?** The authors propose connecting drafting to official information channels but do not test it. Next experiment: compare social-only and social-plus-official inputs using semantically verified figure precision/recall and provenance coverage.
3. **Which judgments remain reliable across events?** Human validation covers only Idai, while judge behavior varies. Next experiment: independently annotate balanced samples across events and report judge accuracy, κ, and missingness by report type.
4. **Do these evaluations predict practical usefulness?** No practitioner or deployment study establishes that link. Next experiment: measure verification effort and decision accuracy when practitioners use drafts with differing completeness and grounding scores.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central distinction and reports several sensitivity checks, but measurement validity and transfer remain limited.

Material information unavailable here includes the rendered bodies of Tables 1–6, complete event-level estimates and confidence intervals, verbatim prompts and full decoding settings referenced in the release, private evidence spans, and generated report text. Independent practitioner validation and operational outcome evidence were not collected.
