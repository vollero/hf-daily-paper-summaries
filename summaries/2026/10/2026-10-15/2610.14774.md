# ProgAgent: Learning Tool Orchestration through Programmatic Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14774) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14774)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProgAgent trains language models to orchestrate tools through persistent Python programs, improving reported benchmark pass rates and showing transfer to direct tool calling, although training conditions and evaluation interfaces complicate some comparisons (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Direct tool calling (DTC) adds tool responses to the model’s context, potentially overwhelming long tasks with intermediate data. Programmatic tool calling (PTC) instead keeps intermediate results in runtime variables and exposes selected outputs.

The authors identify three gaps: scarce verifiable long-horizon training tasks, insufficiently isolated and stateful training runtimes, and limited evidence that **training** with PTC improves orchestration beyond its inference-time benefits (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Verifiable task synthesis:** Generate executable database-and-file environments, tools, Python verifiers, and oracle solutions; independently audit and filter them. The resulting 215 tasks have four environment scales, with instructions, logic, tool schemas, and verifiers held fixed (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Stateful training runtime:** A persistent IPython kernel retains variables across turns, routes tool calls, and returns printed output or tracebacks. Bubblewrap and fresh episode environments provide isolation (§3.2; Appendix C.2).
- **Training and comparative evidence:** Cold-start supervised fine-tuning followed by GSPO reinforcement learning trains Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B under DTC, PTC, and Hybrid interfaces. Cross-harness and environment-scaling experiments examine whether benefits extend beyond interface access (§3.3; §§5.2–5.3).

The contribution is the integrated training stack and its empirical study; executable code actions themselves are acknowledged prior work (Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

Benchmark pass rates below are reported as means over four independent runs (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger large-environment performance | LOCA-bench at **64k environment-state tokens**: PTC ProgAgent-8B/14B score **17.3%/30.7%**; evaluated comparable-size baselines under DTC score **0%**. This is environment size, not model-context occupancy. | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Gains across other benchmarks | ProgAgent-8B/14B: MCPMark **18.3%/29.7%**, Toolathlon-Verified **9.3%/17.1%**, MCP-Atlas **38.6%/45.6%**, exceeding available comparable-size baseline scores. Main-table harnesses differ. | Table 2; §§4.1–4.2 |
| PTC training benefits transfer to DTC | On MCP-Atlas under the **same DTC evaluation interface**, the PTC-trained 14B model exceeds its DTC-trained counterpart by **11.9 percentage points**. | §5.3; Table 6 caption |
| Environment scaling improves transfer | Restricting PTC training to initial-scale environments reduces LOCA-bench average pass rate from **24.3% to 16.0%** at 8B and **38.3% to 23.7%** at 14B. | §5.2; Table 7 referenced |
| Less tool output enters context during training | At 14B, PTC tool-output tokens decrease by approximately **20%**, falling below **20k per episode**; DTC remains around **30k**. Programmatic settings use roughly half as many tool calls. | §5.1; Figure 4 |
| Explicit error signaling matters | Disabling conversion of server errors to exceptions reduces LOCA-bench average pass rates from **24.3% to 19.3%** at 8B and **38.3% to 34.5%** at 14B. | §5.4; Table 7 referenced |

These results support gains for the evaluated models and settings, including evidence beyond an inference-interface advantage. They do not isolate coding-skill transfer as the causal mechanism or establish superior total compute efficiency. Several referenced tables lack their numerical bodies in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Unstructured, unpredictable observations can induce DTC-like behavior. On Playwright, **40.7%** of ProgAgent-14B PTC calls lack orchestration or selective-output structures (§5.4; Table 8 referenced).
- **Author-stated:** Servers omitting MCP’s `isError` field can silently bypass exception-based recovery (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference—training comparability:** Despite broad statements about shared training settings, Appendix C.3 specifies that DTC RL uses only initial-scale environments because larger ones yield no rewards. Matched initial-scale experiments still favor PTC, but full-scale comparisons combine interface and curriculum differences (§D.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation comparability:** Main results use different harnesses and mix newly evaluated with externally reported scores. MCPMark excludes GitHub; MCP-Atlas uses a pre-2.0 release. Some proprietary Toolathlon scores use the original benchmark rather than Toolathlon-Verified (§C.4).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism uncertainty:** Reduced context output and increased success co-occur; the supplied experiments do not separately isolate persistent variables, selective printing, and programming control flow.
- **Analyst inference—data efficiency:** A small task count alone does not establish low training cost: tasks have multiple scales and repeated rollouts, while baseline data counts use heterogeneous units (Table 1; §C.3).

## Open research questions
- **Which PTC component drives improvement?** Existing comparisons bundle several mechanisms. Ablate persistence, selective printing, and control flow separately under matched training budgets (§§2.3, 5.1).
- **How broad is cross-interface transfer?** Reported cross-harness evidence covers LOCA-bench and MCP-Atlas. Repeat matched training and cross-harness evaluation on MCPMark and Toolathlon-Verified (§5.3).
- **Can PTC handle unstructured outputs and unreliable servers better?** Both remain documented weaknesses. Test output-normalization and error-detection variants using controlled browser snapshots and malformed server responses (§5.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the core method and principal results, but omits numerical bodies for Tables 3–8, several figure details, actual prompt/code excerpts, exact SFT trajectory counts, uncertainty estimates, total compute costs, and verified public artifact availability. These omissions limit assessment of statistical reliability, component effects, and reproducibility.
