# LabInstruct: Benchmarking Situated Instructional Video Generation for Lab Procedures

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14020) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14020)


## One-sentence takeaway
LabInstruct finds that eight video generators preserve laboratory appearance more reliably than they complete required procedures, with human evaluation exposing substantial execution failures even among the strongest models.

## Problem and motivation
Laboratory instructions must communicate object interactions, action order, and visible state changes in a particular workspace. The authors argue that existing benchmarks assess visual quality or scientific phenomena without testing whether generated videos correctly demonstrate human-executed laboratory procedures (§1–2). This matters for communicating AI-generated experimental plans to human operators.

## Approach and contributions
- **Task formulation:** Generate a procedural continuation from an initial workspace image and a natural-language instruction; alternative valid executions are accepted rather than requiring reference-trajectory imitation (§3.1–3.3).
- **Benchmark:** LabInstruct contains 204 tasks across five disciplines, with reference executions and human-verified object, action, contact, and state annotations. Level 1 covers single operations; Level 2 covers short procedures with dependencies (§3).
- **Evaluation framework:** Task-specific checklists separate core execution quality, critical completion, scene continuity, and visible safety. Overall scores multiply these components, with scene and safety violations acting as vetoes (§3.4; Appendix B).
- **Comparative study:** Eight existing generators are evaluated with human and VLM judges, including sensitivity analyses for scoring, evaluator sampling, and generation randomness (§4; Appendix C). The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation protocol, rather than a new generator.

## Evidence and main results

All benchmark scores below use the paper’s 0–100 reporting scale. Completion is an averaged partial-credit factor, **not a strict task-success percentage** (Appendix B.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Human evaluation identifies a leading group with substantial remaining failures | Pooled Overall across all 204 tasks: MiniMax H3 44.3, Seedance 2.0 43.0, Wan 3.0 37.3; one sample per model–task pair. | Table 5; §4.1 |
| Longer-task execution is weaker under human judging | MiniMax H3’s Core score falls from 81.6 on Level 1 to 77.3 on Level 2; Completion from 51.9 to 43.6; Overall from 49.7 to 39.0. These are independently curated task sets. | Tables 4–5; Appendix C.4.3 |
| GPT judging can substantially overestimate weaker generators | Pooled Overall for LTX 2.3 is 22.1 under GPT-5.6 Sol versus 3.0 under humans; Cosmos3 Nano scores 19.4 versus 5.8. | Tables 3, 5 |
| Aggregate agreement exceeds task-level agreement | Across eight generators, pooled human–GPT Pearson correlation is 0.980 and Spearman correlation 0.928. Across 139 tasks with non-degenerate rankings, mean Spearman correlation is 0.364 and non-tied pairwise agreement 72.9%. | Tables 6, 14 |
| Evaluator sampling changes absolute scores | Across eight models, GPT’s mean pooled Overall rises from 22.6 at 2 FPS to 30.7 at 4 FPS and 35.2 at 8 FPS; the leading three-model group persists. | Appendix C.4.2; Table 16 |
| Additional samples change ordering within the leading group | On a balanced 40-task subset, best-of-three Overall rises from 55.3 to 57.8 for MiniMax H3, 52.8 to 66.8 for Seedance 2.0, and 53.7 to 64.8 for Wan 3.0. | Appendix C.4.4; Table 20 |

The evidence supports a gap between appearance preservation and checklist-defined procedural execution in this benchmark. It does not establish that procedure length alone causes the cross-level differences, that aggregate judge agreement guarantees reliable individual judgments, or that generated videos improve human laboratory performance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coverage limits:** The benchmark favors clean, continuous, short procedures. Sources, disciplines, and operations are imbalanced, limiting generalization to broader laboratory conditions (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated sampling limits:** Main results use one generation per task; repeated sampling covers only the leading generators on a subset. Exact top-model ordering is therefore setting-dependent (Appendices C.4.4, E).
- **Author-stated contamination risk:** Public source videos may overlap generator training data; this cannot be reliably audited for commercial models (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated safety boundary:** Visible safety checks cannot establish practical laboratory safety (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Moderate pre-adjudication human agreement and weaker task-level judge agreement warrant caution for close comparisons. Human raters were co-authors; independent external validation is not reported (Table 13; Appendices C.3, F.4).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Release is planned, and reconstruction depends on continued source accessibility. Appendix D lists schema and prompt headings but supplies no actual templates in the retrieved text (Appendices D, F.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does explicit procedural planning improve execution?** Failure examples identify omissions and misordering but test no remedy. Compare direct generation against generation conditioned on explicit action-and-state plans using identical tasks and sampling budgets.
- **How much difficulty comes from dependencies rather than task composition?** The levels are independently curated. Construct matched procedures that progressively add dependent steps while holding workspace and operations fixed.
- **Can automatic judges reliably select usable samples?** Best-of-three gains show available variation, while task-level judge agreement remains limited. Test automatic sample selection against blinded human selection and evaluation.
- **Do generated demonstrations help people execute procedures?** The paper evaluates videos, not learner outcomes. Run a supervised study comparing text, generated demonstrations, and reference demonstrations on execution accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark design and descriptive results; lower for generalization beyond its curated setting.

Material information unavailable includes confirmed release status and repository access, actual schema and prompt templates, comprehensive repeated-sampling uncertainty, independent external-rater validation, auditable training-data overlap, and human instructional-effectiveness measurements.
