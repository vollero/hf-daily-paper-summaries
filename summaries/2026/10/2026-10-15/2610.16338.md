# SyncReward: Human-Aligned Synchronization Reward for Audio-Visual Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16338) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16338)


## One-sentence takeaway
SyncReward combines temporal contrastive pretraining with human ratings of jointly generated audio–video to improve synchronization evaluation and reward-guided generation, although generation preference results remain provisional (§§3–4; Table 2 caption).

## Problem and motivation
Generated speech, impacts, and other sounds can occur out of time with visible events. The authors argue that temporal-offset metrics and general-purpose multimodal judges inadequately capture generated-content failures, including missing events and ambiguous sound sources. They identify insufficient human-rated generated data and reliable synchronization benchmarks as key gaps (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Data:** SyncReward-Data-80K covers eight generators through a taxonomy of Speech, Onset, Instrument, and Ambient events. Professionals assess scene–audio relatedness and then synchronization on an ordinal scale (§3.1).
- **Benchmark:** SyncReward-Bench contains 1,000 independently annotated, disjoint generated clips for comparing evaluators against human ratings (§3.1; Appendix D.1).
- **Reward model:** Starting from PE-AV encoders, semantic–temporal contrastive pretraining uses aligned segments, within-clip temporal negatives, and cross-clip semantic negatives. Frozen encoders then feed a trainable cross-modal Transformer and score head supervised by regression and ranking losses (§3.2).
- **Generation applications:** The learned score guides Best-of-16 selection and DiffusionNFT post-training of LTX-2.3 (§4.3).

The stated novelty centers on human supervision for **jointly generated** audio–video and its integration into evaluation and generation optimization, rather than introducing DiffusionNFT itself (§2; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; the supplied text contains table captions and narrative summaries but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–3.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest benchmark agreement among evaluated methods | Across four event families, SyncReward exceeds data-matched Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-FT by **1.9–10.2 points in Spearman ρ×100** and **1.1–5.7 percentage points in pairwise accuracy** on SyncReward-Bench. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Better inference-time selection preferences | LTX-2.3 Best-of-16 selection yields human/Gemini preference scores of **63.67/71.94**, exceeding the next-best reward by **11.1/17.7 points**. Scores are provisional base-referenced estimates. | §4.3; Table 2 caption |
| Better post-training preferences | With LTX-2.3 DiffusionNFT post-training, SyncReward exceeds the next-best reward by **12.1 human-preference points** and **15.2 Gemini-preference points**; these estimates are also provisional. | §4.3; Table 2 caption |
| Human supervision adds substantial agreement | Full model versus Stage 1 alone: **+17.7 points in Spearman ρ×100** and **+9.0 percentage points in pairwise accuracy** on SyncReward-Bench. | §4.4; Table 3 |
| Temporal negatives and ranking supervision matter | Removing temporal versus semantic negatives reduces correlation by **7.6 versus 4.1 points**; removing ranking versus regression reduces pairwise accuracy by **3.8 versus 1.6 points**. | §4.4; Table 3 |
| Human reference ratings retain disagreement | Benchmark annotation has **Fleiss’ κ=0.23**, pairwise annotator Spearman **0.45–0.50**, and exact agreement **52–55%** across clips with three valid scores. | Appendix D.1 |
| Synchronization can improve while content fidelity declines | A qualitative singing example improves visible vocal correspondence but omits the requested instrument after post-training. | §4.5; Figure 5 |

The evidence supports improved agreement within the proposed benchmark and promising reward utility for LTX-2.3. It does not establish generalization to unseen generators, broad generation-quality improvements, or definitive preference margins: complete all-pairs reward evaluation is explicitly pending.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed trade-off:** Synchronization optimization can reduce prompt fidelity, as illustrated by the missing instrument (§4.5).
- **Provisional comparisons:** Table 2 explicitly labels preference scores as estimates awaiting complete all-pairs evaluation.
- **Analyst inference—restricted transfer evidence:** Reward optimization uses one generator and a failure-focused prompt pool that excludes ambient audio and stylized content; broader applicability remains untested in the supplied results (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—split uncertainty:** Training/validation exclusion of shared `(uid, model)` pairs does not itself establish separation by source prompt alone. Benchmark disjointness is asserted, but its exact prompt-level definition is unavailable (§3.1).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The dataset is described as both 80K ratings and 80K rated clips despite five annotators per generated clip. Detailed aggregation is missing. Main-text ranking supervision uses all unequal targets, whereas Appendix C.3 specifies a minimum label difference.
- **Reference uncertainty:** Annotator disagreement and the smaller Instrument subset warrant caution when interpreting category margins (Appendix D.1; Table 1 caption).

## Open research questions
- **Does performance transfer to unseen generators?** The benchmark includes the same generator roster used for data collection. Next experiment: leave one generator out of reward training and evaluate its human-rating agreement.
- **Can synchronization gains preserve requested content?** Figure 5 demonstrates a fidelity failure. Next experiment: compare synchronization-only optimization with a combined synchronization–prompt-fidelity objective using separate human judgments.
- **How stable are the reward preference margins?** Current estimates are provisional. Next experiment: complete matched-prompt all-pairs human evaluation and report uncertainty.
- **Does reward optimization extend beyond the selected prompt pool?** Post-training excludes several benchmark content types. Next experiment: evaluate selection and post-training on held-out Ambient and stylized-character prompts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported directions are clear, but numerical table bodies, complete annotation/aggregation instructions, preference-study sample sizes and judge prompts, statistical uncertainty, post-filter pretraining size, and verified public asset availability are unavailable in the supplied text.
