# Are We Testing Too Much? A Survey of Benchmark Compression for Efficient Evaluation of Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18248) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18248)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey organizes benchmark compression around available information, evaluation timing, and required outputs, arguing that fewer tests deliver useful savings only when recovery reliability and total workflow costs are validated (§§2, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Redundant benchmark items make repeated LLM evaluation expensive. Compression estimates full-evaluation scores, rankings, or decisions from fewer costly observations. The authors identify fragmented methods, incompatible cost assumptions, and inconsistent reliability checks as barriers to choosing an appropriate approach (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is a literature synthesis rather than a new compression algorithm.

- **Common formulation and taxonomy:** The authors report reviewing 119 papers and define compression against a predefined full evaluation. Their hierarchy separates foundational analysis, adaptive evaluation, cross-benchmark compression, and three information-based single-benchmark families (§§1–2; Appendix C). The claim of being the first systematic survey is the authors’ claim.
- **Comparison and practical guidance:** They compare selection, recovery, information requirements, construction and maintenance costs, validation, and fallback choices (§6; Appendix D).
- **Research agenda:** They propose testing transfer across model families and time, refreshing established benchmarks, and incorporating compression before benchmark release (§7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad literature synthesis | Authors report reviewing 119 papers; this measures survey scope, not compression performance. | Abstract; §1 |
| Substantial compression is reported in prior work | Introduction states that fewer than 20% of test samples can preserve results close to full evaluation on benchmarks including LongBench, GSM8K, and MMLU; exact errors, model populations, and per-benchmark settings are absent here. | §1 |
| No universally safe compression ratio | Required sample size depends on score gaps, paired variance, group coverage, and allowed error. | §5 |
| Historical information can lose value under model shift | Survey reports that historical selectors’ advantage may shrink and random sampling can remain competitive; no numerical comparison is supplied. | §3.1.2; Appendix E.2 |
| Fewer observations need not mean lower total cost | Construction, refresh, probes, recovery, audits, fallback, and sequential latency can offset savings. | §6.3; Appendix D.4 |
| Preserving rankings does not establish score fidelity | Overall ranking agreement can coexist with distorted gaps or reversals among close models; winner selection need not recover all scores. | §5; Appendix D.1 |

These findings support a qualitative framework for method selection and validation. The supplied text does not establish a universal best method, pooled savings estimate, or controlled empirical ranking of method families.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Coverage ends in August 2026, detailed treatment is selective, and heterogeneous evaluation settings prevent straightforward quantitative combination (Limitations). Appendix F specifies an August 9 cutoff.

**Surveyed-method risks:** Historical transfer can fail, proxies can introduce systematic error, adaptive sampling requires suitable correction, and near ties may eliminate early-stopping savings (§§3–6).

**Analyst assessment:** The source is truncated, and several comparison tables retain captions without their contents, limiting verification. The stated cutoff also needs clarification: the visible references include a September 2026 identifier cited in Appendix E.2. This alone does not establish how the core corpus was counted.

## Open research questions

- **How long do compressed subsets remain reliable?** Model drift remains unresolved (§7). Freeze subsets, then prospectively evaluate later model families against full results, tracking bias and close-model reversals.
- **When do reusable compressors repay construction costs?** The survey provides accounting principles, without measured cross-family break-even points (Appendix D.4). Compare total costs over repeated targets at matched recovery quality.
- **Can compression before release preserve capability coverage?** This is proposed rather than demonstrated (§7). Build compact and full versions of a new benchmark and compare held-out model scores, subgroup coverage, and discrimination.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The formulation and qualitative taxonomy are well supported by the visible text, but coverage is incomplete because the supplied source was truncated.

Material missing information includes the full catalogue, several table bodies, exact settings behind the introductory compression claim, quantitative cross-family comparisons, confirmed downloadable assets, and clarification of the corpus cutoff.
