# ProbabilityBench: Model Evaluation and Calibration with Exact Ground-Truth Uncertainty

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17016) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17016)


## One-sentence takeaway
ProbabilityBench constructs three synthetic text-classification worlds with exact conditional probabilities, enabling direct probability evaluation and revealing finite-sample errors in calibration and Bayes-error estimators.

## Problem and motivation
A single observed label does not reveal an input’s true outcome probability. Consequently, empirical calibration scores can reflect sampling noise, while log loss mixes probability-estimation error with irreducible uncertainty. Bayes error—the best achievable classification error—is also generally unknown on real data.

The authors identify a gap between tractable synthetic distributions and text benchmarks whose reference probabilities come from finite annotations: neither provides exact uncertainty targets for natural-language outcome prediction (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Exact synthetic worlds:** Specify latent-state distributions, deterministic outcomes, and partially visible attributes; marginalize hidden states to obtain exact class probabilities and Bayes errors. Instantiate peer review, covert-transfer auditing, and credit risk with nested visibility settings (§§3–4).
- **Probability-preserving text:** Prove that rendering preserves conditional probabilities when text reveals no hidden information and allows every visible attribute to be recovered. Fixed templates satisfy these conditions (§3.4).
- **Direct model evaluation:** Compare decision and generative models using KL divergence from exact probabilities, alongside endpoint list-price costs; separately examine demonstrations and agents with test-label access (§5; Appendices A–C).
- **Evaluation of evaluation methods:** Test nearest-neighbor Bayes-error estimators, empirical multicalibration, and repeated-label snapshot calibration against known or independently sampled references (§5).

The evidence establishes these behaviors within the specified synthetic worlds. Exact per-input probabilities eliminate reference-label noise from KL and expected-accuracy scoring, but averages over test inputs still depend on sampled cases. Predictor-dependent population calibration references also retain integration error. Frontier membership uses mean results without claims of statistically significant differences (Appendices A.3, C.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Memos use fixed templates; richer prose must preserve both information conditions. Constructing additional worlds requires substantial manual design and verification (§6).
- **Synthetic validity:** Parameters are author-selected, and memos already contain interpreted assessments rather than raw manuscripts or financial statements. **Analyst inference:** results do not establish calibration quality in corresponding real applications (Appendices A.1, B).
- **Comparison boundaries:** Main probability comparisons use Medium visibility and a fixed demonstration count. Endpoint costs do not represent matched compute; KL ranks depend on the specified probability-clipping threshold. One model–world pair is excluded for invalid replies (Appendices A.2–A.3).
- **Agent experiment:** Test-access conditions change both available data and the stated objective, so their separate effects are not isolated (Appendix A.4).
- **Calibration scope:** Higher-order experiments use prediction bins and population-weighted errors. They do not establish improvement over the uncalibrated predictor’s higher-order error, which is not plotted (Appendix C.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does probability quality survive richer wording?** Only fixed templates are evaluated. Next experiment: compare multiple independently verified, invertible renderers while holding latent cases and demonstrations fixed (§3.4; §6).
- **How stable are model rankings across uncertainty regimes?** The broad comparison covers Medium visibility. Next experiment: repeat it across hiding levels and residual-noise settings (Table 7; Appendix A.3).
- **Can snapshot fitting improve higher-order calibration directly?** The uncalibrated higher-order comparison is missing. Next experiment: score both predictors against the same Bayes-mixture references across snapshot sizes (Appendix C.5).
- **Which representations improve Bayes-error estimation?** Removing irrelevant fields only partly resolves estimator mismatch. Next experiment: compare distances and representations at matched sample sizes against exact error (Appendix C.3).

## Confidence and missing information
