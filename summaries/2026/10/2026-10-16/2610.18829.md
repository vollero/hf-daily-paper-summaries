# OmniCompass: A Holistic Benchmark for Omni-Modal Video Grounding and Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18829) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18829)


## One-sentence takeaway
OmniCompass combines paired textual and image-assisted target specifications with visual and audio-visual grounding and QA, revealing weak localization and inconsistent paired predictions among evaluated models (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify fragmented evaluation: existing benchmarks typically separate visual from audio-visual evidence, test only one target-specification format, or emphasize QA over grounding. This makes it difficult to distinguish failures in identifying a target, locating its evidence, and understanding events (§1; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified benchmark:** OmniCompass-VL contains 820 videos and 2,460 task instances; its 240-video AV subset has 720 separately annotated instances requiring native audio. Both cover temporal grounding (TG), spatial grounding (SG), and five-option QA across short-to-long videos (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Paired target specifications and scoring:** Each instance preserves its video, target, semantics, and ground truth across Text and Text+Image. Cross-Specification Consistency Scores (CS) combine correctness under both settings with prediction overlap for grounding; QA requires both answers to be correct (§3.1).
- **Manual construction and diagnostic evaluation:** Annotators construct and cross-check all instances, followed by expert adjudication. Evaluation covers 50 distinct models, with analyses of reference specification, timestamps, modalities, and frame budgets (§§3.2, 4; Appendices B, D).

These results establish benchmark-specific weaknesses under the reported inputs. They do not isolate architectural causes or establish equal-compute superiority. CS also measures joint correctness: Appendix B.3 explicitly warns that its full gap from single-setting grounding scores is not prediction disagreement alone.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Models receive different frame budgets. Text+Image also replaces part of the textual description, so its effect is not a controlled image-addition effect (Appendices E.1, B.2).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** SG receives the relevant interval and predicts endpoint boxes; it does not test temporal search or dense tracking. Shared VL/AV videos can have different targets and annotations (Appendices E.1, C.1).
- **Author-stated confounding:** Duration, domain, and granularity analyses mix content and target distributions. Reference subgroups can be small, and reported subgroup differences lack significance testing (Appendices A, B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Reference diversity is uneven: entity cues constitute 79.7% of VL references, limiting evidence about other cue types (Appendix C.4).
- **Evidence qualification:** The introduction says audio consistently improves TG, but §4.3 reports a TG decrease for MiMo-V2.5. The detailed ablation supports model-dependent TG benefits.
- **Reproducibility boundary:** Media rights vary by source; provenance does not establish blanket redistribution permission (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
- **How much of the ranking depends on frame budget?** Budgets differ, and frame scaling changes performance (Appendices E.1, B.1). Next experiment: rerun leading models at matched frame budgets and report inference costs.
- **What causes reference-specification failures?** Text wording and image availability change together (Appendix B.2). Next experiment: cross image presence with fixed versus shortened descriptions.
- **When does audio improve temporal localization?** TG gains vary across tested models (§4.3). Next experiment: compare aligned, removed, and temporally shifted audio on identical eligible pairs.
