# ForcingBooster: Adaptive Patch-wise Denoising for Fast Autoregressive Video Diffusion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12625) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12625)


## One-sentence takeaway
ForcingBooster trains a router to stop denoising individual video patches early, with authors reporting about 50% fewer patch-denoising evaluations and 1.3× end-to-end speedup on Causal Forcing while preserving near-baseline VBench quality (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Few-step autoregressive video generators still apply the same denoising budget to every patch. This wastes computation on stable regions, while uniformly shortening sampling can damage difficult regions such as moving objects or detailed textures. The authors target spatially adaptive computation within each chunk, beyond frame- or chunk-level budget selection (§§1, 2.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Patch-wise stopping:** A router reads the frozen generator’s final-layer features and predicts whether each active patch should stop. Exited patches retain their current clean-latent estimate; the original sampling schedule remains fixed (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Sparse inference with retained context:** Stopped patches leave subsequent Transformer updates after their KV representations are cached, preserving context for active patches and later chunks (§§2.3, 3.3).
- **Router-only training:** A stopping loss penalizes exits whose clean-latent predictions differ from full-step generator outputs. A computation loss encourages earlier exits, with continuation costs estimated using the current routing policy. No additional video data or stopping annotations are required, according to the authors (§3.4).

The distinguishing combination is learned spatial stopping in a frozen few-step autoregressive generator, fixed clean outputs for exited patches, and reusable KV context (§2.3).

The evidence supports the authors’ claimed quality–efficiency trade-off against uniform sampling and their spatial-allocation controls. However, the supplied text contains captions and discussion for Tables 1–3 without their numerical cells, preventing verification of exact scores, runtimes, and differences. Agreement with a generator’s full-step output does not establish ground-truth fidelity or physical accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported trade-offs:** Savings are larger for lower-motion videos. Excessive computation penalties increase deviation from the reference with diminishing runtime gains; higher stopping thresholds do not consistently improve LPIPS (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—reference dependence:** Training targets and convergence measurements favor reproducing the frozen generator’s full-step behavior, including any errors it contains (§§3.2, 3.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Experiments compare uniform sampling and allocation controls, but provide no direct empirical comparison against the adaptive methods discussed in related work (§§2.3, 4).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Router architecture, parameter count, evaluation sample counts, uncertainty estimates, and detailed additional-model settings are unavailable. The replay description also leaves its exact relationship to routed on-policy histories unclear (§§3.4, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** Main timing uses one hardware and batch-size setting; general speedups across hardware, batching, and longer generation horizons are not established (§4.1).

## Open research questions
1. **Does early stopping accumulate errors over long interactions?** The main evaluation uses 81-frame videos, and longer-horizon results are not detailed. A next experiment could measure temporal consistency and control adherence over extended routed rollouts.
2. **How much does on-policy training contribute?** The text describes it but provides no isolated ablation. Compare on-policy and fixed-trajectory training at matched patch-NFE and training budgets.
3. **How competitive is routing against other adaptive acceleration methods?** Direct comparisons are absent. Evaluate routing, caching, and frame/chunk allocation on the same checkpoint, hardware, conditions, and quality–runtime curve.
4. **Do speedups survive different execution settings?** Only the main single-A800 timing setup is specified. Benchmark multiple batch sizes and hardware configurations while separating router, cache, Transformer, and decoding costs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The mechanism and headline claims are clear, but quantitative assessment is limited by missing table cells and plot values. Material gaps include exact VBench scores and runtimes, evaluation prompts and sample counts, statistical variability, router architecture, complete training/replay details, additional-model configurations, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
