# ANTE: Anticipate, Then Execute -- Learning Internal World Models for Coding Agents from Verified Beliefs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15368) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15368)


## One-sentence takeaway

ANTE trains coding agents with execution-verified forecasts and belief-based step credit, reporting 57.2% pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified—6.2 percentage points above its matched Reactive baseline (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation

Terminal task rewards provide weak feedback about which earlier actions helped or harmed a coding trajectory. Memory can record mistakes after execution without anticipating them. The authors identify a gap between existing code world models and agent training: explicit pre-action forecasts, real-execution verification, and per-step policy credit are not jointly integrated (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Belief-augmented training:** Before acting, the policy emits a state summary, an outcome forecast, and a trust/abstain decision. SFT on BeliefTraj teaches this format; RL supervises forecasts using execution-derived labels (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Belief-Anchored Policy Optimization (BAPO):** Steps from rollouts of the same task are grouped by predicted outcomes and decisions. Their decision returns produce step adjustments alongside episode-level task advantages (§3.3).
- **ANTE-TTO:** At uncertain or predicted-failure steps, the policy generates alternatives and ranks them using their own forecasts, executing one candidate without an external scorer or weight updates (§3.4; Appendix G).
- **Analysis and evaluation:** The authors provide imitation and forecast-based selection bounds, plus controlled comparisons across repository and terminal harnesses. Their priority claim is author-stated; the supplied text cannot independently establish novelty (§1; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better repository task success | ANTE-32B reports 57.2% pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified, exceeding matched Reactive and Retrospective variants by 6.2 and 4.8 percentage points, respectively; ordinary evaluations use single greedy attempts. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 9 confirms ANTE’s score. |
| BAPO contributes beyond forecasts alone | On SWE-bench Verified, full ANTE exceeds the variant retaining beliefs and prediction loss but removing BAPO by 1.8, 2.6, and 3.0 points at 7B, 14B, and 32B. | §§4.1–4.2. |
| Terminal performance improves | ANTE-9B resolves 24.7% of Terminal-Bench 2.0 using Tmax; the authors’ ECHO reproduction on the same backbone and RL task pool reports 21.4%. | Appendix K; Table 10. |
| Forecast accuracy improves during RL | At 32B, prediction hit rate on conclusive steps rises from 44% to 69%; these are execution-checkable forecasts, not all actions or full repository states. | §4.5; Figure 5. |
| Reported reasoning transfer | ANTE-32B leads matched Reactive-RL by an average 6.2 percentage points across eleven out-of-distribution benchmarks using plain chain-of-thought prompts; individual scores are absent. | §4.3; Table 3 caption; Appendix N.5. |
| Selective inference has lower reported token overhead | At 7B, ANTE-TTO uses 1.31× inference tokens per task versus 4.42× for TTS-4, relative to one greedy attempt; tokens include discarded candidates. Exact success gains are unavailable. | §4.4; Figure 4 caption; Appendix G.1. |

The controlled comparisons support the authors’ claim that the combined belief-training framework improves the tested agents. The BAPO ablation removes both decision rewards and anchored step adjustments, so it does not isolate their individual effects. Forecast improvements and TTO gains do not establish a comprehensive model of repository dynamics. External leaderboard comparisons use differing scaffolds and protocols (Appendices J–K).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Incomplete source:** The supplied text is truncated, and several tables retain captions without numerical bodies. This analysis therefore does not provide complete coverage.
- **Author-described supervision boundary:** Only parsed outcomes are verified; summaries receive no direct RL supervision. Terminal training supervises command exits per step, while task-success decisions are settled at episode end (§3.3; Appendix I).
- **Author-described hindsight mismatch:** SFT labels incorporate future observations. The imitation analysis explicitly retains a corpus-dependent mismatch term; SFT alone cannot eliminate it (§3.2; Appendix C.3).
- **Author-described theoretical qualifications:** Anchoring introduces a shaping term for reasoning tokens. The task-performance analysis concerns an idealized surrogate that differs from the implemented token-level, shaped objective (Appendices C.2–C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported standard errors concern repeated evaluations of one checkpoint, rather than independent training seeds; training variability remains unclear (§4.2 captions; Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference:** Separate models are trained for the two harnesses, with different outcome and reward configurations. These experiments demonstrate applicability across harnesses, but do not establish direct transfer of one coding checkpoint to terminal tasks (§4.1; Appendix I).

## Open research questions

- **Which BAPO component drives the gain?** The available ablation removes decision rewards and anchored advantages together. A factorial experiment could vary each component independently.
- **Does TTO remain reliable as candidate counts grow?** The reported configuration uses three alternatives, and candidate-level forecast calibration is unavailable. Sweep candidate counts and execute discarded candidates offline to measure ranking accuracy and optimism.
- **Can forecasts capture broader regressions?** Supervision targets parsed checks and exit status. Evaluate richer forecasts against held-out regression suites to test whether benefits extend beyond the checks the agent chooses.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline findings are explicit, but the source is truncated and important numerical evidence is missing.

Unavailable information includes complete matched-result tables and standard errors, individual out-of-distribution scores, exact TTO success gains, corpus leakage-filter details, independent training-seed variation, and confirmed release locations for code, weights, and BeliefTraj.
