# SERO: Learning from Executable Oracle Continuations for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19397) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19397)


## One-sentence takeaway
SERO improves agent training on ALFWorld and WebShop by learning executable oracle continuations from policy-visited states, with controlled ablations supporting both multi-step supervision and policy-dependent weighting of downstream targets.

## Problem and motivation
Sparse rewards and long interaction horizons make agentic reinforcement learning difficult. The authors identify a gap beyond credit assignment: when sampled rollouts never contain a useful action sequence, better attribution cannot supply that missing behavior. They further argue that supervising a corrective action alone leaves the subsequent observations and decisions under-supervised (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable oracle continuations:** Restore a state reached during an on-policy rollout, then execute a training-only oracle through the environment under the same remaining horizon. Train on its actions conditioned on the observations that execution actually produces (§4.2).
- **Policy-reachability weighting:** Weight each downstream target by the product of frozen-policy compatibility scores for preceding oracle commands. For tokenized commands, this is a length-normalized compatibility proxy, **not an actual prefix probability** (§4.3; Appendix B).
- **Separate auxiliary optimization:** Admit valid branches only when their realized return improves over the original policy suffix. Add their weighted command-token loss to the base RL objective without changing its rewards, advantages, or policy ratios. Deployment uses only the trained policy (§4.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| SERO improves GraphGPO at both model sizes | ALFWorld seen/unseen success: **90.63/88.02 → 95.83/91.67%** at 1.5B; **91.67/76.30 → 95.57/87.50%** at 7B. WebShop strict success: **77.93 → 79.27%** and **80.47 → 83.67%**, respectively. | Table 1 |
| HGPO success also improves, but task score does not uniformly improve | At 7B, ALFWorld seen/unseen success rises **94.01/90.89 → 94.53/91.41%**; WebShop strict success rises **74.47 → 79.63%**, while task score falls **87.83 → 87.63**. | Table 1 |
| Full continuations outperform root-only targets | With 1.5B GraphGPO, ALFWorld seen/unseen success is **87.76/83.85%** for Root-only, **94.79/88.28%** for Full-Flat, and **95.83/91.67%** for SERO. Full-Flat preserves total branch weight but distributes it equally across steps. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| Weight placement matters under shared oracle data | Fixed-bank ALFWorld replay yields **88.02/85.94%** seen/unseen success for SERO, versus **85.42/84.64%** for Flat, **87.24/82.55%** for Depth decay, and **85.16/80.73%** for Shuffled weights. | Appendix A.1; Table 3 |
| Additional exploration explains part of the gain | Approximately interaction-matched GraphGPO reaches **93.49/90.36%** ALFWorld seen/unseen success, versus **95.83/91.67%** for SERO. The budget match is explicitly approximate. | Appendix A.2; Table 4 |
| Gains do not consistently increase with horizon | On ALFWorld unseen tasks with expert plans of at least seven steps, success rises **83.50 → 87.32%**; the corresponding seen bucket changes only **90.02 → 90.14%**. | Appendix A.8; Table 8 |

Table 1 reports averages over three testing seeds. The evidence supports improvements within the evaluated settings and supports the value of allocating supervision according to prefix compatibility. It does not establish universal gains across tasks, calibrated reachability probabilities, or exact compute efficiency. The horizon breakdown also limits any claim that longer tasks necessarily benefit more.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** SERO requires environment restoration and an executable training oracle. Evaluation covers only ALFWorld and WebShop, with scaling beyond 7B left open. Uniform sampling from failed rollouts may miss rare failure types (Appendix G).
- **Author-qualified:** Compatibility weights are proxies conditioned on sampled reasoning contexts. The prefix-reproduction check follows recorded states without executing fresh environment trajectories (Appendices A.3 and B).
- **Analyst inference:** Strong privileged oracles leave robustness to unreliable teachers uncertain; no oracle-quality ablation is reported (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-backbone WebShop comparisons are less controlled than within-backbone comparisons because horizons, observation modes, and prompt limits differ (Table 11).
- **Analyst inference:** Resource efficiency remains unresolved: the interaction control is approximate, and GPU counts alone do not establish wall-clock or total compute costs (Appendix A.2; Table 10).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The text describes testing seeds but does not clearly establish independent training-seed replication or define the reported “±” statistic.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is SERO to imperfect oracles?** The reported oracles use expert plans or privileged goal metadata. A next experiment could vary oracle error rates while measuring gate acceptance and downstream success.
- **Can approximate restoration preserve its benefits?** Exact restoration is assumed, while generated continuations are proposed as future work. Compare exact snapshots with controlled restoration errors and measure target validity and policy performance.
- **Does compatibility weighting transfer across command representations?** Its token-length normalization makes it a proxy rather than a prefix probability. Compare equivalent command serializations and alternative compatibility scores on the same frozen oracle bank.
- **Would targeted root selection improve difficult failures?** Uniform root sampling may under-supervise rare failures. Compare uniform and prioritized sampling under matched oracle-interaction budgets, reporting task-family and horizon-stratified success.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method summary and reported benchmark comparisons; **medium** in broader causal and efficiency conclusions.

Material missing information includes public code/checkpoint availability, exact evaluation manifests and seed identities, independent training-run replication, the meaning of uncertainty intervals, wall-clock and memory overhead, and robustness to noisy oracles or imperfect restoration.
