# Orbia: Do Generated Videos Form a Consistent 3D World?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20693) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20693)


## One-sentence takeaway

Orbia evaluates whether generated environments preserve observed content, retain newly invented regions, and agree geometrically, finding that visual quality and camera control provide limited assurance of persistent 3D consistency (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation

Interactive video generators must maintain a scene as users explore, leave regions, and return. Convincing individual frames do not establish that objects retain their appearance, position, or geometry.

The authors identify fragmented evaluation coverage: prior benchmarks test various aspects of control, geometry, revisits, and drift, but do not jointly separate fidelity to input evidence, persistence of generated content, and internal 3D agreement (Table 1; §2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **A benchmark with controlled exploration and revisits.** Orbia contains 720 cases and 859 anchor targets, combining public datasets, self-captured scenes, and UE simulation. Six motion templates cover translations, rotations, arcs, orbits, and peek-and-return exploration; short cases have paired minute-long extensions (§§3.1–3.3).
- **A complementary evaluation framework.** Input preservation uses registered RGB-D references and individually scored landmarks. Generated-content persistence compares revisits against the model’s first generated observation. Self-consistency uses recovered cameras, cross-view depth/features, regional scale agreement, and held-out reconstruction (§4).
- **A comparative empirical study.** The authors evaluate 19 camera/action-conditioned world models and eight text-image-to-video (TI2V) models, examining capability gaps, trajectory difficulty, temporal degradation, and agreement with human rankings (§5).

The contribution is an evaluation benchmark and diagnostic framework; the supplied text does not introduce a new video generator.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Different models lead different capabilities | Across 27 evaluated models, SolarWM-H3 leads overall at 76.32; Gen3C leads world-model input preservation at 75.23; HY-WorldPlay leads world-model self-consistency at 70.34. No model leads every tier. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| TI2V models show weaker control but stronger internal consistency | Mean control scores: 57.29 for world models versus 32.48 for TI2V. TI2V mean self-consistency is 69.88, reported as 10.14 points above world models. These are benchmark tier scores. | §5.1 |
| Visual quality weakly tracks consistency | Spearman correlation between visual quality and the mean of the three consistency tiers is 0.33 among world models and 0.21 among TI2V models. | §5.3 |
| Input retention and generated-region retention differ at case level | For short world-model videos, input preservation versus generated-content persistence has model-level Spearman correlation 0.87, but mean within-model correlation 0.35. | §5.3 |
| Longer exploration exposes substantial forgetting | On forward–backward trajectories, generated-content persistence falls from 66.21 to 40.96 for world models and from 67.74 to 44.58 for TI2V models when moving from short to minute-long evaluation. | §5.5; Fig. 5 |
| Automatic rankings broadly agree with human rankings | On 90 cases spanning six models, assessed by 20 raters, human–automatic model-level Spearman correlations range from 0.859 to 0.921 across the six tiers. | §5.7 |

These results support evaluating quality, control, content retention, and geometry separately. They do not establish that any model maintains a fully consistent world, or that a particular memory architecture causes better performance. The authors’ proposed architectural explanations are observational interpretations (§5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Source limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, including a substantial middle omission. This analysis cannot claim complete coverage of metric definitions, adapters, experiments, or author-stated limitations.
- **Evaluation boundary stated in the paper:** Newly generated regions lack ground truth and are compared with earlier generated observations. This measures persistence rather than correctness against a real scene (§4).
- **Analyst inference—estimator dependence:** DA3 supplies generated-video depth, cameras, and reconstruction; some reference geometry is also estimated. Estimation errors could affect measured agreement. Robustness to alternative estimators is not established in the supplied text (§4; Table 5).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Static anchors and predefined exploration trajectories provide limited evidence about dynamic state changes, object manipulation, or longer interactions (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and scoring sensitivity:** Model interfaces require different adapters, and the overall ranking uses chosen tier weights. Adapter details and weight-sensitivity evidence are unavailable in the supplied excerpt (§§4–5).
- **Human validation boundary:** Agreement concerns model-level rankings for six selected models; it does not establish case-level accuracy across all evaluated models (§5.7).

## Open research questions

1. **How robust are rankings to geometry-estimator errors?** The evaluation relies on recovered depth and cameras (§4). A next experiment could recompute scores with alternative estimators and compare rankings on rendered-reference UE cases.
2. **Does explicit spatial memory causally improve persistence?** Several strong models use geometry, but the comparisons do not isolate memory mechanisms (§5.1). A controlled experiment could enable and disable memory within the same generator while fixing training and camera inputs.
3. **Do these findings extend to changing worlds and longer horizons?** The protocols emphasize static landmarks and exploration up to one minute (§3; Table 6). A next benchmark could introduce recorded object-state changes and longer revisit delays, separately scoring legitimate changes and forgetting.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied main sections clearly support the benchmark design and reported findings, but the source was truncated.
