# CarryOn: A Dual-Loop Harness for Personalized and Reliable Robot Assistance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21453) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21453)


## One-sentence takeaway
CarryOn surrounds a frozen robot policy with revisable user/scene memory, geometric action shielding, and reusable recovery skills, improving measured personalization and contact avoidance while increasing cumulative completion through reflection and retries.

## Problem and motivation
Everyday assistance requires deciding what suits a particular user and executing it reliably despite changing scenes and physical disturbances. The authors identify a gap between instruction-following VLAs and systems that preserve preferences, revise stale scene knowledge, prevent hazardous contact, and repair failures while retaining the intended goal (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is a coordinated runtime around a frozen VLA, with three main mechanisms:

- **Revisable personalized planning:** Persistent user profiles and scene records guide subtask and endpoint selection. Dialogue updates durable preferences; observations trigger scene updates enforced by a plan validator (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Proactive geometric shielding:** RGB-D geometry, task-specific contact permissions, and a short-horizon controller-response model predict gripper/carried-object proximity to obstacles. The shield releases a safe action prefix or commands a retreat (§3.3.1; Appendix C).
- **Reusable recovery with offline refinement:** A visual judge triggers control-based re-localization, grasping, verification, and VLA resumption. Between rounds, reflection updates grasp parameters and supported recovery strategies in a versioned library (§3.3.2–3.3.3; Appendix D).

The authors position the novelty in coupling these mechanisms through shared task context, rather than introducing a new VLA (§§1–2).

These results support the individual mechanisms in the tested settings. The recovery headline measures **cumulative task completion**, rather than isolated recovery-skill accuracy. Additional rounds combine refinement with additional attempts, so their gains do not isolate reflection’s contribution. Integrated food-serving runs illustrate coordination, but provide no aggregate end-to-end success estimate (§4.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified future work:** Longer-term adaptation, transfer of recovery experience across tasks, and balancing protection against task completion remain unresolved (§5).
- **Analyst inference—refinement confound:** No retry-matched, unrefined control is reported for the cumulative reflection results. Real-robot revisions also combine grasp-height changes with more attempts and refreshed perception (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited integrated evidence:** Most evaluations assess components separately; the integrated study describes carrot-serving and meat-refusal runs without a repeated-trial success rate (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—perception dependence:** Grounding and geometry determine shielding permissions and recovery targets. The authors identify simulation target grounding as a recovery bottleneck; shield robustness to grounding/depth errors is not quantified (Appendices C, D.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Baseline retry budgets and adapted-VLSA implementation details are insufficiently specified in the supplied text to establish fully matched comparisons. Confidence intervals are not reported.
- **Reporting inconsistency:** Some §4.3 prose references Table 5 for perturbation results and baseline token totals that appear in Table 4.

## Open research questions
1. **How much does reflection add beyond retries?** Additional rounds change both skills and opportunities to succeed. Compare refined and unchanged skills under identical retry, rollout, and API budgets.
2. **Do recovery revisions transfer?** The authors leave cross-task transfer open (§5). Freeze a learned library and test unseen objects, tasks, and disturbance directions without further reflection.
3. **How robust is shielding to perception error?** Safety depends on grounded geometry and contact permissions. Inject controlled segmentation, depth, and attachment errors, then measure violations and violation-free success.
4. **Does personalization remain reliable over long interactions?** Existing studies test retention and revision, but do not establish long-term adaptation. Evaluate extended multi-user interaction sequences with preference reversals, object relocations, and repeated end-to-end tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported results clearly, but stronger causal and deployment conclusions require retry-matched ablations, statistical uncertainty, detailed baseline budgets/adaptations, judge accuracy, perception-error sensitivity, and repeated integrated evaluation. A repository is linked, but its contents and reproducibility were not verified.
