# Routing Policy Optimization for Online Diffusion Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22282) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22282)


## One-sentence takeaway
RPO uses temporary stochastic attention routes and paired rollouts to improve dense image and video generators, with reported training-cost savings whose attribution is limited by unmatched baseline configurations (§§4–5; Appendix D).

## Problem and motivation
Online reinforcement learning can align visual generators with black-box rewards, but sampler-based methods require costly stochastic denoising rollouts. The authors identify a missing interface: an explicit exploration action that preserves the generator’s original deterministic sampler and supports both image and video generation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Attention as exploration:** At selected early denoising levels, sample visible visual-token blocks from native attention mass. Each query retains its own block and can choose dense attention; selected anchor layers remain dense. No auxiliary policy head is added (§§3, 4.1).
- **Dense-velocity surrogate:** Score recorded routed transitions using a Gaussian centered on the dense generator’s velocity. Gradients update that dense velocity directly; this is explicitly a surrogate, not the exact route likelihood (§4.2).
- **Controlled paired estimator:** Two rollouts share their prompt, initial latent, anchor layers, and other non-routing randomness. Their reward difference weights the recorded transitions (§4.3).
- **Transfer to ordinary inference:** Remove routing after training and evaluate with dense attention and the original deterministic sampler. Experiments test this transfer on SD3.5 Medium and Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (§5).

These results support transfer from routed training to dense inference in the tested settings. They do not establish that routing alone causes the cost advantage: rollout backends differ, and DanceGRPO also uses a larger group and longer schedule. The authors explicitly describe the video aggregate ranking as directional (§5.4; Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** RPO requires attention. Blockwise proposals average across layers and heads, hiding finer structure (§6).
- **Authors’ estimator caveat:** Discrete routing deviations are approximated by an isotropic Gaussian; the surrogate omits route probabilities and pathwise gradients through later transitions. Its batch-estimated variance changes gradient scale during training (§4.2; Appendix E).
- **Authors’ failure modes:** Dense-inference gains can peak and fade while routed rewards continue improving. Centering reduces this split on SD3.5, but its mechanism is not isolated. Frozen-reward hacking remains a risk (Appendix E).
- **Comparison limits:** SD3.5 learning curves use one training seed and different rollout backends. DanceGRPO uses a different backend, larger groups, and a longer training schedule; video costs are not compute-matched (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text contains no controlled component ablations separating pairing, attention-based proposals, centering, or anchor layers. Their individual contributions therefore remain uncertain.
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation on short videos and one reward dimension leaves longer-term temporal quality and multi-reward behavior unresolved (§5.1; Appendices C–D).

## Open research questions
1. **Which components drive the gains?** Component effects are not isolated. Run backend- and compute-matched ablations of matched pairing, attention-mass versus random routing, and anchor-layer selection.
2. **Would explicitly crediting route probabilities improve alignment?** The current surrogate omits them. Compare velocity-only and combined route/velocity objectives under equal rollout budgets, measuring dense reward and stability (§4.2; Appendix E).
3. **What causes dense-gain fade?** The authors observe divergence between routed and dense rewards without isolating its mechanism. Compare centered and uncentered rollouts across training seeds while tracking both rewards (Appendix E).
4. **Do gains extend to longer videos?** Evaluation uses short clips and framewise preference rewards. Test longer sequences with temporal evaluation and matched training budgets (§5.1; Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline comparisons are clearly described, but causal attribution and statistical reliability remain limited.

Material missing information includes the numerical bodies of Tables 1–2, exact values for two image metrics, uncertainty estimates, multi-seed training results, controlled component ablations, detailed video training-prompt composition, and confirmed availability of code or checkpoints.
