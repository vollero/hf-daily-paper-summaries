# InfiniSplat v2: Analyzing and Improving Feed-Forward 3DGS from Any Views

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20341) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20341)


## One-sentence takeaway
InfiniSplat v2 combines frozen predicted geometry with trainable Gaussian attributes, GT-camera alignment during training, and wider-angle supervision, reporting the best six-dataset mean LPIPS and DISTS among evaluated methods in both pose settings (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction avoids per-scene optimization, but imperfect predicted geometry can cause blur, ghosting, and missing detail under viewpoint changes. The authors identify three obstacles: geometry updates can degrade point positions, predicted training cameras can conflict with GT supervision, and nearby target views insufficiently constrain Gaussian shapes (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Decoupled reconstruction:** Two independently parameterized DA3 branches separate frozen depth/camera prediction from trainable attribute prediction. Gaussian centers remain fixed; opacity, scale, rotation, and color are learned (§3.2).
- **Training changes:** Scale-aligned GT cameras support back-projection and rendering supervision. Target sampling favors larger optical-axis differences, with GT-depth visibility masks restricting supervision (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Learned refinement:** A recurrent KNN Transformer uses input-view rendering gradients to update attributes while preserving centers and Gaussian counts (§3.3).
- **Empirical analysis:** Six-dataset comparisons support perceptual-quality improvements, while an independent GT-depth diagnostic links low learned opacity to geometric inconsistency (§4).

The stated methodological contribution is this reconstruction/training design; DA3, voxel merging, and rendering-gradient refinement have acknowledged antecedents (§§2–3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fixed centers cannot repair severe geometry errors; quality depends on the geometric prior. Voxel merging leaves redundancy, and limited input coverage leaves holes (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Component ablations cover ETH3D and DTU, while opacity analysis covers held-out Hypersim; their generality across the full evaluation remains untested in the supplied evidence.
- **Analyst inference:** Visibility masks restrict target supervision to input-supported regions, so the results do not establish recovery of unseen content (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** “Large-angle” refers to relative optical-axis sampling, not a guaranteed extreme-baseline regime. Reported mean minimum angles are 17.97° for the proposed sampling and 12.34° for local sampling (Appendix A.1, Fig. 8).
- **Evidence gap:** The Adam comparison tests one learning rate and three updates; it does not establish superiority over well-tuned, longer optimization (Appendix B.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can geometry correction help without destabilizing attribute learning?** Fixed centers fail on severe errors (Appendix C). Compare fixed centers with confidence-gated offsets under controlled geometry perturbations.
2. **Does opacity remain a reliable filter outside Hypersim?** The diagnostic uses one validation source (§4.3). Repeat independent depth-based labeling across datasets and opacity thresholds.
3. **How does performance change with actual viewpoint separation and coverage?** Sampling uses orientation differences and valid-region masks (§3.2). Evaluate separately by rotation, translation, overlap, and unseen-region fraction.
4. **What is the quality–cost trade-off of refinement?** The supplied latency table lacks values, and the optimization comparison is short (Appendix B.3–B.5). Compare refinement and tuned Adam under matched runtime budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported relative gains are clear, but quantitative table bodies are absent. Material missing information includes absolute scores, per-dataset and per-input-count results, latency values, evaluation sample counts, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
