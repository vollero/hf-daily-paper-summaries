# MMSearch-OPD: Easy Made Hard, Hard Made Learnable for Long-Horizon Multimodal Search Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22240) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22240)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that refining easy visual questions into longer search tasks and combining RL with selective on-policy distillation raises a 9B agent’s seven-benchmark mean accuracy from 44.9% to 60.2%, including 2.3 percentage points beyond SFT (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon multimodal search requires agents to collect and connect textual and visual evidence over successive tool interactions. The authors identify two training gaps: existing questions often permit short retrieval or answers from model knowledge, while terminal rewards provide weak feedback about intermediate search decisions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controllable question refinement:** Refine extracts entity chains from easy LiveVQA and FVQA questions, then applies **Expand** to append verified hops, **Blur** to replace entity names with identifying clues, and **Visualize** to require retrieved image evidence. Validation and tool-free probes filter invalid questions and shortcuts (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Selective joint RL–OPD:** After SFT, the student receives correctness rewards and behavior penalties. A frozen teacher supplies token-level guidance only on imperfect trajectories; the shaped reward gap weights this guidance, and exponential annealing reduces its influence (§3.4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors assess search performance and data refinement across seven primary benchmarks and three additional benchmarks used in ablations (§§4.1–4.3). The contribution is the proposed refinement and training combination; the supplied evidence does not independently establish every component’s benefit.

Evaluation uses Qwen3.6-35B-A3B to judge each prediction three times and averages those scores (§4.1). The reported evidence supports the full training recipe and, more directly, refined-data SFT under the matched ablation. It does not isolate OPD, reward-gap weighting, annealing, or behavior penalties. Training-batch efficiency trends also do not establish a controlled evaluation-time efficiency gain.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Filtering cannot eliminate all alternative search routes or knowledge shortcuts; teacher and judge errors can introduce noise; changing web content and tool availability limit reproducibility (Limitations).
- **Author-stated scope:** The difficulty measure concerns a designated evidence route, not the globally shortest route, tool-turn count, or success probability (§3.2.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Without RL-only and component ablations, the additional benefit specifically attributable to OPD or its weighting and decay remains uncertain (§§4.2–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text lacks full result-table cells, uncertainty estimates, and repeated-training evidence, limiting assessment of comparison details and robustness.
- **Analyst inference:** Because FVQA and LiveVQA supply training seeds and also appear in evaluation, documented split separation and overlap checks matter; the supplied text does not establish leakage or provide such checks (§§3.2, 4.1, 4.3.1).

## Open research questions
- **Which joint-training components drive improvement?** The SFT comparison bundles RL and OPD. Next experiment: compare RL alone against joint training, then remove reward-gap weighting, annealing, and penalties individually.
- **Does designated difficulty predict unavoidable search difficulty?** The authors acknowledge residual shortcuts. Next experiment: test refined questions with multiple independent solvers and evidence-removal interventions.
- **Do efficiency gains hold on fixed evaluation questions?** Current evidence compares changing training batches. Next experiment: evaluate early and late checkpoints on identical questions, measuring accuracy, tool calls, and generated tokens.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and headline findings, but material information is unavailable: full Tables 1–2 and plotted values; curated dataset sizes and split details; selected refinement parameters; rollout counts; exact behavior penalties; training compute; uncertainty estimates; overlap audits; and code, data, or checkpoint release status.
