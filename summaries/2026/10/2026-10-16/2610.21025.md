# Learning to Read Out: Unembedding Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21025) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21025)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the studied pretraining runs, hidden states encode some task distinctions before the learned readout expresses them, so output-based capability timelines can partly measure readout development (§§4.3–4.5).

## Problem and motivation
Developmental evaluations often date capability acquisition using output accuracy or logits. However, logits depend jointly on hidden states and a readout matrix that also changes during training. Outputs alone therefore cannot distinguish improved representations from improved expression through the readout (§1, Equation 1).

The authors address this measurement ambiguity by tracking readout development separately and comparing task-supervised decodability with native output behavior.

## Approach and contributions

- **Parameter-trajectory crosscoding:** Fit one sparse dictionary across checkpoint readout matrices, using shared token codes and checkpoint-specific decoder directions. This preserves feature indices through training and requires checkpoint weights rather than an activation corpus (§2.1). The novelty is applying and adapting checkpoint crosscoding to readout parameters; shared codes, decoder heads, gating, and the sparsity penalty build on prior methods (§5).
- **Diagnostics separating encoding and expression:** Compare linear probes, native readouts, and alternative checkpoint readouts on fixed hidden states (§2.3).
- **Empirical developmental findings:** Locate early readout reorganization and lexical-category decodability, then identify agreement information available before native expression (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Controlled training intervention:** Change only the readout learning-rate multiplier across matched small-model runs, testing its effect on developmental timing and early loss (§4.5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse dictionaries capture substantial readout variation | Across Pythia-160M/1B/6.9B and OLMo-2-7B, aggregate explained variance is 0.808–0.920, with 1.17–2.26% of features active per row/checkpoint. EV is measured in sample. | §4; Figure C.1; §A.1 |
| Pythia readouts reorganize early | Selected window: steps 512–1.6k, or 0.4–1.1% of training. Circular-shift-null p-values: 0.005 for 160M and 1B; 0.04 for 6.9B. OLMo’s selected window does not separate from nulls, p=0.12. | §4.1; Figure 2 |
| Readout rows develop lexical organization | Pythia-160M median held-out balanced accuracy across 35 WordNet categories rises from 0.499 at initialization to 0.874 finally; all categories meet the authors’ sustained-decodability criterion by step 1000. | §4.2; Figure 3 |
| Agreement information precedes expression | On simple SVA in Pythia-6.9B, the logistic probe approaches ceiling by step 512 while native and best swept intermediate-readout contrast accuracy remain near chance. Exact accuracies are not supplied in the prose. | §4.3; Figure 4a |
| Intermediate readouts improve early next-token prediction | On 173,229 Wikipedia predictions across nine languages, the step-1000 readout reduces Pythia-1B native NLL by 1.503 nats for step-256 states and 0.147 nats for step-512 states. | §§G.1–G.2; Table G.1 |
| A small feature set carries the reconstructed SVA margin | For Pythia-1B step-1000 states under the reconstructed terminal readout, ablating eight selected features changes held-out contrast accuracy from 0.900 to 0.487; preserving only them gives 0.938; matched random-feature ablation gives 0.931. | §4.4; §G.4 |
| Faster readout optimization shifts timing | In 31M runs, multipliers 0.25×, 1×, and 4× yield median feature-peak steps 128, 64, and 32 across all three dictionary fits per run. Halving warmup preserves baseline median timing on the sampled grid. | §4.5; Figure 5 |
| Early loss gains need not persist to the run endpoint | At approximately 0.27B tokens, 4× has validation loss 1.20 nats below baseline and 2.08 below 0.25×; endpoint losses after 10B tokens agree within 0.06 nats. Training stops before learning-rate decay. | §4.5; §§H.1, H.5 |

The evidence supports a readout contribution to early behavioral timing in these settings. Fixed-state swaps isolate readout effects; training interventions additionally change upstream hidden states (§4.5). Probe accuracy establishes supervised decodability, not open-ended generation or proof that the model uses the decoded distinction.

The multilingual aggregate also needs qualification: English supplies only 2.3% of scored targets, and applying the aggregate-optimal step-1000 readout to step-256 English states **increases** English NLL by 0.726 nats (§G.2). Alignment robustness was tested on a smaller corpus subset, rather than the complete aggregate (§G.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope:** Learning-rate interventions use one 31M configuration and one pretraining run per condition. Its readout accounts for about 42% of parameters, versus about 10% in Pythia-1B; larger-model intervention effects remain untested (§6).
- **Measurement boundaries:** Binary contrast accuracy can diverge sharply from full-vocabulary prediction. Linear probes examine only the final hidden state at the answer position (§§F.5, I.2).
- **Temporal and dictionary sensitivity:** Checkpoint spacing limits onset resolution; lifecycle shares and median peaks depend on dictionary width and sparsity (§I.2).
- **Geometry and intervention validity:** Readout geometry and swaps depend on the coordinate scaling shared with final normalization. Sparse localization uses one reconstructed-terminal-readout configuration and does not identify upstream circuits (§§A.1, G.4).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Archived broader-suite LDA runs lack recovered shrinkage, fold-count, and selection details (§A.3). Frequency-class probe folds can share positions from the same document block.
- **Analyst inference:** Repeated dictionary fits demonstrate stability of the analysis conditional on a training run; they do not establish robustness across pretraining seeds (§H.1).
- **Source coverage:** The supplied source is truncated, including substantial appendix material and missing table bodies; complete methodological verification is unavailable.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the learning-rate effect generalize to larger models?** Only one small-model setting was intervened on (§6). Next experiment: repeat matched readout-rate interventions across model sizes and multiple pretraining seeds.
2. **What couples available task directions to native expression?** Some agreement gaps persist, and swaps isolate only one contribution to logit change (§I.3). Next experiment: track direction alignment with denser checkpoints and separately freeze readout or upstream updates.
3. **Can these diagnostics improve early recipe rankings?** The paper proposes this use without validating forecasting accuracy (§6). Next experiment: compare early native, probe, and swap-based rankings against endpoints from longer runs.
4. **Do localized features generalize across readouts and tasks?** Localization uses one configuration, and IOI ablation where IOI is expressed remains untested (§G.4). Next experiment: repeat selection and ablation at native checkpoints with held-out answer tokens.
