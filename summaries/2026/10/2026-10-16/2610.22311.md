# Velox: Spatial Memory Makes World Models Efficient

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22311) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22311)


## One-sentence takeaway
Velox uses training-free, geometry-guided sparse attention to achieve a reported 2.43× late-chunk denoising speedup on LingBot-Fast-Cam while staying within 0.15 API-free WBench points of dense attention (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Chunked video world models retain historical KV tokens to preserve scene consistency, but dense attention becomes slower as rollouts grow. The authors identify a gap between coarse frame-level memory retrieval and the region-specific history different parts of a generated frame need; conditioning-based spatial memory can also require model adaptation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Region-level spatial routing:** Represent historical token blocks as camera frustum slabs with robust depth intervals. Estimate current-block depth by warping recent reconstructed observations, then score historical correspondence using world-space oriented-box overlap (§3.2).
- **Soft geometry–model fusion:** For sufficiently covered regions, geometric overlap boosts each layer’s pooled query–key importance before selection. Uncovered regions retain model-only routing; previous-chunk retention and local neighbors provide continuity safeguards (§3.3).
- **Inference implementation:** Combine global-aligned Gilbert KV storage, a native Triton sparse-attention kernel, and asynchronous geometry construction sharing the denoiser’s GPU. The pretrained generator remains unchanged (§4.1; Appendices C–D).

## Evidence and main results
All numerical results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Late-chunk attention acceleration | At chunk 48, per-call latency falls from approximately 34.2 ms with dense FlashAttention to 7.8 ms: reported 4.36× speedup on one H200. | §4.2; Fig. 3 |
| Late-chunk denoising acceleration | At chunk 48, denoising falls from approximately 7.19 s to 2.96 s: reported 2.43× speedup while geometry construction shares the H200 asynchronously. | §4.2; Fig. 3 |
| Aggregate quality stays near dense attention | Across all 158 WBench navigation cases, Velox is within 0.15 API-free points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam; the 19-metric overall average changes from 75.52 to 75.60. | §4.3; Table 1 discussion |
| Geometry improves the sparse baseline | Against model-only block-sparse routing under the same protocol, API-free average rises from 73.11 to 73.51, overall from 75.01 to 75.60, and spatial consistency from 71.8 to 74.3. | §4.3; Table 1 discussion |
| Quality gains are uneven | Spatial consistency is 74.3 versus dense attention’s 73.0; authors also report lower perspective and subject consistency, without supplying those values in the retrieved text. | §4.5; Appendix D.5 |
| Fusion increases geometric selection recall | On 48 calibration grids from two navigation trajectories, positive-overlap recall in fused top-80 selections rises from 0.602 to 0.852, changing 9.0% of selected blocks on average. | Appendix A.1 |

The evidence supports improved late-rollout efficiency and near-dense aggregate quality in the tested stack. It does not establish full-rollout wall-clock speedup, statistical equivalence, or transfer to other generators and hardware. Geometric selection recall measures routing behavior, not generation quality directly. Table 1’s full cells are absent from the supplied text; its reported results are recoverable only from surrounding prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated geometry failures:** Depth drift, stale depth after disocclusion, and excessive OBB overlap around thin structures can promote misleading history. Coverage gating and soft fusion mitigate these risks without eliminating them (Appendix D.5).
- **Author-stated quality tradeoff:** Perspective and subject consistency decrease despite improved spatial and photometric consistency. The authors call for separate object-centric evaluation (Appendix D.5).
- **Comparison limits:** Published WBench baselines use Doubao, while the three LingBot-based rows use Gemini. Judge-dependent scores are not comparable across these groups; the API-free average excludes those metrics (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—scaling scope:** Bounded attention routes do not imply bounded total memory or routing cost. Historical KV remains available, and pooled importance considers all visible blocks (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference—validation gaps:** The supplied evaluation covers one generator and navigation benchmark. It provides no uncertainty estimates or reported matched-budget random, recent-window, and frame/FOV comparisons; Appendix D.5 proposes these controls.
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** Appendix B.1 states no route-size floor, whereas Appendix D.4 describes flooring the remaining selection budget to avoid empty older-history routes. The exact interaction is unclear.

## Open research questions
- **Does geometry beat simpler selection rules at equal cost?** Model-only routing is tested, but other proposed controls lack results. Compare random, recent-window, frame/FOV, and Velox routes at matched materialized route sizes and measured latency (Appendix D.5).
- **How robust is routing to geometric error and delayed updates?** The authors identify stale depth and reconstruction drift as risks. Sweep pose/depth perturbations and geometry freshness lag, measuring quality separately on disocclusions and revisits (Appendices C and D.5).
- **Can object consistency improve without losing efficiency?** Subject and perspective consistency regress. Test object-aware retention or confidence gating on object-centric cases alongside background revisits (Appendix D.5).
- **How far does the efficiency benefit generalize?** Measurements stop at the reported rollout and hardware setting. Evaluate longer histories, additional generators, and other GPUs with total wall-clock time, routing overhead, and peak memory reported (§4.2; Appendix B.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied method and headline results are detailed, but full numerical Tables 1–2 and timing plots are unavailable. Material gaps include uncertainty estimates, complete per-metric scores, total rollout wall-clock time, peak memory, isolated component ablations, several exact runtime settings, and confirmed code/checkpoint availability.
