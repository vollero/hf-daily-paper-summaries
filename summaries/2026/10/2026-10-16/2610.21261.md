# Behavioral Compositionality of Sparse Autoencoder Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21261) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21261)


## One-sentence takeaway
Learned SAE coordinates yield more additive downstream responses than matched random rotations, but pairwise interactions improve held-out prediction across every main model–behavior setting tested (§§4.2–4.4).

## Problem and motivation
A linear SAE decoder makes feature contributions additive in activation space, but subsequent model computation can make their behavioral effects interact. This matters when combining features into explanations, circuits, or steering interventions: knowing individual effects may not predict their joint effect. The authors identify a gap in evaluations centered on reconstruction, sparsity, and interpretability, which do not directly measure this behavioral compositionality (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled interventions and SAR:** Retain sampled subsets of selected decoded feature contributions while keeping reconstruction residuals and unselected activation content fixed. SAE Additivity Residuals measure behavioral variance unexplained by the best approximation up to a specified interaction order (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Theoretical characterization:** Under independent Bernoulli feature inclusion, population SAR equals normalized interaction energy above that order. It also bounds behavior-weighted coalition ordering error for the optimal approximation (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Coordinate comparison:** Sum-preserving orthogonal rotations retain the selected subspace, total contribution, and downstream model, enabling comparison with learned SAE coordinates (§§3.1, 4.2).
- **Empirical interaction analysis:** Compare individual-effect and pairwise models, parameter-matched random third-order controls, combined steering responses, and reconstruction quality (§§4.4–4.7).

## Evidence and main results

Main subset experiments use Gemma-3 4B, Mistral-7B, and Llama-3.1 8B across six behaviors, selecting the top 16 active features and sampling 384 coalitions per prompt, split into 256 fitting and 128 evaluation coalitions (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| SAR has an exact population interpretation and ordering guarantee | Under independent Bernoulli inclusion, SAR equals interaction energy above the chosen order; optimal low-order behavior-weighted ordering error is bounded by SAR. | §3.2, Theorem 1; §3.3, Theorem 2 |
| Learned coordinates are more additive than tested rotations | Lower median estimated first-order SAR in all 18 main settings. For Mistral subject–verb agreement, SAE versus rotation medians are 0.0218 versus 0.1196; comparison uses 100 prompts and eight rotations per prompt. | §4.2; Figure 3; Appendix F |
| Pairwise models improve coalition prediction | Across 18 settings, average rank fidelity rises from 0.9782 to 0.9959 and average selection regret falls from 0.0272 to 0.0051. Rank and behavior-weighted ordering improvements persist across all 20 repeated splits. | §4.4; Figure 5; Appendix B.3 |
| Pairwise gains exceed the tested capacity control | Pairwise fits outperform the median parameter-matched random third-order model in rank fidelity and behavior-weighted ordering error in all 18 settings; each prompt has 50 control fits. | §4.4; Appendix H.4 |
| Pairwise modeling improves aggregate steering prediction | For Llama-3.1 8B, 16 fixed CorrSteer features, 1,024 combinations, and 1,400 MMLU-Pro questions: rank fidelity rises 0.5874 → 0.7790; ordering error falls 0.1444 → 0.0606; estimated residual falls 0.6424 → 0.3891. | §4.6; Appendix D.2 |
| Depth trends partly reflect downstream computation | First-order residual decreases in eight of nine early-to-late comparisons for both SAE coordinates and rotations; learned coordinates remain lower in all 33 matched model–behavior–layer comparisons. | §4.5; Figure 6 |
| Reconstruction and compositionality capture different variation | Across six SAEs at a fixed Gemma-3 1B site, pooled SAR correlates positively with sign error and regret across three behaviors; reconstruction FVU shows negative or near-zero associations. | §4.7; Appendix H.6 |

These results support comparatively low-order behavioral descriptions in learned SAE coordinates under the tested interventions. They do not establish independent feature effects, globally additive dictionaries, or increased task accuracy from pairwise modeling: the steering result concerns prediction of coalition responses. The empirical residual estimates also include fitting error, unlike population SAR (§3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Conclusions depend on selected contributions, behavioral readout, and intervention distribution, rather than being global SAE properties (§5; Appendix C.4).
- **Author-stated estimation limits:** Absolute residual magnitudes are less stable than ranking conclusions. The authors explicitly avoid treating fitted second-order residuals as high-precision population estimates (Appendix B.3).
- **Author-stated steering heterogeneity:** Domain effects vary, and pairwise held-out residuals can exceed first-order residuals in some domains (§4.6; Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Rotation controls establish an advantage over the sampled rotations, not optimality among alternative representations. Matched third-order controls test randomly chosen terms, not an optimized higher-order model (§4.2; Appendix H.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Shared depth trends across rotations and SAE coordinates limit attribution of improving additivity solely to representation quality (§4.5).

## Open research questions
- **Can population interaction energy be estimated more precisely?** Residual estimates remain sensitive to fitting budget and coalition draws. Next experiment: increase both budgets and compare estimates with exhaustive evaluation on smaller feature sets (Appendix B.3).
- **Does the coordinate advantage survive stronger alternatives?** Existing controls use sampled rotations. Next experiment: compare learned coordinates against rotations optimized for low held-out SAR (§4.2).
- **What explains steering-domain heterogeneity?** Aggregate improvement does not resolve domain-specific failures. Next experiment: compare domain-level learning curves as coalition fitting budgets increase (§4.6; Appendix D.2).
