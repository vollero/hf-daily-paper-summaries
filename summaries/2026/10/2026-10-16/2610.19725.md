# TimeRSI: Towards Self-Improved Agentic Deployment for Time-Series Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19725) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19725)


## One-sentence takeaway
TimeRSI improves forecasting around frozen foundation models through feedback-guided code revision and adaptive pipeline selection, reporting modest aggregate gains across two benchmarks without updating backbone weights (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Strong pretrained forecasters still require task-specific choices about inputs, calendar features, context, and output corrections. The authors identify two deployment gaps: plausible changes may hurt accuracy, and a pipeline that works well on average may fail under changing conditions. Manual pipeline development requires substantial expertise (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Feedback-guided pipeline development:** A coding agent edits preprocessing, feature construction, context selection, and post-processing. Earlier historical windows provide execution feedback; a separate later block ranks candidates (§2; Appendix B).
- **Adaptive deployment selection:** For each target and forecast origin, a selector compares retained pipelines on similar completed windows. Pipeline code stays fixed during deployment, although auxiliary predictors can be refitted using available history (§2; Appendices B–C).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors evaluate task-adapted deployment across TIME and FEV-bench, compare iterative revision against non-iterative search, and test alternative agents and backbones (§3). The contribution is the deployment framework, rather than a new forecasting backbone.

These results support improved forecasting through task-specific deployment changes and support feedback-guided revision over the reported equal-candidate alternative. They do not establish superiority at equal compute cost: external baseline scores use published configurations. TIME accepted the entry, while the FEV-bench result was still a pending submission in the reported comparison (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Clear task and variable descriptions are required; historical gains do not guarantee future gains under distribution shift (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Public leaderboard comparisons use different configurations and no common compute budget. The local same-backbone comparison more directly isolates deployment gains (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The supplied ablation addresses iterative revision, but provides no separate ablation isolating adaptive selection from a fixed best pipeline (§3).
- **Analyst inference—cost and reproducibility:** Every retained pipeline runs at each deployment origin (§2). Search limits are specified, but realized runtime, cost, and deployment overhead are not reported in the supplied text. The candidate archive also includes programs developed with another agent, complicating attribution to the default agent alone (Appendix E).
- **Evidence availability:** Most referenced tables appear only as captions in the retrieved text. Statistical uncertainty and repeated-run variability are not provided.

## Open research questions
1. **Does adaptive selection outperform a fixed selected pipeline?** Its independent contribution is not isolated. Compare identical candidate archives using nearest-neighbor selection, a fixed historical winner, and the reference pipeline.
2. **How robust is the system to distribution shift?** The authors explicitly leave future generalization unresolved. Evaluate chronological regime changes and measure selector recovery after outcomes become available.
3. **How much do task descriptions affect gains?** Clear descriptions are a stated dependency. Repeat searches with complete, abbreviated, missing, and deliberately ambiguous metadata.
4. **Are improvements cost-effective and repeatable?** Reported budgets do not establish realized cost or variance. Repeat searches across seeds and budgets, measuring accuracy, execution cost, and forecast latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied method and aggregate results are clear, but most full table contents are missing. Material unavailable information includes numerical ablation scores, uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variability, realized computational costs, an isolated selector ablation, and complete candidate-archive provenance.
