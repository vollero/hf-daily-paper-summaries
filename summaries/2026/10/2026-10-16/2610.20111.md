# RepForcing: Representation-Space Initialization for Autoregressive Video Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20111) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20111)


## One-sentence takeaway
RepForcing replaces intermediate autoregressive teacher training and trajectory distillation with frozen-video-feature supervision, improving reported four-step video quality after unchanged DMD refinement, although baseline comparisons are not controlled retrainings (§§4.2, 5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Few-step autoregressive video diffusion reduces repeated denoising costs, but initialization must accommodate causal generation while preserving detail. Intermediate causal teachers provide compatible trajectory targets, yet the authors observe blurred or unresolved detail at short sampling schedules (§1; Fig. 2).

Their identified gap is the supervision objective: causal compatibility alone does not ensure good four-step generation. Latent squared-error regression favors conditional-mean clean predictions, potentially blending plausible continuations (§4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Initialization method:** Train a causal student under teacher forcing, comparing predicted clean video latents with synthetic targets through token-wise cosine distance in a frozen video transformer. Gradients pass through the extractor; no latent MSE or adversarial loss is added (§4.2).
- **Simplified pipeline:** Reuse approximately 3K bidirectional-Wan-generated videos from VidProM prompts, replacing intermediate AR teacher training and causal ODE distillation with one initialization phase. Subsequent asymmetric DMD remains unchanged (§§3, 4.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Analysis and representation selection:** Establish the posterior-mean optimum for direct latent regression and test alternative feature extractors and depths. The default uses the eighth block of frozen Wan2.1-14B on clean latents (§4.1; Table 2; Appendix B.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Latent regression favors conditional means | At fixed positive noise time, finite second moment and nondegenerate conditional targets imply a conditional-mean optimum; this concerns direct predictions, not complete sampler outputs. | §4.1, Proposition 1; Appendix C.1 |
| Better initialization detail is reported | Before refinement, the same-prompt comparison at 4, 8, and 12 steps shows the largest visible advantage at four steps; no numerical initialization scores are supplied. | Fig. 2 |
| Chunk-wise aggregate quality improves | After DMD, four-step RepForcing scores **84.66 vs 84.04** VBench Total and **10.074 vs 6.326** VisionReward against reported Causal Forcing: **+0.62 points** and **+59.25%**. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Frame-wise gains also appear | After DMD, four-step RepForcing scores **84.38 vs 84.10** Total and **10.598 vs 6.798** VisionReward against reported Causal Forcing++; Instruction Following is **80 vs 47**. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| Gains are not uniform | RepForcing Semantic is **81.10 vs 81.84** for chunk-wise Causal Forcing and **79.21 vs 81.37** frame-wise; chunk-wise Dynamic Degree is **69 vs 71** for Causal-rCM. | Table 1 |
| Extractor choice matters | Matched chunk-wise four-step, post-DMD ablation: Wan features score **84.66** Total versus **83.08** for InternVid and **82.82** for DINOv2. | §5.3; Table 2 |
| Intermediate extraction performs best among tested depths | Matched chunk-wise four-step, post-DMD Total scores: block 5 **84.49**, block 8 **84.66**, block 20 **84.58**, block 30 **84.44**. | §5.3; Table 2 |

Evaluation uses 81-frame videos at 832 × 480. VBench uses standard prompts; standalone Dynamic Degree, VisionReward, and Instruction Following use the released 100-prompt motion set and are scaled by 100 (§5.1).

The evidence supports improved reported aggregate quality and reward scores, plus an effect of representation choice under matched ablations. It does not establish superiority on every metric, statistical significance, or an isolated initialization advantage over fully retrained competing pipelines. Instruction Following is an automated VisionReward component, not a human-study score (Appendix B.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated comparison boundary:** Main baseline scores come from published reports or pretrained-weight evaluations, rather than controlled retraining (§5.2; Table 1 caption).
- **Author-stated theoretical boundary:** The posterior-mean result does not characterize full sampling or finite-set memorization. Representation regression need not select a unique video and does not characterize the actual causal student’s blockwise optimum (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond the tested Wan setup, synthetic training data, and fixed evaluation length remains unestablished (§§3, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Removing training stages does not establish lower total compute: no training-time, memory, or cost comparison is supplied, and initialization backpropagates through a frozen extractor (§4.2).
- **Missing evidence:** Initialization comparisons are qualitative; uncertainty estimates, repeated-run results, and a human preference study are absent from the supplied text. Appendix D promises complete prompts but contains no prompt text.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the advantage persist under matched baseline retraining?** Main comparisons use external results (§5.2). Retrain causal-ODE and RepForcing pipelines with shared data, budgets, evaluation seeds, and repeated runs.
2. **What explains the extractor advantage?** Extractor ablations do not isolate temporal modeling (§5.3). Compare temporally restricted and full-video features within the same backbone and depth.
3. **Does initialization reduce total training cost?** Stage removal is demonstrated structurally, without resource measurements (§4.2). Measure end-to-end accelerator time, peak memory, and quality at matched compute.
4. **Does quality hold over longer rollouts?** Evaluation covers fixed-length clips (§5.1). Test increasing generation horizons and measure accumulated appearance, motion, and prompt-alignment errors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported scores are clearly specified, but causal attribution and generality remain limited.

Material missing information includes exact training budgets and optimizer settings, sampling timestep values, repeated-run uncertainty, quantitative pre-DMD comparisons, measured resource savings, human evaluations, longer-horizon results, and the promised qualitative prompt text.
