# Diminishing Returns of Data in Sparse Autoencoders: Functional Behavior does not Guarantee Feature Reproducibility

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20680) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20680)


## One-sentence takeaway
SAEs trained with much less activation data often approach a large-data reference on functional metrics, while strict feature reproducibility remains substantially lower and improves with additional data (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
SAEs aim to expose interpretable features in language-model activations, but commonly use hundreds of millions of activation tokens without systematic evidence that this scale is necessary. The authors distinguish preserving reconstruction and downstream behavior from recovering the same individual features—a distinction that matters when latents support scientific interpretations (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled data-budget study:** Train ReLU, BatchTopK, JumpReLU, and Matryoshka BatchTopK SAEs on online FineWeb activations from Gemma-2-2B, Pythia-160M, and Qwen3-8B, comparing smaller activation budgets with a 500M-token reference (§4).
- **Separate convergence criteria:** Compare SAEBench functional evaluations with independent encoder and decoder Hungarian matching. Strict recovery requires both matchings to choose the same reference latent and both cosine similarities to exceed a threshold (§§4, 6).
- **Matched-budget selection study:** Compare random sampling with farthest-point sampling (FPS), finding different tradeoffs between reconstruction, interventions, and automated interpretability (§5).
- **Conditional theoretical characterization:** Establish asymptotic feature alignment under a common population objective, statistical consistency, successful optimization, and feature identifiability; distinguish assignment agreement from strong directional recovery (§3).

The contribution is an empirical and theoretical study using existing SAE architectures, selectors, and evaluations, rather than a new SAE architecture.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reconstruction approaches the reference early | Gemma-2-2B, BatchTopK, 16K dictionary, trainer 0: random-sampled loss recovered is **0.979 at 5M**, **0.985 at 25M and 50M**, versus **0.987 at 500M** activation tokens. | Appendix A, Table 6 |
| Functional metrics need not improve monotonically | In the same configuration, AutoInterp is **0.815, 0.833, 0.835, 0.821** at **5M, 25M, 50M, 500M** tokens, respectively. | Appendix A, Table 6 |
| Counterpart agreement greatly exceeds strict recovery | Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK at **5M** tokens versus its **500M** reference: mean matched decoder cosine **0.454**, encoder–decoder assignment agreement **66.61%**, but strict shared features only **2.20%**, requiring both cosines **>0.7** and the same counterpart. | §6; Table 2 discussion |
| More data strengthens strict correspondence | For Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK, assignment agreement increases from **66.61% at 5M** to **83.47% at 50M**; strict shared-feature recovery increases from **2.20% to 28.86%**, against the 500M reference. | §6; Table 2 discussion |
| Selection trades reconstruction against AutoInterp | Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK, 16K, trainer 0, **5M** tokens: random/FPS loss recovered **0.979/0.951**, AutoInterp **0.815/0.819**; observed L0 also differs, **79.8/50.9**. | Appendix A, Table 6 |
| FPS frequently improves AutoInterp | Authors report FPS outperforming random sampling in **79%** of matched AutoInterp comparisons across architectures, models, and budgets; the comparison count is unavailable. | §5 |
| Feature alignment requires stronger assumptions than risk consistency | The theorem gives asymptotic encoder and decoder alignment for fixed retained fraction, conditional on optimization consistency and identifiable, separated feature systems as well as statistical consistency. | §3, Theorem 1 |

These results support distinct functional and feature-level data requirements in the tested settings. They do not establish universal budget sufficiency, recovery of a uniquely correct dictionary, or measured end-to-end compute savings. Matching the 500M reference measures correspondence to that trained SAE.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Conclusions inherit SAEBench’s implementation choices, assumptions, and metric limitations (§7).
- **Coverage inconsistency:** The authors say RAVEL and unlearning are supported only for Gemma, yet Pythia tables include “RAVEL Disent.” values. Their validity is unresolved in the supplied text (§7; Tables 12–19).
- **Analyst inference—training-budget ambiguity:** Section 4 says optimization steps remain fixed while retained activations decrease and are used once. With Appendix A specifying a fixed batch size, the exact accounting of steps, buffers, and activation exposure needs clarification.
- **Analyst inference—sparsity confounding:** Fixed sparsity hyperparameters do not ensure equal observed sparsity. FPS changes L0 substantially in some comparisons, complicating attribution of metric differences solely to diversity (Table 6).
- **Analyst inference—conditional theory:** The theorem does not demonstrate that identifiability or optimization consistency holds for the trained SAEs (§3).
- **Evidence gaps:** Several tables have captions without numerical bodies, and some appendix cells are malformed. Seed replication and uncertainty for the central feature-recovery percentages are not established.

## Open research questions
- **How much disagreement comes from data quantity versus training randomness?** Seed variation is discussed as prior work, but replicated budget comparisons are not established here. Next experiment: cross multiple seeds and activation subsets at each budget, including multiple full-data references.
- **Does FPS help AutoInterp after controlling for sparsity and activation norm?** FPS changes both selected norms and observed L0. Next experiment: compare norm-controlled selectors with matched achieved sparsity.
- **Can stable subspaces coexist with poor one-to-one recovery across budgets?** The paper evaluates strict feature correspondence but does not report budget-dependent subspace recovery. Next experiment: measure subspace similarity alongside Hungarian matching.
- **What budget minimizes actual cost for a specified quality target?** Activation-budget reductions are reported without end-to-end timing or compute measurements. Next experiment: account for activation generation, selection, and training costs while enforcing functional and reproducibility targets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central distinction has explicit quantitative support, but broader claims are harder to audit.

Material missing information includes numerical bodies for Tables 1–3 and 24–26, seed counts and uncertainty, paired-test outcomes, exact optimization-step accounting across budgets, selection overhead and runtime, and clarification of the Pythia RAVEL entries.
