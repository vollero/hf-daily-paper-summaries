# H-DDCM: Hadamard Codebooks for Fast Generative Image and Video Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18125) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18125)


## One-sentence takeaway
H-DDCM replaces dense Gaussian codebooks with implicit randomized Hadamard dictionaries, retaining competitive generative compression quality while reducing dictionary overhead and enabling faster streaming video compression in the evaluated settings.

## Problem and motivation
Training-free generative codecs compress images and videos by steering a frozen generator’s sampling trajectory. Turbo-DDCM accelerates this process by combining several Gaussian codebook atoms per step, but dense dictionaries and precomputed combinatorial coding tables become expensive at large latent dimensions. Restricting dictionary size reduces cost at the expense of reconstruction quality; H-DDCM targets this scaling bottleneck (Secs. 1, 3.2; App. F).

## Approach and contributions
- **Implicit structured dictionaries:** Seeded random sign modulation and fast Walsh–Hadamard transforms replace dense dictionary projection and reconstruction. Single-basis thresholding exactly solves the stated sparse approximation problem in the orthogonal, padded domain (Sec. 4.1; App. G).
- **Scalable bitstream coding:** Table-free recurrence and hockey-stick formulations encode unordered atom selections. Grouping bounds individual combinatorial operations, with some rate overhead (Sec. 4.2; App. E).
- **Overcomplete extension:** Multiple randomized bases provide more candidate directions. Selection becomes heuristic because atoms across bases are not mutually orthogonal; comparisons use approximately matched bit budgets (Sec. 4.3; Apps. G, C.5).
- **Streaming video adaptation:** A Causal-Forcing codec refreshes its KV cache using recently reconstructed frames, requiring no additional transmitted context bits. A source-mean proxy initializes noise selection, and a larger selection budget supports the first chunk after refresh (Sec. 6; Apps. B, D).

The evidence supports reduced dictionary and coding overhead without an obvious quality penalty in the tested settings. It does not establish universal quality dominance: classical and trained video codecs achieve higher PSNR, while the authors report stronger perceptual metrics for H-DDCM. Throughput leadership applies to the evaluated CF-based implementations, not all video codecs (Sec. 6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated mathematical scope:** Exact thresholding optimality applies to an orthogonal padded-domain objective. Cropping destroys column orthogonality, and combining bases also makes selection heuristic (Sec. 4.3; App. G).
- **Author-reported trade-offs:** Overcomplete dictionaries require more transforms and index bits. Grouping introduces rate overhead; rate matching estimates balanced basis allocations before actual selections are known (Secs. 4.2–4.3; App. G).
- **Comparison risk:** CF versions of ZeroGVC and Turbo-DDCM are author implementations because official CF implementations were unavailable. Improved bitstream coding is shared with applicable baselines, helping isolate other overhead (Sec. 6; App. B).
- **Analyst inference—limited deployment evidence:** Video evaluation uses one backbone, one operating resolution, and an H200. “Close to real-time” therefore does not establish performance on less powerful hardware or arbitrary stream durations.
- **Metric validity:** Removing first-step initialization improves some aggregate metrics but reportedly worsens temporal alignment, exposing a mismatch between those metrics and the illustrated behavior (App. C.7).
- **Reproducibility:** Detailed configurations and pseudocode are supplied, but runnable code availability is not established. PSC is excluded from runtime comparisons because it is slow (Sec. 5).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is cache refresh over much longer streams?** Evaluated duration limits and drift statistics are not specified. Test increasingly long sequences and measure boundary errors, temporal alignment, and accumulated drift.
- **What is the best dictionary-size/throughput trade-off?** Quality ablations cover a limited set of basis counts. Sweep basis count at matched actual bitrate while measuring quality, latency, and memory.
- **Can evaluation better capture temporal alignment?** Initialization ablations expose metric–visual disagreement. Add explicit alignment measurements and blinded human comparisons to the same ablation.
- **How portable are the gains?** Video evidence centers on CF and H200 hardware. Repeat matched-backbone comparisons with another causal generator and less powerful GPUs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method summary and explicitly reported comparisons; **medium** in broader efficiency and deployment conclusions.

Material missing information includes machine-readable quality/throughput curves, exact bitrates for headline timing comparisons, tested stream durations, seed variability, human perceptual evaluation, implementation release status, and performance beyond the evaluated hardware and backbones.
