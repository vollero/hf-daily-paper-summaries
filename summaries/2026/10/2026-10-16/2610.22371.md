# Gen4P: Video Generators are 4D Perceivers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22371) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22371)


## One-sentence takeaway
Gen4P adapts a pretrained video generator into a single-pass, query-based 4D predictor, achieving strong dynamic tracking and Sintel results while using a frozen depth adapter to improve geometry.

## Problem and motivation
Recovering scene geometry, camera motion, and moving-point trajectories from monocular video requires both geometric and temporal understanding. The authors argue that geometry foundation models provide limited explicit motion priors, while previous generative approaches often use task-specific designs, iterative denoising, or frame-aligned supervision that restricts output flexibility (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Video generator as a perception encoder:** Gen4P passes clean video latents through frozen Wan2.1 components adapted with trainable 4D-LoRA, extracting shared scene tokens without iterative denoising (§3.2.1).
- **Flexible query decoding:** Building on D4RT’s existing on-demand formulation, a cross-attention decoder predicts point positions and auxiliary attributes. The new temporal parameterization interpolates learned start/end embeddings using normalized clip time, supporting variable sequence lengths (§§3.1–3.2.2).
- **Frozen geometry adapter:** The double-LoRA variant, **Ours-II**, adds a frozen DVD depth-LoRA alongside the trainable 4D-LoRA, retaining the same trainable parameter count (§3.3).
- **Unified evaluation:** The paper evaluates world-coordinate tracking, video depth, and camera poses, with qualitative reconstruction examples (§4; Appendix A).

The network predicts point attributes directly; complete camera trajectories additionally require geometric fitting, pose-graph optimization, and chunk alignment (§D.3).

These results support effective transfer of video-generator features to several geometric tasks, especially dynamic tracking. They do not establish universal superiority, metric-scale reconstruction without alignment, or an end-to-end runtime advantage.

Two narrative claims need qualification: Table 2 does not support the text’s “second best” inlier-ratio claim for both Bonn and KITTI, and Table 4(b,e) does not show improvement on every metric when adapting the depth-LoRA instead of the basic 4D-LoRA.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged geometry weakness:** Sparse query supervision provides weaker geometric learning signals than dense geometric pretraining, motivating the depth adapter (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—alignment limits interpretation:** Tracking uses GT scale alignment, depth uses GT scale-and-shift alignment, and camera evaluation uses GT Sim(3) alignment. Scores therefore do not establish absolute metric accuracy (§D).
- **Analyst inference—single-pass scope:** Shared features require one encoder pass per clip, but camera inference includes nonlinear optimization and trajectory fusion. No measured latency or peak-memory comparison is supplied (§D.3).
- **Analyst inference—training distribution matters:** Excluding ADT training data substantially changes held-out ADT performance. The authors explicitly exclude WorldTrack test sequences, but this remains within-dataset generalization rather than wholly unseen-domain evaluation (§B.3; Tables A2–A3).
- **Analyst inference—bounded flexibility evidence:** Length and resolution robustness are demonstrated over finite tested ranges; the text does not establish indefinite temporal scaling or recovery of additional spatial detail merely by increasing query resolution (Fig. 5).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and reproducibility:** No repeated-run variability or statistical significance is reported. Training settings are detailed, but a release of Gen4P-specific code and checkpoints is not established (§4.1; reproducibility statement, p. 11).

## Open research questions
- **How much benefit comes specifically from generative pretraining?** The supplied ablations compare adaptation strategies, without a matched alternative-pretraining control. Train the same query architecture with matched data and compute across encoder initializations.
- **Does temporal flexibility extend to long, irregularly sampled videos?** Normalized timestamps and finite length tests leave broader temporal behavior unresolved. Evaluate longer clips, dropped frames, and varying frame rates while measuring tracking error and memory.
- **When does the depth prior help or hurt?** It improves all main tracking metrics but slightly worsens Sintel depth relative to Ours. Compare both variants by motion, scene type, and depth range.
- **What is the practical efficiency advantage?** Single-pass encoding does not quantify complete inference costs. Benchmark latency and peak memory across clip lengths and query counts, including camera optimization and chunk fusion.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the architecture and tabulated findings; **medium** for broad generalization and efficiency claims.

Material missing information includes Gen4P release status, measured runtime and memory, training duration, repeated-run uncertainty, exact values behind plotted trends, and matched controls isolating the contribution of generative pretraining.
