# EdgeWise: Bringing Long-Horizon Agents to the Edge via Selective Cloud Delegation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17560) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17560)


## One-sentence takeaway
EdgeWise trains a local coding agent to delegate selected steps to cloud models, substantially lowering reported cloud API costs while matching or approaching cloud-only performance, especially under Pass@3 evaluation (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Using a frontier cloud model for every step of a long coding trajectory is expensive, although individual steps differ in difficulty. The authors identify two gaps in prior routing work: limited evaluation on long-horizon agent tasks and dependence on separate routers tied to predefined model pools (§1).

Their motivating experiment finds that some random allocations of cloud calls preserve cloud-only performance with much less cloud involvement, suggesting that delegation placement matters (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Step-level collaboration:** A local SLM normally interacts with the environment; a cloud LLM executes individual delegated steps before control returns locally (§3).
- **Two delegation mechanisms:** A training-free external judge identifies important steps, optionally using a score threshold to adjust cloud usage. Alternatively, the SLM learns to delegate without an additional inference-time decision model (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Delegation-specific training:** SFT teaches the delegation format; RL combines task correctness, a successful-trajectory cloud-call regularizer, and critic-based credit for individual delegation decisions. Cloud-generated tokens are excluded from policy loss (§3.2.2).
- **Empirical design analysis:** The paper studies action types, transferred context, delegation frequency, harness transfer, and workstation deployment. Write/Edit cloud coverage has the strongest measured association with success (§§3.1, 4; Appendices C, G).

The trained EdgeWise-9B uses Qwen3.5-9B, SWE-Smith training data, and Kimi-K3 during training; evaluation substitutes GPT-5.5 or Opus 4.8 as the cloud model (§§4.1–4.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Delegation placement matters | With Qwen3.5-9B and GPT-5.5 xhigh, some random allocations at roughly 40% cloud involvement reach cloud-only performance with at least 25% lower inference cost on the studied coding benchmarks. | §2; Figure 2 |
| Write/Edit coverage correlates most strongly with success | Across 2.4k trajectories, task-and-budget-centered correlation is 0.200, with 95% CI [0.099, 0.297]. | §3.1; Figure 3 |
| GPT-based self-delegation matches Terminal-Bench Pass@3 | Pass@3: 82.02% for both methods; reported cost falls from $279.73 cloud-only to $181.88 with self-delegation. Pass@1 falls from 74.16% to 71.91%. | Table 1 |
| GPT-based self-delegation improves SWE-ReBench results | Pass@3 rises from 63.64% to 66.36%, while reported cost falls from $598.08 to $283.02; Pass@1 rises from 56.36% to 58.18%. | Table 1 |
| Largest reported savings accompany some accuracy loss | With Opus 4.8 on SWE-ReBench, Pass@3 cost falls 62.14%, from $584.70 to $221.39, while accuracy falls from 69.09% to 66.36%. | Table 1 |
| Step-level credit improves accuracy and reduces calls, but increases dollars slightly | On Terminal-Bench with GPT-5.5 xhigh, adding token advantage raises Pass@1 from 65.17% to 71.91% and lowers average delegations from 8.97 to 5.80; cost rises from $55.05 to $56.96. | Appendix B.2; Table 3 |
| Transfer across harnesses preserves much of performance | On Terminal-Bench with GPT-5.5, unseen-harness Pass@3 declines by 2.25–3.38 percentage points versus cloud-only, with reported cost reductions of 25.09–46.32%. | Appendix C.4; Table 7 |
| Workstation deployment is feasible in the reported setting | DGX Spark achieves qualitatively comparable Pass@3 to A100 evaluation and approximately 50% lower API cost than cloud-only. Terminal-Bench mean/median task times are 11.72/8.30 minutes. | §4.4; Figure 6(d); Appendix G.2 |

These results support a useful accuracy–cloud-cost trade-off on the evaluated coding tasks. They do not establish universal accuracy parity, total-system cost savings, or a causal advantage for delegating every Write/Edit step. The authors’ explanation that collaboration reduces overthinking and improves exploration diversity remains a hypothesis (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Deep research and computer-use agents are unevaluated; broader applicability remains hypothetical (Appendix A).
- **Cost boundary:** Reported costs exclude local SLM computation. The RL regularizer counts cloud calls rather than directly optimizing monetary cost, and Table 3 demonstrates that fewer calls can cost more (§§3.2.2, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain parity:** Main-table uncertainty estimates are unavailable, so small accuracy differences cannot establish statistical equivalence or superiority.
- **Analyst inference—correlation versus causation:** The centered action-type analysis controls task and delegation budget, but remains observational (§3.1).
- **Implementation ambiguity:** §3.2.2 assigns no extra advantage when local execution is sufficient, whereas Equation 3 assigns positive advantage whenever SLM and critic agree, apparently including local/local agreement. This affects reproducibility.
- **Reporting gaps:** Several figure results are described without numerical values in the supplied text; some appendix captions and cross-references are inconsistent. The supplied text does not establish code, checkpoint, or training-data release availability.

## Open research questions
1. **Does selective delegation transfer beyond coding?** Appendix A leaves this untested. A next experiment could evaluate deep-research and computer-use tasks with task-specific correctness checks.
2. **Does it reduce total deployment cost or latency?** Local computation is excluded from cost, and runtime lacks a cloud-only comparison. Measure API charges, local energy, hardware costs, and completion time under matched conditions.
3. **How much does success depend on the critic’s criteria?** Existing ablations remove step-level advantage but do not establish whether Write/Edit-focused supervision is optimal. Compare alternative critics and criteria under matched training budgets.
4. **Can delegation enforce a hard budget?** Appendix F states that thresholds only indirectly control usage. Test a policy conditioned on remaining token or dollar budget against threshold delegation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Quantitative tables substantiate coding-task API savings, but broader conclusions are limited.

Material missing information includes main-result confidence intervals, exact numerical context and deployment comparisons, complete delegation prompts and training hyperparameters, resolution of the advantage-rule ambiguity, total-system cost comparisons, and confirmed artifact availability.
