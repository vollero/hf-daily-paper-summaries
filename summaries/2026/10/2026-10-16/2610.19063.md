# WorldMemBench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Memory of Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19063) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19063)


## One-sentence takeaway

WorldMemBench evaluates static retention and off-screen event evolution through controlled camera revisits, finding that strong initial recall does not ensure persistent memory or reliable dynamic behavior across nine video world models (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation

Interactive video worlds must preserve previously observed people, objects, and environments while allowing unseen events to progress coherently. Visual quality and local temporal consistency do not establish these capabilities.

The authors identify a need to jointly evaluate fine-grained static memory, dynamic state evolution, and reliability across repeated revisits and changes in exploration direction, alongside camera accuracy (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** 260 cases across 23 categories: 200 static cases covering humans, objects, and environments, plus 60 dynamic cases covering seven state-transition mechanisms (§3.2).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Controlled translation loops and rotation trajectories create departures and repeated returns. Static revisits use fixed initial references; dynamic cases use expected state transitions and eight binary checklist questions, gated on visible event initiation (§§3.3, 4.1, 4.4–4.5).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Appearance, silhouette, depth, and reconstruction metrics assess static retention; video quality and camera-following errors are reported separately. Experiments compare nine models and three training-free context-access policies on one fixed model (§§4–5; Appendix H).

The contribution is primarily a benchmark and diagnostic framework; the context-access study evaluates existing policy families rather than establishing a new world-model architecture.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Static leadership does not imply strong end-to-end dynamic performance | Among nine models, HY-WorldPlay leads all seven static metrics. On 60 dynamic cases, it starts 19 events (31.7%), with conditional checklist score 0.928 and gated Overall 0.294; LingBot World starts 56, scoring 0.866 conditionally and 0.808 overall. | §5.2; Appendix D.2, Table 5 discussion |
| Repeated returns expose degradation | Under LRLRLR, nine-model mean scores fall from first to third return by 0.229 for Object Appearance, 0.217 for Human Identity, and 0.205 for Object Geometry. | §5.3, Fig. 5 discussion |
| Degradation survives departure/return filtering | Across 431 Environment model–case units from nine models, requiring departure ≥30° and return error ≤30° at all compared returns, Visible, Depth, and Reconstruction decline by 0.152, 0.135, and 0.139 from first to third return. | Appendix G.1, Table 12(a) |
| Switching rotation axes adds difficulty | Relative to repeated yaw, yaw-then-pitch produces greater degradation in 50/63 model–metric pairs; mean excess Environment Visible degradation is 0.152. On 281 pose-qualified paired units, the excess remains 0.128. | §5.4; Appendix G.1, Table 12(b) |
| Automatic assessments partly align with humans | Six static metrics achieve 78.5%–92.5% agreement on decisive human-majority pairs; model-level Spearman ranges from 0.301 for Environment Depth to 0.988 for Environment Visible. Dynamic Overall achieves Spearman 0.732 and PLCC 0.808 across 50 instances. | §5.2; Appendix F |
| Budgeted view-relevant history offers a useful tradeoff | On fixed LingBot World v2, FoV beats Sliding Windows on all 11 capability metrics and leads eight. Across 660 matched 493-frame videos per policy, mean latency is 322.2 s for FullKV, 262.7 s for FoV, and 260.4 s for Sliding Windows: reported speedups of 1.23× and 1.24×. | §5.5; Appendix H |

These results support separate measurement of static retention, event initiation, event continuation, and camera control. They do not isolate a causal mechanism for forgetting. Dynamic conditional scores use different successful-start subsets, and the pose-controlled effects are descriptive means rather than significance tests (Appendices D.2, G.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Controlled trajectories and several-hundred-frame rollouts exclude longer horizons, free-form traversal, and embodied settings with jointly selected camera and object actions (Appendix I).
- **Author-stated evaluator limits:** Dynamic scoring depends on frozen VLM checklists; causal/physical consistency remains difficult. Residual camera position, occlusion, and pose-estimation errors can affect filtered memory scores (Appendices G.1, I).
- **Analyst inference:** Gated Dynamic Overall combines event initiation with subsequent state consistency, so a low score cannot independently diagnose poor memory. The authors’ decomposition makes this distinction explicit (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Human validation is uneven: Environment Depth has relatively weak model-ranking correlation, and the reported study covers six of seven static metrics; reconstruction alignment is not established (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Context-policy benefits may depend on the model and budget because the controlled comparison uses only LingBot World v2 and one fixed budget for the budgeted policies (Appendix H).

## Open research questions

1. **How much degradation comes from memory failure versus viewpoint error?** Filtering preserves the trend but leaves residual confounds. A next experiment could use verified camera poses and matched return views while varying history access (Appendix G.1).
2. **Which policies improve continuation after successful event initiation?** Gated scores mix both capabilities, and conditional subsets differ. Compare policies on a common subset with verified event starts (Appendix D.2).
3. **Do FoV gains generalize across models and context budgets?** The current controlled study uses one model. Repeat the policy comparison across checkpoints and several matched budgets (Appendix H).
4. **Does memory persist under longer, less constrained exploration?** These settings remain outside scope. Extend trajectories with longer unseen intervals and compound movements (Appendix I).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the central findings, but most referenced tables contain captions without their numerical bodies.

Material missing information includes complete per-model scores, full case/category inventories, the assessment prompt announced in Appendix D.3, complete native output specifications, uncertainty estimates for headline comparisons, and confirmed code/data release details.
