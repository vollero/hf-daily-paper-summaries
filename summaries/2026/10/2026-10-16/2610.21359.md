# SWE-at-Scale: Scalable Issue Synthesis from Historical Bug Fixes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21359) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21359)


## One-sentence takeaway
SWE-at-Scale learns bug injection from reversed historical fixes, producing test-validated Python repair tasks with stronger reported realism and sampling diversity than prompting baselines, although missing table contents and uncertainty estimates limit assessment (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation
Executable software engineering tasks require realistic repositories, observable failures, useful issue descriptions, and working test environments. Naturally occurring issues are costly to curate and arrive at the pace of software development. The authors argue that existing synthesis methods depend heavily on general LLMs’ pretrained bug knowledge, causing repeated generation to revisit familiar patterns (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reverse-fix supervision:** Reconstruct fixed and buggy files, reverse historical fixes, and verify round-trip patch application. Filtering, distribution-aware sampling, and manual inspection yield **13,407 transformations from 688 repositories**, excluding repositories represented in SWE-bench Verified (§2.1).
- **Two trained synthesis variants:** Fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B using LoRA. SWE-at-Scale predicts a bug-inducing diff from a complete fixed file; SWE-at-Scale+ predicts an explicit natural-language bug pattern, subsequently implemented by an executor. Its pattern supervision comes from DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 (§§2.2, 3.1).
- **Executable task construction and scaling evaluation:** Retain candidates that break previously passing tests, then generate issue descriptions from the diff and failure evidence. Evaluate realism, resolution difficulty, and within-file diversity under increasing sampling budgets (§§2.3–4.2).

The central methodological contribution is learning reusable correct-to-buggy transformations; test filtering and issue-description generation follow prior pipelines (§2.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger reported usability and realism | Under up to five synthesis attempts per target file, SWE-at-Scale+ achieves **94.0% usability / 61.2% HPR**; direct synthesis **72.6% / 55.9%**; best LLM-Rewrite **72.2% / 51.8%**. HPR compares synthetic and real issue–patch pairs in the same context, using majority votes from three engineers. | §§3.1–3.2, 4.1; Fig. 5 |
| Challenging downstream repair tasks | Across four resolution models, reported Pass@1 ranges are **31.68–48.48%** for direct synthesis and **16.81–37.02%** for the pattern variant. Evaluation uses mini-SWE-agent, temperature 0, at most 250 actions, and three-run means. Exact model-level comparisons are unavailable. | §1; §3.1; §4.1 references Table 1, whose cells are missing |
| Moderate temperature improves within-file diversity | On 50 files across 12 SWE-bench Verified repositories, ten candidates per file per temperature: mean bug-type entropy rises from **0.527 bits at 0.0** to **1.173 at 0.6**, then falls to **0.714 at 1.0**. Reported usability falls from **69.0% at 0.0** to **23.4% at 1.0**. | §4.2; Fig. 7 |
| Diversity grows with sampling budget | On the same 50-file setting at temperature 0.6, entropy increases from **0.583 bits at K=5** to **2.063 at K=200**. At K=200, **4,192/4,749 usable patches (88.27%)** are unique after normalized-diff deduplication; LLM-Modify with DS-V4-Flash reaches **1.22 bits** and **61.74%** unique usable patches. | §4.2; Fig. 8 |
| Direct synthesis uses fewer tokens than rewriting | Average reported consumption per instance: **17.45k tokens** for direct synthesis, versus **4.46–10.23k** for LLM-Modify, **34.92–62.82k** for LLM-Rewrite, and **2.29M** for FeatAdd. | §4.3; Appendix Fig. 9(b) |

These results support improved synthesis yield, evaluator preference, and diversity within the tested Python contexts. They do not establish downstream training benefits or universal realism. Lower repair success measures difficulty under the specified agents; unique diffs do not necessarily imply distinct failure mechanisms. The scaling section’s reported usability near 47% also has an unclear denominator relative to the base-commit definition in §3.2.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers Python only; cross-language transfer remains unknown. Existing tests can miss realistic bugs, causing valid candidates to be discarded (§6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison controls:** SWE-smith edits are restricted to selected functions/classes, whereas SWE-at-Scale receives complete files and learns edit locations. This difference may contribute to performance gaps (§§2.2, 3.1).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism attribution:** The pattern variant introduces teacher-derived supervision and an executor alongside explicit patterns. Reported comparisons do not isolate these factors, so attributing gains solely to natural-language representation is premature (§§2.2, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Changed-line counts are a proxy for complexity, and HPR measures perceived plausibility. Neither directly establishes semantic complexity or real-world incidence (§§3.2, 4.3).
- **Evidence gaps:** Human-evaluation sample size, agreement, uncertainty estimates, and bug-type annotation reliability are unavailable. Ten “independent” generations at temperature 0 produce nonzero entropy without an explanation of their variation (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does reverse-fix learning transfer across languages?** Python-only evaluation leaves this open (§6). Train matched multilingual models and evaluate held-out repositories with comparable test coverage.
2. **What causes the pattern variant’s gains?** Teacher supervision, explicit patterns, and execution are not separately isolated. Compare these components using matched data, executors, and generation budgets.
3. **Do synthesized tasks improve coding-agent training?** The paper evaluates task properties, not training utility. Train agents on matched-size synthetic corpora and test on held-out real issues.
4. **Does diversity persist beyond the tested budget?** Growth slows around 100 samples but continues through 200 (§4.2). Extend sampling and measure distinct behavioral failures alongside entropy and patch uniqueness.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are explicit, but supplied text omits Table 1 and Table 2 contents and numerical figure data beyond prose summaries. Material gaps include detailed optimization settings, model-level resolution results, human-study sample sizes and uncertainty, executor configuration, full bug taxonomy and annotation procedure, scaling-usability denominators, and confirmed corpus/checkpoint release availability.
