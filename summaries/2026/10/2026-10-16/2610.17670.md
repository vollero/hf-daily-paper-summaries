# Cowork-Gym: Synthesizing Interactive Work Environments at Scale

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17670) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17670)


## One-sentence takeaway
Cowork-Gym synthesizes executable workspaces with adaptive simulated users and hybrid verification, producing training data that improves three Qwen models’ mean scores across six agentic benchmarks, although transfer to real users remains untested.

## Problem and motivation
Cowork agents must coordinate tools and files while users revise requirements and shared inputs. Training environments therefore need consistent objectives, executable resources, user interventions, and success checks; checking only the final response can miss incorrect or absent artifacts. The authors identify the cost and fragility of constructing these coupled components manually as the scaling bottleneck (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Environment synthesis:** Taxonomy-guided generation controls coverage, while resource-grounded generation derives tasks from existing artifacts. Separate Builder, Verifier Constructor, and Issue Detector agents iteratively construct, execute, audit, and repair environments (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Stateful user simulation:** A private user policy conditions dialogue and authorized workspace edits on actual agent progress, potentially invalidating earlier inputs and requiring revisions (§3.3).
- **Verified training pipeline:** Deterministic and LLM-based checks filter trajectories; optional student-conditioned NLL or Rank–Surprisal Ratio selection prioritizes training examples. The data supports supervised fine-tuning, outcome-based GRPO, and optional on-policy distillation (§§3.4–4).
- **Empirical assets and findings:** The authors report a large environment pool, trained models, and gains across model scales and execution harnesses. Model and dataset links are supplied on page 1.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large environment pool, substantially filtered supervision | 64,888 environments span 14 scenarios and 17 capabilities. Of 77,729 raw trajectories, 24,732 pass validity filtering and 17,312 remain after model-conditioned selection; RL uses 2,873 environments. | §5.2, Fig. 5, Table 1 |
| Mean agentic performance improves across model scales | Across six benchmarks under the default OpenClaw setting, mean scores rise from 0.3627→0.4494 for 9B, 0.5478→0.6119 for 27B, and 0.5251→0.5665 for 35B-A3B. Gains occur in 17 of 18 matched model–benchmark comparisons. | Table 2, §5.3 |
| 27B is competitive with larger evaluated models | Ours-27B ranks second by six-benchmark mean: 0.6119 versus GLM-5.2’s 0.6157. Its PawBench average score is 0.8400 and ClawEval average score is 0.8508, highest among evaluated models. These are score rankings, not universal pass-rate wins. | Table 2 |
| Gains have a benchmark-specific boundary | On WildClawBench, 9B gains 1.36 score points, while 35B-A3B loses 0.52 points relative to its base checkpoint; all three trained models trail GLM-5.2. | §5.3, Table 2 |
| Multi-turn supervision gives mixed metric-level benefits | For 27B, adding multi-turn data raises ClawMark score from 69.27→72.50 and ClawEval-MT score from 84.01→84.44, but ClawEval-MT pass rate falls from 83.3%→81.6%. | Table 3 |
| Training transfers to an unseen harness | On PawBench with Qwen3.5-9B, Hermes pass rate rises from 15.5% to 22.7% with QwenPaw-only training and to 24.5% with OpenClaw-only or mixed-source training. Hermes is absent from collection. | Table 4, §5.4.3 |
| Student-conditioned selection helps | NLL selection retains 70% of evidence-valid candidates within length bins and improves every reported metric over no selection across the three model sizes. RSR achieves the strongest 35B-A3B PawBench pass rate: 43.03%, versus 39.39% without selection. | Table 5 |
| OPD shows a capability-retention trade-off | On ClawEval, OPD gains 4.5 points in the paper’s “pass3” metric over base, recovering approximately 80% of the teacher gain. On GPQA-Diamond it exceeds the SFT teacher by 4.54 points and is described as remaining close to base. | §5.5, Fig. 7 |

The evidence supports benchmark gains from the combined synthesis, filtering, and post-training pipeline, plus some transfer across harnesses. It does not isolate the contribution of every synthesis component or establish effectiveness with real users. The OPD result is suggestive, but its model identity and full numerical setting are not specified in the supplied prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coverage limits:** Synthetic tasks may omit cultures, languages, accessibility needs, and failure modes. Browser, file, data, and office work dominate; complex visual interfaces, specialized desktop software, and richer audio/video tasks are underrepresented (§7).
- **Author-stated verifier risks:** Deterministic checks can be brittle and LLM graders biased or permissive. Controlled failure tests do not establish false-acceptance or false-rejection rates; the authors call for human audits (§7).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No reported ablation directly isolates workspace edits from dialogue-only simulation, or removes the build–execute–revise loop. The multi-turn comparison therefore does not establish which interaction mechanism causes the gains (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and fairness:** Tables provide no confidence intervals or repeated-run variability. Training-volume matching for the multi-turn and mixed-harness comparisons is unclear, limiting causal interpretation (Tables 3–4).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Construction prompts are abridged; complete training hyperparameters, synthesis costs, repair budgets, and benchmark-overlap checks are not supplied. Definitions of “pass3” and “pass@3” are also insufficiently explained.

## Open research questions
- **Does simulated collaboration transfer to real users?** No human-interaction evaluation is reported. A next experiment could compare base and trained models on held-out human-led workflows with independent artifact grading.
- **Do workspace edits add value beyond dialogue?** The multi-turn ablation does not separate these mechanisms. Train matched-budget dialogue-only and dialogue-plus-edit variants, then test recovery after changed inputs.
- **How reliable are verifier-derived labels and rewards?** Human-calibrated error rates remain unavailable. Blindly audit accepted and rejected trajectories, including adversarial shortcut cases, and measure grader agreement and error rates.
- **How broadly does OPD preserve general capabilities?** Retention evidence centers on GPQA-Diamond and an incompletely specified OPD setting. Repeat across all model scales, several general-capability benchmarks, and multiple seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The pipeline and main benchmark comparisons are clearly documented, but causal attribution and reproducibility are less secure.

Material missing information includes full training configurations and compute costs; construction-agent identities and repair statistics; simulator fidelity against humans; verifier calibration; benchmark contamination checks; uncertainty estimates; OPD model identity and complete scores; and verified contents of the linked releases.
