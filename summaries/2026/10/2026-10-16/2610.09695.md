# Do Better Visual Representations Always Lead to Better End-to-End Autonomous Driving?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09695) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09695)


## One-sentence takeaway

ViRA improves the evaluated camera-based driving planners through training-only alignment with frozen visual foundation models, but gains depend on the target model and auxiliary perception supervision, and better perception does not eliminate planning failures (§§3–4; Appendix D.2).

## Problem and motivation

Visual foundation models (VFMs) offer strong semantic and geometric representations, but their visual capabilities may translate differently into driving performance depending on the planner. The authors identify limited systematic comparisons beyond individual planner–VFM pairings and investigate how target selection and auxiliary perception supervision affect planning gains (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **ViRA alignment framework:** A frozen VFM supervises intermediate planner features using Spatial Dependency Alignment—KL divergence between spatial dependency distributions—and Scene Semantic Alignment—a bidirectional contrastive objective over global embeddings. The teacher and alignment heads are removed after training, preserving the deployed planner (§2.1; Appendix A).
- **Controlled comparative study:** Experiments cover regression-based TransFuser, diffusion-based DiffusionDrive, scoring-based Rap*, and five VFM targets. Random-target controls and removal of TransFuser’s auxiliary supervision help isolate pre-training and supervision effects (§§3–4; Appendix C).
- **ViRA-Diffusion:** DiffusionDrive trained with DINOv3 alignment and without auxiliary perception supervision demonstrates the practical use of the study’s findings; it is a training configuration built on an existing planner (§4.3; Table 7).

These results support improvements across the tested configurations, not a universal ordering in which better visual-task performance always produces better driving. For example, DINOv3 gives TransFuser the highest reported segmentation mIoU but does not give its highest planning score under auxiliary supervision (Tables S.3, S.6). Improvements also need not hold for every component or difficulty: Rap*’s extended comfort declines with default DVGT alignment on navtest, and TransFuser’s HUGSIM Extreme HD-Score declines (Tables 1, 3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Only three planner architectures are evaluated; multimodal sensor fusion, vision-language-action models, and real-vehicle deployment are outside scope (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated cost and bias risks:** Frozen teachers add feature extraction/storage cost during training, and their pre-training biases may transfer to planners. Added training cost is not quantified (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated residual failures:** More complete BEV maps can still accompany unsuccessful trajectories; decoding, temporal reasoning, interaction modeling, and uncertainty remain unresolved (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal generalization:** Auxiliary supervision is directly removed across targets for TransFuser; the same controlled target-by-supervision experiment is not reported for every planner (§4.2; Tables S.3–S.4).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and comparability:** No repeated-run variability or confidence intervals are supplied. Rap* changes the original backbone and omits original augmentation, limiting conclusions about the official RAP system; matching training conditions for all Table 7 comparators are not established.
- **Analyst inference—perception evidence is uneven:** Static-object and pedestrian segmentation remains very weak across targets, limiting what aggregate mIoU gains establish about those classes (Table S.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Does auxiliary supervision reduce target sensitivity across planner families?** The controlled evidence centers on TransFuser. Repeat the full target-by-supervision comparison for DiffusionDrive and additional planners, with multiple seeds.
2. **Which representation properties predict planning gains?** Segmentation and planning rankings differ under auxiliary supervision. Compare teacher semantic and spatial probes against downstream planning scores under fixed training conditions.
3. **Can representation gains improve difficult interactive decisions?** Reported failures persist despite improved BEV maps. Test alignment alongside temporal or interaction-aware decoding on held-out reactive scenarios.
4. **How much training cost is necessary?** Teacher overhead is acknowledged but unmeasured. Compare cached features and selective layer/region alignment using training time, memory, and driving scores (Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark results and method description; **medium** for generalization beyond the tested systems.

Material missing information includes run-to-run uncertainty, HUGSIM episode counts, exact checkpoint identifiers for all teachers, quantified additional training cost, matched conditions across Table 7 comparators, and real-vehicle results.
