# Beyond Policy Rollouts: Embedding-Space Directional Exploration with Anchored Noise for LLM RLVR

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16105) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16105)


## One-sentence takeaway
EDEN improves sampled mathematical solution coverage in the reported Qwen2.5 experiments by replacing some standard training rollouts with anchored embedding perturbations, while keeping the rollout count and test-time inference unchanged.

## Problem and motivation
Small rollout groups can contain similar trajectories with identical rewards, leaving GRPO with zero group-relative advantages. The authors argue that temperature changes and update regularizers leave an additional exploration lever unused: the input conditioning from which rollouts begin (§1).

The proposed gap is therefore how to generate useful trajectory variation within a fixed rollout budget while retaining standard discrete decoding.

## Approach and contributions
- **Anchored input exploration:** Append one vocabulary-token embedding after the prompt, perturb it with norm-scaled Gaussian noise, and generate normally. Perturbed rollouts for one prompt share an anchor but receive independent noise (§3.2.1).
- **Controlled anchor diversity:** Construct a fixed, model-specific anchor bank using training-split prompts, screening for coverage, generation viability, and complementary outputs. Sample anchors across prompt groups and warm up noisy-rollout temperature (§3.2.1; Appendix C).
- **Selective absorption:** Compute advantages jointly across standard and perturbed rollouts, retain noisy trajectories with the largest absolute advantages across the batch, and downweight their updates. Updates remove the appended position and evaluate every trajectory under the clean prompt; no importance correction addresses this conditioning mismatch (§§3.2.2–3.2.3).

The default configuration uses 12 standard and 4 perturbed rollouts, retaining half the perturbed pool with advantage scale 0.5 (Appendix A).

These results support improved benchmark-level solution coverage in the tested pipeline. They do not establish universal per-task gains: EDEN loses on some benchmark/metric combinations in Tables 1–2. The six-benchmark averages above also differ from the eight-benchmark averages reported elsewhere, so they should not be interchanged. Diversity analyses provide supporting proxies, rather than proof of deeper or consistently valid reasoning.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The anchor bank is fixed and heuristically constructed; adaptive anchors and structured or task-dependent noise remain future work (§5; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—update mismatch:** Perturbed trajectories are optimized under clean conditioning without importance correction. Filtering and scaling are empirical controls; the supplied text does not establish their theoretical adequacy (§3.2.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evaluation covers mathematics and one model family. Reported training-seed replication is confined to the smallest model (§4; Table 7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison attribution:** EDEN also uses asymmetric clipping, unlike default GRPO. Clip Higher controls partially address this, but the full EDEN–GRPO difference cannot be assigned solely to embedding perturbation (Appendix A; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—coverage and cost:** Reasoning diversity uses n-gram distances and predefined marker signatures; final-answer correctness does not guarantee valid intermediate steps. Equal rollout counts also do not establish equal total compute, because anchor screening requires additional generation (Appendices C–D, F).
- **Stability dependence:** Removing filtering produces gradient spikes, and removing temperature warmup causes numerical instability in the reported small-model experiment (§4.3; Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Does EDEN transfer beyond this model family and mathematics?** The evaluation does not establish that scope. Test another model family on a non-mathematical task with verifiable rewards and matched rollout counts.
2. **How consequential is the conditioning mismatch?** No importance-corrected comparison is reported. Compare the current update against a corrected variant while measuring stability, coverage, and correctness.
3. **Can adaptive anchors outperform the fixed bank?** The authors leave adaptive construction open. Compare fixed and learned anchor selection while charging both screening and training compute.
4. **Does measured diversity reflect distinct valid reasoning?** Current proxies capture surface variation and marker signatures. Evaluate sampled solutions with step-validity checks and independently annotated strategy categories.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, numerical comparisons, and useful ablations, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes code/checkpoint release status, exact anchor identities, several anchor-screening choices such as the length threshold, total screening and training cost, larger-model seed variability, and systematic sensitivity tests for perturbation scale and advantage downweighting.
