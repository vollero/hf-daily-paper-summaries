# VascuSR: Iterative Anatomy-Aware Diffusion for CT Super-Resolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20509) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20509)


## One-sentence takeaway
VascuSR improves vessel segmentation and structural agreement in 8× in-plane CT super-resolution by iteratively conditioning slice-wise diffusion on refined 3D vascular masks, with small image-fidelity trade-offs and substantial inference overhead (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Severely downsampled CT does not uniquely determine fine vessels: plausible reconstructions can omit branches, introduce vessels, or connect them incorrectly. The authors argue that conventional image-quality metrics inadequately capture these errors and identify a gap in using explicit volumetric anatomy to guide—and evaluate—vascular SR (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Separate anatomical refinement from CT reconstruction.** A frozen VSNet extracts vessel probabilities; a separately supervised 3D rectified-flow model refines hepatic-vein (HV) and portal-vein (PV) masks rather than CT intensities (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Feed refined anatomy back into diffusion.** A lightweight controller adds mask-derived features at multiple ResShift decoder scales. Each feedback round re-extracts and refines anatomy from the latest reconstruction while retaining the same LR input (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Evaluate anatomy beyond image similarity.** The study combines overlap, skeleton agreement, fragmentation, centerline distance, regional intensity fidelity, and a cross-segmenter check. Comparisons support the proposed decoupled design over the particular Dice/clDice fine-tuning baseline evaluated (§4; Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results
Pairs below are **HV/PV**; hepatic comparisons use 8× in-plane SR and the same fixed VSNet evaluator.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better hepatic overlap and skeleton agreement | MSD, 30 test patients, round 3: Dice **0.7072/0.6404** versus ResShift **0.6526/0.5923**; clDice **0.6446/0.5286** versus **0.5877/0.4460**. | Tables 1, 4 |
| Gains also hold on HVM | HVM, 29 test patients, round 2: Dice **0.6206/0.7117** versus **0.5977/0.6914**; clDice **0.6033/0.5731** versus **0.5687/0.5302**. | Tables 1, 4 |
| Less fragmentation and closer centerlines | MSD component counts: **22.7/22.4** versus ResShift **31.0/26.0**; class-mean MCD **5.9331 versus 7.0667 mm**. HVM counts: **31.8/45.0** versus **48.7/66.7**. | Tables 2, 11 |
| Image fidelity is broadly retained, with trade-offs | MSD PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS: **32.77/0.8876/0.0649** versus ResShift **32.83/0.8896/0.0660**. Within the MSD 3 mm vessel ROI, signed intensity bias increases to **+4.14 HU** from **+1.95 HU**. | Tables 1, 8 |
| Improvement extends to another segmenter | Frozen TotalSegmentator on MSD: vessel Dice **0.3183**, versus ResShift **0.2912** and Dice/clDice fine-tuning **0.2803**; authors note annotation-coverage mismatch. | Table 9; Appendix B.3 |
| Feedback helps but does not improve monotonically | Zero-mask controller does not consistently improve anatomical scores; feedback gains largely stabilize after two to three rounds, with later fluctuations. | Tables 5–6 |
| Exploratory coronary gains are modest | ImageCAS, 50 test cases, round 3: Dice/clDice **0.7763/0.8530** versus ResShift **0.7658/0.8376**; PSNR **29.84 versus 29.94 dB**. | Appendix C; Table 13 |
| Refinement dominates inference cost | Reported three-round processing takes approximately **6 min per volume**; individual feedback rounds take roughly **118–123 s**, versus **18.41 s** for initial SR. | §4.7; Table 3 |

The evidence supports better segmentation-derived vascular recovery on the evaluated datasets, rather than uniformly better image reconstruction. It does not establish that every recovered branch is correct or that clinical decisions improve. Cross-segmenter evaluation strengthens the result, but remains limited to one additional evaluator on MSD.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Slice-wise SR may limit volumetric consistency; reliance on a fixed segmenter and separate refiner can permit errors to accumulate across feedback rounds (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Broader vascular and acquisition-setting evaluation remains necessary. HVM annotations contain fragments and incomplete HV coverage, complicating connectivity interpretation (§§4.5, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Most anatomical metrics depend on the segmenter also used for guidance. The TotalSegmentator check partly addresses this dependence but does not eliminate it (§4.3; Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Feedback and zero-mask ablations support anatomical conditioning, but do not isolate rectified flow against a simpler mask refiner or unrefined-mask conditioning (Appendix A.2).
- **Comparison boundary:** Table 12 compares refiner training cost with anatomical fine-tuning, not complete pipeline training costs. Its efficiency claim should be read at that stage level.
- **Reporting gaps:** The supplied results lack confidence intervals or significance tests for quality metrics. Code/checkpoint availability and the precise LR degradation procedure are not established.

## Open research questions
- **Does feedback preserve unusual or diseased anatomy?** Aggregate segmentation scores do not resolve branch-level hallucinations. A next experiment could use blinded expert branch tracing and pathology-stratified error analysis.
- **How much benefit comes specifically from flow refinement?** Existing ablations do not isolate it. Compare raw-mask conditioning, a deterministic 3D refiner, and rectified flow under matched training data and compute.
- **Can runtime fall without losing structural gains?** Refinement dominates inference and later rounds plateau. Test fewer trajectories, fewer integration steps, and validation-selected adaptive stopping.
- **Do gains transfer to real acquisition differences?** The evaluation centers on paired 8× in-plane SR. Test independently acquired LR–HR scans across scanners, reconstruction kernels, noise levels, and slice spacings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and descriptive comparisons; **medium** in broader anatomical validity and generalization.

Material missing information includes the exact LR degradation operator, uncertainty across patients and stochastic runs, inference hardware for reported timings, code/checkpoint release status, comprehensive refiner ablations, and clinical or expert-reader validation.
