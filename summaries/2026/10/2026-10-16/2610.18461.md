# MFVSR: A Multi-Style and Fine-Grained Benchmark for Video Subtitle Removal

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18461) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18461)


## One-sentence takeaway
MFVSR benchmarks video subtitle removal with controlled paired data and separate restoration, preservation, temporal, and delivery measurements, revealing trade-offs that full-frame scores obscure (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Removing burned-in subtitles requires reconstructing hidden backgrounds, preserving unaffected content, and maintaining legitimate scene motion. The authors identify fragmented evaluation sets, poorly structured subtitle variation, and generic inpainting metrics that overlook local errors and incomplete outputs (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired benchmark:** 3,000 videos equally divided among Real-HQ, Real-LQ, and AI-generated sources, with clean targets, glyph masks, bounding boxes, model-input masks, and rendering metadata. An auxiliary collection contains 953 naturally subtitled videos without clean targets (§§2.1–2.3; Ethics Statement).
- **Controlled subtitle rendering:** 16 languages and four configurable factors—spatial layout, temporal behavior, visual style, and language mode—form 24 major configurations (§2.2).
- **Diagnostic evaluation protocol:** Separate blind and mask-guided reporting; five spatial views; restoration, preservation, and temporal dimensions; explicit complete, partial, and failed-output accounting. Lite3000 provides broad evaluation, while Audit300 adds expensive diagnostics (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Empirical analysis:** Six existing methods expose capability and delivery trade-offs. The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation framework; the paper does not introduce a new removal model (§3).

These results support reporting distinct capabilities and delivery coverage rather than one ranking. They do not establish a universally best method, causal effects of subtitle attributes, or equivalent blind and guided performance. Shared video identifiers also do not guarantee equal output coverage; quality remains conditional on verified support (Appendix B.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated perceptual uncertainty:** Temporal judgments have low agreement and many undecided comparisons. Human validation supports regional spatial views more clearly than metric aggregation; SR-Q fails its promotion criterion (Appendix C.6).
- **Author-stated confounding:** Source and resolution are coupled in Audit300. Subtitle-factor groups use different base videos, so their differences are associations rather than causal effects (Appendix C.3).
- **Author-stated annotation boundary:** Text-tight bounding boxes may exclude subtitle backgrounds and effects. Outside scores cannot reliably represent unaffected content for box styles, which receive separate restricted evaluation (§3.1; Appendix C.3).
- **Configuration and selection dependence:** Audit300 comes from the common-evaluable pool and excludes box-style and human-study videos. Delivery depends on frozen cropping, resolution, and temporal contracts; partial output is not automatically an inference failure (Appendices B.1–B.2, C.4).
- **Analyst inference—real-world transfer:** Quantitative paired evidence uses rendered subtitles. The unpaired auxiliary collection does not establish equivalent performance on naturally subtitled videos.
- **Reproducibility and governance:** Some inference seeds are unset; release remains planned and license-dependent. Auxiliary videos received no additional privacy screening. Unequal timing boundaries prevent a controlled efficiency comparison (Table 6; Ethics Statement; Appendix D).

## Open research questions
1. **Which temporal measures reliably match perception?** Low agreement leaves temporal validation unresolved. A next experiment could expand shared-support playback comparisons and separately test flicker, suppressed motion, and persistent reconstruction errors.
2. **What are the causal effects of individual subtitle factors?** Existing groups contain different backgrounds. Render matched variants on the same base videos while controlling subtitle area and line count.
3. **How well do synthetic findings transfer to natural subtitles?** The auxiliary collection lacks clean targets. Evaluate blinded human restoration and preservation judgments on natural videos, with explicit support and delivery accounting.
4. **Can complete-video delivery improve without sacrificing quality?** Frozen temporal contracts contribute to partial outputs. Compare alternative chunking and stitching configurations on shared full-length targets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive benchmark findings; **medium** in perceptual and real-world generalization.
