# Athanor: An Agent-Native System for Autonomous Post-Training Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21055) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055)


## One-sentence takeaway
In one 104-hour campaign, Athanor enabled four agents to post-train Qwen3-4B-Base to statistically indistinguishable average performance from its official instruct release on development and sealed held-out suites, by composing established methods (§4.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Post-training research requires costly, failure-prone experiments across data mixtures, training objectives, hyperparameters, and stage sequences. Agents need reliable execution, protected evaluation, and persistent evidence to sustain this search across days (§§1–3).

The authors identify a gap between specialist post-training benchmarks and autonomous production of one generalist checkpoint: their setting jointly targets six capabilities, starts from a raw base model, prohibits external teacher-model calls, and reserves an unseen evaluation suite (§1). Their claim of being the first demonstration at this level is an author claim, not independently verified here.

## Approach and contributions
- **Research infrastructure:** Athanor separates agent research decisions from asynchronous job execution, frozen evaluation, data decontamination, and checkpoint storage. A durable record links code, data, parent checkpoints, results, transcripts, and notes (§3, Appendix A).
- **Generalist post-training demonstration:** Four Claude Opus 5.5 agents share results and checkpoints while each controls an eight-GPU node. They construct one submitted model from public data without external teacher calls or human research advice (§4.1).
- **Agent-composed recipe and trajectory analysis:** The successful lineage combines supervised fine-tuning, GRPO with some greedy rollouts, cross-agent weight merging and extrapolation, and shortest-correct-answer self-distillation. These are established method families; the contributions are their autonomous composition, implementation adjustments, and documented research behavior (§5.1, Figure 5).
- **Findings about research behavior:** The record shows rapid sharing, early convergence on one dominant corpus, and stronger attention to data format and scorability than answer correctness (§§5.2–5.4).

The evidence supports feasibility within this supplied environment and substantial transfer to different questions and task types. It does not establish universal capability parity, statistically significant superiority over Instruct-2507, or a causal contribution for each infrastructure feature or training adjustment. The headline scores come from a measurement protocol added after the campaign; the original protocol is reported separately.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated generalization limits:** One campaign uses one base model and four identical agents. Shared submissions are not independent replications; other model sizes, families, agent models, and teams without sharing remain untested (§8).
- **Author-stated dependence on prior work:** Although teacher calls are prohibited, much public training data was already distilled from stronger models. Starter code also supplies major training methods (§§3.2, 5.1, 8).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No controlled comparison isolates the benefits of sharing, managed execution, or the greedy-rollout change. That rollout change coincides with rebuilt training problems (§8; Trace 2).
- **Narrow data exploration:** No experiment compares the dominant corpus against another source under otherwise identical conditions. Alternatives receive limited trials, and answer correctness is rarely checked (§§5.3–5.4).
- **Incomplete contamination audit:** Of 108 ancestral datasets, 47 could not be fully scanned afterward because of lost local files or scan limits. The overlap filter also cannot match items shorter than 13 words (Appendices A, C).
- **Analyst inference—protocol sensitivity:** Decoding and output limits materially change reference rankings. The final comparison is informative, but its post-campaign introduction and the performance-dependent stopping time complicate strict prospective replication (Appendix C, Table 8).

## Open research questions
- **How repeatable is the outcome?** One shared campaign cannot estimate campaign-level variance. Repeat the campaign with fixed budgets and independent teams, reporting distributions of held-out performance (§8).
- **Does collaboration outperform independent search at equal cost?** Checkpoint exchanges demonstrate sharing, but no comparison isolates its benefit. Compare shared and isolated teams with matched total GPU and inference budgets (§§5.2, 8).
- **Can diversity and data verification prevent the plateau?** Early corpus convergence and weak correctness checks leave this unresolved. Compare the current setup with heterogeneous agents and controlled, verified data-source trials (§§5.3–5.5).
- **Does the greedy-rollout adjustment independently improve training?** Its observed benefit is confounded with changes to training problems. Hold data and initialization fixed while varying greedy-rollout share, measuring repetition and suite performance (Trace 2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-campaign findings; **medium** in broader conclusions about autonomous research.

Material missing information includes independent campaign replications, controlled collaboration and infrastructure ablations, fully matched rollout/data experiments, a complete retrospective audit of ancestral datasets, and verified release locations and completeness for code, checkpoints, and campaign records.
