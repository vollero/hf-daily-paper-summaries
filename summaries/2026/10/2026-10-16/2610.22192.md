# Terminal-RSI: Recursive Self-Improving Terminal Agents via Recursive Self-Rewrite and Policy-Refine

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22192) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22192)


## One-sentence takeaway
Terminal-RSI alternates verified trajectory rewriting with critic-inheriting policy refinement, reporting a four-suite mean success increase from 20.68% to 50.58%, while leaving individual component effects unisolated (§3.2; §5).

## Problem and motivation
Terminal agents must change executable environments until protected verifiers pass; plausible plans and partial repairs are insufficient. The authors identify three training gaps: token-length-dependent credit decay, critic miscalibration after supervised policy changes, and progress rewards that can favor incomplete solutions over strict completion (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Recursive training framework:** Alternate actor-only Recursive Self-Rewrite (RSR)—discovery, runbook review, fresh execution, and filtered supervision—with actor–critic Recursive Policy-Refine (RPR). Critic weights survive rewriting and undergo phase-local calibration before joint optimization. The contribution is this coupling of existing supervision and PPO infrastructure (§§2.1, 4).
- **Turn-aware credit assignment:** TB-GAE skips observations as recursion nodes, retains token-specific value baselines, and applies discount and trace decay at interaction boundaries. Its fixed-input continuation properties are derived; deployment in evaluated checkpoints remains insufficiently established (§§2.3–2.4; Appendices C, G).
- **Reward evolution:** Family Dense Reward normalizes passed semantic checks by family; Success-Prioritized Reward adds strict-success credit and bounds partial credit below the success threshold. Offline rescoring measures reward allocation separately from training effects (§2.2; Appendix B).

The reported stage sequence supports improved measured completion under the designated evaluation ledger. Mean@3 averages independent inference runs, not pass@3 or independently trained models. Offline rescoring establishes reward redistribution, while neither it nor the stage comparisons establishes causal benefits from TB-GAE, critic inheritance, reward evolution, or recursion individually. Historical coverage and repeat-count discrepancies also limit independent verification of aggregates (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated causal gaps:** No compute-matched alternation study, isolated TB-GAE evaluation, matched online reward ablation, or inherited-versus-reset critic control. Learning rates, rewards, and initialization change together (§5).
- **Author-stated evaluation risks:** Three inference repeats do not establish statistical superiority across training seeds. The retained sequence lacks a validated prospective checkpoint-selection rule; final-benchmark-driven selection could confound results (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated reproducibility gaps:** Some actor–critic pairings, deployed advantage estimators, historical task manifests, and repeat protocols remain unresolved (Appendices A, E, G).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** One backbone and five stages do not establish scaling or universal monotonic improvement. Broader repository and pretraining overlap are incompletely audited (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Completion claims should remain tied to enforced verifier requirements: the Python-backed Rust–C solution directly demonstrates that passing tests need not establish full architectural fidelity (Appendix H).

## Open research questions
1. **Does critic inheritance improve completion beyond recalibration alone?** No matched inherit/reset control is reported. Cross inheritance with warmup under equal calibration budgets and repeated training seeds (Table 5).
2. **Does TB-GAE improve online learning?** Estimator properties are derived, but deployment and isolated gains remain unresolved. Compare generated-token GAE, skip-only GAE, and TB-GAE with matched rewards, histories, and boundaries (§2.4; Table 5).
3. **Which SPR mechanism improves strict success?** Offline rescoring combines success separation, saturation, and family recalibration. Train matched variants that vary these mechanisms separately (§3.4; Table 5).
4. **Does alternation beat continued refinement at equal total cost?** RSR adds discovery and supervision costs. Compare schedules under matched task exposure, total compute, and predefined held-out checkpoint selection (§3.2; Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method definitions and reported results are detailed, but causal attribution and evaluation provenance remain incomplete.

Material missing information includes reconciled run-level outcomes and task manifests, independent training seeds and uncertainty estimates, complete compute costs, exact deployed advantage-estimator and actor–critic checkpoint mappings, a prospective selection protocol, broader overlap audits, and verifiable code/model release destinations.
