# InfoVis: Unified Slide Image Generation and Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16784)


## One-sentence takeaway
InfoVis combines slide-specific training and task-checkpoint fusion in a unified 9B image model that leads evaluated open-source baselines across eleven VisSlideBench metrics, while retaining weaknesses in aesthetics, asset fidelity, and editing locality (Table 1; Appendix G).

## Problem and motivation
Slide creation requires accurate text, coherent layouts, faithful use of supplied images, and edits that preserve unrelated content. The authors identify two gaps: insufficient slide-native supervision and benchmarks that inadequately isolate individual slide-generation and editing capabilities (§1). Their target is slide **images**, with optional reconstruction into PowerPoint, rather than native editable slide objects throughout.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified task formulation and data engine.** Four tasks share a text-and-image interface: text-to-slide, template-conditioned generation, multi-image grounded generation, and instruction-based editing. Real presentations, executed PPTX edits, and synthetic targets yield over 13M continual-pretraining records and approximately 1.9M SFT records; multiple records can share a target image (§3; Table 6).
- **VisSlideBench.** The benchmark contains 1,527 screened cases covering all four tasks. OCR measures required-text rendering, while GPT-4.1 evaluates instruction compliance, visual quality, asset preservation, and editing consistency; blinded human rankings validate selected dimensions (§4; Appendix B).
- **InfoVis training recipe.** The model retains FLUX.2-klein-base-9B’s architecture. Training progresses through text-to-slide pretraining, four-task mixed pretraining, shared SFT, task-specific SFT, and linear checkpoint fusion (§5; Appendix C.1).
- **Document-to-slide integration.** GPT-4.1 plans content and design; InfoVis renders candidates; Qwen3.5-27B selects outputs. Optional PowerPoint export reconstructs editable text while keeping charts and tables as images (Appendix D).

The contributions center on slide-specific data, evaluation, and adaptation; checkpoint averaging itself follows prior work (§5).

These results support substantial gains from the complete slide-adaptation recipe and competitive performance within the evaluated settings. They do not establish general editing robustness or complete presentation quality. The external workflow score also includes planning and verification components, so it cannot be attributed to InfoVis alone.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Editing evaluation uses the training construction pipeline and measures in-distribution performance; English-only data and benchmarks leave multilingual transfer untested (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** Proprietary synthesis links generation SFT and part of the benchmark to one model’s style. Source-stratified rankings are stable, but the authors explicitly caution that different real/synthetic content prevents establishing source independence (Appendices C.3, E).
- **Author-stated:** Charts can be redrawn with corrupted labels or structure, and edits can remove unrelated content. Knowledge-intensive slides and aesthetic polish remain weaknesses (Appendices E–G).
- **Analyst inference:** Human agreement supports the selected automatic rankings, not comprehensive factual correctness. Required-text scoring ignores additional unmatched text, leaving a specific blind spot for unwanted content (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Prompt truncation disadvantages some baselines. Full-prompt comparisons narrow the gaps, although InfoVis retains its open-source lead in those checks (Appendix C.5).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Training configuration is detailed, but exact fusion coefficients are absent, and artifact release is promised rather than demonstrated.

## Open research questions
1. **Does editing generalize to independently constructed slides and edits?** The editing benchmark shares its construction pipeline with training. Next experiment: evaluate independently authored edit requests on unseen presentation sources, measuring execution and preservation separately.
2. **Can grounding preserve exact chart content?** Appendix G documents label and structural corruption. Next experiment: compare current synthesis with an asset-preserving composition variant using exact label, value, and structure checks.
3. **Can aesthetic improvements preserve content fidelity?** Aesthetics remains the largest proprietary-model gap. Next experiment: compare aesthetic-focused training or candidate selection against the current recipe with blinded human ratings and unchanged fidelity checks.
4. **How useful are exported presentations beyond appearance?** Only the external aesthetics track was evaluated, and charts remain image assets. Next experiment: run source-content and editability evaluations on exported PowerPoint decks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported methods and benchmark findings; **medium** for broader generalization claims.

Material missing information includes exact fusion weights, verified release status and artifact licenses, independent OOD editing results, multilingual evaluation, end-to-end content and editability scores, and comprehensive latency or compute-cost measurements.
