# Mine, Sample, Render: Test-Time Diversity Alignment Of Generative Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21185) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21185)


## One-sentence takeaway
MSR mines interpretable attributes from a reference corpus and samples their configurations before generation, improving measured diversity across five settings and distributional fidelity in weather reports and coding-user simulation, with some validity tradeoffs.

## Problem and motivation
Individually plausible generations can collectively repeat a narrow range of behaviors or designs. The authors argue that temperature changes and diversity prompts do not specify what should vary, how frequently, or in which combinations. Their proposed gap is **test-time diversity alignment**: using an observed reference population to guide choices that an ordinary request leaves unspecified (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Corpus-induced semantic controls:** Compare artifact cohorts, consolidate overlapping attribute descriptions, and label the corpus with the resulting schema to estimate frequencies and dependencies. The authors identify artifact-level, compositional attribute discovery through comparisons as a distinguishing contribution (§§2.1, A).
- **Explicit configuration sampling:** Use either positive-NPMI topic modeling with nonnegative factorization or sequential sampling with mutual-information-based support backoff, smoothing, and compatibility masks. The authors explicitly claim the combination for corpus-induced configurations, rather than ancestral sampling or backoff themselves, as their contribution (§§2.2, A, B.1).
- **Replaceable conditional rendering:** Give sampled controls and the original request to a generator or agent. For images, an optional LLM resolver binds placeholders and reconciles controls before rendering (§§2, B.5.3).
- **Cross-domain evaluation:** Test distributional fidelity, set diversity, and selected quality measures across weather reports, user simulation, webpages, images, and articulated chair assets (§3).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Weather distribution matching improves | GPT-5-mini reports, evaluated against held-out 2017 weather across ten cities: prompt-only → MSR forward KL **0.570 → 0.181**, JS **0.484 → 0.203**, support coverage **21% → 68%**. Known-schema control: **0.150**, **0.166**, **83%**, respectively. | §3.1, Fig. 3; B.2.5 |
| Weather schema recovery is incomplete | Recovered **13 of 16** weather attributes. Foggy and storm had no source support; windy occurred in **24** intended source configurations but was not separately recovered. | Table 5; B.2.2 |
| Coding-user simulation moves closer to real behavior | With GPT-5.5 users and a fixed GPT-5.4 assistant, compact MSR reduces cluster-distribution JS **0.5167 → 0.3086** and increases coverage **0.6345 → 0.7813** versus prompt-only; matched evaluation **N = 4,903**. Both MSR schemas improve all four reported metrics over prompt-only for every tested simulator. | Tables 1, 9; B.3 |
| Webpage sets become more diverse | Across **100 tasks**, ten pages per condition: GPT-5.5 with WebSight MSR raises structural Vendi **2.158 → 3.956** and image Vendi **2.446 → 4.116** versus prompt-only. Both schemas improve both scores for both generators. | §3.3, Table 2 |
| Image diversity exceeds rewriting baselines | Across **50 captions**, ten images per arm: GPT Image 2 DINOv2 Vendi is **2.16** prompt-only, **4.32** coordinated rewrite, **4.97** MSR–Direct, and **6.73** MSR–Resolved. Both MSR variants outperform all three baselines on both embedding metrics across all three generators. | §3.4, Table 3 |
| Resolution improves attribute realization but can reduce validity | Model audit of **100 images and 2,000 sampled controls per arm/generator**: GPT Image 2 realization rises **61.6% → 80.2%**, Direct → Resolved, while valid images fall **99% → 97%**. FLUX validity falls **95% → 90%**. | B.5.7, Table 14 |
| Chair diversity improves; quality reporting is inconsistent | With **20 chairs per arm**, mean odd-one-out rank is **48.800** independent, **25.125** coordinated, **17.100** MSR; lower indicates distinctiveness. Table 4 quality totals are **82.35**, **70.80**, **83.55**, but Table 15 gives MSR/coordinated totals **84.70/72.55**. | §3.5, Tables 4, 15 |

The evidence supports improved measured diversity across the tested domains and closer target-distribution matching in the two explicitly distributional evaluations. Diversity scores alone do not establish corpus-distribution fidelity, task success, or quality. Weather forward KL uses a half-uniform mixture of normalized probabilities, while coverage measures occupied reference cells; these details matter when interpreting the reported gains (§B.2.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Acknowledged recovery limits:** Weather mining misses a supported category; the paper cannot distinguish source-rendering failure from mining failure (§B.2.2). Its discovery call also compares almost the entire report corpus, rather than demonstrating small-cohort recovery in that experiment.
- **Acknowledged control limits:** Renderers may omit or modify sampled controls. Image resolution improves realization but sometimes lowers validity; placeholder interpretation also makes the attribute audit imperfect (§§B.5.3, B.5.7).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Model judges assess user behavior, visual distinctiveness, image validity, and chair quality. The supplied text reports no human corroboration or uncertainty intervals for the main comparisons (§3; appendices).
- **Analyst inference—quality coverage:** Webpage checks establish renderability, not content fidelity or usability. Image validity audits cover MSR variants without a matched baseline validity table. Chair motion is modeled rather than physically verified (§§B.4.5, B.5.7, B.6.3).
- **Analyst inference—confounding and reproducibility:** Webpage schemas differ in corpus, miner, vocabulary size, and smoothing, preventing attribution of differences to corpus complexity alone. Chair quality totals conflict across tables, and Table 15’s caption and surrounding prose differ on who selected rubric weights (§§B.4.2–B.4.3, B.6.4).
- **Scope:** User evaluation concerns English coding conversations with reconstructed goals and one fixed assistant; chair evaluation concerns one object class (§§B.3, 3.5).

## Open research questions
1. **How much do learned dependencies contribute beyond extra conditioning?** No matched comparison against independent marginals or source-configuration resampling is reported. Compare these samplers under identical schemas, control counts, renderers, and budgets.
2. **Does greater diversity preserve task quality relative to strong baselines?** Existing validity checks do not establish this across domains. Run blinded human evaluations of diversity, request satisfaction, and usefulness for all arms.
3. **How sensitive is MSR to corpus coverage and annotation errors?** Missing weather categories and sparse support expose this uncertainty. Vary corpus size and rare-mode prevalence, then measure schema recall and generated distribution error.
4. **Does quantitative performance transfer beyond the reference domain?** Out-of-distribution image evidence is qualitative (§C.2.1). Evaluate held-out domains with explicit diversity and validity metrics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive implementation detail and consistent diversity gains, but leaves material uncertainty about statistical variability, human agreement, end-to-end costs, public code/data availability, and the inconsistent chair quality totals.
