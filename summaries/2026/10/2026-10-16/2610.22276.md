# MJev-Spatial: System One Spatial Intelligence via Native Attention Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22276) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22276)


## One-sentence takeaway
MJev-Spatial replaces spatial answer generation with native attention-based candidate classification, reporting the best mean score among eight tested models and substantially lower latency, although most controlled design studies use a smaller pilot training set (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Spatial questions often require compact outputs: an option, a numerical value, or an image point. Autoregressive VLMs nevertheless generate and parse answer text, adding sequential decoding and formatting overhead. The authors seek a shared decision mechanism that uses pretrained visual-language knowledge to predict these outputs directly while preserving numerical and localization precision (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared native candidate scoring.** Candidate descriptions accompany the images and question. The model matches a decision representation against candidate representations using the final attention layer’s pretrained query/key projections, retaining native normalization and positional transformations. It adds no prediction head; retained language parameters are fine-tuned while the visual pipeline stays frozen (§3.2; Appendix A.3).
- **Hierarchical output representations.** Options require one classification; numbers use quantity-specific codebooks with up to two decisions; points use three region decisions with fresh crop encodings between stages. A probability-tree decoder extends the point model to multiple outputs without additional training (§3.1).
- **Joint training and scheduling.** Teacher forcing supervises every stage of a question in one causal language-backbone pass, with losses averaged across stages. Conversion of existing annotations supplies the training corpus; global length grouping and GPU workload balancing reduce execution cost (§3.3).

The distinctive contribution is this combination of native pretrained scoring and a shared spatial classification interface. Direct candidate decisions and continuous-output discretization have precedents acknowledged by the authors (§2).

These are author-reported results. They support a favorable quality–latency trade-off within the tested suite. They do not isolate the final quality gain attributable to classification: the principal autoregressive base is unmodified, whereas MJev-Spatial receives extensive spatial fine-tuning. Native Q/K scoring also has task-dependent trade-offs rather than uniformly beating added heads (Appendix E.1). Pilot results and their timing protocols should not be treated as final-model measurements.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated separation limit:** Exact and conservative near-duplicate image filtering does not establish independence between physical scenes or neighboring video frames (Appendix B.3).
- **Author-stated output constraints:** Unsupported quantities and ambiguous units are rejected; early hierarchical errors can exclude the correct region. Geometric and quantization bounds assume correct class selection (§3.1; Appendix B.1).
- **Author-stated multi-point weakness:** The pilot is trained primarily on single targets. Its counting success trails the autoregressive baseline, and the counting scorer does not certify one prediction per object instance (Appendices E.2, F.2).
- **Author-stated experimental limits:** Readout comparisons vary representation boundary and retained computation alongside scoring architecture. Historical pilot runs retain differing sequence budgets; exploratory visual-token routing was chosen after an initial decoder experiment (Appendices D.1, E.1, F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Approximately 89.5% of training QA derive from RefSpatial, which supplies all current point supervision; broader source generalization remains uncertain (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Small quality differences warrant caution because repeated-seed uncertainty is not reported. The short scheduling study establishes throughput, not convergence or full-run savings (§4.4; Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
1. **How much improvement comes from the decision interface itself?** The final comparison lacks an autoregressive model fine-tuned on the same converted corpus. A matched-data, matched-exposure comparison could separate adaptation gains from output-interface gains.
2. **Can multi-point supervision improve coverage and instance separation?** The decoder composes mainly single-target training, and its scorer has an instance-level validity limit. Train with exhaustive point sets and evaluate per-instance matching alongside existing counting success (Appendices E.2, F.2).
3. **Do pilot design choices hold at full training scale?** Numerical and readout studies mainly use the smaller corpus. Repeat selected alternatives with full-scale training and multiple seeds, measuring MAE, relative error, benchmark quality, and latency.
4. **Do faster predictions improve physical interaction?** Evaluation measures spatial answers rather than closed-loop outcomes. Test placement or navigation under controlled latency budgets and scene changes.
