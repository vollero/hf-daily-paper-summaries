# CoWorld Reasoner: Agentic Multimodal Reasoning with the Evolving Video World Model-Driven Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21910) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21910)


## One-sentence takeaway
CoWorld Reasoner combines adaptive video generation with an evolving agent harness, improving GPT-5.6’s item-weighted accuracy from 58.29% to 59.69% across seven multimodal benchmarks, although gains vary substantially by task (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Some visual questions require reasoning about unseen viewpoints, intermediate motion, future events, or interventions. The authors argue that existing video-world-model reasoning systems typically use fixed workflows centered on a particular operation, limiting their flexibility across these tasks (§1).

The proposed gap is therefore how to select useful visual simulations and interpret their outputs reliably, rather than simply how to generate additional video.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified visual operations:** A shared video world model supports view change, interpolation, continuation, and intervention through standardized tools (§3.1; Table 6).
- **Agent harness:** Reusable cross-view, temporal, and causal skills guide whether to generate, how to construct requests, and how to interpret video. An optional verifier describes generated events, artifacts, and uncertainty (§3.1).
- **Harness co-evolution:** A supervisor revises tools, skills, instructions, and execution code using development-set traces and scoring feedback. Revision proceeds through four reasoning stages while both model weights remain fixed (§3.2; Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors compare against direct reasoning and five evolving agents, and examine tool removal, alternative video models, and adaptation to another VLM (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Overall improvement over direct reasoning | GPT-5.6 with H3 Max Turbo: item-weighted accuracy **59.69% versus 58.29%**, a **1.40 percentage-point** gain; positive overall gains on all seven benchmarks. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Largest benchmark gains | MMSI: **38.35% → 42.89%**, **+4.54 pp**; ERQA: **51.75% → 58.50%**, **+6.75 pp**, against direct GPT-5.6. | Table 1 |
| Strong evolving-agent comparison, with exceptions | Highest aggregate accuracy and leads all five evolving baselines on five benchmarks. ERQA is below Meta-Harness (**58.50% versus 60.00%**); CausalSpatial is below GEPA (**60.79% versus 62.51%**). | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Generated video helps selected subtasks | Against the evolved harness without tools or verifier, gains include CausalSpatial occlusion **+5.36 pp** and collision **+2.55 pp**; SAT perspective taking gains **0.00 pp**. | Figure 3a; §4.3 |
| Tool benefits do not imply improvement over direct reasoning | Full-system CausalSpatial accuracy is **60.79% versus 60.72%** for direct GPT-5.6; occlusion falls **4.76 pp**, while physics rises **4.55 pp**. | Table 11 |
| Recovery exposes both benefit and harm | On GPT-5.6’s incorrect CausalSpatial items, recovery is **28.1%** with video versus **16.6%** without; ERQA recovery decreases from **35.8% to 33.2%**. | Figure 4 |
| Adaptation to another VLM is promising | After transferring the GPT-5.6 harness and running one additional evolution round, Grok-4.5’s item-weighted accuracy across five evaluated benchmarks rises from **32.86% to 39.78%**; SAT declines. | Figure 5; §4.3; Appendix A.3 |

The evidence supports a modest aggregate improvement from the complete system and task-dependent benefits from generated video. It does not establish that generation consistently helps, that every subtask improves, or that harness co-evolution alone causes the aggregate gain. The alternative-VWM experiment also reports weaker results with LTX-2.3, including CausalSpatial performance below direct GPT-5.6 (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged uncertainty:** Generated transitions are predictions rather than exact physical or geometric measurements. Case studies acknowledge approximate mechanics, ambiguous camera motion, and incomplete trajectories (Figures 8–12).
- **Analyst inference—unsupported certainty:** In Figure 12, the verifier notes that the truck leaves the frame and later collisions remain unresolved, yet the final answer declares removal sufficient. This illustrates a failure to carry uncertainty into the answer.
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** The supplied experiments do not include a dedicated initial-versus-evolved harness comparison or separate tool, skill, verifier, and execution-code evolution ablations. The tool-removal experiment disables both tools and verifier (§4.3; Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confounds:** Direct GPT-5.6 uses one stage; CoWorld uses four stages and additional generation. The evolving baselines do not receive VWM tools, so their comparison does not isolate harness optimization under equal capabilities or compute (Appendices A.1–A.3).
- **Analyst inference—measurement limits:** No confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, latency, monetary cost, or total evolution compute are reported. Some open-ended answers use the same model deployment as judge, although judging occurs in an independent context (Appendix A.2).
- **Scope:** VLM transfer includes additional adaptation, and published specialist-model scores use source protocols; neither provides a fully controlled, adaptation-free comparison.

## Open research questions
1. **How much does co-evolution contribute independently?** The paper lacks an initial-harness control. Compare initial and evolved harnesses under identical generation and inference budgets, then ablate each editable component.
2. **Can video-use decisions avoid harmful simulations?** Video reduces ERQA error recovery and contributes unevenly across tasks. Test a generation gate against always-generate and never-generate controls, tracking both corrected and newly introduced errors.
3. **Can final answers preserve simulation uncertainty?** Figure 12 concludes more than its visible trajectory establishes. Evaluate uncertainty-aware stopping or abstention on incomplete and ambiguous generated sequences.
4. **How portable is the harness without adaptation?** Grok-4.5 receives another evolution round. Compare zero-adaptation transfer with matched-budget adaptation across the evaluated VLM and VWM variants.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported settings and descriptive results; **medium** in causal attribution and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes complete executable harness artifacts and prompts, detailed revision histories, baseline optimization budgets, repeated-run statistics, compute and cost measurements, isolated co-evolution ablations, and systematic evaluation of generated-video fidelity.
