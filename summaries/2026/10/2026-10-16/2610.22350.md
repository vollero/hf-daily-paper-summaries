# FDE-Gym: The Last Mile of AI Delivery

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22350) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22350)


## One-sentence takeaway
FDE-Gym evaluates agents on simulated customer delivery, finding that the strongest tested configuration scores 0.56 and resolves 14 of 95 tasks, with substantial losses from discovering constraints, implementing effective systems, and stakeholder contact costs (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Customer requests may misidentify the underlying problem, omit operational constraints, or conflict with business needs. The authors argue that benchmarks built around predefined issues or specifications miss the combined challenge of clarifying requirements, modifying customer systems, and improving customer outcomes (§1).

FDE-Gym tests this combined process: successful implementation of the sponsor’s request can still earn little credit if it fails to improve the customer’s business metric.

## Approach and contributions
- **Delivery benchmark:** 95 tasks derived from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors. Agents inspect documents and code, question stakeholders, and modify systems subsequently evaluated on held-out production data (§§2–3).
- **Controlled information levels:** L0 provides the problem, constraints, and scoring criteria; successive levels withhold information until L3 presents the sponsor’s potentially misdirected request. The remaining environment stays fixed (§2.2).
- **Construction and scoring methodology:** Scripted stakeholders, changed-fact variants, reference solutions, and admission anchors test meaningful improvement while discouraging literal compliance and shortcuts (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Evaluation infrastructure and findings:** The authors evaluate 13 model configurations in a shared framework and provide an open-source harness, experiment runner, and online practice platform; task access is partial (§4).

The score subtracts stakeholder contact cost from incident-adjusted business uplift relative to an unchanged system and an expert reference. Resolution requires a score of at least 0.8 without an incident. Reported averages floor incident-adjusted uplift at −1 before subtracting contact costs (§2.3).

## Evidence and main results

Unless stated otherwise, results use three runs per task–instruction combination in the shared agent framework (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest configuration remains below the reference | Claude Opus 5.5: mean L3 score **0.56**, task-bootstrap 95% interval **0.50–0.62**, with **14/95** tasks resolved; reference solutions score at least **0.95**. | §5.1; Table 3 discussed in text |
| Constraint withholding causes the largest observed decline | On the same **20 original tasks**, averaging paired results across the three leading models: L0→L1 decline **0.28** (95% interval **0.17–0.39**); L1→L3 decline **0.04** (**−0.03–0.14**). | §5.2 |
| Complete stakeholder fact recovery does not ensure strong delivery | Across **73 L3 task–model cases** where a leading model obtained every stakeholder key fact, mean normalized uplift was **0.66**; **27/73** reached uplift ≥**0.8**, before contact-cost deduction. | §5.3 |
| Contact costs materially reduce scores | At L3, Opus achieves uplift **0.70**, minus contact cost **0.14**, yielding score **0.56**; GPT-6 Astra achieves **0.60**, minus **0.22**, yielding **0.38**. | §5.3 |
| Some delivered systems worsen outcomes | Mistral Small 4 produces negative business uplift on **51/95 L3 tasks**, before stakeholder contact costs. Claude Haiku 4.5 has negative final scores on **79/95**. | §5.4 |
| Performance depends strongly on scenario facts | No model averages ≥**0.5** in **5/20 scenarios**; the median within-scenario score range for leading models is **0.34–0.44** across task variants. | §5.5; Figure 7 |

These results establish weaknesses under this benchmark’s simulated environments, scoring rules, and resource limits. The instruction manipulation supports a stronger attribution to withheld constraints than the observational association between fact recovery and delivery quality. It does not establish real-customer performance, human-relative ability, or directly comparable gaps against benchmarks reporting binary success rates (§§4, 7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Scripted stakeholders simplify human behavior; contact prices model stakeholder time rather than measure it (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Instruction-level comparisons cover only the original tasks. The authors claim no large further decline is visible beyond L1, rather than establishing equivalence (§7).
- **Author-stated:** No human baseline is available. Reference solutions establish solvability with full knowledge, not human performance from L3 instructions (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Findings depend on one minimal agent framework; stronger frameworks could change scores and rankings. Isolation strength varied across hosts, and contamination cannot be ruled out (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** Hidden contact prices make scores partly dependent on agents inferring an undisclosed cost structure. No explicit-price ablation is reported (§§2.1, 5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Independent reproduction is constrained by held-out variants and reference solutions, with most original tasks available only on request (§4).

## Open research questions
1. **Would explicit contact prices improve delivery efficiency?** Agents are not told that questions incur costs (§5.3). Compare hidden and disclosed prices while measuring uplift, incidents, and contact cost separately.
2. **Which engineering failures persist after facts are known?** Complete stakeholder fact recovery often fails to produce high uplift (§5.3). Compare ordinary runs with runs receiving verified facts and an explicit implementation-validation stage.
3. **How much do frameworks change the results?** Only one framework is tested (§7). Evaluate the same models with alternative planning and verification scaffolds under matched budgets.
4. **Do simulated scores predict human or customer outcomes?** Human results are pending, and stakeholders are scripted (§7). Compare recorded expert L3 runs and a controlled human-stakeholder subset with the existing simulation.
