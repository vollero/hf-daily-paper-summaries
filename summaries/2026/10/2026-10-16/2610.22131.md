# Intern-Decision: Efficient Multimodal Decision Making with Autoregressive Masked Language Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22131) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22131)


## One-sentence takeaway
Intern-Decision scores multiple structured choices in one causal forward pass, with its 4B model achieving the highest reported seven-suite average, while evidence for visual interaction remains qualitative (Tables 3, 5; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Many agent operations select from predefined actions or labels, yet use free-form generation that adds decoding latency and requires output parsing. The authors identify a need for compact models that combine text and images, answer multiple fields over a shared state, and expose candidate probabilities for downstream control (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Decision modeling:** A complete assistant JSON skeleton contains one placeholder per field. The native language-model head predicts each answer symbol from the position immediately preceding its placeholder. All fields are scored together; neither gold nor predicted answers feed into later fields (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Multimodal model family:** The authors adapt Qwen3.5 backbones at 0.8B, 2B, and 4B, freezing the vision tower and projector. All use the same corpus of 17,880 samples and 23,500 supervised decisions, including 5,500 image-bearing samples (§4.1; Appendix A, Table 9).
- **Probability and deployment studies:** Candidate-restricted softmax provides probabilities; separate temperature scaling, an exact-distribution pilot, and confidence-based handoff evaluate their usefulness (§§3.4, 5.4–5.5). Temperature scaling and routing are established techniques applied here, rather than newly introduced algorithms (§2).

These results support structured decision scoring and aggregate calibration improvements under the reported protocols. They do not isolate the causal contribution of the masked objective, establish controlled speedups over Jev, or demonstrate quantitative visual-agent generalization. The pilot’s aggregate gains also do not imply improvement on every case (§§5.2–5.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated evaluation boundary:** Public Jevbench informed development and is diagnostic rather than independent held-out evidence (§5.1).
- **Attribution remains unresolved:** Matched-data architectural ablations are absent; model-size trends do not establish parameter count as the sole cause (§5.2).
- **Latency comparison is incomplete:** Batch size, warm-up, repetitions, timing boundaries, and Jev transport conditions are unspecified. Handoff timing combines estimated and measured quantities (§§5.3, 5.5).
- **Calibration scope is narrow:** The pilot comprises paired synthetic mechanisms; semantic overlap with training sources is not ruled out. Baseline calibration treatment differs, and pilot ECE uses Jev’s separate confidence in the illustrated case (§§4.2, 5.2, 5.4).
- **Structural constraints:** The interface supports 62 candidate symbols and up to 16 fields; it guarantees neither field-order invariance nor consistency between related answers (§3).
- **Visual evidence is qualitative:** Selected successful trajectories lack controlled success rates, episode statistics, and comparison policies (§6).
- **Reproduction boundary:** Training settings and checkpoint binding are documented, but external-baseline adapters and historical responses are unavailable (Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **What does joint masked scoring contribute independently?** Current comparisons do not isolate it. Compare joint scoring, separate field scoring, and constrained generation using the same backbone, data, and compute budget (§5.2).
- **How robust is scoring to schema changes?** Causal ordering permits different prefixes, and larger schemas remain unvalidated. Permute fields and options while varying candidate and field counts; measure accuracy, consistency, and latency (§3.5).
- **Does calibration transfer to unfamiliar mechanisms and visual decisions?** Evidence comes from a small synthetic pilot and textual Hard items. Test a fixed temperature on held-out mechanism families and labeled screenshot decisions (§§5.2, 5.4).
- **Do local decisions improve complete agent performance?** Demonstrations provide no controlled agent benchmark. Evaluate repeated game and browser tasks with fixed planners, seeds, comparison controllers, and end-to-end timing (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive findings and their stated scope; causal and generalization conclusions remain limited.

Material missing information includes matched architectural ablations, independent generalization tests, complete calibration-fit details, precise pilot metric definitions, full latency protocols, handoff threshold-selection details, quantitative visual evaluations, and reproducible external-baseline adapters.
