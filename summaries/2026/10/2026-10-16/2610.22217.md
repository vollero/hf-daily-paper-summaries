# Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation for Unified Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22217) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22217)


## One-sentence takeaway

SCOPED combines independently trained visual experts through teacher-only skill conditioning and on-policy distillation, improving the eight-benchmark average of Qwen3-VL-8B from 67.30 to 73.13, although reduced reward hacking is not directly demonstrated (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Joint reinforcement learning across heterogeneous visual capabilities can produce conflicting gradients and weaken specialization. Independently trained experts avoid this acquisition-stage interference, but distilling their predictions may also transfer undesirable reward-driven behaviors. The authors identify a gap in explicitly extracting reusable strategies and using them to guide multi-expert integration (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Independent capability acquisition:** Train separate experts for perception, OCR/chart understanding, symbolic reasoning, and multi-image reasoning using capability-specific rewards (§3.1; Appendix A.3).
2. **Explicit skill extraction:** Separate reward-evaluated training trajectories into successful and failed cohorts, reflect on their patterns, and synthesize one procedural skill per capability. Both cohorts contribute complementary guidance (Appendix A.5; Table 7).
3. **Asymmetric multi-expert distillation:** The student generates responses without skills; the corresponding expert receives its skill and supervises those same response prefixes through token-level reverse KL. The resulting student requires no skill prompt at inference (§3.2, Eqs. 8–9).
4. **Diagnostic evidence:** Gradient analyses document interference in Mix-RL, while behavioral and attention analyses support the proposed role of skill conditioning (§§5.3–5.4).

The distinctive contribution is the combination of trajectory-derived, capability-specific skills with teacher-only conditioning during multi-expert on-policy distillation; the paper presents the constituent RL and distillation techniques as existing approaches (§2).

These results support improved capability integration in the tested setting, with most of the gain over Mix-RL already present in distillation without skills. They do not establish superiority on every task, statistical significance, or direct removal of reward-hacking behavior. Attention, embedding similarity, and expert agreement provide supporting diagnostics rather than causal proof of cleaner reasoning.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied text contains no dedicated author-stated limitations section. Appendix A.8 explicitly clarifies that expert-retention metrics measure functional agreement on fixed trajectories, rather than downstream accuracy.

**Analyst inferences grounded in the reported setup:**

- **Restricted generalization:** Experiments cover two sizes of one backbone family and four capability groups; broader architecture or modality transfer remains untested (§4; Table 6).
- **Uncertain small differences:** No repeated-run variability, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported, limiting interpretation of the skill increment and aggregate lead over other models (Tables 1–2).
- **Reward-hacking claim exceeds direct evidence:** The paper motivates skills as filtering reward-driven biases but reports no dedicated reward-hacking measurement or adversarial verifier test (§3.2; §5).
- **Compute fairness is unclear:** Separate expert training, trajectory reflection, and student distillation introduce additional work, but total costs and compute-matched comparisons are not reported (Appendices A.2, A.5).
- **Split documentation is incomplete:** PixMo-Count appears in both training sources and evaluation. Authors state that evaluation is held out, but do not provide the split identifiers or overlap audit in the supplied text (Appendices A.1, A.5).

## Open research questions

- **Do skills reduce reward hacking directly?** Current evidence measures benchmark performance and behavioral proxies. Test skill-conditioned and unconditioned teachers on deliberately exploitable rewards, with independent correctness checks.
- **Does SCOPED retain its advantage under equal compute?** Total pipeline costs are unavailable. Compare SCOPED with longer Mix-RL and unconditioned distillation under matched GPU-hour budgets.
- **How sensitive is performance to skill construction?** The paper tests trajectory sources and matched versus random skills, but not reward thresholds or skill granularity. Sweep success thresholds and compare one capability-level skill with multiple subtype-specific skills.
- **Does the method transfer beyond Qwen3-VL?** Both tested scales share a backbone family. Repeat the controlled ablation on another VLM family with the same data and evaluation protocol.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and tabulated comparisons, but provides limited evidence for the stronger mechanism claims.

Material missing information includes exact diagnostic plot values; actual prompt and extracted-skill contents beyond figure captions; the success threshold and retained trajectory counts; detailed RL optimizer configuration; parameter-merging and baseline training recipes; evaluation decoding settings; uncertainty estimates; total compute costs; and explicit split-overlap checks. Code and data availability is an author claim, not independently verified here.
