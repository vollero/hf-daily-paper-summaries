# Calibrated Typed Decisions for Entity, Relation, and Event Extraction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17113) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17113)


## One-sentence takeaway
CalOn combines schema-conditioned span extraction, sampled-decision rewards, and probability correction to report strong cross-dataset extraction results, although unseen entity types and reliable end-to-end event extraction remain unestablished.

## Problem and motivation
Information extraction must follow user-defined schemas while transferring across domains and annotation conventions. The authors argue that accepting new type descriptions alone does not ensure this transfer, while generative extraction can introduce unsupported mentions or alter boundaries (§1).

The target is a single extractor that preserves input spans, handles entities, relations, and events, and provides useful confidence estimates despite heavily imbalanced extraction decisions.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified typed decisions:** CalOn uses Qwen3-0.6B with its causal mask removed to jointly encode text and schema definitions. Interaction layers and bilinear heads classify spans or ordered span pairs over requested types plus a null class. Events use trigger extraction followed by argument extraction (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Separate extraction from typing:** Candidates are accepted using total non-null probability, then assigned their highest-probability type. Unknown annotation statuses are excluded rather than treated as negatives (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Reward-augmented supervision:** Gaussian perturbations produce alternative decisions. Correct types receive positive rewards, incorrect extractions negative rewards, and null decisions zero; rewards relative to other samples supplement likelihood training without a critic (§3.3).
- **Probability correction:** A null-logit adjustment compensates for down-weighting null candidates during training, followed by calibration refitting. This applies an existing correction principle to span and span-pair extraction rather than introducing a new general calibration principle (§§2, 3.4).

Training uses 3.1M instances from 84 human-labelled, LLM-annotated, and synthetic sources; target-dataset labels are excluded from training, selection, and calibration (§4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** English only; spans capped at 32 tokens; relations confined to one input; LLM annotation biases may persist; calibration can degrade under domain shift; training and evaluation use a single seed (Limitations).
- **Unseen schemas remain untested for NER:** Every evaluated entity type name appears verbatim in training. The NER evidence concerns domain and guideline transfer (§F).
- **Comparison asymmetries:** Some baselines receive names alone while CalOn receives descriptions. Adapted gold-entity scores for several baselines still require mention identification; gold-trigger argument adaptations also differ. Some checkpoints have relevant dataset or sentence exposure (§§4, B.3).
- **Converted-task scope:** LitBank and MAVEN-ERE evaluation excludes cross-chunk links and measures within-input span pairs, not the original document-level tasks (§B.2).
- **Calibration coverage:** ECE concerns returned predictions, not all candidates; event arguments remain poorly calibrated on most evaluated datasets (§E).
- **Analyst inference:** Component-level causal attribution is limited: reward benefits vary by task and schema presentation, while data scaling changes both data volume and compute (§5.3; §H). Single-seed results leave the reliability of small differences unresolved.

## Open research questions
1. **Does CalOn transfer to genuinely unseen entity types?** All evaluated NER names occur in training (§F). A next experiment could withhold semantic type families and their paraphrases, then evaluate names and descriptions separately.
2. **How reliable is the reward contribution?** Reported benefits concentrate on RE and vary with descriptions (§5.3). Repeat matched reward/no-reward training across seeds and report per-task uncertainty.
3. **Can confidence remain reliable under stronger domain shifts?** Calibration uses training-source validation data, and event-argument ECE remains high (§E). Compare fixed calibration with event-specific and limited target-domain calibration on held-out domains.
4. **What limits end-to-end event arguments?** Gold-trigger results substantially exceed predicted-trigger results (§C). Compare gold and predicted triggers while controlling argument candidates to measure error propagation.
