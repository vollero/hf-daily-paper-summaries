# WOVEN: Weaving Visual World Modeling into Multimodal LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12417) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12417)


## One-sentence takeaway
WOVEN provides controlled synthetic visual-transition supervision that improves MLLM reasoning across external benchmarks, with evidence favoring selection by reasoning operation and explicit boundaries on transfer (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
MLLMs struggle to reason about visual change across spatial, physical, embodied, and temporal tasks. The authors hypothesize a shared weakness in relating an initial state, an action, and its outcome, rather than unrelated task-specific deficits. Existing benchmarks measure these weaknesses separately but lack the controlled scene, action, and reasoning variations needed to identify transferable supervision (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled dataset and diagnostic benchmark:** WOVEN contains 36,076 four-option questions organized across 20 scenes, five action types, and eight reasoning operations. Its splits test new transitions, held-out scenes, and initial-state perturbations; typed distractors support error analysis (§2).
- **Synthetic transition supervision:** Wan2.2-I2V-A14B generates outcomes and intermediate frames from initial images and action descriptions, including alternative-action rollouts from the same initial state. Outcomes are generated rather than specified in prompts (§2.3).
- **Transfer study:** The authors compare full-set supervised fine-tuning with controlled action–reasoning subsets trained using SFT followed by GRPO, testing whether different sources teach a reusable capability (§§3–4).
- **Training recipe:** Controlled comparisons favor matching reasoning operations, using larger state changes for robustness, and retaining dedicated temporal, agent-action, and static-perception supervision. Prospective tests support several recipe components, but do not test the robustness component (§5; Appendix J).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Partial replacement of task data | On SAT, ActionEQA, and CLEVRER, replacing 30–50% of task-specific items with selected WOVEN supervision preserves comparable accuracy under a fixed 2,000-item budget, averaged over three seeds. | §4; Figure 3 |

These results support useful, content-dependent transfer from transition supervision. The authors interpret them as establishing a shared training primitive; **analyst inference:** correlations and substitution experiments support that interpretation without uniquely identifying an internal mechanism. The 22-benchmark result combines separately selected models, rather than demonstrating one model improving all 22. Selection and seed checks cover ten representative benchmarks (Appendix I.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** WOVEN evaluates single-step, vision-grounded transitions, not long-horizon planning, symbolic-environment dynamics, or latent world models assessed through control (Appendix K).
- **Observed transfer boundaries:** Temporal reasoning requires direct supervision; passive-event supervision can hurt agent-action benchmarks; static perception and general video QA do not show broad improvements (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—synthetic validity:** Rollouts inherit a generator’s transition priors. Real-image transfer provides supporting evidence, but does not establish comprehensive physical fidelity (§2.3; Appendix H.1).
- **Analyst inference—robustness attribution:** Larger-state-change findings compare different action subsets, leaving other content differences as possible explanations. Prospective validation explicitly excludes this recipe component (§5.2; Appendix J).
- **Reported evaluation caveats:** Prospective tests apply no family-wise correction; ID and OOD temporal-ordering distractors differ; some control advantages partly reflect degraded control models (Appendices J, G.17.3, H.1).

## Open research questions

- **Does transfer extend to multistep action sequences?** Single-step scope leaves this unresolved (Appendix K). Next experiment: train transition-only and sequence-supervised models under matched budgets and evaluate chained predictions and planning.
- **Does state-change magnitude itself cause robustness gains?** Existing comparisons vary action content (§5.2). Next experiment: vary change magnitude within the same action and reasoning operation, then evaluate matched perturbations.
- **How broadly does the recipe generalize across model families?** Prospective validation uses Qwen2.5-VL-3B, while Gemma replication covers one subset and selected tasks (Appendix I.2; Appendix J). Next experiment: repeat the prospective mixture comparisons across families.
- **Can one mixture recover the best-subset coverage?** Broad coverage relies on benchmark-specific selection (§3.3). Next experiment: fix one mixture before evaluation and compare its transfer profile with the selected-subset results.
