# GroundAnything: Reconciling Parallel Decoding with Precise Visual Grounding at Flash Speed

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39600) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39600)


## One-sentence takeaway

GroundAnything applies blockwise masked diffusion to broad visual grounding, reporting faster COCO decoding than its autoregressive counterpart while trading substantial OCR, layout, and pointing accuracy for task-dependent gains in referring and GUI grounding.

## Problem and motivation

Autoregressive grounding serializes labels and coordinates, creating latency for dense spatial outputs and limiting each prediction to its generated prefix. The authors argue that spatial predictions depend jointly on image evidence without requiring left-to-right recovery. They identify a gap between domain-specific diffusion grounding systems and a unified model covering heterogeneous grounding tasks (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified grounding model:** GroundAnything combines MoonViT-V2, a projector, and a Qwen3-4B decoder with shared text, structural, and quantized-coordinate tokens. “4B” describes the language backbone; the complete model has approximately 4.844B parameters (§§3.2, 6.2).
- **Grounding-focused diffusion adaptation:** After multimodal pretraining and spatial SFT, conversion jointly optimizes equally weighted causal prediction and masked denoising. Attention is bidirectional inside response blocks and causal across blocks. Subsequent GRPO uses causal rollouts; the updated weights also support diffusion inference (§§6.4–6.8).
- **Annotation engine:** Field-level teacher fusion, task validation, coverage checks, and iterative expert annotation produce supervision. Empty predictions require explicit absence verification (§6.3).
- **Decoding and acceleration study:** Direct entropy-guided decoding commits tokens without AR verification. Optional self-speculation instead combines diffusion proposals with causal verification. Controlled drafting comparisons and infrastructure experiments examine when parallelism yields actual latency gains (§§7–9).

The contribution is this broad grounding system and its empirical study; the paper explicitly builds on prior block diffusion, conversion, and decoding methods.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Authors report strong overall grounding averages | Across 30 benchmarks: AR GroundAnything-VLM **72.42%**, direct-diffusion GroundAnything **61.75%**, LocateAnything Fast **53.32%**, GPT-6 Astra **71.35%**. These aggregate heterogeneous task metrics. | Abstract; §§4.1–4.2 |
| Direct diffusion accelerates COCO decoding with a quality cost | At block size 32 and entropy threshold 0.8: **127.0 TPS, 60.72 F1mIoU**, versus AR **49.6 TPS, 63.70 F1mIoU**: **2.56×** throughput and **2.98 pp** lower quality. | §9.1 |
| Optional self-speculation gives a better reported COCO operating point | Linear self-speculation at block size 32: **223.8 TPS, 62.96 F1mIoU**; **4.51×** AR throughput with a **0.74 pp** quality gap. This mode includes AR verification. | §9.1 |
| Diffusion gains vary by task | ScreenSpot-Pro action accuracy rises from AR **65.34%** to diffusion **75.96%**; Dense200 diffusion reaches **70.04 F1mIoU**, below AR **75.55**. | §§10.7, 10.2; Tables 27, 17 |
| OCR and layout retain substantial AR advantages | SROIE loose-match F1mIoU: diffusion **43.64**, AR **71.15**. DocLayNet F1mIoU: diffusion **68.38**, AR **85.78**. | §§10.6, 10.8; Tables 26, 29 |
| Controlled DLM proposals can be cheaper than causal MTP proposals | On held-out COCO prefixes at candidate length 16, one-step DLM accepts **3.48** draft tokens/round versus **2.69**, lowering full-round cost per accepted draft token from **7.71 to 5.38 ms**. Shared visual encoding and prefill are excluded. | §8.2; Figure 11 |
| Bidirectional attention improves draft reliability | At matched candidate length 8 and two refinement steps, Dense200 unmatched valid-proposal rate is **14.79%** for bidirectional DLM versus **21.74%** for retrained causal DLM. This is a proposal metric, not ground-truth recall. | §§8.4, 4.3.2; Figure 13 |
| Execution optimizations provide additional implementation gains | SGLang, CUDA Graph, and selective FP8 yield **3.45–4.06×** speedups relative to each mode’s native PyTorch implementation; no matched quality result accompanies every stage. | §9.2; Figure 14 |

The authors average their model results over ten runs (§4.1), but the supplied text provides no corresponding uncertainty estimates. The evidence supports useful parallel grounding and specific speed–quality trade-offs, not uniform superiority. COCO speedups do not establish equivalent gains across the full benchmark suite. Exact self-speculative verification preserves the converted model’s causal output, rather than the separately trained GroundAnything-VLM output (§9.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Reported weaknesses:** Tiny-object boundaries, OCR, document layouts, and point-set prediction remain difficult; diffusion can lose substantial accuracy relative to AR (§§10.2, 10.4, 10.6, 10.8).
- **Restricted speed evidence:** Controlled drafting timing excludes visual encoding and prefill; deadline tests use long-output prefixes. MTP wins under the shortest reported deadline, and additional refinement can increase cost (§§8.2–8.3).
- **Comparison boundaries:** The primary controlled MTP baseline is in-house and is not LocateAnything. Training exposure is matched, but trainable parameter counts and training FLOPs are not necessarily equal (§8.1). Bidirectional block-MTP also improves proposal quality (§8.4).
- **Missing isolation:** The whole-phase “Stage III” ablation does not isolate reward components or optimization choices; its label is distinct from numbered pretraining stages (§9.3). Encoder comparisons do not isolate intermediate visual injection (§9.3.1).
- **Analyst inference—aggregate comparability:** Mixed metrics, unsupported interfaces, external scores, and uncertain prompt alignment limit interpretation of the overall ranking. The exact overall aggregation and missing-score treatment are not explicit in the supplied text (§§4.2, 10).
- **Analyst inference—reporting ambiguity:** RefCOCOg val/test scores in §10.3 differ from its family-summary RefCOCOg entry without a clear reconciliation.
- **Reproducibility limits:** Most table bodies are absent from the retrieved text. Release is promised, not confirmed; FP8 accuracy preservation is unestablished (§9.2; Reproducibility Statement).
- **Deployment scope:** Applications are prospective; real-world robotic control and safety-critical deployment are not evaluated (Ethics Statement; §10.10).

## Open research questions

1. **Can diffusion recover OCR and layout quality while retaining speed?** Large task-specific gaps remain. A next experiment could compare task-adaptive commitment policies against fixed entropy guidance at matched end-to-end latency (§§10.6–10.8).
2. **How much advantage comes from diffusion beyond bidirectional attention?** Block-MTP also benefits from bidirectionality. Compare both methods with matched training compute, capacity, and elapsed inference time (§8.4).
3. **Do the speed gains persist for short interactive requests?** Prefill is excluded from controlled drafting costs, and short deadlines favor MTP. Measure full-request median and tail latency across output lengths (§§8.2–8.3, 10.10).
4. **Does selective FP8 preserve grounding quality?** Infrastructure results report latency without a complete quality ablation. Repeat task evaluations under BF16 and FP8, including strict-overlap and commitment-decision diagnostics (§9.2).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported trade-offs, but independent assessment is limited by missing table bodies, uncertainty estimates, timing hardware and workload details, exact overall-score aggregation, detailed training-data composition and overlap checks, and confirmation that the promised artifacts were released.
