# Scaling Agentic Data for Long-Horizon Terminal Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13166) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13166)


## One-sentence takeaway
TerminalHorizon synthesizes terminal tasks that preserve substantive work and dependencies across stages, with fine-tuning on 2,852 trajectories raising reported Terminal-Bench 2.0 mean pass@1 from 41.6% to 55.8%, although the individual synthesis components are not causally isolated (Table 1; Appendix A.2).

## Problem and motivation
Terminal agents need training experience that connects exploration, implementation, diagnosis, verification, and strategy revision over sustained work. The authors identify two gaps: constructed environments can inadvertently supply work the agent should perform, and increasing task difficulty or interaction count alone need not create meaningful dependencies across stages (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Challenge-guided synthesis:** Agents research professional problems and construct executable task packages. An independent challenger sees only public instructions and the environment; its execution exposes leakage, scoring defects, and work completed prematurely during construction, guiding reconstruction (§2.2).
- **Progressive horizon expansion:** Accepted tasks acquire additional requirements under the same goal. New decisions depend on earlier evidence, artifacts, or system feedback, rather than merely adding steps (§2.3).
- **Behavior-centered data and models:** The authors organize training experience around 27 behavioral patterns in five families, construct TerminalHorizon-3K, and perform full-parameter supervised fine-tuning of Qwen3.5 models. Filtering retains useful feedback-driven trajectories even when final task completion fails (§3; Appendices A.1, E–F).

The reported results support improvement after fine-tuning and illustrate sustained experimentation in four RSI cases. They do not establish that behavioral connections, trajectory length, reconstruction, or expansion individually cause the gains. Fewer trajectories also do not establish lower token, synthesis, or compute cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no dedicated author limitations section. Authors explicitly qualify RSI evidence as one run per model per case, and behavioral-organization examples as qualitative rather than frequency estimates (Appendices C, E.2).

**Analyst inferences:**

- No reported ablation separately removes challenge-guided reconstruction or progressive expansion, and no token-matched short-trajectory control isolates their contribution (§4.3).
- RSI permits voluntary early stopping, so comparisons combine method quality with differences in research time used; repeated, compute-matched runs are absent (Appendix C).
- Filtering and semantic decontamination rely on an automated reviewer. Task-description checks do not establish absence of overlap in environment files or pretraining exposure (Appendices F–G).
- Cross-paper comparisons vary in model size, harness, and training method; even the closely matched Terminal-Universe comparison does not control training tokens or compute (Table 1; Appendix B.2).
- Repeated benchmark runs are described, but confidence intervals are unavailable; several numerical tables and plots are missing from the retrieved text.

## Open research questions
- **Which synthesis stage drives improvement?** Component effects remain unisolated. Run reconstruction/expansion factorial ablations with matched teacher, training tokens, and task coverage.
- **Do connected trajectories outperform equally costly shorter data?** Trajectory counts leave token efficiency unresolved. Compare long connected demonstrations against token-matched short or segmented trajectories.
- **Are RSI gains robust to randomness and research allocation?** Four single-run cases cannot establish stability. Repeat each case across seeds under both equal time and equal execution budgets.
- **How reliable are automated quality judgments?** Filtering and overlap review lack reported human agreement. Audit stratified accepted/rejected trajectories and retrieved/non-retrieved task pairs with independent human reviewers.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several comparisons are explicit, but causal attribution and some metrics remain incomplete.

Material omissions include Table 2’s absolute scores, Table 3’s teacher statistics, numerical scaling curves, uncertainty estimates, synthesis cost, reviewer reliability, full synthesis prompt configurations, and verified contents of the linked data/model release.
