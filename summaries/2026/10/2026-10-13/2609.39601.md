# GroundingPI: A Grounding Foundation Model towards Physical Intelligence with Visual Primitives

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39601) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39601)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that GroundingPI’s specialized grounding pretraining improves aggregate perception, simulated manipulation generalization, and demonstration efficiency, while driving evidence remains limited to open-loop trajectory prediction (§§5.1–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Physical interaction requires locating the intended object and relevant region precisely, especially in clutter or among tiny objects. The authors argue that general-purpose vision-language and video-generation backbones leave this perception burden to scarce action demonstrations, motivating a foundation model that learns spatial grounding before action adaptation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified grounding model:** MoonViT-V2 feeds a Qwen3-4B decoder through a learned projector. Labels, points, and boxes share an autoregressive output vocabulary with 1,000 atomic coordinate tokens. Despite the “4B” designation, the complete multimodal model has approximately 4.844B parameters (§§3.1, 7.1–7.2).
- **Specialized training and annotation:** General multimodal pretraining precedes spatial supervised fine-tuning and language-only GRPO post-training. A multi-teacher data engine validates semantic fields, geometry, and coverage, then iteratively refines annotations (§§3.2–3.3, 7.3–7.6).
- **Controlled downstream transfer:** Driving uses coordinate-token trajectory generation; manipulation couples backbone features to a common flow-matching Action DiT. Manipulation comparisons hold action architecture, data, training budget, and inference procedure fixed (§§4, 8.2).
- **Pretraining analysis:** Scaling and data-mixture ablations examine which perceptual supervision transfers. Dense grounding benefits both downstream domains; OCR’s proposed catalytic mechanism remains a hypothesis (§5.3).

These contributions concern the combined model, training recipe, and transfer evidence; the text does not establish that autoregressive coordinate generation or GRPO itself is new (§2.1; §3.3.3).

All results below are author-reported.

The evidence supports useful transfer under the evaluated interfaces, without establishing universal benchmark dominance or isolating grounding supervision as the sole cause of cross-model differences. Author-model results average ten runs; locally evaluated leaderboard baselines average three, and some scores retain external protocols (§5.1). The supplied text lacks uncertainty estimates needed to assess the significance of small differences.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Remaining gaps include tiny boxes, strict boundaries, TotalText OCR, FSC147 visual prompting, and frontier-level GUI grounding (§11).
- **Author-stated:** Prompt/parser incompatibility constrains baseline measurements; unsupported outputs do not demonstrate absent intrinsic capability (§§5.1, 11–12).
- **Author-stated:** Fixed downstream training does not match pretrained scale, architecture, or supervision, so architectural causes remain unresolved (§§10.1, 11).
- **Author-stated:** Driving is open loop, robots are simulated, and demonstration efficiency is evaluated only in manipulation. Physical prompting and an integrated real-time dual-system controller remain untested (Ethics Statement; §§10.4, 11).
- **Analyst inference:** The OCR-containing-group ablation cannot by itself isolate OCR-specific causality. Missing ablations indicated in Figure 9 further limit mixture attribution (§5.3.2).
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated, and many referenced tables contain captions without numerical bodies. Complete experimental and reproducibility coverage is therefore unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer survive real closed-loop execution?** Offline driving and simulated manipulation leave this open. Next experiment: compare matched policies in real robot tasks while measuring success, perception failures, and observation-to-action latency (§§8.1, 10.4; Ethics Statement).
- **What specifically makes OCR supervision useful?** The observed group-removal effect does not establish the proposed mechanism. Next experiment: hold data exposure fixed and separate transcription, text localization, and other auxiliary supervision (§5.3.2).
- **How much gain comes from supervision versus architecture?** Complete foundations differ in several respects. Next experiment: train matched backbones with and without grounding specialization and compare both perception and action transfer (§§10.1, 11).
- **Can tiny-box precision improve without excessive generation cost?** Strict localization gaps and autoregressive costs persist. Next experiment: vary coordinate resolution under matched training and report tiny-object accuracy alongside latency (§§5.3.4, 12.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the core method and selected results, but the truncated source prevents complete coverage.
