# Thinking Inertia: LLMs Keep Thinking When Told Not To

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11765) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11765)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the evaluated LLMs, disabling thinking does not reliably suppress visible inference, while strict answer-only prompting improves compliance but can reduce open-ended math accuracy (§§3.2–3.3).

## Problem and motivation
Thinking switches, absent reasoning tags, and short outputs are unreliable indicators of answer-only behavior: compact inference can remain visible, while longer text may contain only filler. The authors identify a gap in measuring this behavior independently of task accuracy, motivated by reasoning control, efficiency, and adaptive answering (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Measurement framework:** Decompose responses into visible pre-answer text and a final answer. Measure strict answer-only compliance with Empty-Thinking Rate (ETR), semantic relevance with Question–Pre-answer Relevance (QRel.), and explicit inference with a blinded judge’s Explicit Inference Rate (EIR). Accuracy is evaluated separately (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Controlled behavioral study:** Compare native thinking on/off, step-by-step prompting, soft and strict suppression, and answer-prefix forcing across model families and answer spaces. Same-question rewrites help isolate answer-interface effects (§§2.4, 3.1, 3.5).
- **Validation and findings:** Human annotations, alternative encoders, and an independent judge support the reported answer-space ordering and suppression–accuracy trade-off (§3.6; Appendix D). This is an evaluation contribution; no new trained model is described.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Native-off inference increases as answer space opens | Full-corpus GPT-5.5 EIR under M2: Boolean **5.15%**, MCQ **45.57%**, open-ended **99.69%**; under strict M5: **0.12%**, **25.39%**, **62.00%**, respectively. These are all-output rates across the evaluated corpus. | §3.3; Table D.3 |
| Strict suppression can cost open-ended accuracy | Across the five models with fully disabled thinking, M2→M5 raises mean open-ended ETR from near zero to roughly **40%**, while mean accuracy falls about **15 percentage points**. | §3.3; Figure 5 |
| Identical question content behaves differently across interfaces | Qwen3-32B, M2, matched numeric MMLU/MMLU-Pro items: MCQ→Boolean changes accuracy **77.8%→78.9%**, ETR **10.5%→62.5%**, EIR **86.95%→36.90%**. Open generation yields accuracy **52.6%**, ETR **0%**, EIR **90.14%**. | §3.5; Figure 6; Table I.5 |
| Prefix forcing is not consistently stronger suppression | M5→M6 lowers mean ETR on MCQ/open-ended tasks and increases mean QRel. and EIR in every answer-space family. | §3.3; Figure 5 |
| Human validation supports the metrics | Five annotators on **600 outputs**: mean QRel.–relevance Spearman correlation **0.829**; mean binary inference agreement **98.8%** with GPT-5.5 and **96.7%** with Opus 4.8. | §3.6; Tables D.6–D.7 |
| Independent judging preserves the main result | Opus 4.8 open-ended EIR falls **98.55%→61.02%** from M2→M5, closely tracking GPT-5.5’s **99.69%→62.00%**. | Table D.3 |

The evidence supports persistent **visible inference** and answer-interface dependence in these settings. It does not establish that hidden computation stops, that exposed reasoning faithfully represents computation, or that answer-only output reduces actual inference cost (§§4–5.2). The approximate five-model compliance/accuracy means and full-corpus EIR rates use different aggregation scopes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope:** Results concern observable text. Multilingual, code, tool-use, multi-turn, stochastic settings, and evolving proprietary endpoints remain untested (§5.1).
- **Authors’ measurement limitation:** QRel. detects relevance, not reasoning; EIR depends on rubric interpretation and judge calibration despite human and cross-family validation (§5.1; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—task confounding:** The main open-ended benchmarks are both mathematical. Cross-benchmark answer-space comparisons therefore also vary domain; matched numeric rewrites reduce this confound but do not establish generality outside numeric QA (§§3.1, 3.5).
- **Analyst inference—unequal controls and weighting:** Gemini uses “minimal” rather than fully disabled thinking; local models use full splits while API models use sampled items. Pooled corpus rates consequently reflect unequal model contributions (Appendices B–C).
- **Analyst inference—measurement ambiguity:** §2.2 defines pre-answer text by position, while Appendix E describes retaining explanations after an answer in the relevance input; handling of these cases is not completely specified across all metrics.
- **Analyst inference—reporting inconsistency:** §3.4 reports a STEM ETR range starting at 0.3%, whereas Table I.1 gives Qwen3-235B’s native-off STEM ETR as 6.2%. The broader domain trend remains supported, but exact values require reconciliation.

## Open research questions
- **Does answer-space dependence generalize beyond math?** Open generation is mainly tested on math. Next experiment: matched Boolean, MCQ, and free-response rewrites of nonnumeric questions, evaluated across models.
- **Does compliance reduce actual computation?** Visible output cannot establish hidden reasoning or cost (§5.2). Next experiment: pair ETR/EIR with local-model computation measurements and exposed provider reasoning-token counts.
- **Can calibrated stopping preserve accuracy?** The paper evaluates suppression interventions rather than training a stopping policy (§5.2). Next experiment: train task-aware direct-answer routing and compare accuracy, compliance, latency, and reasoning cost with strict prompting.
- **How stable are the metrics under response-order changes?** Answer-first explanations are audited, but their treatment is not fully clear across metrics (Appendix E). Next experiment: evaluate a manually labeled set of answer-first, malformed, and mixed-channel responses.
