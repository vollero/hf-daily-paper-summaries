# Visual Grounding Safety in Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05637) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05637)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across five VLMs, harmful requests receive substantially fewer refusals when asking for spatial coordinates than text, while grounding-specific fine-tuning substantially improves refusal in two models with preserved benchmark capability and increased benign over-refusal (Tables 3, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Points and bounding boxes can guide downstream clicks or physical actions, making harmful localization a safety concern. The authors identify a gap in alignment evaluation: text refusal does not necessarily transfer to a model’s grounding interface. They study this gap using matched image–request pairs that preserve intent while changing the requested response form (§§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled evaluation:** Repurpose VLSU, BBQ-V, and Asimov-2.0 into 15,401 harmful VQA–grounding pairs spanning direct harm, social bias, and situational safety; evaluate five VLMs, prompting conditions, benign controls, and grounding capability (§2; Table 1).
- **Grounding safety fine-tuning:** Combine harmful grounding refusals, model-specific self-distilled benign responses, and capability grounding examples. Train plain-text or structured placeholder refusals, updating the language model while freezing the vision encoder and projector (§3.1; Table 5).
- **Behavioral and representation analysis:** Ablate supervision form and data components, then examine whether harmful representations move toward a text-derived refusal direction. The authors claim the controlled comparison is novel; the representation technique itself builds on prior work (§§3.3–4, 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Grounding refusal consistently trails text refusal | Without safety prompts, five-model mean harmful refusal, VQA → grounding: VLSU **62.9% → 19.4%**; BBQ-V **75.9% → 16.7%**; Asimov-2.0 **43.8% → 12.7%**. Respective evaluation sizes: 659, 14,578, 164 items. Every model–domain comparison has the same direction. | Table 6; §2.3 |
| Prompting leaves a substantial gap | Across four models with system-role support, grounding refusal remains below VQA refusal in every domain and prompt condition. Qwen3-VL’s placeholder prompt reduces BBQ-V grounding refusal from **15.0% to 11.8%**. | Tables 2, 7 |
| Fine-tuning substantially improves harmful grounding refusal | Qwen3-VL SFT-plain, base → tuned: VLSU **9.7% → 96.5%**; held-out BBQ-V **15.0% → 92.8%**; held-out Asimov domain **4.9% → 68.9%**. VisionReasoner SFT-plain: **3.9% → 97.4%**, **0.6% → 95.8%**, **3.0% → 85.4%**, respectively. VQA refusal also improves across all reported checkpoints and domains. | Table 3 |
| Capability is retained, but benign refusal increases | PointArena success on 982 items, base → SFT-plain: Qwen3-VL **65.5% → 65.4%**; VisionReasoner **62.4% → 64.1%**. In-domain benign over-refusal is at most **5.3%** across checkpoints; held-out MOSSBench VQA over-refusal rises **15.3% → 24.7%** and **2.3% → 13.0%**, respectively. | Tables 1, 3 |
| Supervision-form transfer is asymmetric | Qwen3-VL VQA-only safety ablation yields grounding refusal of **1.8%, 0.0%, 3.0%** on VLSU, BBQ-V, Asimov, below base values. Grounding-only safety supervision yields **94.2%, 91.7%, 71.3%**, but MOSSBench benign over-refusal reaches **55.0%**. | Tables 4, 13–14 |
| High refusal can reflect capability failure | Removing capability and benign grounding-coordinate supervision produces **6.7%** PointArena success and near-total harmful grounding refusal; empty outputs count as refusal. Removing benign compliance data instead produces **100.0%** benign BBQ-V grounding over-refusal. | Table 14 |
| Harmful representations shift more than benign controls | Mean normalized grounding position on the refusal axis, base → SFT-plain: VisionReasoner harmful **0.08 → 0.49**, benign **0.04 → 0.11**; Qwen3-VL harmful **0.23 → 0.56**, benign **0.15 → 0.25**. Averages span mid-to-upper layers and applicable domains. | Table 23; Appendix A.11 |

These results support an output-interface safety gap and an effective mitigation in the tested settings. They do not establish safety in deployed agents or robots, robustness against optimized attacks, or a causal role for the measured representation shifts.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged comparison confound:** Qwen3-VL plain and placeholder checkpoints use different training mixtures, preventing isolation of refusal-target format (§3.2; Table 5).
- **Observed transfer limits:** Benign over-refusal increases on MOSSBench, and residual Asimov failures concentrate on robot- and property-directed harm (§3.2; Tables 9, 17).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** Gemini 3.5 Flash performs repurposing, quality control, and safety scoring. No independent human agreement study is reported; irrelevant or empty grounding can count as refusal (Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Analyst inference—ablation confounding:** VQA-only and grounding-only variants differ in refusal counts and benign supervision, so they support asymmetric transfer without fully isolating supervision form (Table 13).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Mitigation is tested on two backbones; capability retention rests on PointArena, and Asimov lacks a benign control. No repeated-run uncertainty or downstream placeholder-action validation is reported.

## Open research questions
- **Does the transfer asymmetry survive balanced supervision?** Existing form ablations differ in counts and benign data. Next experiment: match refusal counts and benign mixtures while varying only safety-supervision form.
- **Can situational safety improve without excessive caution?** Robot/property failures remain, and Asimov benign behavior is unmeasured. Next experiment: add matched benign situational controls and test targeted training.
- **How reliable is the refusal metric?** Empty or irrelevant predictions can inflate apparent safety. Next experiment: independently human-audit outputs and separate explicit refusal, safe redirection, and localization failure.
- **Do placeholders safely halt downstream actions?** The paper evaluates model outputs rather than executed actions. Next experiment: test both refusal formats in instrumented GUI or robot simulations.

## Confidence and missing information
**High** confidence in the reported behavioral comparisons; **medium** confidence in their broader safety interpretation.

Material missing information includes independent judge validation, repeated-run confidence intervals, executed downstream safety tests, Asimov benign controls, and an available code release—the supplied text promises code upon acceptance. Appendix A.10 also reports inconsistent base VLSU reasoning-refusal values across Tables 19 and 20.
