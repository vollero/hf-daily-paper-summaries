# CrossTimeEdit: A Decade-Spanning Cross-View Dataset and Reward-Guided Editing for Historical Street-View Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36616) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36616)


## One-sentence takeaway
CrossTimeEdit edits recent street panoramas using satellite-derived historical instructions, improving the reported Editing Overall score by 17.12% over its pretrained backbone on VIGOR-his, while leaving historical-detail ambiguity and evaluator dependence unresolved (§5.2, Table 2; Appendix E–F).

## Problem and motivation
Historical street imagery has uneven coverage, leaving gaps in records of urban change. The authors argue that generating entire street scenes from satellite imagery can unnecessarily alter persistent buildings and roads because overhead observations provide limited street-level detail. Existing datasets do not jointly supply temporal street–satellite pairs and local editing instructions, motivating both a new dataset and an editing formulation (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Temporal cross-view dataset:** VIGOR-his contains 43,653 location-level quadruplets across 11 cities on three continents, pairing earlier/recent panoramas and satellite images approximately a decade apart. Its pipeline supplies change categories, satellite-derived descriptions, and validated directional editing instructions (§3, Table 1).
- **Historical editing model:** CrossTimeEdit receives a recent panorama and satellite-derived `MODIFY`/`PRESERVE` instructions. Earlier panoramas serve as training and evaluation references, never generation inputs. Attention LoRA adapters on FLUX.2 [Klein] 4B undergo supervised fine-tuning, including identity supervision, followed by online RL (§4.1–4.2).
- **Task-specific reward and evaluation:** Instruction Alignment (IA), Background Preservation (BP), and Quality and Physical Plausibility (QP) assess target-correct edits, preservation, and panoramic quality. The method combines existing Flow-GRPO and GDPO normalization rather than introducing those algorithms; its contribution is their integration with this task and reward protocol (§4.3–4.4).

These results support improved editing and perceptual performance within the supplied benchmark. They do not establish exact historical recovery. Cross-view baselines receive earlier satellite images, whereas CrossTimeEdit additionally receives a recent panorama and instructions, so that comparison evaluates different information settings. Human agreement supports the evaluator at the model level but does not eliminate bias from using the same VLM for rewards and evaluation.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated:** Satellite evidence cannot reliably recover facade details or occluded structures; some requested changes are not street-visible, and temporal state mismatches survive screening. Viewpoint differences and evaluator-specific preferences remain challenges. Generated images are historical hypotheses, not archival records (§6; Ethics Statement; Appendix E–F).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- Evaluation on changed locations leaves no-change identity performance unestablished (§5.1).
- VLM screening and instruction validation select sufficiently consistent, executable examples; performance on rejected or poorly aligned locations is unestablished (§3.2–3.3).
- The reported human study uses geography-related volunteers and few comparisons per model pair; its model-level agreement does not establish broad population or sample-level agreement (Appendix B.4).
- No independent-evaluator experiment, multi-run uncertainty estimates, or reward-weight sensitivity results are supplied. SFT scripts do not explicitly fix a seed (Appendix B.1, F).

## Open research questions

- **Does improvement persist under independent evaluators?** Shared reward/evaluation prompts leave this unresolved; evaluate frozen outputs with another VLM and a larger blinded human study (Appendix F).
- **How well does the model generalize geographically and to imperfect inputs?** City-held-out and low-consistency results are not supplied; test held-out cities and controlled viewpoint or temporal mismatches (§3, §5.1).
- **Can richer evidence improve historical specificity?** Facade ambiguity remains documented; compare satellite-only instructions with instructions augmented by archival photographs or building records (Appendix E–F).
- **Does editing remain conservative on unchanged scenes?** Identity supervision is included, but the test split contains changed locations; evaluate a held-out no-change set for unauthorized structural edits (§4.2, §5.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods, configurations, ablations, and human-study results, but independent validation is limited.

Material missing information includes city-wise sample distributions and split separation, accounting for dataset samples outside the stated experimental manifests, manual annotation-error audits, statistical uncertainty, runtime/API costs, and independent-evaluator results. Asset availability is author-reported and was not verified.
