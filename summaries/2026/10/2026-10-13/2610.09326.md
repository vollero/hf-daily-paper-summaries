# VIS-Ground: Video Interactive Storytelling with Contextual Grounding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09326) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09326)


## One-sentence takeaway
VIS-Ground converts a source, rendered video prefix, and viewer request into evidence-linked constraints for planning and repair, achieving the highest semantic composite scores across three backbones on a small VIS-Bench evaluation subset (Table 1; Appendix B.1).

## Problem and motivation
Interactive video continuations must satisfy new requests while respecting both source-supported information and events already shown. Dependencies can emerge only from combining these inputs—for example, a requested action may require an object transfer established by the prefix. The authors identify a gap between instruction-responsive video generation and source-grounded storytelling: neither alone explicitly determines which contextual dependencies should constrain the continuation (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Contextual-grounding formulation:** Treat heterogeneous context as material to compile into an executable constraint model for interactive continuation (Sections 2–3).
- **VIS-Ground framework:** Extract evidence-linked video, story, and source states; align them; derive dependencies; and activate constraints relevant to each candidate script. Verification returns pass, fail, or unknown, supporting iterative script and rendered-output repair (Sections 3.1–3.3). This is a prompting and orchestration framework, with no claimed new activation classifier (Appendix C.2).
- **VIS-Bench:** Introduce narrative- and knowledge-grounded continuation instances with separate assessments of source faithfulness, continuity, request fulfillment, and perceptual quality (Section 4.1; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

All benchmark scores below use a 0–100 scale. The overall composite averages narrative- and knowledge-setting JCS equally; JCS averages SF, SC, and IF. VC and VQ are excluded.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest composite across backbones | Omni: **84.2 vs 71.9** for VideoGen-of-Thought; Veo: **84.6 vs 69.3** for Direct Generation; MiniMax: **78.8 vs 75.4** for MovieAgent. Gains: **12.3, 15.3, and 3.4 points**, respectively | Table 1; Appendix D.4 |
| Higher JCS in both grounding settings | Gains over each setting’s strongest baseline: Omni **8.7 narrative / 13.9 knowledge**; Veo **9.2 / 17.0**; MiniMax **4.5 / 2.4 points** | Figure 6 |
| Structured context and iterative planning help in tested configurations | Narrative mean SF/SC/IF falls **7.60 points** with Unstructured State and **4.36 points** with One-Pass Planning relative to the full method | Section 5.2 |
| Automatic judgments show human agreement | **50 outputs**, each rated by **three humans**; ordinal Krippendorff’s α: humans **0.78–0.86**, Gemini versus human reference **0.75–0.83**, Claude **0.64–0.76**, across SF, SC, IF, VC, VQ | Appendix F.3; Table 5 |

The comparisons cover **16 instances per backbone**, shared across five methods, yielding **240 continuations**—rather than the full **2,008 planned instruction–style instances** (Appendix B.1). They support the evaluated configurations’ semantic advantage, not universal superiority, efficiency gains, or guaranteed constraint satisfaction. JCS is an arithmetic average, not an all-constraints success rate (Appendix D.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Extraction and dependency inference can fail; rendering can omit valid planned actions. Budget exhaustion does not establish verified success (Appendix C.4).
- **Author-stated:** VIS-Ground does not lead every IF, VC, or VQ comparison; conflicting requests can trade instruction fulfillment against grounding (Section 5.3).
- **Author-stated:** Baselines omit some native components under the shared renderer. Ablations do not isolate constraint induction from repair or additional computation; realized resource usage is not matched (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** The small evaluated subset limits claims about coverage of the full benchmark. Frame-based perceptual evaluation also leaves fine motion and audio quality unestablished (Appendices B.1, D.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does the advantage persist across broader inputs?** Most planned instances were unevaluated. Next experiment: evaluate a larger stratified sample by source, style, and intervention type (Appendix B.1).
- **How much improvement comes from constraints versus computation?** Current ablations confound these factors. Next experiment: compare structured and unstructured planning with matched calls, tokens, and rendering retries (Appendix E.1).
- **Where does the repair pipeline fail?** Extraction, dependency inference, and rendering remain fallible. Next experiment: annotate dependencies and realized events, then test oracle-state and oracle-dependency variants (Appendix C.4).
- **Is the prefix sufficient over repeated interventions?** Prior instruction history is omitted by formulation. Next experiment: compare prefix-only context with explicit instruction memory over multiple turns (Section 2.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported results, but generalization rests on limited evaluation. Material missing details include the exact evaluated-instance list, per-instance results, confidence-interval construction and confidence level, measured latency/cost/resource use, detailed ablation scores and settings, and confirmed public availability of code and benchmark assets.
