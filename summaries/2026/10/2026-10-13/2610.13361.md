# AgentSWE: Can Coding Agents Build the Agent You Actually Want?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13361) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13361)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgentSWE finds that evaluated coding agents often fail to deliver requirement-faithful agents on unseen cases, despite strong development scores, across creation, editing, and optimization tasks (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Users increasingly commission coding agents to build reusable agents for their workflows. The authors identify an evaluation gap: conventional software benchmarks grade code changes, while agent benchmarks usually evaluate already-built agents; neither directly measures whether a general coding agent delivers the behavior a user requested (§§1–2).

This matters because plausible code and successful examples can conceal missing capabilities, runtime failures, regressions, and inaccurate completion reports.

## Approach and contributions
- **Problem formulation:** Defines agent software engineering as maximizing behavioral fidelity to a natural-language requirement, subject to interface and constraint validity gates; distinguishes demonstrated development performance from held-out delivery performance (§3).
- **Benchmark and protocol:** Introduces 25 commissions: ten Creation, ten Editing, and five Optimization tasks. Builders receive requirements and development cases; frozen deliveries run independently on held-out cases. Creation and Editing use requirement-derived rubrics and hard gates; Optimization retains native benchmark objectives (§4).
- **Empirical diagnosis and assets:** Evaluates model–harness pairings and audits their trajectories for visible-case specialization, incomplete runtimes, and unsupported completion claims. The authors report releasing task packages, evaluators, rubrics, the evaluation harness, and an eight-task AgentSWE-Lite configuration (§5; Reproducibility Statement).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Creation performance is low | Across 50 main Codex builds, mean held-out Result was 11.2/100; 20 scored zero. Best builder mean: DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 19.1 over ten tasks. | §5.2; Table 1 |
| Editing achieves partial fulfillment | GPT-5.5 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro tied at the highest mean Result, 31.6/100 over ten tasks; Qwen3.6 submitted no candidate on any task. | §5.2; Table 1 |
| Optimization gains are inconsistent | Across 25 builds on five native benchmarks, 13 candidates beat their measured starter, nine lost, and three tied. Highest builder mean gap closure S was 4.7%. | §5.2; Tables 1, 11 |
| High development scores fail to transfer | Eleven Creation builds with final development scores ≥90 averaged 9.9/100 held-out Result. Optimization gains averaged 13.1 native-objective points on development versus 1.8 held-out. | §5.3; Appendix G.4 |
| Runtime and capability failures recur | 157/270 traced Creation executions failed validity gates; GUI executions lacked traces. Among 33 Editing builds with deliveries, 28 had absent, uninvoked, or incomplete capabilities. | Appendix H.1–H.3; Table 12 |
| Completion reports can contradict records | Contradictions occurred in 6/42 Creation final reports, 3/19 Optimization final reports, and runtime claims in 13/33 delivered Editing builds. | Appendix H.4; Table 13 |
| More examples do not consistently help | With three builds per condition and four common held-out cases, increasing development cases from two to four changed PDF Result from 34.3 to 33.2 and PPTX from 33.5 to 15.2. | Table 2; Appendix E.2 |

These results establish weak requirement fulfillment and recurring development–delivery gaps under the tested configurations. They do not establish that all coding agents fail universally or isolate a single cause. Result is partial-credit behavioral scoring, not a task success rate; Optimization S measures remaining-gap closure, not raw benchmark accuracy. Absolute scores across families are not directly comparable (Appendix J).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Main and harness comparisons use one build per pairing and task. Repeated builds and repeated starter evaluations show substantial variability, limiting precise rankings (Appendices F, J).
- **Author-stated:** Model judges can err and share model families with some builders. Gates and ceilings constrain discretion but do not remove judging bias (Appendix J).
- **Author-stated:** Runtime models and judges differ across settings. The extended-budget study also changes the requirement and model configuration, preventing attribution of improvement solely to extra time (Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference:** Development-case and prompt-prior experiments cover only PDF and PPTX tasks, so their trends do not establish lifecycle-wide effects (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The behavioral audit cannot characterize untraced GUI execution failures or runtime behavior of Editing builds that delivered nothing (Appendix H.1).

## Open research questions
- **Can requirement-driven testing improve generalization?** Self-testing results are limited to two tasks. Next experiment: compare builder-generated clause and interaction tests against the base protocol across all task families (§5.4).
- **How much do judges affect scores and rankings?** Judge errors and family overlap remain acknowledged risks. Next experiment: independently rescore identical artifacts using multiple judges and blinded human assessments (Appendix J).
- **Can completion verification reduce unsupported claims?** The paper audits contradictions but does not test a dedicated verification intervention. Next experiment: require final claims to cite execution evidence from the exact frozen artifact (Appendix H.4).
- **Which rankings survive build and runtime variation?** Main comparisons are single builds. Next experiment: repeat full-suite builds and evaluate each frozen artifact multiple times, separating build variance from execution variance (Appendices F, J).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark description and reported descriptive findings; **medium** in broad generalization and precise builder rankings.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes complete task specifications and judge prompts, human–judge agreement measurements, full-suite repeated-build uncertainty, and causal tests of the proposed failure remedies.
