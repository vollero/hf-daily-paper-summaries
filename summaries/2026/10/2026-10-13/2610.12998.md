# Evaluating LLM Creativity through Long-Tail Performance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12998) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12998)


## One-sentence takeaway
Quality on lexically novel benchmark subsets often tracks human creative-writing rankings better than quality on common subsets, supporting q@Tail as a creativity-relevant diagnostic rather than a standalone creativity measure (§6; Appendix E).

## Problem and motivation
LLM creativity benchmarks differ in tasks, novelty references, and scoring methods, sometimes producing conflicting conclusions. The authors ask whether these evaluations share a common target that could guide benchmark design and comparison: producing high-quality outputs in uncommon input-output regions (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation taxonomy:** A review of 82 papers organizes creativity evaluations into nine task and metric dimensions, including elicitation operations, reference sources, and judges (§2; Appendix A).
- **Long-tail framework:** The authors distinguish input-tail, output-tail, and joint-tail regions. Under an adopted novelty–quality product formulation with shared benchmark novelty weights, creativity scores integrate quality across nested novelty regions (§3).
- **Diagnostic and empirical assessment:** q@Tail(α) measures mean task quality on the most novel α fraction of existing prompts. Experiments cover 15 benchmarks and 26 open-weight models; a separate study diagnoses creativity-promoting methods (§§4–7).

The implementation measures novelty through n-gram absence from the Pile using Infini-Gram-Mini. It averages output novelty across evaluated models and combines that average with input novelty geometrically, ensuring common prompt subsets across models. Writing quality uses Skywork rewards; answer-based tasks use exact-match evaluation (§4). Appendix F clarifies that this shared-weight diagnostic need not equal the novelty–quality product of each model’s own responses.

These results establish associations under the chosen corpus, quality estimators, and human reference. They do not establish causality, a universal creativity definition, or a creativity signal independent of general capability. The theoretical identity is conditional on the adopted shared-weight formulation (§3; Appendix F). The reported perplexity alternative gives near-zero, directionally inconsistent alignment, further limiting generalization across novelty proxies (Appendix E.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** N-gram rarity misses semantic, conceptual, and cultural novelty. Alternative corpora and quality estimators may change results; robustness across judges remains under-explored. Arena judgments reflect holistic preferences from self-selected users and offer limited validation outside writing. Intervention experiments primarily cover 7–8B open models, and extreme-tail agreement is not consistently monotonic (Limitations; Appendix E).

**Analyst inference:** Because tail selection incorporates outputs from the evaluated model pool, changing that pool could change the selected prompts (§4; Appendix F). Lower evaluation cost is asserted but not quantified in the supplied text.

**Reporting ambiguity:** Figure 4’s input/output novelty values differ substantially from §5’s approximate Arena-Hard values; no explanation is supplied. Arena-Hard also has N=244 in Figure 4 versus N=250 in Table A7.

## Open research questions
- **Does alignment survive semantic novelty measures and different corpora?** Lexical novelty is a stated limitation. Recompute fixed-prompt rankings using domain corpora and semantic novelty assessments.
- **Does q@Tail predict creativity beyond writing preferences?** Human validation is centered on Arena writing. Collect expert novelty-and-quality judgments for mathematical and scientific tasks.
- **How stable is tail selection across model pools and judges?** Both influence the diagnostic. Hold prompts fixed, vary model pools and quality estimators, and measure subset and ranking stability.
- **Can methods improve tail quality while preserving novelty at larger scales?** Aggregate intervention results show a trade-off in small models. Repeat matched comparisons on larger models with human evaluation and explicit generation budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main diagnostic and quantitative findings, but its broader interpretation remains limited.
