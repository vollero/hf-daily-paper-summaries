# Systematic Multi-Agent Vision-and-Language Navigation: Formulation, Benchmark, and Method

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35965) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35965)


## One-sentence takeaway
MAVLN benchmarks language-guided team navigation with dependency and resource constraints, while TRISS demonstrates useful coordination mechanisms but achieves only single-digit mission success with learned navigation on unseen scenes (Table II).

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses teams navigating unseen environments to execute a shared language-described mission. Subtasks can require prior completions, continued presence at a location, or the same agent carrying a resource through successive subtasks.

The authors identify a gap between embodied navigation benchmarks with relatively simple coordination and planning systems that handle richer dependencies using known environments or oracle simulator capabilities. They seek to evaluate scheduling, shared exploration, and physical interference together (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task formulation:** Missions become subtask DAGs with permitted executors, dependencies, presence locks, and holding chains. The ground-truth structure is evaluator-only; agents must recover constraints from instructions (§III-A). The authors claim the first systematic formulation of this kind; the supplied text does not independently establish priority.
- **Benchmark and crafting pipeline:** MAVLN contains 11,724 episodes derived from 3,908 missions across 145 scenes, with decentralized, centralized, and centralized-implicit instructions. Generation combines grounded scene descriptions, mission synthesis, schedule refinement, perception-based instruction checking, and round-trip constraint verification (§III-B–C; §F).
- **Evaluation platform and metrics:** A synchronized Habitat 3 platform measures constraint-consistent mission and subtask success, path efficiency, realized makespan, and inter-agent proximity conflicts (§III-D; §A.1).
- **TRISS baseline:** An LLM extracts and schedules atomic instructions, including advance travel before dependencies permit completion. A trained dual-scale planner uses shared topological memory; Hungarian target assignment and bounded conflict-based search coordinate execution. Verified map updates reduce repeated unreachable proposals and unsupported connections (§IV). These contributions integrate and adapt existing planning components rather than introducing every component from scratch.

The ablations support benefits within MAVLN’s simulated setting and expose separate scheduling, planning, and execution bottlenecks. Oracle results are diagnostic comparisons, not deployable performance. The evidence does not establish real-robot reliability or universal collision avoidance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Predicted viewpoints can omit targets or corrupt memory, limiting performance even with oracle node selection. Bounded search leaves unresolved conflicts that the authors consider unacceptable for physical deployment (§V-C).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation currently requires a pool of emitted atomic instructions; matching systems that consume missions without decomposition remains future work (§III-D).
- **Analyst inference:** Kinematic agents and arrival-based holding semantics test navigation coordination rather than actual object manipulation. Physical carrying and robot deployment remain unvalidated (§III-A; §A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Training uses separate single-agent simulators with shared memory and teleported backtracking, whereas inference executes synchronized team motion. This mismatch could affect control robustness; its contribution is not isolated (§A.1; §E.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Synthetic household missions undergo extensive filtering and human assessment, but the supplied text gives no assessor protocol or agreement statistics. Instruction reliability is therefore only partly assessable (§B.1; §F.4).
- The source was truncated, and several tables retain captions without cells. Coverage is incomplete, especially for supplementary evaluation and method details.

## Open research questions
1. **Can explicit constraint tracking reduce scheduling failures?** Oracle-navigation experiments still show scheduling losses (§C.1). Compare the LLM scheduler with an executable constraint-state representation and validity checks on identical missions.
2. **Can stronger mapping close the residual oracle-planner gap?** Perfect node selection still falls short of complete success (§V-C). Compare waypoint prediction with a more reliable mapping module while holding scheduling and execution fixed.
3. **Can safer conflict resolution preserve task efficiency?** Conflict mitigation incurs waiting and leaves unresolved transitions (§V-C). Sweep search budgets and geometric checks, measuring SR, TS, MAC, and unresolved-transition rates.
4. **Does training with synchronized physical execution improve robustness?** Current training simplifies execution (§E.6). Compare that recipe with team rollouts using physical backtracking under matched training budgets.
