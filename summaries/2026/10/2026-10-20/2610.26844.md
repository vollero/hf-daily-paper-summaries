# AnyGoal: On World Model Generalization Beyond Expert Rollouts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26844) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26844)


## One-sentence takeaway
AnyGoal shows that expert-trained world models can fail on new combinations of familiar physical factors and actions, with oracle-state experiments exposing dynamics failures especially in contact-rich tasks (§§5.2–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Expert trajectories correlate states with goal-directed actions, allowing a world model to predict typical behavior while responding poorly to alternative actions. Consequently, good prediction on expert rollouts need not establish reusable dynamics. The authors identify a missing evaluation regime: new goals that preserve environment dynamics and marginal factor/action coverage while changing their joint combinations (§§1, 3; Appendix A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** AnyGoal introduces eight goal-shifted variants across TwoRoom, Push-T, Insert-Peg, and OGBench Cube, releasing 400 episodes per variant and collection/evaluation tools. Variants are used only for testing (§4; Figure 2; Appendix K).
- **Support-based evaluation:** Factor-wise range, Mahalanobis, and KDE checks assess marginal familiarity; a separate joint KDE diagnoses under-covered combinations (§4.1; Appendices C–D).
- **Failure analysis:** Five visual world models and three oracle-state predictors are compared using rollout MSE, MPC success, and action interventions to investigate dynamics, representations, and behavior-policy shortcuts (§5; Table 1).
- **Conditional theoretical result:** A sufficient condition connects generalization to identified, sparse, regime-dependent mechanisms whose local inputs remain covered. This is an illustrative direction, not a demonstrated solution (§6; Appendix J, Proposition 1).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marginal familiarity passes the benchmark’s empirical checks | All checked factor groups pass the combined support criteria; these allow limited density-test failures and omit range checks for designated circular variables. | Appendix C, Table 3 |
| Visual models struggle on Avoid-T despite strong base performance | MPC success: LeWM **84.70% → 3.30%**, SMWM **84.70% → 4.70%**, DINO-WM **74.00% → 6.70%**, from Push-T to Avoid-T; oracle AdaLN instead achieves **92.70% → 100.00%**. | Table 12 |
| Contact-rich shifts expose dynamics failures without visual inference | Oracle AdaLN’s Stack-T MSE is **501.14×** Push-T at one model step (**5 environment steps**) and **158.5×** at five model steps (**25 environment steps**); MPC success falls **92.70% → 70.70%**. | Tables 10–12 |
| Extract-Peg also degrades oracle planning | Insert-Peg → Extract-Peg success: AdaLN **74.00% → 16.66%**, action-duplicated predictor **71.33% → 25.33%**, Toy-DriftWorld **71.33% → 19.33%**. | Table 12 |
| Prediction robustness does not ensure useful control | DriftWorld’s Avoid-T one-step pixel MSE is **0.71×** its Push-T error, while MPC success falls **43.30% → 0.70%**. | Tables 10, 12 |
| Action sensitivity supports a shortcut hypothesis | Sensitivity decreases in regions associated with constrained expert behavior; authors explicitly caution that low sensitivity alone does not imply incorrect prediction. | §5.4; Figure 9 |

Evaluation uses seeds 0, 1, and 2. Planning targets dataset states 25 environment steps ahead, counts success within 50 steps, and executes each 25-step plan before replanning (§4.2; Appendix H).

The evidence establishes substantial, heterogeneous failures in the tested settings—not universal failure across models or variants. Absolute MSE cannot rank model families because prediction spaces differ. Planning measures short-horizon dataset-goal attainment rather than necessarily completing each variant’s full behavioral objective (Appendices H and I.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ caveats:** Familiar physical factors can produce unfamiliar rendered observations. Oracle-state controls help separate this issue, but visual and oracle models also differ architecturally, limiting causal attribution to representation alone (§5.3; Appendices F.2.2, I.2–I.3).
- **Authors’ caveats:** Native prediction spaces differ; stable rollout error can conceal control-critical mistakes. Action sensitivity is diagnostic rather than proof of incorrect dynamics (§§4.2, 5.4; Appendix I.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Finite-sample support tests provide operational evidence of familiarity, rather than proving exact mathematical support inclusion. The joint KDE also depends on quantization, whitening, and bandwidth choices (Appendices C–D).
- **Analyst inference:** A fixed CEM controller and representation-dependent costs make success partly contingent on planning geometry. Table 12 supplies no variability estimates, limiting assessment of small differences (Appendices H, I.3).
- **Authors’ theoretical boundary:** Proposition 1 assumes mechanism identification, regime inference, and local support coverage; it does not solve those problems or cover genuinely new local interactions (Appendix J).

## Open research questions
- **Can targeted coverage reduce failures efficiently?** The experiments diagnose gaps without establishing a data remedy. Next experiment: compare equal-sized additions of interventional, mixed-policy, and expert trajectories (§1; §5.4).
- **Do explicit local mechanisms improve contact generalization?** The sufficient condition is not implemented as a tested architecture. Next experiment: compare regime-conditioned sparse predictors with existing oracle predictors under matched training budgets (Appendix J).
- **How much failure comes from representation versus planning cost?** Oracle and visual comparisons change more than input modality. Next experiment: hold predictor capacity and controller fixed while varying representations and cost normalization (Appendices F.2.2, I.3).
- **Do short-horizon findings predict complete task execution?** Evaluation targets nearby dataset states. Next experiment: add full-episode variant objectives and vary replanning frequency (Appendix H).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main findings and includes numerical tables, but leaves material uncertainties: per-seed variability, planning episode counts, numerical joint-support scores, compute costs, and sensitivity to support-estimation choices. Prediction-horizon terminology is inconsistent between §4.2 and Appendix I; numerical comparisons above follow the explicit environment-step definitions in Tables 10–11.
