# QD-Rec: Open-Ended Evolution of Recommendations via Quality-Diversity

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23470) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23470)


## One-sentence takeaway
QD-Rec combines contextual-bandit item selection with archive-conditioned LLM explanations to improve recommendation quality in most tested LLM-judged settings, while human-rating experiments show mainly diversity and archive-size gains rather than consistent quality improvements (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors study decisions with a fixed candidate pool but many possible reasons for choosing each candidate. A ranked list can obscure distinct strategic options, particularly in acquisition discovery or customer targeting.

They formalize **open-ended recommendation** as searching for a diverse, high-quality set of item–hypothesis pairs under a limited evaluation budget. Their identified gap is that existing recommendation diversification and foundation-model evolutionary search do not directly address this combination of fixed items and evolving rationales (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Problem formulation:** Recommendations are item–hypothesis pairs; the same item can theoretically support multiple distinct explanations (§3).
- **QD-Rec:** A Bayesian linear Thompson-sampling bandit selects items using estimated quality plus a coverage bonus. An LLM generates explanations using archived examples through mutation, crossover, or exploration (§4.2).
- **Local archive admission:** An LLM checks novelty against nearby embedded recommendations. Novel entries enter immediately; otherwise, a blind pairwise comparison determines whether the newcomer replaces its nearest neighbor (§4.1). Goodreads instead uses held-out ratings for replacement.
- **Empirical component analysis:** Novelty removes both bandit selection and exemplars; Novelty+Evo adds exemplars without the bandit. Experiments span four domains and two proposers, with additional geometric reranker and embedding-sensitivity comparisons (§5; Appendices B–D).

These results support the authors’ claim that the combined method often improves the retained archive under their scoring protocol. They do **not** establish better real acquisition outcomes, customer conversion, or human judgments of explanation quality. Goodreads evaluates previously recorded book preferences, independently of memo quality. The reranker comparison measures feedback and archive machinery together, rather than isolating superior item selection.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged grounding problems:** Appendix E documents unsupported generated claims and outdated company information. The authors state that actual decisions require richer, current inputs.
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Search feedback and reported final quality reuse the same online scores (§5.1). No independent post-search judge or human assessment of the generated hypotheses is reported.
- **Narrow human grounding:** Goodreads covers two selected readers and their already-rated books. Memo changes cannot change quality, and books are sampled without replacement, so this setting does not test repeated hypothesis improvement for one item (Appendix C).
- **Incomplete component isolation:** The reported ablations do not separately isolate the diversity bonus from bandit learning, or remove local novelty admission while retaining the other components (Table 1).
- **Metric and statistical limits:** Diversity depends on the embedding space; coverage is comparable only within its shared fitted grid. Embedding sensitivity uses one run per selected profile and changes batch size. Main significance tests use five seeds and uncorrected comparisons (§§5.1–5.2; Tables 2–3).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Prompts and substantial setup details are supplied, but the text does not establish a code/data release or specify the numerical batch near-duplicate threshold (§4.3; Appendix D).

## Open research questions
- **Do gains survive independent evaluation?** Reported quality reuses search-time feedback. A next experiment could blindly reassess final archives with domain experts and an independent judge.
- **Which selection component drives improvement?** Current ablations combine bandit learning with its diversity bonus. Compare uniform selection, quality-only Thompson sampling, and diversity-aware Thompson sampling under matched budgets.
- **Does evolving explanations improve human usefulness?** Goodreads ratings measure books rather than memos, and no final QD-Rec archive contains repeated items (§4.2). Hold items fixed and test alternative evolved rationales for factuality, distinctness, and decision usefulness.
- **Does improvement continue beyond the tested budget?** Experiments use a fixed search horizon, while semantic diversity stabilizes early (Appendix A.2). Run longer searches and measure marginal quality, independently assessed novelty, and cost per useful addition.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the supplied method and reported results; **medium** in their broader practical significance.

Material missing information includes independent hypothesis-quality assessments, real decision outcomes, broader human-user evaluation, executable release availability, the batch duplicate-filter threshold, and directly logged token costs. Figure captions and surrounding descriptions are supplied, but the plotted images themselves are not available as numerical evidence.
