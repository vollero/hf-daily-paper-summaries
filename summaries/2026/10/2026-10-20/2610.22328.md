# ACEdit: Stable Multi-Turn Image Editing via Asymmetric Conditioning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22328) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22328)


## One-sentence takeaway
ACEdit reduces drift across sequential image edits by training on the editor’s own outputs while a teacher supervises against the original image and cumulative instructions, improving preservation on ten-turn local-edit sequences without a significant instruction-following loss (§§4–5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Repeated image editing accumulates artifacts, color shifts, and lost details because each generated output becomes the next input. The authors identify a supervision gap: training on clean trajectories misses these inference conditions, but ground-truth intermediate images can become inappropriate targets when valid student edits diverge from them. Teachers conditioned on degraded student outputs may also preserve existing artifacts (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Self-generated rollout training:** The student edits its own previous outputs, using score distillation instead of paired intermediate targets. Gradients pass through one sampled denoising step per turn, without backpropagation through earlier turns (§4.1).
- **Asymmetric conditioning:** The student receives the previous output and current instruction; the frozen teacher and learned auxiliary editor receive the original image and cumulative edit intent. Their prediction difference guides distribution matching (§4.2; Fig. 2).
- **Practical adaptation and evaluation:** Rank-16 LoRA fine-tuning preserves the backbone’s four-step inference procedure. The authors also introduce EdiVal-10 to measure preservation across ten cumulative local edits on real photographs (§§5.1, 5.3). Public release availability is not established in the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results

Unless stated otherwise, EdiVal-10 results are measured after ten cumulative edits over 252 chains. KRIS scores use a Gemma-4-31B judge on a 1–5 scale; higher is better. Reported ± values are paired standard errors.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher judged image quality | ACEdit exceeds every comparator; smallest quality-score advantage is over Emu Edit: **0.30 ± 0.08**. | §5.3; Table 1 discussion |
| Better whole-image preservation | KRIS consistency: **3.85**, versus **3.03** for its pretrained backbone, **3.04–3.32** for training-free methods, and **1.29** for MT-OPSD. | §5.3 |
| Lower background change | Background LPIPS: **0.254**, versus **0.351** for its backbone and **0.264** for Emu Edit; Emu’s excess is **0.010 ± 0.005**, reported *t*=2.1. Lower is better. | §5.3; Appendix B |
| Anchor preservation comparable to Emu Edit | DINOv2 distance: **0.203** versus **0.196**; difference is not significant. Evaluated only on **148** chains with usable anchors. | §5.3; Table 1 caption |
| Instruction following remains competitive | KRIS instruction following: **3.38** versus FreqEdit’s **3.39**, without a significant difference. | §5.3 |
| Conditioning choice matters | Consistency falls from **3.85** to **2.81** when score models additionally receive the previous output, and to **2.28** when previous-output/current-instruction conditioning replaces clean-source/cumulative conditioning. Each variant receives **100 student updates**. | §5.4; Table 2 discussion |
| Giving the student the source introduces a tradeoff | Additional source conditioning raises consistency **3.85 → 4.08**, but lowers instruction following **3.38 → 2.80**; illustrated failures undo prior edits. | §5.4; Fig. 5 |
| Single-edit quality proxy improves | Across **606 English GEdit-Bench instructions**, source-to-output HPSv3 change is **−0.18**, versus **−0.41** for its backbone; improvement **0.23 ± 0.02**. | Appendix E |
| Improvement depends on the benchmark | On **100 ten-turn LME-Bench sessions**, using the **512-pixel Gemma judge protocol**, all-turn success is **0.53** versus **0.49** for its backbone, not significantly different. | Appendix D |
| Modest reported training cost | **100 student updates**, about **4.4 GPU-hours on one B200**; inference retains four unguided sampling steps. | §5.1 |

These results support better preservation in the tested local-edit setting, with competitive automated instruction-following scores. They do not establish universal stability or human preference. Longer sequences are illustrated qualitatively, and LME-Bench shows no significant final-turn advantage over the same backbone. The supplied text contains table captions and result discussions, but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–4.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Difficult instructions can damage earlier edits; making a hiker smile removes previously added sunglasses (Fig. 6). Robustness over much longer sequences remains unresolved (§6).
- **Evaluation scope:** Training uses synthetic scenes and local edits; the main quantitative benchmark emphasizes persistent, unedited content. Repeated content replacement can remain coherent even without ACEdit (§§5.1, 6; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** MT-OPSD uses a different backbone variant, resolution, sampler, and guidance setting. Its performance gap cannot isolate the training method’s contribution; Appendix D explicitly attributes much of that gap to backbone and sampler differences.
- **Metric limitations:** Background LPIPS includes regions where objects may legitimately be added, and anchor evaluation covers only a subset of chains. Judge prompts also penalize untouched real sources, complicating absolute quality-score interpretation (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Experiments within one model family do not establish portability to other editors. Human evaluation and variability across independent training runs are not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
1. **How far does stability extend beyond training length?** Longer examples are qualitative (§6). Evaluate substantially longer semantic-edit chains, tracking preservation and instruction survival at every turn.
2. **Can difficult edits preserve earlier modifications?** Figure 6 shows unresolved interference. Test targeted sequences combining facial expression, pose, and accessory edits, scoring each earlier change separately.
3. **How reliable is compound supervision for conflicting edit histories?** The method depends on cumulative edit intent (§4.2), but its reliability is not isolated. Compare supervision for repeated attribute reversals and ambiguous references using checked cumulative instructions.
4. **Does the method transfer across editor families?** Only one family is evaluated (§5.1). Repeat training on another backbone with matched inference settings and human-rated comparisons.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods, ablations, and result discussions support the central finding.
