# SPARC: Spoken Profile Authorship Recognition Corpus

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25265) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25265)


## One-sentence takeaway
SPARC shows that two strong LLMs can verify speaker identity from podcast transcripts under imperfect context controls, while a stricter cross-series evaluation substantially weakens performance and short probes provide only setting-dependent mitigation.

## Problem and motivation
Removing acoustic features leaves transcripts containing word choice, syntax, and conversational habits that may expose speaker identity or attributes. The authors address a gap in evaluating modern LLM adversaries on both identity verification and demographic-label agreement while controlling for shared episode context (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** SPARC combines NaturalVoices and SPoRC into over 12,500 speakers and 1.5 million filtered utterances, with inferred or source-provided sex, age, and accent labels. Filtering uses speech quality, music detection, and agreement between two ASR systems (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Unified verification protocol:** Enrollment–probe pairs test speaker identity and attribute-label agreement across episode conditions and text budgets. SPoRC adds metadata matching and a stricter cross-series identity control (§4.2; Appendices A.1, A.4).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Five representation-based baselines and three prompted LLMs are evaluated using EER, AUC, and low-FPR true-positive rates. Contributions are the benchmark and empirical findings, rather than a new attribution model (§§4.3–5.4).

## Evidence and main results

EER is lower-is-better; chance is 0.5. Unless specified otherwise, results use approximately 200-word probes and 20 enrollment utterances.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong LLMs outperform SentenceBERT across episodes | NaturalVoices identity EER: GPT-5-mini 0.285, Llama-3.3-70B 0.281, SentenceBERT 0.351. SPoRC: 0.253, 0.262, and 0.350, respectively. | §5.1; Fig. 2 |
| Within-episode controls weaken all leading systems | NaturalVoices identity EER, combined → within-episode: SentenceBERT 0.350 → 0.460; GPT-5-mini 0.277 → 0.345; Llama 0.284 → 0.322. | §5.1 |
| Stricter series control substantially reduces SPoRC performance | Cross-series identity EER rises to 0.402 for GPT-5-mini and 0.391 for Llama, from 0.253 and 0.262 in the original protocol. | Appendix A.4; Table 7 |
| Sex and coarse accent labels show signal; age remains uncertain | NaturalVoices cross-episode EER, GPT/Llama: sex 0.377/0.420; accent 0.268/0.320. Best reported age EER is 0.447 cross-episode and 0.440 within-episode, both PCM. | §§5.2.1–5.2.3 |
| Short probes reduce NaturalVoices linkage performance | At 20 enrollment utterances, reducing probes from 200 to 50 words raises cross-episode identity EER by 0.065 for GPT, 0.047 for Llama, and 0.038 for SentenceBERT. The probe-length effect is absent in the stricter SPoRC cross-series control. | §5.3; Appendix A.4 |
| Similar aggregate scores conceal different low-FPR behavior | NaturalVoices cross-episode GPT/Llama EER: 0.285/0.281; AUC: 0.790/0.789. TPR at 1% FPR: 0.277/0.133; at 0.1% FPR: 0.121/0.011. | §5.4; Table 4 |

These results support speaker-linked information in the evaluated transcripts, but do not isolate linguistic style from personal facts, residual context, or ASR artifacts. They establish pairwise verification performance rather than population-scale identification. Attribute results concern agreement with proxy labels, not verified demographic identities.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** English podcasts and clean single-speaker segments may not represent other domains, noisy conversations, overlap, or multilingual speech (Limitations).
- **Authors’ context-control limits:** Episode membership and metadata approximate topic. The original SPoRC protocol also confounds series membership with trial class; the stricter control materially weakens performance (Appendices A.1, A.4).
- **Authors’ label limits:** A category-balanced audit of 50 recordings per attribute reports model–majority agreement of 98% for sex, 88% for accent, and 64% for age. This measures selected-sample perceptual agreement, not demographic ground truth (§3.3; Appendix A.5).
- **Authors’ model limits:** One prompt and one response per LLM-trial pair leave prompt sensitivity and generation variability unmeasured. Rounded, uncalibrated scores limit low-FPR resolution (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Text-length mitigation should be treated as protocol-dependent because its effect does not persist in the stricter SPoRC control. Near-chance age results likewise cannot distinguish weak textual signal from unreliable labels.

## Open research questions
- **Which cues drive linkage?** Decisions are not attributed to style, personal facts, context, or ASR artifacts. A next experiment could remove these cue classes separately and measure verification changes (Limitations).
- **Does shortening text help under stronger controls?** NaturalVoices shows degradation, but cross-series SPoRC does not. A next experiment could vary probe length on a larger, semantically matched cross-series trial set (§5.3; Appendix A.4).
- **Are low-FPR rankings stable?** Score quantization and single generations leave this unresolved. Repeated scoring, prompt variants, and calibrated or likelihood-based scores could test ranking stability (§5.4; Limitations).
- **Does attribute verification survive better labels and balance?** Proxy labels and coarse accent grouping constrain conclusions. Independently validated labels and balanced dialect trials could test this directly (§3.3; Limitations).
