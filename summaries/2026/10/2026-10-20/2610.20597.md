# Co-Evolving Acting and Forecasting for Language Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20597) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20597)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoPE adds success-filtered future-action forecasting to GRPO, improving aggregate performance across five agent benchmarks without extra inference computation, but its Airline regression exposes bias toward easy successful behaviors (§§5, 7–8).

## Problem and motivation

Long agent rollouts are expensive, yet terminal-reward RL provides limited supervision about intermediate decisions. The authors argue that auxiliary value targets inherit sparse outcome information, while observation prediction spends capacity reconstructing lengthy, irrelevant content and typically covers only one transition. They propose using successful trajectories’ future actions as compact, decision-relevant supervision (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multi-step action targets:** From each history in a successful on-policy rollout, predict the current and subsequent actions while omitting intervening observations. The authors characterize the population target as the action-sequence distribution conditioned on eventual success (§4).
- **Joint training in one backbone:** A forecasting prompt activates an auxiliary objective sharing parameters with the acting policy. CoPE combines this loss with GRPO and averages forecasting losses across successful rollouts within each task group. Inference remains ordinary single-action generation (§5; Algorithm 1).
- **Mechanism and empirical analysis:** The authors interpret overlapping forecast targets as implicit temporal ensembling. They test this interpretation through forecasting variants and explicit action reranking, alongside benchmark, efficiency, and backbone comparisons (§§7–9).

The controlled comparisons support improved aggregate task completion and faster learning in the reported settings. They do not establish universal gains: some subtasks regress, and the paper reports no uncertainty intervals or training-seed variability. Explicit reranking results support the authors’ ensembling interpretation, but do not uniquely establish that mechanism (§8; Table 6). RL-step efficiency also does not directly measure environment calls or total compute.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-identified selection bias:** Successful trajectories can overrepresent easy tasks. The authors attribute the Airline regression to rewarded refusals becoming disproportionately common in forecasting targets (§8 Q3).
- **Author-identified horizon noise:** Distant actions depend on unseen intervening observations; longer forecasting horizons introduce irreducible uncertainty. Group normalization also leaves trajectory-length dependence (§9).
- **Restricted evaluation configurations:** WebShop uses synthetic goals and a reduced product catalogue; ScienceWorld excludes task types. Published AppWorld baselines use different subsets and protocols, so they are not controlled comparisons (Appendices A.3, B.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited mechanism isolation:** Most analysis experiments use ScienceWorld, and explicit reranking changes inference behavior. Broader evidence would be needed to establish temporal ensembling as the principal cause of gains (§§6, 8).
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** The Abstract says the codebase is available, while the Reproducibility Statement promises complete code and configurations upon publication. Several internal table/figure references conflict with their captions; this analysis follows the supplied captions and table contents.

## Open research questions

- **Can success-selection bias be corrected?** Airline performance deteriorates as refusal-heavy successes dominate (§8). Compare CoPE with task-stratified or success-frequency-reweighted forecasting on all τ²-bench domains.
- **Can forecasting horizons adapt to uncertainty?** Longer horizons degrade slightly on ScienceWorld (§9). Test fixed versus uncertainty-adaptive horizons across environments with different observation stochasticity.
- **Does temporal diversity explain the gains beyond extra supervision?** Existing variants support this interpretation without fully isolating it (§8). Compare multiple forecast delays against immediate-action training matched for supervised tokens and compute.
- **Do gains persist across training seeds and interaction budgets?** Reported tables lack variability estimates. Repeat controlled comparisons with multiple seeds, tracking environment calls, generated tokens, wall-clock time, and held-out success.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the method and reports controlled comparisons, but leaves training-seed variability, statistical uncertainty, hardware and timing methodology, exact efficiency-milestone calculations, and complete artifact availability unresolved.
