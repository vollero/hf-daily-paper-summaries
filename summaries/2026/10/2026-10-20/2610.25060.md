# Policy Is Not Capability: Some Safety Benchmarks Measure Which Definition of &quot;Unsafe&quot; You Trained On

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25060) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25060)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across this guardrail grid, some safety scores primarily reflect the policy supplied through distillation or final head calibration, although unmatched training conditions and substantial seed uncertainty limit causal attribution (§§4–5, 9).

## Problem and motivation
Guardrail comparisons often treat “unsafe” as an intrinsic property of a prompt, even when training and benchmark labels implement different moderation policies. The authors ask how much benchmark performance reflects harmful-request detection versus agreement with a particular rubric. Their unusual opportunity is a shared prompt pool labelled under both a moderation-oriented policy A and a harmful-request-oriented policy B (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic training grid:** Ten training arms at two ModernBERT scales vary label mixtures, policy anchors, distillation teachers, and frozen-backbone head calibration. These are related interventions, not fully matched experiments (§2).
- **Contamination and stability audits:** Measure normalized exact-match overlap across all training, calibration, and validation pools; evaluate common clean subsets; compare between-arm score ranges against seed variation from neighbouring recipes (§§3–4; Appendix C).
- **Policy-transfer evidence:** Show that teacher supervision and final head calibration can install policy A despite contrary student hard labels; examine analogous disagreements between released guards (§§5–6).
- **Evaluation diagnosis:** Test whether validation accuracy follows policy-sensitive benchmark scores and recommend reporting exposure, seed variation, and operational definitions (§§7–8).

The contributions are empirical diagnoses; the authors do not present head retraining or distillation as new methods (§§1, 10).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Severe benchmark exposure | Normalized exact matches against the union of all pools identify 88/100 JailbreakBench-benign prompts and 1,083/1,319 OR-Bench-hard prompts; subsequent analyses use common clean subsets. | §3, Fig. 2 |
| Benchmark sensitivity differs sharply | Across ten arms, averaging within-scale score ranges over two scales gives approximately 0.99 for HarmBench-copyright versus 0.043 for WildGuard-benign. This range also contains training and seed effects. | §4, Fig. 2 |
| Final head calibration changes policy-sensitive scores | At 149 M, E1 updates 0.04 M parameters, approximately 0.03%, using 8,489 calibration examples; clean copyright catch rate is 100% and OR-Bench-hard over-refusal 4.7%. Data-mixture arms E2–E5 score 0–1.3% and 37.7–56.4%, respectively. | §5, Table 1 |
| Teacher policy can dominate hard labels | At 149 M, all-policy-A hard labels with a policy-B teacher yield 17.3% copyright catch rate and 31.4% OR-Bench-hard over-refusal; policy-B hard labels with a policy-A teacher yield 100% and 6.4%. Teacher training pools are unmatched. | §5; Appendix A |
| Capability preservation is uneven | On the six-benchmark capability catch-rate mean, E8 reaches 91.1% and E7 89.1%, versus 86.8–88.3% for E2–E5; E1 falls to 81.0%. | §5, Table 1 |
| Rank evidence weakens under dependence corrections | Policy-A-last arms occupy the relevant extreme ranks at both scales. Clustered, two-sided tests give p=0.071 at 149 M and p=0.036 at 395 M; excluding head-calibration arms gives p=0.14 and p=0.048. The two benchmarks provide no independent permutation evidence. | §5; Appendix A |
| Validation association favours the policy grouping | Across twenty models, validation accuracy has Spearman ρ=+0.74 with the policy-loaded aggregate versus +0.34 with the capability-loaded aggregate; intervals overlap, and 69.3% of the validation split overlaps policy-A training data. | §7; Appendix A |
| Released guards disagree on the same contested ruling | On 75 clean HarmBench-copyright prompts, Granite Guardian scores 0% catch rate and Qwen3Guard-4 B approximately 99%, under their respective verdict conventions. | §6 |

These results support the authors’ claim that policy provenance can materially affect guardrail scores. They do not isolate a universal causal effect of policy alone or establish general capability parity. In particular, “capability-loaded” is the authors’ benchmark grouping, not an independent ground-truth measure of capability.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Each arm has one run; proxy seed floors use neighbouring recipes and full benchmarks rather than the arms’ own recipes and clean subsets. The intermediate benchmark ordering remains unresolved (§4; Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Arms differ in pool size, initialization, schedules, and checkpoint selection; teacher pools also differ. Rubric and annotation modality change together (§§2, 9; Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Neither label family has a human-adjudicated reference set. Exact-match decontamination misses paraphrases and translations (§§2–3).
- **Author-stated:** Copyright results concern two templates. Clean OR-Bench-hard is enriched for prompts the authors’ jury considers potentially harmful, confounding over-refusal interpretation; the Qwen comparison gap disappears on the small unclear stratum (§9; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** The headline “unchanged capability” is too broad: measured capability varies across arms, and the evidence concerns selected benchmark averages rather than deployment-wide safety.
- **Author-stated:** External comparisons involve benchmark-selected author models, differing verdict conventions, and unknown baseline exposure (§§6, 9).

## Open research questions
1. **Does the policy effect survive matched repeated training?** Arm-specific seed variation is unavailable. Repeat each arm with additional seeds while matching pools, initialization, and selection rules.
2. **Can rubric effects be separated from annotator effects?** The two pipelines differ in both. Cross both rubrics with the same annotation procedure and independent human adjudication.
3. **Does policy transfer generalize beyond contested templates?** Copyright coverage is narrow and OR-Bench-hard labels are disputed. Evaluate independently authored, adjudicated prompts spanning more policy decisions.
4. **When does head calibration preserve detection?** E1 loses capability while other calibrated arms perform better. Vary calibration coverage and anchors under matched starting checkpoints.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The diagnostic patterns are well documented, but causal attribution and generalization remain limited.

Material gaps include arm-specific repeated runs, a human-adjudicated reference, the full proprietary rubric, established public code/data/checkpoint availability, and baseline training exposure. Although referenced, the supplied text omits the numerical bodies of Tables 2–4; surrounding prose supplies only part of their contents.
