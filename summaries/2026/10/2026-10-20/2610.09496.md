# Sparse Feature Policy Unlearning Mitigates State Hallucination in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09496) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09496)


## One-sentence takeaway
SOUL uses region-conditioned sparse features to guide selective policy updates, reducing state hallucination and improving success in the authors’ pick-and-place evaluations with OpenVLA and π₀.₅ (Table I; §V-B).

## Problem and motivation
A VLA can continue manipulating as though an unrealized physical state were true—for example, transporting and placing after closing its gripper without grasping anything. The authors call this **state hallucination**, distinguishing it from contact-based grasp failures such as slippage (§III-A).

Such errors can propagate through subsequent manipulation stages. The authors identify a gap between deployment-time correction methods and persistent modification of the policy representations associated with recurring failures (§§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **Behavioral characterization:** Define grasp, transport, and place hallucinations, and label rollouts as clean success, hallucination failure, or non-hallucination failure (§III-A).
- **Representation analysis:** Compare visual attention and train Top-k sparse autoencoders on visual-token residual activations. Region-conditioned feature scores contrast execution outcomes, identifying features associated with hallucination or success (§§III-B–C).
- **Selective policy unlearning:** SOUL selects forgetting and retention region-feature pairs, suppresses the former on hallucinated failures, and enhances the latter on clean successes. The SAE remains frozen; policy updates use LoRA adapters while original model weights remain frozen (§IV; §V-A).

The proposed contribution is the use of these outcome-associated sparse features as persistent policy-update targets; the SAE and LoRA components are existing methods (§II-C; §V-A).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Improved OpenVLA performance | LIBERO-Plus, original → SOUL: rollout hallucination **70.0% → 44.0%**, overall success **26.0% → 58.0%**, clean success **16.0% → 52.0%**. | Table I |
| Improved π₀.₅ performance | RoboCasa, original → SOUL: hallucination **40.0% → 22.9%**, overall success **41.9% → 54.3%**, clean success **39.0% → 52.4%**. | Table I |
| Gradient-ascent baseline degraded success | Overall success after gradient-ascent unlearning was **0.0%** for OpenVLA/LIBERO-Plus and **4.8%** for π₀.₅/RoboCasa, versus original-policy **26.0%** and **41.9%**, respectively. | Table I |
| Physical-robot improvement | Across **50 rollouts** spanning both models on Franka Research 3, authors report average clean-success improvement of **20 percentage points** and hallucination-failure reduction of **22 points** relative to task-specific policies before SOUL. | §V-B |
| Preservation and correction | Authors report **89% average preservation** across originally successful task executions and non-hallucinated events, **40%** correction of failed task executions, and **78%** correction of hallucinated events. | §V-C; Fig. 7 |
| Region guidance improved feature selection | Averaged simulation results: region-guided selection achieved **33.5% hallucination / 52.2% clean success**, compared with global **45.8% / 38.3%** and tokenized **41.5% / 41.3%**. | Table II |
| Retention objective helped clean success | Averaged simulation clean success fell from **52.2% to 48.3%** when retention was removed; hallucination increased from **33.5% to 36.8%**. | Table III; §V-C |
| Features and attention tracked outcomes | Representative hallucinated executions showed weaker task-region attention and higher hallucination-feature activation; selected activations shifted as intended after SOUL. | Figs. 2–3, 5–6 |

The evidence supports improved measured behavior in these evaluated settings and supports the usefulness of region guidance and retention. Attention and feature associations alone do not establish that the identified features cause hallucination. Successful feature-guided updates also do not establish literal removal of policy “knowledge” or an explicit false internal belief; those are the authors’ interpretations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation deliberately focuses on structured pick-and-place tasks to reduce planning confounds (§V-A). Broader manipulation and long-horizon reliability remain untested here.
- **Analyst inference—architecture/environment confounding:** Simulation pairs OpenVLA with LIBERO-Plus and π₀.₅ with RoboCasa rather than testing both models on both benchmarks (Table I).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** The quantitative baseline is adapted gradient-ascent unlearning. Comparisons against other policy fine-tuning or deployment-time correction methods are absent (§V-A; Table I).
- **Analyst inference—annotation dependence:** Feature selection requires semantic regions and outcome labels; real-world regions are manually annotated. Sensitivity to annotation errors is not evaluated (§III-C).
- **Reporting gaps:** Simulation rollout counts, uncertainty estimates, train/evaluation separation, and full optimization settings are unavailable. Physical results provide aggregate changes without per-model rates (§§V-A–B).
- **Preservation ambiguity:** The detailed analysis describes the reported preservation average as spanning successful tasks and non-hallucinated events; it should not be read as an isolated task-success preservation rate (§V-C).

## Open research questions
1. **Does SOUL generalize beyond pick-and-place?** The evaluation intentionally restricts task structure. A next experiment could test multi-stage manipulation with recovery and planning demands (§V-A).
2. **Are selected features causal and specific to hallucination?** Current evidence combines associations with joint policy updates. Matched interventions on selected versus random features could test specificity (§§III–IV).
3. **How robust is feature selection to imperfect supervision?** The method uses outcome labels and semantic masks. Controlled label corruption and predicted-mask experiments could measure annotation sensitivity (§III-C).
4. **Does SOUL outperform ordinary corrective training?** Gradient ascent is the only reported alternative update baseline. Matched-data, matched-compute comparisons with success-based fine-tuning could isolate the value of feature-guided unlearning (§V-A; Table I).

## Confidence and missing information
