# A Conditional No-Hair Theorem for Transformers: How Language Models Forget Their Prompt

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24133) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24133)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under bounded logits and non-degenerate pre-norm activations, the paper proves that prompt-induced hidden-state differences vanish under a shared continuation, while experiments show that training accelerates this conditional forgetting (§§4–7).

## Problem and motivation
A prompt can lose influence even while remaining visible in the context, potentially weakening persistent instructions. Comparing freely generated trajectories confounds direct prompt influence with differences already recorded in generated text. The paper isolates the former and asks how quickly it decays, what mechanism drives it, and how training changes it (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Measurement:** Compare hidden states for different prompts followed by identical, teacher-forced continuations. Measure the prompt offset, its component shared across continuations, continuation variability, and decay exponents; use an unbiased signal estimator (§3).
- **Conditional theorem:** Bound the final-layer offset by \(O(P(\log t)^{L-1}/t)\) for the specified transformer class. Derive a corresponding next-token total-variation bound and solve a simplified uniform-attention relay model that attains the logarithmic order (§4).
- **Mechanism and training:** Separate attention writes into direct prompt reads and relay through continuation tokens. Track their changes across Pythia checkpoints and intervene by boosting prompt attention (§§6–7).
- **Behavioral application:** Test retention–perplexity trade-offs and French-language instruction adherence. Extra prompt attention helps, but logarithmic compensation shows no clear advantage over a constant boost of matched mean strength (§9).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Conditional prompt influence vanishes asymptotically | Final-layer offset is bounded by \(C_LP(L+\ln t)^{L-1}/t\); next-token total variation has the same asymptotic order, under the theorem’s assumptions. | Theorem 1, Corollary 1, §§4.3–4.4 |
| Training accelerates deep-layer forgetting | On real-corpus pairs with eight shared WikiText continuations per pair, trained Pythia-1b deep-layer α is 0.58–0.61; randomly initialized Pythia shows decreasing α with depth. Pythia measurement windows end at 16–1024 tokens. | §5.2, Table 4 |
| Prompt difference size need not determine decay rate | Pythia-160m, layer 11, real-corpus setting: unrelated versus one-word-change α is 0.35 versus 0.37 at initialization and 0.70 versus 0.69 after training. | §5.2 |
| Relay accounts for the untrained long tail | Pythia-160m decomposition, 25 pairs per axis and eight continuations per pair: relay accounts for 73–97% of the late offset at initialization. Cumulative attention writes reproduce measured α within 0.06 across tested layers/checkpoints. | §6.1, Table 6 |
| Faster forgetting precedes attention sinks | Pythia-160m on the same 400 prompt pairs: forgetting changes during steps 128–512, approximately 0.3–1 billion training tokens; token-zero attention mass remains at or below 0.003 in measured layers through step 1000. | §6.2, Table 7, Fig. 5 |
| Compensating dilution suppresses measured forgetting | Per-head compensation yields α of 0.05–0.13 at initialization and step 512, and negative α after training; calibrated compensation raises perplexity by 140–376%. | §§7.2–7.3, 9.1 |
| Mild compensation offers a retention–prediction trade-off | Trained Pythia-160m, real-corpus pairs: boost strength 0.5 reduces α by 0.17–0.25 with a 2.6% WikiText perplexity increase. | §9.1, Table 9 |
| Behavioral benefit does not establish a special logarithmic advantage | Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, eight turns, 32 conversations per condition: fully French conversations rise from 78% to 84%/91% with logarithmic strengths 0.1/0.25; matched constant boosts yield 84%/88%. Authors describe these as trends, not significant effects. | §9.2 |

The theorem establishes conditional convergence for a fixed shared history, not that freely generated trajectories become independent of their prompts. Its accumulated bound diverges over unlimited horizons (§4.4). Empirical results support dilution and relay as mechanisms in the tested settings; finite-window fitted α values are not supplied by the asymptotic theorem. Signal decay also includes changing offset direction across continuations, rather than only shrinking offset norm (§5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical limits:** Constants can grow exponentially with depth; the bound becomes informative only at potentially very long contexts. Non-degeneracy is checked empirically on finite sequences, with a small margin in Qwen’s first post-attention norm (§10; Appendix B).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Small model families, predominantly English prompts with limited language variants, WikiText continuations, one free-generation temperature, and short fitting ranges restrict generalization (§10).
- **Measurement qualifications:** Hand-written-scene signal estimates bias late decay exponents downward. Untrained Qwen does not quantitatively follow the linear relay model, and trained Qwen exhibits unresolved positional oscillations (§§3.3, 5.4).
- **Intervention qualification:** Strong compensation substantially changes prediction behavior; it does not establish that the modified network computes like the original (§7.3). Instruction echoing and fixed-length filler complicate behavioral interpretation (§9.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The discussion’s statement that all trained models are pretrained-only is too broad: §9 explicitly evaluates Instruct variants. A controlled base-versus-instruction-tuned retention comparison remains absent.

## Open research questions
- **Does the measured decay persist at larger scales and longer contexts?** Existing ranges cannot establish this. Next experiment: repeat unbiased offset measurements on larger models over longer continuation windows (§10).
- **Does instruction tuning change relay suppression?** Behavioral Instruct tests do not provide a matched mechanistic comparison. Next experiment: compare base and instruction-tuned versions using identical prompts, continuations, and decomposition (§§9–10).
- **What drives Qwen’s positional oscillations?** Their relation to rotary frequencies remains unresolved. Next experiment: measure attention and offsets densely across positions while varying rotary settings (§5.4).
- **Can extra prompt attention improve adherence without echoing?** Current benefits include side effects and uncertain multi-turn differences. Next experiment: increase conversation counts and compare matched constant/logarithmic boosts with adherence, echoing, and quality measurements (§9).
