# AdaExploreBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Proactive Exploration and Test-time Adaptation in Evolving Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25950) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25950)


## One-sentence takeaway
AdaExploreBench finds that LLM agents struggle to discover useful evidence and revise hidden rules after unannounced changes, although its ablations only indirectly establish probe quality as the bottleneck.

## Problem and motivation
Agents operating in unfamiliar environments must decide what evidence to collect and recognize when their understanding becomes outdated. The authors argue that existing benchmarks often provide evidence, use stationary environments, or emphasize task completion rather than learning environmental dynamics (§1; Table 1).

The benchmark targets the combination of self-directed exploration and unannounced environmental change, with performance measured throughout interaction.

## Approach and contributions
- **Synthetic benchmark and construction pipeline:** LLM-assisted field generation, programmatic validation, numerical certification, and semantic review produce 256 evaluation episodes and 1,000 training episodes across finance, healthcare, cybersecurity, and manufacturing (§3.1; Appendix D).
- **Controlled evolving environments:** Hidden additive, conjunctive, disjunctive, or lexicographic rules determine utilities for four ordered actions. Drift replaces one relevant factor and reverses another factor’s direction within the same family (§3.1; Appendix C).
- **Interactive evaluation protocol:** Agents design probes, receive utilities for all actions on probed cases, and choose actions for sequential evaluation batches. Default history masking retains probe evidence and realized evaluation utilities while removing completed evaluation inputs and actions (§3.2).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Eight models are compared using decision accuracy, exploration counts, and factor/change recognition; additional settings isolate stationarity, retained evidence, and mandatory probing (§§4–6).

The contribution is a benchmark and analysis, rather than a new adaptation algorithm.

## Evidence and main results

CA denotes utility-sensitive Calibrated Accuracy, with a uniformly random policy scoring zero in expectation and an optimal policy scoring one (Appendix H).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Default performance remains limited | Across eight models, Gemini-3.8-Flash leads at 67.25% CA; GPT-6-Sol scores 61.87%; all remain below 70%. | Table 2 |
| More probing does not guarantee better decisions | Default: DeepSeek records 113.42 mean unique probes and 52.87% CA, versus Gemini’s 88.12 probes and 67.25% CA. | Table 2 |
| Removing drift improves average CA | Across seven models with diagnostic runs, Stationary improves CA by **5.30 percentage points**; model-level Wilcoxon p=0.016. | Table 16 |
| Retaining evaluation evidence helps most tested models | Evidence Given improves mean CA by **10.30 points** across seven models, p=0.031; six improve, while Qwen3.8-27B declines by 3.94 points. | Tables 14, 16 |
| Mandatory probing does not improve average CA | Forced-Probe changes mean CA by **−1.42 points** across seven models, p=0.578; individual effects vary. | Tables 15, 16 |
| Drift causes persistent performance loss | Authors report a 26.9-point average immediate CA drop among six above-floor models; their final-two-batch averages remain 6.9–19.2 points below pre-change performance. | §6; Figure 3 |
| Change recognition is frequently incomplete | Among 2,037 episodes with paired assessments, 36.3% have unchanged reports; only 6.8% recover both the replacement and reversal completely. | Table 13 |
| Generated scenarios receive favorable plausibility ratings | Three annotators evaluated 40 sampled episodes; majority-positive ratings were 100% for scenario plausibility, 100% for replacement, and 90% for reversal. | Appendix E; Table 5 |

These results support difficulty with evidence acquisition and adaptation **within this synthetic protocol**. They do not directly establish that probe informativeness causes the failures: probe quality is unmeasured, and cross-model comparisons confound exploration strategy with other model capabilities.

The aggregate ablation figures are inconsistent across the supplied text. The introduction reports gains of 9.4 and 6.2 points, while Table 16 reports 10.30 and 5.30 across seven models. Table 16 notes that the main figure displays six models. The table above uses its explicitly enumerated seven-model results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Latent numeric rules simplify real environmental complexity and uncertainty; drift covers only within-family factor replacement and direction reversal (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Exploration metrics measure effort rather than informativeness, so exploration-quality conclusions rely on indirect ablations (Limitations; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence Given changes both evidence availability and retained context. Its gains do not isolate a pure reasoning-versus-acquisition distinction (§4.1; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Recognition scores exclude missing or failed assessments, so they describe successfully assessed episodes rather than every attempted episode (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Resource settings differ across models, and Gemini lacks diagnostic runs, limiting controlled comparisons and generalization of ablation findings (Appendix G; Tables 11–16).
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** Appendix D.4 specifies a total probe budget of 32, conflicting with reported mean probe counts exceeding 100 and Appendix F’s per-window limits. The operative budget needs clarification.
- **Analyst inference:** Favorable semantic ratings establish perceived plausibility for the sampled episodes, not validity for operational deployment (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Does informative probe selection causally improve adaptation?** Probe quality is inferred rather than measured. Compare model-selected probes with random and rule-aware diagnostic probes under identical budgets.
2. **Can explicit hypothesis revision improve reversal detection?** Reversal recognition trails replacement recognition (§6; Table 10). Test an agent that maintains competing directional hypotheses against the same model using the default protocol.
3. **Do findings extend to broader environmental changes?** Cross-family drift and systematically varied magnitudes are excluded. Evaluate matched episodes that independently vary change type, magnitude, and frequency.
4. **How much does history masking shape the difficulty?** Retained evidence helps, but also changes context. Compare matched-context conditions retaining inputs, selected actions, and utilities separately.
