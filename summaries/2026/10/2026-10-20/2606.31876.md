# Harnessing Textual Refusal Directions for Multimodal Safety

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.31876) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31876)


## One-sentence takeaway
MARS transfers text-derived refusal directions to image and video models through training-free activation steering, with reported safety gains but residual over-refusal and incomplete numerical evidence in the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation
The authors seek to improve multimodal refusal without collecting unsafe multimodal alignment data, which they describe as costly, modality-specific, and ethically problematic. Their identified gap is whether refusal representations already present in an LLM backbone can support multimodal safety—and how to transfer them without also refusing benign inputs (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Transfer analysis:** The authors show that text-derived refusal directions can increase refusal of unsafe images, while identifying sensitivity to intervention layer, strength, and modality-specific activation bias (§3, Figs. 1–2). They claim this is the first such multimodal analysis; the supplied text cannot independently establish priority.
- **Modality correction:** MARS subtracts an activation mean estimated from randomly colored images before measuring alignment with the refusal direction (§3.1).
- **Adaptive intervention:** Positive alignment triggers a ReLU-gated update; distance to the nearest textual acceptance/refusal centroid determines its magnitude. A text-only score selects one intervention layer, and steering operates during first-token generation (§4).
- **Evaluation:** The method is evaluated against zero-shot models, AdaSteer, ECSO, SASA, and AutoSteer on image safety, cross-modal interactions, utility, and video jailbreaks (§5).

The refusal direction itself uses an existing difference-of-centroids procedure; the proposed contribution is its multimodal analysis and the MARS intervention built around it (§2.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Raw steering can reject benign images indiscriminately | Gemma3 rejects 96.4% of safe ViSU queries at layer 10 with steering strength α = 1.0. | §3, Fig. 1(a) |
| Image refusal improves across the main models | Versus zero-shot, reported unsafe-image refusal gains on ViSU range from +25.1% for Gemma3 to +67.9% for Molmo2, using the paper’s delta notation. | §5.2, Table 1 discussion |
| Cross-modal interaction gains extend beyond explicitly unsafe individual modalities | Molmo2 gains up to +58% refusal versus zero-shot on SIUO, where image and text are individually safe. | §5.2, Table 3 discussion |
| Video refusal improves substantially on several models | On VideoSafetyBench, reported gains versus zero-shot are +59.4% for Qwen3-VL, +52% for Qwen3.5, and +39% for Molmo2. | §5.3, Table 6 discussion |
| Utility preservation has an exception | On MMStar accuracy, MARS reportedly matches zero-shot for four main models but loses 3.7% on Qwen3.5 because of occasional refusal. | Appendix B, Table 11 discussion |
| Centering is materially important | Removing centering produces 99.2% refusal of safe ViSU inputs on Molmo2; utility also deteriorates. | §5.2, Table 5 discussion |
| Weak textual alignment still supports some recovery | LLaVA 1.5 yields only 33 rejected extraction prompts; MARS reports refusal gains of +32.3% on HADES and +28.1% on ViSU versus its near-zero baseline. | Appendix G, Fig. 4 |

The supplied retrieval includes table captions and narrative summaries, but omits the numerical table bodies. Reported “+%” changes above preserve the authors’ notation; the prose does not explicitly distinguish percentage-point changes from relative percentages.

These results support transfer of a useful refusal signal across the tested modalities and models. They do not establish comprehensive harmlessness, universal utility preservation, or robustness to attacks specifically designed against MARS. HADES also uses a modified neutral-prompt setting described in Appendix A, which matters when comparing results with other evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** MARS depends on an existing refusal direction, assumes modality bias can be mitigated linearly, and retains over-refusal on benign inputs (§7). Appendix F also acknowledges potential targeted activation-space bypasses.
- **Observed exceptions:** Video improvement is modest in absolute terms for InternVL 3.5; training-free methods struggle on Gemma3 in full HoliSafe; Qwen3.5 loses utility through over-refusal (§5.3; Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Template matching can miss refusals or count refusal-like language without establishing response harmlessness. The additional safety judge is text-only and receives the unsafe HADES prompt as a proxy for visual context (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—geometric justification:** Distance to the nearest centroid defines the proposed trust region, but the supplied evidence does not demonstrate that every displacement inside that sphere remains within well-supported activation space (§4.2).
- **Evidence availability:** Missing table bodies prevent checking complete safety–utility tradeoffs and baseline comparisons. The supplied ablation narrative does not isolate adaptive strength against a fixed-strength alternative (§5.2, Appendix C).

## Open research questions

1. **Can residual over-refusal be reduced without losing safety?** Qwen3.5’s utility loss and the stated limitations leave this unresolved. A next experiment could compare alternative gating thresholds on held-out benign and unsafe inputs.
2. **When does linear centering fail?** The authors explicitly identify nonlinear activation geometry as a risk. Compare mean subtraction with richer bias corrections under controlled visual distribution shifts.
3. **Does adaptive strength outperform simpler magnitude rules?** Its independent contribution is not isolated in the supplied ablation narrative. Compare centroid-derived, fixed, and capped update magnitudes at the same selected layer.
4. **How robust is first-token steering to targeted attacks?** The paper evaluates benchmark jailbreaks and acknowledges possible bypasses, but supplies no MARS-targeted attack study. Test adaptive attacks with semantic safety assessment of complete responses.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported trends are clear, but full numerical tables and most plotted values are unavailable. Other material gaps include uncertainty estimates, exact neutral-image generation settings and default count, complete baseline configurations, quantitative latency overhead, and a targeted adaptive-attack evaluation. Code is linked, but its contents are outside the supplied evidence.
