# HarnessPro: Recursive Harness Self-Improvement with Pareto Optimization for Performance-Cost Trade-offs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26770) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26770)


## One-sentence takeaway

HarnessPro evolves coding-agent harnesses using fixed generated tests and performance–cost selection, with its heavy mode reporting higher task scores and lower execution costs than RHO across three benchmarks and two model–agent pairings (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Recursive harness optimization can improve agent success by increasing exploration and verification, but this can raise token costs and latency. The authors identify a need to optimize task performance and monetary execution cost jointly, while offering deployment options for different budgets (§§1–3).

The setting excludes ground-truth solutions and official tests during evolution, making reliable candidate evaluation another central challenge (§4.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Cost-aware recursive evolution:** An analyzer diagnoses failures and overhead from repeated training rollouts; a planner proposes updates under three performance–cost priorities; an editor implements executable candidates. Plans can draw on differences between retained harnesses (§4.2).
- **Fixed executable evaluation:** A test synthesizer generates tests once from public task descriptions and initial-harness trajectories. These tests evaluate candidates throughout evolution, replacing RHO’s direct LLM trajectory judgments (§4.3).
- **Multiple operating points:** Candidates below the initial validation score or dominated on performance and cost are removed. Normalized weighted squared distance selects up to three representatives: lite, medium, and heavy (§4.3).
- **Evaluated harness artifacts:** Evolved mechanisms include reasoning-effort routing, staged tool budgets, output compression, and verification guidance. These vary by benchmark: ProgramBench’s documented medium harness uses fixed high reasoning effort rather than routing (§5.3; Appendix D).

The contribution is the combined evolution and evaluation framework; the paper explicitly adopts an existing multi-objective selection criterion (§4.3).

HarnessPro results use three evaluation runs on Terminal-Bench 2 and SWE-Bench Pro and two on ProgramBench; reported uncertainty is sample standard deviation (§5.1). The evidence supports improved measured deployment trade-offs in these settings. It does not establish statistical significance, generalization beyond coding tasks, or savings after paying for harness evolution. Baseline variability is not shown in Table 2.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Generated-test validity — analyst inference:** Tests remain a proxy for official evaluation. Higher preliminary failure recall supports their usefulness but does not establish complete coverage or correctness across benchmarks (§4.3).
- **Selection noise — analyst inference:** Each candidate receives one rollout per validation task. Stochastic outcomes could affect Pareto filtering and mode selection; sensitivity to this choice is not reported (Algorithm 1; Appendix A.2).
- **Narrow transfer evidence — analyst inference:** Evaluation spans three software-oriented benchmarks and two model–agent pairings. The supplied results do not establish transfer of a frozen harness to another benchmark or backbone (§5.1).
- **Incomplete mechanism attribution:** Planner and test-synthesizer ablations are described, alongside individual strategies added to RHO. These do not fully isolate interactions among routing, compression, budgets, and verification (§5.5).
- **Operational risks acknowledged in artifacts:** Example plans warn that routing can underallocate reasoning, output caps can hide diagnostics, and routing overhead can outweigh savings on small tasks (Appendix C.2). Their realized frequency is unreported.
- **Latency uncertainty acknowledged by authors:** Server load and network conditions affect response times, so latency is treated as auxiliary rather than optimized (§3).
- **Incomplete retrieved evidence:** Tables 1, 3, and 4 lack their data bodies in the supplied text, limiting comparison, ablation, and pricing inspection.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is selection to validation randomness?** One rollout determines each candidate’s validation metrics. Repeat evolution with larger validation rollout counts and compare selected harnesses and held-out trade-offs.
2. **Do fixed generated tests become exploitable over longer evolution?** The tests remain unchanged, while the reported evolution is short (§§4.1, 5.1). Extend evolution and audit selected artifacts with independent generated suites and official held-out evaluation.
3. **When do deployment savings repay evolution costs?** Reported costs cover task execution rather than the complete optimization process (§3). Measure trajectory collection, synthesis, planning, editing, and validation costs, then estimate deployment break-even volume.
4. **Which evolved mechanisms transfer?** Documented harnesses differ across benchmarks (Appendix D). Evaluate frozen harnesses across benchmarks and backbones, with component-removal experiments to identify transferable gains.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and main outcomes, but supports only limited causal and generalization conclusions.

Material missing information includes token-price values, complete ablation and confusion-matrix counts, exact validation sizes and split identities, evolution costs, baseline run variability, statistical significance tests, and handling of zero-range normalization in candidate selection. Code availability is author-reported and was not independently checked.
