# LIBERO-Industry: Benchmarking Robotic Manipulation under Industrial Process Constraints

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16782) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16782)


## One-sentence takeaway
LIBERO-Industry exposes severe cross-benchmark transfer failures under industrial manipulation constraints and reports higher physical conveyor-unloading completion from industry-specific simulation co-training than from original-LIBERO co-training.

## Problem and motivation
Generalist robot policies must handle changing products, moving materials, limited interaction windows, and precise placement in flexible manufacturing. The authors argue that existing manipulation and manufacturing benchmarks do not systematically combine these requirements when evaluating generalist policies (§§1–2, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Process-constrained benchmark:** A LIBERO extension containing 216 tasks across static-to-dynamic loading (S2D), dynamic-to-static unloading (D2S), and dynamic-to-dynamic transfer (D2D). Difficulty levels jointly vary fixtures, placement requirements, and environmental conditions (Abstract; §3.4).
- **Task generation and scoring:** Procedural BDDL specifications encode transport schedules, language instructions, and completion predicates. Evaluation uses task-specific horizons, complete-task success rate (SR), target-transfer completion (PCR_T), and terminal slot occupancy including pre-existing objects (PCR_S) (§§3.1–3.5).
- **Evaluation and diagnosis:** Five existing policies undergo cross-benchmark evaluation; a cumulative stress progression diagnoses failures; physical D2S trials compare two simulation–real co-training conditions (§4; Appendix C.1). The contribution is principally an evaluation framework rather than a new policy architecture.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| All evaluated checkpoints fail complete tasks | Each of five original-LIBERO-finetuned policies obtains SR **0.0% (0/360)** across 36 tasks with 10 initial states each; no LIBERO-Industry fine-tuning. | §§4.1–4.2; Appendix C.1 |
| Partial completion is extremely limited | π₀.₅ alone has nonzero overall PCR_T: **0.4%**. Its PCR_S is **12.5%**, close to the **12.31%** initial-state slotting baseline; other policies obtain PCR_S **11.2–12.2%**. Same evaluation setting. | §4.2 |
| Cumulative stress sharply reduces π₀.₅ success | SR falls from **97.0% at D0** to **43.8% at D4**, **2.1% at D5**, and **0.0% at D6**. D0 uses 200 trials; D4–D6 use 240 each. Multiple factors accumulate. | §4.3; Fig. 3; Appendix C.3 |
| Industry co-training improves observed physical transfers | At **0.03 m/s**, S2 PCR_T is **34.7% (25/72)** versus S1 **26.4% (19/72)**; at **0.05 m/s**, **23.6% (17/72)** versus **4.2% (3/72)**. Each condition uses 24 three-object D2S tray trials. | §4.4; Fig. 4B |
| Observed advantage extends to tested physical variations | S2 exceeds S1 by **12.5–34.7 percentage points** in PCR_T across five variations involving illumination, appearance, camera configuration, and table texture. Camera variation: **34.7% versus 0.0%**. Trial counts are unavailable in the supplied prose. | §4.4; Fig. 4C |

These results establish poor transfer for the particular evaluated checkpoints and an observed advantage for S2 in the tested physical workcell. They do not establish that industrial tasks are unlearnable, identify individual causes of failure, or demonstrate production-ready throughput. PCR_S near its initial baseline primarily reflects preservation of already placed objects.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The stress progression changes multiple factors cumulatively and is diagnostic, not causal. Numeric evaluator seeds were not retained for those runs (Appendix C.3.2).
- **Author-stated:** Benchmark windows are not universal factory takt times; production-calibrated cycle times, recovery, repeated arrivals, and sustained throughput remain future work (§5; Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** S1 and S2 differ in both simulation source and corpus size—1,724 versus 1,412 demonstrations—so the comparison does not isolate source content independently of dataset size (§4.4; Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported physical outcomes cover one embodiment and D2S Level 1 trays; they do not establish transfer to physical S2D/D2D or tighter fixtures (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Explicit conveyor and slot position/velocity overrides limit conclusions about fully physical contact and retention behavior (Appendix B.5.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which constraint causes the largest failure?** The cumulative progression cannot isolate factors. Run a factorial experiment varying motion, deadlines, precision, and object count independently.
- **Does the simulation-data advantage survive size matching?** Current corpora differ in size. Compare matched simulation frame budgets across repeated training runs.
- **Can gains extend to precise and sustained production?** Reported physical results emphasize tray unloading. Test grooves, inlays, repeated arrivals, and recovery while measuring successful products per observed hour.

## Confidence and missing information
