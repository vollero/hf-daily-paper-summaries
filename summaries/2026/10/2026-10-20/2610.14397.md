# RealityBench: Simulated Environments to Evaluate a Vision Language Model&#x27;s Ability to Document Human Physical Work

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14397) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14397)


## One-sentence takeaway
RealityBench finds that VLMs handle many text-reading tasks well but struggle with measurements and spatial details in simulated work photographs, with model rankings transferring to real photos on three documentation questions (§§6–7).

## Problem and motivation
Physical-work assistants need to judge whether photographs adequately document work and extract relevant facts from them. The authors identify a benchmarking gap: field photographs are often proprietary, variably captured, and labeled only as large packets, making individual-image conclusions difficult to verify. Existing activity datasets primarily label work rather than assess whether a view provides adequate proof (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Procedural benchmark:** Eleven Blender environments covering rooftop solar installation and vehicle repair, with separate image-acceptability and structured-documentation tasks. Hand-written scorers combine privileged scene state with rendered-image properties; acceptability thresholds are validated against human ratings (§§3–4).
- **Model evaluation and diagnosis:** Evaluation of 26 VLMs on 1,378 frames, including accuracy, acceptability F1, API cost, human comparison, and targeted analyses of perceptual errors and answer order (§§6–7; Appendix D).
- **Sim-to-real comparison:** Three matched documentation questions using a released tape-measure photo set and proprietary vehicle photographs. The authors present the environments as extensible to new tasks and closed-loop guidance, but do not evaluate guidance (§§1, 5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Scorer approximates, but does not reproduce, human consensus | On 95 held-out frames excluding ties, majority agreement was 82% (95% CI 75–90%); individual raters agreed with the other raters’ majority at 76–92%, with one limited-coverage rater at 67%. | §4 |
| Best acceptability result is statistically indistinguishable from the human comparator | GPT-6 Astra: F1 0.61 [0.56, 0.66] against the scorer; single non-author rater: 0.59 [0.50, 0.67]. Model precision uses random frames; recall also includes positive sampling. | §6.1; Table 5 |
| Documentation leader still leaves substantial errors | Gemini 3.8 Flash scored 0.84 [0.82, 0.86], using the mean per-frame field score on scorer-acceptable frames, with the tape-leaning exception. | Table 5; §4; Appendix D |
| Text reading is strong but uneven | Across 26 models, eight of ten reading fields averaged 0.90–1.00 on scorer-acceptable frames; VIN transcription averaged 0.59 and gauge reading 0.66. | §7; Table 10 |
| Tape measurement is particularly weak | On scorer-acceptable tape frames, crossing readings were within ±⅛ inch in 11% of answers on average, best 36%; true-gap estimates were within ±¼ inch in 23%, best 48%. | §7; Table 10 |
| Synthetic rankings correlate with real-photo rankings | Across 22 models: Spearman ρ=0.68 for tape reading, 0.79 for car face, and 0.63 for damage severity; all p≤0.002. Real evaluation used 133 tape readings, 117 vehicle-face frames, and 59 severity photos. | Table 14 |
| Perceptual defect reports accompany most traced wrong rejections | Pooled across models on scorer-acceptable frames, erroneous defect reports accompanied 97% of wrong rejections in tape_gap, 74% in slope_gauge, and 63% in rail_attachment. | §7; Table 12 caption |
| Answer order explains only part of the effect | Re-asking 150 frames to nine models with verdict last or absent left defect reports within rerun noise for seven models; two reported fewer defects without the verdict. | §7; Appendix D |

These results support task-specific perceptual weaknesses and useful ranking transfer within the tested questions. They do not establish general field reliability, real-photo acceptability performance, or equivalence to expert workers. Error co-occurrence and the answer-order experiment support the authors’ perceptual-error interpretation without proving a complete causal explanation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation uses single frames, two work domains, general-purpose models at default settings, and no specialist readers or detectors. Real-world validation covers documentation only; error tracing covers wrong rejections in selected environments (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Acceptability is one interpretation of ambiguous requirements. The scorer is relatively strict, and the human comparator uses non-experts with roughly estimated costs (§4; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Dropping ambiguous acceptability cases and gray zones limits conclusions about disputed field photographs. The wire scorer also omits the requirement’s hard-bend condition (§§3.1, 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Real-photo transfer is only partially reproducible: vehicle photographs remain private, vehicle labels come from one author, and some tape responses truncated by output caps were excluded (Ethics statement; Table 14).

## Open research questions
- **Does real-photo acceptability preserve model rankings?** Transfer was tested only for documentation. A next experiment could collect worker photographs with independent expert acceptability ratings and compare real versus simulated rankings (Appendix A).
- **Can video or guided recapture resolve spatial and measurement errors?** Single-frame results cannot answer this. Compare still images, short videos, and active recapture under matched tasks and resource budgets (§8; Appendix A).
- **How much can specialist perception improve performance?** Specialist readers and detectors were not evaluated. Compare zero-shot VLMs with instrument readers and hardware detectors on the same synthetic and real subsets (Appendix A).
- **How sensitive are rankings to the acceptability reference?** Human disagreement and scorer strictness leave this unresolved. Recompute rankings against expert panels and alternative defensible scorer thresholds (§4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the main findings as reported; broader deployment conclusions remain uncertain.

The supplied text omits the bodies of several referenced tables, including full scorer thresholds, per-environment human agreement, verdict-order counts, and per-model real-photo results. It also lacks real-photo acceptability evaluation, expert-worker comparisons, video experiments, and specialist baselines.
