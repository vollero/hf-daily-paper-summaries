# Think Visually Across Space and Time: Grounded Reasoning for Video Segmentation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25090) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25090)


## One-sentence takeaway
TCoVP combines timestamped entity boxes and temporal spans with language reasoning, improving five-benchmark segmentation macro-average J&F from 57.8 to 62.7 over a controlled text-only CoT baseline, although the comparison also adds intermediate grounding supervision (Table 3; §4.4).

## Problem and motivation
Video Reasoning Segmentation resolves an implicit language query into a target’s pixel-level trajectory. Success requires distinguishing contextual entities from segmentation targets and identifying when a queried action occurs, rather than merely when an object is visible. The authors identify a “video reference gap”: language-only reasoning can describe a plausible answer without explicitly linking its entity and event references to observations across frames (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable grounded reasoning:** TCoVP serializes a chronological chain of primitives containing entity identity, semantic description, observation timestamp, bounding box, temporal support, and optional action/relation text. Repeated identifiers link observations; the final answer selects target primitives whose boxes initialize mask propagation (§3.1).
- **Derived supervision:** TCoVP-SFT-15k and TCoVP-RL-13k augment existing segmentation training data with target and contextual primitives. Kimi-K2.5 generates annotations, followed by structural checks and Qwen2.5-VL semantic filtering (§3.2).
- **Chain-aligned training:** Supervised fine-tuning precedes group-relative reinforcement learning with rewards for structure, temporal alignment, spatiotemporal grounding, and target accuracy. SAM3 propagates selected box prompts; GroundMoRe outputs are restricted to predicted action intervals (§§3.3–3.4).

The contribution is the representation, derived supervision, and training interface—not a new mask propagator.

The controlled evidence supports the combined benefit of grounded chains and their supervision; it does not isolate serialization alone. Backend comparisons also show gains with SAM2, supporting an effect beyond switching to SAM3 (Table 5). Cross-method rankings do not establish equal training resources or protocols. Oracle interventions diagnose substantial remaining headroom, rather than attainable performance under ordinary inference.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Uniform sampling of 16 frames can miss brief events; interactions remain natural-language descriptions without dedicated structure; final masks inherit propagation errors (§5).
- **Observed semantic failure:** Correct localization and entity continuity can coexist with reversed action roles and wrong target selection (§A.3.3; Figure 10).
- **Author-stated data risk:** Derived annotations may inherit errors and social biases from source datasets and annotation models (Ethics statement).
- **Analyst inference:** GroundMoRe results depend partly on benchmark-specific prompting, reward weights, and interval-restricted execution; performance without this task specification is not established (§§A.1.2–A.1.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Ablations use only Qwen2.5-VL-3B, limiting evidence that the same causal contributions hold across backbones (§4.4). Reported tables provide no run variability or confidence intervals.
- **Analyst inference:** Qualitative zero-target success does not establish robust rejection performance; aggregate results are not separated by target cardinality (§A.1.1; Figure 8).

## Open research questions
1. **Can adaptive sampling recover short events?** Sparse sampling is an acknowledged limitation (§5). Compare uniform and adaptive sampling at matched visual-token budgets, stratified by event duration.
2. **Would structured interaction roles prevent target confusion?** Figure 10 shows role inversion despite correct grounding. Add explicit actor–action–recipient records and evaluate role-sensitive queries.
3. **How much benefit comes from representation versus supervision?** Table 3 changes both grounded reasoning and intermediate supervision. Run a factorial comparison separating chain serialization from grounding rewards and annotations.
4. **What drives the temporal-oracle gap?** Table 6 reveals substantial interval-localization headroom. Measure boundary errors by event duration and sampling coverage, then compare denser observations with a dedicated interval predictor.
