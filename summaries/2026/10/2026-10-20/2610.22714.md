# First Passage Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22714) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22714)


## One-sentence takeaway

First Passage Attention uses the deepest intervening dip of a learned scalar field to selectively suppress keys, with restricted theoretical expressivity advantages and strong passkey retrieval in a single-seed, 110M-parameter language-model comparison (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation

Ordinary softmax attention does not directly incorporate intervening events into a key’s score. Token-level forget gates do, but suppress older keys together with newer ones, making selective deletion difficult. Pairwise additive suppression can distinguish keys, but accumulates with an event’s duration and adds computation. The author targets selective forgetting that depends on an event’s depth while preserving older relevant context (§1; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Range-minimum attention bias.** FPA adds
  \(b_{ts}=\beta\min(0,\min_{s<u\le t}\phi_u-\psi_s)\)
  to the content score. Each head learns a field, key levels, and positive strength. The field and levels combine a cumulative walk with token-dependent offsets (§2, Equations 1–2).
- **Unified parameterisation.** Special cases recover FoX, bottleneck suppression, and learned expiry. Sharing the walk between field and levels makes unpenalised keys evolve as a stack; suppression is permanent, although finite penalties do not force attention weights to zero (Propositions 2.2–2.3).
- **Expressivity results.** Constructed families separate constant-dimensional FPA from specified positional and token-gated heads, distinguish deepest-dip suppression from additive suppression, and expose limits of masks shared across heads (§3).
- **Training and implementation.** A hard forward minimum uses a soft-minimum straight-through gradient during training. A deterministic fused Triton implementation computes range minima without materialising a full attention-sized matrix (§2; Appendices D–E).

The reported evidence supports selective suppression and strong retrieval in the tested settings. It does not establish general superiority over deeper softmax models, pairwise selection mechanisms, or larger language models. Perfect passkey retrieval also does not establish broad long-context reasoning ability.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scale limit:** language models used one seed each at 110M parameters; larger-scale comparisons may differ (§6). Reported resampling intervals do not measure training-seed variability.
- **Restricted theory:** lower bounds concern particular head weights and assumptions, not complete layer functions or general deep models. Selective Attention and DCP can realise the same hard-limit alive sets (§3.1).
- **Training sensitivity:** pilot heads could stop learning through closed minima, motivating surrogate gradients. MQAR failures show that suppression can obstruct useful attention circuits (§2; Appendix B.7).
- **Comparison qualifications:** tuning exceeded declared budgets, some configurations came from exploratory runs, and some selected learning rates remained at grid boundaries. The author explicitly avoids comparative conclusions from the stack baselines (Appendices B.1, B.5).
- **Analyst inference:** hardware-specific timings and overhead-dominated decoding measurements provide limited evidence about deployment performance on other accelerators or larger batches (§4.3).
- **Interpretation boundary:** bracket correlations are observational and small; the author does not treat them as evidence of bracket tracking (Appendix C.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the gains persist across scale and training seeds?** The language-model comparison cannot resolve this; repeat matched training with multiple seeds and larger models (§6).
2. **Can selective forgetting preserve associative-recall circuits reliably?** MQAR exposes an early-layer failure; compare first-layer exemption, delayed bias activation, and ordinary FPA across recall and language modelling (Appendix B.7).
3. **Does cache compression preserve harder retrieval and reasoning?** Compressed passkey success coexists with weak broader evaluations; sweep eviction thresholds on multi-needle retrieval, RULER, and BABILong (§4.2; Appendix E).
4. **How does FPA compare with independently masked selection heads at scale?** Per-head Selective Attention succeeds on the two-walk probe but lacks a corresponding language-model comparison; train matched models and measure quality and cost (§4.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives extensive methods, proofs, and experimental qualifications, but many tables contain captions without their numerical bodies. Public code, checkpoint and preregistration locations are not established. Multi-seed language-model results, larger-scale evaluations, and measurements on other GPU architectures are unavailable.
