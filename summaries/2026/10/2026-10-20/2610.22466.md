# Harness-Delta Attribution: Dissecting Gains From Harness Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22466) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22466)


## One-sentence takeaway
HDA attributes much of the evaluated harness-evolution gain to detected benchmark shortcuts or additional inference compute, while treating the unexplained residual as an upper bound on generalizable improvement rather than proof of transfer (§§3, 5).

## Problem and motivation
Harness evolution improves prompts, skills, tools, and orchestration around fixed model weights, but aggregate scores conflate transferable improvements, benchmark-specific shortcuts, and increased inference budgets. The authors argue that held-out evaluation alone cannot distinguish these sources when benchmark artifacts persist across splits (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Attribution protocol:** HDA compares the original and evolved harnesses with a compute-scaled baseline and an evolved harness whose detected shortcuts have been minimally disabled. Their score differences yield scaling \(T\), detected overfitting \(O\), and residual \(G\), satisfying \(\Delta S=O+T+G\) (§3).
- **Semi-automated audit:** An analysis agent proposes compute matching and shortcut neutralization; humans review both decisions. Attribution follows a paired-bootstrap significance check. Reported percentage shares are capped, while raw components remain signed (§3).
- **Empirical analysis and reusable assets:** The study covers 25 settings across 11 executor models, three evolution frameworks, and four benchmarks. The authors provide an HDA skill and evaluation suite; the complete machine-readable skill and harnesses are described as supplementary materials (§§1, 4; Appendices B–C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Detected overfitting and scaling explain most gains in many settings | Among 21 settings with significant search-set gains, mean capped attribution shares are 54% overfitting and 14% scaling; their sum exceeds 50% in 14 settings. | §5, discussion accompanying Table 1 |
| Deterministic solutions can appear to transfer | The Meta-Harness Qwen3.5-0.8B ALFWorld controller makes no LLM calls, has O/T/G = 100/0/0, and achieves 93.3% held-out episode success. | Appendix D, Fig. 8 |
| A shared artifact explains strong subset performance | Meta-Harness’s 0.8B LiveMath executor scores 100% accuracy on test questions containing the recurring meta-option (53/53), versus 31% on other questions (22/71). | §5 |
| Additional compute recovers substantial creativity gains | On CREATE, scaling recovers 49% of Meta-Harness Claude Haiku 4.5’s gain; for AHE it adds 5.1 CU, accounting for 75% of evolution gain. | §5; Appendix F |
| Search gains frequently shrink on test | Excluding ALFWorld, held-out gains are smaller in 13/15 settings, below half the search gain in 8, and negative in 4. | §5, Fig. 4 |
| Validation selection helps inconsistently | Across 16 Meta-Harness settings, validation selection improves held-out scores in 10 and reduces them in 6; in 14, its held-out gain remains below the search-selected harness’s search gain. | §5, Fig. 5 |
| Large residuals do not guarantee transfer | SWE-bench Qwen3.6-27B and Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B regress on held-out tasks despite O/T/G = 0/0/100 search-set attributions. | Appendix G |

These results support the authors’ concern that search gains and within-benchmark transfer can obscure shortcuts and compute increases. They do not establish how much residual gain will survive practical distribution shifts. The aggregate percentages summarize capped attribution shares, rather than a pooled score improvement across benchmarks (§3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author qualification:** Neutralization removes only detected mechanisms that can be separated from unrelated changes. Consequently, \(O\) is a lower-bound estimate and \(G\) an upper bound (§3).
- **Author qualification:** Shared artifacts can survive held-out splits. The suggestion that strong proposers supply memorized benchmark knowledge is a proposed explanation, not directly established by a proposer-knowledge experiment (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Attribution depends on reviewer judgments about benchmark specificity, minimal intervention, and compute equivalence. The supplied text reports no reviewer-agreement or alternative-intervention sensitivity study (§3).
- **Analyst inference:** Compute matching is approximate and task-dependent: for example, AHE CREATE increases mean calls from 1.04 to 2.46, while its baseline match uses \(k=3\). Sensitivity to this choice is not reported (Appendix F).
- **Scope:** Coverage is uneven across frameworks and models; SWE-bench instances are filtered for resolution within one hour. Conclusions therefore apply to the evaluated settings, with broader applicability unestablished (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Does residual gain survive artifact-breaking shifts?** Shared split artifacts and residual-dominated regressions leave this unresolved. Next experiment: preserve task objectives while changing answer patterns, simulator rules, or repository characteristics, then evaluate original and neutralized harnesses.
- **How reproducible are HDA judgments?** Human review is central, but agreement and sensitivity are unreported. Next experiment: independently audit identical harnesses with multiple reviewers and compare interventions and attribution estimates.
- **How sensitive is scaling attribution to budget definition?** HDA permits task-specific cost measures and aggregation rules. Next experiment: compare attribution under matched calls, tokens, monetary cost, and latency.
- **Can evolution optimize transferable gains directly?** Validation selection helps inconsistently. Next experiment: compare standard evolution with explicit inference budgets and artifact-audit feedback on unseen task constructions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The protocol and main narrative findings are clear, but the supplied text omits Table 1’s actual rows, detailed figure contents, and supplementary implementations. Material missing details include bootstrap thresholds and intervals, repeated-run variability, evolution budgets, complete per-setting scores and compute-matching procedures, reviewer agreement, and sensitivity to neutralization choices.
