# HERA: Harness-Environment Co-Evolution for Reliable Agentic Abstention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06563) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06563)


## One-sentence takeaway
HERA jointly adapts agent harnesses and failure-targeted environments, improving abstention and feasible-task completion on its synthetic held-out benchmark, although an inconsistent baseline result prevents a clear claim of best paired success.

## Problem and motivation
Tool-using agents may continue acting when missing evidence, conflicting constraints, or insufficient authorization makes a request infeasible. The authors argue that optimizing against fixed tasks exposes a limited range of failures and eventually provides diminishing feedback; harness optimization for abstention is also comparatively underexplored (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Matched task construction:** Generate executable environments and verified feasible tasks, then mutate environment conditions while preserving the request and public tools. Semantic review and searches for alternative solutions check whether abstention is warranted (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Harness–environment co-evolution:** Diagnose execution failures, generate environments targeting current weaknesses, and iteratively edit the harness while keeping model weights fixed. Select checkpoints using a separate validation set (§3.2; Appendix A.3).
- **Evaluation assets and transfer:** Introduce HERA-BENCH, comprising 60 author-reviewed task pairs, and evaluate whether a harness developed with GPT-5.6-Luna transfers unchanged to other models (§4; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Main-model improvement | On 60 held-out HERA-BENCH pairs, GPT-5.6-Luna improves from **61.7% to 83.3% Abstain**, **68.3% to 76.7% Act**, and **48.3% to 70.0% Pair**. | Table 1; §4 |
| Prompting introduces a trade-off | On the same benchmark and model, adding an abstention prompt changes Abstain from **61.7% to 68.3%**, but Act from **68.3% to 63.3%**. | Table 1 |
| Continued feedback helps on training tasks | On the final 50-pair cumulative pool, co-evolution achieves **86.0% Abstain, 88.0% Act, 78.0% Pair**, versus **76.0%, 82.0%, 68.0%** for task generation from initial-harness failures. | Tables 2, 6 |
| Harness transfer | Across 19 additional models on HERA-BENCH, the authors report average gains of **15.3 percentage points Abstain** and **12.5 points Pair**, without model-specific optimization. | §1; Table 3 |
| Lower inference cost at matched paired success | GPT-5.6-Luna with HERA and base GPT-6-Astra both achieve **70.0% Pair**; estimated API costs are **$0.0091 versus $0.0610 per episode**, approximately **85% lower**. Across 14 models, median cost and token multipliers from HERA are **2.09×** and **3.31×**. | §5.2; Fig. 3 |
| Paired-success ranking is inconsistent | Table 1 gives initial-failure task generation **73.3% Pair**, exceeding HERA’s **70.0%**, despite broader superiority claims. | Table 1; §6 |

The evidence supports improved benchmark performance over the base harness and transfer across the evaluated models. It does not establish superiority on every metric: Table 1 contradicts a blanket paired-success claim. Transfer also does not uniformly preserve feasible-task completion; Table 5 reports declines for some models. The cost comparison concerns estimated inference API cost, not total development cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Reported scope:** Experiments use synthetic, controlled executable environments without human subjects or private personal data; deployment effectiveness is not demonstrated (Ethics statement).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** No repeated-run variability, confidence intervals, or significance tests are supplied. Checkpoint validation uses one rollout per task variant (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—certification:** Infeasibility checks include alternative-route searches and author review, but the text does not establish exhaustive formal proofs or independent label-error measurements (Appendices B.3, D).
- **Analyst inference—split audit:** Section 4 claims domain-disjoint benchmark generation, while Appendix C.1 cautions that task disjointness alone does not establish environment-family or source-domain disjointness; detailed overlap checks are unavailable.
- **Analyst inference—attribution and reproducibility:** Individual harness components are illustrated rather than separately ablated. Exact prompts, complete executable artifacts, and optimization costs are not supplied (Fig. 4; Appendices A–B).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** The Table 1 paired-success discrepancy remains unresolved.

## Open research questions
1. **Does co-evolution generalize to independently built environments?** The benchmark shares HERA’s construction pipeline. Next experiment: freeze the harness and evaluate on externally authored environments with independently audited feasibility labels.
2. **Which harness changes cause the gains?** Figure 4 illustrates mechanisms without isolating their effects. Next experiment: remove each component from the frozen harness and measure Abstain, Act, Pair, and cost.
3. **How stable are gains and checkpoint choices?** Reported results lack run-level uncertainty. Next experiment: repeat complete co-evolution runs and benchmark rollouts across seeds.
4. **When does development cost pay off?** Inference estimates omit environment generation and harness optimization costs. Next experiment: account for the full development budget and measure break-even deployment volume.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text describes the method and evaluation substantially, but contains a material result inconsistency.

Missing information includes the corrected Table 1 baseline score, repeated-run uncertainty, independent certification accuracy, detailed split-overlap audits, component ablations, complete reproducibility artifacts, and end-to-end optimization costs.
