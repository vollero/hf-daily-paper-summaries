# Vision as Proprioception: Estimating Humanoid Robot Pose from Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25288) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25288)


## One-sentence takeaway
HumanoidPose combines frozen human-pose features with robot kinematics to estimate humanoid joint configurations from RGB video, reporting 18–32% lower joint-angle error than the best evaluated baseline on each of three real platforms (§IV-B).

## Problem and motivation
External observers and video-learning systems often cannot access robot joint-state logs. The authors address recovering humanoid articulation and world-space motion from video despite differing robot geometries, occlusion, depth ambiguity, and camera motion. They identify a gap between existing manipulator estimators or humanoid 2D detectors and full-body humanoid joint-state recovery; human-pose estimation followed by retargeting introduces an indirect representation and can amplify errors (§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared robot-state decoder:** Frozen PromptHMR features feed a transformer conditioned on joint geometry, limits, and kinematic-tree descriptors. Shared heads predict joint angles and camera-relative root pose across embodiments, without robot-specific prediction heads (§III-C).
- **Synthetic supervision and detector adaptation:** The authors generate 128,000 labeled images across sixteen humanoids by retargeting AMASS motions with GMR and randomizing rendering conditions. A YOLO11 robot detector is further adapted using pseudo-labels from unlabeled online videos; the 3D decoder uses only synthetic supervision (§I, §III-B).
- **World-space reconstruction and transfer evaluation:** Root predictions are combined with estimated camera motion and forward kinematics. Experiments test real-platform accuracy, decoder transfer to held-out embodiments, and synthetic-data adaptation (§III-D, §IV).

## Evidence and main results

The following are author-reported results; the supplied text contains table captions but omits table cell values.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better synthetic articulation estimates | On the held-out sixteen-robot synthetic test set, average MPJAE decreases 35% relative to RoboPEPP; authors report lowest MPJAE and base-frame MPJPE. Robots are weighted equally. | §IV-A–B; Table I |
| Better real articulation estimates | On encoder-synchronized G1, PM01, and N1 videos, MPJAE decreases 18–32% relative to each platform’s best baseline. Authors report lowest MPJAE and base-frame MPJPE on every platform, without real pose supervision. | §IV-B; Table I |
| Better global root translation | On G1 phone-camera recordings with motion-capture reference, root-translation RMSE decreases 40% relative to height-scaled PromptHMR after fixed-scale rigid alignment; errors pool shared matched frames. | §IV-B; Table II |
| Held-out decoder transfer | Real MPJAE is 11.27° on G1 and 11.44° on PM01; median root errors span 8–16 cm across their sequences. Target embodiments are excluded from decoder training but included in detector training. | §IV-D |
| Main design choices help | Removing joint identity, kinematic conditioning, or human-pretrained features worsens both articulation metrics in synthetic and real domains; replacing direct decoding with retargeting causes the largest reported degradation among variants. | §IV-C; Table III |
| Detector self-training helps | Authors report improved real-video detection coverage, retained keypoint accuracy, and lowest downstream pose error with a fixed decoder, evaluated on frames detected by all stages. Numerical values are unavailable. | §IV-E; Table IV |

These results support improved articulation estimation under the evaluated settings. Base-frame MPJPE explicitly removes root translation and rotation, so it does not establish world-space accuracy. Global translation evidence is limited to aligned G1 trajectories; it does not establish absolute localization without alignment or downstream control usefulness (§IV-A–B, §V).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Sustained occlusion and identity consistency in multi-robot scenes remain unexplored. Effects of pose errors on interaction, prediction, and learning from demonstrations need evaluation (§V).
- **Analyst inference:** Held-out embodiment results establish decoder transfer with an already target-exposed detector, leaving fully unseen-embodiment pipeline transfer unresolved (§IV-D).
- **Analyst inference:** Root recovery depends on intrinsics, nominal robot height, and camera-motion estimation. Sensitivity to errors in these inputs is not reported (§III-C–D).
- **Evidence gaps:** Missing table values prevent checking absolute main-comparison errors and ablation magnitudes. The supplied text gives no real-evaluation frame counts, uncertainty estimates, or runtime measurements (§IV).

## Open research questions
1. **Can temporal modeling maintain accurate poses through sustained occlusion?** Pose decoding is per-frame, and multi-robot occlusion remains open. Compare the current decoder with a temporal tracker on identity-labeled multi-robot sequences (§III-A, §V).
2. **How well does the entire pipeline transfer to unseen embodiments?** The transfer detector already sees target-robot data. Hold out target embodiments from both detector and decoder training and measure coverage alongside pose error (§IV-D).
3. **How sensitive is metric localization to calibration and size priors?** No sensitivity experiment is reported. Perturb camera intrinsics and nominal height, then measure articulation and root-trajectory errors (§III-C–D).
4. **Are recovered trajectories useful for robot learning?** Downstream utility remains untested. Compare imitation learning from estimated trajectories against encoder-derived demonstrations (§V).
