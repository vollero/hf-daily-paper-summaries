# Interlock: From Local Correctness to Task Completion in Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19686) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19686)


## One-sentence takeaway
Interlock synthesizes tasks around explicit evidence–decision–effect relationships and trains on judge-accepted executions, improving two backbones on external benchmarks, although a documented judging error limits claims of verified completeness.

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents can make correct individual decisions while overlooking shared constraints or failing to update every affected artifact. Consistent outputs can also inherit the same unsupported assumption. The authors identify a task-synthesis gap: preserving the complete set of conditional obligations connecting evidence, decisions, and downstream effects, rather than merely composing tool calls or subtasks (§§1–2; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Typed completion graphs:** Evidence, decisions, and required effects are linked through grounding edges, coupling hyperedges for joint constraints, and realization edges for downstream obligations. Effects explicitly encode public basis, object population, applicability, and required outcome (§3.1).
- **Graph-constrained synthesis and review:** Specialized generation roles produce a resettable workspace, public request, private deliverable contract, and rubric. Separate reviews check preservation of the original requirements and support from solver-visible inputs (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Completion-filtered self-training:** An agent judge inspects artifacts and can execute verification scripts. Each backbone undergoes supervised fine-tuning on its own accepted executions. This is rejection sampling followed by SFT, rather than an online reinforcement-learning update (§3.4).

The authors’ central methodological distinction is using completion relationships as synthesis constraints (§2). The external experiments assess the resulting pipeline’s training utility, without isolating every component.

The reported gains support the training utility of the complete pipeline on independently authored tasks whose evaluation instances were excluded from synthesis and training. They do not establish that graph structure alone causes the gains, that longer executions imply better reasoning, or that judge acceptance guarantees actual completeness.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Documented verification failure:** The payroll audit directly shows that all-pass judgments can overlook a required outcome (Appendix F.3). The frequency of such errors is unavailable.
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No reported ablation removes individual relation types or either review stage. Their separate contributions remain unresolved.
- **Analyst inference—baseline comparability:** ClawGym-SFT uses released demonstrations, while Interlock uses each target backbone’s own filtered rollouts. This comparison combines differences in synthesis, provenance, selection, and potentially training budget; supplied text does not provide complete condition-specific budgets (Appendix G.2).
- **Analyst inference—selection scope:** Corpus statistics cover tasks with at least one accepted rollout from each backbone. They therefore do not describe the full generated population or tasks neither backbone could solve (Appendices C–D).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Main-result variability, confidence intervals, and exact repetition counts are unavailable. The supplied text contains table captions without the numerical cells of Tables 1–3.

No dedicated author limitations section appears in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Which graph relations drive transfer?** Component ablations are absent. Remove grounding, coupling, or realization constraints separately while matching domains and supervision tokens, then measure external completion metrics.
- **How reliable is completion acceptance?** The payroll case exposes a false all-pass judgment without estimating prevalence. Independently audit a stratified sample of accepted and rejected trajectories using human review and executable checks.
- **Do gains reflect improved downstream follow-through?** Appendix B defines relevant diagnostics but supplies no measured results. Compare checkpoints on its frozen decision–effect units, including public-fact interventions and unchanged-condition controls.
- **How much does solvability screening shape the findings?** The retained corpus excludes tasks without acceptance from both backbones. Evaluate the full admitted pool and report performance by initial solvability and obligation complexity.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method descriptions and benchmark gain statements are explicit, but several key numerical tables are absent.

Material missing information includes absolute benchmark scores, ClawGym margins, main-result uncertainty and repetition counts, full optimization settings and baseline budgets, judge/generator identities for synthesis, generation rejection rates and costs, aggregate judge accuracy, Appendix B diagnostic results, and code/data/checkpoint release availability.
