# HSPR: Recovering Software-Agent Benchmark Scores via Hierarchical Posterior Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24611) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24611)


## One-sentence takeaway
HSPR combines historical response factors, uncertain task-group success rates, and adaptive querying to recover software-agent benchmark scores, reporting the lowest MAE on three of four SWE-bench banks at a 10% task budget, although the aggregate hierarchy gain remains statistically uncertain (§§3–4; Appendix E).

## Problem and motivation
Small benchmark subsets can preserve agent rankings while badly estimating absolute scores. On SWE-bench Pro, Mid-Range selection at a 10% budget achieves Kendall’s τ_b = .967 but its raw subset mean has MAE of 22.798 percentage points (pp); applying source-fitted Ridge to the same outcomes reduces MAE to 3.434 pp (§1).

The authors identify a transfer problem: tasks with identical historical binary responses can have different outcomes for a new agent. They seek to share evidence across such tasks without assuming identical target outcomes (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Hierarchical score model:** Source-fitted logistic item parameters and residual SVD factors represent global target behavior. Tasks with identical historical response signatures share an uncertain, target-specific Beta success rate. Observations update both global and group posteriors; predictive success counts recover a distribution over the finite benchmark score (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Posterior-guided acquisition:** A score-based criterion exactly equals expected one-step posterior score-variance reduction under the assumed model. Source-only validation chooses between this criterion and latent-state variance reduction; neither establishes optimal final absolute error (§3.4; Appendix B).
- **Score decision and controlled evaluation:** The estimator uses a predictive median with optional source-selected calibration. The study separates hierarchy, acquisition, and decision effects through controls across four archived task banks (§§3.5–3.6, 4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Broad point-estimate advantage | At 10%, lowest MAE on Lite, Full, and Pro; Verified MAE is within **0.055 pp** of ATLAS-port. Lowest seed-averaged MaxErr on all four banks. | §4.2; Table 1 referenced |

| Median decision has a small observed advantage | On identical HSPR paths, with decision-specific source calibration, macro MAE is **1.664 pp** for the median versus **1.674 pp** for the mean. | §4.3 |

The evidence supports accurate score recovery within the evaluated archived cohorts and a substantial benefit from adaptive allocation. It does not establish universal baseline superiority or a statistically reliable aggregate benefit from hierarchy: its bootstrap interval includes zero, and individual-bank hierarchy tests do not survive Holm correction (Appendix E). Several table and figure bodies are absent from the supplied text, so their complete results cannot be inspected.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Evaluation covers four banks from one benchmark family. Generalization to substantial model-family or scaffold shifts remains untested; historical-bank coverage and within-group exchangeability constrain validity (Limitations).
- **Split boundary:** Sources and targets have disjoint configuration identifiers but may share base models or related scaffolds. **Analyst inference:** these splits provide weaker evidence for transfer to wholly unfamiliar agent families (§4.1).
- **Model-conditional guarantees:** The acquisition identity concerns one-step variance reduction, not final MAE. Median absolute-loss optimality applies before calibration; the correction reuses outcomes already used for posterior fitting (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Comparison boundaries:** FAQ and ATLAS are adapted ports; PTA-IRT uses historical trajectory information. Matching target-task counts does not equalize historical information or fitting costs (Appendix C).
- **Cost boundary:** Archived replay counts distinct revealed outcomes rather than measuring fresh agent executions. Token, monetary, and end-to-end wall-clock savings cannot be inferred directly (Limitations; Appendix G).
- **Uncertainty validation:** Numerical integration checks assess approximation precision. **Analyst inference:** they do not establish empirical coverage of score-posterior intervals; such coverage results are not supplied (Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Does recovery remain accurate under unfamiliar agent families or scaffolds?** Configuration splits permit related sources and targets. Next experiment: hold out entire families or scaffold classes and measure score error and posterior coverage.
2. **When does group uncertainty reliably help?** Its aggregate interval includes zero, and matched-path Pro reconstruction slightly favors the no-group mean. Next experiment: vary source-bank size and diversity while measuring group structure and paired hierarchy gains (Appendix E).
3. **Are posterior intervals calibrated under response-model mismatch?** The paper supplies numerical precision checks but no empirical interval-coverage results. Next experiment: measure coverage and interval width on held-out targets, including shifted cohorts.
4. **Does task-count efficiency translate into practical savings?** Source preparation and heterogeneous execution costs are outside the primary budget. Next experiment: prospectively evaluate live agents while accounting for task execution, source fitting, configuration selection, and inference costs.

## Confidence and missing information
