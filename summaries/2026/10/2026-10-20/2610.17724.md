# Evidence-First Agentic Verification

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17724) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17724)


## One-sentence takeaway
Sauron independently checks evidence from agent traces and final state before grading rubric criteria, improving reported verification accuracy and personal-assistant evaluation costs, while robustness against agents trained to exploit it remains untested (§§3, 5; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agent tasks mix objectively checkable requirements with requirements needing judgment. Relevant evidence may be scattered across artifacts, environment state, and execution traces; judges can miss it or trust unsupported success reports. The authors identify a gap between **accessing evidence and validating what it supports**, which becomes consequential when verdicts supply reinforcement-learning rewards (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Separate investigation from verification.** Investigators propose claims with source locators. A separate component retrieves the original evidence, checks exact claims mechanically, and sends interpretive claims to an isolated model-based faithfulness checker. A finalizer checks whether verified claims support each verdict (§§3.2–3.4; Appendix B.4).
- **Retrieve selectively and share evidence.** Bounded queries inspect relevant state and trace portions; clustered criteria share investigation sessions and verified claims. A lightweight planner supplies clusters and an advisory task gist (§§3.2–3.5).
- **Produce auditable decisions and evaluate their mechanisms.** Each evidence-backed verdict carries a receipt linking it to claims and sources. Evaluation combines human-labeled episodes, controlled fault injections, real-error localization, ablations, and disagreement analysis (§§4–6). Public release of code or datasets is not established.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better headline quality and cost | On PA100’s 2,586 criteria, GPT-5.6 Sol Sauron achieves unmet-positive F1 **0.96**, MCC **0.94**, and **$297.69** per full run; Gandalf achieves **0.92**, **0.89**, and **$388.25**—reported as 23% lower cost for Sauron. | Table 1; §5.1 |
| Larger process advantage | With GPT-5.6 Sol, process F1 is **0.97 vs. 0.90** for Gandalf on 411 criteria; outcome F1 is **0.96 vs. 0.93** on 2,175 criteria. Default Gandalf lacks trace access. | Table 1; §§4.1, 5.2 |
| Strong paired-fault separation | On 637 pairs across 13 families using GPT-5.6 Sol, Sauron reports **97% detection**, **2% false alarms**, and **Δfault = 0.95**. Full-trace Gandalf and Autorubric score **0.67** and **0.71**, respectively. | §5.3; Table 2 caption; Appendix D.2 |
| Better real-error localization, with higher same-model cost | On TRAIL’s GAIA split with GPT-5.6 Sol, gains over the best baseline for each metric are **0.07 wF1**, **0.18 location accuracy**, and **0.19 joint accuracy**. Same-model cost is reported as **2–3×** baseline cost. | §5.4; Table 3 caption |
| Evidence access and verification contribute | On PAAbl’s 860 decisions with GPT-5.6 Sol, removing trace access lowers F1 by **0.15**; disabling claim verification lowers it by **0.03** while reducing cost by **40%**. | §6.1; Appendix F |
| Selective access and clustering improve efficiency | On the same ablation setting, full-trace context adds **0.01 F1** at **3× cost**; separate sessions per criterion lower F1 by **0.01** and increase cost by **63%**. | §6.1 |
| Interpretation remains a major error source | Manual review finds **48 of 65** PA100 disagreements involve stricter criterion readings; only **3** disagreements concern something other than criterion interpretation. | §6.2; Appendix G |

These results support the authors’ claim that evidence validation adds value beyond merely supplying traces. However, default-baseline comparisons combine differences in evidence access and architecture; trace-enabled comparisons and ablations help separate those effects.

The fault metric measures correctness against each variant’s expected verdict, averaged across families; “detection” does not always mean predicting unmet. TRAIL location and joint accuracy measure recovery of annotated errors, rather than localization precision (Appendices D.2–D.3). No experiment establishes improved RL training outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is English-only; personal-assistant episodes come from one agent in one domain. Model-family favoritism is not systematically tested (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Model-based faithfulness checks can err, agent-authored content can mislead them, and verification depends on reliably recorded, accessible evidence (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Controlled injections do not establish robustness against policies optimized to exploit Sauron. Persistent-memory and peer/subagent failures are outside the injection suite’s scope (Appendices A, D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Defaulting unresolved criteria to unmet mixes demonstrated violations with insufficient evidence. Receipts distinguish them, but budget sensitivity matters when verdicts become rewards; budget exhaustion caused errors in the reported analysis (Appendices B.4, G).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text omits most numerical table bodies and several prompt/configuration contents despite retaining their captions. This limits independent assessment of complete comparisons and reproducibility.

## Open research questions
- **Does Sauron improve agents during RL?** Training use is explicitly future work. Compare policies trained with Sauron and baseline rewards using independently adjudicated task success and exploit rates (§7; Appendix A).
- **Can adaptive agents defeat the evidence checks?** Controlled faults do not test optimized attacks. Train adversarial policies to manipulate state/trace content, then evaluate on held-out tasks (Appendix A).
- **How should insufficient evidence affect rewards?** Budget exhaustion currently produces unmet. Sweep budgets and compare binary defaults with explicit abstention, measuring accuracy, coverage, and reward distortion (Appendix B.4).
- **Do gains generalize across languages and agent families?** Current coverage leaves both uncertain. Run matched multilingual tasks with multiple trajectory-generating families and test verifier-family interactions (Appendix A).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central method and headline findings, but lacks most complete result tables, full prompts and budget formulas, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed code/data release information. Adaptive adversarial robustness and downstream RL benefits remain unmeasured.
