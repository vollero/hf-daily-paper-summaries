# Learning from Fleet Experience: A Collective Learning Paradigm for Robots

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25736) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25736)


## One-sentence takeaway
Failure-guided retrieval of successful fleet trajectories improves initialized robot policies without further expert action supervision, achieving a reported mean task success of 97.3% across five manipulation tasks on held-out robots (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Robot policies can fail under variations in objects, contact conditions and hardware, especially during multi-stage tasks. Human corrective interventions help, but the authors identify continued teleoperator availability as a bottleneck when fleets grow. Their goal is to turn deployment experience into shared policy improvements while avoiding additional expert demonstrations and corrective actions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Collective experience learning (CEL):** A shared learning loop pools deployment trajectories, updates a common task policy and returns it to the fleet. Expert-supervised initialization remains necessary in the evaluated implementation (§§2–3).
- **Failure-guided experience retrieval fine-tuning (FERT):** Failure clips query successful trajectories using frozen visual features, proprioception and slope-constrained subsequence dynamic time warping. Retrieved segments receive higher sampling priority for supervised flow-matching updates; failed actions are never imitation targets (§3.1; Appendix F).
- **Fault-tolerant asynchronous training system (FATS):** SwarmLink separates interaction and experience delivery; HotSwarm recovers failed workers locally and rejects stale messages, allowing other robots to continue (§3.2).
- **Conditional theoretical support:** The authors establish a local advantage over uniform successful replay under current-policy replay and retrieval-alignment assumptions. This is narrower than a guarantee for the implemented repeated flow-matching updates (§3.1.5).

Humans still label outcomes, reset environments and monitor safety; the reduction concerns additional expert **actions**, not all human labor (§§4.1.2, 4.5).

## Evidence and main results

Policy results below use autonomous evaluation on two robots excluded from training collection, with 60 trials each per policy and task (§4.1.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Improvement beyond initialization | Across five tasks, mean success rises from 81.0% after selected HG-DAgger initialization to 97.3% with FERT at each task’s largest fleet; reported gain: 16.3 percentage points. | §4.2; Table 3 |
| High final task success | Largest-fleet FERT success: flower arrangement 99.17%, clothes folding 98.33%, phone charging 97.50%, stool assembly 93.33%, phone packaging 98.33%; respective training fleets: 16, 16, 16, 32 and 96 robots. | §4.2; Table 11 |
| Fleet-size scaling | Phone Packaging success increases from 86.7% with 16 training robots to 98.3% with 96; collection time per robot is fixed, so total experience also increases. | §4.3; Figure 6 |
| Failure guidance helps | On Phone Packaging with 16 training robots, disabling retrieval and sampling successful experience uniformly reduces success from 86.7% to 64.2%, holding other settings fixed. | §4.4; Figure 7a |
| Neighboring actions matter | In the same ablation setting, removing retrieved-segment extensions reduces success from 86.7% to 57.5%. | §4.4; Figure 7c |
| Advantage over evaluated baselines | Mean success exceeds RLT by 12.3 percentage points and RECAP by 11.5 across five tasks; RLT uses one robot, and RECAP uses one offline improvement round. Both collect additional human corrections. | §§4.1.3, 4.2; Appendix G |
| Collection continues through local faults | A 96-robot run collects 9,834 episodes despite 30 incidents affecting 22 robots; reported incident downtime is 1.3% of fleet robot-hours. | §4.5.1; Figure 8 |
| Alternative system comparisons are simulated | Trace replay produces 33% of observed episodes with synchronous rounds, and 92%, 86% or 74% with whole-system restarts lasting 2, 5 or 10 minutes. | §4.5.2; Figure 8c |

These results support improvement from fleet-generated action targets after substantial expert-supervised initialization, including transfer to other instances of the same platform. The retrieval ablation directly supports targeted experience selection in its tested setting. Fleet scaling does not isolate robot diversity from increased data volume, and the baseline comparisons do not establish superiority under matched data, compute or supervision budgets. System alternatives are trace replays rather than deployed controlled comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers task-specific policies, five tasks and one robot platform. Heterogeneous platforms, broader tasks and long-term adaptation remain future work (§6).
- **Observed regression risk:** Smaller fleets can produce policies worse than initialization, including eight-robot Stool Assembly and Phone Packaging runs. Insufficient successful coverage is an author-proposed explanation, not an established cause (§4.3).
- **Human dependence:** Outcome labeling, resets, safety monitoring and hardware repair remain operator responsibilities (§§3.2.3, 4.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Different fleet sizes, update procedures, data sources and baseline adaptations prevent attributing all performance differences solely to FERT’s learning rule (§4.1.3; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Wilson intervals quantify trial-level uncertainty; repeated independent training runs and variation across training seeds are not established in the supplied text (§4.1.4).
- **Theory and systems boundaries:** The local likelihood analysis relies on restrictive replay/alignment assumptions; the practical update uses a surrogate. Recovery modeling excludes shared cloud and edge-cloud failures and assumes fleet-size-independent fault behavior (§3.1.5; Appendices D.4, F).
- **Coverage limitation:** The source was truncated; some proofs, references and table/figure contents are unavailable.

## Open research questions
1. **Does fleet diversity help beyond collecting more data?** Fleet size and total experience increase together (§4.3). Compare different fleet sizes at matched trajectory counts, compute and collection budgets.
2. **When does retrieval cause harmful updates?** Small-fleet regressions occur, and theoretical improvement requires retrieval alignment (§§3.1.5, 4.3). Vary successful-buffer coverage and measure retrieval relevance alongside policy regression.
3. **Can learned outcome labels replace human labels reliably?** Figure 1 mentions learned reward models, but experiments use human outcomes. Compare learned and human labels under controlled labeling errors.
4. **Does CEL remain effective under sustained distribution change?** Long-term adaptation and heterogeneous platforms are untested directions (§6). Run longitudinal experiments with changed objects, calibration and robot platforms.
