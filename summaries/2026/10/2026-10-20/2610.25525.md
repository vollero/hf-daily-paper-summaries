# StopLoss: Teaching Agents to Predict Failure and Stop Early

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25525) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25525)


## One-sentence takeaway
StopLoss learns inexpensive failure monitors from completed coding-agent trajectories, reducing estimated cost per retained solve in several replay settings while exposing substantial risks when stopping thresholds transfer across benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation
Coding agents spend resources on runs that eventually fail verification. The authors identify two shortcomings in existing monitoring approaches: budget limits can terminate eventual successes, while general-purpose model judges can cost more than the execution they avoid. Their goal is to predict eventual failure cheaply from observable execution text, without changing or accessing the executor’s internals (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Outcome-supervised monitoring:** Final verifier outcomes label local trajectory windows: failed-run windows receive Abort; successful-run windows receive Continue or, near completion, Deliver. LoRA adapts three 7B–8B language-model backbones using these labels (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Cheap stopping decisions:** At tool steps 5, 8, 11, and onward, each ensemble member reads the step count and three recent action–observation pairs. A forward pass supplies an Abort score; averaged scores trigger stopping at a threshold calibrated on successful trajectories (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Economic evaluation:** Replay charges visited monitor checks, execution through stopping, and lost successes. The authors compare trained monitors with six same-family model judges and derive when execution savings exceed monitoring costs and false kills (§§3.2–3.3, 5.3).

The contribution is the combination of terminal-outcome supervision, bounded observable windows, inexpensive scoring, and stopping-policy evaluation. The paper itself discusses earlier text-based failure monitors; it does not establish that failure monitoring generally is new (§6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Modest savings with high retention on Verified/Luna | Primary ensemble reduces estimated cost per retained solve by **4.5%** versus unmonitored completion, retaining **99.0%** of otherwise successful runs on **425** calibration-disjoint tasks. | §§3.1, 4.1 |
| Larger Pro savings involve lost successes | On **213** stopping tasks per executor, primary-ensemble CPS falls **24.2%** on Pro/Luna with **72.2%** retention, and **30.6%** on Pro/Sonnet with **91.6%** retention. | §§3.1, 4.1 |
| Backbone changes alter the trade-off | Qwen3-8B reduces Pro/Sonnet CPS by **48.1%**, retaining **83.2%** of successes, versus **30.6%** savings and **91.6%** retention for the primary ensemble. | §4.1 |
| Training improves early prediction | At tool step **5**, primary-ensemble ordinary AUC is **0.642/0.645** on Verified/Luna and Pro/Luna, versus **0.494/0.450** for the untuned base; evaluated on original runs reaching that checkpoint. | §4.2; Figure 3 protocol |
| Monitoring is inexpensive under the proxy | Primary ensemble costs **$0.000237/check** using batch-one GPU-time accounting; pooled GPT-judge checks cost **9–74×** as much under tested configurations. | §4.3; Appendix C.2 |
| Ranking does not ensure threshold transfer | Verified/Luna and Pro/Luna tier AUCs are **0.622/0.614**, but their frozen-threshold false-kill rates are **1.0%/27.8%** on independent stopping cohorts. | §5.2 |
| Cheap monitoring does not always save money | Verified/Sonnet stops no runs and only adds monitoring cost. Verified/Luna savings disappear at approximately **3.83×** the baseline monitoring charge, holding stopping behavior fixed. | Appendix C.4 |

These results support the authors’ claim that trained small monitors can beat the tested judge policies economically: all three backbones have lower estimated CPS than every applicable judge policy across the four settings (§4.1). They do **not** establish universal savings over unmonitored execution, reliable success preservation under distribution shift, or measured online deployment costs.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Training uses GPT-generated trajectories; evaluation is restricted to Python, with Pro covering three repositories. Cross-executor transfer is partial (§§5.2, 7).
- **Calibration risk:** The nominal false-kill target is empirical, not a distribution-shift guarantee. GPT calibration also reuses model-selection tasks (§§2.3, 3.1).
- **Cost validity:** Monitoring costs exclude training, model loading, tokenization, serving overhead, deployment, and idle resources. The proxy measures amortized scoring computation rather than online latency (§3.3; Appendix C.2).
- **Statistical qualification:** Pro has only three repository clusters. Bootstrap intervals condition on trained models and do not account for the decision to expand judge coverage after initial results were inspected (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Terminal outcomes identify failure under the recorded continuation, not irrecoverability. The paper explicitly acknowledges this distinction; its evaluation does not establish benefits from recovery, restarting, or alternative continuations (Appendix A; §3.3).
- **Evidence availability:** The supplied text includes table captions but omits their numerical bodies, preventing inspection of complete comparisons and uncertainty intervals.

## Open research questions
- **Can calibration remain reliable across benchmarks and run lengths?** Similar AUC accompanies sharply different false-kill rates (§5.2). Next experiment: compare frozen thresholds with length-aware calibration on independently held-out repositories.
- **Does diverse executor training improve transfer?** GPT-only training transfers partially to Claude (§§5.2, 7). Next experiment: compare GPT-only and mixed-executor training with matched data volume.
- **Do savings survive online serving costs?** The current proxy excludes several deployment expenses (Appendix C.2). Next experiment: deploy the frozen monitor alongside live executions and measure end-to-end cost, latency, retention, and utilization.
- **How much context and supervision are necessary?** The supplied results do not isolate window length or the auxiliary Deliver label. Next experiment: ablate these choices while holding training data and calibration protocol fixed.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and headline results, but numerical table bodies, full confidence intervals, rendered prompt boxes, exact table-listed model configurations and prices, and explicit code/model/data release availability are unavailable.
