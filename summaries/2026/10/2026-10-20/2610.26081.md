# DeOverlay: A Benchmark and Source-Anchored Flow for Video Overlay Removal

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26081) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26081)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeOverlay combines source-initialized flow with fixed source guidance and reports better regional reconstruction than four baselines on a new synthetic text-overlay benchmark, while recovery of fully hidden content remains dependent on learned priors (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Video overlay removal must reconstruct scenes beneath subtitles, titles, and watermarks while preserving surrounding content and temporal coherence. Neighboring frames sometimes expose the background, but persistent overlays leave content unobserved. The authors identify two gaps: residual overlays and structural distortions in existing methods, and fragmented evaluation across subtitle and watermark settings (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** DVTE-Bench provides 1,631 paired videos across eight text-overlay categories, OCR-screened clean targets, aligned pixel masks, and regional reconstruction and temporal evaluation protocols (§3).
- **Source-anchored flow:** A shared velocity network learns paired source-to-clean latent corrections with region-weighted flow matching. Inference starts from the source latent and repeatedly updates the state while retaining fixed source guidance (§§4.1, 4.3–4.4).
- **Visibility-aware conditioning:** Local aggregation excludes masked observations and normalizes by visible observations; global pooled source context and mask embeddings supplement it. Additive conditioning preserves one video-token sequence (§4.2).
- **Empirical analysis:** Full-benchmark comparisons, component ablations, step-count studies, and separate timing measurements examine reconstruction quality and computational cost (§5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better aggregate regional reconstruction | On all 1,631 clips, six-step DeOverlay reports Mask PSNR **30.80 vs 26.17 dB** and Mask SSIM **0.8822 vs 0.8044** for MiniMax-Remover; relative reductions are **58.3% LPIPS**, **40.8% DISTS**, and **70.3% VFID**. GT bounding-box inputs and pipeline-specific postprocessing apply. | §§5.1–5.2; Table 2 referenced; Appendix D |
| Gains cover every overlay category | DeOverlay leads PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS among all five evaluated methods in each of the eight categories under pixel-mask scoring. | Appendix H, Tables 10–11 |
| Source guidance contributes | On the 200-video subset at four steps, removing VSA or GSC lowers Mask PSNR by **0.091** or **0.171 dB**; ordinary temporal averaging lowers it by **0.101 dB**. | §5.3; Table 3 referenced |
| Additional updates yield diminishing gains | With a fixed checkpoint on 200 videos, two to four steps increases Mask PSNR from **32.410 to 32.461 dB**; eight steps adds **0.007 dB** over four at **1.50× latency**. | §5.3; Table 4 referenced |
| Flow beats a separately trained residual reference | On the same 200-video subset, four-step flow achieves **32.461 vs 31.300 dB Mask PSNR** for the one-step direct-residual model. Training formulation and inference budget both differ. | Appendix F.1; Table 3 referenced |
| Aggregate temporal diagnostic approaches target | On all 1,631 clips, TWE Gap is **0.0118**, versus **0.6133** for MiniMax-Remover and **1.1801** for ProPainter, relative to clean-target TWE **9.3228**. This compares dataset means. | §5.2; Appendices C, I |
| Limited-workload efficiency | On eight shared clips totaling 650 frames on one A100, one-step DeOverlay reaches **3.0847 FPS**, **1.17×** MiniMax-Remover throughput; four steps reaches **2.0712 FPS**. End-to-end timing excludes model loading. | §5.4; Appendix G |

These results support better covered-region reconstruction for the evaluated synthetic overlays and system configurations. They do not establish equivalent gains for real graphics, estimated masks, or untouched scene regions. Perceptual scoring replaces exterior predictions with target pixels, and aggregate TWE deviations can cancel across videos (Appendices C, I). Several headline numerical results are available through prose, although the corresponding table bodies are absent from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Quantitative evaluation focuses on synthetic text. Graphic logos, stickers, and natural scene-text preservation require broader paired evaluation; application examples retain occasional graphics and texture artifacts (§6; Appendix E).
- **Authors’ stated mechanism limits:** Fixed-coordinate aggregation can mix structures under motion and supplies no observations in fully masked windows; fully hidden backgrounds require inference from the video prior (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—deployment validity:** Main results use oracle bounding-box masks, whereas deployment uses OCR-derived text masks. End-to-end performance with estimated masks is not quantitatively established here (§5.1; Appendices A, D).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The noise-start comparison changes initialization and source conditioning together; the residual comparison changes training formulation and step budget. Neither isolates a single cause of improvement (§5.3; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Pipelines use different mask access and postprocessing; CLEAR is tested beyond its subtitle-specific task. SEDiT lacks matched executable evaluation (§5.1; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated-training variability are reported in the supplied text. Timing covers a small workload and different step settings from the main quality comparison (§§5.1, 5.4).

## Open research questions
1. **How sensitive is reconstruction to detection errors?** Oracle-mask results leave deployment robustness unresolved. Next experiment: compare GT, OCR-derived, perturbed, and incomplete masks on the same paired clips.
2. **Can it remove real graphics while preserving scene text?** Those settings lack broad paired evaluation. Next experiment: construct paired logo/sticker cases containing natural scene text and measure removal plus preservation.
3. **Would motion-aligned guidance improve difficult scenes?** Fixed-coordinate aggregation can mix moving structures (§4.2). Next experiment: compare it with motion-aligned aggregation across motion and visibility strata.
4. **Which design choice drives the source-start advantage?** Initialization and conditioning are confounded (§5.3). Next experiment: cross source/noise initialization with additive/concatenated conditioning under matched training and inference budgets.
