# HandsOn: A Large-Scale Simulation Benchmark for Generalizable Dexterous Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26765) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26765)


## One-sentence takeaway
HandsOn pairs 18,000 simulated human demonstrations with demonstrated and held-out manipulation tasks, showing that evaluated policies struggle especially with functional contact, goal retention, and skill composition despite feasible human solutions (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Dexterous manipulation requires controlling contacts throughout an operation: grasping without accidentally activating a tool, coordinating both hands, and preserving achieved goals. The authors identify limited human-demonstration scale and scarce evaluation-only tasks in existing dexterous benchmarks as obstacles to studying these capabilities (§1; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset and platform:** Curated teleoperated simulation trajectories with synchronized views, state/action supervision, appearance re-rendering, and meaning-preserving instruction variation. Re-rendering changes observations while preserving recorded behavior (§4).
- **Complementary task suites:** Core-50 supports demonstration-based learning and fresh execution tests; Transfer-16 reserves asset/relation changes, skill compositions, and deformable tasks for evaluation without target-task training demonstrations. Native predicates determine success, with ordered-stage progress providing additional diagnostics (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Comparative and diagnostic study:** Evaluation of diffusion, vision-language-action, world-action, and prompted VLM controllers, supplemented by demonstration-scaling experiments, instruction-swap probes, and analyses of contact, coordination, retention, and sequencing failures (§5).

The contribution is primarily a benchmark, dataset, and empirical analysis rather than a new policy architecture. Several tasks and assets explicitly adapt prior resources (Appendix A.1).

These results establish substantial execution and transfer gaps under the implemented simulation protocols. Stage diagnostics identify concrete failure patterns, but do not causally establish which architectural changes would fix them. The comparisons also do not isolate architecture, pretraining, compute, or controller-interface effects.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated, including part of the protocol appendices; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.
- **Embodiment scope:** All reported experiments use ROKAE AR5-08 arms, first-generation Wuji Hands, and PhysX. Platform support for other configurations is not demonstrated cross-embodiment performance (§4.6).
- **Physical validity:** Real-robot videos cover few episodes on one platform, with no measured success rate or matched training controls (Appendix F).
- **Comparison qualifications:** Pretraining and compute differ; DP mostly uses an earlier goal sampler. GPT-6 receives different state information and guidance, has fewer trials, and retains heterogeneous recorded call budgets (§5.1; Appendices C.3–C.4, D).
- **Task qualifications:** GPT-6’s door instruction conflicts with its admitted source configuration; carton scoring waives the force-magnitude threshold (Appendix C.4).
- **Scaling scope:** Only two tasks, one training seed, and one nested subset draw are tested. Small-data runs use normalization statistics from the full training pool (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Transfer-16 combines changes in objects, goals, and physics, so its failures cannot isolate compliance or composition as independent causes; the authors acknowledge these factors are not orthogonal (Appendix B.3).

## Open research questions
- **Would richer controller tools improve prompted manipulation?** Current results conflate planning with direct finger control. Compare a fixed model using move_eef versus object-state estimates and reusable skills under matched resets and budgets (§7).
- **Can contact feedback improve goal retention?** Policies lack touch, and held objects occlude relevant regions. Compare vision/proprioception against added validated tactile sensing using unchanged tasks and predicates (§7).
- **Do improvements transfer across embodiments and to hardware?** Reported performance uses one configuration and qualitative physical evidence. Run paired tasks across supported configurations and a fixed-count physical evaluation (§4.6; Appendix F).
- **How broadly does demonstration scaling help?** Existing curves cover two tasks only. Repeat nested-data experiments across skill groups, training seeds, and subset draws with subset-specific normalization (Appendix E).
