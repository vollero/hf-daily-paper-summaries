# MemPilot: Orchestrating On-Demand Multimodal Memory Curation for LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06830) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06830)


## One-sentence takeaway
MemPilot learns to combine compressed-memory retrieval with query-specific curation by heterogeneous LLMs/VLMs, showing adjustable answer-quality–cost–latency-proxy trade-offs on five multimodal memory benchmarks (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Query-agnostic memory construction can spend computation on irrelevant information while discarding details needed by later queries. The authors identify a gap in runtime alternatives: fixed processing patterns, limited backend choice, and incomplete optimization of monetary cost and latency alongside answer quality (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint runtime orchestration:** A text-only policy iteratively retrieves compressed memories or delegates raw-history curation. It controls retrieval query, evidence count, curation instruction, processing model, and image access (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Multi-objective policy training:** GRPO separately normalizes quality, cost, and latency advantages before weighted aggregation. This adapts an existing objective-decoupling approach rather than introducing that principle (§3.3).
- **Stage-level credit assignment:** The proposed prefix probes generate answers before and after curation stages, using changes in answer quality to refine action-token advantages (§3.3; Appendix A.3).
- **Empirical trade-off analysis:** Preference sweeps, component ablations, and memory-bank substitutions examine resource allocation and compatibility (§§4.3–4.6).

The evidence supports adjustable operating points and gains that persist when separate, non-RL-trained models generate final answers. It does not establish universal superiority: Perf does not lead every F1 comparison, and aggressive cost optimization sacrifices considerable quality. Latency results use a calibrated deterministic proxy, not measured end-to-end response times (Appendices A.1–A.4).

A consequential accounting detail: reported cost sums question-level expenses and divides by unique dataset–conversation pairs; latency divides by question count. Thus the cost values should not be read as ordinary per-question prices (Appendix A.4, Eq. 22).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** External delegation can expose sensitive histories; preference coefficients need more usable calibration; latency modeling needs deployment-aware validation (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—ablation interpretation:** Removed components also sharply reduce spending. These results show degradation under the ablated training configurations, but do not isolate component benefits at matched resource budgets (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Refusal questions are excluded, and OOD testing covers selected open-answer/32K subsets. Results therefore do not establish refusal behavior or generalization beyond these settings (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—resource scope:** Offline memory construction and auxiliary training probes are excluded from reported inference accounting. Local-policy pricing and ideal prefix reuse are assumptions, limiting conclusions about total deployment expense (Appendices A.1, A.3–A.4).
- **Reporting gaps:** No uncertainty estimates or repeated-training-seed results are supplied. Judge prompts receive answers and references without questions, and several official evaluation prompts are adapted (Appendix A.4).

## Open research questions
- **Do proxy-latency gains survive deployment?** The authors leave measured-latency optimization open. Test the learned policies under controlled serving and compare proxy rankings with actual response times (Appendix D).
- **Do components help at matched budgets?** Ablations change both quality and spending. Sweep each ablation’s preferences and compare quality at matched cost and latency (Table 2).
- **Can routing preserve privacy without losing useful evidence?** No privacy-aware routing experiment is supplied. Restrict sensitive chunks to local models and measure quality, cost, and external exposure (Appendix D).
- **How well do policies handle refusal cases?** Those questions are filtered out. Restore them and evaluate answerability detection alongside memory QA quality (Appendix A.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methodology, tables, and accounting definitions support the main analysis. Missing material includes numerical frontier data, exact representative preference coefficients and sweep settings, uncertainty estimates, total training expense, full prompt templates, and verified code/model artifact contents.
