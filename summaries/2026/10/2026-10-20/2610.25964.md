# Token-Set Choice Confounds POPE: A Systematic Audit of Yes/No Extraction in VLM Hallucination Evaluation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25964) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25964)


## One-sentence takeaway
On the authors’ quantized LLaVA-1.5-7B run, changing only the yes/no token readout raises POPE adversarial F1 from 0.7608 to 0.8221, demonstrating an evaluation confound without establishing that any surveyed method’s reported gain is invalid.

## Problem and motivation
POPE evaluates object hallucination through binary image–object questions, but extracting yes/no labels from model outputs introduces an additional measurement choice. The authors identify a two-token readout that compares vocabulary IDs the tested model never generates in the specified prompt context.

This matters because readout differences can move benchmark scores enough to complicate comparisons between hallucination-mitigation methods. The authors’ survey finds that published readouts are unspecified, preventing attribution of baseline differences to this artifact (§§2–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired readout audit:** Compare two-token and eight-token max-logit decisions on identical first-token outputs across all POPE splits, recording generated IDs and prediction transitions (§3).
- **Portable extraction protocol:** Derive single-token yes/no sets from each tokenizer, validate them under the actual prompt, and compare setwise maximum logits. Four additional VLMs test portability (§§5–6; Appendix E).
- **Formal and empirical diagnostics:** Establish conditional equivalence between greedy first-token parsing and setwise readout, with a tie exception; account for score changes through confusion-matrix transitions (Appendices C–D).
- **Secondary negative results:** Evaluate nine inference-time corrections and analyze attention, visual similarity, and logit gaps on adversarial POPE (Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The two inspected IDs are never generated | Across 9,000 POPE questions, LLaVA-1.5-7B emits only space-prefixed IDs 3869 and 1939; inspected IDs 3582 and 1217 appear zero times. | §3.2; Table 1 caption; Appendix A |
| Readout alone changes F1 | Two-token → eight-token F1: adversarial **0.7608 → 0.8221**, popular **0.7961 → 0.8498**, random **0.8397 → 0.8713**; 3,000 questions per split, 4-bit NF4 reference. | §§3.1–3.4; Appendix C |
| Adversarial distortion comes from asymmetric flips | Of 1,644 emitted “No” answers, 924 become yes under two-token readout: 660 true negatives become false positives and 264 false negatives become true positives. Two-token yes-rate reaches 0.760. | §1; §3.4; Figure 2 |
| Fixed IDs fail across incompatible vocabularies | Legacy eight-token F1 is 0.00 on LLaVA-1.6-Mistral and Qwen2-VL across the tested splits; tokenizer-derived readout remains between 0.80 and 0.92 across four additional models and all splits. | §5; Table 4 caption |
| Parsing can introduce another harness error | InstructBLIP string parsing yields all-yes F1 0.6667 across three balanced splits, versus tokenizer-derived F1 of 0.82–0.88; the parser reads the wrong text span. | §5; Appendix D |
| Tested corrections do not improve the reference | All nine variants fall below adversarial F1 0.8221; closest is CLIP-B/32 gating at β=1.0, reported 0.005 F1 below baseline. | Appendix F.2; Table 8 caption |
| More image attention associates with errors | Separate 3,000-question adversarial probe: mean attention 0.0801 for wrong versus 0.0738 for correct predictions, layers 14–20; Cohen’s d=−0.46, two-sided Mann–Whitney p=1.45×10⁻²⁴. | Appendix F.3; Figure 8; Table 9 |
| Published gains remain unresolved | The 6.13-point adversarial readout shift exceeds gains reported by five of six surveyed papers, but none specifies its readout and precision is unmatched. | §4; Appendix H |

The paired experiment establishes a readout-induced score change in the tested setup. Cross-model results support tokenizer-specific validation. They do **not** establish that surveyed papers used the faulty readout, quantify corrected published gains, or show that inference-time correction generally cannot work. The attention result is associational; the proposed explanation that uncertainty triggers more looking remains an author hypothesis.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The main audit uses one checkpoint at one quantization level; higher-precision effect sizes are unknown. Published comparisons lack matched readout, precision, prompts, and decoding (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Individual excluded-token logits were not saved. Transition arithmetic verifies bookkeeping rather than independently testing the logit mechanism (Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Corrections were tested only on adversarial POPE. The instrumented pass differs on two near-tie questions, and secondary verification encountered environment-dependent discrepancies and a failed regeneration (§7; Appendices F.3, G).
- **Analyst inference:** Single-token equivalence does not establish validity for multi-token answers or outputs whose first token falls outside the answer sets (Appendix D).
- **Supplied-text ambiguity:** Listing 1 displays duplicate unspaced answer forms despite prose requiring space-prefixed variants. Several referenced tables contain captions without their numerical bodies, limiting implementation review and detailed comparisons.

## Open research questions
1. **How much does precision change the readout gap?** Only the NF4 reference isolates it here. Rerun identical questions and prompts at multiple precisions with both readouts and saved logits.
2. **Which published gains survive matched evaluation?** Surveyed readouts are unknown. Rerun available methods and their baselines under a common extraction protocol and precision.
3. **How should extraction handle multi-token or non-answer prefixes?** The theorem assumes answer-set membership, while InstructBLIP exposes parsing fragility. Test answer-span extraction against annotated outputs across model–template pairs.
4. **Can corrections exploit signals beyond generic uncertainty?** The nine tested variants fail on adversarial POPE. Evaluate an object-specific evidence signal on held-out questions across all splits.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the documented paired readout effect; **medium** in broader portability and diagnostic interpretations.

Material missing information includes full bodies of most result tables, original excluded-token logits, matched higher-precision experiments, verified readouts for surveyed papers, and complete original-run environment details for the additional models. Code and records are reported as released but were not inspected.
