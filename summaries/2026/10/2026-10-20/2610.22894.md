# Competence-Based Evaluation of Logical Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22894) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22894)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors’ competence-based evaluation reveals ordering failures hidden by aggregate accuracy, while reasoning effort and training extend successful performance without ensuring correctness across equivalent presentations and tested sizes (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
A single benchmark accuracy mixes reasoning with language interpretation, shortcuts, and answer biases. The authors argue that evaluation should instead test whether a model reliably applies a fixed rule across a scalable problem family and answer-preserving formulations. Their proposed gap is the lack of an explicit competence condition connecting complexity scaling, presentation invariance, and correctness (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation framework:** CBE specifies a parameterized generator, exact oracle, presentation set, model, and correctness condition. Every tested presentation of an instance must receive the correct answer. Paired outcomes separate both-correct \(R\), both-wrong \(D\), and exactly-one-correct \(U\) behavior (§3).
- **Ordering benchmark:** Generated total orders use adjacent comparisons and balanced endpoint queries, with controlled reshuffling, converse phrasing, relation vocabulary, negation, and distractors. Increasing entity count preserves the ordering rule (§4; Appendix B).
- **Diagnostic analysis:** The authors evaluate GPT and Qwen models, reasoning effort, supervised LoRA, and GRPO, plus an illustrative Horn-clause instantiation. The methodological contribution combines existing generation and invariance ideas under an explicit family-level condition; it does not originate those ingredients individually (§§2, 5–6; Appendices C–D).

## Evidence and main results

Here, invariance includes both correct and shared-wrong answers; \(R\) requires both presentations to be correct. Unless stated otherwise, accuracy counts invalid answers wrong, while paired measures condition on jointly parsed answers.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy hides paired deterioration | GPT-4.1, front/behind, Protocol P, 200 pairs per size: from \(n=4\) to \(1024\), common-support mean accuracy falls 98.3%→73.1%, while \(R\) falls 97.0%→56.8%; terminal \(U=32.7\%\). | §5.1; Fig. 2 |
| Vocabulary changes performance | Same latent instances, pooled base grid \(n=4,\ldots,1024\): GPT-4.1 original accuracy falls 93.1%→70.9% from front/behind to left/right; mean chance-corrected invariance falls 0.75→0.33. | §5.2; Table 1 discussion |
| More reasoning helps but retains failures | GPT-5.4 medium/high effort maintains near-perfect aggregate performance through \(n=1024\). In the separate \(n=2048\) condition, high effort yields 94.0% original accuracy, 65.0% equivalent accuracy, and 67.0% invariance, without truncation; presentation distribution also changes. | §5.4; Table 2; Fig. 3 |
| Completion coverage changes interpretation | Qwen3.5-27B thinking, 24k-token budget: finished-answer accuracy is 100.0%/99.6% and jointly finished invariance 99.9%; all-prompt accuracy is 87.0%/79.1%, with strict invariance 75.4%. | §5.4 |
| SFT transfers little; selected GRPO improves transfer | Qwen3.5-9B LoRA, held-out front/behind: SFT invariance 56.6–58.3% versus base 57.3%. Across twelve clean held-out relations, selected GRPO seed raises invariance 60.0%→83.5% and mean chance-corrected invariance 0.10→0.60. | §6; Table 3 |
| Main GRPO gains cannot be credited to its agreement bonus | Per-prompt normalization makes the nominal bonus effectively inert: logged advantage-vector cosine against correctness-only advantages is 1.0000 at every nonzero-update step. A redesigned bonus produces inconsistent gains across two seeds. | Appendix G |
| Presentation sensitivity exceeds measured repeat variability | GPT-4.1, front/behind, Protocol P, pooled \(n=4,\ldots,1024\): identical-original-prompt agreement is 96.4%, versus cross-presentation invariance 87.9%. | Appendix H.2 |

These results establish failures of the authors’ operational condition within the tested families. They do not establish an internal failure mechanism, a pure inference-depth limit, or inability to reason usefully elsewhere. Repeat controls strengthen the behavioral diagnosis but do not causally isolate presentation error.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Finite samples cannot certify an unbounded family. Ordering endpoint queries admit a shortcut, and size measures input/presentation load rather than isolated inference depth (Limitations; §4.1).
- **Author-stated:** Valid-pair metrics can obscure unfinished or invalid responses. Reasoning budgets and completion coverage materially affect conclusions (§5.4; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** SFT and GRPO differ in data, objectives, and budgets, preventing a general method ranking. Probes cover limited models and settings (§6; Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting the main GRPO seed using reported held-out invariance risks optimistic presentation of transfer; Appendix G explicitly discloses this selection and retains both seeds.
- **Coverage boundary:** English templates provide no multilingual or demographic coverage, and optimization against a known generator may encourage benchmark-specific strategies (Ethical Considerations).

## Open research questions
- **Does failure track inference depth independently of input load?** Endpoint shortcuts prevent an answer here. Next experiment: independently vary required derivation depth, distractor count, and presentation complexity.
- **Can consistency training improve transfer beyond correctness-only training?** The main bonus was inert and the redesigned version had inconsistent two-seed gains. Next experiment: compare matched objectives and budgets across more seeds with independent model selection.
- **How much disagreement comes from decoding versus presentation?** Repeat controls primarily resample originals and are incomplete across conditions. Next experiment: repeatedly sample both presentations of every matched instance.
- **Does CBE generalize to richer reasoning tasks?** Horn-clause results are illustrative and confound rule count, depth, and distractors. Next experiment: apply independently controlled generators to additional task families.
