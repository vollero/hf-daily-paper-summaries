# Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22791) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791)


## One-sentence takeaway
Self-HPO alternates self-proposed harness revisions with reinforcement learning, raising Qwen3.5-35B-A3B’s average resolve rate across three software-engineering benchmarks from 57.8% to 64.8%, above either component’s optimization alone (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Coding-agent performance depends on both model weights and the harness controlling tools, feedback, context, and termination. Optimizing only the harness leaves model capabilities fixed; training only the policy restricts learning to behaviors elicited by a fixed harness. The authors identify a further gap: joint optimization must track changing policy failures, ideally without requiring a separate harness proposer (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Alternating self-improvement:** In each H-step, the frozen current policy examines execution failures, proposes prompt or executable harness changes, and selects the best candidate on a search set. The P-step freezes that harness and trains the policy using binary verifier rewards with GRPO and Balanced Adaptive Rollout (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Policy-driven search targets:** Tasks never solved during the preceding P-step become the next harness-search set. The updated solver also becomes the next proposer, changing both the proposer and its diagnostic tasks (§2.2; Appendix C).
- **Empirical characterization:** Cross-pairing optimized and base components tests their complementarity; repeated attempts assess reliability and task coverage. An external-proposer HPO variant uses Opus 4.8 instead of self-proposal (§§3.3–3.4).

The demonstrated configuration uses two rounds, six harness candidates per H-step, and 50 RL updates per P-step on 2,484 training tasks (§3.1).

These results support complementary harness and policy improvements in the tested configuration, including transfer beyond Python training projects. They do not establish broad model independence, sustained open-ended improvement, or superiority at matched total compute. The search-set ablation directly supports external-proposer HPO; an equivalent Self-HPO ablation is not reported in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported qualifications:** Improvement is uneven across tasks, including regression on the Base-resolved group (§3.4). The case study also identifies a false-positive PR-intent reminder (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** One base model, one seed harness, and two rounds limit conclusions about generality and long-run recursive improvement (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—supervision:** “Self” refers to the proposer model, not absence of external supervision: search has reference fixes, and learning requires executable verification (Appendix D; §2.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison:** Equal harness-candidate budgets and RL configurations are documented, but total compute, wall time, and token costs are unavailable; efficiency advantages remain unestablished (Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** The detailed four-check harness example belongs to external-proposer HPO. Appendix E explicitly distinguishes the Self-HPO harness, so those exact controls should not be attributed to Self-HPO generally.

## Open research questions
- **Does self-proposal work across models and harnesses?** Only one pairing is evaluated. Repeat the experiment with several policy sizes and seed harnesses.
- **How much does reference-fix access contribute?** The proposer receives reference fixes, but their contribution is not isolated. Compare failure-only search against failure-plus-reference search under matched budgets.
- **Does alternating optimization remain beneficial over longer runs?** The reported process stops after two rounds. Run longer schedules and compare held-out gains against additional harness-only and policy-only optimization at matched cost.
- **Which harness interventions cause gains or regressions?** Aggregate cross-pairing does not isolate individual edits. Remove interventions separately and measure resolve rate, submission validity, and regressions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and main results, but several table bodies and figure contents are absent despite their captions and prose summaries.

Material missing information includes total optimization cost, repeated independent training/search seeds, a quantitative search-set stabilization threshold, intervention-level ablations, verified release contents, and complete Self-HPO harness implementation details.
