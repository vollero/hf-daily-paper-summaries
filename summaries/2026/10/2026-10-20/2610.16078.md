# Heterogeneous Agentic Coevolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16078) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16078)


## One-sentence takeaway
HACE jointly updates a specialist, an LLM agent, and their interaction harness, reporting gains over converged specialists and evaluated self-improvement systems across recommendation, protein function prediction, and crystal stability ranking (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Specialized models can plateau under their original supervised training schedules, leaving further improvements dependent on human intervention. The authors argue that a fixed agent also becomes less useful as the specialist’s weaknesses change: improving the specialist requires adapting the agent’s strategies alongside it (§2).

The identified gap is reciprocal learning between models with different capabilities and learning signals, rather than improving only an agent’s harness or updating a student under a fixed teacher (§§2–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reciprocal parameter learning:** Agent exploration selects instances for specialist training using original task labels. Fresh agent responses that outperform the retained specialist provide data for on-policy reinforced self-training; labels never enter agent prompts (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Evolving interaction harness:** Evolutionary search changes information presentation, candidate visibility, and training curricula. A proposed specialist–harness pair is retained only when its combined predictions improve validation performance, with fusion weights fitted through two-fold cross fitting (§4.2; Appendix B.3).
- **Conditional theoretical analysis:** A source-risk bound separates insufficient exposure from the final specialist’s loss on accepted experience. It gives sufficient learning conditions, rather than establishing that HACE necessarily satisfies them (§4.3; Appendix B.1).
- **Cross-domain evaluation:** Experiments and component ablations examine improvement beyond the authors’ operational definition of supervised convergence (§5; Appendix C.3).

## Evidence and main results

The following are author-reported results. Tables use three random seeds, but most numerical table bodies and their standard deviations are absent from the supplied text (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Consistent recommendation gains | Highest mean HR@10 in all 15 settings: five datasets × SASRec/HSTU/Wukong. Relative improvement over Base ranges from 2.9% to 18.5%, averaging 12.1%. | §5.1, referring to Table 1 |
| Larger protein ranking gains than evaluated competitors | CAFA mean NDCG@5/10 rises from 0.6285 to 0.6745 with ESM-2 and from 0.6241 to 0.6739 with ProtT5-XL. Competing methods’ largest improvements over their respective Base scores are 0.0040 and 0.0032. | §5.1, referring to Table 2 |
| Smaller materials ranking advantage | WBM/CHGNet mean NDCG@3/5 rises from 0.6388 to 0.6541; HACE exceeds the strongest competing mean by 0.0028. Without specialist optimization, it reaches 0.6395. | §§5.1–5.2, referring to Tables 2–3 |
| Agent learning matters on recommendation | On Toys/SASRec, removing agent optimization lowers HR@10 from 0.0706 to 0.0622. | §5.2, referring to Table 3 |
| Exploration contributes beyond prediction fusion | Removing agent-guided training experience lowers Toys HR@10 by 0.0044, CAFA mean NDCG@5/10 by 0.0170, and WBM mean NDCG@3/5 by 0.0034. | §5.2, referring to Table 3 |
| Agent size does not predict gains monotonically | In a matched 30-round CAFA/ESM-2 650M sweep, mean within-run NDCG gains are 0.0460 for E4B, 0.0012 for E2B, and 0.0054 for 26B-A4B. | §5.3; Appendix C.3 |
| Learning guarantee depends on coverage and retention | The source-risk bound becomes small with sufficient accepted exposure and sufficiently low final loss; population extension additionally assumes independent source draws and a class fixed independently of them. | §4.3; Appendix B.1 |

These results support improvements for the evaluated complete systems and a useful role for the ablated components. Most reported scores combine specialist and agent predictions, so they do not directly quantify specialist-only improvement (§5). HACE also does not lead every reported metric: the authors identify DGM as having the lowest mean protein semantic distance on ProtT5-XL (§5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only one specialist and one agent coevolve. Sustained learning, multiple learners, and computation allocation remain untested (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated comparison limitation:** Baselines differ in which parameters or programs they can update. Results compare complete systems, without isolating the advantage under identical update permissions (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—validation adaptation:** Cross fitting separates fusion fitting from evaluation within a round, but validation data are reused across rounds. This leaves adaptive validation overfitting as a potential risk; final test data are reserved separately (Appendices B.3, C.1).
- **Theory boundary:** The bound assumes a finite, predetermined specialist class and bounded fixed losses. Neither improved verification nor reduced agent training loss automatically establishes the required exposure or verification improvements (Appendices B.1–B.2).
- **Evidence availability:** Missing table bodies prevent checking most uncertainty estimates and detailed comparisons. Evolution plots show recorded selection scores; the CAFA trajectory contains missing entries (Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions
- **How long does useful coevolution persist?** Appendix A leaves this unresolved. Extend runs under fixed compute budgets and track held-out scores, acceptance rates, and forgetting.
- **How much improvement resides in the specialist itself?** Main scores generally use fusion. Evaluate each retained specialist alone, with the agent, and after removing agent inference.
- **Does HACE retain its advantage under matched update permissions and compute?** Baselines currently differ in allowable updates. Compare systems given identical adaptation access and total resource budgets.
- **Which agent properties explain useful contributions?** The matched size sweep is nonmonotonic. Measure verified-response yield and candidate coverage across agents under matched sampling and training budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and selected results, but omits most numerical table bodies, standard deviations, exact harness search-space tables, and complete prompt templates. Dataset sizes, full baseline configurations, total compute costs, and code/checkpoint availability are also unavailable. These gaps limit assessment of statistical reliability, resource fairness, and reproducibility.
