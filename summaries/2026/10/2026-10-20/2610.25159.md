# Legal Guidance in the Wild: How Users Seek Legal Help in Real-World LLM Conversations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25159) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25159)


## One-sentence takeaway
The paper contributes a taxonomy and benchmark for detecting legal guidance seeking in real conversations, with ModernBERT improving topic classification over TF–IDF but no statistically significant model differences on the small gold evaluation set (§§3, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Legal discussion does not necessarily mean a user wants practical guidance: translation, academic explanation, and advice about personal rights require different interpretations. The authors identify a gap between legal-document benchmarks and understanding guidance intent in naturally occurring, multi-turn conversations. Detecting that intent could support behavioral research and, after deployment testing, caution notices or specialized routing (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Taxonomy:** Separate legal relevance, interaction characteristics—including request type and guidance onset—and legal domain, with primary and optional secondary topics (§3.1).
- **Annotated resource and behavioral analysis:** Screen English WildChat-1M conversations with GPT-5.6 Terra, then annotate 2,122 retained conversations with GPT-5.6 Sol. Analyze guidance intent, request formulation, topics, and onset (§§3.1, 4).
- **Human-reviewed benchmark:** Two computer-science reviewers assess 200 pre-annotated conversations and resolve disagreements. The remaining 1,922 conversations supply silver training and validation data (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Classification comparison:** Evaluate TF–IDF logistic regression and ModernBERT-base/large in a guidance-detection → primary-topic cascade; additionally train guidance detectors on conversation prefixes. The authors report releasing annotations, checkpoints, prompts, and evaluation artifacts (§§3, 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Guidance dominates legal-content conversations in this sample | Silver labels identify guidance in 972/1,367 legal-content conversations (71.1%); enriched sample, not population prevalence. | §4 |
| Rights-and-options advice is the leading primary request | 712/1,367 legal-content conversations (52.1%) receive this primary request type. | §4 |
| Requests often cross topics or emerge later | Among 972 silver guidance positives, 25.3% have multiple topics; 305 (31.4%) first seek guidance after the opening turn. Onset is not human-validated. | §4; Table 2 caption |
| Guidance review shows high agreement | On 200 evaluation conversations, reviewer guidance agreement is 96.5%, Cohen’s κ=.927; reviewers assessed existing silver labels. | §3.2 |
| Full-conversation guidance detection has a small numerical leader | On 200 gold conversations, ModernBERT-large accuracy/macro-F1=.845/.845; TF–IDF accuracy=.840. Large detects 79/84 positives but produces 26 false positives. | §6.1; Table 4 |
| Prefix training yields the highest reported final-turn guidance score | Prefix ModernBERT-large accuracy=.860 and macro-F1=.859, evaluated after the final user turn on the 200-conversation gold set. | §6.1; Table 4 |
| Dense models score higher on topic classification | Given gold guidance labels for 84 positives across 12 observed topics, macro-F1 is .671 for large, .646 for base, and .559 for TF–IDF; correct predictions are 64, 63, and 57 respectively. | §6.2; Table 6 |
| End-to-end gains remain modest | On all 200 gold conversations, correct final routing counts are 151 for large, 148 for TF–IDF, and 145 for base. | §6.2; Table 6 |
| Comparative evidence is inconclusive | No exact paired comparison reaches p<.05; bootstrap macro-F1 intervals overlap. | §6.3 |

The evidence supports the feasibility of detecting guidance intent and suggests contextual encoders help more with topic assignment than binary detection. It does not establish statistically reliable superiority, population-level legal-help demand, legal-answer correctness, or deployment effectiveness. Initial-turn analyses predict eventual conversation intent rather than validated guidance onset (§§6–7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated annotation risks:** Silver labels come from one model; reviewing visible labels may introduce anchoring. Neither reviewer is a legal expert, and request types, secondary topics, uncertainty flags, and onset lack independent human validation (§7).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** The gold set has only 84 guidance positives, covers 12 of 14 topics, and includes five topics with at most five examples. Training uses one random seed (§§3.3, 7).
- **Author-stated transfer limits:** Enriched sampling prevents prevalence estimation; English-only, jurisdiction-agnostic annotation and single-topic routing constrain generalization (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** Descriptive findings about request types and topic overlap are less independently substantiated than guidance detection because their underlying fields were not human-validated (§7).
- Deployment benefits remain proposals: calibration, user testing, and human-support arrangements were not demonstrated (§7).

## Open research questions
- **How well do labels survive independent legal review?** Existing review exposed silver labels and lacked legal experts. Next experiment: collect blinded, from-scratch annotations from legal and nonlegal reviewers and compare disagreement patterns (§7).
- **Does multi-label routing better represent legal needs?** Multi-topic requests are common, but classifiers output one primary topic. Next experiment: evaluate multi-label models against human-validated primary and secondary topics (§§4, 7).
- **Can models detect actual guidance onset?** Current onset annotations are unvalidated, and early-turn evaluation uses eventual labels. Next experiment: annotate onset turn by turn and measure false alerts and detection delay (§§6.1, 7).
- **Do routing or caution notices help users?** Their usefulness is proposed rather than tested. Next experiment: assess calibrated thresholds and user outcomes in a controlled routing or notice study (§7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the central findings, but table bodies and supplementary material are absent. Missing details include exact training configurations and thresholds, full per-class scores, numerical confidence intervals and paired-test results, and complete taxonomy/prompt specifications. Public-release availability is reported by the authors and was not independently verified.
