# CoworkEnv: Synthesizing Training Environments for Long-Horizon Cowork Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24960) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24960)


## One-sentence takeaway
CoworkEnv synthesizes validated, executable coworker workflows that improve compact models through supervised training, while its RL results support optimization feasibility without isolating RL’s incremental benefit.

## Problem and motivation
Coworker agents must coordinate tools, gather information across services, communicate over multiple turns, and respect action constraints. The authors identify a training-data gap: useful examples require mutually consistent requests, service records, executable tools, and graders—not merely plausible final answers (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Executable environment synthesis:** Capability plans guide generation of requests, linked service records, completion checks, and prohibited actions; shared service implementations and grader templates instantiate them (§3.1; Appendix D).
- **Validation and repair:** Parsing, service consistency, identifier leakage, and synthetic grading tests gate acceptance, with diagnostic feedback permitting regeneration (§3.2).
- **Shared training foundation:** Filtered teacher executions supply LoRA SFT demonstrations; student interactions reuse environments for online RL rewards. Separate generation and grading roles support the documented safety-task family (§§3.3–3.4; Appendix C).
- **Training-utility study:** PocketWorker experiments assess SFT across two benchmarks, training-data scale, and existing RL configurations. The contribution is their integration with synthesized environments, rather than a new RL algorithm (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest controlled SFT result | Qwen3-VL-8B: mean task score **46.6 → 64.1**, +17.5 points, across 199 Claw-Eval General/Multi-turn tasks; three trials per task under the same evaluation configuration. Scores use a 0–100 scale. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 |
| Larger improvement on Multi-turn | Same comparison: **45.9 → 77.8**, +31.9 points, on 38 Multi-turn tasks; General improves **46.7 → 60.9** on 161 tasks. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Repeated-trial reliability improves | Overall Pass@3 / Pass 3: **28.6 / 21.6 → 50.8 / 37.2**. These percentages require at least one / all three trial scores to reach 0.75. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 1 |
| Second-benchmark SFT gain | Distinct ClawMark SFT branch: mean score **11.8 → 19.4**, +7.6 points, over 100 tasks on a 0–100 scale. | §4.3; Table 2 description |
| More supervision improves measured performance | Claw-Eval Overall scores for 20%, 50%, and 100% SFT data: **50.8, 55.6, 64.1**, versus base **46.6**. Fractions are sampled separately within each SFT stage. | §4.5; Table 4 description |
| Warm-up-plus-RL pipelines exceed the base | Claw-Eval Overall: base **46.6**, GRPO **56.6**, GDPO **57.2**; Multi-turn: **45.9, 72.5, 74.9**, respectively. Overall covers General plus Multi-turn, excluding Multimodal. | §4.4; Table 3 description |
| RL optimization is feasible | Authors report increasing training rewards across four configurations over approximately three epochs; supplied image placeholders do not expose numerical curves. | §4.4; Appendix B, Figure 3 |

The evidence supports useful SFT supervision across two coworker settings and increasing performance with more training data. It does **not** establish superiority over published frontier references, whose evaluation settings are not confirmed aligned. RL comparisons against the base combine supervised warm-up and RL; without the warm-up checkpoint’s benchmark score, the incremental RL effect remains unknown.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Graders can miss errors outside encoded checks; teacher-success filtering may retain redundant behavior and discard useful recovery trajectories; regenerating demonstrations may require proprietary access (Limitations).
- **Concrete grading gaps:** The safety example checks evidence access and forbidden calls, without establishing fraud recognition or explicit refusal. A ticket-update check verifies the target ticket but not resolution text (Appendices C–D).
- **Author-disclosed comparison confounds:** GDPO changes reward processing and batch size together; Dr.GRPO changes advantage and loss normalization; GiGPO lacks a matched zero-step-advantage ablation (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Length filtering may limit evidence about especially long workflows: conversion rejects trajectories exceeding 20 tool calls and shortens observations to 1,000 characters; SFT uses a 4,096-token limit (Appendices A, E).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported data-scale ablation does not isolate the contributions of validation, repair, capability planning, or teacher filtering (§4.5).
- **Scope:** Experiments concern simulated work environments; real organizational deployment validity is not established (Ethical Considerations).

## Open research questions

- **What does RL add beyond warm-up?** The warm-up-only benchmark score is missing. Evaluate that checkpoint and each RL successor under identical settings and repeated seeds (§4.4).
- **Do validated graders capture semantic correctness?** Documented checks omit refusal semantics and resolution content. Compare grader scores with independent human review and adversarial trajectories (Appendices C–D).
- **Which synthesis components drive gains?** Only data-scale ablation is reported. Run matched-data comparisons removing feedback repair, separate grading roles, or capability coverage planning (§§3, 4.5).
- **Would failure and longer trajectories improve recovery?** Filtering discards unsuccessful or lengthy examples. Compare the current curation against budgets matched for tokens that retain recovery behavior and longer workflows (Limitations; Appendix E).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and principal SFT findings, but omits full bodies of Tables 2–5 and readable Figure 3 curves. Material gaps include exact checkpoint-specific training-set sizes and stage composition, complete warm-up/RL hyperparameters and task counts, warm-up-only evaluation, uncertainty estimates, synthesis acceptance rates and costs, and confirmed release availability.
