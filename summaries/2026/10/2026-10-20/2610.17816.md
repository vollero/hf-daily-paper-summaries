# Empowering Edge Agents: A Systematic Analysis of Edge-Cloud Collaboration in LLM Agent Execution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17816) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17816)


## One-sentence takeaway
EC²-Bench shows that selective cloud assistance improves local agents’ task completion at lower cloud cost than cloud-only execution, while stronger local models narrow performance differences between collaboration strategies without eliminating differences in privacy exposure.

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents repeatedly interact with tools and workspace state, so deciding when to use the cloud affects subsequent actions, cumulative costs, and disclosure of sensitive information. The authors identify a gap in evaluations centered on individual queries or routing decisions rather than complete execution trajectories (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and evaluation framework:** EC²-Bench combines 128 executable digital-workspace tasks, adapted from six benchmarks plus supplementary tasks, with unified execution and scoring. Its privacy annotations cover 3,385 sensitivity units across 100 tasks (§4; Appendix A.3–A.5).
- **Comparative empirical study:** The authors evaluate query routing, per-step routing, edge-controlled delegation, and edge-controlled advisory against edge-only and cloud-only execution. These are representative existing collaboration designs, rather than four newly proposed algorithms (§3.2, §5).
- **Capability analysis:** Experiments vary edge capability and cloud model, then probe invocation timing with a matched random baseline and guidance utilization with instruction-following training (§6; Appendix A.8–A.10).

## Evidence and main results
Completion is a weighted task-check score, rather than a binary success rate. Results average three runs per task–configuration pair; Pass³ requires completion above 0.7 in every run. Privacy exposure is evaluated on the annotated subset (§4.2–5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Partial cloud execution improves a weak edge model at lower cloud cost | Qwen3.5-2B per-step routing achieves 39.97% completion versus 12.75% edge-only; cloud cost is $12.80 versus $56.42 for GLM-5.3 cloud-only. Exposure is 41.29% versus 87.01% cloud-only. | Table 2; §6.1 |
| Stronger edge collaboration approaches cloud-only performance | Qwen3.7-Plus advisory achieves 76.84% completion and 67.19% Pass³, versus GLM-5.3 cloud-only at 82.70% and 71.88%; reported cloud costs are $6.80 and $56.42, respectively. | Table 2; §6.2 |
| Similar completion can coexist with very different exposure | With Qwen3.5-27B, query routing achieves 65.53% completion and 12.53% exposure; advisory achieves 70.13% completion and 48.46% exposure. | Table 2 |
| The highest-completion collaboration strategy changes with edge capability | For Qwen3.5-2B, per-step routing achieves 39.97% versus advisory’s 14.75%; for Qwen3.7-Plus, advisory achieves 76.84% versus per-step routing’s 72.08%. | Table 2; §6.3 |
| A second cloud model supports the broad trend | With DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, Qwen3.7-Plus advisory achieves 75.31% completion at $0.61 cloud cost, versus cloud-only at 81.42% and $4.08. | Table 11 |
| Instruction-following training improves fixed advisory execution | Qwen3.5-2B completion rises from 24.8% to 28.8% after 600 training steps under predetermined advisory calls; edge-only performance is described as largely unchanged. | §6.3; Appendix A.10; Figures 4–5 |
| Automated exposure detection agrees closely with sampled human labels | GPT-5.4-mini achieves 97.7% precision, 96.2% recall, and 96.9% F1 on 200 sensitivity-unit–context pairs against adjudicated labels. | Appendix A.6; Table 8 |

These results support useful trade-offs within the tested configurations. They do not establish one universally preferred strategy: advisory’s completion gains can accompany higher exposure and local computation. The guidance-training experiment supports an association between instruction following and advisory utilization, rather than a complete causal explanation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Benchmark selection — analyst inference:** Filtering uses Qwen3.5-2B, Qwen3.5-9B, and GLM-5.3 endpoint performance and removes jointly easy or unproductive tasks. Results therefore characterize a collaboration-oriented workload, not an unfiltered deployment distribution (Appendix A.3).
- **Deployment scope — explicit design boundary:** Qwen3.5-27B and Qwen3.7-Plus are future-edge proxies. Resource evaluation estimates FLOPs; it does not report measured device latency, energy, or memory, and Qwen3.7-Plus FLOPs are unavailable (§5; Appendix A.11).
- **Privacy scope — analyst inference:** Exposure measures annotated information reaching cloud context, without severity weighting. No redaction is applied, and synthetic content substitutes for real private user data; downstream misuse and protection effectiveness are not tested (§4.2; Ethics Statement; Appendix A.9).
- **Annotation coverage:** Human validation reports precision for sampled identified sensitive units, but does not establish recall of sensitive information missed during annotation (Appendix A.7).
- **Comparison ambiguity:** Appendix A.9 describes query-router calibration in terms of step-level participation but per-step-router calibration in terms of tasks assigned to cloud. Exact budget comparability is consequently unclear.
- **Reporting inconsistency:** §6.2 reports a 3.67-point completion spread for Qwen3.7-Plus, whereas Table 2 implies **4.76 percentage points**: 76.84 minus 72.08. No confidence intervals or significance tests are supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Does the strategy ranking survive broader workloads?** Endpoint filtering may shape the observed advantages. Next experiment: evaluate the same configurations on an unfiltered held-out task suite using different model families.
- **Can privacy protection preserve collaboration gains?** Current calls forward unredacted histories. Next experiment: compare selective context sharing and redaction at matched completion and cloud budgets.
- **Do stronger-edge trends translate into practical device efficiency?** Capability proxies and FLOPs do not establish deployment feasibility. Next experiment: measure completion, latency, energy, and memory on specified edge hardware.
- **Does instruction-following training generalize to autonomous advisory use?** The training analysis fixes invocation timing. Next experiment: evaluate trained checkpoints with autonomous calls and matched cloud budgets across additional edge models.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main comparative findings and includes substantial implementation detail, but some comparisons remain ambiguous.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates, exact endpoint-filtering thresholds, validation-set composition and router thresholds, complete invocation prompts and inference settings, measured hardware performance, and sensitivity-identification recall. Artifact availability is author-reported and was not independently verified.
