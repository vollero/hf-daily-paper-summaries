# Predicting Privileged Information Effectiveness for On-Policy Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22512) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22512)


## One-sentence takeaway
Error-step KL contrast predicts useful privileged information more consistently than teacher reward gains or shift magnitude in the tested settings, and improves PI selection over random choice when candidates differ in source or quality.

## Problem and motivation
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a student against the same model conditioned on privileged information (PI), which the student never receives at inference. Successful agent trajectories can supply actions, observations, reasoning, or compressed hints, but testing every PI construction requires expensive training.

The authors identify two gaps: limited controlled comparisons of PI construction across agent tasks and student types, and a lack of reliable ways to assess PI before training. Teacher performance with PI may be misleading because directly copying privileged answers need not produce transferable supervision (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled PI comparison:** Evaluate 16 configurations across three benchmarks and three Qwen students, varying trajectory components, hint format, and generator. The authors claim this is the first controlled study of this scope; the supplied text cannot independently establish priority (§§1–2).
- **Offline diagnostics:** Propose sampled-token shift magnitude \(D\), the mean absolute student–teacher log-probability ratio, and error-step KL contrast \(\Delta d^{-}_{\mathrm{err}}\). The latter measures suppression of student tokens at judge-identified errors relative to task- and position-matched steps. Although named “reverse KL,” \(D\) uses an absolute sampled-token statistic (§2.2).
- **Controlled validation and selection:** Test leakage through ALFWorld-Passcode, corrupt or length-match PI, and compare metric-guided per-task selection against random selection (§5; Appendix E).
- **Combined training:** Mix OPSD and GRPO advantages to test whether effective PI also benefits outcome-reward training (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| PI content and student type matter | Trajectories generally outperform hints; observations help Instruct students on ALFWorld and AppWorld, while added reasoning can hurt the Thinking student. | §3.1; Fig. 2 |
| Generator choice especially affects hints | For 4B-Instruct, GLM-5.2 PI beats Qwen3.7-Plus PI in 20/21 comparisons; its pass@1 lead reaches 36.0 points for ALFWorld hints, versus at most 3.2 points for trajectory formats. | §3.1; Fig. 2 |
| Error contrast has the most consistent correlation | Across nine student–benchmark combinations, Spearman \(\rho=0.10–0.94\) between error contrast and post-training performance. \(D\) is positive in eight combinations (\(\rho=0.65–0.95\)), but negative for 4B-Thinking on tau2-retail (\(\rho=-0.84\)). | §3.2; Fig. 3 |
| Utility can reward copying without learning | On ALFWorld-Passcode, correct-key-only PI gives the PI-conditioned student 98.1 pass@1, but OPSD produces no improvement over its starting performance. | §5.1; Fig. 5 |
| Error-guided selection helps heterogeneous pools | On ALFWorld with 4B-Instruct, error-contrast selection improves over random with mixed sources or wins/losses. For successful self-rollouts, its lead is 5.1 pass@1 points at step 40, then closes by the end of the third epoch. | §5.2; Fig. 6 |
| Strong PI improves matched GRPO results | With 4B-Instruct, trajectory PI and \(\lambda=0.99\), combined-training pass@1 versus GRPO is 95.13 vs 84.96 on ALFWorld, 43.90 vs 42.91 on AppWorld, and 55.04 vs 48.27 on tau2-retail. | §4.2; Fig. 4 |
| Rapid fitting can accompany poor generalization | On AppWorld, trajectory PI at \(\lambda=0.9\) reaches nearly 100% training success but 39.14 test pass@1. | §4.2; Fig. 4 |
| Shift magnitude alone can favor misleading PI | Failed self-rollouts can induce larger \(D\) than successful rollouts despite being worse supervision; repeating action lists to match trajectory length changes metrics only slightly. | Appendix E; Fig. 7 |

The correlations support screening PI within these settings, rather than a universal predictor of training gains. Controlled leakage experiments strengthen the case against using utility alone, and selection experiments provide evidence beyond correlation. However, a positive correlation as low as 0.10 offers weak ranking evidence, and selection benefits depend on candidate diversity. Reported main performance uses the best three-checkpoint window (§2.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ observations:** Rich PI can disrupt thinking models; static PI becomes less useful during training and may interfere with further GRPO improvement. Small training sets can overfit (§§3.1, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** All students belong to Qwen3. Controlled selection is evaluated only with 4B-Instruct on ALFWorld; combined GRPO experiments also use only that student (§§2.3, 4–5).
- **Analyst inference—judge dependence:** Error contrast relies on one judge configuration marking up to two errors per failed rollout, with reasoning truncated before judging. Judge accuracy, agreement, and sensitivity to alternative judges are not reported (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation selection:** Best-window reporting makes results dependent on checkpoint selection. The supplied text does not establish an independent checkpoint-selection set (§2.3; Appendix G.1).
- **Incomplete retrieved detail:** Tables 1–4 have captions but no cell contents, and plotted values are absent. Consequently, full hyperparameters, per-configuration results, and uncertainty for most comparisons cannot be inspected. Selection curves explicitly report three or four seeds (§5.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does error contrast transfer beyond the tested family and domain?** Selection evidence is confined to ALFWorld and one student. Repeat matched random-versus-metric selection on AppWorld, tau2-retail, and other student families.
2. **How robust is selection to error-label quality?** Judge sensitivity is untested. Compare expert annotations, alternative judges, and deliberately perturbed labels while holding rollouts and PI pools fixed.
3. **Can adaptive PI avoid late-training interference?** The authors observe plateaus with static PI but do not test their proposed remedies. Compare fixed weighting against held-out-performance-triggered OPSD decay and periodically refreshed PI (§5.3).
4. **Does offline scoring save compute overall?** Scoring requires multiple rollouts, teacher evaluations, and error judging, but end-to-end savings are not quantified. Compare total cost and final performance against random selection and training a small set of PI candidates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the central findings and explains the protocols, but omits table cells and numerical plot data.
