# Where is Physical RSI capability from? An Observational Scaling Law of LLM-as-policy

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26848) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26848)


## One-sentence takeaway
PhysRSI-eval separates robot-policy improvement into five outcomes and finds evidence that retained workspaces aid adaptation, while capability bottlenecks and scaling predictions remain exploratory observations across six systems.

## Problem and motivation
The paper asks what enables language-model agents to improve robot manipulation through feedback while their model weights remain fixed. Endpoint scores obscure initial competence, improvement timing, physical cost, and whether repeated attempts produce reusable experience. The authors address this gap by tracking attempts alongside persistent code, tools, and notes, then intervening on that retained information (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation protocol and metrics:** PhysRSI-eval evaluates six model-plus-runtime systems on 18 RoboDojo manipulation tasks, with up to 17 Harness rounds and stopping at first success. Five axes measure first-attempt performance, gain timing, attained score, physical budget savings, and recovery from initial failure (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Capability diagnosis and prediction:** Public benchmark profiles are associated with improvement outcomes, motivating the authors’ **Physical Barrel Hypothesis**: different capabilities may constrain different outcomes. Cross-model ranking and transferred learning-curve forecasts probe predictability (§§4–5).
- **Retained-experience evaluation:** History ablations, frozen-workspace tests on unseen layouts, and three cross-task transfer cases examine whether improvement survives beyond its original setting (§6).

Here, “self-improvement” means revising external artifacts; model weights do not change.

The evidence supports distinct improvement profiles and a benefit from retained information under the tested protocols. It does not establish causal capability requirements or a broadly validated scaling law. A3 is a partial-credit high-window score, A2 measures gain timing rather than wall-clock speed, and A4 excludes unsolved tasks. Frozen-test outcomes share sources and tasks, so they are not independent replications (Appendices B, F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** The main comparison generally has one improvement chain per task; repeated K0 attempts do not estimate full-chain variability. First-success stopping does not establish repeatable execution (§7).
- **Configuration confounding:** Systems include different runtimes and revision windows. Astra solves 15 tasks in the main evaluation versus 13 without supplementary hints; these conditions are reported separately (§3.3; Table 3).
- **Exploratory capability evidence:** Only six systems are analyzed, public benchmark settings differ from the robot runs, and there is no independent validation. The adjusted association results leave substantial statistical uncertainty (§7; Appendix D).
- **Narrow generalization evidence:** Ablation and frozen tests cover Astra on six tasks; cross-task transfer comprises three first-run cases. Seen-layout and held-out-layout comparisons have different denominators (§§6.2, 7).
- **Analyst inference:** The no-history intervention removes several information channels together, so its advantage cannot identify a single responsible artifact. Likewise, one source–target forecast cannot establish transferability of the curve shape across model families.

## Open research questions
- **Which capabilities causally enable recovery?** Rank associations do not isolate causes. A next experiment could selectively add perception or spatial tools while holding the model, runtime, and budget fixed.
- **How broadly do the predictions generalize?** Timing predictions lack independent-system validation, and curve transfer is demonstrated for one model pair. Test preregistered predictors on additional systems and source–target pairs.
- **Which retained artifacts produce reliable transfer?** Workspace tests aggregate code, skills, and notes, while cross-task evidence is limited. Compare isolated artifact types across repeated target-task chains and repeated execution after success.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed protocols and supports the descriptive findings, but broader causal and predictive conclusions remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes the contents of supplementary hints, complete raw trajectories and workspace artifacts, raw public benchmark scores, independent validation results, and uncertainty across repeated full improvement chains. Public availability of benchmark code and artifacts is not established.
