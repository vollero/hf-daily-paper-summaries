# Omni-Woodpecker: Hallucination Correction via Evidence-Prior Rebalancing for Omni LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27103) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27103)


## One-sentence takeaway
Omni-Woodpecker corrects cross-modal hallucinations through training-free, query-conditioned activation edits, reporting accuracy gains of 12.9 percentage points on CMM and 8.6 on AVHBench with Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (§IV-B; §VI).

## Problem and motivation
Audio-visual models can follow a salient but irrelevant modality or language prior instead of the evidence a question requires. The authors argue that existing output-level corrections leave this competition unresolved inside fused representations (§I).

Their central hypothesis is that some hallucinations arise despite correctly encoded evidence because competing cues suppress it during fusion. This motivates restoring available evidence internally; it does not address cases where the backbone fails to perceive that evidence (§I; §V-A).

## Approach and contributions
- **Query-conditioned conflict diagnosis:** Compare full audio-video, audio-only, video-only, and text-only views of a frozen model. Query modality, answer disagreement, and source-normalized attention determine which evidence to protect and which source competes with it; ambiguous cases are skipped (§III-B).
- **Evidence-preserving decomposition:** Construct a target evidence direction from cross-view hidden states, separate an orthogonal competing direction, and measure the evidence deficit in the fused state (§III-C).
- **Adaptive intervention:** Divide a correction budget between attention-path edits and residual-state edits, suppressing competing contributions and restoring evidence without updating weights or changing the language head (§III-D).
- **Distinctive contribution:** Relative to the fixed evidence–prior split described for earlier representation editing, OWP selects streams, directions, heads, and intervention allocation for each input (§II-C). AV Matching uses a separate joint-evidence construction with counterfactual clip replacements (§IV-B).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; percentage-point changes are denoted “pp.”

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Main accuracy gains | Qwen2.5-Omni-7B: CMM overall accuracy 86.1%, +12.9 pp over backbone and +4.9 pp over MAD; complete AVHBench accuracy 83.7%, +8.6 pp over backbone and +7.7 pp over MAD. Evaluation covers all 1,200 CMM conflict cases and 5,302 AVHBench examples. | §IV-A–B; Table I caption; §VI |
| Hallucination resistance improves with a perception cost | On CMM, HR rises from 57.0% to 85.5%; final PA is 86.7%, with a 2.80 pp PA cost reported in the component analysis. | §IV-B–C; Tables I/IV discussion |
| Joint evidence is retained | Qwen2.5-Omni-7B AV Matching accuracy reaches 81.1%, exceeding backbone, MAD, and AVCD by 6.6, 13.1, and 17.9 pp, respectively. | §IV-B |
| Transfer to another backbone | VideoLLaMA2-AV accuracy: CMM 76.2%→80.8%; AVHBench 70.2%→74.1%. Native-protocol transfer adapts edited layers and residual gains. | §IV-A–B |
| General reasoning gains | Qwen2.5-Omni-7B closed-answer accuracy gains: OmniBench +1.93 pp, WorldSense +2.25, MUSIC-AVQA +1.00, AVQA +0.67, using unchanged OWP settings. | §IV-B; Table II discussion |
| Shared-setting transfer is positive but smaller | Under a separate shared binary executor, gains are +2.85 pp on Qwen and +2.14 pp on VideoLLaMA2-AV; media-clustered 95% CIs are [1.95, 3.75] and [1.41, 2.88]. | §IV-C; Table V discussion |
| Adaptive allocation helps | On Qwen under the native ablation protocol, replacing fixed allocation raises directional AVHBench accuracy 83.93%→85.17%, CMM PA 84.67%→86.70%, and HR 82.33%→85.50%. | §IV-C; Table IV discussion |
| Runtime overhead remains material | On RTX A6000, average latency is 1.71× backbone for Qwen and 1.77× for VideoLLaMA2-AV; MAD requires 2.81× and 2.36×. Measurements average three runs. | §IV-B; Table III discussion |

These results support correction of the evaluated cross-modal conflicts and transfer between the two controlled backbones. View ablations and semantic-direction controls support the proposed mechanism (§IV-C–D), but do not establish that representation imbalance explains all omni-modal hallucinations. Native-protocol results and shared binary-executor results use different scoring and must remain separate.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** OWP targets cross-modal hallucination, cannot restore evidence the backbone misperceives, requires model internals, and adds inference passes (§V-A).
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative evaluations use finite answer sets; qualitative autoregressive examples do not establish broad effectiveness for unrestricted generation (§III-D; §IV-B, §IV-E).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-architecture evidence is limited to two controlled backbones. The strongest transfer results involve adaptation; unchanged-setting transfer is demonstrated separately with smaller gains (§IV-A–C).
- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark success depends partly on reliable target-modality diagnosis. Its reported reliability on directional benchmarks does not establish reliability for more ambiguous queries (§IV-B).
- **Evidence availability:** The supplied retrieval includes table captions and narrative results but omits table bodies and most plotted values, limiting independent checking of complete comparisons.

## Open research questions
1. **Does OWP improve unrestricted generation?** Quantitative evidence uses finite-answer scoring. A next experiment could evaluate open-ended audio-video descriptions with claim-level grounding judgments (§III-D; §IV-B).
2. **How does it behave when single-stream evidence is wrong?** The authors explicitly identify this boundary. Stratify examples by target-view correctness and measure correction versus newly introduced errors (§V-A).
3. **Can the diagnosis handle temporal or acoustic attributes?** These are proposed extensions, not demonstrated results. Test event-order conflicts and speaker/prosody attribution using tailored evidence views (§V-B).
4. **How broadly do fixed intervention settings transfer?** Only two controlled architectures are evaluated. Apply a prespecified configuration to additional backbones and report both accuracy and latency (§IV-A–C).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and headline findings, but full table bodies and figure values are unavailable. Other material gaps include complete diagnostic prompt wording, detailed counterfactual selection for AV Matching, general-benchmark absolute scores and sample counts, and the data/procedure used to select global settings before evaluation. Public code availability is an author claim and was not independently checked.
