# Co-adaptive Rule Routing and Constraining for Structured Knowledge Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14465) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14465)


## One-sentence takeaway
CoRE alternates retrieval-feedback-driven rule routing with specialized retriever training, achieving the strongest average retrieval scores on three STaRK-QA datasets without requiring externally supplied query–rule labels (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Structured retrieval must combine query relevance with graph traversal constraints, but the appropriate rule for each query is often unknown. The authors argue that unconstrained retrieval misses useful structure, while heuristic or LLM-generated rule labels can favor semantically plausible paths that perform poorly with the actual retriever (§§1, 3; Appendix C).

The target is therefore to learn rule selection from query–evidence supervision while adapting retrieval under those rules.

## Approach and contributions
- **Latent rule selection:** Factor retrieval into selecting a rule from a schema-derived candidate set and retrieving evidence under that rule; training requires query–evidence pairs rather than externally supplied query–rule pairs (§§3–4).
- **Alternating adaptation:** Train rule-specific retrievers on router-assigned queries, then update the router using ground-truth reciprocal-rank feedback. Classifier routers evaluate all rules; the generation router uses sampled GRPO rollouts (§4.3).
- **Concrete retrieval implementation:** Constrained DFS constructs candidates; learned scorers combine BM25 relevance along traversal paths with rule embeddings (§4.2; Appendix A).
- **Router variants and analyses:** Compare BERT classification, LLM classification, and LLM generation; investigate transfer, component adaptation, rule utility, and inference efficiency (§5; Appendices B–F). Automatic rule discovery is proposed as future work, not demonstrated.

These results support CoRE’s average retrieval advantage and the usefulness of adapting both components in the evaluated settings. They do not establish universal baseline dominance, improved downstream answer generation, or transfer without target-domain retriever training. The authors’ explanations involving representation quality and rollout coverage remain interpretations rather than isolated causal findings (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified limitation:** Generation routing receives sampled feedback, which the authors associate with weaker performance in PRIME’s larger rule space (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—coverage boundary:** A single selected rule and a pool capped at 200 candidates limit which answers can be ranked. Appendix C directly illustrates how an added constraint can exclude correct evidence (§4.4; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Evaluation covers three text-rich graph datasets. Transfer experiments retain trained target-domain retrievers; they do not demonstrate a wholly unadapted retrieval system (§§5.1, 5.4).
- **Training-description ambiguity:** Appendix A specifies hard-target training for BERT-C, while §4.3 presents advantage-based classifier optimization and Appendix D reports RLFT superiority. The training strategy underlying Table 1’s BERT-C scores is consequently unclear.
- **Supervision qualification:** PRIME’s generation router receives filtered teacher demonstrations before GRPO. Thus, absence of externally supplied query–rule labels should not be read as absence of all rule-selection demonstrations (Appendix A).
- **Reporting gaps:** The supplied text gives no seed variability or statistical uncertainty, omits exact values for several figure-based comparisons, and leaves the Table 1 MFAR asterisk unexplained.

## Open research questions
- **Can rule inventories improve automatically?** Rule pruning and expansion are motivated by uneven oracle utility but not tested. Compare fixed inventories with utility-guided pruning and expansion, measuring retrieval quality and candidate coverage (Appendix B; §5.5).
- **How much does rollout coverage explain generation-router weakness?** The paper proposes this explanation without a rollout-budget ablation. Vary rollout count on PRIME while measuring accuracy, reward variance, and training cost (§5.2).
- **Would multiple-rule inference recover excluded evidence?** Current inference selects one rule. Compare single-rule selection with budget-matched top-rule candidate fusion, tracking recall and latency (§4.4; Appendix C).
- **How broadly does routing transfer?** Existing transfer uses the same benchmark family and target-trained retrievers. Evaluate a held-out domain separately with frozen and adapted retrievers (§5.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and tabulated retrieval results are well supported, but training inconsistencies reduce implementation confidence.

Material missing information includes exact figure scores and timings, statistical uncertainty, baseline implementation/provenance details, complete candidate construction and truncation procedures, and an unambiguous mapping from router training strategies to reported results. The authors claim a comprehensive code release; its contents were not inspected.
