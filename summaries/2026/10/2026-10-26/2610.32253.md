# RSIHub: A Composable Framework for Agent-Evolution Methods

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32253) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32253)


## One-sentence takeaway
RSIHub separates agent-evolution policies from shared execution and protected evaluation infrastructure, demonstrating setting-dependent improvements while leaving the stability of gains and adaptive recipe revision unestablished.

## Problem and motivation
Agent-evolution systems repeatedly build similar machinery for candidate workspaces, execution, evaluation, recovery, and lineage. Bundling that infrastructure with evolution policy increases implementation effort and complicates comparisons. Mutable evaluation or record keeping can also make apparent improvements difficult to attribute. The authors address these engineering and measurement problems with common infrastructure and explicit mutation boundaries (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Composable policies:** Recipes bind reusable lifecycle operators and declare mutable resources. The authors adapt selected policies from AHE, Hyperagents, A-Evolve, and GEPA; these are interface adaptations, with exact correspondence to original implementations explicitly qualified (§§3.1–3.2, 4.1).
- **Shared execution and provenance:** Built on Harbor, RSIHub manages candidate commits, surface checks, evaluation, recovery, and lineage. Framework-certified results remain separate from policy-controlled parent admission and annotations (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix D).
- **Common evaluation and released infrastructure:** The study compares fixed recipes across seed agents and benchmarks, alongside execution-fidelity, integrity, overhead, and integration measurements. The authors report releasing Apache-2.0 code, recipes, operators, documentation, and a viewer (§1; §4; Appendices B–C).

An outer agent can also revise recipe bindings between iterations, but this mode is not evaluated (§3.2; §6).

## Evidence and main results

Evolution uses GPT-5.4 target and mutation models under fixed configurations, one trial per task, one child per generation, and at most ten generations. Terminal Bench 2 has 50/19/20 train/gate/sealed tasks; τ³ Banking has 50/20/27 (§4.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Largest overall improvement | MiniSWE on Terminal Bench 2: Hyperagents and A-Evolve reach 69.7% pass@1; the largest improvement is 13.5 percentage points over the seed. | §4.3; Table 1 caption and accompanying prose |
| Rankings depend on seed and benchmark | On τ³ Banking, Hyperagents improves MiniSWE from 27.8% to 33.0%; AHE improves Codex from 24.7% to 34.0% overall pass@1. | §4.3 |
| Overall gains can conceal held-out losses | MiniSWE/Hyperagents on τ³ Banking improves overall but loses 2.1 percentage points in the sealed split’s contribution to full-benchmark pass@1. This is not a sealed-only pass-rate change. | §§4.2–4.3 |
| Execution equivalence remains unresolved | Across three repetitions on Terminal Bench 2, both agents’ framework Avg@3 is 2.6 percentage points below Direct. Codex cross-mode agreement is 82.0%, versus 88.0% between direct executions. | §4.4; Table 2 discussion |
| Tested recovery and boundary cases succeed | All 420 fault-injection and boundary-enforcement trials meet the required outcome without admitting corrupted state; each scenario has 30 trials per benchmark campaign. | §4.5 |
| Measured framework overhead is small | Exclusive framework spans occupy a median 0.02% of generation time on Terminal Bench 2 and 0.019% on τ³ Banking, measured on different hosts. | Appendix B.2 |
| Adaptations require no core edits | Four adaptations use 47–61 recipe lines and 216–1,087 method-specific operator lines, with no framework-source changes at the locked commit. | Appendix B.3 |

These results support composability and the feasibility of improvement under the tested configurations. They do not establish a universally superior policy, statistically stable evolution gains, execution-mode equivalence, or security beyond the tested boundaries. Several referenced tables appear only as captions in the supplied text; the findings above use explicit accompanying prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Evidence guarantees depend on trusted framework services, host/filesystem protections, Git/digest operations, external services, and valid evaluators. RSIHub does not contain hostile code; fingerprints do not provide host attestation (§6).
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Evolution results are descriptive point estimates without repeated runs establishing close differences. Fidelity controls measure task agreement rather than full behavioral equivalence (§§4.3–4.4, 6).
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Only fixed recipes are evaluated, and policy adaptations need not exactly reproduce source implementations (§§4.1, 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate recipe comparisons do not isolate individual operators’ effects; the supplied evaluation reports no controlled operator-swap ablation. Different intended mutable surfaces also limit attribution to search policy alone (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Protected records do not eliminate benchmark overfitting, specification gaming, or harmful intermediate actions (§2; §7).

## Open research questions
1. **How stable are gains and rankings?** Evolution is not repeated sufficiently to establish stability (§4.3). Repeat complete runs with independent seeds and report uncertainty for overall and sealed pass@1.
2. **Which policy components cause improvement?** Aggregate adaptations combine multiple choices (§4.1). Swap selection, analysis, or admission operators individually while fixing mutable surfaces and budgets.
3. **Does adaptive recipe revision improve transfer?** The outer-agent mode is unevaluated (§6). Compare adaptive and fixed recipes under matched budgets, with sealed feedback excluded.
4. **What causes cross-mode execution disagreement?** Fidelity controls leave equivalence unresolved (§4.4). Randomize execution order and expand paired repetitions using unchanged candidates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architectural description and narrative results are clear, but the supplied text omits most table bodies, including complete per-method scores, detailed integrity scenarios, operator contracts, and recovery cases. Full experimental recipes, exact task identities, per-run costs, and repeated evolution estimates are also unavailable here.
