# RynnUnify: Towards Unified Embodied Brain

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32965) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32965)


## One-sentence takeaway
RynnUnify combines embodied reasoning and video prediction for long-horizon planning, with reported gains in anticipatory reasoning and robot control, although the supplied text omits detailed experimental results.

## Problem and motivation
Robots must understand scenes, anticipate action consequences, and coordinate successive subtasks. The authors identify accumulated prediction errors, lost scene context, and insufficient modeling of intermediate dynamics as obstacles to long-horizon planning. They also argue that dedicated evaluations of sustained visual planning and generation-assisted future-state reasoning are lacking (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified reasoning and prediction:** A 16B model pairs an 8B autoregressive reasoner with an 8B diffusion generator, adopting Cosmos3’s Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. The reasoner specifies each subtask, expected visual evolution, and duration; generated outcomes inform subsequent planning (§§1–2.1).
- **Persistent visual context:** Four scene-reference frames and a continuous prefix from the previous clip condition generation. The initial reference remains fixed while historical references update during rollout (§§2.2–2.5).
- **Agentic extension:** An additional 8B agent is post-trained from the reasoner using reinforcement learning with final-answer feedback. It chooses whether to answer directly or invoke frozen RynnUnify for video evidence (§1).
- **Robot-control extension:** A World-Action Model uses latent planning and joint video-action training; implementation details are unavailable in the supplied excerpt (§1).
- **Evaluation assets:** Rynn-VP targets sustained visual planning; Rynn-GEU compares direct and generation-assisted reasoning about unobserved states (§1). Release availability is not established.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Evidence availability:** The supplied text ends at the header of Table 1, before data counts and detailed experimental sections. No explicit author limitation discussion is available.
- **Analyst inference—error propagation:** Each generated clip supplies the observation for the next planning step (§2.1). Prediction mistakes could therefore influence later decisions; robustness is asserted but not quantified here.
- **Analyst inference—limited demonstrated control scope:** Selected simulation tasks and three real-robot tasks support a bounded demonstration, without establishing broad robot-control generalization (Abstract).
- **Unresolved attribution:** The excerpt provides no ablations isolating scene memory, temporal prefixes, self-forcing, or agentic tool selection.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Training quantities, optimization settings, evaluation protocols, and asset availability are not established in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
1. **How much does scene memory improve planning as horizons grow?** No component-level results are supplied. Compare matched rollouts with and without reference frames and temporal prefixes, measuring task completion and consistency by horizon.
2. **When does generated video help future-state reasoning?** The aggregate Rynn-GEU gain does not reveal task-specific benefits or failures. Compare direct, always-generate, and selectively generated answers by question category.
3. **How broadly does the control extension generalize?** Results cover selected simulation tasks and three real-robot tasks. Evaluate held-out tasks, scene layouts, and objects under a fixed trial protocol.
