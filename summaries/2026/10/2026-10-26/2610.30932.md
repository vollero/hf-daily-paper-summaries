# UserArena: Benchmarking Interactive User Simulation for Long-Horizon Shopping Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30932) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30932)


## One-sentence takeaway
UserArena combines real shopping trajectories, an executable environment, and production A/B validation to evaluate user simulators, with the authors’ OPTD method reporting the strongest overall fidelity while still underestimating the tested production policy effect.

## Problem and motivation
Shopping agents need simulators that preserve users’ preferences and decision patterns across extended interactions. The authors identify two gaps: datasets rarely align profiles, visual observations, product context, and actions across repeated visits; and evaluation often measures next-action agreement without testing complete trajectories or agreement with production policy effects (§1).

These gaps matter because locally plausible simulated actions can still produce misleading long-term behavior and offline policy comparisons.

## Approach and contributions
- **UserArena benchmark:** A reconstructed shopping environment paired with 64,729 sessions from 11,010 users, covering 566,102 products and 13 action types. A subset links repeated visits chronologically (§2.1).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Five levels covering executable outputs, local decisions, sequential behavior, personalized population behavior, and outcomes. Replay isolates next-action prediction; Rollout evaluates freely generated sessions (§2.2).
- **On-Policy Trajectory Distillation (OPTD):** A frozen teacher receives abstract intent extracted from complete training trajectories and supplies token-level targets on student-generated responses. Trust-region regularization limits intent’s influence, while recorded-response supervision grounds training (§3).
- **Empirical comparisons:** Shared-backbone simulator evaluation, component ablations, human source attribution, efficiency analysis, and offline comparison against a production ranking-policy experiment (§4; Appendix C).

Crucially, “on-policy” training samples response tokens at logged real states; it does not train on fully student-generated environment trajectories (§3.1).

The supplied prose supports the authors’ ranking claims, but missing table cells prevent checking effect sizes across most fidelity metrics. Production validation establishes relative calibration for one policy pair, not general counterfactual reliability. Human attribution measures perceived realism rather than policy-evaluation accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged calibration gap:** Every evaluated simulator differs from production; even OPTD substantially attenuates the tested effect (§C.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited external validity:** Evidence comes from one platform and one production policy pair. Apparel accounts for 54.90% of clicks, further qualifying domain coverage (§2.1; §A.2).
- **Analyst inference—partial exposure-bias treatment:** Student-generated token prefixes are sampled at real logged states. Robustness to environment states induced by the student’s own earlier actions is not directly established (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—cohort fidelity is narrower than individual fidelity:** PF measures grouped behavioral signatures, so high PF does not establish faithful modeling of each shopper (§A.3.4).
- **Analyst inference—bounded interaction scope:** Rollouts stop at 31 actions per single session or 42 per cross-session constituent session, constraining conclusions about longer sessions (§B.2).
- **Reproducibility gaps in the supplied rendering:** Main result tables lack cells, several mathematical distinctions are lost, and the learning-rate exponent is missing. Intent extraction safeguards are described, but a complete extraction/validation procedure is unavailable (§§3.2–3.3, B.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does calibration generalize across policy changes?** Only one ranking-policy pair is validated. Next experiment: test multiple held-out production interventions and compare simulated effect magnitudes and policy ordering.
- **Would training on student-induced environment states improve long rollouts?** OPTD currently samples real states. Next experiment: compare its training procedure with closed-loop state collection under matched compute.
- **How reliably does abstract intent avoid hindsight dependence?** Filtering rules exclude explicit future details, but quantitative validation is absent. Next experiment: compare full-trajectory intent with prefix-only intent and audit extracted records for prohibited information.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark design and training approach are well described, but most numerical fidelity results cannot be inspected.

Material missing information includes complete result-table values, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run results, production effect magnitudes and observation-window details, exact ranking-policy specifications, complete intent extraction prompts and validation rules, and intact equations and training hyperparameters.
