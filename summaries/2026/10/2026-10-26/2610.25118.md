# How Much Feedback Is Needed to Break Template Protection? The Geometric Vulnerability of Biometric Authentication

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25118) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25118)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under arbitrary feature-vector queries and known matching thresholds, the authors show that binary authentication decisions enable accurate face-template reconstruction, with a diffusion prior reducing reported query requirements by approximately fivefold (§§III-C, V-B, V-E).

## Problem and motivation

Encrypting biometric templates protects their storage and computation, but authentication outputs can still disclose information about them. The authors investigate whether accept/reject feedback alone permits reconstruction and how template-distribution priors reduce query costs. Their central security concern is cumulative leakage through repeated authentication attempts rather than decryption of protected templates (§I).

## Approach and contributions

- **Binary-feedback geometric reconstruction:** Starting from an accepted query, the attack locates decision-boundary points through binary search. These points yield equations identifying the center of an SED acceptance sphere or the axis of a cosine acceptance cone (§III-C).
- **Distribution-aware score queries:** PCA directions estimated from facial embeddings provide informative score queries; low-variance directions are replaced with real-template samples. Reconstruction uses least squares (§IV-B; Appendix A-B5).
- **Diffusion-assisted reconstruction:** A pretrained facial diffusion model generates images under guidance from boundary-derived feature constraints, allowing reconstruction from fewer constraints. The implementation uses IDiff-Face and differentiable FaceNet features (§IV-C; Appendix A-B1).

The authors claim the first binary-feedback reconstruction attack. However, their references include their own earlier work titled *Reconstructing protected biometric templates from binary authentication results*; this text does not explain the relationship, so priority is unresolved (§I; reference [20]).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Binary reconstruction succeeds across evaluated configurations | With 512-dimensional ArcFace/FaceNet templates, binary-search precision \(P=20\), LFW/CelebA, SED/cosine rules, and FMR operating points of 1.0% and 0.1%, the authors report **97.6–100%** of targets below **1% of the authentication threshold**, versus **0–4.0%** for accepted-template averaging. Cosine comparisons use normalized angular distance. | §§V-A–B; Table II caption and accompanying prose |
| Geometric attack has explicit expected query costs | Including acquisition of an accepted starting point: \(1/\mathrm{FMR}+P(d+1)\) for SED and \(1/\mathrm{FMR}+Pd\) for cosine, under the stated oracle assumptions. | §III-C; Eqs. 8, 12 |
| PCA improves score-query efficiency | The authors report **26–72% lower reconstruction loss** than random selection from the target feature distribution across evaluated budgets, and reconstruction approaching numerical precision after roughly **50 queries**. Evaluation budgets extend to **500 queries**. | §V-C; Fig. 5 |
| Table I favors distribution-aware score queries | For its **reconstruction error <1%** criterion, PCA needs approximately **50 queries**, versus approximately **60** for algebraic reconstruction with input selection and **>500** for hill climbing. | Table I; threshold-relative criterion defined in §V-A |
| Diffusion helps when score inputs cannot be selected | With randomly sampled probes, Table I reports approximately **90 queries** for diffusion to reach its **error <1%** criterion; Lagrange, algebraic, and Gaussian-prior methods require **>500**. | §V-D; Fig. 6; Table I |

These results support substantial template leakage under the specified interface access. They do not establish compromise of every protected biometric deployment, recovery of the original enrollment image, or success under active defenses.

Several numerical claims require caution. Table II’s individual cells and the plotted curves are absent from the supplied text. Table I does not identify the configuration or aggregation behind its query counts. Its threshold-relative success criterion also should not automatically be equated with the stronger prose claim of “numerical precision.” The claimed fivefold score-query advantage lacks a clearly matched baseline endpoint in the supplied numerical summaries.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Stricter access constraints, mismatched distributions, and active query detection remain untested; other biometric modalities and surrogate extractors are future work (§§VI–VII).
- **Analyst inference—deployment applicability:** The attacks require arbitrary embedding submissions and known thresholds. Image-only interfaces, unavailable initial accepted queries, or restricted repeated access could materially change feasibility (§III-A; Algorithms 2–3).
- **Analyst inference—unequal preprocessing:** Only the diffusion solver receives SVD-whitened constraints. No reported ablation separates the effects of whitening and the generative prior (Appendix A-B1).
- **Analyst inference—extractor coverage ambiguity:** The evaluation describes ArcFace and FaceNet, but the detailed diffusion implementation explicitly returns FaceNet embeddings. ArcFace-specific diffusion results are not recoverable from this text (§V-A; Appendix A-B1).
- **Analyst inference—geometric notation ambiguity:** The SED derivation uses \(T\) both as a sphere radius and as a squared-distance equation value, leaving the exact threshold convention unclear (§III-C2).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility and cost:** Hyperparameters and a code link are provided, but hardware, runtime, repeated-seed uncertainty, and detailed data partitions are unavailable. Query savings alone do not establish lower computational cost.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is reconstruction to active defenses?** The paper proposes query limits, detection, and randomized responses without evaluating them (§VI). A next experiment could measure reconstruction success and genuine-user acceptance under each defense at matched operating points.
2. **Can an attacker use a mismatched extractor or population?** Exact-extractor relaxation and distribution mismatch remain open (§§VI–VII). Test surrogate-extractor priors and disjoint source populations while holding query budgets fixed.
3. **How much improvement comes from diffusion rather than whitening?** Preprocessing differs between solvers (Appendix A-B1). Compare algebraic, Gaussian-prior, and diffusion solvers using identical constraints, whitening, and rank thresholds.
4. **Does the method transfer beyond faces?** Other modalities are proposed but not evaluated (§VII). Test the geometric attack on fingerprint, iris, or voice embeddings under explicitly specified matching rules.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methodology and prose support the central leakage finding, but precise comparative interpretation is limited by missing Table II cells, unavailable plot values, unspecified summary aggregation, unclear numerical-precision tolerances, incomplete data-split details, absent runtime measurements, and unresolved diffusion extractor coverage and novelty claims.
