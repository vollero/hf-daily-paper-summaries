# A path-following framework on fiber bundle for variational inequalities

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.00778) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00778)


## One-sentence takeaway
The paper proposes a fiber-bundle predictor-corrector solver for general variational inequalities, claiming convergence without monotonicity and reporting success on synthetic tests, although proof gaps and missing experimental details limit confidence in its strongest guarantees.

## Problem and motivation
The goal is to solve finite-dimensional variational inequalities (VIs) with continuous maps on nonempty compact convex domains. The author identifies global convergence without monotonicity or related structural assumptions as the central gap in existing methods (§1). Applications across optimization and equilibrium problems motivate the framework, but none is evaluated directly.

## Approach and contributions
- **Approximate reduction:** Represent an inner polytope approximation using simplex coordinates, then smooth the continuous map through Dirichlet averaging. The paper derives analyticity and relates the transformed VI gap to domain and map approximation errors (§2, Proposition 1).
- **Fixed-point bundle:** Interpret the fixed-point KKT solution set as a bundle with one-dimensional affine fibers. Its geometry separates changes in the simplex variable from changes in the perturbation parameter; fiber jumps provide the proposed singularity-avoidance mechanism (§§3–5).
- **Solver and convergence analysis:** Combine tangent predictors, softmax coordinates, and Newton correctors. The author claims global linear convergence for nonsingular endpoints and linear reduction to any fixed positive precision near singular endpoints; two regularized correctors support practical implementation (§6).
- **Additional theoretical result:** An odd-number-of-solutions theorem is stated for analytic maps whose solutions are all nonsingular (§5.1, Theorem 8).

The experiments support reported reliability within one synthetic map family. They do not establish performance on general continuous maps, preprocessing costs, or superiority over other solvers. The convergence results are author claims whose supporting arguments have unresolved issues described below.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Preprocessing is theoretically controllable but needs tighter integration with path-following (§8). Near singular solutions, the uniform conditioning bound deteriorates as prescribed precision approaches zero (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—finite-jump proof gap:** Proposition 9 infers that a decreasing positive sequence must tend to zero and that avoiding nonzero eigenvalues rules out infinitely many avoidance events near the endpoint. The supplied argument does not establish either implication, weakening support for the global path construction (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—oddness proof gap:** Theorem 8’s sketch infers that an accumulation of zeros forces the multivariate analytic system to vanish identically, without additional justification. This theorem is explicitly separate from the solver’s convergence argument (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference—notation inconsistency:** The stopping-criterion prose uses \(\mathbf1^\top\tilde{\mu}\), although that quantity is defined as zero; Algorithm 1 correctly uses \(\check{\mu}\), the gap-related section (§§4.2, 6.1).
- **Evidence limits:** The convergence analysis centers on standard Newton, while experiments use regularized variants. No external baselines, runtime results, preprocessing experiments, or singularity-avoidance ablations are supplied (§§6–7). The singular-case sublinear statement provides no quantitative rate (§6.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Can finite singularity avoidance be rigorously guaranteed?** Proposition 9 leaves the finite-jump argument incomplete. A next study could strengthen the proof and stress-test maps with eigenvalues approaching zero.
2. **How practical is the general-domain reduction?** Experiments begin with analytic simplex maps. A next experiment could measure original-domain gap, lifted dimension, and total runtime while varying both approximation tolerances.
3. **Do practical correctors preserve the claimed convergence behavior?** The analysis and evaluated variants differ. Compare standard Newton and both regularized correctors on controlled nonsingular and singular problems, recording gap trajectories and conditioning.
4. **How competitive is the solver?** Only internal variants are compared. Evaluate against other VI solvers at matched gap tolerances, including runtime and derivative costs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium** in the method summary and reported experimental scope; **low** in the strongest global guarantees pending resolution of the proof issues.

Material missing information includes Table 1’s numerical entries, full dimension list, random-generation distributions and seeds, concrete step-size and fiber-jump policies, runtime and hardware, implementation/code availability, and quantitative singular-endpoint convergence rates.
