# FLOMSS: Generative Music Source Separation by Flow Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17821) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17821)


## One-sentence takeaway
FLOMSS applies conditional flow matching to full-band stereo music separation, improving spectral and embedding metrics over a comparable discriminative model while sacrificing SDR and achieving competitive listening scores on MUSDB18-HQ (Tables 1, 3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Music source separation reconstructs individual stems from a mixture. The authors target Vocals, Bass, Drums, and Other for high-fidelity music production. They identify a gap between strong discriminative separators and earlier generative approaches that generally lack explicit mixture-to-source generative mapping or full-band stereo output, alongside limited systematic evaluation of flow matching for this setting (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Conditional generative separator:** FLOMSS learns mixture-conditioned transport toward source spectrograms in a power-compressed complex-STFT representation, using an NCSN++ U-Net. Inverse compression and inverse STFT recover audio (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Controlled design study:** The authors compare velocity versus source prediction, optimal-transport-inspired versus Schrödinger-bridge-inspired paths, separate versus joint source models, and soft versus hard mixture consistency. Subsequent benchmarks use OT-CFM with source prediction (§§3.2–3.3, 5.1).
- **Broader evaluation:** Signal reconstruction metrics are combined with paired perceptual distances, embedding-distribution distances, and listening tests to expose differences between reconstruction fidelity and perceptual similarity (§4.2).

The contribution is primarily the framework and systematic study for full-band stereo MSS; the underlying flow-matching formulations and embedding metrics are adopted from prior work. Code release is promised upon acceptance, rather than established as available (§1).

## Evidence and main results

All objective results below average across four stems on MUSDB18-HQ; lower Mel, FD, and LPAPS are better, while higher SDR is better.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Generative modeling improves spectral similarity but reduces SDR | FLOMSS-S versus same-backbone discriminative baseline: Mel **1.63 vs 2.30**, SDR **3.77 vs 4.95**, FD-VGG **0.87 vs 2.55**, LPAPS-CLAP **2.45 vs 2.58**. | Tables 1, 3; §5.1 |
| Prediction target changes the trade-off | OT-CFM velocity prediction versus source prediction: FD-CLAP **0.23 vs 0.31**, but SDR **3.23 vs 3.77**. | Table 1 |
| Joint modeling loses quality under the tested capacity setting | Unconstrained joint model versus separate source models: SDR **3.07 vs 3.77**, FD-VGG **2.30 vs 0.87**. Soft consistency raises joint-model SDR to **3.69**, while FD-VGG worsens to **2.87**. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| Scaling improves the benchmarked source-specific models | FLOMSS-M versus FLOMSS-S: Mel **1.55 vs 1.63**, SDR **4.34 vs 3.77**, FD-PaSST **205 vs 261**. Here M denotes the larger model, not joint-source modeling. | Tables 3–4; §§4.1, 5.4 |
| Strong discriminative methods retain substantially higher SDR | FLOMSS-M versus SCNet: Mel **1.55 vs 1.66**, FD-VGG **0.65 vs 1.62**, but SDR **4.34 vs 9.69** and LPAPS-CLAP **2.30 vs 2.24**. | Table 3 |
| Listening results support competitive perceived quality | With **23 participants** rating ten **8-second excerpts** on a **0–100** scale, FLOMSS-M scores **59.12**, versus SCNet **62.59**, HTDemucs **58.71**, and FLOMSS-S-Disc. **40.28**; it ranks second among systems evaluated on every stem. | §4.2; Table 4 |

These results establish a metric trade-off in the evaluated setting, supported by listening-test means. They do not establish universal perceptual superiority or statistically significant ranking differences. Comparisons with prior generative systems are also incomplete: SAM Audio and MGE-LDM lack Other-stem results, and baseline training data and native audio formats differ (§5.4; Table 3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** SDR remains weaker than strong discriminative methods; improving it is future work (§5.4).
- **Author-stated:** Hard mixture consistency degrades performance under the tested waveform/STFT paths. Auxiliary signal losses were omitted, and the authors explicitly limit this finding to their configurations (§5.3).
- **Author-stated:** Baselines use different training datasets and audio formats, complicating attribution of differences (§5.4; Table 3).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation on one dataset and a fixed stem taxonomy leaves generalization to other music distributions and source definitions unestablished (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Joint-source quality losses cannot be cleanly separated from capacity allocation: the joint model keeps roughly the same backbone size while separate modeling uses a model per stem (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** No confidence intervals, significance tests, or repeated-run variability are supplied. Distributional embedding distances compare sets rather than corresponding excerpts, so they alone cannot establish faithful reconstruction (§4.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can reconstruction improve without losing perceptual gains?** Intermediate predictions show competing metric trends (§5.2). Next experiment: compare stopping points and auxiliary reconstruction losses using both objective metrics and blinded listening.
- **Can hard consistency help with a different training design?** Current negative results omit auxiliary signal losses (§5.3). Next experiment: ablate flow paths and auxiliary losses under matched capacity.
- **Can joint modeling match separate models at comparable resources?** The current comparison changes capacity per stem (§5.3). Next experiment: compare joint and separate models at matched total parameters and measured inference cost.
- **Do gains generalize beyond MUSDB18-HQ?** No other evaluation dataset is reported (§4.1). Next experiment: evaluate unchanged models on independently collected mixtures with compatible stems.
