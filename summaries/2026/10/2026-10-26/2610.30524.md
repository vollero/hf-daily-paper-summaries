# LLM2Cert: LLM Synthesis of Certificates with SMT/Lean Verification

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30524) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30524)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that combining LLM certificate synthesis with SMT verification and Lean proof repair verifies 81/105 dynamical-system instances under unrelaxed conditions, compared with 63/105 for proposal without feedback (§5.3).

## Problem and motivation
Lyapunov functions and barrier certificates establish stability and safety through state-space inequalities, but finding and verifying them becomes difficult for nonlinear, high-dimensional systems. The authors argue that solver limitations have encouraged both relaxed verification conditions and benchmarks dominated by low-dimensional polynomial dynamics (§1).

The paper addresses both gaps: broader benchmark coverage and a verification pipeline that preserves the original mathematical obligations (§§3–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **DynaCertBench:** A benchmark of 105 instances built from 80 curated literature systems plus extensions and new systems, covering stability, region of attraction (ROA), forward invariance, and safety (§4; Abstract).
- **Hybrid certificate verification:** An LLM proposes a symbolic certificate and, when required, a feedback controller. After sampled screening, individual obligations go to SMT or Lean; SMT timeouts and inconclusive `δ-sat` results can escalate to Lean. Verification keeps each candidate’s obligations fixed (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Structured proof repair:** Lean compiler errors, remaining goals, and local lemma-search results guide proof revisions. Accepted proofs must pass compilation and an axiom audit (§3.3; Appendix D.2).

The authors claim the first end-to-end integration of Lean into Lyapunov and barrier certificate synthesis for continuous- and discrete-time systems; the supplied text alone cannot independently establish priority (§1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

These reported results support complementary SMT/Lean coverage and the usefulness of structured repair within the evaluated setup. The frozen-certificate analysis is conditioned on full-pipeline successes, so it does not establish backend performance across all benchmark instances. Acceptance combines SMT verdicts and Lean proofs; it does **not** mean every obligation receives a Lean-kernel-checked proof (§§2.2, 3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The benchmark is curated rather than exhaustive; broader high-dimensional, hybrid, and uncertain dynamics remain future work. The framework primarily addresses deterministic systems and does not optimize certified-region size (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Search and proof repair are budget-limited; unresolved outcomes can reflect exhausted search rather than a false property (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting frozen certificates from full-pipeline successes favors analysis of successfully discovered witnesses, limiting generalization of backend coverage claims (§5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The one-shot row is reconstructed from successful runs requiring no feedback, rather than clearly described as an independent experimental run (Table 2 note).
- **Analyst inference:** Observed routing and the composed SMT-first comparison use different trace constructions, so their small coverage difference does not cleanly isolate routing quality (Appendix E.3).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Appendix D.1.2 restricts new candidate generation to replayed counterexamples, while §3.4 and Algorithm 1 also describe regeneration after unresolved diagnostics; the operational rule is unclear.
- **Source limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, and many table bodies are absent; detailed results and implementation evidence cannot be fully assessed.

## Open research questions
1. **Does routing improve cost on unseen instances?** The available fallback comparison is composed from existing records. Run routing and SMT-first fallback prospectively on held-out systems with identical budgets (Appendix E.3).
2. **Can neural synthesis recover complementary failures?** Baselines solve systems left unresolved by LLM2Cert. Test neural candidate proposals inside the same strict verification loop (§5.2).
3. **Can the framework optimize certified-region size?** Current synthesis uses prescribed domains. Add a region-size objective and compare certified set size, verification success, and cost (Appendix A).
4. **How does verification extend to stochastic dynamics?** Probabilistic guarantees remain future work. Formalize suitable stochastic certificate obligations and evaluate them on disturbance-driven systems (Appendix A).
