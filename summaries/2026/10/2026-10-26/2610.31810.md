# PaperFormalizer: Author-in-the-Loop Autoformalization of Research Papers in Lean

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31810) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31810)


## One-sentence takeaway
PaperFormalizer combines agentic mathematical review, author-approved repairs, and Lean proof generation, with three author-owned case studies demonstrating useful corrections but no controlled evaluation of cost or comparative performance.

## Problem and motivation
Technical proofs can conceal missing assumptions, boundary cases, and invalid steps. Formal verification can expose these gaps, but manual formalization is costly, and proving a formal statement does not establish that it matches the author’s intended claim. The authors identify ambiguity resolution and choosing appropriate repairs as decisions requiring author participation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Review before formalization:** Agents convert LaTeX into a statement dependency graph, audit faithfulness, ambiguity, and correctness, and propose independently reviewed repairs. Formalization then proceeds through planning, scaffolding, proof filling, and statement linking (§2).
- **Author-controlled interpretation and repair:** A local interface presents clarifications and changes in mathematical language. Substantive changes to main results require author decisions; other changes remain reviewable (§§2–3).
- **Reusable verification artifacts:** The system produces revised and annotated papers, a change report, and linked Lean declarations. An author-owned toolkit and optional public sharing support reuse across papers (§§3–4).
- **Case-study demonstrations:** The authors report applications to extreme-event treatment effects, optimal transport, and neural causal models (§5). The contribution is the integrated workflow; the paper does not experimentally isolate the benefit of its components.

These are author-reported demonstrations of finding errors and formalizing revised mathematics. They do not establish error-detection recall, preservation of intended meaning across arbitrary papers, or advantages over competing workflows. The neural causal-model paper’s remaining seven results are not individually accounted for in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated interpretation risk:** Agents can misread papers, and automatically selected repairs may differ from author intent. Fully automatic mode is recommended only as a first pass (§§3, 6).
- **Conditional verification scope:** Some cited results remain explicit assumptions because formalizing them can be costly. The extreme-event analysis specifically assumes a cited angular Wasserstein rate (§2, Stage 3; §5).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evaluation covers three papers written by the system’s authors, with their libraries and personal toolkit. Performance for independent users and unfamiliar fields remains unestablished (§5).
- **Analyst inference—unmeasured component benefits:** No controlled baselines or ablations quantify the effects of author participation, separate reviewers, mixed model families, or toolkit reuse.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The introduction says 15 of 29 results in another paper needed revision, while §5 reports 20 proved and two unrepaired refutations. These counts describe different outcomes, but their relationship is not explained.
- Runtime, monetary cost, author effort, repeated-run variability, and detailed execution versions are not reported.

## Open research questions
1. **How much does author participation improve semantic fidelity?** The paper motivates it but provides no controlled comparison. Compare author-mediated and automatic runs on the same papers, with blinded author assessment of interpretations and repairs.
2. **How reliably does the system detect errors?** Case studies show discoveries without measuring missed errors or false findings. Evaluate on independently curated papers with adjudicated errors and valid controls.
3. **Does toolkit reuse reduce verification effort?** Reuse is described and demonstrated structurally, but savings are unquantified. Formalize related papers with and without the accumulated toolkit, measuring completion, cost, and author time.
4. **How well does the workflow transfer to independent authors?** All reported applications are author-owned. Test unfamiliar papers across fields under fixed resource budgets and document unresolved statements and required interventions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The workflow and detailed case-study findings are clearly described, but broad effectiveness remains weakly evaluated.

Material missing information includes controlled comparisons, error-detection metrics, runtime and cost, author workload, repeated-run results, precise model/library versions, and a complete outcome breakdown for the neural causal-model paper.
