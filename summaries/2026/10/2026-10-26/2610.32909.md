# HoloBoxing: A Simple Recipe for Humanoid Boxing with 7 Minutes of Single-GPU Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32909) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32909)


## One-sentence takeaway
HoloBoxing achieves simulated humanoid boxing with 7.0 minutes of accounted additional training above a frozen motor controller, ranking second in a six-policy league, while leaving total development cost and component-specific speedups unestablished (§§5.2–5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Humanoid boxing requires selecting movements in response to an opponent while maintaining balance through contact. Learning these decisions directly through joint commands couples task learning with motor execution. The authors investigate how little additional training is needed when a capable whole-body controller already exists (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is an integrated adaptation recipe and its evaluation, rather than newly introducing controller reuse, latent actions, imitation, or PPO (§2).

- **Compact target interface:** A learned decoder maps normalized 32-dimensional actions into 225 position and orientation coordinates covering five body points at five future times; a frozen HoloMotion v2 controller executes these requests (§§3–3.1).
- **Measured-state initialization:** Reference tracking supplies actual robot observations paired with future reference targets expressed in the measured robot frame. Imitation updates the planner through the frozen decoder (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Short task refinement and diagnostics:** Standing warmup and interaction PPO refine the planner while preserving the decoder and controller. Timing, component removals, observation studies, and physical tracking checks assess the combined recipe (§§3.3, 5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Minute-scale additional training | 422.2 seconds: 375.0 seconds for a new RTX 4090 process plus 47.2 seconds of previously recorded representation fitting; excludes inherited controller/opponent training, preparation, evaluation, and search. | §5.2; Table 1 caption and accompanying text |
| Competitive behavior within the tested pool | Primary version ranks second among six policies across 150 simulated bouts; wins 7/10 against GGSD and 4/10 against RoboStriker. Four primary-version wins contain no scored hand contact. | §5.4; Figure 2; text accompanying Tables 3–4 |
| Longer training path improves league outcomes | The 21.4-minute version ranks first and wins 8/10 against the primary version; hardware, training path, and training opponent differ. | §§5.2, 5.4–5.5 |
| Combined recipe outperforms component removals | Primary version ranks above both removals and produces more scored contacts at the same 491,520 learner transitions, using one training seed; exact outcome totals are absent from the retrieved table content. | §5.5; Appendix A |
| Current-state observations support imitation | Across eight observation choices with three seeds each, current state plus opponent information has the lowest mean supervised validation loss, 16.9% below ten-frame history plus opponent input. | §5.6; Table 6 accompanying text |
| Compression preserves measured tracking success | Selected decoded and uncompressed targets each complete 80/81 predefined validation windows, failing the same source window. | §5.6; Appendix C.2 |

These results support the authors’ claim that the combined recipe produces scored boxing interaction after short additional training. They do not establish an end-to-end seven-minute development process, a controlled speedup over other methods, reliable performance across training seeds, or superior offensive skill. The long-version comparison also cannot isolate the effect of extra updates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** Small motion corpus, one selected task-training seed, simulation-only evaluation, and untested alternative lower controllers constrain generalization (§6).
- **Accounting boundary:** Controller pretraining, the standing opponent’s original training, preparation, evaluation, and search are excluded; representation fitting comes from an earlier run (§§5.2, 6).
- **Confounded component comparisons:** Removing the decoder changes action dimension and exploration geometry. Matched learner transitions do not isolate component effects on convergence time (§5.5).
- **League comparability:** Policies retain different observations and auxiliary inputs; external checkpoints receive no destination fine-tuning. Wins can result from opponent falls (§§4, 6).
- **Representation coverage:** Validation windows are correlated within motions. A seated test clip begins below the upright failure gate, preventing a meaningful execution comparison for that motion (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Missing numerical table bodies limit independent assessment of outcome margins and diagnostic variability, despite useful results being stated in prose.

## Open research questions
- **Do imitation and the decoder reliably reduce time to useful boxing?** Single-seed, fixed-budget controls do not establish learning-speed effects. Run multiple seeds with matched hardware and report performance-versus-time curves (§§5.5, 6).
- **Does the planner generalize beyond the fixed training opponent and reset coverage?** The authors identify broader coverage as future work. Evaluate unseen opponents and systematically varied initial states (§6).
- **How dependent is the recipe on HoloMotion v2?** Alternative controllers remain untested. Repeat adaptation with another frozen controller under the same evaluation protocol (§6).
- **Can target compression preserve non-upright behavior?** The seated-motion height gate leaves this unresolved. Compare decoded and original seated targets using motion-appropriate execution criteria (Appendix C.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose clearly supports the method, timing boundary, and main outcomes, but numerical bodies for Tables 1–6 and appendix tables are unavailable.

Material missing information includes exact Elo/contact/fall totals, most diagnostic losses and variability, complete tabulated implementation settings, and confirmed availability of HoloBoxing code, checkpoints, and replay assets. Independent task-training seeds and real-robot results are not provided.
