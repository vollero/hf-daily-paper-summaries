# RoboJudge: Multimodal Language Models as Judges for Embodied Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27782) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27782)


## One-sentence takeaway

RoboJudge introduces embodied-video judge benchmarks and training data, producing a specialized judge with reported pooled Pearson agreement of 0.719 with human ratings, although downstream planning benefits remain untested (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation

Generated embodied rollouts must obey plausible physics and complete the requested task. Reference similarity can obscure these distinct requirements, while existing multimodal judges have not been systematically calibrated for manipulation errors such as invalid contact, disappearing objects, or incorrect final states. The authors therefore evaluate the reliability of the **judges**, rather than only the video generators (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **RoboJudge-Bench:** 800 generated videos from eight source corpora and ten generators, covering grippers, dexterous hands, and human hands. Expert-adjudicated human ratings separately measure Physical Adherence (PA) and Instruction Alignment (IA) on a five-point scale (§3).
- **Judge reliability study:** Comparison with 29 model judges and eight non-MLLM metrics, including qualitative analysis of missed physical and task-completion failures (§§5.1–5.3; Figure 4).
- **Training data and specialized judge:** RoboJudge-Train contains 12,351 videos and 24,702 axis-level annotations. Humans revise model-proposed labels; GPT-5.5 structures their evidence notes into rationales. RoboJudge-9B starts from Qwen3.5-9B, receives supervised fine-tuning, then GRPO with binary rewards for exact score agreement (§4).
- **Transfer and downstream evaluation:** External benchmark tests and a generator leaderboard demonstrate additional evaluation uses; online rollout selection is proposed rather than tested (§§5.5–5.6).

The evidence supports improved agreement with the benchmark’s adjudicated human scores and some external transfer. It does not establish objective physical correctness, universally reliable individual judgments, or improved robot planning. Statistical superiority is explicitly supported for overall agreement and IA; the text reports only the highest observed PA correlation.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied paper has no explicit limitations section. Relevant boundaries and **analyst inferences** are:

- **Human-reference uncertainty:** Calibration-round exact agreement reaches 52.9%, despite 92.1% agreement within one point. Expert adjudication strengthens the reference, but final production inter-annotator agreement is not reported in the supplied text (Appendix A.1).
- **Potential annotation anchoring — analyst inference:** Human review reduces reliance on raw model labels, but the training pipeline begins with model-proposed scores and uses model-written rationales. No comparison against fully independent human annotation is reported (§4.1).
- **Comparison mismatch — analyst inference:** Specialized judges retain native prompts and mapped scoring dimensions; general and embodied models receive a unified rubric. Performance differences therefore need not isolate model capability alone (Appendix B).
- **Limited generalization evidence:** Source clips are disjoint, but training and testing share a generation pipeline and overlapping generators. No generator-held-out or corpus-held-out study is reported (§§3.3–4.1).
- **Sampling and downstream validity:** General/embodied baselines use at most 32 frames at 4 FPS. Sampling ablations cover GPT-5.5 and the base Qwen model, not the final judge; online planning applications remain proposals (Appendix B.1; §§5.4, 5.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Does agreement persist for unseen generators and corpora?** Clip separation does not establish this. A next experiment could hold out entire generators and source corpora, with fresh human adjudication.
2. **How much do model-assisted rationales and label proposals contribute?** SFT/RL ablations do not isolate annotation components. Compare independently human-labeled supervision, verified model proposals, and training with versus without rationales.
3. **Can the judge improve embodied planning?** The paper proposes rollout selection but evaluates offline scoring. Test judge-guided rollout selection against random selection and existing judges, measuring real task success.
4. **Does sparse sampling miss decisive interaction failures?** The final judge lacks a reported sampling ablation. Evaluate it across frame budgets on human-annotated brief-contact and final-state errors.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and principal results are explicit, but the retrieved text omits table bodies and numerical figure contents. Material gaps include complete baseline scores, confidence intervals, external benchmark protocols and PC/SA definitions, production annotation agreement, detailed subgroup results, and confirmed code/data/checkpoint release locations.
