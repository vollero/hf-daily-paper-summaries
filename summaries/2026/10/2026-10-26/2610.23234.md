# MultiAgent-OPD: End-to-End Training of Hierarchical Multi-Agent Systems with On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23234) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23234)


## One-sentence takeaway
MultiAgent-OPD combines teacher-guided Searcher distillation with outcome-based Leader and Answerer training, achieving the highest reported seven-dataset QA accuracy among the evaluated systems while requiring more retrieval and input processing (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Final-answer rewards provide weak credit assignment for hierarchical agent teams: a Searcher can retrieve useful evidence that later agents fail to use, and group-relative rewards provide no signal when all sampled workflows have the same outcome. Prompted judges provide local feedback, but their assessments can be unreliable and report-level scores offer limited guidance for individual search decisions (§§1, 3.3).

The authors address this gap by supplying dense supervision for Searchers while retaining outcome rewards for coordination and answering.

## Approach and contributions
1. **Task-trained Teacher.** Stage I jointly trains a cascaded Searcher–Answerer workflow using answer correctness plus a small Searcher behavior bonus. The resulting policy becomes a frozen Teacher (§3.2).
2. **On-policy supervision for delegated search.** During hierarchical training, the Teacher scores student-generated query and summary tokens using the same subtask, retrieved passages, and prefix. Teacher–student log-probability differences supply token-level advantages through sampled reverse-KL distillation (§3.3, Eqs. 3–5).
3. **Joint training with role-specific signals.** A shared student model plays Leader, Searcher, and Answerer. Searchers receive distillation advantages; the Leader receives team-success rewards plus a search-activity bonus; Answerer candidates receive individual correctness rewards. Only the student is needed at inference (§3.3, Eqs. 6–7).

The authors’ claimed methodological distinction is applying a task-trained Teacher to delegated subtasks within a jointly optimized hierarchy, rather than introducing on-policy distillation itself (§2).

## Evidence and main results
All main accuracy results use the same backbone and retriever, with four independent workflows per question on a fixed 5,000-question set. Macro scores weight datasets equally; avg@4 averages correctness across attempts, whereas pass@4 measures at-least-once success (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported average accuracy | MultiAgent-OPD: **38.68% macro avg@4**, versus Prompted Critic’s 34.62% and C-MAS + RL’s 36.07%; gains of 4.06 and 2.61 percentage points. Authors report positive paired 95% intervals for both differences. | §4.1; Tables 1–2 |
| Lead over strongest included search agent | Tree-GRPO achieves 37.37% macro avg@4, versus 38.68% for MultiAgent-OPD. This comparison has no paired interval. | §4.1; Appendix A.2 |
| Greater coverage and repeated success | Macro pass@4: 48.73%, versus 45.97% for C-MAS + RL and 45.96% for Prompted Critic. Correct on all four attempts: 29.27%, versus 27.05% and 23.24%, respectively. | §4.4; Table 4 |
| Task-trained Teacher and direct Searcher losses matter in diagnostics | At step 500, macro avg@4 is 38.68% for the full method, 29.50% with a base-model Teacher, and 29.82% without direct Searcher supervision; training batches differ. | §4.2; Table 3 |
| Outcome-only Searcher variant loses retrieval | Step-500 variant achieves 17.78% macro avg@4 and records no search actions across 20,000 test workflows, although 99.03% produce final answers. | §4.2; Appendix A.5 |
| Accuracy gains require greater workload | Dataset-macro searches/input tokens per workflow: 5.39/9,172 for MultiAgent-OPD; 4.45/6,786 for Prompted Critic; 2.86/3,886 for C-MAS + RL. | §4.5; Figure 4 |

These results support improved correctness for the evaluated checkpoints and settings. They do not establish gains under equal retrieval or token budgets, robustness across training seeds, or causal credit assignment. Pass@4 assumes access to a successful attempt and supplies no answer-selection procedure (§4; Limitations).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** Evaluation covers one backbone and fixed English QA benchmarks. Exact match misses semantic equivalence and does not detect pretraining contamination (Limitations).
- **Authors’ uncertainty limits:** Question-bootstrap intervals characterize evaluated checkpoints, not independently trained runs. External search-agent comparisons have aggregate scores only (Appendix A.2).
- **Authors’ mechanism limits:** Teacher preferences are not causal token contributions. Teacher errors, full-question-to-subtask transfer, coupled role updates, and reduced exploration under reverse KL remain concerns. Leader and Answerer objectives are not separately ablated (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** Differing workflow budgets and greater retrieval prevent attributing the entire accuracy gain to better supervision at equal resource use. Diagnostic batch differences also limit clean causal interpretation (§§4.2, 4.5).
- **Reproducibility boundary:** The authors describe an artifact for reproducing summary statistics; the supplied text does not establish release of trained weights or complete training code. Table bodies and prompt templates are absent from the retrieved text.

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains persist at equal cost?** No matched retrieval/token-budget comparison is reported. Evaluate all systems across identical budget caps and compare accuracy–workload curves.
2. **How stable are training and retrieval behavior?** Reported uncertainty excludes training-seed variation. Repeat full training and the outcome-only Searcher variant across seeds with controlled batches.
3. **Does subtask-specific Teacher training improve supervision?** The Teacher learns on full questions but scores delegated subtasks. Compare full-question, subtask-trained, and mixed Teachers while holding student training fixed.
4. **Are correct answers grounded in retrieved evidence?** Exact match does not evaluate evidence support or attribution. Add blinded evidence-support assessments and controlled removal of relevant passages (Ethical Considerations).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method and headline results, but complete numerical tables, interval endpoints, prompt contents, and example trajectories are missing. Training compute, seed-level variability, matched-budget results, and model/training-code release details are also unavailable or not established.
