# WORTH Benchmark: Evidence Discovery under Not-Fully-Cooperative Information -- A Two-Episode Pilot

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27597) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27597)


## One-sentence takeaway
WORTH’s two-story pilot reveals mismatches between models’ selected evidence and participant judgments, but does not establish whether narrative misdirection causes those errors or whether the patterns generalize (§§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that reasoning evaluations often supply organized premises, leaving evidence discovery largely untested. WORTH asks whether models can identify useful evidence when truthful information is presented through narratives that direct attention toward suspects while obscuring counter-evidence (§1).

The identified gap is the **combination** of autonomous evidence selection, professionally designed narrative obfuscation, and separate scoring of evidence and conclusions; the authors acknowledge that evidence selection and mystery-based evaluation individually have precedents (§§2.2–2.4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation design:** Models read complete, numbered Chinese mystery narratives, select a limited number of clue sentences, identify participants, and explain their reasoning. Clue overlap with the author’s designated set \(K\) and participant selections are scored separately; explanations support diagnosis but receive no independent score (§§3.1, 4.3–4.5).
- **Authored materials:** Reasoning hubs are designated during story design, then embedded through narrative techniques intended to reduce their salience. Originality improves provenance tracking but does not guarantee absence of prior model exposure (§§3.2–3.3, 4.2).
- **Pilot findings:** The study documents evidence–judgment mismatches, shared incorrect participant selections, and unequal selection of suspicion-oriented and exonerating information. These are descriptive behavioral findings, not isolated internal mechanisms (§5).

Only the full-story condition was implemented; evidence cueing and evidence modification remain proposed controls (§3.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot scope and exclusions | EP01: 6 models × 5 runs, all 30 content-scorable; EP02: 8 models × 5 runs, 37/40 content-scorable. Exclusions were not scored as zero. | §4.7; Appendix A.4 |
| More clue hits did not imply better participant selection | In EP01, across five runs each, GPT-5.6 Sol averaged 3.4/12 clue hits and 0.8/3 correct participants; DeepSeek-V4-Flash averaged 2.2/12 and 1.6/3, respectively, with zero incorrect selections. | §5.1.1 |
| Shared incorrect selections | In EP02, 31/37 scorable runs (83.8%) included all three distractors Father, Jia Changgeng, and Director Huang; 16/37 (43.2%) selected exactly that trio. | §5.2.1 |
| Adjacent evidence had unequal selection rates | EP02 suspicion-oriented S113 was selected in 25/37 runs (67.6%); adjacent exoneration-oriented S112 was selected in 0/37. | §5.2.2 |
| Scoring omitted information models consistently considered useful | All 37 EP02 scorable runs selected S124, a puzzle premise outside \(K\), earning no clue points for it. | §5.2.4 |
| Participant errors outweighed clue rewards | EP02 produced negative gameplay-equivalent totals in 28/37 runs (75.7%); the highest run scored 16/47. Scores incorporate character-specific penalties. | §5.2.6 |
| Participant definitions may affect apparent errors | EP01’s correct participant “I” was selected in 0/30 runs, although some explanations recognized the narrator’s causal involvement. | §§5.1.2, 6.5 |

The evidence supports reporting evidence selection and participant judgment separately and identifies concentrated errors under these particular conditions. It does not establish stable model rankings, causal effects of salience, general epistemic-vigilance deficits, or human–model differences. Missing a sentence in \(K\) also does not establish that its information was unused (§§6–7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Narrow evaluation:** Two stories and five runs per model per episode cannot establish general capability rankings or reliable cross-domain tendencies (§7.1).
- **Missing controls:** No evidence-cueing, evidence-modification, salience-rewrite, or scoring-disclosure experiment was completed. Model behavior and story-design effects remain unresolved (§§3.4, 6.2, 6.6).
- **Reference-set validity:** \(K\) measures author-intended reasoning hubs, not all valid evidence. Adjacent selections and S124 demonstrate why clue overlap is an incomplete measure of reasoning (§§4.2, 6.3, 7.2).
- **Ambiguous target:** “Participant” can differ by intent versus causal contribution; EP02 also leaves the target homicide insufficiently delimited. Some attribution errors may therefore reflect interpretation differences (§§4.9, 6.5).
- **Comparability and reproducibility:** Product interfaces and tiers differ, some settings are incompletely recorded, and versions change across episodes. Public-release timing prevents ruling out prior exposure (§4.6; Appendix A.3).
- **Incomplete validation:** Explanations lack independent scoring and inter-rater reliability assessment; human records remain uncleaned and unreported (§§6.7, 7.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Because sentence and participant selections are submitted together, selected clues cannot be assumed to have causally driven the conclusion; the paper explicitly recognizes this issue for S081 (§5.2.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does reducing narrative salience change the shared errors?** Current observations cannot separate model tendencies from unusually strong distractors. Next experiment: rewrite suspicion emphasis while holding case facts fixed (§6.2).
- **Does explicit evidence access improve attribution?** Cueing was proposed but not tested. Next experiment: compare matched full-story and highlighted-\(K\) conditions, recording both correct and incorrect participant selections (§§3.4, 7.3).
- **How much does sentence-overlap scoring miss?** S124 and neighboring selections expose reference-set boundaries. Next experiment: independently code explanations and alternative evidence selections for inferential function, with inter-rater agreement (§7.2).
- **How much error comes from participant semantics?** Some models acknowledged causal involvement without selecting the narrator. Next experiment: explicitly distinguish knowing participation from unwitting facilitation and specify the target deaths (§§4.9, 6.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive summary; **low** in causal or general conclusions.
