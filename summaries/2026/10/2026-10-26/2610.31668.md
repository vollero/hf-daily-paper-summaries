# Reading, Writing, Sharing, and Interacting with the World through Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31668) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31668)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey organizes video understanding, generation, unification, and interaction around latent world state, arguing that convincing video alone does not establish persistent, controllable, or physically consistent world modeling (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Video models increasingly connect perception, synthesis, and action, but these research areas use different objectives and evaluation criteria. The authors identify a missing unified account of how video observations relate to evolving world states (§2).

Their central distinction matters because video is a partial and lossy observation: smooth frames or accurate answers need not demonstrate correct underlying dynamics, memory, or causal reasoning (§§3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions
This is a **survey and conceptual framework**, rather than a new trained model.

- **Four-operation framework:** reading infers world properties; writing generates observations from conditions; sharing couples understanding and generation; interacting predicts and updates state under actions or conditions (§3.3).
- **Consistency distinction:** temporal coherence concerns local agreement between frames; world consistency requires compatibility with an admissible trajectory under specified dynamics and constraints (§3.2).
- **World-model taxonomy:** prediction space—observation space or latent state—is separated from transition interface—passive, action-conditioned, or interactive (§4.4).
- **Literature synthesis and resources:** the authors claim a categorized review of methods, datasets, benchmarks, and recurring challenges, plus public reading lists, taxonomy, code, and data links (§2). The supplied excerpt supports only partial assessment of that coverage.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Coverage limitation:** the source is truncated during §6.1.1 and resumes in the references. Most substantive material on generation, unified models, interaction, and future directions is unavailable.
- **Author-stated conceptual limits:** represented state can be incomplete or inaccurate; reading and writing are not exact inverses; world consistency depends on specified constraints and unobserved sensing and dynamics (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Author-stated scope:** models with primarily explicit 3D persistent state and purely symbolic or text-based world models are excluded; reinforcement-learning world models qualify only when state is learned from video (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** survey-selection reproducibility cannot be assessed from the excerpt. It describes eligibility broadly but supplies no search procedure or screening counts (§2).
- **Analyst inference:** several tables appear only as captions, limiting inspection of benchmark coverage, architectural comparisons, and the evidence ladder. Resource availability is claimed but cannot be verified from the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **How should world consistency be measured under partial observability?** §3.2 defines a target rather than an executable metric. A next experiment could use controlled scenes with known states, occlusions, and revisits to compare visual coherence with state-constraint violations.
- **When does understanding-generation sharing produce measurable synergy?** §3.3 requires matched-resource gains but supplies no such comparison here. A next experiment could compare shared and separate models under matched data and compute on both understanding and generation tasks.
- **Can alignment preserve physical state information?** §5.2.1 reports a matching–regression trade-off without detailed results. A next experiment could compare contrastive and predictive objectives using both retrieval and frozen-feature physical regression.
- **Which evidence-acquisition policies improve reliability within a fixed budget?** §5.4 describes hypothesis-driven search errors and noisy tool outputs. A next experiment could compare observation policies under fixed tool-call and latency budgets, measuring answer support and repeated-run variance.
