# Predictive Closure: A Set-Level Characterization of JEPA&#x27;s Feature Selection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27364) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27364)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under explicit distributional assumptions, JEPA favors jointly predictive feature sets, and controlled experiments show that even an unpredictable feature can become better represented when it helps predict other retained features (§3; §5.1).

## Problem and motivation
JEPA learns both its prediction targets and its representations, leaving unclear which observation features survive a capacity bottleneck. Missing features cannot support downstream decisions, even when latent predictions are accurate. The authors identify a gap in per-feature accounts: a feature’s own predictability overlooks its contribution to predicting other features (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Set-level characterization:** Predictive closure selects a feasible feature set maximizing mutual information between its present and future values. The exact equivalence to squared-error minimization requires the assumptions in Appendix A, including matched latent distributions and isotropic Gaussian conditional prediction errors (§3.1; Appendices A.1–A.3).
- **Interpretive decomposition:** The objective balances irreducible future uncertainty against “predictive leakage”—predictive information available in the observation but absent from the selected set. Independent feature evolution recovers additive selection; ranking additionally requires equal feature entropy (§3.2; Appendices A.4–A.5).
- **Controlled tests and steering:** Synthetic image sequences test unpredictable-feature retention, competition between sets, capacity dependence, and indirect steering through reconstruction or predictor side information (§§5–6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Unpredictable features can benefit from predictive usefulness | Light decoding improves as its control over image replacement increases, while its future remains independently sampled and each image’s marginal persistence stays fixed. | §5.1; Fig. 2; Appendix B.2 |
| Changing one set can displace an unchanged competitor | Strengthening the C→D copying relationship shifts image-detection accuracy toward C/D and away from A/B. The endpoint preference switches in all three runs at every tested regularization weight. | §5.2; Fig. 3; Appendix D, Fig. 8 |
| Larger predictive groups become less decodable | For cycle sizes \(N\in\{2,4,8,16\}\), competing-group mean decoding accuracy decreases at every size increase in all three main-setting seeds; at \(N=16\), A/B is better decoded in all three. Average copying probabilities remain fixed. | §5.3; Fig. 4; Appendix E |
| Reconstruction can steer an untargeted partner | Reconstructing C alone can improve D decoding and weaken A/B; results use one optimization seed per condition and are not uniformly monotonic. | §6.1; Fig. 5 |
| Side information produces mixed redistribution | Supplying C improves D decoding for \(p_D\geq0.75\), but reduces it at \(p_D=0.60\). A improves while B generally weakens, a separation the characterization does not explain. | §6.2; Fig. 6 |
| Predictors learn transition-specific information | On 5,000 held-out transitions per system, actual-next versus incompatible-alternative accuracy is reported above 50% chance; shuffled predictions remain near chance. Exact table values are absent from the supplied text. | Appendix C; Table 1 caption |

These results support the predicted directions of several effects in small synthetic systems. They do not establish effect sizes in natural-data JEPA models or downstream planning benefits. The authors’ exact information-theoretic equivalence is conditional: without the Gaussian assumption, prediction-loss minimization instead maximizes a mutual-information lower bound (Appendix A.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope:** Experiments test directional predictions with a three-dimensional latent; effect sizes in large models on natural data remain untested (§5; §7).
- **Theoretical assumptions:** Exact distribution matching, finite precision, representable conditional means, Gaussian errors, and ideal optimization restrict the characterization’s guarantees (Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Rendering confound:** Group-size changes also alter tile scale and layout, so capacity cost is not isolated from extraction difficulty (Appendix B.3).
- **Steering uncertainty:** Reconstruction uses one seed and weights selected through a preliminary two-target search. Side information adds encoder capacity, and its A/B divergence is unexplained (§§6.1–6.2; Appendix B.4).
- **Readout limits:** Image-detection probes do not establish position binding; low main-latent decoding does not establish information absence from combined main and side inputs (Appendix B.3; §6.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Optimization-seed replication gives limited evidence about data-generation variability because the group-size runs share image pools and generation randomness (Appendix B.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Do the effects persist at practical scale?** Natural-data effect sizes are explicitly unresolved (§7). Next experiment: repeat predictive-relevance interventions across latent sizes in a larger video JEPA.
2. **How robust is the theory outside its error assumptions?** Exact equivalence depends on Gaussian conditional errors (Appendix A.2). Next experiment: measure residual distributions and compare prediction-loss rankings with estimated predictive information.
3. **Does group size matter independently of visual extraction difficulty?** Rendering changes remain confounded with feature count (Appendix B.3). Next experiment: vary cycle size using fixed-format symbolic observations.
4. **What explains asymmetric side-information effects?** Predictive closure does not explain A/B separating (§6.2). Next experiment: compare informative and uninformative side inputs with matched encoder capacity, probing both separate and combined representations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported qualitative findings and their stated scope; quantitative effect sizes cannot be assessed fully.

Material missing information includes numerical decoding values underlying the figures, Table 1’s actual results, direct checks of the theoretical distributional assumptions, and evaluations on natural data, genuine action conditioning, or downstream tasks.
