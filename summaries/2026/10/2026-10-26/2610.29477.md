# TenetBench: A Clause-Anchored Russian Corpus for Evaluating Language Models against Professional Codes of Ethics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29477) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29477)


## One-sentence takeaway

TenetBench pairs clause-conflicting professional requests with benign controls and finds frequent violations across fourteen model configurations, while its own validation shows that the ranking primarily measures selective refusal rather than broad professional competence (§§6–7).

## Problem and motivation

General safety evaluations do not identify whether an assistant’s professional drafting breaches a specific provision of an occupational code. Such requests can appear routine despite asking for text that violates confidentiality, disclosure, or procedural duties (§1).

The authors target an evaluation gap: verdicts tied to named clauses, paired with legitimate requests to detect indiscriminate refusal. They acknowledge that clause anchoring, norm-based task generation, and benign controls individually precede this work (§2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Clause-anchored paired corpus:** The pipeline extracts eligible clauses, generates professional scenarios, and creates conflicting requests with benign twins sharing the scenario substrate. The full corpus contains 3,433 pairs across fifteen Russian-language professional codes; 2,235 pairs are released (§§3, 7.6).
- **Auditable evaluation procedure:** A frozen model judge independently labels instrumental action, norm violation, and refusal type. A deterministic function converts these labels into outcomes; warnings never cancel a violation. Human validation follows the freeze (§§4–5).
- **Model diagnostics:** Fourteen model–reasoning configurations receive the same evaluation tasks, with additional paired experiments on reasoning and framing (§§4.4, 6).
- **Implemented language transfer:** The pipeline processes two English-language codes from other jurisdictions, with separately validated judging (§7.4).

The distinctive contribution is this combination of clause anchoring, shared-scenario benign controls, independently defined judgment axes, and validation—not the invention of each component (§2.3).

These results support a diagnostic finding about the generated tasks and evaluated configurations. Agreement supports consistency with adjudicated specialists, not objective correctness; clause substitution supports sensitivity, not correct interpretation. The authors explicitly narrow the ranking’s meaning to domain-scoped caution, and the benchmark does not grade answer usefulness (§§5, 7.1, 7.5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Shared construction cues:** Judge and human annotators both see fields describing the intended ethical tension and violation mechanism. Removing them shifts net violation judgments by 0.060 across 700 paired observations, exceeding the declared 0.020 bound; agreement therefore cannot exclude shared cue dependence (§§4.2, 7.3).
- **Imperfect benign controls:** Twins are not semantically checked against their clauses. Some invite violating answers, so the authors argue that reported over-refusal can be inflated and paired discrimination depressed (§7.5).
- **Uneven validation coverage:** Only 24 benign tasks overlap between human validation and the completed set of 279 matched twins; the subsequently added twins are unannotated (§5.1).
- **Weak evidence traceability:** Under the declared verbatim rule, cited evidence is locatable in only 0.623 of prohibition-clause violation verdicts; permissive matching raises this to 0.937 (§7.2).
- **Serving and truncation confounds:** A truncation audit pushes one row above the unresolved-output blocking threshold. The route comparison cannot exclude effects large enough to reorder models (§§6.6, 7.5).
- **Narrow construct and reproducibility:** Generated Russian text from one legal system excludes duties breached only by later actions. Evaluation omits one corpus harm domain, uses one response per task, and lacks the subject-model runtime version. Partial release prevents complete independent reconstruction (§§7.5–7.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Stronger selective refusal should not be interpreted as better overall professional assistance, because useful completion quality is unscored and some “compliant non-violation” outcomes contain no performed action (§§4.1, 7.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does agreement survive removal of construction cues?** Both instruments saw those cues, leaving shared bias unresolved. A next experiment could collect fresh, blinded specialist labels without them and compare both judge configurations (§7.3).
2. **How clean are the benign controls?** Construction does not guarantee clause compatibility. Specialists could audit every evaluation twin before rerunning refusal and discrimination metrics (§7.5).
3. **Would rankings survive controlled serving conditions?** Existing route bounds permit consequential effects. Repeat the same weights, modes, and tasks across routes with matched output budgets and repeated response collection (§7.5).
4. **Can models provide useful compliant alternatives?** Current scores primarily reward refusal and do not grade usefulness. Add specialist ratings of task utility and clause compliance for alternative completions (§§7.1, 7.5).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives extensive methods, results, and disclosed failures, but supports narrower conclusions than broad professional-code conformance.

Material information unavailable here includes the actual cells of Tables 1–5, the full profession/code inventory, complete model configurations and prompts, detailed English-arm sizes and code identities, and Online Resource 1. The full unreleased corpus and validation of the newly added benign twins are also unavailable.
