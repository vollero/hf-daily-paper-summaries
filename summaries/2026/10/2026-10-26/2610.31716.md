# Does LeWorldModel Preserve Local Action Geometry?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31716) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31716)


## One-sentence takeaway
Pretrained LeWorldModel partially captures local action sensitivity on Reacher and OGBench Cube, but its directional alignment and response magnitudes depend strongly on perturbation scale, with benefits for control still untested (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Accurate prediction for a sampled action does not establish that a world model correctly represents the relative effects of nearby actions. The authors investigate this gap because action sensitivity may matter for planning, while explicitly separating their diagnostic from evidence of successful action selection or control (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Matched finite-scale diagnostic:** Compare simulator and pretrained model responses using a shared frozen encoder. Central finite differences estimate action-response matrices, whose Gram matrices describe response magnitudes across action directions. Trace normalization separates relative geometry from overall gain (§2).
- **Calibration and robustness analysis:** Compare geometry against isotropic, randomly rotated, and shuffled-state references; examine perturbation scales, eigenspace stability, numerical floors, and replay consistency (§2; Appendices A, C).
- **Held-out validation across output metrics:** Test whether coordinate-derived Grams predict response magnitudes along unused directions, and compare physical-pose, pixel, and embedding geometry (§3; Appendix D).

This is an evaluation of existing checkpoints without training, rather than a new world-model architecture (§2).

## Evidence and main results

All primary-scale results below use \(\epsilon=10^{-3}\), with 458 retained Reacher anchors and 500 Cube anchors (§2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitive directions remain misaligned | Median dominant-direction angles are approximately 36° on Reacher and 42° on Cube. Median normalized Gram discrepancy \(D_G\) is 0.465 [95% episode-bootstrap interval: 0.423–0.500] and 0.508 [0.487–0.529], respectively. | §3 |
| State matching helps, but baseline superiority varies | Paired median model-minus-shuffle \(D_G\): −0.047 on Reacher [−0.071, −0.014] and −0.059 on Cube [−0.074, −0.029]. Cube beats all three references; Reacher beats rotation and shuffle, but isotropy is closer. | §3; Appendix C |
| The model underestimates sensitivity strength | Median model/environment gain ratio, \(\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}G_{\rm model}/\operatorname{tr}G_{\rm env}}\), is approximately four-fifths on Reacher and one-half on Cube. | §3 |
| Geometry agreement changes substantially with scale | Raising \(\epsilon\) from \(10^{-3}\) to \(10^{-2}\) reduces median model/environment \(D_G\) by approximately 70% on Reacher and 47% on Cube. | §3 |
| Output metric strongly affects scale dependence | Median environment normalized-Gram change between those scales: physical pose below \(10^{-6}\), pixels ≈0.11, embeddings ≈0.43 on Reacher; approximately 0.03, 0.08, and 0.49 on Cube. | §3; Appendix D.1 |
| Environment Grams predict held-out embedding magnitudes better than isotropy | With eight held-out directions per state, median centered-response WAPE is approximately 17% versus 26% on Reacher and 28% versus 38% on Cube, using an isotropic reference with the environment trace. | Abstract; §3; Appendix D.3 |
| Model magnitude predictions remain worse than isotropy | Paired median model-minus-isotropic WAPE has 95% intervals of 0.046–0.069 on Reacher and 0.138–0.189 on Cube. Oracle environment-trace correction changes these to 0.007–0.031 and −0.065 to −0.036. | Appendix D.3 |
| Centered-response accuracy does not transfer directly to one-sided responses | Environment embedding-Gram median WAPE rises from approximately 17% to 32% on Reacher and 28% to 43% on Cube when predicting one-sided displacements. | §3; Appendix D.2 |

The evidence supports partial, scale-specific preservation of response-magnitude geometry. It does not establish correct signed outcome directions, useful goal-directed action rankings, or improved closed-loop control. Equal Grams can accompany opposite responses, and trace normalization removes overall gain (Appendix B). The oracle correction diagnoses gain error using simulator information; it is not demonstrated as a deployable correction (Appendix D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Only two environments and one checkpoint per environment are evaluated. Bootstrap intervals capture sampled-anchor uncertainty, not training-seed variation (Appendix A.4; §4).
- **Measurement dependence:** Physical units and coordinate weights are explicit but non-unique. Convergence to a smooth derivative is untested, and rendering, encoding, dynamics, and contact contributions remain unresolved (Appendices C, D.1).
- **Numerical and tail behavior:** Condition-number floors affect 32.4% of Cube environment metrics. Physical-response outliers remain, including one Reacher anchor with approximately 117% primary-scale WAPE, despite low median errors (Appendices C, D.3).
- **Reproducibility qualifications:** The original collection runtime is unavailable. Revised Cube trajectories differ from archived states, and later validation required amended replay gates and an unchanged retry after a threshold failure; the intermittent discrepancy remains unexplained (Appendices A.5, C, D.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Applying one shared offset across the action block tests a restricted family of temporal perturbations; these results do not establish geometry for independently varying each action (§2).
- **Evidence presentation:** Referenced result tables and figures are absent from the supplied text, limiting inspection of full distributions and exact all-scale results.

## Open research questions
- **Does better geometry improve control?** Gram agreement leaves signed responses ambiguous and control is untested. Next experiment: measure signed goal-directed predictions and closed-loop success alongside geometry scores (Appendix B; §4).
- **What causes visual scale dependence?** The paper cannot isolate rendering, encoding, dynamics, or contact effects. Next experiment: repeat matched perturbations with controlled rendering settings and live contact-event labels (§3; Appendix C).
- **Do findings generalize across training runs and temporal perturbations?** One checkpoint per environment and shared-offset perturbations leave both questions open. Next experiment: evaluate multiple checkpoints with independently perturbed action steps (§2; §4).
- **How stable are Cube results across executions?** Accepted outputs satisfy reported gates, but intermittent replay failures remain unexplained. Next experiment: repeat the complete validation bank across fresh simulator executions while retaining all failures (Appendix D.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, uncertainty definitions, and protocol qualifications, but supports a narrow empirical conclusion.
