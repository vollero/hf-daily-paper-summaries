# FocusVLA: Future-Intent Guided Dynamic View Routing for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31423) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31423)


## One-sentence takeaway

FocusVLA predicts future interaction demands to route global and wrist-view features during action generation, improving reported success over π₀.₅ by 10.10 percentage points on RoboTwin 2.0 and 13.3 points on six physical manipulation tasks (§§4.1–4.2).

## Problem and motivation

Multi-view VLA policies receive complementary global scene information and local interaction cues, but their reliance on these views may fail to change with manipulation stages. The authors identify persistent local-view dependence—even during global planning—as a weakness of static fusion. Attention analysis and a view-masking intervention motivate explicitly conditioning visual allocation on upcoming action requirements (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Diagnosis:** The authors characterize view imbalance and temporal adaptation using attention metrics, supplemented by stage-conditioned masking to probe actual view dependence (§3.1).
- **Future Intent Bridge:** Multimodal context, robot state, and recent actions predict a future sequence of local-interaction probabilities. Trajectory heuristics provide weak supervision without manual phase labels (§3.2.1; Appendix A).
- **Action-side routing:** Predicted intent produces both a chunk-level conditioning token and step-specific weights for separately attended global/local contexts. A bounded mapping preserves complementary evidence from both views (§3.2.2; Appendices C–D, G).

The implementation augments π₀.₅. Integration without changing core backbone architectures is an author claim; transfer across different VLA backbones is not demonstrated.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline remains strongly local-view dependent during planning | Stack Blocks Three, π₀.₅, 20 rollouts per setting: success was 85.0% unmasked, 65.0% with global view masked, and 20.0% with local view masked | §3.1, Table 1 |
| Higher overall simulation success | Across 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, 100 trials/task: FocusVLA 76.92% versus π₀.₅ 66.82%, a 10.10-point gain; π₀ and RDT scored 47.06% and 34.76% | §4.1, Table 2 |
| Higher physical-task success | Six Piper-arm tasks, 30 trials/task: FocusVLA averaged 85.0% versus π₀.₅ 71.7%, a 13.3-point gain | Abstract; §4.2, Fig. 5 |
| Intent guidance and both conditioning pathways help | Nine tasks, two training seeds, 100 trials/task/seed, 20K training steps: Full 73.7% [95% CI 71.6–75.7], learned-static routing 67.5%, action-loss-only routing 55.7%, routing without intent token 63.3% | §4.5, Table 3 |
| Additional controls favor future step-specific, action-side fusion | Four tasks, 100 trials/task: Full 79.5%, chunk-shared multimodal routing 55.5%, feature-side fusion 67.0%, paper-based BFA-style labels 72.0% | Appendix E, Table 4 |
| Labels and predictions show measurable agreement | Trajectory labels achieved human-reference F1 of 82.9% versus 65.1% for BFA-style labels; held-out intent prediction achieved 78.4% F1 and 91.2% AUROC | Appendix F, Table 5 |

The authors report matching demonstration data, optimization steps, test seeds, and evaluation protocols across compared methods (§4). These results support improved success in the tested settings and benefits from the tested design components. They do not establish universal backbone compatibility or cross-embodiment generalization. Gains are also not universal across tasks: Table 2 includes regressions. Spatial-rearrangement benefits are described qualitatively without numerical results in the supplied text (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Richer progress representations, cross-embodiment transfer, diverse camera configurations, and unseen camera layouts remain future work (§6; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic scope:** The masking intervention covers one task. Attention alone is not causal evidence, as the authors acknowledge; broader claims about persistent view dependence need wider intervention testing (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—supervision validity:** Intent labels encode deterministic movement/interaction heuristics, including press-like and handover-like rules. Human agreement supports their usefulness, but annotation sample size and annotator protocol are unavailable (Appendices A, F).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** The BFA-related comparison uses a paper-based reconstruction because usable code and complete labeling rules were unavailable. It therefore does not establish performance against an authoritative BFA implementation (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Ablations report confidence intervals and training seeds, but corresponding uncertainty and training-seed replication are not specified for the main simulation and physical results.

## Open research questions

1. **Does routing transfer across backbones and embodiments?** Only the π₀.₅ implementation and specified evaluation setups are demonstrated. Test matched integrations into additional VLA backbones and robots (§3.2; §6).
2. **Does the router remain useful after camera changes?** Unseen camera layouts remain explicitly untested. Evaluate controlled camera relocation and view occlusion without retraining (Appendix F).
3. **Would richer progress signals outperform binary interaction intent?** The authors identify subgoals and object interaction states as alternatives. Compare these signals under matched capacity, data, and supervision budgets (§6).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, controls, and evaluation results, but leaves material gaps: code/checkpoint availability, physical-task identities and per-task scores in readable text, human-annotation and held-out split protocols, quantitative spatial-rearrangement results, and main-result uncertainty across training seeds.
