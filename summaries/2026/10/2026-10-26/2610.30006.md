# Building Auditable AI Text Detectors with Sparse Autoencoders

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30006) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30006)


## One-sentence takeaway
Filtering sparse-autoencoder features by description fidelity yields inspectable AI-text classifiers with promising detection performance, but incomplete explanations, dataset shortcuts, and uneven adversarial results limit the auditability claim (§§6–8; Limitations).

## Problem and motivation
Neural AI-text detectors can influence consequential decisions while providing little basis for inspection or contestation. The authors identify a gap between accurate neural detectors and transparent but weaker linguistic-feature classifiers: token importance alone does not explain the textual concepts driving a decision (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Concept-based detection pipeline:** Extract residual-stream activations, encode them with SAEs, max-pool across tokens, concatenate across layers, and train an L1-regularized logistic-regression classifier (§4.1).
- **Description-based filtering:** Gemini generates feature descriptions and predicts activation quantile bins from them. A global threshold on Spearman correlation removes features whose descriptions poorly predict their activations. The authors identify this filtering rule, together with training SAEs directly on detectors, as their methodological novelty (§§3–4.2).
- **Comparative and intervention evidence:** Compare pretrained SAEs, SAEs adapted to base-model activations, and SAEs adapted to a fine-tuned detector; test explanations through targeted paraphrases and feature-guided attacks (§§5.3, 7).
- **Auditing interface:** Display feature descriptions, contributions, activation highlights, and training-corpus frequencies (§F). Public release is not established by the supplied text.

The evidence supports useful feature-level inspection and partial agreement between descriptions and model behavior. It does not establish complete semantic explanations or deployment reliability. Stronger OOD results belong primarily to the base-model SAE conditions; converting a fine-tuned detector also inherits its generalization failures (§6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated description imprecision:** Auto-interpretation can omit contextual dependencies while receiving a high validation score. The authors’ informal checks find recall bias in many descriptions, qualifying the conclusion’s stronger precision claims (Limitations).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Only GemmaScope/Gemma-2-2B is tested. Training and evaluation domains overlap, weakening the interpretation of “OOD” transfer (Limitations; §C.2).
- **Author-identified shortcuts:** Predictive features include formatting quirks and domain cues. Near-perfect PRDB accuracy may reflect confounds rather than broadly reliable detection (§§6, 8).
- **Analyst inference—attack comparison is not controlled for information and effort:** SAE prompts receive detector-specific passages, contributions, and planning instructions; the iterative condition also receives additional attempts. Their advantage cannot isolate feature descriptions alone (§E.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal evidence is partial:** Paraphrases change non-target features too, and feature-level sampling excludes rare or otherwise undescribable features. Correlation therefore does not establish exact causal attribution for every feature (§E.1; Tables 13–14).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Main-text and appendix attack-success claims conflict, and edit budgets differ across descriptions. Figure 20 is described as AUROC in §G but recall at fixed FPR in its caption.

## Open research questions
- **Does the method transfer across model families?** Only one base family is tested. Repeat the matched pipeline and evaluation with additional base models and SAEs (Limitations).
- **Can description precision improve beyond corpus-conditioned scores?** Missing contextual dependencies can escape validation. Test descriptions against synthetic context contrasts and hard negatives, then measure intervention agreement (Limitations).
- **Do detection gains survive removal of domain and formatting shortcuts?** Audits reveal both kinds of cues. Evaluate feature removal on domain-disjoint data with standardized formatting (§8; §C.2).
- **How much attack advantage comes specifically from explanations?** Current prompts differ in information and iteration. Match edit budgets, calls, and planning while varying descriptions, activation passages, and contribution scores (§E.2).
