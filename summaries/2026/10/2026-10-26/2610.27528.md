# Judging the Justification: Decision Certificate Supervision for Generative Reward Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27528) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27528)


## One-sentence takeaway
DeCert ranks generated justifications by evidence validity, issue coverage, and verdict support, improving preference judging and rationale quality across two 8B model families without training on reference explanations (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
A generative reward model can select the correct response while giving unsupported or inconsistent reasons. Verdict-only training cannot distinguish these judgments from well-supported ones. The authors argue that matching reference explanations is costly and does not directly establish whether a rationale supports its verdict (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Decision-certificate assessment:** Represent a rationale as atomic claims and dependencies, then score soundness, coverage of independently extracted material issues, and conservative support for the declared verdict. Combine these dimensions geometrically (§3.2).
- **Outcome-controlled preference construction:** Correct verdicts always outrank incorrect ones. When every sampled judgment is correct, sufficiently different certificate scores produce rationale preference pairs, isolating justification quality from verdict correctness (§3.3).
- **Training and validation:** Apply SFT followed by DPO with LoRA to Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B. Evaluate preference accuracy, rationale quality, evidence sensitivity, and response-order robustness (§§3.3–4).

“Reference-explanation-free” still requires preference labels, evaluation policies, and automated analyzers and verifiers.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Automated certificate assessment inherits analyzer reliability and bias; evaluation covers only pairwise judging, two 8B families, and limited benchmarks; rationale quality does not establish faithfulness to internal decisions (§6).
- **Observed residual weakness:** Generic unsupported claims remain common (§4.4), and omitted support remains the hardest tested harmful perturbation for the combined score (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Excluding unparseable judgments and instances without verifiable material issues may narrow the training distribution; exclusion rates are unavailable (Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied all-correct-pair ablation adds training pairs; it does not document a size-matched alternative, leaving additional data volume as a possible contributor (§4.3).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Missing table and prompt bodies prevent checking exact rubric weights, checkpoint/decoding settings, complete baseline scores, and operational prompts.

## Open research questions
- **How much do analyzer errors affect training gains?** Analyzer dependence is acknowledged, while Appendix D compares issue extraction rather than downstream training. Retrain with different analyzers and a human-audited subset.
- **Do rationale rankings help beyond additional data volume?** The reported ablation adds pairs. Compare certificate-ranked pairs with an equal-size alternative under matched training budgets.
- **Do better certificates improve downstream alignment?** No downstream RLHF experiment is reported. Use the trained judges as reward providers and evaluate resulting policy behavior independently.
- **Will gains persist at larger scales or beyond pairwise judging?** These settings remain outside the evaluation (§6). Test larger judges and listwise comparisons with matched outcome-only controls.
