# From Evidence to Action: How Tool-Using Agents Fail

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07753) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07753)


## One-sentence takeaway
SafeActBench shows that tool-using agents often act before establishing required evidence or stop investigating too early, while successful execution after complete investigation is substantially more reliable (§5.3.1).

## Problem and motivation
A correct final outcome can conceal an unsupported consequential action: an agent may refund the right charge without first verifying its eligibility, or use an earlier action’s result incorrectly downstream. The authors identify a gap in evaluations that emphasize task completion without checking whether evidence was established **before execution**, bound to the correct entity and state, and propagated through dependent actions (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation formulation:** Assess observable evidence-to-action chains, including investigated non-action, exact action arguments, and downstream dependencies; hidden reasoning is outside scope (§3).
- **SafeActBench:** Introduce 656 synthetic cases across six operational domains and five protocols: static Allow/Block/Defer judgment, investigated non-action, single-action execution, linear workflows, and dependency-DAG workflows (§4).
- **Provenance-aware verification:** An Evidence Ledger records evidence sources and bindings; a deterministic evaluator checks investigation, actions, receipts, dependencies, and resulting states without an LLM judge (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Empirical diagnosis and interventions:** Compare ten model–harness configurations, manipulate decisive evidence, and test supplementary training-free runtimes. SCGR-Select structures model-selected investigation; SCGR-EG additionally automates evidence collection in multi-action protocols (§5; Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Static judgment can greatly exceed interactive success on identical cases. | On the same 100 V1 cases for DeepSeek–Inspect, GLM–Inspect, and GLM–ZCode, static valid-action judgment accuracy was 95.0–99.0%, versus interactive ECS of 28.0–52.0%. Paired gaps were 43.0, 69.0, and 66.0 percentage points; all reported CIs excluded zero. | C.2; Table 9 |
| Failures frequently precede supported execution. | Across ten configurations, V0 stopping-before-investigation rates were 21.7–62.9% of scored episodes. V1 premature-action rates were 37.0–66.9% **among episodes attempting an action**. | §5.3.1; Table 3; B.6 |
| Single-action success is high conditional on completed investigation and an attempt. | V1 conditional ECS was 93.2–100% for nine configurations and 83.3% for DeepSeek–Inspect. Main-evaluation rates average three repetitions. | §§5.1, 5.3.1; B.6 |
| Harness effects depend on the model. | On the same 570 interactive cases, family-harness-minus-Inspect ECS differences were +4.4 points for DeepSeek and −6.8 for GLM; both case-paired bootstrap CIs excluded zero. Claude, GPT, and Qwen intervals included or reached zero. | §5.3.2; Table 4 |
| Withholding decisive evidence reduces action but leaves substantial continued submission. | Three configurations on a 43-case V1 cohort: paired action-probability reductions were 37.2–45.2 points, yet action occurred in 46.5–53.5% of completed Withheld episodes. | §5.3.3; Figure 3; C.4 |
| Requester claims suppress action more than presenting missing evidence alone. | For DeepSeek, GLM, and Qwen respectively, Withheld action counts were 23/43, 20/43, 23/43; evidence-package counts were 18/43, 14/43, 13/43; requester-claim counts were 6/43, 10/43, 6/43. All requester-claim reduction CIs excluded zero. | §5.3.4; Figure 4 |
| Automated evidence collection helps overall ECS, while model-selected structuring has mixed effects. | SCGR-EG overall gains ranged from 0.3–16.2 points across ten configurations. Among four full-coverage SCGR-Select configurations on 570 interactive cases, ECS changed by +11.6 points for Qwen, −9.1 for Claude, −2.8 for GLM, and −2.6 for GPT. | E.4–E.5; Tables 13–14 |
| Evaluator audits support, but do not prove, scoring validity. | At 300 audited action checkpoints, human adjudication found false acceptance in 3/150 evaluator-positive judgments and false rejection in 2/150 evaluator-negative judgments. | B.3 |

The evidence supports a distinction between endpoint success and trace-supported execution. It does not establish what agents internally relied on, whether withheld actions were correct refusals, or whether extra retrieval caused better evidence use. High conditional success also does not prove that completing investigation would causally repair every failed episode. Main evaluation had complete coverage; intervention estimates generally exclude runtime failures (§5.1; B.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Synthetic environments do not certify safety in deployed operational domains; audits involved research-team members rather than external participants (Ethics Statement).
- **Observable versus internal evidence:** Traces establish available support, not internal reliance; affected-tool querying does not prove the exact missing record was observed before action (§3.1; C.4).
- **Comparison ambiguity:** The primary matched static–interactive comparison changes both evidence availability and responsibility for action construction/execution. The balanced control measures decision accuracy and includes output-format reliability (C.2–C.2.1).
- **Sampling and uncertainty:** Most evidence interventions use a small V1 cohort and single intervention rollouts; bootstrap intervals reflect case variation rather than repeated-generation variability (C.4).
- **Evaluator validity:** Passing reference solutions establishes implementation consistency, not necessity or completeness of every requirement. Audits found scoring disagreements and date-handling errors, corrected before final evaluation (B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** SCGR-EG’s gains cannot be attributed to evidence organization alone because it also changes acquisition through automatic scans. SCGR-Select’s partial DeepSeek cohorts and changing completion rates further limit comparisons (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated confounding:** Cross-harness intervention runs occurred at different times with different timeout and recovery histories (C.8.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can checkpoint-specific interventions improve dependent workflows?** The authors leave multi-action evidence interventions for future work (§6). Next experiment: withhold or contradict predecessor results and prerequisites separately at V2/V3 checkpoints.
- **Which runtime component produces improvement?** SCGR-EG combines organization with automatic collection, while SCGR-Select has mixed outcomes (Appendix E). Next experiment: a matched factorial ablation of ledger organization, public checks, and automatic collection under equal budgets.
- **Does completed investigation causally improve execution?** Conditional V1 success is observational (§5.3.1). Next experiment: randomly supply complete, provenance-matched evidence while preserving autonomous action construction.
- **How robust are requirements outside synthetic settings?** Internal audits support validity, but deployment safety remains unestablished (B.3; Ethics Statement). Next experiment: external expert review and evaluation on independently authored operational tasks with alternative valid evidence paths.
