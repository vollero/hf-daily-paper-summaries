# Judged Useless, Queried Anyway: Tool-Using Agents Rarely Turn Their Own Evidence Judgments into Stopping Decisions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06191) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06191)


## One-sentence takeaway
In controlled retrieval failures, most tested agents recognize useless results but rarely stop because of accumulated useless evidence, while a harness-enforced stopping rule improves success and reduces dependence on the deadline.

## Problem and motivation
An agent can recognize that retrieval is unhelpful yet keep spending calls on it. The authors distinguish three possible causes: inaccurate usefulness judgments, mistaken beliefs about continuing, and failure to translate judgments into actions.

Their identified gap is measurement: answer accuracy alone cannot distinguish evidence-driven stopping from answering at a fixed time or deadline. These policies can achieve similar success while consuming different resources and responding differently to budget changes (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled diagnostic environment:** ReAct agents answer HotpotQA questions and verify FEVER claims using small knowledge bases with injected persistent, recovering, and late-onset failures. Judgment probes, belief probes, and actions are recorded separately (§2).
- **Time-matched stopping measure:** The contrast \(\Delta\) compares answering probabilities at the same step between uninterrupted useless-result histories and histories containing earlier useful evidence. This addresses the time confound in the authors’ original integration index (§2; Appendix C.1).
- **Enforced integration intervention:** After five consecutive results judged useless, the harness permits only answering. Controls test prompting, budgets, call costs, explicit counts, and alternative judgment signals. A keyword reader can instead use judgments already stated in reasoning (§§4–5; Appendix D.3).
- **Replication and scope tests:** Fresh questions, fact verification, harder failures, larger models, reasoning modes, and a search-trained agent test how broadly the observed dissociation holds (§§6–7).

## Evidence and main results

Here, **mean6** is success averaged equally over six regimes; HotpotQA answers count as correct at token F1 ≥ 0.6 (§2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate judgments often fail to trigger answering | Recorded judgments label injected failing results useless 97–100% of the time on HotpotQA and FEVER. On persistent HotpotQA test300 trajectories, unaided Llama-3.1-8B answers after five consecutive useless judgments on 1/282 questions; Qwen3-8B on 1/292. | §3; Appendix C.1 |
| Unaided open models show no positive time-matched integration signal | On HotpotQA test300, own-judgment \(\Delta\) ranges from −0.11 to −0.02 across four open models; enforced-rule values are +0.33 to +0.37. | Table 6 |
| Enforcement improves aggregate success | Enforced rule versus unaided: mean6 gains of +0.026 to +0.097 across four open models and Claude Haiku 4.5, reported Holm-significant. | §5; Table 15 |
| Budget cues induce deadline-following | On persistent test300 failures, the three budget-cued 7–8B models’ median answering action moves from 8 to 16 when the action budget doubles; persistent-source success shows no consistent improvement. | Table 16 |
| Central effects replicate on fresh questions | On fresh300, four open models have enforced-rule \(\Delta\) of +0.30 to +0.35 and mean6 gains over unaided agents of +0.030 to +0.061; all nineteen registered tests pass. | §6; Table 22 |
| Transfer gains are larger on fact verification | On 292 balanced FEVER claims, unchanged enforcement raises mean6 by +0.129 to +0.201 for the three 7–8B models. | §6; Tables 14–15 |
| Reading stated judgments lowers intervention overhead | On test300, the reader’s mean6 differs from side-channel enforcement by 0.000, +0.010, and +0.001 for Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3-32B; utility improves when every extra call costs 0.05. | Tables 19–20 |
| Enforcement can stop just before recovery | When recovery occurs after five failures, rule-versus-unaided success falls by −0.213 for Qwen3-8B and −0.137 for Qwen3-32B. | Table 26 |

The evidence supports a behavioral gap between judging and acting in these environments. Positive \(\Delta\) under enforcement is expected by construction; the informative findings are success gains, call savings, and replication. The rule is not universally superior: budget prompting can match or exceed its success, and Claude Sonnet 5 partly stops on evidence unaided, with its mean6 intervention gain not statistically significant (§§4, 7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope limits:** Failures are simulated in fixed knowledge bases; natural partial, stale, or erroneous outputs remain untested. The threshold depends on the recovery distribution, and answering from memory can be wrong (§9).
- **Measurement limits:** Side-channel judgments and self-reported beliefs may differ from the acting process. Stated judgments use LLM annotation with author adjudication rather than independent human annotation. Haiku judgments are partly drawn from intervention trajectories; Sonnet’s incomplete judgment logs require a design-label fallback (§2; Appendices B.2, B.4).
- **Protocol and comparison risks:** Registration was not deposited externally; \(\Delta\) was introduced after earlier results, although fresh300 addresses this retrospectivity. Multiplicity correction is within hypotheses. Placeholder repairs alter some comparisons, and Haiku’s later prompt conditions receive formatting fixes absent from earlier conditions (Appendices A, C.5, D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** These experiments identify an actionable control failure, but do not establish a unique internal cognitive mechanism. Earlier useful evidence also affects stopping, complicating interpretation of negative \(\Delta\) (§3; Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does the gap persist under natural failures?** Synthetic substitutions leave this unresolved (§9). Next experiment: evaluate matched stopping contrasts on logged stale, partial, and erroneous retrieval outputs.
- **Can stopping thresholds adapt to recovery probability and cost?** Late recovery causes demonstrated losses (Table 26). Next experiment: compare fixed and adaptive thresholds on held-out recovery schedules and call prices.
- **Can training teach evidence-sensitive stopping?** One search-trained agent does not establish what targeted training could achieve (§7). Next experiment: train policies with explicit judgment-to-action objectives and evaluate \(\Delta\), success, and calls under changed budgets.
- **What should agents do after abandoning a source?** Memory fallback has validity risks, while backup-tool experiments offer only a limited alternative (§9; Appendix D.5). Next experiment: compare answering, switching, and abstaining under controlled fallback reliability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported judgment–action dissociation in the controlled settings; lower for generalization to real tools.

Material gaps include natural-failure evaluations, a third-party registration record, complete Sonnet judgment logs, independent human judgment labels, and comprehensive monetary or latency accounting. The supplied text reports an available repository, but does not establish its contents beyond the authors’ descriptions.
