# Technical Report on the Turba Fertilizer Machine Learning Stack in Morocco

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05949) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05949)


## One-sentence takeaway
Turba packages Moroccan fertilizer-system access, versioned recommendation data, and offline surrogates that closely reproduce upstream outputs on random test splits, while leaving geographic transfer and agronomic efficacy unvalidated (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Interactive recommendation interfaces, mutable upstream services, and unavailable trained artifacts hinder reproducible fertilizer research. The authors address this gap around Morocco’s Fertimap system by making recommendation retrieval, preserved datasets, and model benchmarking reusable while separating system outputs from field observations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Access and provenance architecture:** `turba-client` retrieves site profiles, crop workflows, target-yield ranges, and fertilizer recommendations, with validation, batch processing, and controlled soil overrides (§2.1).
- **Versioned dataset:** `turba-data` preserves recommendation scenarios as registered Parquet snapshots, decoupling analysis from future upstream changes (§§2.2, 3).
- **Offline surrogates and reproducible benchmarks:** `turba-models` packages crop-specific regressors predicting N, P₂O₅, and K₂O from coordinates and soil variables, alongside model registries, benchmark reports, and a training workflow (§§2.3–5).

The contribution is primarily reusable infrastructure and explicit provenance separation; the paper does not introduce a new regression algorithm (§7). Multimodal data, microbiome integration, and reinforcement learning are proposed extensions, not demonstrated contributions (§6.3).

These results support agreement with Fertimap outputs within the evaluated distribution. They do not establish yield improvement, fertilizer optimality, profitability, environmental benefits, or spatial/temporal transfer. Although nine model families were evaluated, the supplied text lacks their complete comparative scores, so performance margins behind model selection cannot be assessed.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Nearby sites remain spatially dependent despite no duplicate coordinates within each crop-specific split. Random evaluation therefore measures interpolation. Maize support is small; crop and irrigation scenarios lack field-level confirmation; inputs are static; uncertainty is uncalibrated; and future retrieval depends on upstream availability and implementation (§§3–4, 6.1).

**Analyst inference:** Evaluation at only medium target yield does not establish surrogate fidelity at other yield settings (§3). The text reports no feature ablations, leaving the contribution of coordinates versus soil predictors unresolved (§§2.3, 4). Reproducibility is supported by described artifacts, but cannot be independently verified from the supplied text (§5).

## Open research questions
- **Does fidelity survive geographic separation?** Random splits leave spatial dependence unresolved. Next experiment: site-grouped spatial blocking and region holdouts, reporting nutrient-specific errors (§§4, 6.2).
- **How stable are surrogates across yield settings and upstream changes?** Training uses medium-yield scenarios, and the service can change. Next experiment: collect versioned snapshots across yield levels and retrieval dates, then test transfer (§§2.1, 3).
- **Can uncertainty flag unreliable predictions?** Calibration and out-of-distribution detection are future priorities. Next experiment: evaluate predictive intervals and failure detection on spatial holdouts (§§6.1–6.2).
- **Do reproduced rates improve field outcomes?** Output agreement supplies no agronomic validation. Next experiment: prospective fertilizer-response trials measuring yield, nutrient-use efficiency, profitability, and environmental performance (§§4, 6.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the architecture and restricted interpretation of the reported benchmark; **medium** in comparative performance assessment.

Material omissions include Table 1 and Table 2 cell contents, complete model-family and nutrient-level scores, explicit units beside reported absolute errors, exact estimator settings and dependency versions, uncertainty estimates, feature ablations, spatial/temporal validation results, and field-trial evidence.
