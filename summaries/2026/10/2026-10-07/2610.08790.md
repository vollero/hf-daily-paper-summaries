# Building Rome from a Single Image

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08790) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08790)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors adapt Trellis 2 to generate indoor and outdoor scene meshes from one image through adaptive chunks, explicit surface and visibility conditioning, and synthetic outdoor supervision, reporting improved reconstruction but supplying no numerical table entries in the retrieved text.

## Problem and motivation
Single-image scene generation must recover visible surfaces and invent plausible geometry behind them. The authors identify two gaps: object generators operate within a fixed canonical volume that poorly accommodates large scenes, and available scene supervision is predominantly indoor. The intended applications include content creation, virtual reality, and robotics; these applications are motivation, not demonstrated downstream results (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive scene chunks:** Small nearby chunks preserve detail, while larger distant or tall-region chunks extend coverage. Geometry and camera translation are scaled together so one canonical-volume generator can process different physical scales (§4.1).
- **Explicit 2D–3D conditioning:** DINOv3 features are lifted only near estimated visible surfaces. A signed depth residual tells the sparse-structure model whether a voxel lies in free space, on the surface, or behind it; the structured-latent stage receives surface features (§4.2).
- **Outdoor supervision:** An agentic pipeline constructs approximately 4,000 outdoor scenes through image generation, object completion and meshing, terrain construction, layout solving, and critic refinement. Rendering the assembled meshes produces consistent training images and geometry despite imperfect reference-image reconstruction (§4.3; Appendix A.4).

The model adapts Trellis 2 through LoRA and new conditioning modules. At inference, MoGe-3 estimates geometry and intrinsics; chunks are generated sequentially with overlapping latents copied or resampled to encourage agreement (§§4.3, 5).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Outputs lack textures, may contain holes because watertightness is not guaranteed, depend on potentially inaccurate depth and intrinsics, and may propagate early errors through reused overlap latents (Appendix A.3). Synthetic outdoor scenes can contain inaccurate shapes and layouts (§4.3).

**Analyst inferences:** Ground-truth geometry conditions training, whereas estimated geometry conditions inference, creating a potential sensitivity gap (§4.3). Geometry evaluation covers limited scene subsets, and ICP alignment means reported scores do not directly establish accuracy in the original predicted coordinate frame (§5). Aggregate metrics do not separately quantify hidden-region completion. Missing table entries and user-study details limit independent assessment of comparative strength.

## Open research questions
- **How sensitive is generation to geometry-estimation errors?** Appendix A.3 acknowledges this dependence without quantifying it. Compare oracle conditioning, MoGe-3 estimates, and controlled depth/intrinsics perturbations.
- **How accurate is occluded completion specifically?** Current reporting aggregates geometry quality. Evaluate visible and hidden surfaces separately on complete held-out meshes.
- **How do errors accumulate across chunks and scales?** Overlap reuse may propagate mistakes (Appendix A.3). Measure seam quality and reconstruction error by generation order and chunk count, comparing alternative orders.
- **Which outdoor supervision components drive generalization?** The data ablation tests inclusion of the corpus, not its construction components (§5.2). Compare matched-size corpora varying category diversity, critic refinement, and layout quality.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method is described clearly, but quantitative verification is incomplete.

Material missing information includes Tables 1–4 numerical entries, uncertainty estimates, user-study participant and trial counts, ablation validation-set composition, hidden-region-specific scores, detailed baseline execution settings, and code/model/dataset release status.
