# Rethinking Cross-Tokenizer On-Policy Distillation: From Alignment Coverage to Supervision Reliability

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08448) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08448)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across three heterogeneous teacher–student pairs, strict cross-tokenizer distillation with student-selected top-16 supervision retains at least 96% of full-support accuracy gains, while adding mismatch-span MSE lowers accuracy in every tested positive-weight setting (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

On-policy distillation trains a student on its own responses using teacher predictions. Different tokenizers create incompatible token boundaries and vocabularies, making those predictions difficult to compare directly.

The authors question whether recovering more alignment necessarily improves learning: static vocabulary overlap may underestimate useful supervision on actual student trajectories, and supervision recovered from mismatched spans may conflict with the strict objective (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Coverage-versus-value experiment:** Align tokens through shared response boundaries, apply reverse KL at strict one-to-one positions over the renormalized shared vocabulary, then add weighted MSE between observed mismatch-span log-probabilities. This isolates the effect of expanding structural supervision coverage (§§2–3).
- **Compact supervision:** Select the student’s highest-probability shared vocabulary entries at each strict position, renormalize both models on that subset, and train with reverse KL. The contribution is testing this concentrated support under heterogeneous tokenizers, rather than introducing top-k distillation generally (§§4, 6).
- **Explanatory diagnostics:** Measure retained predictive mass, mismatch–strict gradient cosine, and relative gradient norms to interpret performance differences. These support a possible explanation, not a demonstrated causal mechanism (§§3.3, 5).

The authors also evaluate top-16 supervision with a larger teacher and on interactive ALFWorld tasks (Appendices D–E).

## Evidence and main results

For the three main pairs, mathematics accuracy is mean@32 across seven benchmarks; code accuracy is mean@8 across three. **Full** equally weights the two domain averages, with distilled models evaluated at training step 100 (Appendix A).

The evidence supports compact strict supervision in the tested settings and rejects the tested span-MSE additions as beneficial. It does **not** establish that every way of supervising mismatch spans is harmful. Strict methods also do not win every individual benchmark or code-domain comparison, and gradient diagnostics only suggest why span MSE might hurt.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied paper has no dedicated limitations section. Material boundaries and **analyst inferences** are:

- **Objective-specific conclusion:** The coverage sweep tests fixed-weight span log-probability MSE. Its failure cannot establish that other mismatch objectives or adaptive weighting would fail (§3.2).
- **Diagnostic causality:** Gradients are measured on strict-only trajectories, without clipping, optimizer preconditioning, or parameter updates. Their relationship to actual mixed-loss optimization remains indirect (§5; Appendix B).
- **Average-mass interpretation:** Mass measurements use pre-distillation rollouts and exclude several unscorable response categories. Averages do not establish reliability at rare positions or throughout training (Appendix B).
- **Comparison differences:** Strict training includes paired EOS positions, while ULD, Extended ULD, and GOLD exclude terminal EOS. No EOS-matched ablation is supplied (Appendix A).
- **Uncertainty and generalization:** Main accuracy tables provide no confidence intervals or repeated-training-seed variability. Training spans three main pairs and a limited training schedule; appendices broaden tasks and scale but do not repeat the full coverage and diagnostic study.
- **Data validity:** Training prompts are not deduplicated, retained counts vary by tokenizer, and train–evaluation overlap measurements are not supplied (Appendix A).
- **Efficiency:** Compact support is demonstrated experimentally, but measured training-time, memory, or teacher-computation savings are not reported.

## Open research questions

- **Can reliable mismatch supervision improve accuracy?** Fixed-weight MSE underperforms; compare alternative span objectives and gradient-aware weighting against strict-only training with matched settings.
- **Does compact support remain sufficient throughout learning?** Mass is probed only before distillation; repeat mass measurements at training checkpoints, stratified by domain and token type.
- **How much do implementation choices explain baseline gaps?** EOS handling differs; run EOS-matched comparisons and report repeated-seed uncertainty.
- **Do span gradients cause the observed degradation?** Strict-only replay is indirect; measure gradients during mixed-loss training and test norm balancing or conflict mitigation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported results and method description; lower for causal explanations and broad generalization.

Material missing information includes main-run seed variability and statistical uncertainty, probe exclusion counts and retained training-pool sizes, EOS-controlled comparisons, train–evaluation overlap analysis, measured efficiency gains, and full-support or span-MSE comparisons in the larger-teacher and ALFWorld extensions. Code availability is author-reported and was not independently verified.
