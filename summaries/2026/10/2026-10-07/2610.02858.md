# Harness-Aware Distillation for Small Language Model Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02858) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02858)


## One-sentence takeaway
Harness-Aware Distillation improves small agents’ use of a fixed deployment harness by contrasting teacher actions with and without harness information and filtering invalid preference targets, with reported gains over on-policy distillation baselines (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Small agents must interpret the context, state records, and feedback supplied by their deployment harness. The authors argue that ordinary on-policy distillation (OPD) leaves this ability implicit: it imitates complete teacher responses without identifying decisions affected by harness information or rejecting targets that contradict recorded state. Because the harness remains available after distillation, the proposed goal is to transfer the teacher’s ability to act on its information (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Harness-aware action preferences:** At student-visited states, query the same teacher independently with harness information and with that information stripped. Prefer the harness-conditioned action, scoring both actions after the student’s own reasoning using a reference-free logistic loss. Retain full-response imitation alongside this auxiliary loss (§3.1).
- **Validity filtering:** Remove preference pairs whose positive action contradicts available-action, inventory, or failed-action records. Filtering leaves the response-imitation target unchanged (§3.1; §§A.1, B.4).
- **Formal characterization and evaluation:** The authors show that isolated preference scores recover a teacher action-distribution contrast unavailable from positive-response imitation alone, under specified assumptions. Experiments and ablations test whether this additional signal improves performance and recovery (§3.2; Appendix A; §4).

The stripped history retains earlier harness-influenced responses and observations; it is not an independently generated trajectory without a harness (§3.1). The training objective uses no task rewards, success labels, or future information, although harness thresholds are calibrated using successful validation episodes (§B.2).

These results support HAD within the tested settings. They do not establish generalization to arbitrary harnesses or production agents. The formal analysis identifies teacher-input provenance contrasts, not measured action utility or guaranteed task improvement (§A.3). Partial harness internalization is the authors’ interpretation of removal experiments; different category firing frequencies complicate that interpretation (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation uses text environments, students up to 2B parameters, teachers up to 30B, and a hand-designed harness. Larger models, tool-calling/software agents, and learned harnesses remain future work (§5).
- **Author-stated supervision limits:** Validity filtering cannot detect valid but incorrect actions, and rejected targets still enter response imitation (§3.2; §A.1). Reference-free preferences can flatten probabilities; the isolated auxiliary objective does not preserve useful reasoning-dependent action variation (§§A.4, A.7).
- **Author-stated sampling caveat:** Independent teacher sampling means action disagreements include sampling variation as well as effects of harness information (§B.4).
- **Analyst inference—ablation attribution:** Full-response preferences change both the scored span and reasoning context; positive-only supervision does not isolate student versus teacher reasoning. These controls therefore cannot independently attribute gains to student-prefix conditioning (§§A.2, A.7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Results apply to the implemented baselines, including top-token KL approximation and a modified Guided-OPD teacher-turn loss (§B.4).

## Open research questions
1. **How much preference signal comes from harness information versus sampling noise?** The paper acknowledges both sources (§B.4). Compare repeated within-condition teacher samples against between-condition contrasts.
2. **Does student reasoning independently improve supervision?** Existing controls do not isolate this choice (§A.7). Hold action pairs and scored spans fixed while varying student and teacher prefixes.
3. **Can invalid imitation targets be handled more effectively?** HAD filters only preferences (§A.1). Compare preference-only filtering with filtering or repairing response-imitation targets.
4. **How robust is HAD to changed harnesses?** The deployment harness is fixed and hand-designed (§5). Evaluate transferred students under altered templates, noisy records, and learned harnesses.
