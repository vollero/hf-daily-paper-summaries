# Source Identification Is Not Fitness Testing: Measuring the Limits of Synthetic-Data Attribution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00417) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00417)


## One-sentence takeaway
Generator attribution deteriorates sharply after rewriting, while two substantially different synthetic-data selection rules show no statistically detected difference in recursive degradation—a result too uncertain to establish equivalence (§§IV, VII–VIII).

## Problem and motivation
The paper asks whether identifying synthetic text’s source helps select examples that improve later models. It separates generated-versus-human detection, exact generator attribution, and recognition of unseen generators from the downstream question of training usefulness. The identified gap is that accurate provenance does not establish which generated examples should be retained during recursive training (§§I, II-D).

## Approach and contributions
- **Separate source-identification measurements:** Evaluate TF-IDF classifiers and fine-tuned DistilRoBERTa on financial-risk text, distinguishing binary detection from generator attribution before and after four rewriting transformations (§§III-A–D, IV).
- **Robustness and rejection diagnostics:** Test training on known rewrites, holding out rewrite types, recognising unseen generators, and split-conformal abstention under distribution shift (§§III-D–F, V–VI).
- **Recursive selection comparison:** Starting from Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, compare low source-classifier-margin selection with low perplexity under a frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct reference model. Each retains half the generated pool across three retraining transitions; random and fixed-draw controls provide context (§III-G).

These are empirical contributions; the paper does not establish a sufficient measure of future training value (§IX-D–F).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Attribution weakens after rewriting | Seven-source word-TFIDF mean accuracy: original **98.7%**, roundtrip translation **96.7%**, paraphrasing **53.1%**, style transfer **29.0%**, generic evasion **46.9%**. Separately trained DistilRoBERTa falls from **96.0%** to **9.4%** under style transfer. | §IV; Fig. 1; Table II |
| Binary detection remains strong within the tested comparison | Generated-versus-human TF-IDF AUROC remains **>0.9999** under every rewrite against register-matched, task-mismatched human 10-K paragraphs. | §IV; Table II caption |
| Rewrite-aware training improves within-family robustness | Character-TFIDF accuracy is **99.3–99.8%** on represented transformations; holding out a rewrite type gives **95.7%** mean accuracy and **83.4%** on held-out style transfer. | §V; Table III |
| Abstention does not reliably catch style-transfer errors | Eight-way conformal prediction at **α=0.05**: style-transfer mean singleton rate **0.5467**, wrong singleton rate **0.4083**, and worst-source wrong singleton rate **0.8567**. Rates use all evaluated examples as denominator. | §§III-F, V; Table IV |
| Unseen-source recognition varies by setting | Leaving one reference generator out: AUROC **0.7700–0.9923**, mean **0.9261**. Five test-only generators: **0.9714–0.9929**, mean **0.9822**. Training uses six versus seven known sources, respectively. | §VI; Table V |
| Primary recursive comparison detects no slope separation | Five lineages per main rule, three transitions, evaluation on **200** frozen human paragraphs: mean perplexity slopes **7.543** for reference-perplexity selection versus **7.367** for provenance selection; difference **+0.176 points/generation**, exact two-sided permutation **p=0.746**, **95% interval [−0.794, +1.267]**. | §§III-G, VII, VIII-A–B; Fig. 2; Table VI |
| Main rules select substantially different examples | Mean overlap among **80** retained items: **36.0** at G1, **37.1** at G2, **36.2** at G3; maximum absolute score correlation **0.255** across measured pools. | §VII; Table VII |
| Small differences are poorly detectable | Under a normal location-shift simulation using observed pooled SD **0.716**, five-versus-five lineages yield estimated power **0.157** for a **0.5-point** slope difference and **0.799** for **1.48 points/generation**. | §VIII-C; Table VIII |

The evidence supports separating source identity, selection score, and downstream training outcome. Different selections did not produce a statistically detected difference in mean recursive slopes. The interval and sensitivity analysis leave meaningful differences unresolved; they do not establish equivalence, provenance superiority, or universal generated-text detection. The two unseen-source evaluations also cannot be directly ranked because their known-source training sets differ (§§VI, VIII–IX).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** One recursive model scale and domain, three updates, few lineages, and four transformations from one broad rewriting family restrict generalisation. Human paragraphs match the register but not the generated-summary task. The recursive generator is absent from detector training, so classifier confidence should not be interpreted as calibrated source-membership probability (§X).

**Statistical assumptions:** The permutation test requires exchangeable lineage slopes; its inverted interval assumes a constant additive shift. Power estimates additionally assume a normal location-shift model (§VIII).

**Analyst inferences:** Binary detection may exploit differences between summaries and paragraph extracts; the supplied experiment does not isolate this possibility. Held-out corpus perplexity establishes performance on that metric, leaving other aspects of training usefulness unmeasured. No arm retains the entire generated pool, so the experiment does not establish whether either selector improves on using all candidates (§§III-B, III-G).

## Open research questions
- **Does strong binary detection survive a task-matched human baseline?** The current baseline differs in document task (§III-B). Next experiment: compare generated summaries with human-written summaries matched for prompts, register, and length.
- **Do smaller selector effects emerge with more lineages or longer recursion?** The interval remains broad and the study stops after three updates (§§VIII, X). Next experiment: increase independent lineages and transitions with a prespecified minimum effect of interest.
- **Can rewrite robustness and abstention transfer beyond this transformation family?** Held-out tests remain within four rewriting methods, and style transfer produces many wrong singletons (§§V, X). Next experiment: evaluate an independently developed transformation family without detector retraining.
- **Which selection signals predict broader downstream training value?** Neither tested score is established as sufficient (§IX-F). Next experiment: compare selectors against full-pool and random controls using both held-out perplexity and an explicitly defined financial-risk task outcome.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied narrative clearly supports the main findings and their restricted interpretation, but several referenced tables contain only captions.

Material missing information includes full per-source and per-lineage results, numerical conformal coverage, exact detector splits and calibration procedures, detailed rewrite prompts and model assignments, recursive fine-tuning hyperparameters, and public availability of code, data, or checkpoints. These omissions limit independent verification and reproduction.
