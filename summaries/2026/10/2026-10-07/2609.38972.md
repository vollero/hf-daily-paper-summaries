# Making LLMs Say What They Think: Measuring and Improving CoT-Interpretability Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38972) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38972)


## One-sentence takeaway
Interpretability-guided post-training improves agreement between verbalized and detected reasoning strategies on three controlled tasks, but the evidence supports task-specific alignment rather than complete faithfulness of a model’s reasoning (§§3–6).

## Problem and motivation
Chain-of-thought (CoT) explanations can describe reasoning that the model does not actually use, undermining their value for monitoring. The authors identify two gaps in prior parametric-faithfulness evaluations: reliance on misleading-hint tests and limited inspection of internal representations (§2).

Their goal is to measure and improve agreement between explicit explanations and internally detected strategies, separately from answer correctness.

## Approach and contributions
- **CIA metric:** CoT-Interpretability Alignment is macro-F1 between binary labels for verbalized strategy use and internally detected strategy use. It assumes a task-specific target strategy and treats interpretability-derived labels as the reference (§3.1).
- **Training framework:** Rejection sampling retains aligned completions regardless of correctness. DPO and GRPO use answer correctness plus a reward for agreement between internal and verbalized labels (§5.1).
- **Evaluation and mechanism analysis:** The authors evaluate factual composition, hint influence, and multiplication; test transfer across tasks and measurement tools; and use category transitions and causal interventions to distinguish changes in reporting from changes in computation (§§3.2, 5.2, 6).
- **Assets:** The abstract provides a [code and data repository](https://github.com/yihuaihong/CIA-minimal-repro); its contents are not verified here.

The results establish that the paper’s operational alignment measure can improve, with supporting auxiliary and causal evidence. They do not establish that CoT reveals every internal computation or that improvements transfer broadly. The main-text claim of consistently improved CIA is stronger than Table 2 supports.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Internal-strategy labels depend on imperfect interpretability tools; even perfect alignment need not receive perfect CIA. Evaluation mainly isolates basic capabilities rather than combined, long-chain reasoning (§3.1, §8).
- **Analyst inference:** A binary strategy label compresses potentially mixed or alternative reasoning pathways. Detecting a bridge entity does not alone establish its causal use (§3.2).
- **Measurement ambiguity:** The main text places the hint probe at the last hint token, while Appendix C.2 and Table 6 place it at the generated answer letter. Appendix C.5 describes behavioral answer-switch labels as probe training targets, whereas C.2 specifies probability-shift labels.
- **Auxiliary evidence is partially dependent:** Multiplication attention heads are selected using corruption labels; TwoHop tool agreement is dominated by shared negatives; the paper itself cautions that Hint-tool agreement is not independent validation (Appendices C.3–C.5).
- **Selection and reproducibility limits:** Hint CIA excludes outputs without answer letters. About half of base DeepSeek thinking outputs are excluded, and post-training changes answer coverage. Thinking-mode training uses one seed (Appendix E.2).
- **Dataset ambiguity:** Appendix A reports approximately 3,000 multiplication problems from “all unique pairs” of operands 10–99 without explaining the enumeration or filtering rule.

## Open research questions
- **Can alignment objectives coexist in mixed reasoning?** Task-dependent mechanisms and weak multiplication transfer leave this unresolved (§8). A next experiment could train on questions combining factual composition and arithmetic, measuring each strategy separately.
- **Do internal labels remain reliable after training?** Probe gaming is investigated causally, but label validity remains tool-dependent (§6, §8). Re-label post-trained outputs using fresh interventions and compare frozen versus retrained probes.
- **Can hint consideration be separated from causal reliance?** The thinking-mode judge can count discussion of a non-decisive hint as acknowledgment (Appendix E.2). Human-label consideration, rejection, and reliance separately, then compare each against hint-removal effects.
- **Are gains robust across training seeds and output failures?** Thinking-mode gains use one training seed and answer-filtered comparisons (Table 10). Repeat training across seeds and report CIA jointly with answer coverage and format failures.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the core metric and reported improvements, but measurement inconsistencies limit stronger interpretation.

Material unavailable or unresolved information includes full figure plots and prompt bodies, complete dataset counts and filtering rules, human validation of the CoT judge, independent post-training probe calibration, definitions of RS-B/DPO-A, and clarification of Table 10’s DeepSeek training setup, which the preceding prose describes only for Qwen.
