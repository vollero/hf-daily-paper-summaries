# World Models&#x27; Last Exam in Physics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08791) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08791)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across 40 controlled physics tasks and eight video models, the proposed measurement-based benchmark reports a best composite score of 57.76/100 and better human agreement than direct VLM scoring, while validating its measurement module only under controlled synthetic conditions (§§3.2–3.3).

## Problem and motivation
Visually coherent generated videos can violate physical relationships, potentially undermining their use for prediction and planning. The authors identify three evaluation gaps: VLM judgments depend on evaluator reasoning, reference comparisons can confound physical correctness with video similarity, and direct measurement benchmarks primarily cover mechanics (§1).

The paper addresses how to test observable physical relationships across more domains without requiring reference videos or simulator ground truth during model evaluation.

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 40 image–prompt tasks across nine physical categories, with explicit assumptions and observable criteria covering mechanics, optics, fluids, phase changes, electromagnetism, and surface effects (§2.2; Table 1).
- **Evaluator:** Task-specific tracking, segmentation, geometric fitting, and temporal analysis recover measurements for physical tests. Scale-canceling ratios avoid absolute calibration where applicable (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Scoring protocol:** Qwen3.6-27B screens consistency and observability. Measurements are attempted independently; the composite score is \(S=0.15C+0.85\widetilde P\), where physical scores contribute only when \(C\ge80\) and a score is recorded. Diagnostic measurements are retained (§2.4).
- **Evaluation evidence:** Eight models are compared using shared inputs and four samples per task–model pair, supplemented by synthetic measurement validation and human agreement comparisons (§§3.1–3.3).

The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation protocol; the paper does not introduce a new video generator.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best overall performance remains limited | On 1,280 videos across 40 tasks and eight models, Seedance 2.5 scores **57.76/100 composite**, versus MiniMax H3’s **54.89**; scoring uses the per-video consistency gate. | Table 2; §3.2 |
| Consistency screening does not establish physical agreement | Every free-fall video passes \(C\ge80\), but the cross-model mean is **26.61 composite** and **13.88 physical**, both on a 0–100 scale. | §3.2; Tables 4–5, P2 |
| Performance differs sharply across tasks | Communicating vessels scores **93.09–99.01 composite** across models, with all videos passing the gate. Ice containing a stone has **zero recorded physical scores** for all models and a cross-model composite mean of **3.45**. | Table 2; §3.2, P21/P26 |
| Measurement module scores prescribed synthetic behavior highly | **480 2D animations**, 12 per task, receive **97.53/100 mean physical score**, with the VLM gate bypassed. | §3.2; Figure 3 |
| Better within-task ranking agreement than Direct VLM | On **917 assessable pairs** from 320 videos, exact ordering/tie agreement is **51.58% (473 pairs)** versus **43.40% (398)** for Direct VLM; Human–Human is **56.60%**. | §3.3; Figure 4a |
| Better confirmed pairwise agreement than Direct VLM | Over **160 predefined pairs**, retaining pairs without a human majority in the denominator, agreement is **53.12% (85)** versus **45.62% (73)**; Human–Human is **58.54%**. | §3.3; Figure 4a |

These results support task-specific diagnostic value and improved agreement relative to the tested direct VLM baseline. They do not establish comprehensive physical correctness, reliable embodied planning, or robustness in complex scenes. Composite scores combine observability and physical evidence; operational zeros can indicate unresolved measurements rather than measured violations (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Tests verify only specified observable relationships under task assumptions; unmeasured properties remain unverified. For example, the collision task measures momentum agreement without separately scoring elasticity (§2.1; Appendix B, P5).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** Visibility, tracking, boundary detection, observation duration, and localization uncertainty constrain evaluation. An unresolved extraction can receive an operational zero without establishing a physical violation (§5; Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference—validation coverage:** High scores on physically prescribed synthetic animations demonstrate agreement on controlled positive cases. The supplied validation does not report systematic detection of deliberately introduced violations or realistic extraction failures (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—score sensitivity:** No sensitivity analysis is reported for the consistency threshold, composite weights, or normalization scales. Some task rules award substantial credit to incomplete behavior, such as stationary blocks receiving 0.7 on both sliding metrics (Appendix B, P6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Models receive identical inputs but produce different resolutions and frame counts; their effects on generation and measurement are not isolated (§3.1). Difficulty groups are derived from these models’ composite scores, so they are specific to this evaluation (Appendix B).
- **Human-validation limits:** Agreement remains below the Human–Human reference. Ranking analysis excludes insufficient-evidence pairs, while confirmed pairwise agreement retains unresolved human judgments in its denominator (§3.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Can the evaluator distinguish violations from extraction failures?** Positive synthetic validation leaves this unresolved. Next experiment: introduce graded physical violations alongside matched occlusion, blur, and tracking disturbances, then measure detection and abstention behavior.
2. **How stable are model rankings under scoring choices?** Threshold and normalization sensitivity are unreported. Next experiment: rescore the existing videos across alternative gates, weights, and error scales, reporting ranking changes and evidence availability.
3. **Does measurement remain reliable in more complex scenes?** The authors identify visibility and extraction robustness as constraints (§5). Next experiment: vary viewpoint, clutter, and occlusion while preserving known physical relationships.
4. **Do benchmark scores predict planning reliability?** Planning motivates the benchmark but is not evaluated (§1). Next experiment: compare physical scores with downstream prediction and planning outcomes in tasks with independently verifiable dynamics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed tasks, scoring rules, and results, but broader evaluator validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes public asset/code availability, complete generation and evaluator prompts, pilot-selection details, extraction-error rates, deliberately incorrect synthetic controls, scoring ablations, numerical confidence intervals, and exact per-video annotator assignments.
