# RLHND: Video Foundation Models as Physically Grounded Hand Trackers for Robot Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09455) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09455)


## One-sentence takeaway
RLHND adapts a video diffusion backbone to estimate bimanual hand motion, contact, and pressure from monocular video, with reported tracking and robot-learning gains whose main benchmark magnitudes are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Problem and motivation
Human videos could expand robot-training data, but their usefulness depends on reliable action labels. The authors identify two gaps: existing trackers produce unstable depth and changing hand size, and ordinary videos lack measured contact and force. Both undermine physically feasible robot actions (§1, Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Video feature extraction:** RLHND feeds clean video latents through Cosmos 3’s conditioning interface, adapting the backbone with a trainable patch embedding and LoRA. It uses deterministic clip features rather than iterative video generation (§3.1).
- **Consistent, constrained pose:** A cached MANO shape conditions tracking across a recording; anatomical axes reduce finger articulation from 45 to 29 degrees of freedom, with the thumb unconstrained. Training labels are converted through damped inverse kinematics (§3.2, Appendix A.2).
- **Separate tactile expert:** After pose training, the entire first-stage model is frozen. Bone tokens are spread to hand vertices using fixed MANO skinning weights, producing contact probabilities and distributed pressure without per-vertex attention (§§3.3–3.4, Appendix A.5).
- **Robot-learning validation:** Outputs are evaluated through dexterous-hand retargeting and Dexterous Point Policy training. This is a validation contribution; no new robot-policy architecture is established (§4.3).

These results support the authors’ account of useful pose labels and tactile predictions, but missing main tables prevent quantifying overall gains or checking every ranking. The robot comparison isolates keypoint-label quality; it does **not** isolate the benefit of automatic contact labels or predicted pressure. Training-window errors do not establish held-out policy generalization.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** External shape calibration is demonstrated only with ARCTIC ground-truth shape. Robustness to a poor initial visible clip remains untested; if no clip qualifies, inference falls back to per-clip shapes (Appendices A.1, C).
- **Author-stated:** Pressure supervision comes from one glove and one planar pad. Sensor conventions complicate broader training, and only subsets of the hand surface are sensed (Appendices A.2, C).
- **Author-stated:** Robot control uses fixed inverse kinematics, locked abduction joints, and a heuristic contact offset. Exploiting predicted pressure and transferring across different compliance remain outside scope (Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Processing is offline, the backbone is large, and dataset diversity may conceal variation across subjects, hand sizes, and skin tones (Appendix C; Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference:** Geometric contact scores validate agreement with mesh-derived labels rather than directly measured touch. The signed-distance rule labels every penetrating vertex as contact, regardless of penetration depth (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The encoder ablation changes both backbone and input design, so it does not separately establish the benefit of clean-latent conditioning (§4.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The Ethics Statement says no new human data were collected, while Appendix A.5 says the authors collected human demonstrations for robot experiments.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is the shape cache to bad initialization?** Appendix C leaves this open. Test deliberately occluded initial clips against repeated estimation and measured operator shape.
- **Does pressure transfer across sensors and surfaces?** Current supervision covers two sensing setups. Run leave-one-sensor-out evaluation after harmonizing calibration and surface labels.
- **Do inferred contact and pressure independently improve robot success?** Both deployed conditions share contact labels, and pressure-driven control is outside scope. Compare keypoint-only, binary-contact, and pressure-conditioned policies with matched training data.
- **How much does the conditioning interface itself contribute?** The reported encoder swap combines multiple changes. Compare clean-conditioning and denoising interfaces on the same backbone under matched training.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and protocols are well described, but supplied text omits numerical contents of most tables, including primary pose/tactile results, robot success rates, and inference timings. Release status, variability across repeated runs, and statistical uncertainty are also unavailable.
