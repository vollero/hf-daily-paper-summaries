# Conditional Trajectory Peaks: Single-Pass Multimodal Policies over Action Chunks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06104) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06104)


## One-sentence takeaway
CTP combines single-pass trajectory mixtures with training-time peak specialization and history-aware candidate selection, improving matched Push-T performance and reducing locally measured π₀.₅ policy latency while retaining comparable manipulation success (§IV-B, §IV-D–F).

## Problem and motivation
Robot demonstrations can contain several valid futures for the same observation. Averaging these futures can produce infeasible actions, while iterative diffusion or flow generation adds latency. Predicting several trajectories directly also leaves two problems: candidates may collapse onto the same behavior during training, and replanning may switch between incompatible behaviors during execution (§I, Fig. 1).

The authors identify a gap in jointly learning distinct, data-supported trajectory modes and preserving their behavioral identity across exchangeable candidate sets, while allowing new observations to override previous choices (§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **CTP trajectory model:** A shared network predicts complete action-chunk centers, normalized mixture probabilities, and bounded scales in one forward pass. One Gaussian-mixture component spans the whole horizon; this builds on trajectory-level mixture modeling rather than introducing mixtures themselves (§III-B, Eqs. 1–3).
- **Distribution-Aware Peak Specialization (DAPS):** Mixture likelihood supplies soft posterior responsibilities, while an overlap penalty weighted by candidate probabilities and scales discourages redundant high-probability peaks without clustering or predefined mode assignments (§III-C, Eqs. 4–8).
- **Evidence-Gated Trajectory Belief Transport (ETBT):** Geometric matching links previous unexecuted trajectory suffixes to current prefixes. Transported beliefs act as a soft prior, released when current policy evidence strongly disagrees or correspondence is unreliable (§III-E, Eqs. 9–11).

Mixture probabilities describe conditional mode mass, not execution success; scales describe normalized residual dispersion, not physical path width (§III-B).

The evidence supports improved aggregate Push-T performance, execution of both demonstrated plate modes, and lower latency in the tested local configurations. It does not establish universal superiority, statistically equivalent real-world success, or accurate recovery of demonstration mode probabilities. The diffusion timing comparison specifically uses 100 network evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** D3IL prior results are not fully protocol-matched. Published LIBERO baselines also use differing protocols; CTP and local π₀.₅ share the authors’ evaluation (§IV-C–D).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism evidence:** Incremental ablations support component utility, but do not separately isolate DAPS responsibility assignment from its overlap penalty, or ETBT correspondence from evidence gating. Direct collapse and switching metrics are absent (Table V).
- **Analyst inference—mode coverage:** The plate experiment tests a balanced two-mode distribution. Its successful outcomes establish preservation of both modes in that setting, not robustness to rare modes or distributional calibration (§IV-F1).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** The supplied setup omits numerical DAPS/ETBT hyperparameters, task-specific transforms, correspondence-normalization choices, and backbone auxiliary losses, despite referencing such choices (§III–IV-A).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Most aggregate results lack confidence intervals; bottle and pen trial counts are missing. These omissions limit assessment of small success differences (§IV-B–F).

## Open research questions
1. **Does DAPS preserve rare modes?** The explicit real-world mode test uses balanced demonstrations (§IV-F1). A next experiment could vary plate-mode imbalance and measure candidate coverage, execution frequency, and success.
2. **Does ETBT release history correctly after unexpected changes?** Evidence gating is defined, but no dedicated intervention test isolates it (§III-E, Table V). Compare full ETBT with gating disabled under sudden obstacle or goal changes, measuring switching delay and failures.
3. **How robust is the latency–success advantage to stronger iterative settings?** Push-T timing uses NFE=100, and π₀.₅ uses ten integration steps (§IV-B2, §IV-D). A matched sweep of iterative step counts and CTP candidate counts could measure success–latency trade-offs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline measurements are clearly described, but causal and generalization claims have weaker support.

Material missing information includes complete training configurations, DAPS/ETBT parameter values, detailed unified-baseline procedures, most uncertainty estimates, real-world comparison trial counts, direct mode-collapse/switching measurements, and confirmed availability of code, weights, or collected demonstrations.
