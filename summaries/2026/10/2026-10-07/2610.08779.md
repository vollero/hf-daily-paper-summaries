# ALIVE: Interaction-Aligned Object Insertion for First-Frame-Guided Video Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08779) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08779)


## One-sentence takeaway
ALIVE combines interaction-focused training pairs with optional VLM-predicted temporal guidance to improve first-frame-guided object insertion, reporting substantially stronger interaction scores than evaluated editors (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Inserted objects must respond to existing actions—moving with a grasping hand or deforming during manipulation—while preserving the surrounding video. The authors identify scarce interaction-aligned editing pairs and the need to infer an absent object’s behavior from source actions as the main gaps (§1). An edited first frame establishes appearance and placement but does not specify subsequent interactions.

## Approach and contributions
- **Interaction-focused data:** 35,800 object-absent/object-present pairs combine aligned 3D renders, generated videos, real interaction videos, and reversed ROSE removal pairs. Multi-level annotations range from object identity to temporally localized interaction descriptions (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Interaction-guided editor:** A LoRA-adapted LTX-2.5 diffusion editor concatenates source and noisy target latents, keeps the edited first-frame condition clean, and routes chunk-specific text through temporal-overlap-weighted cross-attention (§4.2).
- **Automatic guidance:** A LoRA-adapted Qwen3.6-27B predicts interaction descriptions and intervals from source frames, the edited first frame, and object-only text; no additional user interaction descriptions are required (§4.3).
- **Benchmark:** ALIVE-interaction evaluates coherent object responses alongside source preservation and visual quality, supplemented by a general insertion benchmark (§3.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large interaction-editing advantage | On 128 ALIVE-interaction cases, object-only ALIVE scores **86.71 vs. 60.24** for the strongest evaluated baseline on VLM Overall, scaled 0–100. | §§3.3, 5.2; Table 1 caption |
| General insertion also improves | On 103 ROSE/EffectErase cases, Overall is **92.28 vs. 88.39** for the strongest evaluated baseline. | §§3.3, 5.2 |
| Better interaction motion | ALIVE scores **86.13 vs. Señorita’s 36.52** on interaction-benchmark motion/propagation, scaled 0–100. | §5.2 |
| Predicted guidance has a modest, task-dependent benefit | Predicted P4 raises interaction Overall **86.71 → 87.66**, but lowers general insertion Overall **92.28 → 91.71**. Both variants share editor weights and original user inputs. | §§5.1–5.2 |
| Interaction data matters | With P1, rank-128 LoRA, and 5,000 training updates, mixed-source training scores **82.35 vs. ROSE-only 60.12** on interaction Overall. | §5.3.1 |
| Detailed temporal annotations perform best | With editor, visual inputs, and seeds fixed, interaction Overall is **85.38 / 86.71 / 87.37 / 88.69** for P0/P1/annotated P3/annotated P4. | §5.3.2 |
| Guided inference adds cost | Across six profiled videos on one A100 80GB, P1 editing averages **154.16 s**; predicted-P4 editing takes **184.50 s**, plus separately measured VLM prediction of **22.75 s**. Outputs are 768 × 512 × 81 with 30 denoising steps. | Appendix A.2, Table 4 |

These author-reported results support the value of interaction-focused training and a smaller additional benefit from predicted guidance. Overall uses a reference-aware VLM judge examining sampled frames, rather than direct physical measurements (§5.1; Appendix A.4). The evidence does not establish physically correct dynamics, statistical significance, or superiority over every editing interface. Full numerical bodies of Tables 1–3 are absent from the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Inputs leave geometry, contact, forces, and material properties ambiguous. Guidance errors can produce incorrect behavior; complex cloth deformation and rapid, large-angle rotations remain difficult (§5.3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Sparse frame judging may miss brief contact failures or temporal artifacts. Human agreement and judge repeatability are not reported in the supplied text (§5.1; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference:** P4 comparisons change both description structure and temporal routing; the supplied results do not isolate routing’s contribution (§§4.2, 5.3.2).
- **Comparison scope:** Instruction-only and mask-guided configurations are excluded for stated conditioning differences; AnyV2V and PropFly receive additional interaction descriptions (§5.1).
- **Reproducibility:** Training details are substantial, but availability of data, code, and checkpoints is not established.

## Open research questions
- **Does temporal routing itself drive P4’s benefit?** Description format and routing change together. Compare identical P4 text with interval routing versus shared clip-level conditioning.
- **How reliable are the small predicted-guidance gains?** No uncertainty estimates or human validation are supplied. Repeat generation across seeds and compare blinded human ratings with VLM scores.
- **Can explicit physical conditions resolve ambiguous responses?** The authors identify missing contact and material information. Test controlled stiffness/contact annotations on deformation and rotation cases.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline findings are clearly described, but the supplied text omits most table bodies, numerical dimension weights, uncertainty estimates, human validation, subset-level results, and confirmed asset-release information.
