# MEND: RL For Flow Models via Proximal Velocity Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05954) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05954)


## One-sentence takeaway
MEND selects reward-improving latent targets before velocity regression, reporting better equal-budget text-to-image reward scores than ReFL and DiffusionNFT while guaranteeing improvements only for selected targets, not the trained model.

## Problem and motivation
Reward post-training should improve generated images without unnecessarily changing strong samples or reducing diversity. The authors argue that sample reweighting can suppress difficult modes, while unchecked reward-gradient updates move samples without testing whether their gains justify their displacement (§1).

The identified gap is a per-sample decision about **whether to move and how far**, beyond distribution-level regularization.

## Approach and contributions
- **Verified target construction:** Within each prompt group, MEND caps rewards using a quantile and global floor. Below-cap samples receive proposals along their normalized reward gradient. A proposal is accepted only when its capped reward gain exceeds a quadratic displacement cost; staying unchanged is always an option (§2, Eqs. 1–3).
- **Proximal velocity matching:** The model regresses a stored clean prediction toward the behavior prediction plus the accepted displacement. Kept targets retain the behavior prediction. Training uses a lagged behavior adapter, without a KL term, frozen reference, advantage weights, or sampler backpropagation (§2, Eqs. 4–5).
- **Scoped theoretical results:** Accepted targets have bounded displacement and positive reward gain. At the behavior parameters, the regression gradient uses only accepted samples’ normalized reward gradients, scaled by selected step lengths (§3, Propositions 1–2).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The authors test multiple rewards, three image backbones, matched-budget baselines, diversity controls, and over-optimization behavior (§§4.1–4.5; Appendices E–F).

The evidence supports improvements in learned reward metrics under the reported settings. It does not establish statistical superiority over every baseline, direct human preference gains, or universal diversity preservation. The headline Flow-GRPO comparison matches base distance and evaluation guidance, but training guidance and budgets differ; Protocol O provides the matched-budget comparison (Appendix B.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** MEND requires differentiable rewards. Guarantees concern each round’s targets, with no guarantee for finite optimizer steps, generated endpoints, held-out metrics, or complete training runs (§6; Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Reward over-optimization remains possible; held-out scores can decline within the standard budget, and incorrect rewards eventually certify harmful moves (Appendices E.3, F.4).
- **Observed implementation gap:** Kept seeds can move as much as accepted seeds after model updates. Thus, “kept” describes targets rather than invariant generated images (Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Robustness evidence is limited by single-seed ablation arms and mostly single-seed reward runs. The reported repeat launch checks only a limited configuration (Tables 12–13; Figure 16 caption).
- **Analyst inference:** Equal-budget PickScore gains are supported by means, but missing baseline uncertainty prevents a statistical comparison. Several supplied tables contain captions without their numerical bodies.
- Qualitative examples are selected by eye; an uncurated sheet is also described, but the supplied text does not expose the image contents (Appendix G).

## Open research questions
- **Can verification constrain actual model updates?** Current guarantees stop at targets, and endpoint movements need not follow certified directions. Next experiment: compare certified and realized displacements across learning rates and regression constraints (Appendices C, F.1).
- **Can held-out monitoring reduce reward exploitation?** Single-reward runs show declines despite valid target checks. Next experiment: compare a predefined held-out stopping rule with fixed-budget training across rewards and repeated seeds (Appendices E.1, E.3).
- **How reliable is the reward–diversity tradeoff?** Main component ablations use one training seed at the development budget. Next experiment: repeat cap, price, and fixed-step controls at full budget, reporting paired uncertainty and both diversity and reward (Tables 12–13).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and guarantee boundaries are explicit, but some empirical comparisons are incompletely represented.

Material missing information includes numerical bodies of several result tables, matched-baseline confidence intervals, broad training-seed replication, direct human evaluations, code-release contents, and baseline compute costs needed to establish wall-clock efficiency.
