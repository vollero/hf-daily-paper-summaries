# Learning Functional Subspaces for Neural Network Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40127) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40127)


## One-sentence takeaway
Learning discarded subspaces jointly against a frozen model’s outputs improves aggressive low-rank compression in the reported language and vision evaluations, while shared factors improve measured decoding speed and analytically estimated cache capacity (§§3.1–3.3).

## Problem and motivation
Low-rank factorization reduces transformer memory and compute using dense matrix operations. The authors argue that existing activation-energy, reconstruction, and local loss-surrogate criteria choose directions independently and fail to capture downstream error propagation under aggressive compression. Their proposed gap is selecting *which directions to remove* directly through the full network’s objective (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Learned functional subspaces:** LSP jointly optimizes orthogonal projectors while freezing pretrained weights. QR enforces orthonormality; the default objective distills dense-model outputs, while LSP T uses the original task loss. Compatible Q/K/V and gate/up layers share projectors (§§2.1, 2.3).
- **Initialization and rank allocation:** Whitened SVD initializes discarded directions, with an orthogonal recast and extension to tied groups. Isolated truncations are ranked by measured output-KL cost per parameter saved. NoLSP preserves initialization, ranks, and ties without training, isolating the learning stage (§2.2).
- **Deployment:** Projectors merge into ordinary low-rank factors. Shared input factors reduce matrix multiplications and permit a single latent K/V cache when attention projections are tied on their input side (§2.3; Appendix A.6).

The novelty claimed is end-to-end learning of the removed subspace; whitening, rank allocation, and factor sharing build on earlier approaches (§§1, 4).

The NoLSP comparison supports a substantial benefit from projector learning at fixed initialization and allocation. The evaluations also support benefits beyond calibration-domain perplexity. They do not establish universal superiority, practical latent-cache throughput, or reliable behavior at the analytically attainable context lengths.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors:** Isolated-unit KL allocation omits interactions and has no global-optimality guarantee; uniform allocation sometimes performs better. Compression requires additional measurement and training cost (Appendix A.7).
- **Authors:** Cache benefits depend on input-side K/V tying. Qwen3-4B’s preferred output-side configuration trades cache efficiency for accuracy (Appendices A.7, C, E).
- **Authors:** Latent caching requires attention implementation changes, and rotary keys must be reconstructed; the reported decoding speeds use a full-width cache (§3.3; Appendix A.6).
- **Authors:** Compression loss may disproportionately affect underrepresented populations, domains, or rare behaviors (Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference:** Long-context memory capacity does not establish long-context quality; §3.3 explicitly notes capacity beyond Llama-2-7B’s training context.
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation breadth is limited to the reported decoder models and one ViT. Full numerical table bodies and seed deviations are mostly absent from the retrieved text, restricting independent assessment.
- **Evidence inconsistency:** §3.4 generalizes attention-versus-MLP allocation across LLMs, but Appendix D explicitly describes OPT-125M Q/K/V as least compressed; that generalization should be treated cautiously.

## Open research questions
- **Can interaction-aware allocation improve compression?** Isolated costs omit joint effects (Appendices A.7, B). Next experiment: compare greedy allocation with iterative joint-KL refinement at matched parameter and compression-time budgets.
- **Do latent-cache capacity gains translate into useful deployment gains?** Memory is estimated and speed uses full-width caching (Appendix E). Next experiment: implement latent attention and measure memory, latency, and task quality across context lengths.
- **How robust is functional-subspace learning to calibration coverage?** Vision diversity helps, while rare-behavior loss remains acknowledged (§3.2; Ethics Statement). Next experiment: vary calibration composition at fixed size and evaluate domain and subgroup retention.
