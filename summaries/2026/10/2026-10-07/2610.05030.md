# EVISKILL: Grounding Skill Evolution in Replayable Evidence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05030) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05030)


## One-sentence takeaway
EviSkill links procedural skill edits to replayable execution evidence and preserves locally supported changes across update attempts, with reported improvements across three interactive benchmarks and six agent backbones (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Trajectory-derived skill revisions can sound plausible without correcting agent behavior, and rejecting a complete revision can discard useful constituent edits. The authors identify a gap in preserving each edit’s execution context and reconsidering its support over time (§§1–2). This matters because persistent procedural guidance can repeatedly propagate an incorrect or overly specific rule.

## Approach and contributions
The framework evolves external Markdown skills without updating model parameters through three linked mechanisms:

- **Evidence-linked synthesis:** Replayable Evidence Cards retain proposed corrections, persistent identifiers, and supporting task/trajectory ranges. Grounding verification checks those references before cards are grouped for localized edit synthesis (§3.1; Appendix C.2).
- **Behavioral verification:** The agent reconstructs a source context and re-executes the relevant segment under an edited skill. An LLM evaluator accepts, rejects, or requests one feedback-guided revision followed by another replay (§3.2; Appendix C.1).
- **Cross-epoch refinement:** A Working Skill includes provisional edits, while a separate Validated Skill changes only after improved validation accuracy. Rejected revisions can yield individually replay-supported edits for later refinement; unresolved cards and adjacent-epoch comparisons also persist (§3.3).

The distinctive contribution is this combined separation of edit provenance, local behavioral support, and global revision acceptance. The supplied related-work discussion acknowledges that SkillOpt already retains rejected edits as feedback, so retention alone is not established as novel (Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

All numbers below are author-reported; several referenced tables and figures have captions and prose summaries but no underlying cells or plotted values in the supplied text.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad test-performance gains | Across 18 benchmark–backbone settings, EviSkill reports best task-success accuracy in 14 and top-two accuracy in 16; mean gain over NoSkill is **17.93 percentage points**. | §4.2, Obs. 1; Tables 1/4 referenced |
| Evolution can degrade an initial skill | ScienceWorld, GPT-5.5: EviSkill **83.41%**, fixed LLM Skill **76.78%**, Trace2Skill **65.40%**, EvoSkill **54.50%**, SkillGrad **67.30%** task-success accuracy. | §4.2, Obs. 2 |
| Replay contributes to accuracy | Removing replay lowers accuracy averaged over three GPT backbones by **2.49 pp** on ALFWorld, **4.56 pp** on AppWorld, and **2.85 pp** on ScienceWorld. | §4.3; Figure 5 |
| Both persistence pathways contribute | ALFWorld, averaged over three Qwen backbones: Cards Only and Edits Only gain **1.74 pp** and **2.24 pp** over w/o Cross-Epoch; Full reaches **88.06%**, exceeding those variants by **1.74 pp** and **1.24 pp**. | Appendix E.2; Table 5 |
| Replay filters and repairs proposals | Across 18 runs/72 epochs, initial reflection-or-rejection rates are **20.7%**, **41.8%**, and **42.5%** for ALFWorld, AppWorld, and ScienceWorld. Reflected edits later accepted: **1/1**, **13/16**, and **49/54**, respectively. | §4.4.1; Figure 6 |
| Locally supported edits survive global rejection | Of **155** consolidated edits from rejected revisions, **128** receive post-rejection replay, **96** pass, and **70/96 (72.9%)** subsequently enter a globally accepted skill. | Appendix E.5; Figure 12 |
| Evidence is reused | Of **3,361** unique cards across the formal runs, **23.0%** enter Evidence Windows in multiple epochs. | Appendix E.5; Figure 11 |
| Local replay reduces measured workload | Evidence-window replay uses **18,007** executed steps versus **53,242** full-source-trajectory steps (**66.18%** reduction); post-rejection replay uses **1,205** versus **3,863** (**68.81%** reduction). | Appendix F.2; Table 9 |

The ablations support contributions from replay and persistence in these settings. Edit promotion and card reuse demonstrate that the mechanisms operate, but do not independently establish each retained edit’s causal benefit. Local replay acceptance is explicitly distinct from global validation acceptance. Step reductions also do not establish lower total runtime or monetary cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified scope:** Relative gains are less consistent on AppWorld. Some retained edits remain unpromoted when the four-epoch budget ends (§4.2; §4.4.2).
- **Author-qualified interpretation:** Cross-task evidence breadth does not establish unseen-task generalization; replay recovery rates concern only edits selected for reflection (§4.4.1; Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference:** All extractors, editors, and evaluators use GPT-5.5, including runs with Qwen action agents. Backbone breadth therefore does not establish evolution using only the smaller action models (Appendix C.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Bounded segment replay may miss failures outside the selected contexts or interactions introduced during consolidation. Global validation offers broader screening, but the supplied text provides no quantified replay-judge reliability (§3.2; Appendix C.5).
- **Reporting gap:** No uncertainty intervals, repeated-seed variation, or complete cost accounting are supplied. Referenced result tables lack their cells, limiting independent verification.

## Open research questions
- **How reliable are replay judgments?** No human agreement or evaluator-calibration results are supplied. Compare LLM decisions against blinded human assessments and longer rollouts.
- **Does the framework improve performance at equal total cost?** Rollout-normalized gains and replay-step counts omit full computation costs. Compare methods under matched token, environment-action, and monetary budgets.
- **Does provisional retention remain useful over longer horizons?** The study ends after four epochs with unresolved edits. Extend evolution and track promotion, regression, and evidence staleness.
- **Which auxiliary-model capabilities are necessary?** The evolution roles use one fixed model. Vary extractor, editor, and evaluator models independently while holding action agents and tasks fixed.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and narrative results are detailed, but complete numerical table cells, figure values, statistical uncertainty, seed variation, replay-judge validation, and total resource costs are unavailable. Code availability is author-stated and was not independently checked.
