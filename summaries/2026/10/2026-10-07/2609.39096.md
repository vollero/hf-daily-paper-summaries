# DeCoPrune: Efficient KV-Cache Pruning for Autoregressive Video Diffusion via Denoising Consistency

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39096) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39096)


## One-sentence takeaway
On CMBench with LingBot World v2, DeCoPrune retains tokens with large intermediate-to-final denoising discrepancies, achieving 0.6701 DINO versus FullKV’s 0.6803 while reducing cumulative historical KV token counts by 85.43% and speeding continuation generation by 4.14× (§§1, 4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Autoregressive video diffusion accumulates historical KV entries, increasing memory and attention costs. Removing history can lose evidence needed to reproduce previously seen objects or scenes. The authors argue that fixed windows, local attention signals, and adjacent-frame similarity do not directly assess whether new content is already predictable from the retained context (§1).

They also identify an evaluation gap: general video quality can remain stable despite declining historical recall, motivating targeted, reference-grounded continuation tasks (§1; Fig. 6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Denoising-based retention:** DeCoPrune computes token-wise mean squared error between an intermediate clean prediction and the same trajectory’s final output. It retains high-discrepancy tokens, treating discrepancy as a proxy for information missing from existing context. Online scoring requires no additional model evaluations or training (§3.1, Eq. 1).
- **Physical cache compression:** A shared mask compacts KVs across layers after chunks leave a protected recent window; the initial sink chunk remains dense. Observed prefixes require re-noising and additional context-conditioned predictions because their original trajectories are unavailable (§§3.1–3.3).
- **CMBench:** The benchmark introduces Reappear and Revisit tasks testing recovery of specific observed entities or scene anchors, rather than merely plausible replacements (§3.2).
- **Supporting extensions:** Temporal RoPE re-indexing improves access to distant retained evidence. DeCoPrune–HS combines the new selection criterion with an existing ForcingKV head partition; these components are adaptations rather than wholly new selection mechanisms (§3.3; Apps. A–B).

These results support the retention heuristic under the tested settings, especially through matched-budget random and reverse controls. They do not establish that discrepancy universally measures future usefulness. PR measures cumulative historical token exposure, **not peak memory savings or total computation**; speedup excludes prefix processing (§3.2). The separate RoPE diagnostic should not be conflated with the main comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** Retained memory continues growing; backbone generation errors complicate attribution of recall failures; and future-agnostic scoring may discard rare, easily denoised details requested later (App. F).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Generalization remains uncertain: minute-scale pruning results primarily use one backbone, while the additional LongLive experiment uses shorter contexts (§4.1; App. C.1).
- Benchmark coverage favors generated footage; the real-video analysis uses one seed and its numerical table is absent from the supplied text (§3.2; App. D).
- Maximum-over-frames target similarity can reward a single successful appearance without establishing sustained identity or complete scene reconstruction. Revisit evaluates a salient object anchor (§3.2, Eq. 3).
- Table extraction limits verification: Table 1’s numerical body is missing, and RoPE/token-selection table references are inconsistent. The displayed FullKV RoPE delta also does not exactly match its displayed endpoints (§4.3).

## Open research questions
- **Can memory be strictly bounded without losing recall?** Cache growth remains unresolved (App. F). Test capped budgets over progressively longer rollouts against the current threshold policy.
- **Can future relevance rescue easy-to-denoise details?** The authors identify this failure risk (App. F). Construct delayed prompts targeting such details and compare discrepancy-only selection with task-aware retention.
- **How broadly does the criterion transfer?** Other-backbone pruning evidence is limited to shorter contexts (App. C.1). Run matched-budget comparisons across backbones, context lengths, and denoising schedules.
- **Does target similarity reflect persistent recall?** The metric selects the best frame (§3.2). Compare it with human identity judgments and sustained temporal-consistency measurements.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline results are explicit, but several numerical tables are missing.
