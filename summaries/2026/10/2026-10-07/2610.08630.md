# Towards In-Parameter Memory Augmentation for Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08630) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08630)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey defines deployment-time parametric memory through acquisition and composition operators and organizes existing methods by placement and acquisition timing, but provides qualitative comparisons rather than measured performance gains (§§2–5, 8).

## Problem and motivation
LLMs need to incorporate documents, new facts, preferences, and interaction experience after pretraining. The authors argue that repeatedly presenting this memory as text consumes context capacity and increases inference cost (§1).

The identified gap is a focused framework for independently acquired, memory-bearing parameter objects that enter the forward pass at deployment, distinguished from ordinary KV caches and permanently merged fine-tuning (§§1, 2.3).

## Approach and contributions
This is a survey, with three principal contributions:

- **Operator formulation:** a memory object \(\phi\), acquisition operator \(\mathcal A\), and deployment composition operator \(\mathcal C\), supporting both one-shot creation and stateful updates (§2.2; Figure 2).
- **Taxonomy and scope:** classify methods by embedding, attention, FFN, or hybrid placement and by online or offline acquisition; require parametric form, independent acquisition, and distinct deployment augmentation (§§2.3–3; Tables 1–2).
- **Comparative synthesis:** catalog existing methods and deployment properties, then identify interference, safety, hybrid textual memory, and self-improvement research directions (§§4–6; Tables 3–4).

The contribution is an organizing framework; the paper does not introduce a new trained memory mechanism or empirical benchmark.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| IPM inclusion depends on deployment lifecycle, beyond tensor form. | Pluggable PEFT passes the scope tests; ordinary KV caches lack independent acquisition; full-parameter SFT lacks distinct deployment augmentation. | §2.3; Table 1 |
| Placement and acquisition timing separate otherwise similar memory objects. | Adapters, prompts, and memory banks appear in different placement and timing classes. | §§3–4; Tables 2–3 |
| The authors associate online acquisition with immediate adaptation and offline acquisition with persistent memory. | Table 4 assigns online objects per-request or per-token lifecycles and offline objects predeployment, persistent lifecycles. | §5; Table 4 |
| Cost comparisons are qualitative. | Table 4 assigns medium writes to per-request online objects, low writes to per-token objects, high writes to offline objects, and low reads throughout. These are inferred bands. | §§5, 8; Table 4 |
| Textual memory retains practical advantages for provenance and revision. | Authors favor ICL/RAG when exact quotation, targeted deletion, or human revision is required. | §5.1 |

The evidence establishes a conceptual taxonomy and mechanism-based synthesis. It does **not** establish measured latency savings, accuracy improvements, retention rankings, or general superiority over ICL: no numerical evaluation is supplied (§8).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** coverage is restricted to text modality, excludes memory designs within MLE, MLA, and mHC architectures, and lacks comparable numerical cost or retention measurements (§8).

**Analyst observations:**

- Acquisition timing is ambiguous in some descriptions: §4.2 calls certain KV-bank methods offline despite saying they can encode new sentences on the fly, whereas §3.2 defines timing by when the deployed object is formed.
- §3.2 describes offline augmentation operators as training processes, conflicting with §2.2’s definition of augmentation as inference-time composition.
- The stated text-only scope includes a CLIP prompt-adaptation example (§4.1), leaving the modality boundary unclear.
- Table 4’s uniformly low read-cost labels offer little basis for ranking deployment efficiency without measured workloads (§5; Table 4).

## Open research questions
- **When does IPM amortize its acquisition cost relative to textual memory?** The survey lacks numerical comparisons (§8). A next experiment could vary document length and query reuse while measuring total acquisition-plus-inference cost and answer quality.
- **How can fixed-capacity memory retain conflicting sequential updates?** The authors identify unresolved conflict detection and allocation (§6). Compare write gates and allocation policies on increasingly long update streams, measuring retention and general capability.
- **Can provenance-aware writes prevent persistent memory poisoning?** Safety-aware acquisition remains open (§6). Test provenance-conditioned gates against untrusted memory sources, measuring attack persistence and useful knowledge uptake.
- **Which information should remain textual rather than become parametric?** Joint IPM–ICL selection is proposed but unevaluated (§6). Compare routing policies on tasks requiring factual answers, exact quotations, and deletion.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the survey’s framing and stated limitations; **medium** for consistent application of its classifications.

Material missing information includes numerical performance and cost measurements, shared evaluation datasets, hardware settings, uncertainty estimates, reproducible cost-band criteria, a systematic literature-selection protocol, and clarification of the noted scope and timing ambiguities.
