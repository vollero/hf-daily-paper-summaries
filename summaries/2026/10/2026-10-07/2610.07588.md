# Personal-Agent Mediated Recommendation with Cross-Platform User History

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07588) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07588)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that PAMO improves personal-agent recommendation mediation over matched outcome-only RL across five test datasets by combining cross-platform history dependence with value-constrained advantage allocation, although the supplied text omits the numerical comparison tables (§§5–6.2).

## Problem and motivation
A user-side agent can access cross-platform history unavailable to a platform, but the platform’s ranking contains population evidence unavailable to the agent. Changing that ranking can rescue a missed target or remove a correctly recommended one. The authors identify a gap in platform-side agent benchmarks and outcome-only training: neither directly addresses when personal history justifies overriding an existing strong ranking (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task formulation:** Personal-Agent Mediated Recommendation formalizes selective revision of a frozen platform proposal, with rescue and harmful-override outcomes measured relative to that proposal (§3).
- **Benchmark:** MediateRec separates platform-local information from agent-only cross-platform history. It combines four Amazon category proxy platforms with an external OpenPlay test linking Steam, Nintendo, and Xbox records (§4). Release is conditional on internal approval (§1, footnote).
- **Training method:** PAMO compares the likelihood of each sampled visible rationale with and without cross-platform history. It uses that dependence score to redistribute rank-aware GRPO advantages among platform-disagreeing responses, subject to a floor on average direction-aligned NDCG magnitude. It adds one masked scoring pass during training and no inference component (§§5.1–5.3).
- **Theory:** The authors prove cutoff-level advantage-mass preservation and local optimality of the allocation direction for fixed sampled responses; these results do not guarantee global policy optimality or held-out improvement (§5.4).

The supplied prose supports the reported direction of improvements and describes a closely matched RL comparison. It does not expose effect sizes, uncertainty, or significance for the main comparisons. Rationale annotation describes expressed evidence; it does not independently establish causal reliance on that evidence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Amazon categories approximate independent platforms; external validation uses only Steam as the target, with Xbox history limited to genres and mobile activity omitted (Appendix A.5).
- **Author-stated construction caveats:** Amazon catalogs and popularity counts are not temporally restricted, and fixed platform assignment order can favor earlier categories (Appendices A.1, A.5).
- **Author-stated signal/theory limits:** Rationale likelihood contrast measures model-relative history dependence, not correctness or usefulness; allocation guarantees concern fixed rollout groups (§§5.1, 5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Target-inserted candidate sets establish candidate-conditioned mediation rather than retrieval quality or deployment performance. Single-target offline metrics leave broader user satisfaction unestablished (§3.2; Appendix A.5).
- **Evidence availability:** Numerical Tables 2–3, plotted values, actual prompt text, repeated-run uncertainty, and measured computational overhead are unavailable in the supplied text. Benchmark release remains conditional (§1).

## Open research questions
- **Does PAMO work with realistic retrieval failures?** Every evaluated candidate set contains the target. Next experiment: evaluate larger retrieved pools where relevant items may be absent (Appendix A.5).
- **Does transfer extend beyond Steam?** Real-service testing has one target platform. Next experiment: hold out Nintendo or another linked service as the target without adaptation (§4.1.2).
- **Does rationale dependence track useful decision dependence?** The support score measures history sensitivity, and rescue analysis uses visible rationales. Next experiment: perturb specific supporting records and measure slate changes against human evidence judgments (§§5.1, 6.5).
- **How does platform strength affect rescue–harm balance?** Evaluation uses one frozen SASRec–Sonnet construction. Next experiment: vary platform ranker quality while holding episodes and agent training fixed (§4.2.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method, scope, and theoretical claims are well specified; empirical magnitude remains uncertain because main result tables and figure values are missing. Material gaps include exact PAMO–GRPO differences, statistical uncertainty, complete prompts, measured training cost, and confirmed availability of benchmark artifacts and trained checkpoints.
