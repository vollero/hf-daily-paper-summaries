# We Query, Therefore We Compute: On Oracle Computation beyond the Machine, with an Application to Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09243) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09243)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors construct an oracle-augmented abstract machine that unifies Agents and Workflows and derives storage and control invariants, then describe a gem5 prototype whose detailed evaluation is unavailable in the truncated source.

## Problem and motivation
Agent harnesses borrow operating-system ideas such as caching, scheduling and isolation, but the authors argue that these mechanisms lack a shared foundation for stating and comparing guarantees. Agents and Workflows are also commonly studied separately despite overlapping execution patterns (§1.1).

The proposed foundation aims to explain what makes a harness an operating system for model-driven tasks, relate task histories to model caches, and identify guarantees that survive arbitrary Oracle answers.

## Approach and contributions
The authors present **one construction in three parts** (§1.4):

1. **Oracle-augmented machine:** Extend a Turing-complete two-stack pushdown automaton with an instruction that queries an entire stack and appends the answer. Append-only interaction produces an autoregressive history without assuming an autoregressive Oracle internally (§§2.3–2.5).
2. **Derived operating-system structure:** Use a call-by-push-value fragment to distinguish data from suspended computations, then impose storage and transition symmetry breakings. These yield task confinement, a fixed service entry and data-only answers to privileged code. Agents and Workflows become different placements of task programs (§§3.1–3.5).
3. **Computer realization:** Construction V maps stacks into memory segments and introduces an Oracle instruction and mode. ArchNights extends RISC-V and supplies a Linux-style kernel; ArchNights-SE runs in gem5 syscall-emulation mode (§§1.2–1.4, summarizing §§5–6).

The authors explicitly classify cache, scheduling and safety analyses as **instances of the construction**, rather than additional contributions (§1.4). Their novelty claim concerns the particular statements derived together on this machine; they do not claim that other formalisms could never express them (§1.2).

The strongest directly inspectable evidence is the machine definitions and proofs in §§2–3. Cache and scheduling findings are available mainly through introductory summaries; empirical comparability is an author claim without inspectable results here. Background-study numbers in the appendices are not evaluations of this paper’s method.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The prototype has no refinement proof and receives no security guarantee (§1.4). Kernel services and scheduling require their own verification (§3.4).
- **Author-stated:** The base construction is synchronous, without concurrency, fault tolerance or recovery; no time or space bound is proved (§2.5). A transition budget does not bound a slow or stalled Oracle call (§3.4).
- **Author-stated:** Cache results depend on named codec and execution assumptions. The Lynchpin hypothesis remains a working hypothesis, and semantic delegation’s benefit is a quantitative open question (§1.4; Appendix E.8).
- **Author-stated:** The contract is for one Oracle and one program; a privilege tower does not inherit a composition theorem, and mixed-trust Oracle combinations remain open (Appendix E.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Structural confinement provides a foundation for mediation, but does not establish that tool handlers enforce an intended policy, given the explicit separation between machine guarantees and service verification (§3.4).
- **Source limitation:** The supplied text omits most of §§4–8 and substantial appendices. Missing evaluation details prevent assessment of effect sizes, variability, overhead or ablations; their absence here does not establish their absence from the full paper.

## Open research questions
1. **Does ArchNights faithfully implement the abstract contract?** The authors provide no refinement proof (§1.4). A next step is to formalize implementation-to-machine simulation and check fault and budget paths against K1–K5.
2. **When does semantic delegation improve correctness and cost?** Appendix E.8 identifies shorter histories versus larger operator declarations as an unresolved tradeoff. Compare delegated and explicit execution while varying catalogue size and history length, measuring correct calls, task success and token cost.
3. **How do guarantees compose across multiple Oracles and asynchronous execution?** Appendices E.7–E.9 leave these extensions open. Construct a mixed-trust asynchronous machine and test candidate invariants under delayed replies, cancellation and overlapping calls.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied definitions and proofs support the core theoretical account, but the source is substantially truncated and does not permit complete coverage.

Unavailable material includes detailed cache/scheduling derivations, full realization semantics, benchmark scores and metrics, trial counts and uncertainty, runtime overhead, evaluation ablations, and a confirmed release location for ArchNights-SE.
