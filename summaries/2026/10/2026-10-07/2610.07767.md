# TRACE: Rollout-Guided Quantization-Aware Training for FP4 Reinforcement Learning of MoE Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07767) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07767)


## One-sentence takeaway
TRACE guides training-side FP4 rounding with compact rollout-side records, achieving task scores close to BF16 rollout across four tested MoE models and up to 5.4× decoding throughput on a specified GB200 setup (§§4.1–4.2).

## Problem and motivation
FP4 rollout can reduce RL generation costs, but small activation differences between training and rollout can cross rounding boundaries and amplify policy mismatch, potentially destabilizing training. The authors identify a gap in prior approaches: reducing each execution path’s quantization error independently does not directly minimize disagreement between the two quantized paths (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Rollout-guided QAT:** Record rollout-side quantization outcomes for routed-expert activations and KV states. During training, use rollout scales and choose the neighboring FP4 codeword closest to the rollout reference. With exact information, this cannot increase local discrepancy relative to nearest rounding under the same scale and candidate set (§3.1, Equations 2–3).
- **Compact guidance caching:** Retain one mantissa bit plus scale information from the latter half of layers. Training reconstructs an approximate reference using these bits and the current activation; it does not recover the exact rollout codeword (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Empirical validation:** Compare joint FP4 weight/activation/KV rollout against BF16, FP4 RL baselines, and post-hoc quantization across reasoning, coding, and long-horizon tasks, with format and caching ablations (§4).

These results support the authors’ claim that TRACE controls mismatch while retaining useful FP4 efficiency in the tested settings. They establish close point estimates rather than statistical equivalence to BF16. The 5.4× result concerns decoding throughput, not end-to-end RL acceleration; the post-hoc comparison evaluates different training regimes rather than isolating rounding guidance alone.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The exact-information guarantee is local; it does not guarantee decreasing full-network discrepancy or policy divergence (§3.1).
- **Author-stated:** Compact reconstruction is approximate and uses the current training activation’s sign. TRACE also leaves underlying activation differences from policy staleness intact (Appendix A.1; §4).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains bounded by four Qwen MoE models, the specified GRPO/R3 stack, and selective quantization. Dense models, other model families, and broader module quantization are not evaluated (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty intervals or variation across independent RL training seeds are supplied. Repeated task evaluations therefore do not establish statistical significance or training reproducibility (§4; Tables 1–7).
- **Comparison caveat:** Table 3 quotes some isolated-quantization baselines from QUADS; Table 6 selects SC by benchmark-average checkpoint performance. These comparison procedures deserve attention.
- **Efficiency caveat:** Appendix A.4’s Flash-Next throughput comparison uses FP8/BF16 as its reference; the 128K measurement is a single run and includes increased concurrency from KV capacity.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is compressed guidance to policy staleness?** TRACE acknowledges residual staleness differences but supplies no staleness sweep. Vary asynchronous policy lag and measure codeword reconstruction errors, policy mismatch, and task scores.
- **Is deeper-layer guidance consistently the best allocation?** Table 5 varies coverage, but does not compare equal-budget shallow or distributed selections. Test layer-selection strategies at matched cache sizes.
- **How much does TRACE depend on routing replay?** All reported implementations use R3 (§4). A factorial TRACE/R3 ablation could separate routing alignment from quantization alignment.
- **Does decoding acceleration yield a substantial total training saving?** The main efficiency text gives guidance overhead relative to vanilla FP4, but no numerical BF16 end-to-end saving. Measure complete training time against BF16, including storage I/O, across hardware and sequence lengths.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported point estimates, but broader reliability remains uncertain.
