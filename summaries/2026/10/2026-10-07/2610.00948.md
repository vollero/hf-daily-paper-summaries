# GUI-HARVEST: Self-Improving GUI Agents through Evidence-Driven Harness Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00948) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00948)


## One-sentence takeaway
GUI-HARVEST uses repeated screenshot-grounded executions to edit and validate frozen-model GUI harnesses, improving held-out OSWorld scores across six backbones and transferring gains to WindowsAgentArena (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
GUI performance depends on the executable harness that constructs observations, dispatches actions, and controls recovery and termination. The authors identify three gaps in generic harness optimization: textual traces can disagree with visible effects, repeated executions can produce different outcomes, and task-specific fixes need evidence that they address recurring failures and generalize (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Repeated multimodal diagnosis:** An Evidence Analyst aligns model intent, executed actions, and before/after screenshots, comparing failed runs with alternative runs of the same task. Deterministic checks verify citations and mechanical facts; semantic interpretations remain model assessments (§3.2).
- **Cross-task code adaptation:** A Clusterer groups findings into recurring mechanisms, and a Harness Engineer proposes bounded source edits with behavioral predictions recorded before evaluation (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Combined validation:** Promotion requires code checks, nondecreasing Search and Validation scores with a strict gain on at least one split, and evidence supporting every predicted behavioral change. Model weights stay frozen and Test remains sealed (§3.5).

The distinctive contribution is this integrated evidence-to-edit loop for GUI runtimes, rather than a new backbone or benchmark (§1).

The paired held-out results support useful harness adaptation under the reported protocol. Ablations support the components within one backbone, and transfer supports usefulness beyond OSWorld for two backbones. They do not establish universal gains, statistically significant differences, or equal-compute superiority over every comparator. Full-suite scores include optimization tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-described bottleneck:** Remaining failures often involve correctly executed actions aimed at the wrong object or evaluator-relevant state, which screenshots and logs may not expose clearly (§4.6; Table 9).
- **Comparison boundaries:** LFF is compared through released final patches, not a reproduced optimization process. Published system and cost comparisons retain differing protocols and budgets (§G.8; §4.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited robustness evidence:** Component ablations use one backbone and a fixed split; repeated complete optimization searches across seeds are not reported (§G.4).
- **Analyst inference—selection uncertainty:** The hard gate accepts strictly positive measured gains without an uncertainty threshold. Repeated Validation feedback could select noise; sealed Test reduces this concern but does not quantify it (§3.5; §G.7).
- **Analyst inference—diagnosis coverage:** Only zero-score runs are diagnosis targets, potentially leaving partial-score failure mechanisms less directly addressed (§G.9).
- **Cost scope:** Reported inference costs exclude optimization and local compute, so total adaptation economics remain unestablished (§F.1).

## Open research questions

- **How stable are optimized harnesses across search seeds and task splits?** One fixed split is reported; rerun complete optimization across several splits and seeds, measuring held-out score dispersion (§G.7).
- **Can richer state evidence resolve visually plausible semantic failures?** These remain a stated bottleneck; compare screenshot-only diagnosis with structured application-state evidence on the residual failures (§4.6).
- **Does the method retain its component benefits across backbones and optimizer models?** Ablations cover one target backbone and all roles use one optimizer model; repeat matched-budget ablations across model families (§4.1; Table 3).
- **When does adaptation repay its total cost?** Optimization costs are excluded; measure complete search expenditure and subsequent inference savings over repeated workloads (§F.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported paired results, but leaves material uncertainty about robustness and reproducibility.

Unavailable details include complete role prompts—Appendix J stops after its introduction—exact numeric configuration values for several claimed fixed settings, full patch contents, optimization API and compute costs, repeated-search variability, and confidence intervals or significance tests for the main gains. Some prose cross-references also point to Table 2 where the supplied paired adaptation results appear in Table 1.
