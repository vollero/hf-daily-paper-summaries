# SlimWise: Decoupling Expert Pruning Across Prefill and Decode for Efficient MoE Serving

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34117) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34117)


## One-sentence takeaway
SlimWise preserves full-model prefill and prunes only decode, recovering quality in many evaluated settings while increasing decode throughput, although cache handoff alone can worsen generation behavior (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Batched MoE decoding can access nearly every expert despite sparse activation per token, making expert-weight traffic a bottleneck. Conventional pruning reduces this traffic but also prunes compute-bound prefill, where the authors observe little throughput benefit. Their identified gap is phase-specific capacity allocation that preserves prompt representations while accelerating decode (§§1–2).

The paper also argues that accuracy alone misses pruning-induced changes in response length and termination behavior (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Phase-specific expert pruning:** Full-model prefill passes its KV cache directly to a pruned decoder. Pruning preserves the attention architecture and cache format, so no conversion is required; active experts per token remain unchanged (§3.1).
- **Cache-conditioned distillation:** The student continues from teacher-generated prefix caches at randomly sampled split points. Training updates routers, normalization layers, and selected always-on components while freezing routed experts and attention; an additive shared-expert correction is exported in standard checkpoint form (§3.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Serving implementation and behavioral analysis:** A vLLM implementation supports separate pruned decode checkpoints under PD disaggregation and phase-aware router masking under colocated serving. Accuracy, output-length distributions, greedy decoding, and phase-swap experiments expose distinct prefill and decode effects (§§3.1, 4.3; Appendices A.2–A.3).

Routing mass, EAN, and REAP are adopted pruning criteria, rather than new contributions (§2.3).

## Evidence and main results
Accuracy entries below are reported proportions; sampled evaluations use thinking enabled and mean accuracy over three runs (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Decode throughput improves in both serving configurations | Qwen3.6, REAP, two A100-80GB GPUs, 1k input/8k output tokens: at 50% pruning, maximum per-GPU decode throughput meeting 30/40/50 tokens/s SLOs improves **1.40–1.81×** under disaggregation and **1.36–1.72×** under colocation versus the full model. Decoders include the distilled architecture’s enlarged shared expert. | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Cache handoff can restore coding behavior without training | Qwen3.6, 50% REAP pruning, HumanEval+: full/conventional/SlimWise accuracy **0.917/0.902/0.929**, with median outputs **2.3k/0.3k/2.3k** tokens. | Table 1 |
| Distillation mitigates excessive generation but can reduce accuracy | Same backbone/pruning, MATH-500: conventional/SlimWise/distilled medians **6.6k/5.8k/2.6k**, versus full-model **2.4k**; accuracies **0.964/0.971/0.969**, versus **0.983**. | Table 1 |
| Training-free handoff can substantially worsen quality | Gemma 4, 50% EAN pruning, GSM8K: conventional accuracy **0.816**, SlimWise **0.173**, distilled SlimWise **0.965**, versus baseline **0.971**. SlimWise’s median reaches the generation budget, reported as **32.8k** tokens. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Teacher-cache training has a task-dependent trade-off | Qwen3.6, 75% REAP pruning, SlimWise inference: teacher-cache versus own-cache distillation reduces MATH-500 p90 **22.4k→14.0k** and raises accuracy **0.928→0.949**; MBPP+ p90 falls **8.1k→3.5k**, but accuracy falls **0.929→0.890**. | Table 2 |
| Distillation uses limited trainable parameters, but requires substantial hardware | Trainable fractions: **0.42%** for Qwen3.6 and **2.12%** for Gemma 4; student-processed training tokens approximately **102M/87M**, respectively. Each run takes approximately **six hours on four B200 GPUs**. | §3.3; Appendix A.1 |

These results support phase-specific pruning and evaluating generation behavior alongside accuracy. They do not establish universal quality recovery, statistical significance for small accuracy differences, or equivalent end-to-end service speedups. Throughput uses fixed output lengths, whereas quality experiments show that pruning changes generated lengths.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Prefill cost remains unchanged, so end-to-end gains depend on decode’s share of serving time. Colocated serving retains the full expert-weight footprint (§5).
- **Observed:** Handoff and distillation are not consistently beneficial across criteria and tasks; residual long-generation tails and accuracy–length trade-offs remain (Tables 1–2).
- **Analyst inference:** Throughput generalization is limited by evaluation on one backbone, one GPU configuration, and fixed input/output lengths; quality evaluation covers two backbones and the specified benchmarks, including only the first 500 GSM8K problems (§4.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons against runtime expert-selection methods are conceptual rather than measured head-to-head serving results in the supplied text (§§2.3, 4.3). Public code/checkpoint availability is not established.

## Open research questions
- **What are the end-to-end gains under realistic traffic?** Fixed-length decode measurements leave this unresolved. Measure request completion time, TTFT, SLO compliance, and total GPU cost with variable prompts and actual generated lengths.
- **Can pruning avoid termination distortions before distillation?** Strong criterion-dependent failures remain. Compare layer-specific retained counts and length-aware expert selection against the paper’s uniform-count configurations.
- **Which distillation components drive recovery?** Teacher-cache conditioning is ablated, but separate contributions of router updates, normalization, and additive correction are not established. Run component ablations at matched training budgets.
- **How broadly does cache handoff generalize?** Quality uses two backbones and throughput only Qwen3.6. Repeat both evaluations across additional compatible MoE architectures and hardware configurations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported settings and numerical results; broader deployment conclusions remain uncertain.

Material unavailable information includes public artifact availability, measured end-to-end serving cost and cache-transfer overhead, throughput variability, component-level distillation ablations, and head-to-head runtime-selection comparisons. Figure descriptions are supplied, but their underlying plotted data are not.
