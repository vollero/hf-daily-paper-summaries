# JLD: Perceptual Distance Through A Jacobian Lens

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05967) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05967)


## One-sentence takeaway
JLD projects early frozen-encoder features onto output-sensitive directions, achieving the highest reported four-dataset mean SRCC among evaluated distances, with an unlabeled lens fit but human-guided configuration selection (§§3, 5.1; Appendix H).

## Problem and motivation
Pixel error treats visually different distortions similarly, while perceptual distances fitted to human judgments can lose agreement when resolution changes. The authors identify a complementary gap in frozen encoders: early features retain spatial detail but count irrelevant changes, whereas late features lose detail while capturing output-relevant information (§1).

JLD aims to combine these properties in an inexpensive, interpretable full-reference distance suitable for image comparison and potentially optimization.

## Approach and contributions
- **Output-sensitive feature projection:** Estimate \(M=\mathbb{E}[J^\top J]\), where \(J\) maps an early patch-token change to the frozen encoder’s output. Gaussian output probes estimate this matrix without explicit Jacobians. JLD retains its leading eigenvectors, weighting retained directions equally rather than by eigenvalue (§3.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Image distance and geometry:** The default uses DINOv2-S block-1 features projected into a 64-dimensional lens, RMS pooling, a fixed chroma pre-filter, and a half-weighted output cosine distance. The lens term is a pseudometric with an image-dependent local pullback geometry; full JLD lacks a triangle-inequality guarantee (§3.3; Appendix B).
- **Variants and validation:** JLD-fast stops after block 1 and omits the output term; JLD-lin uses the linear patch embedding; JLD-video uses VideoMAE space-time features. Each tested encoder receives its own fitted lens—one identical lens is not transferred across encoders (Appendices A.7, K, L).

The claimed novelty is transferring output sensitivity into a fixed early-feature perceptual projection; the authors acknowledge prior work on pullback metrics and Jacobian-based representations (§2).

These results support the usefulness of output-sensitive early features and the measured speed–accuracy tradeoff. They do not establish universal superiority: DeepDC remains stronger on KADID-10k test and PIPAL, VSI on TID2013, and VMAF on held-out AVT (Appendices F, L). The lens fit excludes human labels, but the complete method’s selection does not.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** JLD assumes spatial alignment, inherits encoder weaknesses, performs poorly on global contrast changes, and has a substantial lens null space that optimization could exploit. Full JLD sacrifices the triangle inequality and is slower than DISTS; the speed advantage belongs to JLD-fast. The additional human study has only ten participants (Appendix N).

**Analyst inferences from the reported protocol:**
- The aggregate image mean includes development-used CSIQ and pre-filter-informed TID2013. It is therefore not a wholly held-out generalization estimate (§5.1; Appendix H).
- Waterloo’s video advantage is measured on configuration-selection data; AVT supplies the stronger independent test (Appendix L).
- Resolution experiments reuse existing human ratings, so they establish scoring stability rather than perceptual validity under newly rated display conditions (Appendix G.2).
- Encoder output sensitivity only partly matches human vision: luminance frequency tuning remains mismatched, and a hand-designed chroma correction is necessary (Appendix I).
- Differentiability alone does not demonstrate successful use as a training loss; the supplied text reports no downstream training experiment and explicitly warns about null-space exploitation (§3.3; Appendix N).

## Open research questions
- **Can JLD serve reliably as a training loss?** Its discarded directions permit potential exploitation. Next experiment: compare restoration training with JLD, a full-tensor variant, and a complementary loss, followed by independent human evaluation (Appendix N).
- **Does resolution robustness survive new human judgments?** Current scores remain fixed after resizing. Next experiment: collect ratings at multiple controlled display resolutions and compare full JLD and its lens term (Appendix G.2).
- **Can encoder-derived sensitivity correct remaining perceptual mismatches?** Global contrast and luminance frequency tuning remain weak. Next experiment: evaluate alternative output targets or encoders on separately held-out contrast and grating judgments (Appendices I, N).
- **How broadly does video transfer hold?** Only one reported video database is held out from selection. Next experiment: freeze the Waterloo-selected configuration and evaluate additional temporal distortion settings with independent ratings (Appendix L).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and headline findings, but several referenced tables appear only as captions or prose summaries.

Material gaps include complete baseline tables, numerical bootstrap intervals, detailed video calibration-corpus provenance, and downstream loss-training results. Release availability is also unclear: the reproducibility statement both describes released assets and promises public release upon publication.
