# Harness Engineering for Software Engineering via Modular Executable Dev-Primitives

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07832) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07832)


## One-sentence takeaway
HERMES assigns repository artifacts their own LLM-based editors and coordinates them through selective activation, directed messages, and execution-driven diagnosis, reporting a 12.4-percentage-point average improvement over matched harnesses with GPT-5.6 Sol at medium effort (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Long software-engineering workflows require agents to repeatedly reconstruct state distributed across code, configurations, tests, and runtime behavior. The authors argue that history compression alone cannot resolve this burden because edits change both components and their dependencies. Their proposed gap is an artifact-bound reasoning interface that keeps local implementation knowledge with its owner and communicates requirements directly across components (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dev-Primitives:** Each persistent repository artifact receives a resident LLM that reads and optionally edits its artifact, maintains task-specific communication context, and sends directed natural-language requirements to related active primitives. Files are the default granularity; model hidden states do not persist across calls (§3.2; Appendix F.5).
- **HERMES harness:** LLM-based activation inspects candidate files and dependencies, assigns local objectives, and selectively invokes primitives. Execution supplies observable failures; diagnosis routes structured feedback into targeted replanning. The default permits three revision rounds after the initial execution (§3.3; Appendices B, F.1).
- **Controlled evaluation and model allocation:** The paper compares harnesses, ablates mechanisms, tests centralized editors, and allocates stronger models to activation and diagnosis while retaining small local editors (§4; Appendix G). These are empirical contributions; no software release is established in the supplied text.

The evidence supports improved benchmark performance and a useful division of model capacity between coordination and local editing. It does not establish universal superiority or lower cost than baseline harnesses: most main comparisons spend more inference. The centralized-editor control supports benefits beyond localization and additional compute in its tested setting, but does not isolate every aspect of artifact ownership and communication. Selection recall’s association with success is observational (§4.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Additional inference cost and latency, smaller gains in saturated settings, dependence on component selection and informative execution signals, file-level granularity, limited benchmark scope, and deployment/model-update sensitivity (Appendix I).
- **Author-stated measurement caveat:** Reference-patch overlap can penalize alternative valid repairs. Refactor selection analysis labels any non-zero composite score “solved,” rather than requiring complete migration success (Appendices F.4, H).
- **Analyst inference:** Baselines combine published scores and controlled runs without row-level provenance, limiting auditability of matched comparisons (Appendix F.8).
- **Analyst inference:** Broad repeatability remains uncertain: Refactor has a reported repeated-run check and Terminal has repeated trials, but comparable uncertainty is not supplied for all analyses. The promised context-capacity control is not shown, and Appendix C contains headings without actual prompt templates (Appendices C, G).
- **Observed boundary:** The case study’s diagnosis accepted a patch without a successful task-specific reproducer; hidden tests subsequently confirmed that instance, but this does not validate diagnosis reliability generally (Appendix H).

## Open research questions
- **How should artifact granularity vary with a task?** Only file-level primitives are evaluated. Compare file, symbol, and adaptive grouping under matched inference budgets (Appendix I).
- **How reliable is diagnosis with weak execution evidence?** The case study lacks a valid task-specific reproducer. Remove or degrade visible tests and logs, then measure premature acceptance against hidden outcomes (Appendix H).
- **Which coordination mechanism produces the residual benefit?** Centralized compute matching leaves a gap, while context-capacity evidence is absent. Compare artifact-bound editors with context-managed centralized editors and alternative message routing at matched cost (Appendix G).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive methods, benchmark results, and controls, but lacks actual prompt templates, implementation/release details, row-level baseline provenance, the announced context-capacity experiment, and uncertainty estimates for many ablation and scaling results. These gaps limit reproducibility and causal attribution.
