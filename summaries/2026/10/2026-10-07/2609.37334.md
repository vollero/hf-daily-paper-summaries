# Taming VLAs under Robot Execution Errors: Self-Compensation and Stress Testing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37334) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37334)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that online policy adaptation using command–execution residuals improves VLA manipulation success under simulated mechanical errors and on two physical arms, while introducing RoboStress to evaluate such errors systematically (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Robots may execute motions differently from VLA commands because of wear, payload changes, and operating conditions. The authors identify a gap in evaluations centered on visual disturbances or synthetic end-effector command noise: these do not explicitly capture joint-specific, state-dependent, and history-dependent execution errors (§1).

Testing diverse mechanical conditions on physical hardware is costly and can risk damage, motivating controlled simulation alongside physical experiments (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Self-compensating VLA:** Measure executed motion through proprioception, compute the residual as executed minus commanded motion, and subtract it from the original command to form a pseudo-target. Online flow-matching updates train only action-expert LoRA adapters, with an anchor penalty limiting parameter drift. Corrections affect subsequent predictions; adaptation requires no task rewards, labels, or explicit error model (§3, Eqs. 1–3).
- **RoboStress:** Combine established friction, gravity-compensation error, backlash, and compliance models at joint-level control stages into seven deployment scenarios. The contribution is their benchmark integration and scenario design, rather than new dynamics models (§4; Fig. 3).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Test two VLA backbones across LIBERO, two Piper arms, unseen object variants, and adaptation alongside a disturbance observer (§5).

These results support improved success in the tested settings. They do not establish broad hardware generalization, statistical significance, or safety guarantees. Reported success includes episodes that supply adaptation feedback, with learned state retained across episodes; it therefore measures cumulative deployment adaptation rather than independently reset trials (§§B.2, B.6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged:** The method relies on proprioception; external motion sensing is proposed as future work (§6). The ethics statement explicitly says success improvements are not safety guarantees.
- **Analyst inference—limited physical scope:** Two arms of the same platform and four tasks leave other robot types and manipulation behaviors untested (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—order dependence:** Simulation carries adaptation across tasks in fixed LIBERO order; physical and unseen-object evaluations also retain adaptation state. No shuffled-order or per-episode-reset comparison is supplied (§§B.2, B.6).
- **Analyst inference—restricted benchmark validation:** Physical matching is demonstrated for a calibrated payload replay, which does not validate all modeled error sources or scenarios (§5.2).
- **Evidence accessibility:** Retrieved tables contain captions but no cell values, and some narrative table references are inconsistent. Per-scenario comparisons and uncertainty cannot be independently checked from this text.
- Gripper errors are excluded from compensation, and simulated observations receive no added sensor noise (§§B.3, B.6; §A.3).

## Open research questions
1. **How robust is compensation to inaccurate proprioception?** Reliance on proprioception remains explicit (§6). Compare proprioception-only adaptation with externally measured motion under controlled sensing errors.
2. **How much do carry-over and task order influence gains?** Adaptation persists across evaluation episodes and, in simulation, tasks (§§B.2, B.6). Compare fixed order, shuffled order, and per-episode resets while plotting success against accumulated feedback.
3. **Do RoboStress scenarios predict failures beyond payload changes?** Physical profile matching covers only calibrated payload execution (§5.2). Measure joint and end-effector trajectories under controlled friction, backlash, and compliance changes, then test held-out conditions.
4. **Does the method transfer beyond the tested policies and hardware?** Evaluation uses two backbones and two Piper arms (§5). Repeat the protocol with another policy architecture and robot platform.
