# MiniCorp: The Last Mile of the AI Agent Firm

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05912) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05912)


## One-sentence takeaway
MiniCorp couples a persistent LLM-agent firm with a checkpointable e-commerce simulator, demonstrating selected market-response patterns and organizational coordination while leaving real-world transfer and robustness unestablished (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Business decisions require coordination and produce delayed consequences without a single verifiable correct answer. The authors argue that existing task benchmarks lack persistent organizations and evolving markets, while enterprise archives are scarce, incomplete, and unable to reveal alternative decision outcomes (§1).

The identified gap is an interactive environment that records organizational decisions, their information context, and subsequent business outcomes, with replay for counterfactual comparisons.

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent firm–market loop:** Standing agents communicate, analyze business tables with SQL/Python, and propose catalogued actions for CEO approval. Approved actions affect the next simulation period; latent market parameters remain hidden (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Explicit e-commerce mechanisms and fidelity evaluation:** Search matching, ranking, clicks, conversion, advertising auctions, and adaptive competitors connect decisions to outcomes. The authors evaluate integrated response trajectories against patterns reported in empirical sources (§§3.2, 3.5).
- **Enterprise-data generation and replay:** Logs preserve communications, queries, proposals, approvals, and world-side causal records; checkpoints support alternative decisions from the same state. Scalability and training usefulness are proposed benefits rather than quantitatively demonstrated outcomes (§§1, 3.4, 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate demand responds to price | A 10% price reduction and increase yield absolute arc elasticities of 2.9 and 3.2, respectively. Authors report proximity to an empirical benchmark, without giving its numerical value. | Table 1; RQ1 |
| Discounts have persistent simulated benefits | A 20% discount for four weeks increases units by 71%; after price restoration, units remain 31% above control for four weeks and 11% above control for the following six weeks. | Table 1 |
| Stockout losses persist after replenishment | Following a two-week stockout, 59% of cumulative unit shortfall occurs after restocking; sales remain 36% below control in week 26. | Table 1 |
| Advertising creates delayed benefits and costs | Reported advertising intervention increases ad-attributed units by 23–32%; organic gains grow from 0.5% to 5.8%. Additional advertising returns $0.53 contribution per dollar within the observation window; 84% of incremental units displace competitors. Intervention magnitude and window length are unspecified. | Table 1 |
| Work is mostly inherited or colleague-triggered | Among 30,248 work items, 58% carry over, 31% arise from messages or interrupts, and 11% are self-planned. These are organizational-pattern observations, without a human-company comparator. | RQ2; Table 2 |
| Strategic guidance changes investment and outcomes | One paired 26-week comparison uses the same initial state and market rules. Guided versus unguided firms spend about $1,700 versus $50 on advertising and earn $1,913 versus −$7 gross profit after advertising, excluding refunds, write-offs, and overheads. | RQ3 |
| Shock handling is incomplete | Ten scenarios, each repeated twice for eight weekly periods: 11/20 runs reach the expected approved action; four additional runs partially respond. Scoring concerns actions rather than final profit. | RQ4; Tables 5–6 |
| Agents initiate title optimization | In the guided run, a title revision goes live in week 12; weekly charger sales rise from 91 to 189 units at an unchanged $24.95 price. No isolated counterfactual for this revision is reported. | RQ5 |

The evidence supports selected response patterns in the default simulator and demonstrates coordination and adaptation in the reported runs. It does not establish real-world business performance, broad simulator fidelity, or effective agent training from generated data. The authors’ attribution of the title-revision gain remains observational in the supplied account.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Fidelity robustness across independently initialized worlds remains unevaluated (RQ1). Simulator overfitting is an explicit concern (§3.5).
- **Author-stated:** Shock tests assess approved actions because their horizon is too short to evaluate eventual economic payoff; reported gross-profit measures omit several costs (RQ3–RQ4).
- **Analyst inference:** One model substrate, one demonstrated industry, and one guided–unguided pair provide limited evidence of generalization (§4.1; RQ3).
- **Analyst inference:** Collaboration’s necessity is assessed through trace audits, without reported communication-removal experiments (RQ4; Table 6).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Table 5 permits holding, auditing, or replacing the defective supplier, yet Table 6 scores both runs unsuccessful despite reporting audits and stock holds. The success criterion is therefore unclear.
- **Reporting inconsistency:** The six category counts in Table 3 do not account for all work items claimed to have been classified.

## Open research questions
- **Does fidelity persist across worlds?** RQ1 explicitly leaves this open. Repeat checkpointed interventions across independently initialized worlds and report variability in pathways and outcomes.
- **Does guidance reliably improve economic performance?** RQ3 reports one pair and incomplete cost accounting. Replicate across seeds and models, using full operating-profit measures.
- **Does communication causally improve shock handling?** Trace audits suggest contributions but do not isolate them. Compare matched runs with selected communication links enabled or removed.
- **Do generated records improve agent training or transfer?** This is a proposed use, not a demonstrated result. Compare agents trained with and without MiniCorp records on held-out worlds and independently grounded business evaluations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and reported outcomes are clear, but their generality is uncertain.

Missing information includes complete simulator equations and parameter settings, prompts and API configurations, fidelity-test replication and uncertainty estimates, advertising intervention details, precise shock-scoring rules, compute/data-generation throughput, confirmed code or dataset releases, and downstream training or real-world transfer results.
