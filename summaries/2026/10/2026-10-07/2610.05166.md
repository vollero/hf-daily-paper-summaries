# A Safe Action Is Not Enough: Feasible-Future Decoding for Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.05166) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05166)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors derive a policy-relative safe-completion target and introduce a training-free VLA reranker that reduces observed safety cost across six simulated settings while preserving near-policy completion, although its heuristic future correction has no established uniform benefit (§§3–4; Appendix G).

## Problem and motivation
A likely, locally safe action can leave a frozen vision-language-action (VLA) policy with little or no probability of safely completing its task. The authors call this the **feasibility–likelihood gap**: next-action likelihood and local safety do not measure the successful futures an action preserves (§2).

This matters because lower cumulative safety cost can also result from premature termination. The paper therefore evaluates cost alongside completion and episode length, and distinguishes policy-supported viability from what a different controller might achieve (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Exact safe-completion target.** Starting from an established KL-regularized trajectory construction, the authors derive a next-block marginal combining policy likelihood, local reward–cost terms, and **feasible-future mass**. Its support indicates whether safe completion has positive probability under the frozen policy–environment process; its magnitude weights the remaining feasible continuations (§3.1, Theorem 1; Appendix B).
- **Conditional approximation theory.** The analysis separates intervention, candidate coverage, support errors, and ranking errors. Recovery of the best surviving viable candidate requires support separation and sufficiently accurate score differences; it is a per-decision result (§3.2, Theorem 2; Appendix B).
- **Practical selective decoding.** VICS-G reranks only when an alarm fires, combining the frozen prior, history-based local robustness, and a bounded heuristic continuation correction. VICS-S adds task-specific symbolic rules; VICS-R adds limited simulator lookahead. VICS-G requires neither retraining nor candidate rollouts (§3.3; Appendices F, H).

The trajectory tilt itself is inherited machinery; the authors position the policy-relative marginal, support–magnitude distinction, and selective approximation analysis as their contributions (Appendix J).

| Feedback-aware decoding performs favorably under tested execution noise | Safety-aligned Fetch, IID and persistent perturbations at **ρ=0.1**, 688 executions per method over 172 paired tasks: **55.67% success**, **15.9% lower cost**, and **16.6% fewer violations** than policy sampling, at virtually identical mean length. | §4.3; Table 2 discussion |

| Limited rollout augmentation gives an exploratory improvement | Modified simulator, safety-aligned Fetch, 172 tasks, 200-action budget: VICS-R versus VICS-G raises zero-cost safe success from **27.91% to 30.23%**, holds task success at **58.72%**, and lowers mean cost from **3.233 to 3.116** (**3.6%**). Paired safe-success difference: exact McNemar **p=0.344**. | §4.4; Appendix H, Table 6 discussion |

These results support favorable **observed operating points for the complete decoder** in the evaluated simulator. They do not establish uniform incremental safety gains from the continuation correction or feedback, nor statistically significant rollout gains. The theoretical guarantees require conditions that the experiments do not measure or verify (Appendices D, G–I).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers three simulated task families; feedback uses two perturbation models at one severity. Physical deployment and broader disturbances remain untested (§4.5).
- **Author-stated:** VICS-G’s continuation score is uncalibrated and does not certify viable support. Recovery guarantees depend on candidate coverage, support separation, and score accuracy; there is no full-trajectory guarantee (§3.3; Appendices B, D).
- **Author-stated:** VICS-R follows VICS-G from a reconstructed simulator state, rather than the ideal history-conditioned frozen-policy law. It also adds an intervention opportunity, and replay checks cannot certify exact hidden-state restoration (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Configuration selection and reported evaluation overlap: main coefficients were selected on approximately 10% of episodes included in the reported full sets, and rollout aggregates include development tasks. Independent held-out estimates would better assess generalization (§4.1; Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons against RCD cannot attribute gains solely to future scoring because gating, admission, and authorization also differ. The matched zero-weight study is more targeted, but its Fetch candidate budget differs from the main evaluation (Appendices F–G).

## Open research questions
1. **Can future scoring provide reliable gains beyond history-based recovery?** The zero-weight ablation is mixed and statistically inconclusive. Next experiment: compare heuristic and learned safe-completion scores on held-out episodes with identical candidates, gates, and authorization.
2. **How often does selective intervention miss viable alternatives?** The gate can retain a dead end, while latent support and score errors are unmeasured. Next experiment: audit gated-off decisions in a branchable simulator using larger continuation budgets (§3.2; Appendix D).
3. **Does rollout evidence help independently of extra intervention?** VICS-R changes both evidence and intervention opportunity. Next experiment: match intervention timing across rollout and no-rollout arms, then vary continuation budgets (Appendix H).
4. **Do gains transfer to physical execution and broader disturbances?** Current feedback evidence is narrow. Next experiment: test real robots and multiple disturbance severities with matched feedback-aware and masked decoders (§4.5).

## Confidence and missing information
