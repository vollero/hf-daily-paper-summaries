# TRIAGE: Direction-Aware Mismatch Stabilization of Native NVFP4 Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07043) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07043)


## One-sentence takeaway
TRIAGE selectively reweights mismatch-amplifying policy updates and repairs severe segment-level gaps, sustaining native NVFP4 RL on two Qwen3 models with near-BF16 mathematical reasoning scores and measured end-to-end speedups up to 1.30× (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Low-precision rollout accelerates LLM RL, but sampler and learner execution can assign different probabilities to identical sampled tokens, destabilizing optimization. The authors argue that magnitude-based corrections miss whether a policy update amplifies or contracts an existing discrepancy, while response averages hide concentrated local tails (§§1–3).

Their target is stable RL that preserves native weight-and-activation 4-bit forward execution on both sampler and learner, rather than relying on high-precision fake quantization (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Directional analysis:** A local mismatch-energy decomposition identifies amplifying diagonal gradient contributions when advantage and learner–sampler log-probability gap have the same sign. Cross-token interactions and optimizer residuals prevent this from being a general token-level prediction guarantee (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Selective stabilization:** TRIAGE diagnoses negative-advantage responses in short segments, gates only negative-gap tokens, and normalizes weights within each response to redistribute relative update mass. A scheduled pseudo-Huber repair term corrects sufficiently negative segment means on positive-advantage responses (§4; Appendix C).
- **Native execution recipe:** The method augments an importance-corrected objective while retaining native NVFP4 forward computation, evaluated on dense and MoE Qwen3 models (§5; Appendix D). The supplied text claims a reproducibility artifact, but provides no artifact link (Appendix B.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Stable evaluated training | TRIAGE remains stable through 600 updates on Qwen3-4B and 1,700 on Qwen3-30B-A3B; naive NVFP4 destabilizes around 300 and 700 updates, respectively. TIS does not comparably diverge on 30B within its schedule. | §5.2; Fig. 4 |
| Near-BF16 benchmark averages | Unweighted five-benchmark mean: TRIAGE/BF16 = 58.49%/58.26% on 4B and 70.96%/72.41% on 30B. Checkpoint evaluation uses sample-averaged pass@1. | §5.2; Table 1 caption; Appendices B.1, D.2 |
| Gains at a shared checkpoint | At update 300 on 4B, TRIAGE’s five-benchmark average exceeds native NVFP4 by 5.93 percentage points and NVFP4+TIS by 2.27 points. | §5.2 |
| Local concentration exceeds a matched null | Pre-terminal, negative-advantage responses with 64-token segments: top-5% segments contain 25.68%/25.01% of tail tokens on 4B/30B, versus within-response shuffle medians of 18.17%/18.50%. | Appendix B.4.5; Fig. 7 |
| Token-level indicator has limited transfer | Late-stage 4B negative-advantage, negative-gap tokens: weighted sign agreement is 47.73% overall and 63.05% for absolute gaps above 3. The 30B routing-conditioned audit remains below 50%. | Appendix B.3.2 |
| Natural-generation acceleration | Qwen3-30B-A3B on eight B300 GPUs, ten measured iterations: batches 256/512 yield 6,745/8,717 output tokens/s, or 2.27×/2.30× BF16; iteration times improve from 208.2/254.5 s to 172.1/195.8 s. | §5.3; Appendix E.1 |

These results support stability and useful efficiency gains within the tested settings. They do not establish statistical equivalence to BF16 or a universal 2.30× rollout gain: natural-generation timing includes precision-dependent output lengths, and fixed-output results differ. Full Table 1 cells are absent from the supplied text, preventing assessment of individual benchmark scores.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** No evaluation at 100B+ scale, substantially longer horizons, or asynchronous RL (§8).
- **Theory boundary:** The directional criterion describes diagonal current-gradient contributions. It does not guarantee realized token motion; its token-wise predictive trend fails on the routing-conditioned 30B estimand (Appendices A and B.3).
- **Diagnostic sensitivity:** Length normalization reduces the pre-terminal directional ratio to 1.434 on 4B and 1.049 on 30B. These occupancy ratios do not measure total amplifying-versus-contracting gradient energy (Appendix B.6).
- **Localization qualification:** Adjacency above the pooled chance reference does not beat the response-conditioned token-shuffle null. A separate exploratory ordering test finds enrichment but does not establish causal propagation (Appendix B.4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Ablations branching from a TRIAGE checkpoint at update 300 support continuation-stage comparisons, but do not isolate every component’s necessity when training from initialization (§5.4).
- **Precision qualification:** Characterization retains several modules in BF16, FP32 master parameters and optimizer states, and a BF16 KV cache; native W4A4 forward execution should not be read as an entirely four-bit training system (Appendix B.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does TRIAGE handle asynchronous staleness?** This setting is untested (§8). Next experiment: vary sampler refresh delays while measuring mismatch, reward, and benchmark accuracy.
- **Does stability persist at larger scale and longer horizons?** The authors explicitly leave both open (§8). Next experiment: extend dense and sparse model runs beyond the reported schedules with repeated seeds.
- **What explains weak token-wise prediction on MoE?** The audit cannot separate routing-conditioned residual structure from cross-token effects (Appendix B.3.2). Next experiment: compare routing-replay and ordinary-gap audits on matched batches.
- **Which components matter from initialization?** Existing ablations share a trained branch point (§5.4). Next experiment: run gating, repair, normalization, and full-method variants from the same base checkpoint.
