# CheckerBench: Can Long-Horizon Agents Synthesize Static-Analysis Checkers?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07557) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07557)


## One-sentence takeaway

CheckerBench evaluates agents that build static-analysis checkers from vulnerability fixes, reporting 32.30% mean Pass@1 and 45.33% best Pass@1 across 21 model–harness configurations on 300 tasks (§§4–5.2, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Checker development requires understanding defect semantics, implementing analyzer-specific logic, integrating builds, and refining diagnostics through execution feedback. The authors argue that existing coding and vulnerability benchmarks rarely evaluate this complete workflow or jointly measure analyzer compatibility, semantic correctness, localization, and false positives (§1).

The intended payoff is reusable executable analysis rather than a single patch or vulnerability finding. The authors’ claim that CheckerBench is the first comprehensive benchmark of this kind is not independently established by the supplied text (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **CheckerBench:** An executable benchmark containing 300 tasks derived from 297 CVEs across 167 repositories and 85 CWE labels. Tasks expose vulnerable/fixed code, the fixing patch, pinned environments, and checker scaffolds (§§3–3.2).
- **Construction pipeline:** Recover analyzable revision pairs, package tasks, freeze independent verifiers, collect human-guided calibration attempts, and curate verified checker–trajectory pairs. Admission requires a passing calibration checker and a trajectory meeting the interaction-horizon rule (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **CheckerLab:** A shared evaluation framework that freezes submitted source, rebuilds it independently, and combines differential diagnostics, patch relevance, semantic/process judgments, mainline-alert triage, and completion requirements (§4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Cross-model and cross-harness comparisons, a KNighter comparison, component ablations, and sensitivity analyses characterize where synthesis succeeds and fails (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full checker acceptance remains difficult | Seven models × three harnesses, the same 300 tasks, three independent repeats, 50-minute generation budget: mean Pass@1 **32.30%**; best **45.33%**, Claude Opus 4.8 + OpenCode. | §4; §5.2; Table 2 |
| Compilation and differential detection exceed full acceptance | Across the same configurations/repeats, mean Build **78.25%**, Diff. SR **73.65%**, versus Pass@1 **32.30%**. Full acceptance adds semantic, process, precision, and completion gates. | §5.2; Table 2 |
| Harness choice changes outcomes | Mean Pass@1 across seven models: OpenCode **36.95%**, Claude Code **33.81%**, Hermes Agent **26.14%**; model rankings also change by harness. | §5.2; Table 2 |
| C/C++–CSA is the weakest language/backend stratum | Every model’s lowest language-specific Pass@1 is C/C++; the leading model, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, reaches **24.53%**, averaged equally across harnesses on **159 tasks**. | §5.2; Figure 6; Appendix H.4 |
| Skill-guided synthesis outperforms the reported baseline | On KNighter’s **61 Linux-kernel tasks**, all methods use DeepSeek-V4-Pro: OpenCode skill-guided workflow **80.3% valid-checker rate**, KNighter **67.2%**, workflow without skills **29.5%**. This metric differs from main-benchmark Pass@1. | §5.3; Table 3 reference |
| Ablations show large performance gaps, with confounding | DeepSeek-V4-Pro/OpenCode on **300 tasks**: Full Pass@1 **39.67%**, without skills/references **8.33%**, hidden execution feedback **13.33%**, one-shot **0.33%**. Authors caution that historical/new run conditions differ. | §5.4; Table 4 reference |
| Longer completion windows capture more successes | Retrospective cutoffs over existing trajectories: aggregate Pass@1 **7.24% at 10 minutes**, **26.05% at 30**, **32.30% at 50**; unfinished runs remain failures. | §5.5; Figure 8; Appendix H.3 |
| Recorded-cohort acceptance is relatively insensitive to FP cutoff | Holding adjudications and other gates fixed across 21 configurations: aggregate acceptance **32.05%** at a **10%** cutoff versus **32.83%** at **50%**. | §5.6; Figure 9 |
| Identity-cue reduction has a small observed synthesis effect | On **100 language-stratified tasks**, one generation per condition/model using OpenCode: paraphrase-to-renaming Pass@1 drops **3 percentage points** for Claude Opus 4.8 and **2** for DeepSeek-V4-Pro; exact CVE recall drops **9** and **7 points**, respectively. | §5.8; Appendix C.3 |

These author-reported results establish difficulty under the benchmark’s task selection and acceptance protocol. They do not establish general checker reuse across unseen repositories, isolate language from analyzer effects, or causally attribute ablation gaps to individual components. The prior-exposure intervention measures sensitivity to identifying cues; it does not rule out training contamination.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Truncated coverage:** A substantial middle portion of the source is missing, including referenced evaluation details and rubrics; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.
- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Ablations mix historical and new conditions, while time-budget curves reuse existing trajectories rather than rerunning agents under shorter deadlines (§§5.4–5.5).
- **Author-stated scope confounding:** Language and analyzer effects cannot be separated. CWE groups differ in language, repository, and backend composition; small groups do not establish reliable rankings (Appendix H.4).
- **Author-stated exposure uncertainty:** First-public patch/checker auditing remains unresolved, and release dates are not training cutoffs. Failure to recall a CVE does not establish non-exposure (Appendices C.1–C.3).
- **Analyst inference—selection bias:** Admission excludes tasks without a passing human-guided calibration attempt and selects sufficiently long successful trajectories, conditioning the benchmark on that construction process (§3.1; Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference—precision and efficiency limits:** Reported FP fractions cover recorded alerts from passed checkers, not repository-wide false-positive rates. Main-table interaction costs cover successful runs, so they do not establish total expected resource cost (§4; supplied Appendix H.2 fragment).
- **Judge dependence:** Semantic/process and alert judgments use one LLM judge. Reported pooled human agreement is κ=0.85, but detailed validation sampling and stage-specific results are unavailable here (§5.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which guidance components cause the improvement?** Existing ablations change bundled components and run conditions (§5.4). Next experiment: contemporaneous factorial ablations of workflow skills, analyzer references, and execution feedback with matched versions and repeats.
2. **Do synthesized checkers transfer to unseen defect instances?** Differential and mainline checks do not directly establish cross-repository detection generalization (§4). Next experiment: freeze checkers and test independently labeled positive and negative instances in repository-disjoint projects.
3. **What drives the C/C++ performance deficit?** Language and backend are confounded (Appendix H.4). Next experiment: evaluate matched defect instances with multiple analyzer backends where feasible.
4. **How do explicit shorter deadlines change agent behavior?** Retrospective curves measure completion timing under the original budget (§5.5). Next experiment: rerun matched tasks with declared shorter budgets and independently verify their final artifacts.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Main findings and metric definitions are explicit, but the supplied source is truncated.

Material missing information includes complete verifier/judge rubrics, detailed KNighter comparison conditions, full ablation comparability records, uncertainty estimates for main rankings and prior-exposure effects, judge-validation details, release access/licensing, and final audit/removal statistics that the text says will accompany the frozen release.
