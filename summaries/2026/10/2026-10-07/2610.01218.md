# AGO AI Quality Gate: Evidence-First Release Decisions for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01218) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01218)


## One-sentence takeaway
AGO makes missing evidence, judge validation, and statistical uncertainty explicit release criteria; its public experiments show substantial judge variability and a simulated reduction in unsafe promotion that costs additional review and some release throughput.

## Problem and motivation
Enterprise RAG teams must decide whether to promote, review, or block a version despite incomplete traces, small evaluation sets, and fallible LLM judges. The authors identify a gap between producing quality scores and making accountable release decisions: missing measurements can be mistaken for failures or passes, point estimates conceal uncertainty, and judge reliability may not transfer across domains (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is an integrated decision layer built from established evaluation and statistical components, rather than a new judge model (§1).

- **Evidence-aware decisions:** deterministic checks, local guardrails, and structured LLM scoring feed four outcomes—promote, manual review, block, or not evaluable. Fixed critical violations override profile settings; reports preserve evidence gaps and failure reasons (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Probabilistic regression gating:** separate beta-binomial posteriors for each stratum are aggregated with explicit weights. Baseline–candidate comparisons estimate the probability of regression beyond a profile-defined tolerance. This is an operational hierarchy, not Bayesian partial pooling (§3.4).
- **Per-engagement judge validation:** a human-labelled, balanced golden set supports agreement statistics, threshold sweeps, and uncertainty intervals before judge metrics can become blocking criteria (§3.5).
- **Two-boundary evaluation:** public RAGBench experiments assess judges; synthetic experiments assess release-policy trade-offs. Neither establishes end-to-end production effectiveness (§5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Judge choice materially changes adherence discrimination | Identical stratified RAGBench test instances, 12 datasets, **N=1,200 per judge**: gpt-4.1-nano AUROC **0.603 [0.570, 0.634]** versus gpt-4o **0.783 [0.756, 0.807]**, with 95% bootstrap CIs | Abstract; §5.1, Table 2 discussion |
| Valid output does not establish judge quality | Nano produced **zero protocol errors**; no threshold in the **0.1–0.9** sweep achieved MCC above **0.17** on the reported test evaluation | §5.1, results |
| Stronger-judge reliability varies by dataset | gpt-4o, **N=100 per dataset**: AUROC **0.620–0.883**; MCC at **θ=0.8** ranged **0.040–0.618** | §5.1, Table 3 discussion |
| Decision-grade policy reduces simulated unsafe promotion | **1,000 replicates per scenario/sample size**, **N=20–200**, candidate pass probabilities reduced by **0.05** in every stratum: AGO promoted **22.2%–35.1%** versus naive **29.3%–41.8%**; lower at every tested N | §5.2, Table 4 discussion |
| Protection has operational costs | Same simulation: under no change, decision-grade review/block rate **23.8%–48.6%**; under improvement of **0.05** per stratum, promotion **71.5%–99.4%**, versus naive **76.7%–99.8%** | §5.2 |
| Protection depends on policy threshold | With identical observations/posteriors, balanced profile review threshold **0.50** versus decision-grade **0.40**; balanced unsafe promotion **29.8%–43.9%**, potentially exceeding naive | §5.2 |

These results support measuring judge quality and exposing release-policy trade-offs. They do not establish production incident reduction or end-to-end gate validity. RAGBench labels are LLM-generated references calibrated against humans, and sampling was designed for controlled comparison rather than population estimates (§§2, 5.1). Published evaluator comparisons use different protocols and are explicitly indicative.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated boundaries:** proprietary engagement data prevents public production evaluation; public experiments separately test judges and synthetic statistical gating (§5).
- **Metric mismatch:** holistic context relevance differs from RAGBench’s span-fraction relevance; correlations are exploratory. Answer relevance lacks a counterpart, while utilization and completeness remain unevaluated (§5.1).
- **Modelling limits:** baseline and candidate posteriors are independent; paired comparison and repeated-sampling analysis remain future work. Coverage weights represent evaluation design unless target weights are supplied (§3.4; §6).
- **Analyst inference:** the controlled threshold comparison supports a policy-specific benefit, not a general advantage of Bayesian gating. No reported end-to-end ablation isolates missing-evidence semantics, guardrails, or mandatory judge validation (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Reproducibility gap:** the supplied text contains table captions and narrative summaries, but no table bodies, code link, rubric, or sample manifests.

## Open research questions
- **Does AGO improve real release outcomes?** Production outcomes are unavailable. Next experiment: prospectively compare gate policies on enterprise releases using adjudicated regressions and review effort.
- **How does pairing change decisions?** Shared-item dependence is approximated by independence. Next experiment: compare paired and independent posterior models under controlled item correlations.
- **How stable are judge decisions across repeated calls?** Per-case variance analysis is deferred (§6). Next experiment: repeat frozen judge calls and measure threshold-crossing and release-decision variability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline findings are explicit, but detailed tables and reproducibility artifacts are absent. Material missing information includes per-sample-size outcome rates, full judge statistics, exact rubric and manifests, golden-set validation criteria used in deployments, implementation availability, and independently assessed production outcomes.
