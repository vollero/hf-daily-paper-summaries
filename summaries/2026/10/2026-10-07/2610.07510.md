# Understanding and Enhancing Backdoor Persistency in LLM Agent Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07510) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07510)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the evaluated software-engineering agents, benign SFT weakens inherited backdoors but subsequent RL preserves residual attacks, while PersistBD raises their survival by refining the supplied model before post-training (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
Developers may adapt compromised third-party models into agents without knowing that a trigger activates malicious tool use. The authors examine whether benign supervised fine-tuning (SFT), followed by task-reward reinforcement learning (RL), removes this inherited behavior. Their identified gap is understanding survival across this software-engineering pipeline and whether attackers can deliberately improve persistence before releasing model weights (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Pipeline study:** Track a fixed comment-triggered credential-upload behavior through benign SFT and GRPO RL in three Qwen coder models using SWE-agent. Evaluate attack success separately from false triggering and benign issue resolution (§4).
- **Persistence diagnostics:** Derive a local, first-order explanation involving initial backdoor strength and compatibility between benign and backdoor gradients. Approximately controlled adapter sweeps associate both factors with greater post-SFT survival (§5).
- **PersistBD:** Refine an already-backdoored model with a single merged LoRA adapter whose objective combines backdoor strength, a gradient-compatibility surrogate, and benign-behavior regularization. Main experiments use attacker and developer data from disjoint task instances (§6). The abstract links code; the ethical statement says trained backdoored models will not be released.

## Evidence and main results

ASR measures emission of the specified parsed malicious tool command, using one greedy continuation for each of 100 triggered prefixes per placement setting. Main endpoints are 3,000 SFT updates and 150 RL steps; benign resolved rate (RR) uses 300 SWE-bench Lite tasks (§4.1; Appendix A.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| SFT substantially erodes Base backdoors | Random-position ASR falls from 100% before developer training to 8%, 20%, and 7% after SFT for 3B, 7B, and 30B, respectively. | §4.2; Table 2 |
| RL preserves residual attacks | Base random-position ASR changes from SFT to RL as 8%→7% (3B), 20%→20% (7B), and 7%→8% (30B). | §4.3; Table 2 |
| PersistBD improves survival across scales | Random-position post-SFT/post-RL ASR: 3B, 21%/22% versus Base 8%/7%; 7B, 74%/76% versus 20%/20%; 30B, 37%/36% versus 7%/8%. | §6.2; Table 2 |
| Trigger placement affects observed RL increases | First-position ASR rises from 6% to 12% for 30B Base and from 34% to 42% for 7B PersistBD between SFT and RL. | §4.3; Table 2 |
| Endpoint utility matches or exceeds Base, with an insertion cost | Both 7B variants resolve 27/300 tasks after RL. PersistBD matches or exceeds Base endpoint RR across scales, but 30B insertion reduces clean-model resolution from 101/300 to 20/300 before refinement. | §6.2; Table 2 |
| Diagnostics support the two-factor association | With compatibility approximately held within [−0.009, 0.008], strength-sweep post-SFT ASR ranges from 23% to 95%; compatibility sweeps generally improve ASR at approximately fixed strength. These use the earlier overlapping split. | §5.3; Figure 3; Appendix F |
| Objective ablations reveal coupled effects | On 7B, full PersistBD achieves 74% post-SFT ASR; removing strength yields 5%, while removing compatibility or the benign anchor yields 0%. The latter variants also have released-model FTR of 0.99 and 0.87, respectively. | Table 4; §6.2 |
| Fresh-data continuation further reduces residual ASR | For 7B Base, original-corpus continuation reduces ASR from 20% to 17% over 3,100 additional updates; a fresh unresolved-data branch subsequently reduces 17% to 2% over another 3,100 updates. | Appendix B.1; Figure 4 |
| Supplementary baseline comparison favors PersistBD | Under the overlapping split, 7B post-SFT ASR is 40% for Base, 52% for adapted P-Trojan, 80% for PersistBD, and 100% for their combination. | Appendix F.3; Tables 7–8 |

The evidence establishes survival and enhancement under these specific protocols. It does not establish independent causal effects of the two diagnostic factors, reproducible RL amplification, successful real-world exfiltration, or utility preservation relative to uncompromised models. Zero observed false triggers during RL applies only to the evaluated clean prefixes (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One software-engineering scaffold and fixed malicious target limit generalization. Persistence depends on the selected training procedures and budgets (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The gradient explanation is local and first-order; it does not predict complete SFT trajectories or RL effects. Factor sweeps only approximately separate strength and compatibility (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Fresh-data continuation differs in training budget and trajectory outcomes, preventing attribution to data novelty or a claim of preserved utility (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Diagnostic sweeps and P-Trojan comparisons offer weaker evidence for the main disjoint-data threat model because they use an overlapping split; repositories also recur across main partitions (§4.1; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Small RL changes require replication before being treated as reliable amplification. Main endpoint uncertainty is not reported, and PersistBD-stage seeds are not explicitly fixed (§4.3; Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Attack evaluation measures command emission from supplied trajectory prefixes, leaving full live-episode attack success unestablished (Appendix A.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does RL reliably amplify backdoors?** The authors leave mechanism and reproducibility unresolved (§4.3). Repeat matched branches across seeds with larger trigger evaluations and track uncertainty.
- **What are the independent effects of strength and compatibility?** Sweeps and ablations change multiple properties (§§5.3, 6.2). Construct factorial variants matched on strength, compatibility, and false triggering.
- **Can benign training eliminate PersistBD without sacrificing utility?** Extended SFT tests only the Base backdoor and lacks utility evidence (Appendix B.1). Compare equal-budget fresh-data and same-data continuations for PersistBD while measuring RR.
- **Does persistence transfer beyond the known scaffold and target?** Current assumptions are narrow (Limitations). Test different scaffolds, malicious targets, and attacker–developer domain mismatches.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol-specific findings; **medium** in causal explanations and broader applicability.

Material missing information includes main-result seed replication and confidence intervals, reproducibility of RL increases, disjoint-partition P-Trojan comparisons, utility after extended SFT, live end-to-end attack outcomes, and evaluations across other domains or safeguards. Code availability is asserted in the supplied text but was not independently checked.
