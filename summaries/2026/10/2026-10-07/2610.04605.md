# ConEx: Human-Interpretable Saliency Maps via Concept-Aware Attribution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04605) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04605)


## One-sentence takeaway
ConEx combines automatically grounded visual concepts with model relevance to explain CNN predictions spatially and semantically, while supporting global concept explanations for ViTs; reported comparative gains cannot be quantified because the supplied tables lack numerical entries.

## Problem and motivation
Pixel saliency shows where a classifier responds but often leaves the visual meaning unclear; concept explanations describe attributes but may lack spatial grounding or require curated examples. The authors aim to connect these perspectives without manual concept annotation or classifier retraining, making explanations easier to inspect for meaningful evidence and possible confounds (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Automatic concept discovery and grounding:** GPT-4o generates candidate class attributes through an existing concept-generation procedure; GroundedSAM localizes them, and occurrence and spatial-coverage filters retain candidates (§§3.2, 4.1; Appendix C).
- **Segment-based concept representations:** CAVs are differences between positive and negative embedding centroids. CNN embeddings use layer-wise masking with early neighborhood padding; ViT embeddings average overlapping patch tokens from an unaltered image’s intermediate layer (§§3.3–3.4). The contribution is the automated, architecture-specific construction pipeline; centroid differences and masking draw on prior work.
- **Concept-aware attribution:** For CNNs, pooled CAV weights generate concept-presence maps, which multiply LRP relevance. Concept-masking probability drops weight the final aggregation; dataset aggregation supplies class-level explanations (§3.5).
- **Validation metrics:** VCM measures concept-vector alignment with segment embeddings; CCM measures alignment with class-logit gradients (§3.6). These are proposed alignment measures, rather than direct tests of causal influence.

The text supports a specified method, several complementary evaluation protocols, and author-reported favorable rankings. Missing table entries prevent assessing effect sizes or independently checking those rankings. Object-boundary alignment does not itself establish explanation faithfulness, as the authors acknowledge (Appendix I.1). Likewise, the reported tests do not establish the broader claim that explanations reliably exclude spurious correlations.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated:** GroundedSAM errors can introduce spatial uncertainty; ViT explanations remain global; the multi-stage pipeline is slower than Grad-CAM, without timing measurements supplied (Appendix J). LLM-derived attributes may transfer societal biases into explanations (Impact Statement).

**Analyst inferences from the supplied evidence:**

- Both concept masks and reference object masks used for filtering come from GroundedSAM, so coverage validation is not an independent semantic check (Appendix C).
- VCM evaluates segments using the same embedding mechanism used for construction, while gradient alignment in CCM does not by itself demonstrate causal concept influence (§§3.6, 4.2.2).
- Human understandability evidence covers five ImageNet classes and one classifier; broader populations and practical auditing performance remain unestablished (§4.2.4).
- Reporting inconsistencies impede reproduction: Appendix C alternates between spatial-coverage thresholds of 20% and 0.15, and reports both 3,602 and 4,602 ImageNet concepts. Appendix H.1’s CCM values also require an unstated scale.
- Ablations are described, but missing numerical tables prevent judging their magnitude. Multi-label extension is proposed without reported validation (Appendix D.2).

## Open research questions

- **Can ViTs support faithful local concept maps?** Localization is explicitly unresolved (Appendices E, J). Next experiment: test a concept-specific token-to-pixel attribution method against annotated parts and controlled part interventions.
- **How sensitive are explanations to grounding errors and inherited attribute biases?** Both dependencies are acknowledged, but their effects are not quantified. Next experiment: vary attribute generators and grounders, inject mask errors, and evaluate semantic accuracy and faithfulness against independently annotated concepts.
- **Do gains persist for classifiers that use known spurious cues?** The paper claims robustness without a dedicated reported spurious-correlation experiment. Next experiment: train classifiers with controlled background-label correlations and measure whether explanations expose the background dependence under distribution shift.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Method and scope are clearly described, but comparative performance remains largely an author claim in this retrieval.

Material missing information includes numerical entries for all referenced tables, uncertainty estimates and significance tests, CCM scaling, resolution of threshold/count inconsistencies, participant demographics, runtime and memory measurements, exact generation prompts, and verified code or asset contents.
