# Attacca: Goal-Directed Control under State Continuity for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07785) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07785)


## One-sentence takeaway
Attacca combines cross-world visual goals, supervised target grounding, and behavioral-phase conditioning to improve Minecraft task execution across inherited states, achieving 54%, 30%, and 28% completion on three scripted task chains (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Completing one task changes the agent’s position, camera orientation, and world, potentially leaving the next target outside its view. The authors call the resulting difficulty the **skill hand-off gap**: planning a sequence does not ensure that the controller can execute its next step (Section 1).

They identify two shortcomings in existing visual goal policies: evaluation often assumes initially visible targets, and goals sampled from the demonstration’s own world permit scene-matching shortcuts. Attacca instead studies continuous search, approach, and interaction with visual references from another world (Sections 2–3).

## Approach and contributions
Attacca initializes from ROCKET-2 and trains a visual goal-conditioned causal Transformer through behavior cloning plus two auxiliary objectives (Section 3; Appendix C).

- **Context-decoupled goal sampling:** Each demonstration receives a masked image of the same target class from a different world, resampled during training to remove direct scene and pose correspondence (Section 3.2).
- **Target grounding with action feedback:** A patch-level mask head learns target visibility and location using binary cross-entropy and Dice losses. Its predictions enter the policy through a residual vector; absent targets receive zero masks (Section 3.3).
- **Behavioral-phase conditioning:** A supervised head predicts Search, Approach, or Interact, then modulates the action readout through FiLM. Both auxiliary heads use stopped gradients through their prediction-feedback paths (Section 3.4).
- **Training and evaluation assets:** HumanPlay demonstrations cover complete search-to-interact behavior with renderer-derived masks and phase labels; evaluation includes held-out target classes and task chains preserving execution state. Public release is not established (Appendices B, D–E).

The evidence supports improved low-level target-directed control in the tested Minecraft settings. The diamond-stage analysis suggests that maintaining and acting on a discovered target matters alongside search. It does not establish autonomous planning, unrestricted Minecraft competence, or transfer to physical robots. The authors’ scene-matching explanation is supported by controlled goal changes and rollout examples, but remains a mechanistic interpretation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Goal selection, completion detection, GUI operations, quotas, and item changes depend on scripted controllers and helpers. An integrated learned-planner system has not been evaluated (Appendices E, H).
- **Author-stated:** All experiments are in Minecraft; robotic navigation and manipulation remain future work (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** One fixed world per long-horizon chain limits evidence for robustness across layouts, despite variation in policy seeds and inherited states (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Different native goal interfaces complicate cross-method attribution. Fine-tuned cross-world ROCKET-2 provides the closest comparison, sharing data, initialization, and evaluation goals (Section 4.2; Appendix F).
- **Evidence gap:** The supplied component ablation starts with target grounding already enabled; it does not expose a full factorial isolation of sampling, grounding supervision, feedback, and phase conditioning (Section 4.4).
- **Reporting gap:** Several table bodies are absent, including architecture settings and ablation uncertainties. Section 4.4 also inconsistently references the component-ablation table.

## Open research questions
- **Does the gain survive autonomous goal dispatch?** The controller and helpers are scripted (Appendix H). Next experiment: integrate a learned planner and goal-image library, reporting planner, controller, and completion-detection failures separately.
- **How robust is hand-off across layouts and longer chains?** Each evaluated chain has a fixed world (Appendix E). Next experiment: vary layouts and chain length while measuring both unconditional completion and conditional transition success.
- **Which components independently cause the improvement?** Existing ablations show interaction between feedback and phase conditioning but incompletely isolate all components (Section 4.4). Next experiment: run a matched factorial ablation of goal sampling, grounding supervision, feedback, and phase conditioning.
- **Can the supervision scheme transfer beyond Minecraft?** Training masks come from the renderer, and no other domain is evaluated (Appendices B, H). Next experiment: test robotic navigation or manipulation with measured annotation noise.
