# Rationale-Guided Policy Optimization: Learning to Reason with Adaptive Rationale Scaffolding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-10-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.07342) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07342)


## One-sentence takeaway
RGPO improves reported no-hint reasoning performance by adaptively supplying rationale prefixes during training and distilling only verifier-improving, policy-generated solutions back onto the original prompts (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can stall when every sampled solution is wrong, leaving little useful reward variation. The authors identify a gap between unguided exploration and direct imitation of external solutions: expert traces can supply successful reasoning, but their format and reasoning style may poorly match the current policy (§1).

RGPO aims to make difficult problems learnable while retaining independent exploration and avoiding dependence on hints at inference time.

## Approach and contributions
- **Guided generation with unguided training:** Preserve the ordinary RLVR update, then generate standalone solutions using rationale prefixes and verifier feedback. Accept a guided candidate only when its reward exceeds the best unguided response; train its likelihood under the original prompt (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Adaptive scaffolding:** Start with an unguided epoch. Subsequently increase each example’s revealed prefix after an epoch without an unguided success, and decrease it after a success. Experiments segment rationales by character count (§4.1; Algorithm 1).
- **Conditional theoretical analysis:** Establish when guidance increases successful-trajectory discovery at equal response budgets, and analyze transfer, hint removal, and rare-success learning in a stylized model (Appendix B).
- **Empirical extensions:** Test language and vision-language models, corrupted rationales, and solver-trace or diagnostic-feedback guidance. The authors announce an [implementation](https://github.com/VietHoang1512/rgpo); its contents are not verified here (Abstract; §§5, D.4–D.6).

The distinguishing combination is adaptive guidance, verifier-gated policy-generated targets, and distillation onto unguided prompts; adaptive hints alone also appear in the described prior work (§2).

These results support improved verifier-scored, no-hint performance in the tested settings. They do not establish faithful reasoning, universal gains across tasks or backbones, or lower total compute cost. Table 1 averages combine different evaluation metrics, while Table 2’s broader model comparisons are explicitly not all matched for backbone, data, or training recipe (§C.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Guidance quality and granularity matter; excessive answer information can induce memorization, while insufficient or mismatched guidance may not rescue failures. Feedback-only training requires task-specific diagnostics (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** Acceptance guarantees improvement only under the verifier, allowing verifier bias, reward hacking, and formatting gains. Guided generation and auxiliary updates add compute (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Training-step efficiency does not establish wall-clock or token efficiency. Budget accounting is also inconsistent across settings: the simulation gives GRPO four unguided samples and RGPO additional guided samples, despite describing a shared optimization budget (§C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Neural-model result tables lack reported uncertainty, and the supplied text does not fully establish matched training budgets for every baseline. Robustness comparisons lack a fully specified backbone and evaluation protocol (§§5.2–5.3, D.6).
- **Analyst inference:** The rare-success theorem concerns a two-response policy with monotonic updates and a useful-hint assumption; it does not guarantee neural-model convergence (§B.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does RGPO improve accuracy per unit of compute?** Step-count gains coexist with additional training work. Compare against GRPO under matched generated-token and GPU-hour budgets (§5.3; Appendix E).
2. **How much does adaptive prefix scheduling contribute independently?** Existing comparisons emphasize full hints and synthetic ablations. Test fixed partial prefixes, random prefixes, and adaptive prefixes with matched guided-sample counts (§§5.1, 5.3).
3. **When does diagnostic feedback replace rationales successfully?** The rationale-free result is limited to 24-Points. Evaluate feedback-only training on additional verifiable tasks with controlled diagnostic specificity (§D.4).
4. **Do accepted targets improve reasoning beyond answer formatting?** The authors acknowledge verifier-only guarantees. Evaluate accepted trajectories with independent process checks and separate correctness from formatting rewards (Appendix E; §A.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reported outcomes, but leaves material comparison and reproducibility details unresolved.

Unavailable or ambiguous information includes exact prompt templates referenced but not rendered, actual selected auxiliary-loss weights, neural-experiment seed counts and uncertainty, complete baseline budget accounting, absolute efficiency thresholds and total training costs, detailed protocols for supplementary robustness/24-Points evaluations, and implementation or checkpoint availability beyond the authors’ announcement.
