# Verifier-Induced Support Reshaping in On-Policy Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00220) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00220)


## One-sentence takeaway

On-policy RL with verifiable rewards can improve its immediate objective while making successful trajectories for a later objective too rare to sample, largely by changing the model’s opening response route.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether sequential RLVR preserves not only prior task performance but also the successful trajectories needed to learn future objectives. The authors argue that conventional catastrophic-forgetting evaluations are retrospective, whereas on-policy learning also requires a forward-looking notion of trainability: reward-positive trajectories must remain likely enough to appear within a finite rollout budget (Section 1; Figure 1).

They call changes to this availability **verifier-induced support reshaping** and define **effective rewardable support** as successful trajectories reachable under a specified sampling budget. The motivating gap is that endpoint pass rates can hide loss of prompt coverage, reward variation, and joint capabilities needed by later optimization.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Support-based evaluation of continual RLVR.** The authors use repeated verifier-scored sampling—primarily 32 rollouts per prompt—to measure pass@1, best@k, and all-wrong/mixed/all-correct prompt groups (Sections 3 and B.4).

2. **Bidirectional cross-task study.** Starting from Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen2.5-Math-7B, they compare Math-RLVR and instruction-following RLVR (IF-RLVR), then train in both sequential orders to test effects on later learning (Sections 3–4).

3. **Mechanistic localization and intervention.** Teacher-forced token-distribution comparisons localize the largest changes to the response opening; forced-token and forced-prefix interventions test whether opening-route choice affects later math searchability (Section 5).

4. **Preservation and joint-capability tests.** The study evaluates reference-policy KL constraints, supervised routing priors, on-policy distillation, and same-response correctness-plus-compliance on MathIF and ReasonIF (Sections 4.3, 6, and 7).

The results establish, for the tested policies, verifiers, tasks, and rollout budgets, that a higher single-sample score can coexist with lower best-of-\(k\) coverage and weaker later reward variation. The interventions support a causal effect of observable opening-route selection on math searchability in these settings.

They do not establish erasure of mathematical ability, irreversibility, a universal mechanism across objectives or model families, or statistical significance for the joint-capability comparisons. They also do not show that all later-token changes are irrelevant.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Each training configuration has only one fixed-seed run; repeated rollouts characterize within-policy sampling variation, not variability across training runs (Section 3; Appendix A.2).

- **Author-stated:** Effective support depends on the rollout budget, so best@32 is not a budget-independent characterization of policy support (Sections 1 and 8).

- **Author-stated:** Opening labels describe visible output form, not internal reasoning state (Sections 4.1.2 and 5.2).

- **Author-stated:** The JS analysis is a zero-padded, renormalized top-64-union approximation rather than a full-vocabulary comparison (Appendix D.1).

- **Author-stated:** Reverse opening interventions are not symmetric across models; DAI-side forcing is harmful for Qwen3 but neutral or beneficial in some Qwen2.5-Math settings (Section 5.3).

- **Author-stated:** The four-state OPD scan cannot identify the best teacher over the full trajectory (Section 6.2.2).

- **Author-stated:** The joint tests use independent step-720 endpoints and make no statistical-significance claim; filtering also removes unsupported or incompletely scored prompts (Section 7; Appendix F).

- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is limited by two closely related Qwen model families, two verifier objectives, and mostly math/constraint-following evaluations.

- **Analyst inference:** Single-seed training makes it unclear whether the reported support shifts and mitigation rankings are stable across optimization randomness.

- **Analyst inference:** The deterministic opening classifier’s reported precision exceeds 98.5% on 500 spot-checked rollouts, but no recall, confusion matrix, or independent human-labeling protocol is reported (Appendix C.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The semantic shortcut audit relies on DeepSeek-V4-Pro; the supplied text does not report human validation or judge-agreement measurements (Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How stable is support reshaping across training seeds and model families?** The paper uses one seed per configuration and two Qwen families. A next experiment could repeat every training order across several seeds and unrelated architectures, reporting confidence intervals for pass@1, best@k, and mixed-group share.

2. **Can support be preserved adaptively instead of through a fixed prior or teacher?** The DRI prior is eventually overwritten, while OPD depends on teacher choice. A next experiment could adjust KL, route regularization, or teacher snapshots online using held-out best@k and mixed-support signals.

3. **Does opening-route control help beyond mathematics and instruction following?** The causal intervention is evaluated on AIME and MATH-500 after IF training. A next experiment could repeat bidirectional training and route interventions for code execution, tool use, or other independently verifiable objectives.

4. **What rollout-budget metric best predicts later learnability?** The paper shows that support is budget-dependent but primarily uses \(k=32\). A next experiment could measure full best@k curves and test which statistic most strongly predicts improvement during a subsequent RLVR stage.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental design, numerical results, and authors’ scoped conclusions because the supplied text includes the main paper and substantial appendices.

Material missing or unresolved information includes exact numerical values embedded only in several figures, uncertainty across independent training seeds, statistical tests for most comparisons, complete implementation details beyond the referenced repository, human validation of the semantic judge, and evidence outside the two tested model families and objective pair.
