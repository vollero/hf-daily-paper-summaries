# Can We Defend Against AI-Generated Video Attacks on Real-World Crisis Events? A Systematic Evaluation of Detectors, Generators and Social Dissemination

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14391) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14391)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors’ crisis-focused RA-Bench evaluation finds that traditional detectors, zero-shot multimodal models, and detection-fine-tuned MLLMs all fail to generalize reliably across generators and dissemination transformations, with some systems depending on timestamp cues or collapsing toward predicting videos as real.

## Problem and motivation

Realistic generated videos of wars, disasters, public emergencies, and other consequential events could be mistaken for authentic evidence and amplify misinformation. The authors argue that prior benchmarks largely use general video content and provide limited systematic evidence about crisis-grounded generation, human deception, generation conditions, and detector robustness after social-media-like processing (Introduction; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **RA-Bench:** 17,886 videos comprising 1,830 real anchors from 338 source videos and 16,056 first-frame-conditioned clips from four open-source and five closed-source generators, organized into 10 social-risk categories and 44 subcategories (Sections 3.1–3.5; Figures 2–3).
- **Broad detector evaluation:** Seven traditional detectors, ten zero-shot multimodal models under three prompt formats, and two fine-tuned MLLM detector families are compared across generation sources (Sections 4.1.1–4.1.3).
- **Controlled generation analysis:** The study examines quality dimensions, text-to-video versus first- and first+last-frame conditioning, and three sampling seeds (Section 4.2).
- **Human-focused assets:** RA-Bench-HumanProof contains 633 generated clips labeled real by five reviewers; RA-Bench-LastMile applies controlled transcoding, spatial, temporal, and presentation transformations (Sections 4.3.2–4.3.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional-detector transfer is weak and source-dependent. | Public-reference AUCs of 67.6–98.6% become seven-detector source means of 43.9–57.3% on nine RA-Bench sources; 26/63 detector–source AUCs are below 50%. References are contextual, not matched-domain baselines. | Section 4.1.1; Table 2 |
| Changing temporal sampling offers only a small gain. | Global–Local-8 improves five-detector mean AUC by 0.99 points over Uniform-8 under the same eight-frame budget (95% CI 0.69–1.29). | Section 4.1.1; Figure 6 |
| Zero-shot multimodal performance depends on prompt and source. | Gemini reaches 63.4% Binary BAcc, 63.1% Diagnostic BAcc, and 63.6% Rating AUC overall, but Binary BAcc ranges from 54.3% on Seedance2.0 to 74.5% on LTX. | Section 4.1.2; Table 3 |
| Fine-tuned MLLM results contain protocol and class biases. | Replacing timestamps with identical-frame indices lowers Skyra-SFT/RL mean BAcc from 68.5/69.5% to 54.4/54.9%; BusterX++ FakeR is only 4.1–9.1%. | Section 4.1.3; Tables 4 and 19 |
| Quality dimensions relate differently to detector outputs. | Within-source interquartile increases in Condition Fidelity correspond to a 14.4-point decrease in Gemini Diagnostic FakeR and a 9.5-percentile-point decrease in the traditional-detector mean; these are adjusted associations, not causal effects. | Sections 4.2.1 and C.1; Table 22 |
| Conditioning has detector-family-specific effects. | Across T2V, first-frame I2V, and first+last-frame I2V, traditional-detector mean AUC is 33.4%, 50.9%, and 44.6%, while mean fine-tuned-MLLM FakeR is 70.5%, 42.7%, and 28.3%. | Section 4.2.2; Table 5 |
| Source-level patterns are stable across the three tested seeds. | Source-level seven-detector mean AUC varies by at most 1.05 points; the 28 detector–source cells have pairwise Spearman correlations of 0.978–0.989. | Sections 4.2.3 and C.3; Tables 6 and 25 |
| Human recognition varies strongly by source. | Reviewers label 68.6% of open-source versus 52.9% of closed-source generated videos as generated; Seedance2.0 and Kling reach 40.7% and 45.1% FakeR. | Section 4.3.1; Table 7 |
| Human-deceptive cases also challenge detectors. | On 633 clips labeled real by five reviewers, traditional detectors average 47.5% AUC; Gemini Binary/Diagnostic reach 54.7/54.5% BAcc; timestamp-free Skyra checkpoints each reach 53.7% BAcc. | Section 4.3.2; Table 8 |
| Dissemination transformations suppress fake detection. | Under LastMile Full, traditional mean AUC falls from 51.4% to 47.3%, while mean FakeR across five fine-tuned configurations falls from 46.0% to 1.4%, reflecting a shift toward real predictions. | Section 4.3.3; Figure 11 and Table 31 |

These results support the authors’ claim that none of the evaluated detector families provides consistent cross-source and cross-protocol reliability in this benchmark. They do not prove that every current or future detector fails, establish causal effects for observational quality associations, or measure real-world misinformation prevalence and impact.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The benchmark is a snapshot covering nine I2V sources and visual-only detection; rapidly evolving generators and detectors may change its difficulty (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Generation and dissemination are evaluated as separate controlled stages, excluding richer attack chains involving selective editing, synthesized audio, captions, and repeated platform processing (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Human judgments were collected in a controlled interface without surrounding social context (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** HumanProof is conditionally selected, is skewed toward Kling and Seedance2.0, includes repeated real anchors, and cannot use its selection judgments as an independent human-accuracy estimate (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The public-reference detector scores are explicitly not matched-domain baselines, so the reported performance drop mixes domain, dataset, and protocol differences and should not be interpreted as a controlled causal comparison (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference:** Closed-source generators lack controlled-seed evaluation because their APIs do not expose seeds, limiting seed-stability evidence to four open-source generators (Section 4.2.3).
- **Analyst inference:** LastMile uses 150 anchors and synthetic, predefined transformations; this establishes sensitivity to those operations, not robustness under the full diversity of organic platform pipelines (Appendix D.3).
- **Source limitation:** The supplied paper text is truncated, with 20,114 characters omitted from the appendix. Coverage of omitted implementation details, extended tables, and any additional qualifications is therefore incomplete.

## Open research questions

1. **Can detectors generalize to unseen generators without exploiting protocol metadata?** The evaluated methods remain source-sensitive or timestamp-dependent. A next experiment could train with explicit metadata randomization and evaluate on held-out generator families using both timestamp and frame-index protocols.

2. **Which forensic signals survive realistic multi-stage dissemination?** LastMile tests a controlled set of transformations, not repeated real-platform processing. A next experiment could apply logged, sequential processing pipelines from several platforms and measure cue survival at each stage.

3. **Does audio-visual evidence improve crisis-video detection?** RA-Bench removes audio and evaluates visual evidence only. A next experiment could create matched authentic and synthesized audio-video pairs and compare visual-only, audio-only, and fused detectors.

4. **How do social context and provenance cues affect people and detectors jointly?** Human review omits captions, accounts, engagement signals, and surrounding posts. A next experiment could randomize contextual credibility cues while holding video content fixed and measure changes in human judgments and multimodal-model predictions.
