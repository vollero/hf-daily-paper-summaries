# Forecast Collapse in Time-Series Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14106) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14106)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that low target predictability and per-series training objectives jointly produce flat, poorly ranked panel forecasts, and that their composite MSE–correlation objective, CalibRank, improves cross-sectional ranking while controlling forecast scale.

## Problem and motivation

The paper studies “forecast collapse”: point forecasts with both very low amplitude and poor cross-sectional ordering. This matters when downstream decisions depend on ranking many series at each timestamp, as in equity selection, because ordinary per-series errors can miss failures in that joint structure.

The motivating contrast is that the same forecasting protocol collapses on hourly equity returns but largely not on traded-volume changes. The authors identify a gap in conventional time-series training and evaluation: low predictability constrains calibrated point forecasts, while objectives and benchmarks that treat series independently do not identify or score cross-series relationships (Sections 1, 3.2, and 4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Two-mechanism explanation.** The paper separates amplitude attenuation caused by limited predictable variance from poor ordering caused by per-series objectives leaving cross-series coupling unidentified (Sections 4–5).

2. **Formal results.** It derives an attenuation relation between forecast-target correlation and predictable variance, proves that optimally rescaled amplitude equals absolute correlation for centered forecasts, and constructs equal-risk forecasts with different cross-sectional correlations (Propositions 4.1–4.3; Example 5.4).

3. **CalibRank objective.** CalibRank combines scalar MSE with differentiable Pearson correlation computed across series at each timestamp, with \(\lambda\) controlling the calibration–ranking tradeoff (Equation 24, Section 6).

4. **Multi-setting validation.** The claims are tested with controlled synthetic panels, 12 forecasting backbones, TimesFM and Chronos over 97 GIFT-Eval configurations, and Finance1K (Sections 7–9).

5. **Released evaluation asset.** Finance1K provides aligned hourly return and volume-change targets for 1,000 US equities, including chronological splits, ticker order, validity masks, and checksums (Sections 7.1 and 11).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| MSE exhibits collapse on returns. | On Finance1K close returns using the controlled encoder, MSE gives raw amplitude \(0.027\pm0.005\), IC \(0.046\pm0.003\), and MSE \((4.30\pm0.13)\times10^{-4}\), over three seeds. | Table 4; Section 8.1 |
| Pure ranking improves IC but destroys scale calibration. | IC-only training reaches IC \(0.124\pm0.000\), but raw amplitude \(25.260\pm1.114\) and MSE \((4.22\pm2.84)\times10^{-1}\) on the same return task. | Table 4; Section 8.1 |
| CalibRank balances the two objectives. | Uniform CalibRank at \(\lambda=1\) reaches IC \(0.126\pm0.002\), raw amplitude \(1.836\pm0.118\), and MSE \((1.43\pm0.06)\times10^{-3}\). Relative to MSE, this is about a 2.7-fold IC increase while remaining within a factor of two of target amplitude. | Table 4; Section 8.1 |
| The ranking benefit generalizes across tested architectures. | Across 12 backbones, mean IC rises from 0.062 under MSE to 0.123 under CalibRank; mean MSE changes from \(4.3\times10^{-4}\) to \(5.8\times10^{-4}\). Every backbone improves in IC. | Table 5; Section 9.1 |
| The theoretical amplitude identity is recovered synthetically. | Across two decades of SNR and three skill levels, held-out best-scaled amplitude matches Equation 5 within 0.0015 and correlation within 0.0021. | Figure 2; Section 8.3 |
| Equal per-series risk need not imply equal cross-sectional quality. | In the two-series Gaussian construction, squared-error risk remains 1.40 while expected IC changes from 0.371 to 0.194 to 0.032 as coupling varies. | Example 5.4, Section 5.5 |
| Pretrained-model amplitude is associated with achieved predictability. | Across 97 GIFT-Eval configurations, raw amplitude correlates with \(\sqrt{R^2}\) at 0.88 for Chronos and 0.87 for TimesFM; both retain under 6% amplitude in the six configurations with achieved \(R^2<0.05\). | Figure 3(f); Section 8.4 |
| The effect weakens on the more predictable target. | On Finance1K volume change, MSE gives amplitude 0.391 and IC 0.530, while CalibRank gives amplitude 0.391 and IC 0.538 under the matched protocol. | Table 4; Section 8.5 |
| Improvement transfers to an unoptimized decision metric. | Held-out decision return is \(0.052\pm0.010\) for MSE, \(0.143\pm0.029\) for IC-only, and \(0.170\pm0.030\) for weighted CalibRank. | Figure 3(g); Section 8.5 |

The evidence supports an exact best-scaled amplitude identity under the stated assumptions, an empirical calibration–ranking tradeoff on Finance1K, and consistent ranking gains across the 12 tested backbones. It does not establish that baseline-achieved \(R^2\) is the true predictability ceiling, that raw pretrained-model amplitudes obey the theoretical bound, or that the cross-sectional failure generalizes beyond the single real financial panel.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The 97-configuration GIFT-Eval analysis uses achieved \(R^2\) from fitted baselines, which is only a lower bound on available predictability; it therefore shows association rather than testing the theoretical ceiling (Sections 8.4 and 11).
- The cross-sectional diagnosis is demonstrated on one real panel. Twelve backbones reduce architecture-specific risk but do not establish cross-domain generality (Section 11).
- Finance1K’s coverage and volatility stratification do not support market-wide performance claims (Section 11).
- CalibRank targets Pearson correlation, not rank dependence, tails, calibrated scenarios, or a full joint distribution (Sections 4.2 and 11).
- The scalarized frontier is not fundamental; listwise or other regression-compatible objectives could dominate it (Section 11).
- The method requires a meaningful cross-section and does not directly apply to a single series (Section 11).
- IC aggregation can suffer Simpson effects, and a ceiling based on average predictability is Jensen-loose; the paper consequently avoids comparing time-averaged IC with a pooled ceiling percentage (Section 11).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The reported uncertainty is primarily three-seed standard deviation; formal significance tests or confidence intervals for most model comparisons are not provided.
- The main \(\lambda=1\) operating point is prespecified, but the weighted \(\lambda\)-sweep and estimated unit-amplitude crossing are explicitly post hoc, so they should not be read as independently validated deployment choices (Sections 7.4 and 8.2).
- The held-out decision-return comparison comes from weighted controlled-encoder runs rather than the uniform headline configuration, limiting direct attribution to the exact headline objective (Sections 7.4 and 8.5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does forecast collapse occur outside finance?** The real cross-sectional evidence comes from Finance1K alone. A next experiment could repeat the matched MSE/CalibRank protocol on aligned traffic, energy, sensor, or clinical panels using their decision-relevant dependence metric.

2. **Can stronger joint objectives dominate scalarized CalibRank?** The authors acknowledge that Pearson correlation captures only one functional and that listwise objectives may yield a better frontier. A next experiment could compare Pearson, Spearman, listwise, tail-dependence, and probabilistic joint losses under identical backbones and validation constraints.

3. **How closely does true predictability determine raw model amplitude?** Baseline-achieved \(R^2\) does not estimate the true ceiling. A next experiment could use domains with known data-generating processes or controlled information sets to estimate predictable variance and separately measure raw and optimally rescaled amplitude.

4. **How should \(\lambda\) adapt under time-varying predictability?** The results show early IC saturation and continued amplitude growth, while the paper notes temporal drift. A next experiment could select a time-varying \(\lambda\) solely on rolling chronological validation windows and test it on untouched future periods.
