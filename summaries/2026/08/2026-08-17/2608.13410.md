# Who Speaks Matters: Authority-Aware Multi-View RAG over Italian Parliamentary Proceedings

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13410) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13410)


## One-sentence takeaway

ParliamentRAG combines query-dependent speaker authority, per-group evidence selection, and deterministic quotation extraction to improve source coverage and quotation fidelity in summaries of Italian parliamentary debates, though its advantages over NotebookLM were not statistically significant on a 15-topic evaluation.

## Problem and motivation

Parliamentary positions are distributed across many sessions and speakers, making manual reconstruction costly and ordinary keyword search insufficient for multi-perspective synthesis. The authors identify three risks in applying RAG to political proceedings: overrepresenting frequent speakers, treating topical experts and incidental commenters alike, and fabricating or misattributing quotations (Sections 1–2).

The proposed system targets citizens and political professionals who need balanced summaries across parliamentary groups, topic-sensitive source authority, and traceability to official transcripts.

## Approach and contributions

- **Authority-aware retrieval and ranking:** ParliamentRAG computes an interpretable, query-dependent authority score from profession, education, committee membership, legislative activity, prior interventions, and institutional roles. Activity signals use temporal decay, and group changes are resolved at query time (Section 4.2).
- **Multi-view evidence selection:** Dense chunk retrieval is combined with graph retrieval through relevant legislative acts, their signatories, and optionally topic-matched committees. Ranking also incorporates relevance, speaker diversity, group coverage, authority, and salience (Sections 4.1–4.2).
- **Group-structured generation:** The system selects a high-authority representative from each group and follows an Analyze–Generate–Integrate–Cite pipeline, explicitly reporting when no supporting evidence exists (Section 4.3; Figure 3).
- **Quotation fidelity by construction:** The language model produces offset placeholders rather than quotation text; these are deterministically replaced with substrings from raw transcripts (Section 4.3).
- **Integrated parliamentary knowledge graph and evaluation:** The authors construct a Neo4j graph from Italian Chamber data and evaluate the complete system against NotebookLM using automated criteria and 67 paired judgments from six experts (Sections 3 and 5).

The evidence supports exact quotation extraction by the proposed architecture in the tested outputs and suggests a consistent, small source-oriented advantage, alongside weaker prose quality. It does not establish statistically reliable superiority, isolate which architectural components caused the results, or provide a fully independent end-to-end retrieval comparison: NotebookLM received curated chunks partly selected and reranked by ParliamentRAG.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The benchmark contains only 15 topics and has moderate statistical power; no human-evaluation difference remained significant after multiple-comparison correction (Sections 5.2 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** The study evaluates the full system without internal ablations, so the effects of dual-channel retrieval, authority scoring, per-group generation, and other components remain unresolved (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** NotebookLM was evaluated on a curated, topic-specific context rather than the complete corpus, making this primarily a response-quality comparison under curated retrieval conditions (Sections 5.1 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** Group coverage is not absolute because a group cannot be represented when retrieval finds no relevant evidence; only quotation extraction receives a structural guarantee (Sections 5.2 and 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Authority validity is uncertain because component weights are set empirically from expert judgment, and the paper reports no learned calibration, sensitivity analysis, or external ground-truth evaluation of who qualifies as an expert (Section 4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is narrow: evaluation covers one chamber, one legislature, Italian-language proceedings, ten groups, and a fixed “positions on topic” query template (Sections 3 and 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is partial. Code is released and the graph is described as reconstructible, but the supplied text does not state that the exact evaluation outputs, expert judgments, or topic-level benchmark artifacts are released (Supplemental Material Statement).

## Open research questions

1. **Which architectural component produces the source-oriented gains?** This remains open because no internal ablation was performed. A next experiment could remove graph retrieval, authority reranking, per-group generation, and deterministic citation handling one at a time.

2. **Does the authority score correspond to independent judgments of topical expertise?** The weights are expert-designed, while evaluation mainly measures authority using the same model. A next experiment could collect blinded topical-expertise rankings from parliamentary specialists and compare learned, fixed, and metadata-free scoring variants.

3. **How does ParliamentRAG compare in a genuinely end-to-end retrieval setting?** NotebookLM received curated ParliamentRAG-derived context, so retrieval quality was not independently compared. A next experiment could benchmark systems with equal corpus access and measure evidence recall, group recall, latency, and final-answer quality separately.

4. **Do the results generalize across institutions and time periods?** The paper evaluates only the Italian Chamber’s XIX Legislature. A next experiment could reproduce the protocol on earlier legislatures, the Italian Senate, and another national parliament while testing robustness to different party systems and metadata schemas.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, evaluation design, and numerical results because the supplied text includes the complete method and result sections.

Material missing information includes topic-level scores and variance, corrected p-values and confidence intervals, detailed authority-decay formulas, sensitivity analyses for ranking weights, retrieval recall or latency measurements, model decoding settings, the exact composition of partial expert assignments, and confirmation that all benchmark inputs and raw human judgments are publicly released.
