# MobileMem: Learning from a Year of Mobile Experiences

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13606) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13606)


## One-sentence takeaway

MobileMem introduces text and multimodal benchmarks plus the KEME synthesis framework for evaluating long-horizon on-device personal memory, finding that current systems remain weak on temporal, multi-hop, visual, and abstention tasks and are often too costly for mobile deployment.

## Problem and motivation

Persistent mobile assistants must accumulate and update knowledge from heterogeneous, cross-application, multimodal experiences over months or years. Existing memory benchmarks largely emphasize conversations or cloud-style settings and do not capture fragmented app activity, evolving preferences, privacy concerns, or mobile constraints such as storage, computation, latency, and power (Sections 1–2.3, pp. 1–6).

The authors address the lack of realistic, privacy-conscious evaluation data for on-device memory. Collecting genuine year-scale personal trajectories is expensive and sensitive, motivating synthesis grounded in limited real user profiles, app metadata, and temporal knowledge anchors (Section 3.1, pp. 6–8).

## Approach and contributions

- **MobileMem benchmark:** A textual setting in which seven app types send templated records to a system-level memory layer. It tests recall, multi-hop and temporal reasoning, relationship understanding, summarization, preferences, and unanswerable-question recognition (Sections 2.3 and 4.1).

- **MobileMem-Omni benchmark:** A bilingual multimodal setting where screenshots expose otherwise disconnected app interactions. It contains 16 trajectories, 19,060 images, and 7,415 QA pairs across seven task categories (Section 5.1, Table 3).

- **KEME synthesis framework:** A knowledge-guided engine that combines top-down hierarchical temporal planning with bottom-up experience-driven revision, grounds immutable prior sessions, tracks persona evolution, and synthesizes QA pairs over increasingly broad contexts (Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3).

- **Composite memory architecture:** The paper proposes a system-level “blackboard” for cross-app abstractions alongside application-specific memories that filter, store, and retrieve domain information through standardized interfaces (Section 2.2, Figure 3). This is an architectural vision rather than a deployed system result.

- **Comparative evaluation:** Long-context, RAG, structured-memory, and multimodal-memory methods are compared for answer quality and, where reported, memory-construction token cost (Sections 4.2 and 5.2).

These results support the authors’ claim that current memory systems struggle with evidence retrieval, updating, answer generation, multimodal reasoning, and resource cost. They do not establish real-device latency, energy consumption, storage use, privacy guarantees, or effectiveness during live continual interaction. The KEME hardness study is suggestive rather than definitive because it uses only 10 selected questions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Reconstructed long-horizon data can contain noise, inaccuracies, and inconsistencies that quality control cannot fully eliminate (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** User models remain coarse and may miss the complexity of evolving preferences, habits, goals, and context (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** The evaluated systems and models may not represent future state-of-the-art personal agents (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Global trajectory consistency lacks a reliable automatic metric. Manual inspection found minor numerical discrepancies, and some synthesized multi-hop questions were compositionally unnatural (Section 3.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Population validity is narrow: MobileMem draws on two volunteers, while MobileMem-Omni uses eight real and eight LLM-generated profiles. Generalization to broader demographics is therefore unestablished (Section 3.1).

- **Analyst inference:** MobileMem-Omni omits KEME’s bottom-up revision loop because trajectories can exceed two million tokens, and its dialogues are generated from textual memory-point descriptions rather than the resulting images. This may weaken visual–dialogue coupling and explicit persona evolution (Sections 3.2.2–3.2.3).

- **Analyst inference:** LLM-as-a-Judge introduces measurement uncertainty; the reported 80.7% human agreement for MobileMem-Omni leaves a meaningful disagreement rate, while equivalent human validation is not reported for the MobileMem judge (Sections 4.2.1 and 5.2.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Claims about on-device suitability are only partially tested. Token cost is measured, but device latency, memory footprint, power, bandwidth, and privacy leakage are not quantitatively evaluated (Tables 1 and 4; Figure 7).

- **Analyst inference:** The paper alternately describes the benchmark as grounded in authentic smartphone activity and as synthesized from profiles, metadata, and generated content. Exact proportions of authentic versus synthetic events are not supplied, making ecological realism difficult to assess (Sections 3.1 and 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Do benchmark rankings transfer to real mobile histories?** Most content is synthesized from a small user base, so fidelity is unresolved. A next experiment could evaluate the same systems on consented, fully anonymized real trajectories and compare rankings and error distributions.

2. **Can memory quality improve while meeting actual device budgets?** The paper measures token construction cost but not latency, storage, energy, or thermal impact. A next experiment could deploy representative systems on mobile hardware and report accuracy–latency–energy–storage Pareto curves.

3. **How much does each KEME component contribute?** The full and Omni pipelines differ in anchor grounding, bottom-up revision, and QA construction, but controlled component ablations are not reported. A next experiment could remove each component while measuring consistency, diversity, question validity, and downstream difficulty.

4. **Can multimodal memories preserve visual evidence without caption interference?** Raw images help visual questions, while captions can degrade text-oriented retrieval. A next experiment could compare captions, visual embeddings, structured OCR/UI parsing, and learned cross-modal memory units under identical retrieval budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and tabulated results; **medium** for broader claims about realism and on-device applicability.

Material missing information includes the size and statistics of the textual MobileMem dataset, exact data splits, prompt and sampling details beyond those summarized, per-language numerical results behind Figure 9, numerical token costs behind Figure 7, device-level resource measurements, inter-annotator details for human checks, complete proportions of real versus generated content, and precise release contents and licensing.
