# Scaling Domain Data Repetition in LLM Pretraining

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14071) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14071)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under fixed tokens per parameter, the empirically optimal repetition of a single high-quality domain rises mildly with model size, varies strongly by domain loss, and is nearly unchanged across the tested unique-data fractions.

## Problem and motivation

Scaling LLMs requires larger token budgets, but high-quality domain data is harder to expand than general web data. Its mixture share may therefore shrink with scale, potentially weakening domain learning; repetition can restore that share but risks overfitting. The authors argue that prior cross-scale repetition studies usually hold the total data budget fixed, whereas practical scaling often holds tokens per parameter (TPP) fixed, leaving repetition behavior in this regime insufficiently understood (Introduction; Figure 1; Section 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Fixed-TPP experimental framework:** Models of different, unspecified sizes are trained with \(D_N=\mathrm{TPP}\cdot N\), with TPP stated only as greater than 100. Each run repeats one of four domains—Code, Math, Wiki, or Medical—within otherwise non-repeated web data (Sections 3–4.1).
- **Controlled repetition sweep:** Unique-domain fractions are \(\alpha\in\{1/40,1/20,1/10\}\), repetition counts are \(e\in\{1,\ldots,7\}\), and the total budget is fixed within each model size. Quadratic fits to final validation loss provide continuous estimates of optimal repetition (Sections 3 and 4.1).
- **Empirical scaling findings:** The authors relate estimated optimal repetition to domain validation loss, model size, unique-data fraction, learning-rate schedule, and substitution of repeated for unique data (Figures 4–7).
- **Theoretical explanation:** A power-law one-hot linear-regression model decomposes risk into unrepresented knowledge, knowledge-acquisition error, and noise-fitting error. Theorems characterize effects of noise, fixed-budget model scaling, and proportional data–model scaling (Section 4.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Domain loss is the strongest reported correlate of optimal repetition. | Quadratic-fit optimum versus minimum validation loss: Pearson \(r=-0.944\), pooling domain, model-size, and unique-fraction settings. | Section 4.1, Figure 4 |
| Larger models mildly favor more repetition at fixed TPP. | Estimated optimum versus model size: Pearson \(r=0.400\); total tokens scale proportionally with parameters. | Section 4.1, Figure 4 |
| Tested unique-data fraction has almost no linear association with the optimum. | Estimated optimum versus \(\alpha\): Pearson \(r=0.018\), for \(\alpha\in\{1/40,1/20,1/10\}\). | Sections 3 and 4.1, Figure 4 |
| Optimal ranges differ across domains. | Approximate minima: Math 5–6 repetitions, Code 4–5, Wikipedia 3–4, and Medical 3–4. | Appendix B, Figures 8–11 |
| Repeated data is not universally equivalent to fresh data. | At fixed total domain fraction, Math changes only slightly from 1 to 4 repetitions; Wikipedia degrades sharply beyond 2, while Code and Medical also degrade as repetition replaces unique data. Exact loss values are not supplied in the retrieved text. | Section 5.1, Figure 5; Appendix C, Figure 12 |
| OOD loss is comparatively stable in the tested controlled setting. | With total Math and web fractions fixed, ArXiv and News loss differs only slightly among 1, 2, and 4 repetitions, without a consistent monotonic trend. Exact values are unavailable. | Sections 5.2, Figure 6 |
| Learning-rate scheduling changes repetition tolerance. | WSD decay starts at 0%, 25%, 50%, or 75%, plus a constant-rate condition; later or absent decay delays degradation. | Section 5.3, Figure 7 |
| Theory predicts later optimal stopping with lower noise. | For fixed \(D,N\), Theorem 4.2 gives monotonicity in noise and \(r^*=\log(1/\sigma^2)/[-\log(1-\eta/D)]+O(1)\) as \(\sigma^2\to0\). | Section 4.2, Theorem 4.2 |
| Theory distinguishes fixed-budget from fixed-TPP scaling. | Above an explicit crossover, fixed \(D\) makes \(r^*\) non-increasing with \(N\); when \(D/N\to C_0\), \(r^*=\Theta(D^{\alpha/\beta})\), assuming \(\beta>\alpha\). | Section 4.2, Theorems 4.3–4.4 |

The evidence establishes consistent associations within the tested training grid and supports the authors’ qualitative proxy-model heuristic. It does not establish causality between validation-loss level and repetition tolerance, a universal formula for transferring optima across model scales, or generality beyond the undisclosed model sizes, corpora, and fixed-TPP regime.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only one high-quality domain is repeated per run, so interactions among simultaneously repeated domains remain untested (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** The work does not yet provide a quantitative rule that predicts a large model’s optimum from small-model results (Section 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Exact model sizes, parameter counts, TPP, corpus identities and sizes, validation-set construction, and numerical loss values are absent from the supplied text. This materially limits reproducibility and effect-size assessment.
- **Analyst inference:** The central correlations pool heterogeneous domains and settings; no uncertainty intervals, significance tests, or controls for domain identity are reported in the supplied text (Figure 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Quadratic interpolation can place an optimum between tested integer counts, but no comparison with alternative fit forms or sensitivity analysis is described (Section 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that a proxy-safe count remains safe at larger scale is empirical and qualitative; the reported positive correlation does not by itself guarantee transfer for every domain or model pair.
- **Analyst inference:** OOD repetition analysis is described only for Math and two validation corpora, limiting broader claims about outside-domain effects (Section 5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The linear-regression theory abstracts LLM training substantially and depends on power-law, source-condition, Gaussian-noise, and—in Theorems 4.3–4.4—\(\beta>\alpha\) assumptions (Section 4.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How accurately can small-model optima predict large-model optima?** The paper offers only a conservative qualitative heuristic. A next experiment could fit and prospectively test a transfer rule across held-out model sizes at identical TPP.

2. **What happens when several scarce domains are repeated together?** Every reported run repeats only one domain. A factorial experiment could jointly vary repetition for two or more domains and measure IID, cross-domain, and OOD interference.

3. **Is validation loss itself predictive, or merely a proxy for domain identity?** The strong pooled correlation does not separate these explanations. A next study could use within-domain perturbations of quality or noise and test whether loss changes predict shifts in optimal repetition.

4. **How robust are the optima beyond the tested recipe?** Only three unique-data fractions, repetition counts up to seven, and a limited schedule study are reported. Broader sweeps over TPP, optimizer, model architecture, domain fraction, and schedules could test stability.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The qualitative design, correlations, theoretical statements, and approximate domain-specific optima are clearly reported. Material missing information includes exact model sizes and architectures, exact TPP, dataset names and token counts, validation-set sizes, raw losses, variance across training seeds, confidence intervals, compute costs, detailed preprocessing, and any code/model/data release information.
