# DFM Mimir v1: An Open HRM Delivering Frontier Performance at 1B Parameters Using Only Permissible Post-Training Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13517) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13517)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors train a 1B-parameter Danish–English hierarchical reasoning model from scratch on a 70.48B-token-per-epoch, permissibility-oriented instruction corpus, obtaining the best Danish aggregate score and strong English and math/code results among the reported 1B-class models.

## Problem and motivation

Capable language models typically require large, multi-stage training pipelines and massive corpora, creating computational and data-access barriers for smaller research communities. The problem is particularly acute for Danish, where high-quality permissible data are limited and some data used by the original HRM-Text model do not meet the Danish Foundation Models project’s standards (§1).

The authors aim to produce a compact Danish–English model without prohibited data sources or conventional large-scale pretraining. They also seek to replace non-compliant instruction datasets with synthetic, audited “transplant” variants (§1, §2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Compact bilingual HRM:** Mimir v1 is a 1B-parameter model trained from scratch using the HRM-Text architecture, with two high-level and three low-level reasoning cycles (§3).
- **Post-training-style corpus as initial training:** Training uses 161 instruction, knowledge, reasoning, translation, tool-use, science, and summarization datasets totaling 70,479,308,606 sampled tokens per epoch (§2, Tables 1–4 and 10).
- **Danish-focused permissibility strategy:** The mixture is 68.5% English, 24.7% Danish, and 6.4% bilingual Danish–English; sources are openly available, agreement-supplied, or otherwise considered permissible under the authors’ stated policy (§2.2, §6).
- **Synthetic transplant data:** Seventy generated-and-audited datasets replace non-compliant Sapient-derived tasks, often reformulating classification into free-form generation (§2.1, §2.3).
- **Released implementation:** The authors state that their HRM-Text training framework is openly available (§4). The supplied text does not clearly establish whether Mimir’s weights or every training dataset are publicly released.

These results establish strong benchmark performance for Mimir under the authors’ evaluation configuration, especially on Danish tasks and relative to the listed small models. They do not isolate the effects of the HRM architecture, Danish data, synthetic transplants, corpus size, repetition, tokenizer, or training duration. In particular, the text claims that synthetic replacements perform comparably or better, but supplies no direct original-versus-transplant ablation supporting that causal conclusion.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Mimir trails Gemma 4 E2B on the Math & Code aggregate and has weaker assistant capabilities than state-of-the-art systems (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Reinforcement learning has not been explored for this architecture, and HRM scaling behavior remains unknown (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Full openness has not yet been achieved. Some agreement-supplied data cannot be publicly shared, and future work is intended to improve licensing openness (§2.3, §6).
- **Analyst inference:** The claimed benefit of synthetic transplant datasets is not demonstrated through a controlled ablation in the supplied text. Performance could also reflect other substantial differences in the 161-dataset mixture.
- **Analyst inference:** Training data are highly concentrated: the top ten datasets provide 66.5% of tokens, the top three 38.1%, and several Danish datasets are repeatedly sampled. This may limit attribution and make results sensitive to mixture design (§2.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Some baseline comparisons are not perfectly uniform. Gemma 4 is reported in thinking and non-thinking modes, English shot counts vary by benchmark, and Mimir uses a different serving path because of PrefixLM requirements (Evaluation Setup).
- **Analyst inference:** The paper does not report uncertainty, repeated evaluation runs, contamination checks, safety evaluation, or human assessment of assistant quality.

## Open research questions

1. **How much do synthetic transplants contribute?** No controlled comparison isolates them. Train matched models with original-compliant subsets, transplant replacements, and neither, holding token count and sampling fixed.

2. **Which component drives the gains over HRM-Text?** Mimir changes the tokenizer, chat template, languages, corpus composition, and task formulation alongside the data policy. A factorial ablation could vary these elements independently.

3. **How does HRM performance scale?** The report evaluates one 1B configuration and explicitly leaves scaling behavior open. Train several parameter sizes under matched token and compute budgets and measure English, Danish, and reasoning scaling curves.

4. **Can assistant quality improve without sacrificing permissibility?** The authors identify limited assistant capabilities and untested reinforcement learning. Compare permissible SFT-only training with preference optimization or RL using held-out instruction-following and human evaluation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, corpus composition, evaluation settings, and tabled results; **medium** for broader claims about frontier performance and synthetic-data efficacy.

Material missing information includes direct transplant-data ablations, benchmark uncertainty, contamination analysis, safety tests, human assistant evaluations, detailed audit criteria and acceptance rates by dataset, exact parameter count, total training tokens actually consumed, compute or energy totals, complete model-weight release status, and public reproducibility for agreement-restricted data.
