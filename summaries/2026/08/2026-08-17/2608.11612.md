# Dion3: Full-Stack Orthogonal Updates

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11612) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11612)


## One-sentence takeaway

Dion3 combines cheaper Gram-matrix orthogonalization, symmetry-aware GPU kernels, fractional row updates, and megabatched communication to preserve or improve Muon/NorMuon training quality while reducing optimizer-step time by as much as 6.5× in the tested language-model settings.

## Problem and motivation

Muon can reach a target language-model loss in fewer steps than AdamW, but its Newton–Schulz orthogonalization is cubic in matrix size and requires extra communication when weights are sharded. These costs grow with model dimensions, rectangular weight matrices, and distributed training, limiting Muon’s general scalability (§§1, 2.2–2.3). The authors identify a gap in prior work: existing variants generally improve convergence or shrink the orthogonalized matrix, but do not jointly address arithmetic, kernels, update compression, and distributed communication (§3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Gram Newton–Schulz:** Reformulates standard Newton–Schulz to iterate primarily on the smaller symmetric Gram matrix. It uses only two rectangular matrix multiplications and is mathematically equivalent in exact arithmetic (§4, Theorem 2). A restart after iteration 2 mitigates half-precision instability (§4.2, Algorithm 3).

2. **Symmetric GPU kernels:** CuteDSL kernels compute only one triangle of symmetric products and copy it to the other, approximately halving their arithmetic. They target NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs (§5, Figure 2).

3. **Fractional Dion3 update:** Selects the highest-\(\ell_1\)-norm fraction \(f\) of momentum rows or columns, orthogonalizes and updates only those entries, and uses error feedback so omitted entries can accumulate and later be selected (§6, Algorithm 4).

4. **Megabatching:** Groups all matrices of a common shape into one distributed collective, reducing communication rounds from depth-dependent \(O(N/\texttt{world\_size})\) to \(O(1)\) for transformer models with few distinct shapes (§7).

5. **Released implementations:** The `gram-newton-schulz` and `dion` packages provide the kernels, optimizer rules, baselines, and support for FSDP, DDP, and mixed sharding (§§1, 7).

The evidence supports substantial optimizer-only speedups and comparable or modestly better training outcomes within the tested transformer, hardware, precision, and tuning regimes. It does not establish an equivalent reduction in total training time: optimizer work may constitute only part of a step and may overlap other computation. It also does not establish that the quality gains generalize beyond the reported models, datasets, or training budgets. Several referenced tables lack their numeric bodies in the supplied text, so only explicitly stated comparisons can be retained.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The fractional update changes the optimization trajectory, and the authors explicitly say further work is needed to determine how broadly its unexpected quality improvement generalizes (§9).

- **Author-stated:** Naive Gram Newton–Schulz is unstable in half precision; the proposed restart controls observed failures but is not foolproof, particularly with more iterations or sensitive coefficients (Appendix B.1–B.2.2).

- **Author-stated:** Forming \(XX^\top\) squares the condition number, so Gram Newton–Schulz may be unsuitable when a highly accurate polar decomposition is required (Appendix B.2.2).

- **Author-stated:** Symmetric kernels target Hopper and Blackwell, and square matrices fall back to standard Newton–Schulz because Gram Newton–Schulz launches more GEMMs (§§4.1, 5).

- **Analyst inference:** Quality evidence is narrow: it concerns decoder-only language models trained chiefly on ClimbMix, with additional smaller FineWeb-Edu experiments. No non-language task or long-horizon frontier-scale quality evaluation is reported.

- **Analyst inference:** The main quality comparison tunes NorMuon and Dion3 learning rates, but reported evidence does not include variance across random seeds or statistical uncertainty.

- **Analyst inference:** The headline 6.5× result measures optimizer GPU time, not end-to-end training throughput; practical gains depend on how much optimizer work is exposed rather than overlapped (§8.2, Appendix E).

- **Analyst inference:** Some results are estimates or idealized case studies—not measured full runs—notably the Kimi K2 and Llama3-70B end-to-end shares (Appendix E).

## Open research questions

1. **Why can fractional row updates improve loss?** The paper observes better loss but offers no demonstrated mechanism. A next experiment could compare top-\(\ell_1\), random, cyclic, and structured selection while tracking row-selection frequency and gradient statistics.

2. **How robust is the quality gain across data and architectures?** The principal evidence comes from dense language models on ClimbMix. Replicated, multi-seed training on additional corpora, MoEs, and non-language models would test generalization.

3. **When does optimizer acceleration translate into end-to-end savings?** Appendix E shows that the exposed optimizer fraction can vary widely. Full training runs across pipeline-, tensor-, expert-, and data-parallel configurations could measure total throughput and cost directly.

4. **Can Gram Newton–Schulz support higher-accuracy applications safely?** Its Gram construction worsens conditioning, while the restart is empirically motivated for five Polar Express iterations. Experiments across condition numbers, precisions, iteration counts, and adaptive restart criteria could map its reliable operating range.
