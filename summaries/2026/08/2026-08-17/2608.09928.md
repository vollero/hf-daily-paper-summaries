# Multimodal Model Diffing for Feature Discovery and Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09928) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09928)


## One-sentence takeaway

MMDiff compares aligned sparse-autoencoder features before and after multimodal adaptation to identify visually grounded, task-specific directions whose removal selectively suppresses spatial, OCR, or unsafe behavior and whose steering can improve spatial and OCR accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Multimodal large language models can perform visual reasoning, OCR, and image-conditioned safety judgments, but the internal features responsible are difficult to identify and control. The authors argue that SAEs trained directly on multimodal activations mix language-backbone features with features altered by multimodal training, while existing representation-level model comparisons do not provide sufficiently targeted intervention handles. MMDiff addresses this gap by isolating feature directions changed during the LM-to-MLLM transition and testing their causal and steering effects (Introduction; Sections 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Multimodal model diffing:** Warm-start a layer-wise multimodal SAE from the matching base-LM SAE, train it on text-token activations conditioned on images, and identify adapted features using both low base-to-multimodal decoder cosine and nonzero visual activation energy (Sections 3.1–3.2).

2. **Task-specific discovery:** Contrast per-token feature firing on spatial, safety, or OCR inputs against generic VQAv2, retaining candidates with odds ratio ≥3 and firing-frequency increase ≥0.05, then filter features that do not remain active under lexically neutral prompts (Section 3.3; Algorithm 1).

3. **Feature-level causal control:** Project selected decoder directions out of text-token residual computations and measure target-task, VQA-spillover, and domain-control effects (Section 4).

4. **Layer-targeted steering:** MMDiff-CAA combines CAA directions across discovered task layers with an SAE decoder direction injected at its associated layer (Section 4; Algorithm 2).

5. **Mechanistic diagnostics:** Attribution patching identifies attention heads associated with selected features, while automated interpretation provides qualitative feature labels (Sections 6.2–6.3).

The results support the authors’ claim that the selected directions are intervention-relevant and more task-selective than the tested firing-only, random-feature, and from-scratch-SAE alternatives. They do not establish that each SAE feature represents a uniquely identifiable semantic mechanism, that projection removal is free of distributed off-target effects, or that the safety intervention reliably induces refusal rather than other response failures. Steering results are also limited to PaliGemma 2 and modified evaluation settings described in Section 5.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only three backbones are studied, and safety and OCR experiments use PaliGemma 2 alone (Section 8).
- **Author-stated:** MMDiff-CAA requires an instruction-tuned reference model for extracting steering directions; otherwise it reduces to ordinary SAE-feature steering (Section 8).
- **Author-stated:** Some safety-feature ablations cause generation collapse rather than refusal and require post-hoc exclusion (Section 8). This complicates interpreting lower ASR as improved safety.
- **Author-stated:** Stage-wise diffing assumes a shared architecture and vocabulary and assumes adaptation does not induce wholesale residual-space rotations (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Spatial features are evaluated on subsets derived from their top-activating samples, so reported causal effects may not estimate performance over the full VSR distribution (Section 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** OCR steering changes open-ended OCR into four-way multiple choice, limiting conclusions about unconstrained text generation (Section 5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Safety ASR relies on a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct judge; no human agreement or alternative-judge analysis is supplied in the retrieved text (Section 5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported MMDiff-Qwen discovered-feature count is extrapolated from lexical filtering on a 400-feature sample rather than measured over all candidates (Appendix D.4, Table 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Projecting one direction at three points in every layer is a broad intervention; the supplied evidence tests behavioral controls but does not fully characterize collateral internal effects (Section 4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does MMDiff scale to larger or architecturally different MLLMs?** The evaluation covers three relatively specific backbone families, with safety and OCR restricted to one. A next experiment could repeat the full discovery and intervention protocol on larger and mixture-of-experts models while measuring feature correspondence.

2. **Do discovered features generalize beyond feature-selected evaluation subsets?** Spatial causal effects are measured on subsets associated with top activations. A next experiment could pre-register features on one dataset and evaluate them on complete, independently sampled spatial benchmarks.

3. **Can safety features be suppressed without generation collapse?** The authors report that some ablations collapse generation. A next experiment could compare projection removal, graded attenuation, and feature steering using human-rated refusal quality, helpfulness, and fluency.

4. **How robust are features across datasets and prompt distributions?** Discovery uses VQAv2 as the baseline and small neutral-prompt banks for lexical filtering. A next experiment could test whether the same features and causal effects persist under new image sources, paraphrases, languages, and baseline distributions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method and in-paper numerical results because the retrieved text includes the main paper and extensive appendices.

Material missing or unclear information includes code and checkpoint availability; exact sample counts for several per-feature evaluation subsets; uncertainty intervals or repeated-run variance for downstream interventions; human validation of the safety judge; the incidence and filtering rule for generation collapse; full MMDiff-Qwen lexical filtering; and broad cross-dataset generalization tests.
