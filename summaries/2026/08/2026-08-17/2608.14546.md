# CPI-Bench: A Comprehensive,Practical and Intelligent Benchmark for Real-World Image Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14546) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14546)


## One-sentence takeaway

CPI-Bench evaluates image-editing models across general, deployment-oriented, and reasoning-intensive settings, exposing substantially larger model differences than prior benchmarks while producing rankings the authors report as closest to the Arena human-preference leaderboard.

## Problem and motivation

Existing image-editing benchmarks emphasize relatively simple, single-image operations and offer limited coverage of multi-image consistency, authentic consumer applications, and knowledge-intensive reasoning. The authors argue that this produces saturated scores, weak model differentiation, and results that are insufficient for deployment decisions or targeted model improvement (Sections 1 and 2.1).

CPI-Bench addresses this gap by evaluating editing capability at three progressively harder levels: broad editing operations, real-world applications, and expert-domain reasoning (Figure 2; Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **CPI-General-Bench:** A general-capability benchmark containing 2,039 samples and, according to the detailed statistics, 24 tasks—14 single-image and 10 multi-image tasks. It adds multi-image composition, reference-guided operations, subject consistency, and viewpoint changes (Section 3.2; Table 2; Figures 5 and 7).
- **CPI-Practical-Bench:** A deployment-oriented set of 558 samples covering 51 applications organized into four domains and 13 intermediate categories, including portrait enhancement, content creation, interior design, and e-commerce/advertising (Section 3.2; Table 2; Figure 6).
- **CPI-Intelligent-Bench:** A filtered and expanded derivative of ExpertVerse containing 1,181 reasoning-intensive instances across eight expert domains and 67 sub-disciplines (Sections 3 and 3.2; Figure 9).
- **Construction process:** A four-stage pipeline combines taxonomy design, curation from public and licensed sources, VLM-assisted instruction generation with human and expert review, and face anonymization (Section 3.1; Figure 4).
- **Automated evaluation:** Task-specific VLM prompts assign 1–5 scores for dimensions such as instruction adherence, naturalness, detail consistency, style fidelity, and identity consistency; the intelligent subset follows ExpertVerse’s evaluation metrics (Section 3.3).
- **Comparative validation:** Ten open- and closed-source models are evaluated against CPI-Bench and three existing benchmarks, followed by rank comparison with the Arena Image Edit Leaderboard (Tables 1 and 3–6; Section 4).

These results establish stronger differentiation among the ten evaluated models under the paper’s VLM-based protocol and support an association between CPI-Bench rankings and the referenced Arena ranking. They do not establish that CPI-Bench predicts individual user satisfaction, generalizes to untested models or populations, or provides unbiased absolute quality measurements. The supplied text gives no statistical uncertainty or exact Arena correlation values.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Analyst inference—internal reporting inconsistency:** Section 1 says CPI-General contains 30 tasks split into 20 single-image and 10 multi-image tasks, whereas Section 3.2, Table 2, and Figure 5 report 24 tasks split into 14 and 10. The text does not reconcile these counts.
- **Analyst inference—VLM-judge validity:** Most results depend on automated VLM scoring, but the evaluator model, decoding configuration, score aggregation details, repeated-run stability, and direct item-level human agreement are not supplied (Section 3.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited human-alignment evidence:** Alignment is assessed indirectly through model rank agreement with Arena. The exact Spearman coefficient, MAE, Arena sample overlap, leaderboard uncertainty, and significance tests are unavailable (Figure 1; Section 4).
- **Analyst inference—benchmark composition uncertainty:** The paper describes more than 100,000 candidate images and extensive review, but does not report annotator counts, inter-annotator agreement, filtering yields, source proportions, or contamination checks (Section 3.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison ambiguity:** Table 1 compares benchmark variances after rescaling GEdit, but does not report confidence intervals or account for differing datasets, task mixtures, and evaluator protocols.
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Public availability of samples, prompts, evaluator implementation, and model outputs is not established in the supplied text. Proprietary systems were accessed through official APIs, with no reported versions or inference settings beyond model names (Table 1).
- No dedicated author-stated limitations section appears in the supplied paper.

## Open research questions

1. **How reliable are the VLM scores relative to direct human judgments at the sample level?** Arena rank agreement is aggregate and indirect. A next experiment could collect blinded human ratings on a stratified sample and measure per-task correlations, calibration, and disagreement with the automated judge.

2. **Which benchmark components drive the stronger model differentiation?** The paper reports subset-level gaps but no ablation of task difficulty, multi-image structure, prompt specialization, or scoring dimensions. A factorial ablation could vary these elements while holding models and examples fixed.

3. **Do CPI-Bench rankings remain stable across evaluator models and inference runs?** Evaluator identity and robustness tests are not reported. A next experiment could rescore all outputs with multiple VLM judges, repeated seeds, and a human reference panel.

4. **Does performance generalize beyond the represented languages and application taxonomy?** The benchmark is described as bilingual, while the principal cross-benchmark table uses English splits and demographic/geographic coverage is unspecified. A next experiment could add independently sourced languages, regions, and long-tail professional workflows and test rank stability.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive benchmark descriptions and score tables, but material information is unavailable: exact Figure 1 correlation and MAE values; evaluator model and prompting details; aggregation formulas beyond CPI-Overall; uncertainty estimates and significance tests; annotator statistics; reconciliation of the conflicting CPI-General task counts; API/inference configurations; contamination analysis; demographic and geographic coverage; licensing terms; and confirmation that the benchmark, prompts, outputs, or evaluation code are publicly released.
