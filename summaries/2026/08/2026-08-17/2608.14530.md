# Marionette: Predicting World States, Rendering Geometry, Painting Appearance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14530) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14530)


## One-sentence takeaway

Marionette factorizes interactive game generation into explicit articulated-state prediction, deterministic geometric rendering, and diffusion-based appearance synthesis, enabling direct action control and rule-based correction of long-horizon physical failures without a detected FVD penalty.

## Problem and motivation

Pixel- or latent-autoregressive game world models must implicitly preserve pose, geometry, occlusion, identity, and action effects while repeatedly consuming their own outputs. The authors argue that accumulated errors make long-horizon consistency and control fragile, and that purely visual models provide no interpretable state on which exact constraints can operate (§1).

Their proposed gap is an interactive model whose forward dynamics live in a metric, render-ready state: neural networks predict decisions, motion, and appearance, while deterministic computation handles quantities requiring exact bookkeeping (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Explicit two-stage dynamics.** ActionGPT, a roughly 2.5M-parameter causal transformer, predicts per-entity action tokens, root displacement, and orientation. PoseGPT, a roughly 25M-parameter causal transformer, converts actions and recent body state into articulated pose. Together they autoregress a 276D state for a hunter, monster, and weapon (§§3.1–3.2; Table 3).

2. **Zero-parameter graphics bridge.** A fixed operator reconstructs metric world-space skeletons, integrates root motion, applies camera projection, resolves depth ordering, and rasterizes terrain and skeletons into a three-channel pose-control video (§3.3; Appendix C).

3. **Appearance-only observation model.** A Wan2.2-Fun-5B control-conditioned video-diffusion model translates pose-control video plus a first-frame appearance reference into RGB, using chunk relay for longer horizons (§3.4).

4. **Editable state interface.** Discrete actions can be controlled by overwriting one token, while continuous travel and heading can be controlled by overwriting root displacement and rotation (Appendix D).

5. **State-level repair and evaluation.** Terrain collision and character-separation rules are applied between dynamics and rendering; the paper evaluates both physical state properties and RGB quality (§§4.2–4.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Forced action tokens affect generated articulation | On 48 held-out segments at horizon 120 with a 64-frame seed and deterministic decoding, hunter RA-MPJPE rose 31%, from 0.272±0.135 m under Force-GT to 0.357±0.120 m under shuffled actions; Force-GT beat Force-Shuf in 33/48 segments. Free rollout scored 0.281±0.141 m. | §4.3; Table 4; Appendix F |
| Marionette outperformed the matched Pixel-AR baseline on reported FVD point estimates | Across twelve 16-second rollouts, FVD16 was 831 for Marionette versus 975 for Pixel-AR; final-chunk FVD was 1013 versus 1198. The reported bootstrap intervals overlap. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Predicted rather than recorded state caused no detected appearance degradation | With the same observation model, aggregate FVD was 831 from predicted state versus 799 from recorded state; the authors say the results do not separate at this sample size. | §4.4; Conclusion |
| Unconstrained generated state developed substantial physical failures | Across twelve held-out windows, collision-frame ratio was 0.337 versus 0.082 for recorded state; mean maximum penetration was 0.444 m versus 0.023 m; final separation was 21.2 m versus 4.8 m. | Table 2; §4.4 |
| The terrain collider reduced penetration | It lowered collision-frame ratio from 0.337 to 0.114, a 66% reduction, and mean maximum penetration from 0.444 m to 0.157 m, while final separation remained 21.2 m. | Table 2; §4.4; Figure 3 |
| The separation cap kept the entities engaged but increased foot-skate | Adding a 6 m cap produced 5.1 m final separation versus 21.2 m without it; foot-skate increased from 1.14 to 1.22 m/s, reported as 7%. | Table 2; §4.4; Figure 3 |
| Strong differentiable terrain penalties produced an unintended deformation | With penetration/contact loss weights of 2.0/0.5, monster bone-length error rose from about 3% in the terrain-free baseline to about 13%, motivating weaker regularization and post-hoc projection. | Appendix C, “Terrain in the training objective” |

The evidence supports causal responsiveness to injected action tokens under controlled offline rollouts and shows that named state constraints selectively improve their targeted physical metrics. It also suggests competitive RGB quality against the paper’s matched baseline.

It does not establish real-time human playability, generalization across diverse monsters or games, superiority to broader world-model families, or equivalence in visual quality: the FVD intervals overlap, the predicted-versus-recorded comparison is small, and the observation evaluation uses only twelve rendered rollouts.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Appearance identity shifts over long horizons because only the first frame and chunk-relay history preserve appearance; persistent entity references are proposed but not tested (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Some visible companions and small monsters lack recorded joints, so the observation model can generate entities with no corresponding explicit state (§5).
- **Author-stated:** The observation model trains on ground-truth poses but receives generated poses at inference, creating a distribution shift (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Dynamics cover only one monster type and primarily one-on-one combat with a fixed weapon type, despite broader appearance training data (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Strong soft physical penalties can be traded against skeleton rigidity, as demonstrated by increased bone-length error (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The baseline evidence is narrow: only one matched Pixel-AR system receives a quantitative head-to-head, while other systems are excluded because their protocols and interfaces differ (§4.1; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** The controllability test establishes action-token influence, not that every requested action is executed accurately; Force-GT versus Force-Shuf had the desired segment-wise ordering in 33 of 48 cases (Table 4).
- **Analyst inference:** The separation cap succeeds partly by construction and introduces measurable foot sliding, so improved constraint satisfaction is not equivalent to fully realistic dynamics (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility remains prospective: the text promises code, terrain fields, configurations, and protocols but does not confirm their release or provide compute and training-cost details.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the explicit dynamics generalize across heterogeneous rigs and action vocabularies?** The evaluation fixes one monster type. A next experiment could train a shared model across several monster rigs and report per-species control, penetration, and separation metrics on held-out identities.

2. **Can appearance remain stable independently of chunk length?** The authors report identity drift and rely on a single first-frame reference. A controlled ablation could compare first-frame-only conditioning with persistent per-entity reference images across increasing rollout horizons.

3. **Can learned dynamics reduce failures without relying on hard post-processing?** The collider and separation cap repair state but the latter adds foot-skate, while strong differentiable penalties deform the rig. A next experiment could compare rigid-body-aware losses, constrained decoding, and projection using penetration, bone stability, foot-skate, and separation jointly.

4. **Does the interface support genuine interactive use?** Experiments use scripted offline injections. A next study could measure command latency, frame rate, action success, and stability during live user-controlled sessions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the architecture, evaluation settings, and reported numerical results because the supplied text includes the full method, experiments, and appendices.
