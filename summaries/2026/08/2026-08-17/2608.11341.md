# Apodex Discovery: Reality Benchmarks and Environments for Evaluating and Building Discoverative Artificial Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11341) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11341)


## One-sentence takeaway

Apodex Discovery evaluates complete AI solver systems on extended, tool-using investigations through executable environments, hidden outcome verification, and HDS6 process scoring, with early results showing environment-dependent gains but limited causal and prospective validation.

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that frontier models are usually evaluated after objectives, tools, and success criteria have already been specified, whereas consequential real-world problems require the system to formulate and pursue a verifiable investigation. Existing static benchmarks lack evolving state and external action, while interactive benchmarks typically use narrowly specified tasks and terminal success signals (Section 1, pp. 4–5).

They identify missing infrastructure for problem formulation, reality-based environments, intermediate and final verification, and feedback-driven repair. This matters especially when definitive outcomes are delayed, incomplete, or unavailable, as in scientific and clinical discovery.

## Approach and contributions

- **Problem scouting:** A ten-person team surveyed 561 industries in 16 sectors over two months, assembled 423 candidate real-world problems, and selected 20 for the initial release using technical-fit, value, access, verifiability, and impact criteria (Sections 2–2.2; Figure 2, pp. 8–12).

- **TRACES benchmark:** The authors release executable environments organized around a common environment–task–episode abstraction. Each episode fixes inputs, tools, budgets, submission format, hidden verifier, and scoring rules while recording the solver trajectory (Sections 3.2–3.3; Figures 6–7, pp. 20–24).

- **Heavy-duty solver formulation:** Evaluation targets the complete system—foundation model, harness, tools, memory, agent loop, and control policy—rather than the model alone (Section 1.1; Figure 1, pp. 5–7).

- **Dual verification:** Hidden outcome verifiers score final and intermediate artifacts, while HDS6 scores visible trajectories on Tools, Repair, Alternatives, Coherence, Evidence, and Scope without seeing the outcome (Sections 3.1–3.1.2, pp. 14–20).

- **Repair mechanism:** Structured process diagnoses are converted into outcome-blind repair notes and supplied to a new run, creating a measure–diagnose–improve loop (Sections 1.1.4 and 4.4, pp. 8, 35–37).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that task-specific environments, harnesses, and feedback mechanisms can materially affect complete-system performance. It also shows an association between HDS6 and hidden outcome scores.

It does **not** establish that HDS6 is a causal predictor of success, that repair gains exceed ordinary rerun variance, or that performance transfers to undisclosed test sets and genuinely prospective discoveries. The abstract’s “7% across all four [AAV] tasks” summary is not fully reconciled with the task-specific figures in Section 4.1.3, which show different absolute and relative margins.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Drug-repurposing results use only three runs on a public development set and are descriptive rather than definitive test-set estimates (Section 4.2.3).

- **Author-stated:** Repair evaluation lacks a matched control in which the same deficient trajectories are rerun without repair notes, so gains cannot be separated from run-to-run variation (Section 4.4).

- **Author-stated:** Episode-mechanism ablations use one run per instance, and some prompt conditions are not fully controlled; small differences have weak causal interpretation (Sections 4.3.1–4.3.2).

- **Author-stated:** Harness coverage is asymmetric, making uncontrolled harness or model averages potentially misleading (Section 4.3.1).

- **Author-stated:** Outcome–HDS6 correlations pool tasks with different verifier scales and should be treated as agreement summaries, not calibrated magnitudes (Section 3.1.2; Table 3).

- **Author-stated:** Some scientific outcomes rely on measurable proxies because definitive in-vivo or prospective outcomes are unavailable (Sections 1.1.3 and 3.1.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Selecting only trajectories with HDS6 below 3 for repair creates a regression-to-the-mean risk, reinforcing the need for an unguided rerun control.

- **Analyst inference:** HDS6 is judged by multi-model procedures, but the supplied text does not provide inter-rater reliability, sensitivity to judge-model choice, or independent human validation.

- **Analyst inference:** The initial executable environments cover only a small fraction of the 423-problem registry, limiting claims of broad real-world representativeness (Section 2.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does HDS6 predict prospective discovery success rather than merely correlate with retrospective outcome verifiers?** Definitive outcomes are delayed or absent for the discovery challenges. A next experiment could preregister HDS6 scores and test whether they predict later wet-lab or clinical results.

2. **How much of repair improvement is attributable to the repair note?** The paper lacks unguided matched reruns. Randomly assigning deficient trajectories to repair-note, generic-feedback, and no-feedback reruns would isolate the causal effect.

3. **How stable is HDS6 across process judges?** Judge-model robustness and human agreement are not reported in the supplied text. A blinded comparison across multiple judge families and domain experts could measure reliability by capability and task.

4. **Do environment and harness gains generalize beyond the reported configurations?** Several comparisons have asymmetric coverage or one run per instance. A fully crossed model × harness × environment study with repeated seeds could estimate main effects, interactions, and variance.
