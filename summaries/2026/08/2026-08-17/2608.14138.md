# SPARGen: Unifying Spatial Perception and Reasoning through Native Multimodal Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14138) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14138)


## One-sentence takeaway

SPARGen uses one instruction-conditioned multimodal model to generate 3D geometry, optical flow, camera poses, and spatial answers through native image-generation and autoregressive pathways, achieving strong multi-task results while remaining limited by relative-scale outputs and VAE compression.

## Problem and motivation

Spatial perception requires recovering geometry and correspondences, while spatial reasoning turns that evidence into semantic conclusions. The authors argue that existing systems usually separate these capabilities through task-specific architectures, geometric encoders, regression heads, or external modules, limiting shared supervision and knowledge transfer. Their goal is therefore to support reconstruction, correspondence, and question answering through one native multimodal generative interface (Abstract; §1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified task formulation:** Reconstruction, optical flow, and spatial reasoning are cast as instruction-conditioned generation from RGB images and natural-language instructions (§3.1, Eqs. 1–2).

- **Two native output formats:** Dense depth, point maps, and flow become image-aligned fields generated by rectified flow in VAE latent space; camera poses, sparse geometric quantities, and answers become serialized token sequences generated autoregressively (§3.2).

- **Shared architecture:** Both pathways use a Bagel-initialized Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts backbone with joint multimodal self-attention, without task-specific regression heads or external geometric prediction modules (Fig. 1; §§3.1–3.2).

- **Joint supervision:** Sequence cross-entropy and dense-field flow matching train the model on spatial reasoning, visual geometry, and optical flow data (§3.3, Eqs. 10–12).

- **Cross-task evidence:** Leave-one-supervision-group-out ablations provide preliminary evidence of transfer—and some capacity competition—among geometry, correspondence, and reasoning (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Frozen-VAE spatial compression is a bottleneck for sharp geometric boundaries and high-precision physical quantities (§5).

- **Author-stated:** Normalized depth and point maps prevent metric-scale recovery; evaluation therefore uses scale alignment (§4.1).

- **Author-stated interpretation:** The slight optical-flow improvement after removing reasoning supervision may indicate capacity competition between autoregressive reasoning and dynamic dense-field prediction (§4.3).

- **Analyst inference:** The ablations support associations between supervision groups, but not the specific mechanism of knowledge transfer; the paper reports only removing whole supervision categories and supplies no numerical ablation cells in the retrieved text.

- **Analyst inference:** Dependence on MoGe pseudo-labels for sparse or incomplete geometry annotations introduces possible teacher bias, but its magnitude is not separately evaluated (§4.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility cannot be fully assessed because the supplied text does not establish code, weights, sampling settings, task-mixture proportions, or full appendix details.

- **Analyst inference:** The optical-flow table numbering is internally inconsistent: the caption labels zero-shot KITTI results as Table 3, while §4.2 attributes them to Table 4.

## Open research questions

1. **Can native generation recover metric geometry?** The current normalization discards metric scale. A next experiment could add metric-scale supervision or calibration tokens and evaluate absolute depth and translation without scale alignment.

2. **How much accuracy is lost through VAE compression?** The authors identify compression as a precision bottleneck but provide no direct comparison. An experiment could vary VAE compression ratios or compare latent generation with a high-resolution geometric decoder.

3. **Which supervision pairs produce genuine transfer?** Whole-category removal cannot isolate task-pair effects or distinguish transfer from data-volume changes. A controlled factorial study could balance training examples while testing every geometry–flow–reasoning combination.

4. **How robust is the unified model outside the reported benchmarks?** Optical-flow transfer is tested zero-shot only on KITTI, while geometry and reasoning use their respective benchmark suites. Cross-domain tests covering different motion, scene, and image conditions would clarify generalization.
