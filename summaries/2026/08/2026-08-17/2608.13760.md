# Amplified Does Not Mean Predictive: Reasoning Behaviors in Thinking Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13760) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13760)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 15,282 traces, thinking models produced much more self-correction, hypothesis testing, and explicit uncertainty, but these behaviors were less associated with correctness than confidence calibration, knowledge alignment, and self-awareness.

## Problem and motivation

Reasoning-oriented training often produces longer, more deliberative traces, but accuracy alone cannot reveal whether the newly prevalent behaviors actually accompany successful reasoning. The authors identify a gap in prior trace analyses: behavioral frequency is commonly described without separating it from association with correctness. They therefore ask whether thinking models preferentially amplify the behaviors most diagnostic of correct answers and whether recovery from intermediate failures explains their gains (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Behavioral Lift:** The authors define \(P(\text{correct}\mid b)-P(\text{correct}\mid \neg b)\), separating a behavior’s prevalence from its association with correctness. They explicitly treat this as descriptive, not causal (Section 2.3, p. 4).
- **Recovery Rate:** They measure accuracy among traces containing at least one annotated failure, exposing whether models can succeed after reasoning errors (Section 2.3, p. 4).
- **Cross-modal taxonomy and annotations:** Nine shared higher-order behaviors, modality-specific failure modes, reasoning-quality labels, and VLM grounding labels are applied to 15,282 traces from 15 open-weight LLMs and VLMs over six benchmarks (Sections 2–3, pp. 3–5).
- **Empirical findings and assets:** The paper documents an “Amplification-Lift Gap,” analyzes task-dependent recovery, and states that annotation prompts, metrics code, run materials, and behavioral annotations will be released (pp. 2–3; Appendix A, p. 14).

The evidence establishes robust observational associations across the studied traces and shows that behavioral amplification and correctness association are empirically distinct. Same-question, length-stratified, per-model, noise-sensitivity, and probing analyses constrain several alternative explanations. It does **not** establish that high-Lift behaviors cause correctness, that reasoning-oriented training itself causes each behavior difference, or that visible traces faithfully represent internal computation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Behavioral Lift is observational; a high-Lift behavior may cause success, follow from already-good reasoning, or correlate with another property (Sections 2.3 and 5, pp. 4, 10).
- **Author-stated:** Visible traces may be incomplete, post hoc, or unfaithful to internal computation (Section 5, pp. 9–10).
- **Author-stated:** Automated annotation may contain systematic judge bias despite cross-judge and manual validation. Self-awareness and some organizational labels had lower cross-judge agreement (Section 3.3, p. 5; Section 5, p. 10).
- **Author-stated:** The prompting intervention changes several trace properties simultaneously, so it cannot isolate individual behaviors; its GPQA differences are not statistically significant (Appendix F, p. 18).
- **Analyst inference:** “Thinking” and “instruct” endpoints can differ in full training recipes and decoding settings, limiting causal attribution. Table 6 explicitly warns that most pairs do not isolate one training stage, while Appendix A reports family- and variant-specific decoding configurations (pp. 14–15, 23).
- **Analyst inference:** The main study covers six benchmarks and primarily 3B–9B open-weight models. Scaling and frontier-model supplements broaden the evidence, but deployment settings and non-benchmark reasoning remain untested (Section 3, pp. 4–5; Section 4.5, pp. 8–9).

## Open research questions

1. **Do high-Lift behaviors causally improve reasoning?** Lift and same-question comparisons remain correlational. A next experiment could independently train process rewards for calibration, knowledge alignment, and self-awareness while holding data and optimization fixed.

2. **Which training stage creates the amplification gap?** The SFT checkpoint comparison shows the gap before DPO/RLVR but does not isolate any stage. A controlled checkpoint study could evaluate the same base model after SFT, DPO, and RLVR with identical decoding.

3. **Can models learn context-sensitive uncertainty rather than pervasive hedging?** Explicit uncertainty is amplified but negatively associated with correctness, while calibration is strongly positive. An intervention could reward uncertainty only when evidence strength or answer entropy warrants it and compare calibration and task accuracy.

4. **How well do visible behavior labels track internal computation?** Linear probes provide supporting evidence but do not demonstrate faithfulness. A plausible experiment would combine causal activation interventions with trace annotations to test whether modifying behavior-linked representations changes reasoning outcomes.
