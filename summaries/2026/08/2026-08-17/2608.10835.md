# UniProbe: A Learnable Token-Level Hallucination Detector for Large VLMs using Multi-Structural Internal Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10835) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10835)


## One-sentence takeaway

UniProbe detects and mitigates token-level hallucinations by learning over the hidden states and attention structure of a frozen vision-language model, outperforming reported trained baselines while adding modest inference latency.

## Problem and motivation

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) can generate text unsupported by an input image. Response-level detection is insufficient for targeted correction: mitigation requires locating the specific hallucinated tokens.

The authors identify three shortcomings in prior detectors: full-backbone fine-tuning is expensive and may alter the protected model; external verifiers require another model and ignore the generation process; and existing internal probes flatten hidden states or attention into isolated features, losing spatial, sequential, and cross-modal structure (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structured internal-trace detector:** From one forward pass, UniProbe constructs a directed graph whose nodes are selected image patches, query tokens, and all response tokens. Node features come from a chosen intermediate layer’s hidden states, while attention weights define typed, pruned edges (§3–§4; Figure 2).

- **Multi-structural architecture:** Alternating blocks combine a GNN for attention-mediated relations, a ViT for image-patch geometry, and a BiGRU for response order. A linear head produces a hallucination probability for every response token; the LVLM remains frozen (§4).

- **Online mitigation:** Replacing the BiGRU with a unidirectional GRU makes detection causal. During generation, tokens exceeding a threshold are rejected and resampled (§4, “Online Detection”; Figure 5).

- **Target-model self-adaptation:** To address the shift from third-party, teacher-forced responses to a model’s own generations, UniProbe is further trained on captions generated by the target LVLM for 500 Objects365 images, with object mentions automatically labeled using CHAIR (§4; Appendix A).

These results support the authors’ claim that explicitly preserving relational, spatial, and sequential structure improves detection within the tested backbones and benchmarks. They also show that detector quality can translate into lower CHAIR hallucination rates under the paper’s reject-and-resample procedure.

They do not establish effectiveness for inaccessible closed models, transfer of one trained detector across backbones, hallucinations outside the evaluated image–text settings, or general factuality beyond the supplied token-, object-, and caption-focused evaluations. Some detailed results from Tables 2–4 are absent from the retrieved text, limiting independent comparison across every backbone and baseline.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** UniProbe requires hidden-state and attention access, restricting use to open models or providers exposing those signals (Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** A separate detector is trained for each backbone; cross-backbone transfer is not evaluated (Limitations).

- **Author-stated design constraint:** The best intermediate layer is selected separately per backbone using validation F1, adding model-specific tuning (§6; Appendix A).

- **Analyst inference:** Self-adaptation depends on object annotations and CHAIR’s category/synonym matching. Its strong mitigation results therefore may not extend to non-object hallucinations or domains without comparable structured labels (§4–§5).

- **Analyst inference:** The primary mitigation experiment uses freely generated COCO captions from GLM-4.1V. Although detection is evaluated across several backbones, broad streaming-mitigation generality is not numerically demonstrated in the supplied text (§5).

- **Analyst inference:** The human study reports aggregate preferences but no uncertainty estimates, inter-rater agreement, or significance testing (Appendix B).

- **Reproducibility risk:** Appendix A provides architecture, optimization, adaptation size, hardware, and threshold details, but the supplied text does not establish public availability of code, weights, splits, random seeds, or variance across training runs.

## Open research questions

1. **Can one detector transfer across LVLM backbones?** The paper trains a separate readout for each model. A next experiment could train on several backbones and evaluate zero-shot and lightly adapted transfer to a held-out backbone.

2. **Does self-adaptation improve non-object hallucination detection?** Adaptation labels are derived from object annotations, while HalLoc includes attributes, relationships, and scenes. A next experiment could obtain automatic or human labels for these categories and report category-specific pre/post-adaptation results.

3. **How robust is mitigation outside captioning?** The main streaming results concern COCO captions. A next experiment could apply causal detection and resampling to multi-turn visual dialogue, instruction following, and reasoning responses while measuring both grounding and task success.

4. **Can the method work with cheaper or more restricted traces?** Hidden states plus attention may be unavailable or costly to expose. A next experiment could ablate hidden-state-only, attention-only, quantized, or sparsely sampled traces and measure accuracy, memory, and latency.
