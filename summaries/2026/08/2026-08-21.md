# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-08-21

## Executive summary

- Agent infrastructure dominates the collection: environment adaptation, executable task synthesis, persistent skills, harness evolution, workflow compliance, and feedback-rich inference all move improvement outside model weights.
- Execution-grounded evaluation repeatedly exposes misleading surface success. SWE-bench Science (`2608.19799`) finds a best Pass@1 of 47.90% despite 96.64% public-test score, while QuoteBench (`2608.13547`) shows that one extra parser can erase 55.4–73.2 percentage points of fixed-command success.
- Robot work splits between long-horizon policy composition and dexterous grasp synthesis. EXIMO (`2608.19891`) learns from VLM-orchestrated successes; τ₀-VLA (`2608.16885`) spends test-time compute on consequence-aware subtask search; GOAG (`2608.19759`) and CoToGrasp (`2608.19776`) learn contact priors without training objects.
- Capability acquisition is increasingly evaluated alongside retention. Greek reasoning adaptation (`2608.17744`) changes trace language and formatting without a defensible accuracy gain, while IAR (`2608.20281`) explicitly separates document injection, QA alignment, and recovery of general behavior.
- Structured conditioning is central to human-centric generation: 4DAnyone (`2608.20335`) routes context across generated camera groups, and WithEveryone (`2608.20336`) binds identities to planned people and locations.
- Two audits show why matched aggregate scores can conceal failure mechanisms: ASR systems may follow benchmark-specific acoustic cues (`2608.19936`), while terminal commands may fail only after transport reparsing (`2608.13547`).
- Systems results span very different regimes and should not be compared numerically: FlashPrefill V2 (`2608.19758`) accelerates H20 long-context prefill, TinyCast (`2608.15767`) brings probabilistic forecasting to a Cortex-M7, and NAPE (`2608.19863`) simplifies audio self-supervision.
- Memory is not uniformly beneficial. MemTrapBench (`2608.20202`) constructs histories where every tested memory strategy underperforms no memory, whereas Chain-of-Experience (`2608.18027`) finds that full in-context attempt-and-feedback histories often improve reasoning when the feedback is informative.
- Several results remain narrow: simulation-only robotics, small physical trial counts, private training data, missing numerical table cells, single training runs, and model-judged evaluation recur across the collection.

## Main results worth noting

- `2608.19799` provides the clearest warning against visible-test optimism: Claude-Opus-5/Claude Code scored 96.64% on public tests but only 47.90% exact Pass@1 across the same 119 scientific repository tasks. GPT-5.6-sol instead led PrivateScore at 78.82%, illustrating that metric leaders differ.

- `2608.13547` causally isolates command transport damage. Across eight same-window configurations and 56 tasks, fixed raw-conditioned replies lost 55.4–73.2 percentage points after one extra parser. For GPT-5.6-sol, a −3.6-point matched gap concealed −64.3 points of transport damage and +60.7 points of contract-conditioned compensation.

- `2608.18580` reports consistent Terminal-Bench 2.1 gains after full-parameter SFT on 1,200 successful FACET trajectories: Qwen3.5-4B 17.60→24.72, 9B 27.34→35.58, and 27B 40.82→47.57, using Terminus-2 and three attempts per task.

- `2608.19880` improves both SWE-bench success and average steps under its skill-learning setup: 52.58 SR and 49.61 steps versus 49.88 and 55.01 for original-environment skills. Combining Stage/Contract with Chain reaches 54.30 SR and 43.12 steps.

- `2608.20336` reports a well-qualified identity result: on 210 identity-disjoint five-to-ten-person examples, Sim(Tgt) is 0.499 versus 0.462 for GPT-Image 2, paired difference +0.038, bootstrap interval [+0.027,+0.048], Wilcoxon \(p=3.8\times10^{-11}\). This does not control all resolution and planning differences.

- `2608.20202` shows a large diagnostic memory effect. On Gemini, no memory scores 85.16% versus 71.17% for the best tested memory strategy; on Qwen, 81.83% versus 70.13%. In the Task Boundary control subset, no-trap memory scores 94.39% but trap memory 31.05%.

- `2608.13120` attributes a 15.4-point final task-success difference to multi-turn rather than single-turn feedback within its otherwise retained pipeline: 81.8% versus 66.4%. Governance constrains line-count growth to +2.8%, versus +16.2% without it.

- `2608.19758` delivers a hardware-specific speed–quality trade-off. At 128K on H20, batch 4, Qwen3-30B-A3B reports 17.54× BF16 and 30.49× FP8 operator speedups over an FA3/4-aligned dense baseline; across three models, BF16 RULER averages remain within roughly 0.3–1.0 points of full attention.

- `2608.17744` demonstrates why accuracy alone is incomplete. Across 5,156 Greek questions, the tested bases produced no traces above the strong Greek-script threshold, while language-matched SFT reached 97.4–98.1%. In the controlled Qwen RLVR study, fallback fell 24.1%→2.5% and answer-channel leakage 3.53%→0%, while the random-reward arm did not reproduce the repair.

- `2608.20281` reports a recurring domain–retention improvement rather than universal dominance: IAR beats Vanilla SFT on all four metrics in 7 of 8 corpus–model settings. On Qwen3-4B/CC, domain accuracy is 50.5% versus 42.4%, IFEval 59.8% versus 51.1%, MMLU 19.5% versus 8.8%, and MSBench 63.0% versus 51.0%.

- `2608.16885` reports controlled high-level planning gains with a fixed low-level VLA. Book Organization improves from 6/10 success and 66.67% progress to 9/10 and 93.33% under test-time computation; the small ten-trial cells preclude precise population estimates.

- `2608.19776` illustrates a semantic–stability trade-off. On 10 MultiDex objects, it has higher topology entropy than four unconditioned planners but only 36.94% SR versus their 63.30–77.90%. Against the topology-aware Dexonomy on filtered pairs, it improves SR from 27.16% to 29.75% and topology-average compliance from 23.80% to 27.56%.

- `2608.12875` finds no supported overall quality winner between the best compared pipelines: 77.6 for Gemini 3.1 Pro versus 77.2 for Octen-8B, paired Δ +0.3, 95% CI [−2.4,+3.1], \(p=0.85\). Under the paper’s accounting, the LLM pass costs $154.14 versus $0.108, or 1,431× more.

- `2608.15767` occupies a distinctive embedded regime: 146,505 parameters, host GIFT-Eval scores of 0.774 nGMASE and 0.545 nWQL over 97 configurations, and a complete STM32H753 path taking 4.08 seconds per 48-step call. Its nominal 80% interval covers only 60.0% at long horizons.

- `2608.19863` supports causal next-embedding prediction as a viable audio SSL objective: removing causal masking changes AS-2M/AS-20K/ESC-50 from 49.6/39.1/94.2 to 41.8/24.8/68.9. NAPE-L’s 68.8% IEMOCAP result exceeds the strongest listed imported baseline, but training was not compute-matched.

## Open research directions

1. **Standardize executable correctness across agent tasks.** Motivated by `2608.18580`, `2608.19799`, and `2608.13547`, build matched tasks scored by visible tests, hidden tests, exact final state, and mutation-tested validators. The discriminating evidence is how often each layer reverses apparent success.

2. **Measure whether adaptive environments transfer or overspecialize.** For `2608.19880`, run a cross-domain transformation matrix with held-out task families, several designer models, and fixed rollout budgets. Report gains and regressions separately, especially for transformations targeted to one policy weakness.

3. **Compare persistent-improvement substrates under one protocol.** For `2608.13120`, `2608.08466`, `2607.21596`, and `2608.18027`, hold the base model, tasks, verifier, and context budget fixed while varying textual Skills, executable skills, whole-harness edits, and full-context trajectories. Measure retention, transfer, bloat, rollback, and cost.

4. **Test memory influence as a calibrated decision.** Motivated by `2608.20202` and `2608.18027`, compare always-use, never-use, prompt-gated, and learned risk-gated memory on both trap-heavy and ordinary memory-benefit tasks. The key evidence is a selective-risk curve rather than one aggregate score.

5. **Separate robot planning, reward, and low-level control bottlenecks.** For `2608.19891` and `2608.16885`, factorially vary privileged versus learned success signals, offline distillation, residual RL, world prediction, value scoring, and reflective search. Use the same tasks and low-level policy, with enough real-robot trials for uncertainty estimates.

6. **Evaluate dexterous grasp semantics in physical tasks.** For `2608.19759` and `2608.19776`, run repeated trials varying object mass, friction, partial visibility, approach pose, and requested topology. Score lift success, topology preservation under contact, and downstream manipulation—not only static force closure.

7. **Stress retention-oriented adaptation beyond current probes.** For `2608.17744` and `2608.20281`, repeat across model families and seeds with human-written target tests, adversarial paraphrases, instruction-following suites, and non-target capability checks. This would distinguish real retained capability from benchmark-specific recovery.

8. **Test structured human consistency under harder geometry.** For `2608.20335` and `2608.20336`, stratify by occlusion, loose garments, camera motion, group size, demographic conditions, and detector failures. Hold resolution and conditioning information fixed, and include human identity/geometry judgments.

9. **Map sparse-attention portability and approximation failure.** For `2608.19758`, reproduce accuracy and serving measurements across other accelerators, workloads, block sizes, and traffic traces. Directly correlate within-block logit–value covariance with approximation error.

10. **Expand modality-specific evaluation beyond favorable task formats.** For `2608.19936`, `2608.13210`, `2608.15767`, and `2608.19863`, test multilingual ASR audits, modality ablations and open-ended long-video answers, firmware-level calibration, and temporal audio tasks. These experiments would reveal whether current findings survive outside selected benchmarks and classification-style metrics.

## Coverage and uncertainty

All 26 supplied current-paper analyses were covered. Taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical connections were available and were treated as editorial aids rather than authoritative evidence.

Collection failures affected Hugging Face Markdown retrieval for 19 papers: `2608.19880`, `2608.18580`, `2608.19799`, `2608.20336`, `2608.20202`, `2608.13120`, `2608.14022`, `2608.19854`, `2608.19758`, `2608.20281`, `2608.19891`, `2608.15767`, `2608.13210`, `2608.16885`, `2608.08466`, `2608.19776`, `2608.12875`, `2608.19863`, and `2607.21596`. Full PAPER_ANALYSIS blocks were nevertheless supplied for every current paper, so none was omitted.

Several analyses themselves report missing numerical table cells, especially `2608.20335`, `2608.19891`, and `2608.19759`; claims from those papers are therefore retained as author-reported qualitative comparisons or headline values rather than reconstructed margins. Repo0 (`2608.19854`) contains an unresolved `django` coverage discrepancy. Chain-of-Experience (`2608.18027`) has an unidentified eighth model and uses oracle-like best-round selection for several headlines.

The lowest-confidence same-day connection is `2608.20335`–`2608.20336`, because the shared structured-consistency mechanism spans different generation tasks and evaluation protocols. The EXIMO–RynnValue historical connection is medium confidence because it motivates a complementary experiment rather than documenting a shared deployed component. Historical evidence was cached and sometimes service-truncated; no first-seen date was used to infer publication order, priority, citation, dependence, or influence.

Recurring uncertainty sources include private data, prospective rather than confirmed releases, model-based judges, single training runs, absent significance tests, narrow model families, simulated robotics, ten-trial physical conditions, and unequal baseline resolutions or compute budgets.
