# CADENA: Stepwise CAD Reverse Engineering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00799) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00799)


## One-sentence takeaway

CADENA reconstructs editable CAD programs operation by operation using rendered geometric residuals, outperforming reported learned baselines most clearly on complex mechanical parts while exposing limitations from restricted views, DSL coverage, and uncontrolled training-data comparisons.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses reverse engineering a 3D mesh into an editable parametric CAD program. Existing learned systems generally generate the whole program at once, so they cannot inspect intermediate geometry, correct accumulating errors, or explicitly condition each decision on what remains unmatched.

The authors also identify an evaluation gap: common datasets emphasize simple sketch–extrude objects, while IoU and Chamfer distance measure geometric similarity without establishing that the correct surface types or construction logic were recovered. Existing real-part corpora also contain trivial objects and near-duplicates (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Stepwise reconstruction:** CADENA appends one executable operation at a time. A Qwen2-VL policy observes the target and current build through one aligned image containing six orthographic and two isometric views, plus a 3D hint point identifying a discrepancy region (§3.1–3.2; Fig. 1).
- **Geometry-only state:** The policy does not read the existing program text; execution of the current prefix carries the history. Its CadQuery-based DSL generally consumes and rebinds one solid variable (§3.1, §3.3).
- **Two-stage learning:** Supervised fine-tuning uses procedurally generated stepwise examples—1.86 million warm-up samples and 18.0 million main-stage samples—followed by one-step online GRPO using executed-mesh IoU rewards (§3.4).
- **CADENA-Bench:** The authors release 3,396 deduplicated, nontrivial mechanical parts from three source corpora, organized into six families (§4.1).
- **Generalized Match Score:** GMS combines positional and surface-normal matching and works on open geometry, aiming to distinguish matching surface types from merely similar occupied volume (§4.2).

The results support the authors’ claim that iterative geometric feedback is useful, especially for difficult parts, and that RL improves the reported accuracy–validity trade-off. They do **not** isolate the causal contribution of stepwise inference from the authors’ synthetic training corpus, because no single-pass model trained on that same corpus is reported. Means also exclude invalid outputs, so quality metrics and invalid rate must be interpreted together. BenchCAD does not provide a controlled comparison because inputs and test-time selection differ.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- CADENA trails direct optimization on gears & bearings and struggles with repetitive rotational features (§6; Table 2).
- No same-corpus single-pass baseline is reported, preventing isolation of the stepwise contribution (§6).
- CadQuery programs may not transfer faithfully into industrial CAD systems (§6).
- Fixed canonical views hide internal cavities, blind bores, and undercuts (Appendix A.7).
- Features outside the DSL—such as freeform blends, draft, and some helices—are approximated (§6; Appendix A.7).
- Early plane or origin errors cannot be undone; later operations only work around them (Appendix A.7).
- Executing and rendering after every operation is costlier than one forward pass (§6).

**Analyst inferences:**

- CADENA-Bench family-level conclusions are noisy because labels have only 70% measured agreement, and tooling & gauges contains just 17 parts.
- Comparisons can be affected by survivor bias because invalid outputs are excluded from CD, IoU, and GMS means; CADFit is especially advantaged because unconverged, harder cases are omitted.
- The claim that GMS indicates a correct construction tree remains indirect: §4.2 explicitly treats matching surface types as evidence for tree correctness, not proof.
- Training relies heavily on procedurally generated programs; the supplied text does not report how closely their operation or shape distribution matches industrial CAD histories.

## Open research questions

1. **How much improvement comes from stepping rather than the synthetic corpus?** This remains open because the paper lacks a same-data single-pass baseline. Train matched single-pass and stepwise models with identical architecture, corpus, optimization, and inference budget.

2. **Can hidden features be recovered without sacrificing the executable loop?** Eight external renders cannot reveal some internal geometry. Add point-cloud, sectional, or volumetric observations and evaluate specifically on blind bores, cavities, and undercuts.

3. **Can repetitive features be modeled with exact structural parameters?** Tooth and blade counts remain weakly visible in residual images. Compare the current DSL with explicit pattern/count operations on a controlled repetitive-feature test set.

4. **Can CADENA transfer reliably to industrial CAD systems?** CadQuery execution does not guarantee equivalent editable histories elsewhere. Export reconstructed programs into multiple industrial kernels and test geometry preservation and downstream parameter editing.
