# 3DZip: Spatial-Aware Feature Diversity-Guided Token Compression for 3D Question Answering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01185) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01185)


## One-sentence takeaway

3DZip compresses projection-based 3D VLM inputs through voxel aggregation, feature-diverse anchor selection, and spatially constrained merging, retaining 94.7% of LLaVA-3D’s aggregate benchmark performance with 128 tokens while reducing SQA3D latency from 342 to 178 ms/sample.

## Problem and motivation

Projection-based 3D VLMs lift multi-view RGB-D features into world coordinates, generating thousands of tokens and substantial attention, memory, and latency costs. The authors distinguish point-level redundancy from repeated views of the same surface and object-level redundancy from multiple spatially separated surfaces of one object. They argue that voxelization addresses the former but leaves an imbalanced, long-tailed allocation favoring large objects; attention- and semantic-relevance-based 2D compression methods also fail to explicitly preserve 3D geometry (Sections 1–3; Figure 8).

## Approach and contributions

- **Three-stage compression:** Coarse voxelization mean-pools nearby tokens; a greedy Determinantal Point Process selects \(K\) anchors with diverse normalized visual features; non-anchor tokens are assigned by feature similarity and merged only when within a grid-distance threshold (Section 4; Algorithm 1; Figure 3).
- **Redundancy diagnosis:** The paper separates point-level overlap from object-level duplication and measures the latter as uneven per-object token allocation (Section 1; Appendix 0.B.1–0.B.2).
- **Feature diversity plus geometry:** Feature-space selection seeks broader semantic/object coverage, while spatial constraints prevent geometrically distant tokens from being merged (Sections 4.2–4.3).
- **Training-free, backbone-agnostic insertion:** 3DZip is placed after token construction without changing or retraining LLaVA-3D, Video-3D-LLM, or SR-3D (Appendix 0.A.1 and 0.D.1).

The results establish a consistent empirical efficiency–accuracy advantage over the listed compression baselines under matched backbones and token budgets. Ablations support both feature diversity and spatial constraints, and additional backbones broaden the evidence beyond LLaVA-3D. They do not establish performance outside projection-based 3D VLMs or primarily indoor benchmarks, nor provide statistical uncertainty, repeated-run variation, or evidence that the fixed hyperparameters are universally optimal.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Coarse voxelization can merge small objects with nearby objects or background, weakening fine-grained cues (Section 6; Table 12).
- **Author-stated:** Fixed \(\delta\) and \(\tau_g\) do not adapt to scene size, density, or complexity; small and large scenes favor different settings (Section 6; Table 13).
- **Analyst inference:** The principal evaluations are dominated by ScanNet indoor scenes; OpenEQA uses only its ScanNet subset, so transfer to HM3D or other environment types remains untested here (Appendix 0.D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No confidence intervals, significance tests, or repeated-run variance are reported, despite temperature being fixed to zero (Section 5.1.2).
- **Analyst inference:** OpenEQA depends on an automated GPT-4o matching score, introducing evaluator dependence; the supplied text gives the rubric but no evaluator-agreement analysis (Section 5.2.2; Appendix 0.D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** A project page is linked, but the supplied text does not explicitly establish code, model, or artifact availability.

## Open research questions

1. **Can voxelization preserve small objects and fine details without sacrificing overall compression?** Table 12 identifies these as failure cases. A next experiment could compare object-boundary-aware, multi-resolution, or learned voxel aggregation against fixed mean pooling.

2. **Can scene-adaptive thresholds outperform fixed \(\delta\) and \(\tau_g\)?** Table 13 shows different optima for dense small scenes and sparse large scenes. A next experiment could predict both parameters from scene volume, object density, or token statistics and test on held-out scenes.

3. **How well does 3DZip generalize beyond indoor, projection-based models?** The tested backbones share projection-based construction, and the datasets are largely ScanNet-based. A next experiment could evaluate outdoor environments and models using native point-cloud or object-centric representations.

4. **Does feature diversity preserve task-relevant evidence for every query type?** Category results vary, and selection itself is query-independent. A next experiment could compare query-agnostic DPP with a hybrid diversity-and-query-relevance objective while retaining spatial constraints.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, datasets, ablations, and numerical comparisons because the supplied text includes the main paper and extensive supplementary material.
