# Roomer: Reflective Object-Grounded Model Editing and Repair for 3D Indoor Layout Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01973) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01973)


## One-sentence takeaway

Roomer repairs localized violations in generated 3D indoor layouts using object-grounded diagnosis, structured edits, deterministic candidate search, and full-scene verification, substantially improving physical validity and practical usability while largely preserving unaffected content.

## Problem and motivation

Indoor-layout generators can produce globally plausible scenes that still contain local collisions, out-of-bounds furniture, obstructed openings, insufficient clearance, or blocked circulation. Regenerating the full scene risks damaging already-valid arrangements.

The authors identify two gaps: existing generators rarely attribute measured violations to responsible object instances, and standard evaluations emphasize distributional similarity or basic physical validity without adequately measuring whether layouts remain practically usable (Introduction; “Physical Validity and Practical Spatial Usability”).

## Approach and contributions

- **Verification-gated local repair:** Roomer converts a layout into the object-addressable **RoState** and represents each measured violation, implicated entity, role, and geometric deficit in **RoReview**. A geometry-conditioned vision-language planner predicts a schema-constrained **StatePatch** using MOVE, ROTATE, SCALE, INSERT, DELETE, or REPLACE (Method; Fig. 2).

- **Deterministic and safe execution:** The planner’s parameter seed is tested first; if rejected, a deterministic solver searches an ordered, finite action-specific neighborhood. A patch is committed only when it resolves the target, creates no new hard-violation key, preserves protected satisfied relations, remains structurally valid, and strictly reduces the global residual (Method, Eq. 3; Appendices F–G).

- **Roomer-CC supervision:** The authors create 67,550 verified repair pairs from controlled corruptions of valid 3D-FRONT layouts. Each pair contains a faulty layout, object-grounded evidence, and a known-feasible inverse StatePatch (Introduction; Tables A1 and A5).

- **Roomer-Eval:** The evaluation protocol combines FID, KID, SCA Gap, assembled-mesh OOB and collision metrics, and a new Practical score covering five residential usability rule families (Roomer-Eval, Eq. 4; Appendix J).

The evidence supports effective post-generation repair under the paper’s frozen residential rules, action space, evaluators, and datasets. It also shows transfer across four upstream generators and provides controlled component comparisons.

It does not establish unrestricted repair of arbitrary 3D scenes, building-code compliance, aesthetic quality, or superiority on every distributional metric: ReSpace remains better than Ours-Final on FID and KID in Table 1, and two of twelve external-generator distributional comparisons worsen (Table A9).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Repair is limited to violations covered by the predefined residential rule set and to edits reachable within finite action-specific candidate spaces; uncovered or unsolved issues remain unresolved (Conclusions).

- **Author-stated:** Practical measures five rule families, not scene-level all-pass usability, building-code compliance, exhaustive ergonomics, or aesthetics (Roomer-Eval; Appendix J).

- **Author-stated:** External systems use different native inputs and generation pipelines, so evaluation standardizes scenes and metrics but does not provide identical conditioning information (Table A13).

- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond bedrooms, living rooms, and dining rooms is unproven because training and principal evaluation use only these residential room types (Tables A4–A5).

- **Analyst inference:** Roomer’s verifier and final Practical score share frozen rule definitions. Although final scores are recomputed independently from full outputs, improvements may favor the specific rule coverage optimized during repair (Roomer-Eval).

- **Analyst inference:** OOB surface sampling lacks an explicitly fixed random seed, preventing guaranteed bitwise-identical reruns of that metric (Appendix I, assembled-mesh OOB implementation).

- **Analyst inference:** Professional validation supports association rather than exhaustive validity: it uses 90 stratified layouts, a binary approval threshold, and ten experienced evaluators; inter-rater reliability is moderate (\(\alpha=0.65\), 95% CI [0.54, 0.75]) (Appendix M).

## Open research questions

1. **Can repair generalize to broader spaces and requirements?** Coverage is restricted to three residential room types and predefined rules. A next experiment could evaluate newly added rules on unseen kitchens, offices, bathrooms, and multi-room layouts.

2. **Can learned candidate generation address failures outside finite search neighborhoods?** The authors state that unreachable candidates remain unresolved. A useful experiment would compare the current finite solver with a learned continuous optimizer under the same verification gate and compute budget.

3. **How robust is Practical across cultures, accessibility needs, and formal standards?** Its five rules are deterministic but not building-code or exhaustive ergonomic criteria. A next study could validate multiple threshold sets against accessibility experts and region-specific standards.

4. **How much does repair quality depend on upstream representation and parsing accuracy?** Roomer requires conversion into canonical object geometry, while current transfer uses method-specific adapters. An experiment could inject controlled parsing, category, pose, and asset-retrieval errors and measure target resolution and safety degradation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, datasets, evaluation definitions, and numerical findings because the supplied text includes extensive methods, tables, ablations, and supplementary details.

Material missing or unresolved information includes public availability of code, trained models, Roomer-CC, and evaluation artifacts; wall-clock inference latency; complete per-generator compute costs; behavior outside the supported room and rule families; and independent replication of the reported results.
