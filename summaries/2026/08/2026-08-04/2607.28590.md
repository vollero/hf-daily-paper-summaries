# VAD: Attributing Visual Evidence for Target Reconstruction in Multimodal On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28590) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28590)


## One-sentence takeaway

VAD improves fine-grained visual accuracy by reconstructing distillation targets from the signed portion of a privileged teacher’s correction that aligns with an evidence-present versus evidence-removed visual intervention.

## Problem and motivation

Multimodal on-policy distillation can transfer local visual knowledge from a crop-conditioned teacher to a full-image student, but the teacher’s next-token correction mixes visual evidence with language priors and teacher-specific behavior. Consequently, strong teacher–student disagreement does not show that a correction is visually justified: only 23.2% of top-quartile corrections at 4B and 22.8% at 9B also had top-quartile visual alignment in the authors’ diagnostic (Introduction; Appendix 6).

The authors argue that direct teacher matching retains these source-mixed directions, while positive visual-advantage weighting changes where or how strongly to distill without reconstructing the target and may fail to identify cases where evidence should suppress a mistaken token (Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Counterfactual visual attribution:** At each student-generated prefix, the same frozen teacher evaluates an evidence-present crop and an evidence-removed/degraded version. Their centered log-probability difference, \(u_t\), is a signed proxy for how the evidence supports or refutes candidate tokens (Sections 3.1–3.2).

- **Projected, student-anchored target reconstruction:** VAD projects the complete privileged-teacher correction onto \(u_t\), discards the proxy-unexplained residual from its primary target, and applies the attributed shift around the student’s current distribution rather than copying the teacher distribution (Equations 3–5).

- **Separate support and refutation budgets:** Positive and negative coordinates receive branch-specific allocations under a shared visual-correction budget, with a cap on the support branch (Equations 6–7).

- **Stable training without inference overhead:** The reconstructed target supplies token-level Jensen–Shannon supervision, supplemented by a weak direct-teacher regularizer. Cropped and degraded views are training-only; deployment uses the ordinary full-image student (Section 3.4; Algorithm 1).

- **Empirical contribution:** Matched experiments cover six fine-grained benchmarks, 4B and 9B students, target ablations, held-out benchmarks, semantic token analysis, and compute measurements (Section 4; Appendices 8–9).

The matched Qwen3.5 comparisons support the authors’ claim that target reconstruction improves aggregate fine-grained visual accuracy under the reported data and update budgets. The ablations further associate much of the improvement with branch-aware reconstruction.

The results do not establish identifiable causal separation of visual and nonvisual information, universal superiority beyond the tested models and views, or pairwise statistical superiority for the offline token-shift analysis. Cross-family comparisons are contextual because architectures, data, and compute differ (Section 4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** A single contrastive vector from one evidence-view pair may inadequately represent compositional evidence; multiple views or learned directional bases may be richer (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** The projection provides semantic enrichment, not identifiable separation. The attributed component can retain nonvisual teacher effects, while the residual remains source-mixed (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** Visual-only training caused longer, repetitive responses and unstable formatting, commitment, and stopping, motivating the direct-teacher regularizer (Section 3.4).

- **Analyst inference:** Generality is uncertain because controlled training uses only Qwen3.5 at 4B and 9B, one 6,241-example synthetic dataset, marked evidence regions, and a particular crop-degradation intervention (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Most accuracy tables do not report variation across random seeds or confidence intervals. Table 5 explicitly states that timing variation is across steps rather than seeds, so training and evaluation stability across independent runs is not established.

- **Analyst inference:** Primary accuracy depends on a GPT-OSS-120B judge through the official pipeline; judge sensitivity or agreement with human scoring is not examined in the supplied text (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The student top-100 candidate support plus a tail bucket may omit relevant low-probability token distinctions; no supplied ablation tests this restriction (Section 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can multiple interventions yield cleaner attribution?** The current method compresses one view pair into one direction and the authors acknowledge possible compositional bias. A next experiment could compare single-pair VAD with several crops, masks, and degradation levels combined through a learned directional basis.

2. **How dependent is VAD on evidence-region supervision?** Training examples provide marked relevant regions, leaving performance with automatically localized or noisy regions unresolved. An experiment could perturb region boundaries or substitute predicted regions and measure accuracy and attribution composition.

3. **Does the method generalize across model families and larger data regimes?** Controlled evidence covers only Qwen3.5-4B/9B and 6,241 synthetic examples. A factorial study across different architectures, teacher–student pairs, and dataset sizes could test whether gains persist.

4. **Can language stability be preserved without direct source-mixed teacher matching?** The weak regularizer addresses drift but reintroduces the full privileged-teacher target. A plausible experiment would compare explicit format/EOS constraints or a text-only language-preservation objective against the current regularizer.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, reported table values, and author-qualified conclusions because the supplied text includes the core equations, setup, ablations, benchmark results, and appendices.

Material missing information includes independent-run variance and statistical tests for benchmark accuracy; detailed judge validation; code, checkpoint, and dataset-release status; performance under noisy or automatically generated evidence regions; broader architecture coverage; and ablations of candidate-support size, degradation type, and multiple counterfactual views.
