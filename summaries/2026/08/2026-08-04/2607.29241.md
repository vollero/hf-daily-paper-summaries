# RecHarness: A Bandit-Routed Agentic Harness for Self-Evolving Recommender Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.29241) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29241)


## One-sentence takeaway

RecHarness improves recommender-model iteration under fixed trial budgets by using Thompson sampling to choose human-defined edit directions while an LLM generates and learns from concrete code modifications within those directions.

## Problem and motivation

Recommender optimization requires repeated changes to architectures, objectives, training strategies, and implementation details, with every candidate consuming code-generation, training, and validation resources. General-purpose MLE agents often let the LLM choose both the search direction and the specific edit; the authors argue this produces unstable, inefficient exploration when trials are limited.

The identified gap is a mechanism that combines scalar validation evidence across trials with textual evidence such as logs, convergence behavior, and failure modes, while preventing unconstrained LLM reasoning from controlling trial allocation (Introduction; “Self-Evolving Agentic RecSys”).

## Approach and contributions

- **Separated decision roles:** A Thompson-sampling router selects semantic edit arms from accumulated validation outcomes; a frozen LLM then proposes hypotheses and executable mutations within each selected arm (Methodology, Eqs. 6 and 11–13).
- **Dual feedback:** Binary success signals update arm-specific Beta posteriors, while an automatically refreshed “Experiment Skill” summarizes incumbents, successful edits, rejected directions, errors, and validation trends for the LLM (Eqs. 10–14).
- **Basin-aware structural exploration:** When incumbent improvement slows, structural jump arms become eligible and receive a local-retuning window before acceptance (Eqs. 15–17).
- **Broad recommender evaluation:** The system is tested on two offline recommendation scenarios spanning eight model backbones, plus one production advertising experiment (Experiments; Conclusion).
- **Author-claimed novelty:** The authors describe RecHarness as the first optimization harness for self-evolving recommender systems; the supplied text does not independently establish priority (Introduction, contributions).

The evidence supports consistent improvement over each model’s cold-start implementation and suggests that posterior routing uses limited trials more effectively than the reported random, LLM-routed, and unconstrained-search variants. The production test further establishes short-term metric gains for one selected candidate under the reported traffic allocation.

It does not establish that RecHarness is globally optimal, that the gains generalize beyond the included recommender settings, or that the online changes are statistically significant: confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, traffic counts, and variance estimates are not supplied.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Human-defined search space:** Experts must specify the edit arms, objective, and validation metric. Performance may depend materially on their coverage and quality (Figure 1; Methodology).
- **Incomplete retrieved results:** The numerical contents of Tables 4–6 are missing, although narrative summaries are present. This prevents verification of the KuaiRec results and detailed online statistics.
- **Ablation scope:** Routing ablations use only SASRec across the four Amazon datasets; whether the same component effects hold for the other seven backbones or production setting is untested (Ablation Study).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** The text says variants share the same budget and update rule, but does not report LLM identity, prompting, sampling variability, arm priors, or complete trial counts. Reproducibility and sensitivity to these choices remain unclear.
- **Analyst inference—online validity:** A single seven-day experiment on 10% traffic may not capture long-term user, advertiser, or marketplace effects. No uncertainty estimates or guardrail metrics are provided.
- **Analyst inference—nonstationary bandit:** The authors acknowledge that arm rewards depend on the incumbent and context rather than fixed success probabilities, yet use accumulated Beta statistics as local routing evidence. The extent to which stale outcomes bias routing is not empirically isolated (Recommender Optimization as a Bandit Problem).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation protocol:** Amazon evaluation uses 99 sampled negatives, so the reported ranking metrics need not transfer directly to full-catalog retrieval (Experimental Settings).

## Open research questions

1. **How sensitive is performance to the human-defined arm taxonomy?** The paper evaluates configured arm sets but provides no arm-quality sensitivity study. A next experiment could compare expert arms, automatically induced arms, deliberately incomplete arms, and different arm granularities under identical budgets.

2. **Does posterior aging improve routing in the incumbent-dependent setting?** The reward distribution changes as the incumbent evolves, while the reported Beta updates accumulate historical outcomes. Compare the current router with discounted, sliding-window, and contextual Thompson sampling.

3. **Do the routing and jump mechanisms generalize independently across models?** Ablations are restricted to SASRec on Amazon. Repeat the full factorial ablation across KuaiRec backbones and the other sequential recommenders, reporting trial efficiency and final test performance.

4. **Are production gains durable and statistically reliable?** The seven-day test lacks uncertainty and longer-term effects. Run a preregistered, longer experiment with confidence intervals, repeated traffic splits, guardrail metrics, and post-deployment drift monitoring.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methodology, Amazon results, several ablation aggregates, and online headline metrics. Material omissions include the numerical bodies of Tables 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6; supplementary search details; LLM and prompt configuration; arm definitions and priors for each benchmark; jump thresholds and retuning parameters; trial counts; compute hardware; variance beyond the stated three-run averaging; statistical tests; online sample sizes; and complete code/reproducibility instructions.
