# GPTQ-2D: Cubic-Time Two-Sided Adaptive Rounding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27042) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27042)


## One-sentence takeaway

GPTQ-2D exactly reproduces vectorized adaptive rounding for a two-sided Kronecker metric while reducing the rounding sweep from \(O(m^2n^2)\) to \(O(mn\max(m,n))\) work through parallel anti-diagonal processing and separable feedback.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses integer rounding of \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\) under the two-sided quadratic objective \(\|A(Z-X)B\|_F^2\), where both nonsingular basis matrices couple the residual. Vectorization permits standard adaptive rounding, but its dense sweep costs \(O(m^2n^2)\)—quartic for square matrices—because the right basis prevents independent column processing (Sections 1 and 3.2).

The identified gap is an implementation that exploits the Kronecker structure without changing the rounded result or incurring this extra asymptotic factor.

## Approach and contributions

- **Kronecker feedback formulation:** The inverse-metric feedback factors as \(U^\top\otimes L\), making the corrected matrix \(Y=X+LEU\). Each scalar rounding error therefore induces a separable rank-one update supported on a lower-right rectangle (Section 4.1, Equations 10–13).

- **Anti-diagonal dependency structure:** Entries on a common anti-diagonal cannot affect one another and can be rounded in parallel. Theorem 1 proves that any order respecting feedback dependencies yields the same trajectory as the standard forward sweep (Section 4.1; Figure 1).

- **GPTQ-2D algorithm:** A buffer \(C=LE\) propagates each error only along its row and column, lazily reconstructing the rectangular feedback. Theorem 2 proves exact trajectory equivalence to vectorized adaptive rounding (Section 4.2, Algorithm 3).

- **Blocked implementation:** A blockwise variant aggregates short updates into two band-like matrix products per block while preserving the same output and asymptotic work (Section 4.3, Algorithm 4).

- **Implementation layout:** A padded skew representation makes anti-diagonals, matrix rows, and matrix columns fixed-stride slices in array frameworks (Section 5; Figure 2).

These results establish exact algorithmic equivalence and asymptotic reductions in arithmetic work and dependency depth. They do **not** establish measured runtime, hardware efficiency, numerical stability, quantization accuracy, objective-value improvements, or superiority over alternative rounding rules: the supplied paper contains no empirical experiments.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The bases \(A\) and \(B\), and thus \(L\) and \(U\), must remain fixed. Data-dependent adaptation during rounding can invalidate exact equivalence (Section 6, p. 13).

- **Author-stated:** GPTQ-2D reproduces a fixed-order greedy trajectory; it does not globally minimize the quadratic objective (Section 6, p. 13).

- **Author-stated:** Choosing or estimating useful basis matrices is outside scope. The method only executes a supplied Kronecker-factored rounding problem efficiently (Section 6, p. 12).

- **Author-stated:** The padded layout uses \((m+n-1)(2m+n-2)\) cells for \(mn\) data entries, so its overhead grows with aspect ratio; transposition may reduce it (Section 5, p. 12).

- **Analyst inference:** Practical speedups remain unverified because no implementation benchmarks, hardware measurements, or comparisons with YAQA’s runtime are reported.

- **Analyst inference:** Numerical behavior of Gram inversion and LDL factorization is unclear for ill-conditioned bases; the text provides no stability analysis.

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is limited by the absence of a stated code release, implementation parameters such as block width selection, and empirical test cases.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the asymptotic gain translate into wall-clock speedups?**  
   This remains open because the paper reports only complexity analysis. A next experiment could benchmark vectorized rounding, YAQA-style dense sweeps, unblocked GPTQ-2D, and blocked GPTQ-2D across matrix sizes and accelerators.

2. **How should the block width and layout be selected?**  
   Section 4.3 allows any width asymptotically, but gives no measured tuning rule. A hardware study could sweep block widths and compare the padded layout with a fused masked kernel.

3. **How robust is the method for ill-conditioned Gram matrices?**  
   The algorithm explicitly inverts and LDL-factorizes Gram matrices, but numerical stability is not evaluated. Experiments could vary condition numbers and measure output agreement, residual objectives, and finite-precision failures.

4. **Can feedback factors adapt during rounding without losing useful guarantees?**  
   Fixed factors are required for the present equivalence proof. A follow-up could test restricted update schedules and seek weaker invariance or approximation guarantees.
