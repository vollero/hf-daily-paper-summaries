# DeepVoyager-VL: Incentivizing Vision-in-the-Loop Search for Long-Horizon Multimodal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01827) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01827)


## One-sentence takeaway

DeepVoyager-VL combines graph-structured training-data synthesis with selective visual acquisition, enabling supervised-only multimodal agents to use images discovered mid-search to guide later retrieval and substantially improve long-horizon search accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Multimodal agents often use vision only at the initial query or final answer stage. Their training chains are typically constructed in text first, so visual evidence discovered during browsing rarely determines the next retrieval action. This limits performance on open-world questions requiring evidence to be linked across pages, modalities, events, and time.

The authors identify two gaps: insufficient supervision for intermediate visual dependencies and poor management of visual context during long interactions. Loading every candidate image is costly, while retaining only textual summaries may discard necessary visual details (Introduction; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **EventVoyage-VL data synthesis:** Builds a provenance-preserving multimodal event graph from Wikipedia and multi-domain news, then composes inference programs whose intermediate variables must be resolved visually. Necessity, leakage, alignment, and constraint checks are applied before question realization (Sections 3.2 and B.1).

- **Vision-in-the-loop agent:** Separates lightweight image discovery from explicit observation. The agent can search, visit pages, fetch images, crop reusable regions, and preserve visual observations across turns; auxiliary summaries reduce irrelevant context (Section 3.3; Algorithm 1).

- **Difficulty-aware trajectory curation:** Removes questions answerable without tools, stratifies the remainder using tool-assisted rollouts, and retains verified teacher trajectories under a unified interaction protocol (Sections 3.4 and B.3).

- **Supervised-only training:** Fine-tunes the language backbone on 27,180 tool-use trajectories while freezing the visual encoder and multimodal merger, without reinforcement learning (Section 3.4; Appendix C.2).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that supervised trajectory training and the proposed tool framework improve answer accuracy, particularly on image-centric, extended-search benchmarks. The cumulative data ablation supports incremental value from the VIL trajectories, while component removals support the joint usefulness of acquisition and context-management mechanisms.

It does not isolate every element of the data-generation pipeline, establish real-world latency or cost benefits, or demonstrate full autonomous research ability. Comparisons with prior specialized agents also combine results produced under different papers’ settings, limiting strict comparability (Appendix C.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation targets answer-oriented search, not open-ended research involving hypothesis revision, conflicting-source reconciliation, long-form synthesis, or citation organization (Appendix D.1).

- **Author-stated:** Auxiliary goal-conditioned summarization adds latency and deployment cost; the accuracy–context–latency–cost trade-off is not measured (Appendix D.1).

- **Author-stated:** Visual memory is cumulative and trajectory-local, with no learned retention, consolidation, compression, or eviction. Redundant evidence may accumulate over longer horizons (Appendix D.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The incremental VIL-data experiment is cumulative rather than a size-matched replacement study, so it does not fully separate data type from the benefit of adding 7K more trajectories (Section 4.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation depends on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as an LLM judge, but judge reliability or agreement with humans is not reported in the supplied text (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference:** No validation split or checkpoint selection is used; the final step-199 checkpoint is evaluated directly, leaving generalization during training unmonitored (Appendix C.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Prior-agent results are self-reported under their original settings, so cross-system rankings are not uniformly controlled (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does VIL structure outperform an equal quantity of conventional training data?** The cumulative ablation adds 7K trajectories without a size-matched control. Train separate models with equal-sized VIL and non-VIL additions under identical compute.

2. **How much efficiency does selective image loading actually deliver?** The paper reports accuracy but not token use, latency, or monetary cost. Compare eager loading, selective loading, and selective loading with summaries on accuracy, context tokens, wall-clock time, and cost.

3. **Can visual memory remain effective over substantially longer horizons?** Current memory accumulates observations without retention or eviction. Evaluate learned consolidation and retrieval over controlled tasks exceeding the 50-turn budget.

4. **Does vision-in-the-loop training transfer to open-ended research?** Current benchmarks emphasize concise answers. Test objective decomposition, conflicting-evidence handling, long-form synthesis, and citation faithfulness in a multimodal research benchmark.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, training configuration, ablations, and summarized numerical results, but the rendered numeric contents of Tables 1–4 are absent. Consequently, individual benchmark scores, full baseline comparisons, variance or significance estimates, exact per-component ablation values, and some table-number references cannot be independently checked. Public availability and licensing of the synthesized tasks, trajectories, code, and model checkpoints are also not established.
