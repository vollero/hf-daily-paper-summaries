# Motion Beyond Morphology: Bootstrapping Cross-Category Motion Transfer from Abstract Motion Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01628) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01628)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors train a video generator to transfer motion across morphologically different objects by bootstrapping cross-category pairs from multiple abstract motion views and then internalizing that supervision for direct reference-video-conditioned generation.

## Problem and motivation

Video motion-transfer methods typically assume that source and target share poses, skeletons, or other structural correspondences. These assumptions break down for transfers such as animal motion to an object or deformation between unrelated shapes.

The authors identify two gaps: no single explicit or implicit motion representation reliably captures global displacement, articulation, and local deformation across arbitrary categories; and real video collections rarely contain paired examples showing which dynamics should remain invariant when morphology changes (Introduction). Their goal is therefore open-category motion transfer that adapts what is preserved—such as pose, trajectory, rhythm, or deformation—to each source–target relationship.

## Approach and contributions

- **Abstract Motion Bootstrapping (Stage I):** A shared video-like conditioning interface learns from five complementary views: semantic skeletons, depth-aware trajectories, dense point tracks, 6-DoF axes, and foreground edges. Applicable views are paired with targets from other categories to synthesize candidate motion-equivalent pairs, which are filtered for motion fidelity, target fidelity, source leakage, and video quality (Method, “Multi-Granularity Abstract Motion Views” and “Stage I”).

- **Cross-Category Motion Internalization (Stage II):** The generator is initialized from Stage I, but the abstract condition is replaced by the raw reference video. Training on cross-category pairs is intended to discourage appearance and morphology shortcuts. Inference needs only the reference video, target text, and optionally a target image—no explicit extraction or per-video optimization (Method, “Stage II” and “Inference”).

- **Evaluation and training assets:** OpenVMT-Dataset contains 10K cross-content, motion-equivalent pairs. OpenVMT-Bench evaluates image- and text-conditioned transfer across Same, Near, and Far category gaps (Experimental Setup).

The evidence supports the value of cross-category supervision and indicates strong performance on the authors’ benchmark, including human preference over one selected baseline per track. It does not permit independent verification of the state-of-the-art claim because Table 1’s numerical entries are missing. Nor does it establish generalization beyond the curated object-centric data, benchmark cases, or evaluated clip configuration.

## Limitations and validity risks

No dedicated author-stated limitations section appears in the supplied text.

- **Analyst inference — benchmark independence:** The method, bootstrapped dataset, benchmark, and several filtering/evaluation criteria are introduced by the same authors. Evaluation on an external cross-category benchmark is not reported.

- **Analyst inference — incomplete quantitative record:** Table 1’s actual values and most of Table 3 are absent, preventing verification of effect sizes, baseline margins, and per-split behavior.

- **Analyst inference — metric validity:** G-Mot. and G-Leak rely on Gemini-based judgments, but their definitions and protocols are deferred to unavailable supplementary material.

- **Analyst inference — limited human evaluation:** The preference study uses 12 experienced evaluators and compares only against DisMo for I2V and FlowMotion for T2V; sample counts, agreement, uncertainty, and blinding are not supplied.

- **Analyst inference — extraction and filtering dependence:** Stage I requires several external estimators and only uses views deemed reliably extractable. The validity checks, filtering thresholds, retention rate, and failure distribution are unavailable.

- **Analyst inference — partial ablation coverage:** The supplied main text gives one cross-category-pair ablation and qualitative descriptions of motion-view behavior. Full per-view and filtering ablations are deferred to the supplement.

- **Release uncertainty:** The dataset and benchmark are planned for release only upon acceptance, so availability is not established (Abstract).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does the learned transfer generalize beyond the authors’ curated categories and benchmark?** This remains open because only OpenVMT-Bench results are reported. A next experiment could evaluate frozen models on independently curated objects, motions, and morphology gaps.

2. **Which abstraction views contribute most to different motion regimes?** The paper describes complementary behaviors but omits complete per-view quantitative results. A factorial ablation could remove or combine skeletons, trajectories, tracks, axes, and edges and report metrics separately for articulated, rigid, and deformable motion.

3. **How sensitive is Stage II to bootstrapping errors and filtering choices?** Filtering criteria are described, but thresholds and retained-sample statistics are unavailable. A controlled experiment could vary filter strictness and synthetic-pair noise while measuring downstream fidelity and leakage.

4. **Does internalization recover motion information absent from any single Stage-I abstraction?** Stage II consumes RGB video, but its supervision originates from selected abstractions. Tests designed around dynamics deliberately omitted or corrupted in Stage I could determine whether Stage II learns richer motion or mainly reproduces the bootstrap signal.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The central method, training setup, benchmark composition, human preference results, and one numerical ablation are clearly described. Material missing information includes the numerical contents of Tables 1 and most of Table 3; supplementary dataset statistics; motion-view applicability mappings; filtering rules, thresholds, and retention rates; full metric protocols; human-study sample counts and uncertainty; compute requirements; exact baseline outputs; and confirmed public availability of the proposed assets.
