# SKT: Skill-Use Training at Scale via Verified Synthetic Data Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02287) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02287)


## One-sentence takeaway

SKT generates executable, verifier-filtered skill-use trajectories for supervised fine-tuning, improving two language-model backbones across four agent benchmarks while retaining some gains across different agent harnesses.

## Problem and motivation

Agent Skills package procedural instructions and resources, but merely supplying them does not ensure that an LLM can recognize when they apply, follow their constraints, coordinate multiple skills, or use associated tools correctly. The authors argue that prior work largely emphasizes skill retrieval, representation, internalization, or self-improvement rather than directly training effective use of externally supplied skills (Section 1). They address the resulting lack of scalable, high-quality supervision for single- and multi-skill execution.

## Approach and contributions

- **Verified synthesis pipeline:** SKT curates suitable skills, composes sets of one to three skills, and generates executable task packages containing instructions, environments, evaluators, and reference solutions (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figure 2).
- **Layered quality control:** Tasks undergo deterministic checks, semantic LLM review, paired with-skill/without-skill dependence testing, difficulty filtering, and feedback-guided repair. Completed trajectories must receive full evaluator credit and demonstrate substantive, correctly ordered use of every designated skill (Sections 3.2–3.3; Appendix B).
- **End-to-end skill-use training:** Complete verified trajectories supervise assistant reasoning, tool calls, and responses through masked autoregressive full-parameter SFT (Section 3.4).
- **Training corpus:** The pipeline produces 4,000 accepted tasks and 27,164 verified trajectories from 2,000 public skills (Section 4.1).
- **Evaluation asset:** SkillEval is a held-out, executable 100-task benchmark created with the same pipeline from a skill pool disjoint from training (Section 4.1; Appendix C.2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| The largest reported matched gain was on SkillEval. | Qwen3.5-9B with DeepAgents increased from 51.62 to 70.53, a gain of 18.91 points. | Section 4.2; Table 1 |

These results support the authors’ claim that verified trajectory SFT improves use of externally provided skills across the tested models, harnesses, and benchmarks. The skill-withholding experiment suggests that the training primarily improves skill exploitation rather than simply internalizing their contents.

The evidence does not establish generalization to arbitrary backbones, unseen harness families, more than three skills during synthesis, or real-world deployments. It also does not isolate the causal contribution of every individual verification and repair component.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **No explicit author limitations section:** The supplied paper does not present a dedicated discussion of limitations.
- **Analyst inference — verifier dependence:** Task authoring and several verification decisions rely on DeepSeek V4 Pro, while difficulty control uses another model. Systematic verifier bias or correlated generator–verifier errors are possible; no human audit or verifier-accuracy measurement is reported (Section 4.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete component attribution:** The reported ablation compares the full verified pipeline with raw unverified synthesis, but does not separately ablate deterministic checks, semantic review, skill-dependence testing, difficulty control, repair, or trajectory validation (Section 4.3; Figure 4).
- **Analyst inference — constrained model and interface coverage:** Evidence is limited to two trained backbones and two harnesses. Cross-harness transfer remains materially below matched training, retaining only 49.1%–58.1% of its gain (Sections 4.1 and 4.4).
- **Analyst inference — benchmark construction coupling:** SkillEval uses the same synthesis pipeline as the training tasks, although its skills, task packages, and trajectories are held out. Shared generation and verification conventions could favor models trained on those conventions (Section 4.1; Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference — limited statistical resolution:** Results use four complete benchmark runs. The supplied text gives standard-deviation methodology but omits most table cells and uncertainty values, preventing detailed significance assessment (Section 4.1; Tables 1–2).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Verifier prompts and implementation source are expressly not reproduced, and public availability of the data, benchmark, trained checkpoints, or pipeline code is not established (Appendix B).

## Open research questions

1. **Which verification stages produce the gains?** The paper contrasts full verification with an unverified pipeline but does not isolate individual gates. A factorial ablation could remove task execution checks, semantic review, skill-dependence tests, difficulty filtering, repair, and trajectory validation one at a time.

2. **How robust is SKT to independent or human verification?** Model-based validators may share biases with synthetic task generation. A blinded human audit and evaluation with independently trained verifier models could measure false acceptance and rejection rates.

3. **Does training generalize to broader interfaces and longer compositions?** Only two harnesses and synthesis configurations of one to three skills are tested. A next experiment could evaluate unseen harnesses and tasks requiring four or more distinct skills without additional SFT.

4. **Does scaling continue because of skill diversity or simply more trajectories?** The scaling experiment jointly increases skill count and the corresponding trajectory pool. A controlled study could hold trajectory count fixed while varying skill diversity, then reverse the control.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text provides detailed methods, protocols, and several numerical summaries, but the contents of Tables 1–9 are missing beyond captions and prose-selected values. Material unavailable information includes most per-condition means and standard deviations, intermediate scaling scores, task/trajectory qualification acceptance rates, repair and retry budgets, verifier prompts, hardware quantities beyond eight workers, human validation results, detailed costs, and confirmed release or licensing information for the corpus, benchmark, code, and checkpoints.
