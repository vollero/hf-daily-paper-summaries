# DAPD: Dual-Anchored Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01735) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01735)


## One-sentence takeaway

DAPD reduces “privilege illusion” in on-policy self-distillation by aligning teacher and student distributions under matched information conditions and combining reference-based correctness with student-reachable rollout guidance.

## Problem and motivation

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a language model from a same-model teacher given privileged information—such as a reference solution—that the inference-time student cannot access. The authors argue that this information asymmetry transfers privilege-dependent behavior alongside useful guidance, causing unsupported answer claims and degraded reasoning performance (§§1–2.2).

Existing filtering and routing methods still retain the asymmetric teacher–student information conditions and rely mainly on reference guidance. The identified gap is therefore structural: supervision should align distributions with matched information access while also exploiting useful on-policy reasoning (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Information-asymmetry diagnosis:** The paper defines “Entangled Distillation” as OPSD’s mixing of reproducible guidance with behavior dependent on unavailable privileged information. Replacing the unconditioned student with an information-matched, self-conditioned distribution substantially reduces the measured failure behavior (§2.2, Figure 2).

- **Dual-Path Anchoring (DPA):** A trainable `Self` distribution, conditioned on the completion it predicts, bridges the unconditioned `None` and privileged `Cross` distributions. An unconditioned path combines the original OPSD term with an Inference Anchor, while a privileged path aligns `Self` and `Cross` (§§2.3, 3.1–3.2; Equations 2–6).

- **Dual-Source Anchoring (DSA):** DPA is applied in both rollout-to-reference and reference-to-rollout directions. A coefficient \(\lambda\) balances reliable but potentially off-policy reference guidance against reachable but potentially incorrect rollout guidance (§3.3; Equation 7).

- **Supporting analysis and variants:** The appendix derives conditional and sequence-level alignment bounds under explicit bridge-consistency and compatibility assumptions, while experiments explore scale-dependent weighting and reference-free variants using independent or verifier-selected rollouts (Appendix B; §§4.4–4.5).

The results support the authors’ claim that DAPD improves the tested Qwen3 models and that both path and source components contribute. The isolated anchor intervention and wrong-claim dynamics provide evidence consistent with the proposed information-asymmetry mechanism, but they do not prove it is the sole causal explanation. Headline training comparisons use one matched seed, and the supplied text does not report uncertainty intervals for benchmark scores, so statistical robustness across training runs is not established.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Constructing multiple anchored distributions increases training-time computation, although inference cost is unchanged (§6).
- Loss and source weights may require recalibration across model scales or architectures (§6).
- The verified reference-free variant requires an automatic correctness signal, which may be unavailable for open-ended tasks (§6).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Evaluation is restricted to the Qwen3 family and LoRA adaptation; transfer to other architectures or full-parameter training is unestablished (§4.1; Appendix A.6).
- Headline results use a single seed, 42. Matched randomness improves comparison control but does not measure run-to-run variability (Appendix A.6).
- The wrong-claim probe captures a narrow pattern—failed derivation followed by attribution of an incorrect answer to memory or an external source—so it may miss other forms of privilege-dependent behavior (Appendix C.2).
- The theoretical alignment result depends on bridge-consistency and local-compatibility assumptions. Compatibility is checked only on a small Qwen3-4B math sample, not across tasks, scales, or training (§3.2; Appendix B).
- Exact wall-clock, FLOP, or memory overhead for the multiple distributions is not reported in the supplied text, despite the acknowledged training-cost increase.
- The source states that official benchmark metrics are used, but the retrieved tables omit many underlying cell values, limiting independent inspection of per-task effects.

## Open research questions

1. **Does DAPD generalize beyond Qwen3 and LoRA?**  
   This remains open because all reported models belong to one family and use parameter-efficient adaptation. A next experiment could compare DAPD and OPSD across several unrelated architectures under both LoRA and full-parameter training.

2. **Can source and anchor weights be selected online?**  
   Optimal reference weight changes from 0.5 at 1.7B to 0.2 at larger tested scales, showing that a fixed global coefficient is unreliable (§4.4). A next experiment could learn token- or example-level weights from rollout uncertainty or verifier confidence and compare them with tuned fixed coefficients.

3. **How broadly does the information-matching mechanism apply to other privileged inputs?**  
   Experiments use completions, while retrieved documents, tool traces, plans, and verifier feedback are only proposed as future directions (Appendix E). A next experiment could repeat the anchor ablations with one such source and directly measure unsupported reliance at inference.

4. **Is the proxy bridge valid throughout training and across domains?**  
   The theory assumes bridge consistency and compatible shared-parameter gradients, with limited empirical validation on math examples (Appendix B). A next experiment could track bridge distance and gradient compatibility over checkpoints in reasoning, coding, and instruction-following models and test whether they predict DAPD’s gains.
