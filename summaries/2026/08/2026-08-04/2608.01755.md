# Deferred Exposure of Future Trajectories for Verifiable Reasoning in Autonomous Driving VLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01755) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01755)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that revealing future trajectories before reasoning encourages post-hoc rationalization, then improve driving reasoning by making a VLM decide from scene evidence before selecting among explicit trajectory candidates under verifiable reinforcement learning.

## Problem and motivation

Autonomous-driving VLMs are often trained on chain-of-thought annotations generated with access to the logged ground-truth future trajectory. The authors argue that this reverses the desired causal direction: teachers can justify an already-revealed outcome instead of deriving a decision from visible scene evidence.

Their controlled study finds that trajectory exposure increases severe hallucinations and lowers human preference, particularly on causally demanding maneuvers (Section 2; Appendix B). Simply hiding the future is insufficient because open-ended coordinate generation mixes high-level reasoning with continuous geometry and vehicle dynamics. The identified gap is therefore a scene-first reasoning interface that retains exact, trajectory-level verification without requiring the VLM to synthesize trajectories.

## Approach and contributions

- **Trajectory-anchoring study:** A paired, human-scored experiment varies only whether the teacher receives the logged future, testing whether future exposure damages causal faithfulness (Section 2; Appendix B).

- **AD-MCQ benchmark:** Planning becomes exact selection among six scene-specific, decoded trajectory prototypes. A K-means codebook quantizes logged futures, while similarity-controlled distractors preserve distinctions in geometry, braking, and speed profile (Section 3; Appendix D.1).

- **Deferred Exposure of Future Trajectories (DEFT):** Turn 1 produces a high-level decision from multi-view history, ego state, and navigation alone; Turn 2 reveals candidates and asks the model to ground its prior commitment in an explicit trajectory (Section 4.1).

- **DEFT-RLVR:** GRPO jointly optimizes both turns. Exact candidate correctness gates a weighted, instance-specific rubric reward applied to the normalized candidate-blind reasoning trace. Rubrics are generated offline from scene history, while online grading is text-only and receives neither images nor future-related answers (Sections 4.2–4.3).

The results support the authors’ claim that exposure order is consequential: matched JEFT/DEFT comparisons isolate when identical candidate information becomes available, and gains recur across two backbones, distillation, RLVR, an external domain, and resampled candidate sets. Human–judge validation also reports CFS Spearman correlation of 0.78 and HLD exact agreement of 92.0% on 200 outputs (Table 12).

They do not establish improved closed-loop driving safety or control performance. ACC measures agreement with a quantized logged future among constructed options, while CFS and HLD are mostly model-judged; neither proves that the selected trajectory is uniquely safe or optimal.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** AD-MCQ is a verification interface, not a replacement for a downstream continuous planner, and the logged future is not assumed to be the only safe trajectory (Appendix A.2; Appendix D.1).

- **Demonstration-relative labels:** Exactly one option represents the quantized logged future. Thus, correctness means matching demonstrated behavior within the supplied candidate set, not exhaustively evaluating feasible alternatives (Section 3; Appendix D.1).

- **Narrow causal distribution:** Dev and Test deliberately emphasize stopping, hard braking, and sharp turns; routine straight and low-speed scenes are discarded. This strengthens causal stress testing but limits claims about the full natural driving distribution (Appendix D.1).

- **Private data component:** Training, codebook construction, and the anchoring study include an internal driving corpus. The supplied text does not establish whether that data or the resulting benchmark/codebook will be released.

- **Model-based evaluation and rewards:** CFS, HLD, rubric generation, and online rubric grading depend on Qwen models. Human validation is encouraging, but the CFS audit uses one human label per output and is not stratified by method (Appendix E).

- **Resource intensity:** Main RLVR uses 64 accelerators for policy training; rubric variants use an additional separate pool of 64. Reported wall-clock comparisons exclude offline rubric generation and do not report total accelerator-hours (Appendices D.3–D.4).

- **Analyst inference:** Because the main evaluation is open-loop candidate selection from logged scenes, performance may not transfer directly to interactive, closed-loop conditions where other agents react to the ego vehicle.

## Open research questions

1. **Does deferred exposure improve closed-loop safety and progress?** The paper evaluates logged-scene candidate selection, not interactive execution. A next experiment could place DEFT-derived decisions in a simulator and compare collision rate, route completion, comfort, and intervention frequency against JEFT.

2. **Can correctness accommodate multiple safe futures?** AD-MCQ uses one quantized logged future as the oracle even though the authors acknowledge alternatives may be safe. A next experiment could label several feasible candidates using expert review or a validated planner and train with set-valued rewards.

3. **How dependent are gains on the rubric generator and judge?** The same model family generates and grades rubrics, and the paper does not report cross-judge robustness. A next experiment could swap independent graders and compare reward agreement, learned policies, and human-rated faithfulness.

4. **Does deferred exposure generalize beyond hard-causal scene categories?** Test construction emphasizes braking, stopping, and turns. A next experiment could evaluate a natural-distribution, closed-loop suite spanning merges, yielding, dense interactions, unusual weather, and low-speed maneuvers.
