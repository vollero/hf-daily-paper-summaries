# Relax Within, Balance Across: Geometry-Guided Load Balancing for Vision-Language Mixture-of-Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00574) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00574)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReBA prevents image–text routing errors from canceling at one token mix by balancing modalities separately and weighting each image equally, substantially improving load balance across tested vision-language workloads without materially changing mean task accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Vision-language MoE workloads vary in their proportions of image and text tokens because of resolution, tiling, image count, and prompt length. The standard token-level Switch auxiliary loss balances only their combined load, allowing large, opposing modality-specific errors to cancel at one composition and reappear when that composition changes. On Split-Qwen3VL-4B, the authors observe more than a fivefold imbalance change across resolutions despite using the same router (§3.1, Fig. 1).

The identified gap is that balance at one mixed-token composition does not ensure balance across compositions, while existing objectives discard modality and sample identity (§2–§3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Composition-shift analysis:** For fixed image and text routing profiles, the authors derive an exact quadratic law relating squared RMS-CV to image-token fraction. Its curvature is determined by the image–text conditional-profile gap (§3.4, Eq. 2–3; Appendix B.1).
- **Geometry-guided routing units:** Router-input measurements indicate a strong modality boundary and a weaker but measurable boundary between source images (§3.3, Fig. 4).
- **ReBA objective:** ReBA uses separate image and text hard–soft balancing terms, preventing cross-modal cancellation. It averages patches within each image and then gives images equal weight; text remains pooled (§4.1–§4.2, Eq. 4–7).
- **Workload-wide evaluation:** The method is evaluated on four dense-to-sparse split backbones, seven vision-language benchmarks, controlled composition curves, and physical resolution or tiling shifts (§5; Tables 1–3, Fig. 5).

The evidence establishes consistent reductions in dispatch imbalance across the reported split-backbone benchmarks and workload shifts, with broadly similar mean task accuracy. It does not establish end-to-end latency gains, generalization to trained native MoEs, or robustness outside the tested composition and preprocessing ranges.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The fixed-profile law isolates token-ratio effects and does not model resolution-, tiling-, or content-induced changes in conditional routing profiles (§3.4; Appendix B.1). Native physical sweeps show that such drift can flatten or reverse fixed-profile predictions (Table S14).
- **Author-stated:** The expert-compute result is not measured latency or throughput; it assumes equally fast experts, perfect placement, and excludes communication, kernels, shared experts, and non-MoE work (§5.6; Appendix C.3).
- **Author-stated:** ReBA training is evaluated only on four dense-to-sparse split backbones. Native MoEs receive diagnostics only, so applicability to their training remains untested (§5.1; Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** The 500-sample routing probe comes from the Cambrian training corpus rather than a held-out set (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Training appears to use one run per method/coefficient; the supplied text reports bootstrap uncertainty for paired physical evaluations but no across-training-seed variability.
- **Analyst inference:** The method depends on correct modality and image-instance segmentation, and the evidence does not test missing, noisy, or ambiguous identifiers.
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation covers one epoch of supervised fine-tuning and image–text workloads; longer training, video, and other modality combinations are not evaluated.

## Open research questions

1. **Does ReBA improve real serving latency?** The paper reports only an idealized compute proxy. A next experiment should benchmark end-to-end latency and throughput under expert parallelism while separately measuring communication, kernels, and straggler time.
2. **Does ReBA train native sparse vision-language MoEs effectively?** Native models are used only for diagnosis. A next experiment should fine-tune matched native-MoE checkpoints with Std-Aux and ReBA across multiple seeds.
3. **How robust is equal-image weighting under heterogeneous image costs?** Images receive equal auxiliary-objective weight even when token counts differ. A next experiment should compare equal, token-weighted, and cost-aware instance weighting across broad resolution and image-count distributions.
4. **Can the objective handle additional structured modalities?** The experiments cover image and pooled text routing. A next experiment should introduce video frames, audio segments, or multi-turn text as explicit routing instances and measure both load and task quality.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated methods and reported numerical comparisons because the supplied text includes the main sections, tables, and appendices.

Material missing or unresolved information includes training-seed replication, measured wall-clock overhead and end-to-end latency, results from training native MoEs with ReBA, behavior beyond the tested workload ranges, and evidence for video or non-image multimodal inputs.
