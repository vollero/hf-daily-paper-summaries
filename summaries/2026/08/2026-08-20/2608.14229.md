# The More Popular, The Harder to Forget: Adaptive Popularity for LLM Unlearning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14229) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14229)


## One-sentence takeaway

AdaPop uses an external fact-popularity proxy to sustain stronger unlearning pressure on popular facts and self-limit pressure on rare ones, improving paraphrase and adversarial robustness over stable baselines while preserving tested general capabilities.

## Problem and motivation

Gradient-based LLM unlearning typically applies uniform or model-confidence-derived pressure to all facts, although popular facts appear more deeply encoded and harder to erase. The authors call the resulting failure mode the “popularity gap”: popular facts remain recoverable under reformulation, while excessive pressure on rare facts risks collateral retention damage (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

Prior work characterized this gap but did not offer a training-time remedy. The authors argue that current output confidence is insufficient because it reflects surface behavior rather than encoding depth (§§1–2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Popularity-aware forgetting:** AdaPop maps each external popularity score \(s_i\) to a fact-specific exponent \(\beta_i=a s_i^{-b}\). This exponent modifies confidence-weighted token ascent so popular facts receive pressure-sustaining updates (\(\beta<1\)) and rare facts receive self-limiting updates (\(\beta>1\)); exponents are clipped to \([0.05,2.0]\) (§3.2; Appendix A–B).
- **Adaptive retention control:** An epoch-level projected dual-ascent controller adjusts the retain-loss coefficient when relative retain drift exceeds a tolerance, avoiding a fixed per-dataset retain weight (§3.3; Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical study:** The method is evaluated with LoRA on three 7–8B instruction-tuned model families, DUET and RWKU, standard and reformulated queries, output metrics, internal-state diagnostics, and general-capability benchmarks (§§4–5).
- **Proxy and component analyses:** The paper compares Wikidata, LLM-judged, and measured corpus-frequency signals and separately ablates popularity weighting and the retain controller (§5.5; Appendices E–F, N).

The results support the authors’ claim that popularity-conditioned updates improve factual erasure under the tested paraphrase and adversarial settings without measurable MMLU degradation. Internal metrics are consistent with deeper disruption than WGA, but they are diagnostics rather than direct proof that knowledge is irrecoverably removed. The evidence does not establish effectiveness under full fine-tuning, at larger scales, outside entity-centric factual QA, or against every possible extraction or relearning attack.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- AdaPop requires an external, per-sample popularity proxy; Wikidata coverage does not naturally extend to procedural or creative knowledge (Limitations, p. 10).
- The coefficients were calibrated on DUET’s Wikidata-score range. Proxies on materially different scales require rescaling or recalibration, and using mismatched coefficients reduced retention (§5.5; Limitations).
- Evaluation is LoRA-only on three 7–8B models; full fine-tuning was excluded and left for future work (§4.2; Limitations).
- The optimization is non-convex, so the authors do not claim formal convergence of the dual-ascent controller (Appendix M.1).

**Analyst inferences**

- Numerical robustness conclusions are confined to DUET paraphrases and RWKU’s nine-strategy adversarial subset; they do not demonstrate resistance to unrestricted extraction.
- ROUGE-L is averaged over three seeds, but cosine similarity is single-seed in Tables 1–3, weakening uncertainty estimates for that metric.
- The internal metrics show model changes correlated with stronger forgetting, but the paper does not establish that these changes prevent later relearning.
- Some method-wide statements rely on appendices and fixed shared settings; although a five-rate sweep is reported for primary methods, equally exhaustive tuning of all 11 additional baselines is not established by the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the approach unlearn non-entity knowledge?** Sitelinks do not apply naturally to procedural, creative, or code content. A next experiment could construct continuous difficulty proxies for one such domain and compare calibrated AdaPop against uniform weighting.

2. **Does popularity weighting transfer to full-parameter unlearning and larger models?** The paper tests only LoRA on 7–8B models. A controlled study could compare LoRA and full fine-tuning using separately tuned hyperparameters across multiple scales.

3. **Does AdaPop resist relearning and stronger extraction attacks?** The reported tests cover paraphrases and a predefined adversarial subset, not post-unlearning retraining. A next experiment could measure knowledge recovery after small amounts of relearning data and adaptive black-box attacks.

4. **Can calibration be learned without manual proxy anchors?** Different proxy scales require rescaling or recalibration. A next experiment could estimate the exponent mapping from a small validation set and compare it with the fixed two-anchor power law.
