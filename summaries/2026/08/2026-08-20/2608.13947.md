# Scaling Creative Writing Beyond Story-Centric Data with Attribute-Guided Genre Expansion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13947) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13947)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors convert human-written story prompts into a 50K-example, 13-genre instruction corpus using manually curated genre constraints, and report that fine-tuning on it improves creative-writing quality, genre coverage, and output novelty.

## Problem and motivation

Public creative-writing data is dominated by stories, while practical requests also include lyrics, rap, scripts, game designs, and other formats with distinct structural and functional conventions. The authors argue that scaling story data alone cannot teach these conventions, and that generic synthetic-instruction pipelines lack compact validity signals for genre fidelity. They identify gaps in systematic genre coverage, genre-form adherence, and variation between open-ended and tightly specified prompts (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Attribute-guided genre expansion:** The method separates thematic diversity from format control. Five human-authored r/WritingPrompts examples supply themes, while a randomly sampled subset of manually reviewed, genre-specific attributes supplies structural constraints (Section 2; Figures 2–3).
- **Controlled instruction synthesis:** GPT-5-mini generates five queries per template using verbalized sampling; Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking generates responses; Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct filters responses falling more than two standard deviations below the mean quality score (Section 2, p. 3).
- **Multi-Genre Collection:** The pipeline produces an English corpus of 50K query–response pairs across 13 primary genres, plus a long-tail “Etc.” category (Figure 4).
- **Empirical study:** The paper compares fine-tuning gains across three base models, equal-size 2K subsets of competing writing datasets, genre-count conditions, independent LLM judges, and a 50-prompt human evaluation (Section 3; Tables 1–5).

These results support the author claim that the corpus is useful for the tested models and benchmarks, with genre breadth associated with higher novelty. They do not establish that genre count alone caused the gains: the genre-count conditions are insufficiently detailed, statistical uncertainty is unreported, and the t-SNE visualization is suggestive rather than proof of semantic distinctness.

## Limitations and validity risks

No dedicated limitations section is supplied.

- **Analyst inference — synthetic-data dependence:** Queries, responses, and filtering all rely on specific LLMs, so their biases or stylistic signatures may propagate into the corpus (Section 2).
- **Analyst inference — reference contamination risk:** The Multi-Genre test uses outputs from the same model family and exact response generator named in data construction, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking, as references. The paper does not quantify resulting preference effects (Sections 2 and 3.1).
- **Analyst inference — narrow language scope:** The corpus and evaluations are filtered or constructed in English, so multilingual generalization is not tested (Sections 2 and 3.1).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete causal ablation:** Table 3 varies genre count, but the supplied text does not specify genre identities, sampling repetitions, training-data-size controls, or uncertainty. This weakens the claim that diversity directly causes novelty.
- **Analyst inference — limited human validation:** Human evaluation covers only 50 WritingBench prompts; annotator count, agreement, uncertainty, and genre breakdown are absent from the supplied text (Section 3.2; Table 5).
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility gaps:** The exact attribute lists, filtering rubric, score distribution, rejection count, prompts, random seeds, and corpus access location are not provided in the retrieved text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does genre breadth help independently of dataset size and composition?** Table 3 lacks detailed controls. A next experiment could train multiple seeds on equal-sized corpora with randomly selected versus structurally diverse genre subsets and report confidence intervals.
2. **How much does manual attribute curation contribute?** The paper does not compare curated attributes against raw extracted attributes or genre labels alone. A factorial ablation could isolate manual review, attribute sampling, thematic seeds, and verbalized sampling.
3. **Does the method transfer beyond English and the selected genres?** Only English and 13 primary genres are evaluated. A next experiment could build multilingual or unseen-genre test sets and measure zero-shot genre adherence.
4. **Are gains robust to non-LLM and blind human evaluation?** Most evidence uses LLM judges, while human evaluation is small. A larger blinded study with multiple annotators, agreement statistics, genre-stratified results, and pairwise preferences would test robustness.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported pipeline and tabulated results; **medium** for causal interpretations.

Material missing information includes statistical significance or confidence intervals, dataset release details, exact genre-attribute lists, filter rejection totals and rubric, genre-ablation construction, random seeds, human-annotation procedures and agreement, per-genre performance, and training compute or cost.
