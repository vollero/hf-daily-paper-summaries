# SemComp-Bench: Benchmarking Semantic Task Completion in Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17426) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17426)


## One-sentence takeaway

SemComp-Bench evaluates whether generated videos both reach an instructed outcome and preserve task-relevant semantics from a reference image, finding that the best tested model achieves only 37.8% joint Outcome Achievement despite substantially higher visual-reliability scores.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses outcome-oriented video generation: producing a visibly completed target state grounded in a reference image, without requiring the intermediate procedure or irrelevant appearance details to remain unchanged. The authors argue that existing benchmarks emphasize fidelity, temporal coherence, prompt adherence, identity, motion, or physical plausibility, but do not jointly test outcome completion and task-relevant semantic grounding (Sections 1 and 2.1).

This matters because a video can look coherent or depict activity while never reaching the requested result—or can reach a superficially correct result by replacing the referenced entity with an unrelated one (Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **New task formulation:** Semantic Task Completion Video Generation requires outcome realization plus preservation or modification of reference attributes relevant to the instruction; intermediate steps are optional (Section 1, Figure 1).

- **SemComp-Data:** A dataset of 1,273 instances derived from Koala-36M. Each contains a reference frame, paired brief and detailed instructions, and an outcome-centric clip from the same source video (Sections 3 and 5.1; Table S5).

- **Four-stage curation pipeline:** Candidate Filtering, State Mining, Video Extension, and Instruction Structuring convert full-context videos into standardized instances (Sections 3.1–3.4; Figure 3).

- **SemComp-Bench:** A VLM-based protocol uses evidence-backed binary judgments to report Outcome Achievement (OA) across four conjunctive criteria and Generation Reliability (GR) as the mean of five criteria (Sections 4.1–4.2).

- **Conditioning analysis:** A controlled comparison on three model families examines image versus text conditioning and detailed versus brief instructions (Section 5.4; Table 3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The best OA result remains below 40%. | On 60 domain-balanced SemComp-Core instances, with I2V reference conditioning, detailed instructions, one 720p video per instance, and three VLM evaluation calls, HunyuanVideo-1.5-720P-I2V scores **37.8% OA**; Wan2.2-I2V-A14B is second at **28.3%**. | Sections 5.1–5.2; Table 1 |
| Reliability is much higher than task completion. | Under the same detailed-I2V setting, Seedance 2.0 reaches **91.8% GR**, while its OA is **20.0%**; HunyuanVideo leads OA at **37.8%** but has **79.1% GR**. | Tables 1–2; Sections 5.2–5.3 |
| Within-scene coherence is the weakest GR criterion. | Across seven tested models, within-scene spatiotemporal-coherence pass rates range from **0.328 to 0.739**, lower than the other reported GR criteria in most model profiles. | Table 2; Section 5.3 |
| Image conditioning substantially improves joint completion. | Detailed I2V versus detailed T2V OA: Wan2.2 **28.3% vs 4.4%**; CogVideoX **14.4% vs 5.0%**; HunyuanVideo **37.8% vs 4.4%**. Checkpoints are stated to have the same parameter scale within each family. | Table 3; Section 5.4 |
| Detailed text improves T2V outcome completion over brief text. | Detailed versus brief T2V OA: Wan2.2 **4.4% vs 0.6%**; CogVideoX **5.0% vs 0.6%**; HunyuanVideo **4.4% vs 1.7%**. | Table 3; Section 5.4 |
| Evaluation variability is sometimes material. | Across three evaluator calls, OA-score standard deviations range from **0.96 to 4.41 percentage points** in the main detailed-I2V comparison; GR-score standard deviations range from **1.35 to 7.20 points**. | Tables S6–S7 |

The results support the authors’ claim that visual reliability and semantic task completion are distinct: high GR does not guarantee high OA. They also support a benefit from reference-image conditioning within the three matched model families.

They do not establish general performance across the full 1,273-instance dataset, repeated video-generation samples, alternative evaluators, or domains outside the six-category taxonomy. The conditioning results are associations within the tested model families and settings, not a universal causal comparison of I2V and T2V architectures.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** Whether SemComp-Data improves models through task-specific training has not been tested (Section 6).

- **Small evaluation subset:** Only 60 of 1,273 instances are used for model evaluation—10 per domain (Section 5.1; Table S5). This may limit precision and coverage of the 21 categories.

- **Single generated sample per condition:** Each model produces one video per instance, so generation-seed variability is not measured; fixed seeds are used only where supported (Section 5.1).

- **Evaluator dependence:** Curation and scoring both use Doubao-Seed-1.8, and no human-agreement study or comparison with another evaluator is reported in the supplied text. This is an analyst-identified validity risk based on Section 5.1.

- **VLM-call variability:** Some three-call standard deviations are sizable, including 7.20 points for Phantom’s GR and 15.84 points for its within-scene-coherence pass rate (Table S7).

- **Filtering bias:** Direct, case-sensitive title substring filtering may miss differently capitalized narration-dependent videos or remove visually suitable ones. This is an analyst inference from Section A and Table S1.

- **Instruction ambiguity:** The main experiment says Tables 1–2 use detailed instructions, while the supplied OA evaluation prompt labels its textual input “brief instruction” (Section 5.1; Figure S2). The text does not clearly reconcile this discrepancy.

- **Reproducibility constraints:** Reconstruction metadata, timestamps, and prompts are described, but the supplied text does not state licensing terms, exact model/API version pinning, evaluation cost, or whether all source videos remain accessible.

## Open research questions

1. **Does training on SemComp-Data improve semantic task completion?**  
   This is explicitly unvalidated in Section 6. A next experiment could fine-tune a fixed I2V model on SemComp-Data and compare OA, GR, and criterion-level changes against an unchanged checkpoint on a held-out split.

2. **How well do VLM judgments agree with humans and other evaluators?**  
   No human-validation or evaluator-transfer results are reported. A next experiment could collect blinded human labels and compare agreement, calibration, and ranking stability across several VLM judges.

3. **Do results hold beyond SemComp-Core?**  
   The reported model evaluation covers only 60 balanced instances. A next experiment could evaluate the full 1,273-instance collection and report confidence intervals and per-domain, per-category, and alignment-type results.

4. **How robust are model rankings to generation randomness?**  
   Only one generated video per instance is evaluated. A next experiment could generate several seeds per condition and separate generation variance from evaluator-call variance.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported tables because the supplied text includes the full main paper and supplement.

Material missing or unresolved information includes human-evaluator validation, cross-VLM evaluator comparisons, full-dataset model results, per-domain performance, multiple generation seeds, training experiments, statistical significance tests, resource/cost measurements, licensing details, and clarification of the brief-versus-detailed evaluator input discrepancy.
