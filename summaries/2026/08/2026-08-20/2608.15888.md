# Bounded Agents: Delegation Security for Multi-Agent AI Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15888) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15888)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper proposes infrastructure-enforced, session-scoped authorization that attenuates delegated authority and blocks prohibited action sequences, eliminating observed exfiltration in its evaluated benchmarks while reducing AgentDojo utility by 8.6 percentage points in the interactive setting.

## Problem and motivation

LLM agents can invoke tools and recursively delegate work, yet conventional authorization typically grants static permissions and evaluates each request independently. This permits an agent to violate task intent, pass excessive authority to sub-agents, or combine individually allowed actions—such as reading confidential data and sending external messages—into a prohibited outcome.

The authors argue that prompt injection’s consequences therefore depend partly on authorization architecture, not solely on model robustness. They identify three gaps: security controls inside model prompts are not reliable enforcement, per-action checks ignore unsafe compositions, and delegation lacks systematic per-hop attenuation and blast-radius containment (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Agentic Principal Chain (APC):** A signed, session-level authorization state recording the principal chain, attenuated scope, six-dimensional delegation budget, declared intent, and prior actions. Infrastructure narrows this state at every delegation hop (Section 4).

- **Six-condition runtime enforcement:** An external policy gateway checks identity, scope/composition/budgets, context binding, approval binding, evidence commitment, and task intent before executing each tool call (Section 5.3).

- **Composition closure:** Pairwise and ordered \(k\)-tuple restrictions prohibit unsafe action-type combinations across a session. Composition Soundness is proved conditional on complete restrictions, static policy, and serialized admission (Theorem 4.8; Proposition 4.9).

- **Delegation containment:** Blast Radius Monotonicity proves that reachable authority cannot increase down a delegation chain when scope narrows and consumed budgets propagate cumulatively (Theorem 4.6).

- **Compromised-model evaluation:** Ground-truth malicious calls are inserted into the execution pipeline, testing authorization independently of whether the LLM resists an attack (Section 6.4).

- **Released artifact:** The authors report a Python reference implementation, tests, evaluation tools, configurations, and data (Section 6.1; Appendix I).

The evidence supports deterministic enforcement of configured action restrictions and strong observed exfiltration prevention in the tested synthetic settings. It also demonstrates measurable utility loss and low isolated authorization-path latency.

It does not establish worst-case security, production effectiveness, semantic correctness of policy authoring, cross-session protection, or safety of malicious parameters in an otherwise authorized action. The formal composition guarantee is relative to complete restrictions and serialized admission, while the empirical rates are explicitly observed outcomes rather than security bounds.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The action taxonomy and restriction set require expert judgment; incomplete restrictions weaken the guarantee, as the InjecAgent partial-policy result demonstrates (Sections 6.2 and 7.4).
- Compromised-model calls are injected after the first legitimate action, so the evaluation does not cover arbitrary multi-turn interleaving or adaptation to enforcement feedback (Section 7.4).
- Benchmarks are synthetic, adaptive attacks are self-designed, and no production deployment is evaluated (Section 7.4).
- APC does not address parameter-level attacks, single-action misuse within scope, model alignment, or compromise of trusted infrastructure (Sections 3.5 and 7.4).
- Composition state is session-local, allowing coordinated cross-session attacks unless durable lineage tracking is added (Sections 6.5 and 7.4).
- Composition Soundness requires complete restrictions, serialized admission, and a static effective restriction set (Section 4.7).
- The proofs are not machine-checked (Section 7.4).
- Reported latency excludes network, inference, remote PDP, and evidence-store costs and rises under host contention (Appendix D).
- The matched Progent comparison covers only available domains and lacks a redistributed Progent-side artifact (Section 6.4).

**Analyst inference:** Because policy taxonomy errors caused ASB residuals and intent overlap caused most AgentDojo residuals, deployment quality may depend heavily on domain-specific policy engineering. The paper quantifies authoring burden only incompletely in the supplied text because Table 6’s detailed values are absent.

**Analyst inference:** The single-author evaluation and self-designed adaptive suite increase the value of independent replication and external red-teaming; the text reports neither.

## Open research questions

1. **Can APC stop adaptive, long-horizon attacks?** The injected attack occurs at one fixed point and does not adapt to denials. A next experiment could let a white-box adversary interleave benign and malicious calls over many turns while observing enforcement feedback.

2. **How can composition state safely cross session boundaries?** Session splitting is admitted by design. An experiment could add durable lineage identifiers and measure security, privacy, storage cost, and false positives across linked sessions.

3. **Can policy restrictions be generated and validated reliably?** Missing pairs and taxonomy errors produce substantial residual ASR. A next study could compare expert-authored, automatically inferred, and hybrid policies on held-out prohibited outcomes, measuring coverage and utility.

4. **Can parameter-aware enforcement cover authorized single-action misuse?** APC operates primarily at action-type and resource granularity. A next experiment could combine APC with tool-specific parameter validation and reevaluate InjecAgent direct-harm cases and intent-overlap residuals.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated architecture, assumptions, and reported aggregate results because the supplied text is extensive and includes methods, formal claims, evaluation settings, and limitations.

Material missing information includes the detailed numeric contents of several referenced tables—especially Tables 4–7, 10–18—some appendix attack-level results, exact per-domain policy-authoring effort, full utility values by suite, statistical uncertainty beyond selected results, and independent reproduction or production-deployment evidence.
