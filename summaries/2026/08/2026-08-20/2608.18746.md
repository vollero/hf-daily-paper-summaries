# Decision-Metric Alignment in Latent World Models: Diagnostics and Action-Conditioned Objectives for MPC Planning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18746) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18746)


## One-sentence takeaway

DA-LeWM adds inverse-dynamics and demonstration-conditioned goal-action supervision to a JEPA latent world model, improving how Euclidean latent distance ranks MPC candidates and raising online success across four simulated tasks despite nearly unchanged linear-probe scores.

## Problem and motivation

JEPA-style world models can plan by minimizing Euclidean distance between a predicted terminal latent and a goal latent. The authors argue that decodability of task variables does not ensure that this distance orders candidate action sequences by actual task progress—a distinct property they call **decision-metric alignment** (Sections 1 and 3; Figure 1).

This matters because CEM selects candidates by relative latent-cost rank. Existing probes answer what a representation contains, but not whether its geometry supplies a useful planning objective. The identified gap is therefore both diagnostic and methodological: latent MPC lacks direct measurements of rank alignment and objectives explicitly designed to improve it.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Alignment diagnostics:** Plan-Real Spearman compares latent-cost and simulator-cost rankings across random plans; CEM-stage Spearman repeats the comparison at random, intermediate, and elite stages of CEM search (Section 3; Figure 2).

2. **Sufficient-condition analysis:** Under pointwise terminal-rollout consistency and a bi-Lipschitz encoder assumption, Proposition 1 bounds latent cost using real cost, encoder constants, and rollout error. Corollary 1 gives a sufficient candidate-margin condition for rank preservation (Section 4).

3. **DA-LeWM:** The method augments LeWM with two training-only MLP heads: inverse dynamics and demonstration-conditioned goal-action prediction, each weighted by 0.1. MPC and its Euclidean latent cost remain unchanged at inference (Section 4, Equation 5; Appendix A).

4. **Empirical distinction between information and geometry:** Across non-collapsed PushT variants, probe scores are similar while rank agreement and control success differ substantially (Section 5; Tables 2 and 6).

These results support the authors’ claim that probe-based information sufficiency and planner-relevant geometry can diverge, and that action-conditioned training is associated with better global rank alignment and control. They do not demonstrate causal mediation from improved Spearman scores to success, certify the analytical bi-Lipschitz or rollout-error assumptions, or show reliable discrimination among final CEM elites.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is restricted to four short-horizon simulated tasks, ViT-Tiny LeWM-family models, Euclidean goal costs, and CEM. Generalization to physical robots, partial observability, larger models, other planners, learned costs, and PSD latent metrics remains untested (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Each configuration has one training run; reported uncertainty reflects evaluation seeds, not training-initialization variation (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Plan-Real requires simulator rollouts and loses power when real costs tie. Cube is sufficiently tie-dominated that cross-variant Spearman is not reported (Section 7; Appendix G).

- **Author-stated:** Sensitivity of the inverse-loss weight \(\alpha=0.1\) was not evaluated. Goal-loss sensitivity was limited, and \(\beta=0.3\) regressed on Reacher (Section 7; Appendix C).

- **Author-stated:** Encoder distortion and terminal rollout constants are assumed, not estimated; empirical diagnostics cannot certify them out of distribution (Section 4; Appendix D).

- **Author-stated:** The all-heads reward/value proxy targets used incorrect PushT state coordinates, so that checkpoint cannot establish the effect of correctly specified reward or value supervision (Section 4; Appendix A).

- **Analyst inference:** Because training-run replication is absent, some method differences may depend on initialization despite matched randomness; the additional PushT checkpoint only partially addresses this risk.

- **Analyst inference:** Global alignment is insufficient for the optimizer’s final decision region: all variants show near-zero elite-stage correlation, limiting the diagnostics’ evidence for improved local plan selection (Table 3; Appendix E).

## Open research questions

1. **Can local alignment among near-optimal plans be improved?** Elite-stage Spearman remains near zero across variants. A next experiment could train with losses constructed from CEM elite pairs and evaluate whether elite rank correlation and success both improve.

2. **Do the results transfer beyond CEM and Euclidean costs?** The experiments fix both. Comparing random shooting, gradient-based planning, and learned or Mahalanobis latent costs under identical checkpoints would test whether alignment is planner- and metric-specific.

3. **Does DA-LeWM remain effective under realistic observation uncertainty?** The study uses short-horizon simulations and in-distribution demonstration goals. Testing partial observability, visual distractors, longer horizons, and physical-robot rollouts would probe robustness.

4. **Which auxiliary objective causes the additional gain beyond inverse-only?** Inverse-only supplies most of the measured Plan-Real improvement, but DA-LeWM has much higher PushT success. Replicated factorial experiments over inverse and goal-action losses, including \(\alpha\)/\(\beta\) sweeps, could isolate their effects.
