# Temporal Multi-Signal Fusion for Token-Level Hallucination Detection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18115) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18115)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that modeling hallucinations as temporally persistent token sequences with fused text, NLI, and proxy-LM signals raises RAGTruth token-level AUC from 0.730 for independent logistic regression to \(0.840\pm0.007\) for a BiGRU.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses token-level localization of hallucinated content in retrieval-augmented generation, including outputs from closed-source models. The authors argue that existing detectors typically score tokens independently and therefore discard the strong persistence of hallucinations across neighboring tokens: in RAGTruth, the median hallucinated span is 5 tokens and \(P(H_t\mid H_{t-1})=0.902\), versus an onset probability of 0.006 (Section 1; Appendix C). The identified gap is a black-box detector that jointly models temporal dependencies while combining complementary external signals.

## Approach and contributions

- **Temporal sequence labeling:** Each response is represented as a sequence of 33-dimensional token features and jointly labeled by a two-layer BiGRU rather than classified token by token (Sections 3.1–3.3).
- **Multi-signal fusion:** The features combine 20 surface/context-overlap features, 7 sentence-level NLI features from DeBERTa-v3-large, and 6 surprisal-related features from TinyLlama-1.1B; 12 features explicitly encode cumulative, windowed, or differential context (Section 3.2; Appendix A, Table 13).
- **Controlled attribution:** Capacity-matched, shuffled-sequence, and directional comparisons are used to separate nonlinear capacity, sequence aggregation, and genuine token-order effects (Sections 5.2–5.4).
- **Generalization and calibration studies:** The paper evaluates leave-one-generator-out and cross-dataset transfer, attention-feature augmentation, and correct forward-backward scoring of CRF models (Sections 5.7–5.10).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| BiGRU substantially outperforms independent classification | On RAGTruth with all signals and 10 seeds, BiGRU obtains \(0.840\pm0.007\) token AUC versus \(0.730\pm0.001\) for LogReg, an 11.0-point gain; paired Wilcoxon \(p=0.002\). | Sections 5.1–5.2; Tables 5–6 |
| Token order is the largest measured component of the gain | Randomly shuffling feature-label sequences reduces the temporal-order contribution by 4.9 AUC points; the authors assign token order 44% of the 11-point gap (\(p=0.001\), 10 seeds). | Section 5.3; Table 7; Figure 2 |
| Individual signals are weak relative to fused temporal modeling | Best standalone feature, NLI entailment drop, reaches 0.641 AUC; LM entropy reaches 0.551. The authors decompose the gap to BiGRU into +8.9 points from fusion to LogReg and +11.0 from temporal modeling. | Section 5.5; Table 9 |
| Hallucination labels are highly temporally redundant | On RAGTruth, persistence is 0.902 and onset is 0.006; one previous label resolves 76% of marginal label entropy, while both neighbors resolve 91%. | Sections 5.6 and 6.1; Equation 2; Appendix C |
| Performance transfers imperfectly across generators | Leave-one-source-model-out evaluation over six generators averages 0.808 AUC, a reported 3.8% relative decrease from 0.840; targets range from 0.781 for GPT-4 to 0.829 for LLaMA-2-70B. | Section 5.7; Appendix N, Figure 6 |
| Cross-dataset transfer is asymmetric | PsiloQA→RAGTruth reaches 0.744 AUC, while RAGTruth→PsiloQA reaches 0.634, each without target fine-tuning. The authors attribute the difference to PsiloQA’s denser positive annotations. | Section 5.8; Table 10; Appendix V, Figure 13 |
| Attention signals complement black-box features | On the open-LLM RAGTruth subset over 5 seeds, Qwen3-14B Lookback features score 0.838 AUC, the proposed features 0.819, and their combination 0.866. | Section 5.9; Table 11 |
| CRF evaluation requires structured marginals | BiGRU-CRF rises from 0.666 AUC with emission softmax scores to 0.845 with forward-backward marginals, a 17.9-point recovery. | Section 5.10; Table 12; Appendix T, Figure 11 |
| Token ranking does not imply precise span boundaries | The proposed model reports 0.840 token AUC but 0.394 span F1, versus 0.589 span F1 for LettuceDetect under the paper’s comparison. | Section 6.2; Limitations |

These results support the authors’ claim that cross-token modeling adds predictive value beyond identical per-token features on RAGTruth. They also demonstrate transfer across the evaluated generators and between two English datasets, but not universal robustness. The evidence does not establish real-time effectiveness, multilingual performance, calibrated deployment thresholds, causal error prevention, or superiority on span-boundary accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks

Author-stated limitations include substantial inference overhead from DeBERTa-350M and TinyLlama-1.1B, English-only evaluation, no use of potentially beneficial generator internals, binary labels without severity or type, 400-word NLI context truncation, and lower span F1 than LettuceDetect. Token-level AUC also treats correlated tokens within an example as separate observations; example-level AUC is lower at 0.795. RAGTruth’s 5.6% positive-token rate limits thresholded F1 (Limitations).

Further validity risks inferred from the supplied evidence:

- **Bidirectionality prevents causal online use:** BiGRU uses future tokens, so the main result does not directly demonstrate streaming detection; the conclusion explicitly leaves a unidirectional streaming experiment to future work.
- **Dataset-label structure may inflate temporal gains:** Persistent span annotations naturally create correlated adjacent labels. The shuffle experiment shows that order is useful under this labeling regime, but does not by itself establish how much gain transfers to different annotation practices.
- **Transfer explanation remains correlational:** The claim that annotation density drives asymmetric transfer is plausible, but the reported experiment changes dataset, prevalence, task distribution, and source models simultaneously.
- **Release status is uncertain:** Section 3.4 promises code and trained models “upon publication”; the supplied text does not confirm that they are currently available.
- **Baseline comparability is limited for span F1:** The paper notes major differences in model scale and optimization target between its detector and LettuceDetect, so that comparison does not isolate temporal modeling.

## Open research questions

1. **Can temporal fusion work during generation without future context?** The main BiGRU is bidirectional, while streaming detection is untested. A next experiment could evaluate ForwardGRU or a causal convolution at every generation step, measuring onset latency, false alarms, and intervention success.

2. **Which new features can break the shared 0.843–0.845 AUC ceiling?** Multiple recurrent, state-space, and attention architectures converge near the same ceiling, suggesting a representation bottleneck. A controlled experiment could add claim-level retrieval alignment or learned contextual features while keeping the sequence model fixed.

3. **Does annotation density causally determine transfer quality?** PsiloQA→RAGTruth transfers better despite fewer examples, but several dataset properties are confounded. One could subsample and reweight each dataset to independently match positive-token count, prevalence, task mix, and total examples.

4. **Can boundary accuracy improve without losing black-box applicability?** Span F1 remains 0.394 despite strong token AUC. A next experiment could add boundary-aware or segment-level losses and compare span F1, token AUC, and calibration using the same 33 external features.
