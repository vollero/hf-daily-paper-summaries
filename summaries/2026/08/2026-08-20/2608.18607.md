# VA-Judger: Reward Modeling from Human Preference Feedback for Joint Video-Audio Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-20.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18607) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18607)


## One-sentence takeaway
VA-Judger learns holistic text–video–audio preferences through structured human-grounded reasoning, reaching 68.43% agreement on its benchmark and providing a reward that makes an LTX-2 variant the human-preferred output in 62.30% of three-way comparisons.

## Problem and motivation
Reinforcement-learning post-training for joint video-audio generators needs a reward that reflects overall human preference. Existing rewards combine separate measures of visual quality, audio quality, text alignment, and synchronization, but may miss semantic and temporal interactions among prompt, video, and audio. The authors argue that optimizing these fragmented metrics permits reward hacking: outputs can score well while remaining incoherent or unfaithful to viewers (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

The identified gap is the absence of both holistic human-preference data and reward models specifically designed to jointly assess text, video, and audio rather than individual modalities (Introduction, p. 3).

## Approach and contributions
1. **VAPref-10K preference data.** Prompts are derived from short real-world video-audio clips and used to generate difficulty-aware pairs. Annotators select the preferred clip and the dimensions supporting that choice. The resulting dataset contains about 9K prompts and 10.3K fine-grained comparisons (Abstract; Sections 3.2–3.3).

2. **VA-Judger-Bench.** The 1,150-pair benchmark contains 400 easy, 250 in-domain, and 500 out-of-domain comparisons, with no training overlap. Its out-of-domain split uses unseen closed-source generators (Section 4.1, p. 7).

3. **VA-Judger reward model.** Initialized from Qwen3-Omni, it evaluates two clips on prompt alignment, audio-visual consistency, audio quality, video quality, and completeness/coherence before selecting a winner (Sections 3.1 and 3.3).

4. **Progressive training.** The model first learns its rubric on easy pairs using Gemini-generated explanations, then undergoes SFT on hard pairs whose generated explanations survive rejection sampling against human choices, and finally dimension-wise GRPO using both answer correctness and agreement with human-identified supporting dimensions (Sections 3.3–3.4; Figure 2).

5. **Generator post-training.** VA-Judger replaces five independent rewards in OmniNFT’s framework to post-train frozen-backbone LTX-2 through LoRA updates, routing audio, video, and shared cross-modal rewards to the corresponding branches (Section 4.1; Appendix D.2).

These results support the authors’ claims that VA-Judger predicts held-out preferences better than the evaluated baselines and that its reward can improve one generator under the tested protocol. They do not establish universal reward quality across generator architectures, prompt distributions, clip lengths, or evaluation rubrics. Automatic-metric gains are mixed, and the human study reports preference shares without uncertainty estimates or agreement statistics.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated motivation, not an explicit limitations section:** Separate expert metrics can be hacked and do not reliably represent holistic human preference (Introduction; Section 4.2).
- **Analyst inference — narrow post-training evidence:** Reward-guided generation is tested only with LTX-2 as the policy, so transfer to other generator architectures is unestablished (Sections 4.1 and 4.3).
- **Analyst inference — modest benchmark size:** VA-Judger-Bench has 1,150 pairs, including only 250 hard in-domain and 500 out-of-domain examples (Section 4.1).
- **Analyst inference — teacher-conditioned explanations:** Structured explanations originate from Gemini, with human verification checking final choices rather than the full factual correctness of every explanation. The paper therefore does not establish that its chain-of-thought rationales are faithful (Sections 3.3.1–3.3.2).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablation:** Stage-wise results isolate cumulative training gains, but the supplied text gives no controlled ablation of dimension rewards versus answer-only GRPO, alternative rubric dimensions, or rejection sampling without generated explanations (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — human-study uncertainty:** The study reports 20 eligible adults and aggregate forced-choice percentages, but not annotator assignment, votes per triplet, inter-rater agreement, confidence intervals, significance tests, or blinding (Section 4.3).
- **Analyst inference — sampling variance:** Generator evaluation uses one uniformly random 200-prompt subset from 10,140 JavisBench prompts; Table 3 shows category differences from the full pool, and repeated-sample variability is not reported.
- **Analyst inference — metric trade-off:** DeSync worsens substantially after VA-Judger post-training, complicating any blanket claim of improved synchronization (Table 2).
- **Source inconsistency:** The abstract mentions seven broad categories, whereas Section 3.2 and Figures 6–7 specify six. The contribution summary also mentions outputs from eight models, while training-pair construction explicitly identifies three generators; this appears to count out-of-domain benchmark models, but the text is not fully clear.

## Open research questions
1. **Does dimension-wise GRPO outperform answer-only GRPO?** The paper reports only the combined answer-and-dimension reward. A direct ablation with equal compute could measure preference accuracy, rationale consistency, and generator quality.

2. **How well does the reward transfer beyond LTX-2?** Only one policy architecture is post-trained. Applying identical reward protocols to several open generators would test architecture and model-family generalization.

3. **Are the generated explanations perceptually faithful?** Human filtering verifies the final winner, not necessarily every stated reason. Experts could annotate dimension-level factual accuracy and compare it with score ordering and final choices.

4. **How robust are the human preference gains?** The study omits uncertainty and agreement measures. A preregistered, blinded evaluation with more raters, repeated judgments, confidence intervals, and category-stratified analysis would test stability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported methods and numerical results because the supplied text includes the full method, tables, figures, and implementation appendices.

Material missing or unclear:

- explicit artifact-release status and licensing for data, benchmark, and model weights;
- complete annotation instructions, annotator counts for VAPref-10K, quality-control procedures, and agreement statistics;
- statistical uncertainty or significance tests for benchmark and human results;
- dimension-reward and rubric ablations;
- reconciliation of the six-versus-seven category and three-versus-eight generator descriptions;
- evaluation on generators other than LTX-2;
- human validation of the explanations themselves;
- training duration and total compute cost.
