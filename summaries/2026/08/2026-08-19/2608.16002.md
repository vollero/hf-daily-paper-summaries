# From Sequence to Structure: Relational Uncertainty Propagation for LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16002) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16002)


## One-sentence takeaway

RUPA models an LLM agent’s execution history as a relational graph and propagates uncertainty through dependent steps, improving trajectory-level failure prediction and uncertainty-guided action selection across three agent benchmarks and six open-source models.

## Problem and motivation

Existing uncertainty-quantification methods rely largely on local generation confidence or linear aggregation over prior steps. The authors argue that this misses how an early reasoning, tool-use, or feedback error can affect later dependent decisions while unrelated branches remain harmless. Their empirical motivation is that traditional confidence signals perform poorly on long-horizon tasks and that high-risk events occur throughout failed trajectories rather than only near their ends (Section 3; Table 1; Figure 2).

The identified gap is therefore structural: prior methods do not explicitly represent the heterogeneous dependencies through which execution risk accumulates across an agent trajectory (Sections 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Relational trajectory representation:** RUPA converts each execution prefix into a directed graph whose nodes are user instructions, assistant reasoning/actions, tool calls, and environment observations. Seven relations are considered: sequential, latest-instruction, repetition, progression, parallel branch, feedback, and goal alignment (Section 4.1).

- **Relation-aware propagation:** Each node receives local uncertainty—predictive entropy for assistant nodes and observable failure/conflict signals for environment nodes. Historical uncertainty is propagated over graph edges weighted by relation reliability, strength, and temporal decay (Sections 4.2 and A.1).

- **Long-range risk retention:** Graph-derived risk is combined with exponentially decayed uncertainty momentum and current-step uncertainty; step scores are then aggregated into a trajectory-level estimate (Equations 3–5).

- **Outcome-blind graph construction:** Deterministic detectors use embeddings, tool-call matching, and lexical cues without future outcomes or verifier labels. Relation calibration uses unlabeled training trajectories (Appendix A.1–A.2).

- **Empirical evaluation:** The method is evaluated on three benchmarks, six open-source LLMs, three failure-prediction metrics, prefix-based detection, uncertainty-guided action selection, ablations, and parameter sensitivity (Sections 5 and A.3).

The evidence supports better discrimination of successful versus failed trajectories, earlier detection from partial histories, and improved action selection under the tested framework. It does not establish calibrated probabilities, real-world safety, causal correctness of inferred graph relations, or superiority on models that do not expose reasoning traces and token probabilities.

The broad claim that RUPA is best on every individual benchmark/metric is not strictly supported by Table 2: for example, SAUP’s GAIA AUROC on Gemma4-26B is 0.874 versus RUPA’s 0.872, and Entropy’s GAIA F1 on GPT-OSS-120B is 0.794 versus RUPA’s 0.781. The consistent result is strongest **average** performance within each model block.

## Limitations and validity risks

No dedicated limitations section or explicit author-stated limitations are present.

- **Analyst inference — restricted observability:** All models provide complete reasoning traces and token probabilities, which may exclude closed or API-only agents lacking these signals (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference — narrow benchmark scope:** Evaluation covers three benchmarks and six open-source models. Generalization to other environments, modalities, model classes, or deployed agents is untested (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference — heuristic relation quality:** Graph edges depend on embeddings, lexical cues, fixed relation strengths, and a five-step visible window. The paper does not report relation-label accuracy against human-annotated dependencies (Appendix A.1; Tables 5–6).

- **Analyst inference — baseline reproducibility risk:** SAUP and UProp lacked official implementations and were independently reproduced by the authors, potentially affecting comparison fidelity (Appendix A.2).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete downstream protocol details:** The supplied text says that multiple actions are sampled but does not state the candidate count for Table 3 or report uncertainty estimates for its accuracy differences.

- **Analyst inference — calibration not evaluated:** AUROC, AUPRC, and best-threshold F1 measure discrimination or classification, but no calibration metric such as expected calibration error or Brier score is reported (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference — no component-level relation ablation:** The paper varies edge weights and removes graph components, but does not report leave-one-relation-type-out results establishing which of the seven relations are essential (Table 4; Figure 5).

## Open research questions

1. **How accurate are the automatically inferred dependency relations?** The paper evaluates downstream UQ but not graph-edge precision or recall. A next experiment could annotate dependencies in a trajectory subset and compare heuristic, embedding-based, and learned edge constructors.

2. **Can RUPA work without token probabilities or exposed reasoning traces?** These are required in the reported setup. A next experiment could replace predictive entropy with black-box sampling consistency and construct graphs only from actions, tool calls, and observations.

3. **Does improved discrimination correspond to calibrated failure probabilities?** The reported metrics do not assess calibration. A next experiment could measure Brier score, expected calibration error, and selective-risk curves before and after held-out calibration.

4. **Which relation types matter across task domains?** Aggregate graph ablations do not isolate sequential, repetition, feedback, progression, parallel, latest, and goal-alignment edges. A next experiment could remove each type separately and report per-benchmark effects with confidence intervals.
