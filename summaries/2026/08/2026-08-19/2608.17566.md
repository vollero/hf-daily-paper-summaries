# CoinVE-200K: A Large-Scale High-Quality Dataset for Compositional Instruction-Guided Video Editing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17566) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17566)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors introduce a 200K-pair compositional video-editing dataset, an 11-metric benchmark, and a 22B region-aware model that improves multi-instruction editing accuracy over evaluated open- and closed-source systems while remaining less competitive on some perceptual-quality metrics.

## Problem and motivation

Instruction-guided video-editing datasets largely emphasize one operation on one subject or region, whereas practical requests may combine several additions, removals, replacements, and stylizations. According to the authors, such composition requires jointly parsing multiple intents, grounding them to spatial-temporal regions, avoiding interference and unintended edits, preserving unrelated content, and maintaining temporal consistency. Existing datasets provide insufficient multi-intent supervision, target diversity, and systematic combinations of edit types (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **CoinVE-200K dataset:** 200K source–edited video pairs at 1080p and 81–201 frames, each containing 2–5 atomic edits across objects, backgrounds, and other subjects. Construction starts from 420K filtered OpenVid-HD videos and combines taxonomy-based instruction generation, key-frame segmentation, mask propagation, mask-guided synthesis, and strict automatic filtering (Sections 3.1–3.5; Figures 2–3).
- **CoinVE-Edit model:** A 22B model combining Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with a Wan2.1-T2V-14B DiT backbone. Instruction-specific mask prediction and local/global gating produce soft, query-wise Q-Blending weights that reduce interference outside target regions (Sections 4.1–4.2; Figure 4; Equations 1–5).
- **Progressive training:** Feature alignment on image edits, mixed single-instruction image/video training, offline training of the region-control modules, and final end-to-end compositional tuning on CoinVE-200K (Section 4.3; Section 6.1).
- **CoinVE-Bench:** A disjoint benchmark of 361 source videos and compositional combinations of six atomic operations, evaluated through seven checklist-based correctness/naturalness metrics and four specialized video-quality metrics (Sections 5.1–5.2; Table 1).

The results support the authors’ claim that explicit instruction-specific localization and soft attention blending improve compositional correctness within the tested benchmarks. The ablation also provides component-level evidence beyond a baseline-only comparison.

They do not establish superiority for all aspects of video quality, long videos, unseen editing taxonomies, or real-user workflows. CoinVE-Edit loses several perceptual and preservation metrics to Seedance 2.0, and the experiments do not isolate the benefit of CoinVE-200K from all differences in model architecture, training data, and scale.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** CoinVE-200K does not cover reference-based editing, fine-grained motion editing, or complex camera control. Long videos and highly interactive scenes remain challenging, while model cost and long-range temporal consistency require improvement (Section 7).
- **Author-stated scope constraint:** Training pairs contain at most 201 frames, and CoinVE-Bench clips last only 3–10 seconds (Sections 3.5 and 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The 361-video benchmark is modest relative to the breadth of claimed editing scenarios; uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variance, and human-evaluation results are not reported.
- **Analyst inference:** Much of dataset filtering and seven benchmark metrics depend on proprietary MLLM judgments. The text gives checklist dimensions but not evidence of evaluator calibration, agreement with humans, or sensitivity to evaluator choice (Sections 3.4 and 5.2; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline compositional instructions are supplied as comma-separated prompts, which may not be the optimal interface for every baseline and complicates attribution of performance differences (Section 6.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 4 tests the mask/Q-Blending design but does not directly compare training with versus without CoinVE-200K, so the dataset’s independent causal contribution is not established.
- **Analyst inference:** The reported average dataset quality score of 4.85 lacks a stated scale, evaluator, comparator values, or validation protocol in the supplied text (Section 3.5).
- **Reproducibility risk:** Training requires 32 H200 GPUs, and the text does not report training duration, inference cost, random seeds, or statistical uncertainty (Section 6.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does the method scale to long videos and dense interactions?** The paper explicitly identifies these as unresolved. A next experiment could stratify performance by duration, shot complexity, and interaction density on substantially longer clips.
2. **Does CoinVE-200K itself drive the gains independently of architecture and scale?** No matched-data ablation is reported. Train the same backbone with and without CoinVE-200K, holding update count and all other data fixed.
3. **How reliable are the automated evaluators for compositional editing?** Human agreement and evaluator sensitivity are absent. A blinded human study could measure correlation and disagreement across each of the 11 benchmark metrics.
4. **Can the taxonomy extend beyond appearance and object-level edits?** Reference guidance, motion editing, and camera control are omitted. Add controlled subsets for each capability and evaluate both isolated and cross-category compositions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, dataset composition, benchmark design, and tabulated results because the supplied text covers the full paper and includes the relevant sections and tables.

Material missing or unclear information includes human evaluation, confidence intervals or significance tests, benchmark instruction-count distribution, evaluator prompts and calibration, the split used for mask IoU and gate accuracy, the definition of the 4.85 dataset-quality score, training duration and inference efficiency, random seeds, and a controlled ablation isolating the dataset’s contribution.
