# From Corpora to Co-Evolving Capabilities: Capability-Centric Data Design for Generalist Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18076) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18076)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors organize large-scale image data around interdependent capabilities—grounding, editing, and knowledge association—and schedule them through a five-stage curriculum, but provide limited controlled evidence that this design causes the reported gains.

## Problem and motivation

Generalist image generators must acquire several dependent capabilities, yet conventional pipelines curate text-to-image (T2I), editing, and related datasets largely in isolation. The authors argue that sample utility depends not only on quality but also on when and for which capability it is used; isolated pipelines may duplicate concept coverage and inhibit cross-task transfer. The paper therefore reframes data curation as an adaptive infrastructure that coordinates heterogeneous supervision according to a capability-acquisition order (Sections 1 and 4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Three interoperable data engines.** Separate pipelines create T2I grounding data, image-editing relations, and image–entity/structured-knowledge associations while sharing filtering, metadata, and annotation conventions. The resulting corpora contain 440M T2I images, 120M editing pairs, and more than 27M image–entity pairs (Sections 3.1–3.3).

2. **Cross-task captioning framework.** VLM-based caption experts provide multi-granularity T2I descriptions and express editing instructions using compatible vocabulary, references, transformations, and preservation constraints. Specialized dense captions target layouts, OCR, and structured images (Section 3.4; Figures 4–5).

3. **Capability-aligned curriculum.** A five-stage schedule progresses from 256px T2I pre-training to joint 1024px T2I/editing fine-tuning, jointly changing task mixture, concept distribution, quality, and resolution (Section 4.1).

4. **Evaluation-driven refinement loop.** Capability-stratified failures seed targeted retrieval, expert-generated examples, and adaptive resampling for later training stages (Section 4.2; Figure 6).

5. **Models trained from scratch.** The infrastructure supports 3B- and 6B-parameter multimodal diffusion models evaluated quantitatively for editing and qualitatively across generation and editing scenarios (Abstract; Section 5).

The evidence establishes that the authors built the stated data infrastructure and that their two models achieved the reported aggregate CPI scores. It also illustrates broad qualitative behavior. It does **not** isolate the effects of capability-centric organization, caption alignment, natural-pair mining, curriculum order, or feedback-driven resampling. With no external model baselines, ablations, uncertainty estimates, or quantitative T2I results in the supplied text, superiority over alternative data strategies is not demonstrated.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied text does not contain an explicit author limitations section.

- **Analyst inference — causal attribution:** No ablation compares the proposed curriculum with a fixed mixture, reverses stage order, or removes individual engines, caption alignment, natural-pair mining, or feedback resampling. The contribution of each component is therefore unresolved.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete comparisons:** Table 1 includes only the authors’ 3B and 6B models, without external baselines. The nearly identical scores also leave the benefit of increased model scale unclear.
- **Analyst inference — narrow quantitative evaluation:** Quantitative evidence is limited to two editing subsets; CPI-Intelligent-Bench is described but not reported, and T2I evidence is qualitative.
- **Analyst inference — evaluator validity:** Editing scores come from VLM assessment, but the supplied text does not report evaluator identity, agreement with humans, variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests.
- **Analyst inference — selection risk:** Qualitative figures cannot establish typical performance because case-selection procedures and failure rates are not given.
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility:** Exact training mixtures, sampling weights, capability thresholds, compute, optimization settings, model architecture details, and corpus/model release status are not established.
- **Analyst inference — data governance:** Although provenance metadata, deduplication, safety filtering, and AIGC detection are mentioned, the text does not explain licensing, consent, privacy handling, or bias evaluation for web, celebrity, popular-IP, and commercial data.

## Open research questions

1. **Does capability ordering outperform a fixed or differently ordered mixture?** This remains open because no curriculum ablation is reported. A controlled experiment could hold data and compute fixed while comparing the proposed five stages with fixed-mixture, shuffled-stage, and editing-from-start schedules.

2. **Which data engine or captioning mechanism drives transfer?** The paper offers no component-wise measurements. A factorial ablation could remove natural editing pairs, knowledge-grounded pairs, T2I-aligned instructions, or dense captions and evaluate both originating and downstream capabilities.

3. **Does feedback-driven resampling improve persistent failures without harming resolved or long-tail capabilities?** The loop is described but not quantitatively traced. A longitudinal experiment could report capability-stratified scores and forgetting after each retrieval/resampling cycle against a no-feedback control.

4. **Do CPI scores generalize to human judgment and stronger reasoning edits?** VLM scores are the only quantitative evidence, and CPI-Intelligent results are absent. A next experiment could combine blinded human evaluation, evaluator-agreement analysis, and all three CPI-Bench subsets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the description of the proposed infrastructure and reported results, because the supplied text is extensive and includes the methods, appendix, and main evaluation table; **confidence is lower for effectiveness claims** because causal comparisons are absent.

Material missing information includes external baselines, ablations, quantitative T2I evaluation, CPI-Intelligent results, per-task CPI scores, statistical uncertainty, human-evaluation details, exact training and compute specifications, data-mixture weights, threshold values, licensing and governance analysis, and public availability of the corpora, models, or code.
