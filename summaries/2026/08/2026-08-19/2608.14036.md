# Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work-Until They Don&#x27;t

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14036) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14036)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across controlled terminal and tool-use evaluations, distilled agent skills outperform matched workflow memories mainly by stabilizing procedures rather than supplying facts, but their value degrades through retrieval confusability, misapplication, and context mismatch.

## Problem and motivation

LLM agents increasingly reuse prior execution experience, but skill evaluations typically report only aggregate task success. This leaves unclear why skills help, how they differ from direct workflow memories, whether outcome annotations and framework changes matter, and where retrieval or application fails. The authors address this gap by decomposing skill use into representation, construction, transfer, retrieval, invocation, and execution stages (Introduction; §3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled representation study:** Raw execution, Workflow Memory, and standardized `SKILL.md` artifacts are compared while holding source trajectories, target tasks, harness, and trial budgets fixed. Trajectory mixtures range from five successes/no failures to no successes/five failures (§3.3; Appendix A.4).
- **Outcome-label ablation:** Standard skill construction is compared with a no-hint variant that removes explicit success/failure annotations but retains the trajectories (§3.3; Appendix A.5).
- **Cross-framework test:** Artifacts constructed from Codex/GPT-5.3-Codex trajectories are evaluated with Gemini CLI/Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview (§3.3; Appendix A.6).
- **Retrieval decomposition:** Matched skill pools of size 5–100 are studied using independent embedding ranking, explicit agent selection, and full-pool execution, with random, similar, or dissimilar distractors (§3.3; Appendix A.7).
- **Contrastive taxonomy:** From 8,135 normalized records, the authors open-code 240 trajectories, retain 238 valid labels, and derive 12 modes in three categories. Human validation reports 95.8% exact taxonomy-mapping agreement and Cohen’s κ = 0.952 (§4; Table 3).

## Evidence and main results

The controlled Skill-versus-Workflow comparison supports the authors’ claim that representation matters when source experience is held fixed. The taxonomy and failure-frequency evidence further supports a procedural-stabilization interpretation.

It does not establish that skills universally beat raw execution: the paired Skill-versus-Raw confidence interval includes zero. Nor does stable success under low ground-truth precision prove that retrieval quality is irrelevant; benchmark annotations may not capture every procedurally useful distractor, and the three retrieval arms are independent rather than a causal pipeline (§5.5; Appendix A.7).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Evaluation is restricted to terminal- and tool-using benchmarks and excludes settings such as long-horizon web interaction and open-ended collaboration (§7).
- Only a limited set of agent–model configurations is tested, restricting generalization across scaffolds, families, and versions (§7).
- The taxonomy’s open-coding sample covers approximately 3% of normalized records, so rare modes may be missed (§7).
- The 528 paired triples remain LLM-assisted rather than exhaustively human-coded, despite human validation of taxonomy construction (Appendix A.3).
- One deterministic representative trajectory per arm and task-setting is labeled rather than aggregating all repeated trials (Appendix A.3).
- Some Skill-arm token metadata is incomplete, limiting efficiency analysis to a matched 83-task subset (Appendices A.3, A.9).

**Analyst inferences**

- The mechanism percentages depend on an LLM judge seeing truncated trajectory excerpts; human validation establishes taxonomy stability, but not exhaustive correctness of every paired assignment (Appendix A.2).
- The claimed role of outcome annotations is heterogeneous: Table 16 contains both large benefits and reversals, so a blanket causal conclusion would be stronger than the reported evidence supports.
- Retrieval “ground truth” is defined by SkillsBench skill identifiers. The paper itself observes that related non-ground-truth skills can help, creating construct-validity tension between exact-match precision and operational usefulness (§5.5; Appendix A.7).
- Cross-framework evidence covers one source-to-target direction and one transferred target pairing; broader portability is not established (§3.2; Appendix A.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Can applicability-aware routing reduce misapplication without losing procedural benefits?** The Skill arm shows a 10.0% misapplied-or-ignored mode, but no routing intervention is tested. A next experiment could compare exact-match retrieval with a router that predicts applicability conditions and permits abstention.

2. **What relevance labels better predict execution value than canonical skill identity?** Success remains stable while annotated actual-use precision collapses. Human or verifier-assisted grading of whether each accessed distractor supplied useful procedure could be compared with identifier-based precision.

3. **How robust is the taxonomy across agent domains and judges?** The taxonomy comes from terminal/tool-use tasks and full paired labeling is LLM-assisted. A preregistered study could exhaustively human-label samples from web, collaborative, and multimodal agents and measure cross-domain mode coverage.

4. **How should skills adapt when assumptions change?** The paper identifies brittle, over-specific, or incompatible guidance but does not test online adaptation. An experiment could compare fixed skills with skills revised after failed execution, measuring success, new regressions, and revision cost.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental design and numerical results because the supplied text includes detailed methods, tables, and appendices; **medium** for the mechanism-level causal interpretation because most paired trajectory assignments are LLM-generated.

Material missing or unresolved in the supplied text includes a public artifact or code location, exhaustive human annotation of paired trials, confidence intervals for most subgroup results, formal significance tests beyond the paired bootstrap comparisons, broader framework/domain coverage, and complete token metadata for all runs.
