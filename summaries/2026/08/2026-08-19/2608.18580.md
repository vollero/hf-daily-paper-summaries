# FACET: Preserving Source Intent and Executable State in Terminal Task Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-19.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18580) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18580)


## One-sentence takeaway

FACET synthesizes terminal-agent tasks by reconstructing multi-skill scenarios and grounding every task artifact in one realized container state, yielding more valid executable tasks and training data that improves Qwen3.5 models on Terminal-Bench 2.1.

## Problem and motivation

Terminal supervision couples an instruction, initialized environment, reference solution, and executable verifier; mismatched assumptions can make a task unsolvable or incorrectly evaluated. The authors identify two gaps in existing multi-stage synthesis: source details such as dependencies, intermediate states, and procedural constraints are progressively lost, while separately generated artifacts drift away from one another and from the actual runtime state (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Scenario reconstruction:** FACET collects and normalizes reusable skills, retrieves related skill combinations, and reconstructs each combination across five dimensions: goal, context, capabilities, state transitions, and inputs/outputs/tools (Sections 2.2–2.3, pp. 3–4).
- **Executable-state grounding:** It materializes and repairs the environment first, then generates the instruction, solution, and verifier sequentially against the same realized initial state; the verifier additionally observes the executed final state (Algorithm 1, p. 5; Section 2.4, pp. 5–6).
- **Execution validation and targeted repair:** Acceptance requires a buildable environment, rejection of the untouched state, successful reference execution, and acceptance of the resulting state. Failures are routed to the responsible artifact, with up to three environment repairs and five task-level repairs (Equation 2, p. 3; Section 2.4, pp. 5–6).
- **Training and evaluation assets:** The pipeline produces 6,078 validated tasks; 1,200 successful trajectories are selected for full-parameter supervised fine-tuning of Qwen3.5 models at three scales (Appendix A.2, p. 14; Tables 2 and 9, pp. 8, 21).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that FACET can produce validated terminal tasks and that its successful trajectories improve the three evaluated Qwen3.5 scales. The controlled paired result specifically supports generating the solution before the verifier relative to reverse ordering. It does not establish that every FACET component independently causes the training gains, that Forward is superior to Joint, or that lower solver pass rates alone prove greater intrinsic task difficulty.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated comparison limits:** Dataset comparisons remain descriptive because datasets differ in domains, construction methods, distributions, and verifier designs; trajectory, task, and solver statistics also use independently selected samples (Appendix A.6, pp. 17–18).
- **Unequal repair budgets:** Forward receives five repair rounds, while Reverse and Joint receive three, so final yields do not isolate generation order or repair efficiency (Section 3.5, p. 9; Appendix B, p. 19).
- **Reproduction uncertainty:** The TerminalWorld-style comparison is a best-effort adaptation, and required input, packaging, and validation adapters may differ from the original system (Appendix E, pp. 21–22).
- **Different retained subsets:** Pipeline P@1/P@3 comparisons evaluate different subsets of the common inputs and therefore are not controlled causal estimates of task difficulty (Appendix E, p. 22).
- **Correlated task properties:** Skill tags overlap, and instruction length, verifier breadth, output structure, and topic co-vary; reported difficulty patterns are descriptive rather than causal (Appendix A.3, p. 15).
- **Behavioral analysis scope:** Command statistics cover successful trajectories only; the parser cannot fully recover dynamically generated operations, and no failed-trajectory comparison establishes which behaviors cause success (Appendix A.7, p. 18).
- **Analyst inference — limited generalization:** Evaluation is confined to terminal/container tasks, one benchmark, one agent scaffold, and Qwen3.5 models for SFT, so transfer to other interactive environments, scaffolds, or model families remains untested.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete component attribution:** The end-to-end ablation changes scenario construction, artifact organization, information flow, validation, and repair together; it cannot isolate the effect of scenario reconstruction or shared-state grounding individually (Appendix E, p. 21).

## Open research questions

1. **Which FACET component drives the validity gain?** The end-to-end comparison changes several components simultaneously. A factorial ablation could independently toggle scenario reconstruction, shared-state exposure, sequential generation, and targeted repair on the same skill pairs.

2. **Does the supervision generalize beyond Qwen3.5 and Terminus-2?** The paper tests three sizes from one model family under one scaffold. A next experiment could fine-tune additional model families and evaluate them with multiple terminal-agent scaffolds under matched context and attempt budgets.

3. **Can near-successful failed trajectories improve training?** Failed rollouts often satisfy most verifier checks, but only successful trajectories enter SFT. An experiment could compare success-only SFT with filtered partial-trajectory training or verifier-weighted reinforcement learning.

4. **How should verifier density be normalized?** More collected tests may reflect broader coverage, different granularity, or redundancy, and the authors caution that checks differ in importance. A next study could annotate requirement coverage and fault independence, then compare these measures with raw pytest-item counts.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported results because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.
