# DumpsterCluster: From Dumpster Diving to Serving LLaMA-70B on $60 GPUs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14614) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14614)


## One-sentence takeaway

A 128-V100 cluster built from second-hand components can serve LLaMA-3.1 models cheaply using device-level pipeline parallelism, but its throughput, cost, and especially carbon viability depend strongly on workload and electricity supply.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether retired datacenter GPUs can provide economically viable, reliable, and environmentally responsible LLM inference. The authors identify a gap between abundant, inexpensive second-hand accelerators and serving systems designed around newer GPUs with larger memory and faster interconnects. They also argue that prior discussion insufficiently captures the joint effects of capital cost, operational energy, embodied carbon, reliability, and regional electricity conditions (§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Physical deployment:** The authors constructed a pod of 128 second-hand V100 GPUs—along with used CPUs, memory, motherboards, and networking—and operated it for one year (§2.2, Figure 3).
- **Serving system:** A custom Rust engine uses device-level, pipeline-first model parallelism, supporting up to 80 pipeline stages for LLaMA-70B (§1, §2.3).
- **Communication optimization:** CPU-managed asynchronous transfers prefetch the next pipeline input while GPUs compute, intended to hide communication latency and reduce GPU idle time (§2.3, Figure 5).
- **Empirical evaluation:** LLaMA-3.1-8B and 70B are evaluated on prefill-heavy, decode-heavy, and ShareGPT workloads against B200, H100, and A100 configurations (§3; Appendix C–D).
- **Lifecycle analysis:** The paper combines throughput, capital and operational cost, embodied and operational carbon, regional energy mixes, and observed one-year reliability (§3.2, §4, Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| High 8B aggregate throughput | On LLaMA-3.1-8B prefill-heavy inference, 128 V100s reach 223,936 TPS versus 62,651 for one B200, 29,343 for one H100, and 12,451 for one A100. | Table 2 |
| Much lower 70B throughput than modern pods | On LLaMA-3.1-70B prefill-heavy inference, 128 V100s reach 1,512 TPS versus 37,045 for eight B200s and 19,885 for eight H100s. | Table 3 |

| Some favorable total-cost settings | For 8B prefill-heavy under the China energy mix, the 128-V100 configuration reports $0.0008/MT over three years versus $0.01/MT for one H100; for 70B prefill-heavy, it reports $0.12/MT versus $0.08/MT for eight H100s. | Tables 2–3 |

| Observed reliability was mostly recoverable | During one year, 58 incidents included 8 replacement-requiring hardware failures and 50 software-recoverable incidents; the authors derive a 6.25% annual device failure rate and provision 10% spare capacity. | §4.1, Figure 7 |

The evidence establishes that deep pipelining makes 70B inference technically possible on 16GB V100s and that scale-out throughput can be attractive for particular workload and cost regimes. It does not establish universal superiority: comparisons use unequal device counts and capital outlays, throughput varies sharply by workload, and V100 operational emissions remain substantially higher in the reported scenarios. The text also does not provide a controlled ablation isolating the contribution of pipeline-first scheduling from asynchronous host transfers.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The approach primarily targets inference; limited interconnect bandwidth, reliability demands, and gradient synchronization make training less suitable (§4.4).
- Used hardware has uncertain history, lifespan, warranties, and maintenance overhead (§4.1).
- Economic conclusions depend on volatile secondary-market supply and prices; adoption itself could raise prices (§4.3).
- Environmental viability depends strongly on electricity price and carbon intensity (§4.2).
- Lifecycle analysis excludes end-of-life disposal and does not explicitly isolate upstream material sourcing; B200 embodied-carbon inputs are estimated (§E.2).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- No component-level ablation quantifies pipeline-first parallelism, asynchronous CPU communication, or hardware scaling separately.
- Baseline comparability is imperfect: B200 uses vLLM 0.17, H100/A100 use vLLM 0.6, and the prose says V100 uses the custom engine, although table footnotes ambiguously group V100 with vLLM 0.6 (§3.2; Tables 2–3).
- “Production-quality” evidence is limited to throughput and incident counts; latency distributions, service-level objectives, output correctness, utilization, and downtime are not reported.
- Reliability evidence covers one 128-GPU V100 pod for one year, limiting generalization to other hardware batches and longer deployments.
- Carbon results depend on modeled PUE, hardware lifetime, regional averages, and alternative embodied-carbon allocation rules rather than a full measured lifecycle (§3.2, Appendix E).
- Cost accounting is potentially inconsistent: GPU-only capital figures appear in Tables 2–3, while §2.2 and Appendix B also report a comprehensive pod cost including peripherals.

## Open research questions

1. **Which optimization produces the throughput gains?** The paper provides no controlled ablation. A next experiment could compare naive pipelining, device-level pipeline-first scheduling, CPU-managed transfers, and their combination on identical hardware and workloads.

2. **Can the system satisfy interactive latency objectives?** Only aggregate TPS is reported. Measure time to first token, inter-token latency, tail latency, and request-level failure rates under controlled concurrency.

3. **How durable is second-hand hardware at larger scale?** One year and one V100 fleet may not capture batch or age variation. A multi-year, multi-pod study could stratify failures by prior use, binning outcome, component type, and workload intensity.

4. **Where is the cost-and-carbon break-even frontier?** Results vary with workload, electricity, lifetime, and market price. A sensitivity analysis could sweep these variables and compare equal-throughput or equal-latency deployments.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains detailed tables, methods, workload definitions, and reliability observations, but important information remains unavailable or ambiguous: code and artifact availability; complete latency and power measurements; statistical variability or repeated-run uncertainty; optimization ablations; exact service utilization during the one-year deployment; downtime and repair effort; consistent accounting of full-system versus GPU-only capital cost; and clarification of the V100 software-version discrepancy.
