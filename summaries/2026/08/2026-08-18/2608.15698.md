# ConceptFormer: Learning Adaptive Latent Concepts for Query-Document Alignment in Visual Document Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15698) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15698)


## One-sentence takeaway
ConceptFormer improves visual page retrieval by training a retriever with query-conditioned latent concepts whose length adapts to VLM-proposed evidence coverage, reaching 75.97 average NDCG@10 across six benchmarks without requiring concept generation at inference.

## Problem and motivation
Visual document retrieval must match natural-language queries to pages containing relevant text, layouts, charts, tables, or other visual structures. Standard page-level contrastive learning supplies only coarse relevance labels and does not identify which local evidence supports a match. Existing fine-grained approaches use either textual descriptions, which may omit spatial or visual structure, or localized image regions, which may lack global semantics. The authors seek an intermediate representation that combines local grounding, page context, and query relevance while adapting to the amount of evidence needed (Sections 1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Adaptive concept capacity:** A training-time VLM proposes query-relevant bounding boxes on each positive page. Their coverage on the retriever’s visual-token grid determines the number of latent concept tokens (Equations 6–11 and 19–20).
- **Query-conditioned latent concepts:** The retriever generates continuous concept states conditioned on the query, positive page, and concept-token prefix; mean pooling produces a retrieval-oriented intermediate representation (Equations 12–13).
- **Ranking-distribution supervision:** A forward KL loss makes the concept-induced document-ranking distribution reproduce the query-induced distribution, jointly optimized with the standard contrastive retrieval loss (Equations 14–17).
- **Inference-neutral design:** Evidence proposal and latent-concept generation are used only during training; inference retains the original dense visual retrieval pipeline (Section 3.2; Appendix A.4).
- **Empirical analysis:** The paper compares latent concepts with textual and visual proxies, tests loss and capacity choices, and analyzes concept geometry and allocation behavior (Sections 5.2–5.5).

|---|---|---|
| Best overall reported performance | Qwen ConceptFormer obtains average R@10 **88.33** and NDCG@10 **75.97** across six datasets. The strongest listed visual baseline by average NDCG@10 is DSE at **65.05**, and the strongest OCR baseline is NV-Embed-v2 at **62.24**. | Table 1 |
| Relative average NDCG@10 gains | Authors report **16.7%** over the strongest visual retriever and **22.1%** over the strongest OCR-based retriever; these correspond approximately to 75.97 versus 65.05 and 62.24. | Section 5.1; Table 1 |
| Particularly large map gain | On Wikimedia Maps, Qwen ConceptFormer records **61.69 NDCG@10**, versus **29.04** for the strongest listed prior visual baseline on that dataset, VisRAG-Ret. | Table 1; Section 5.1 |
| Benefits are present with two backbones | Phi3V ConceptFormer reaches **67.00** average NDCG@10, while the Qwen variant reaches **75.97**. | Table 1 |
| Latent concepts outperform evidence proxies | Average NDCG@10 is **75.97** for ConceptFormer, **74.85** for the textual proxy, and **72.81** for the visual proxy. | Table 2; Section 5.2 |
| KL ranking supervision contributes | Removing KL loss lowers average NDCG@10 from **75.97** to **74.22**; marked significance is reported on selected datasets and for the average. | Table 2 |

| Alignment weight is sensitive | On Qwen2.5-VL, \(\lambda=0.2\) gives the best average NDCG@10, **75.97**; tested alternatives range from **73.18** to **74.37**, while \(\lambda=0\) gives **74.22**. | Table 3; Section 5.2 |

| Training and evaluation scale | Training uses approximately **38,000** pairs from six datasets. Evaluation spans six benchmarks, from **131** queries for OWID Charts to **1,048** for InfoVQA. | Section 4; Tables 4–5 |

The results establish improved top-10 retrieval ranking under the paper’s six-benchmark protocol, with ablations supporting contributions from KL supervision and adaptive capacity. They do not establish better downstream question answering or RAG generation, robustness beyond the evaluated document formats, or independence from the quality of the training-time evidence proposer. Figure 2 supports the authors’ qualitative geometry claim, but the supplied plot does not expose exact numerical values.

## Limitations and validity risks
No dedicated author-stated limitations section is present.

- **Analyst inference — dependence on generated supervision:** Capacity allocation relies on Qwen3.6-Plus-generated evidence boxes. The paper supplies the prompt and decoding settings but does not report proposal accuracy, annotation validation, or sensitivity to proposer errors (Appendix A.4; Figure 5).
- **Analyst inference — backbone attribution:** The strongest ConceptFormer model uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B, whereas many baselines use different architectures and sizes. The Phi3V result helps separate method effects from backbone scaling, but no same-backbone vanilla Qwen result is shown explicitly beyond the \(\lambda=0\) ablation.
- **Analyst inference — limited task endpoint:** Evaluation measures Recall@10 and NDCG@10 only; no downstream multimodal RAG or answer-quality experiment is reported (Section 4; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — uneven benchmark sizes:** Some test sets are small—131 queries for OWID Charts and 150 for ChartQA—which may make dataset-specific conclusions less stable, although permutation significance testing at \(P<0.05\) is reported (Tables 1 and 5).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete efficiency evidence:** Training requires four 40GB A100 GPUs and an external training-time proposer, but proposal cost, training time, indexing time, latency, and memory comparisons are not reported (Section 4; Table 7).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Section 4 names the loss as \(L_{\text{repr}}\), while the method and tables call it \(L_{\text{align}}\). This appears to refer to the same weighted KL objective, but the notation is inconsistent.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is ConceptFormer to inaccurate or incomplete evidence boxes?** Proposal quality is not evaluated. A next experiment could corrupt, omit, or expand boxes systematically and measure retrieval degradation.
2. **Can adaptive capacity be learned without a proprietary or external evidence proposer?** The current method uses Qwen3.6-Plus during data construction. A plausible experiment would compare self-predicted capacity, heuristic patch allocation, and human-annotated boxes at matched training budgets.
3. **Do retrieval gains improve downstream visual RAG answers?** Only retrieval metrics are reported. An end-to-end experiment could hold the generator fixed and compare answer accuracy and evidence attribution across retrievers.
4. **What drives the gains: adaptive length, grounded conditioning, or additional computation?** Fixed-length tests address token count partially, but no matched-compute adaptive-control experiment is given. Such a study could equalize total concept tokens across training runs while varying per-example allocation.

## Confidence and missing information
