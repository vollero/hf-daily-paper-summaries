# TRACE-Bench: Decomposing and Diagnosing Multi-Reference Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16765) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16765)


## One-sentence takeaway

TRACE-Bench represents multi-reference image generation through four composable operators and shows that current models struggle more with extracting and correctly binding referenced attributes than with arranging scenes.

## Problem and motivation

Existing multi-reference image-generation benchmarks group examples into predefined tasks and score outputs holistically. The authors argue that this produces incomplete coverage of combinatorial requests, prevents capability-level failure diagnosis, and leaves structural complexity uncontrolled (Section 1). Their goal is an evaluation framework that can express varied reference-conditioned requests, control their complexity, and identify whether errors arise from locating entities, extracting attributes, binding them, or composing the scene.

## Approach and contributions

- **Capability decomposition:** Requests are represented using **Anchor** \(f\), **Disentangle** \(g\), **Apply** \(\oplus\), and **Compose** \(C\), organized from entity expressions to scene expressions and complete prompt formulas (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Controlled benchmark construction:** TRACE-Bench contains approximately 1,600 cases, 631 formula templates, and around 4,000 reference images, covering formula slot counts 1–8. Slot count is defined as \(|f|+|g|\) (Section 3.4, Equation 2).
- **Operator-aligned evaluation:** Binary checklist questions separately assess entity existence/consistency, attribute existence/consistency, binding/integration, and compositional coherence. Scores come from a VLM judge (Section 3.5; Appendix B.5).
- **Diagnostic trees:** Complex formulas are recursively simplified by stripping global references, flattening compositions, simplifying relations, and removing attributes, enabling failures to be localized through behavior changes across nodes (Section 3.5; Appendix C.2).
- **Comparative diagnosis:** Nine proprietary and open-source generators are evaluated, revealing capability-specific differences obscured by aggregate or CLIP-based scoring (Sections 4.1–4.2).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that operator-aligned evaluation exposes distinct reference-grounding and binding failures that holistic scoring can conceal. It does not establish that the four operators exhaust every real-world capability, that slot count fully measures difficulty, or that the rankings generalize beyond the benchmark, selected models, and judging protocol.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope boundary:** Formulas encode only reference-conditioned content and assume the backbone already handles ordinary text instructions reasonably well; failures in pure textual control are therefore outside the representation (Appendix B.3).
- **Author-stated structural simplification:** Compose is limited to at most two nested levels. The authors say this covers most benchmark cases, but provide no quantitative coverage estimate (Appendix B.3).
- **Author-stated complexity caveat:** Slot count measures formula structure but does not determine empirical difficulty; clutter, entity composition, and attribute granularity also matter (Section 3.4; Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference — judge dependence:** Human agreement of 85.4–88.4% shows meaningful residual disagreement. Full-benchmark scores use only Gemini-2.5-Pro rather than the better-performing ensemble, so some model comparisons may reflect judge error (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference — limited diagnostic validation:** Aggregate diagnostic conclusions are based on 200 Emu3.5 cases; their generality across other generators is not established (Sections 4.4; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference — synthetic and model-mediated construction:** Gemini-2.5-Pro performs tagging and prompt realization, Nano Banana Pro supplies about 200 synthetic references, and GPT-5.4 filters prompts. Potential generator or judge biases from this model-dependent pipeline are not ablated (Section 3.4).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete quantitative transparency in supplied text:** Several referenced tables and figures lack their underlying values, including full per-model Table 2 results, most Table 3 percentages, attribute-subtype scores, and slot-level curves.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the four operators cover difficult requests outside the curated formula space?** Coverage is asserted, but formulas omit text-only operations and restrict composition depth. A next experiment could have independent annotators encode unconstrained real-user prompts and report coverage and disagreement.
2. **How robust are rankings to evaluation design?** Full results use one VLM judge despite imperfect human agreement. Re-scoring all systems with humans, multiple judges, and an ensemble would quantify ranking stability.
3. **Can operator-targeted training improve weak capabilities without harming stronger ones?** The paper diagnoses Disentangle and Apply but does not train a model from that feedback. A controlled study could fine-tune on failed \(g\) or \(\oplus\) cases and re-evaluate every operator.
4. **Do diagnostic patterns transfer across model families?** Aggregate trees are evaluated only for Emu3.5. Applying identical trees to all nine models could test whether composition and global-reference interference are model-general or architecture-specific.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and explicitly reported findings; **medium** for comparative interpretation because several tables are not rendered numerically in the supplied text.
