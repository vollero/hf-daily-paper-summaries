# PACE-Bench: Benchmarking Physics Adaptation via Code Evolution in Dynamic Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14441) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14441)


## One-sentence takeaway

PACE-Bench shows that current LLM agents struggle to repair executable physical designs after environment changes, with simulator-grounded reflection helping most but even the strongest reported configuration leaving substantial room for improvement.

## Problem and motivation

Existing self-evolution benchmarks primarily test improvement across task streams, transfer to related tasks, or generalization to held-out settings. The authors identify a missing deployment-relevant capability: adapting a previously successful design when changed execution physics makes it fail while the goal and interface remain fixed (Introduction; Figure 1; Table 1).

This setting requires agents both to infer what changed and to redesign the underlying mechanism. Controlled simulation makes those changes reproducible, while executable code allows proposed adaptations to be tested directly (§§3.1–3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** PACE-Bench contains 36 base tasks across six physics domains. Four mutated targets per task produce 144 source-to-target pairs and 180 total environments. Every pair has a verified target solution, while its source solution fails in the target (§§3.2–3.4; Figures 2–3).

- **Adaptation protocol:** Agents revise Python structure and control code using structured Box2D diagnostics, without initially knowing the changed physics, for at most 20 attempts. Evaluation uses Pass@2 and Score@2 over two independent runs (§3.2; §4.1.2).

- **Comparative evaluation:** The study tests ten self-evolving methods from context-based, memory-augmented, inference-time-search, and parameter-based paradigms across multiple model sizes (§4.1.1; Appendix C).

- **Diagnostic analysis:** Code similarity and a nine-category failure taxonomy distinguish fixation, stagnation, undirected exploration, late convergence, and physical or numerical failures (§4.2.2; Appendix B).

- **Interventions:** Change-exposed experiments separate parameter inference from mechanism redesign, while VLM-generated video descriptions test whether visual feedback improves adaptation (§4.2.3).

These results establish difficulty within the paper’s controlled simulator, models, method adaptations, and attempt budgets. They support the authors’ claim that verified reflection is comparatively effective and that fixation and non-convergent exploration are prominent failure patterns. They do not establish superiority in 3D or real-world robotics, nor that parameter inference is universally easier than redesign: the disclosure comparison covers only selected domains and methods. Some Table 2 denominators are below the nominal 144 pairs because incomplete executable runs were excluded (Appendix D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Evaluation is confined to controlled 2D Box2D simulation, so transfer to 3D environments or robots with noisy sensing and actuation is unknown.
- The fixed 20-attempt budget leaves longer-term adaptation untested.
- Frontier-model, parameter-disclosure, and visual-feedback studies cover only selected domains and methods because of computational cost (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences**

- **Benchmark-selection bias:** Difficulty was escalated until Qwen3-4B failed two independent runs. This intentionally creates challenging tasks but makes results less representative of naturally occurring environment shifts (Figure 2; §3.3; Appendix A.2).
- **Construction dependence:** Claude Code assisted reference-solution derivation, feedback design, and difficulty tuning. Hard-coded evaluation and human audits reduce scoring bias, but the benchmark may still reflect the construction system’s preferred design patterns (Appendix A.1–A.4).
- **Unequal valid denominators:** Excluding truncations, parse failures, crashes, and context overflows yields an average Table 2 denominator of 139.20 rather than 144. Exclusion can favor configurations prone to invalid outputs, although the authors report unchanged method ordering (Appendix D.3).
- **Limited repetitions:** Two stochastic runs provide reasonable reported agreement but leave uncertainty, particularly at smaller model scales where run correlation is lower (Table 10).
- **Intervention generality:** Claims about exact parameter disclosure and video feedback should remain scoped to the tested subsets, backbones, and methods (§4.2.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can agents balance exploration with convergence more effectively?** Fixation dominates several memory methods, while ToT and test-time RL often stagnate or explore without progress. A next experiment could add novelty-aware candidate selection plus explicit convergence checks and compare it against Reflexion and ToT under equal simulator calls.

2. **Does adaptation improve with longer horizons?** Performance rises with additional attempts, but the paper stops at 20. Evaluating 40-, 80-, and 160-attempt budgets with matched compute reporting could determine whether failures reflect insufficient time or fundamental search limitations.

3. **Do the findings transfer beyond controlled 2D simulation?** The paper provides no evidence for 3D or noisy real-world settings. A plausible experiment would recreate a small task subset in a 3D simulator and on hardware while progressively adding sensing and actuation noise.

4. **Which feedback representation best supports each agent paradigm?** Video descriptions help memory-based methods but often harm context-based methods. A controlled ablation could compare raw diagnostics, concise causal summaries, images, video descriptions, and combinations while holding token and compute budgets fixed.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes the complete protocol, principal tables, appendices, and stated limitations.

Material uncertainties remain around per-cell valid denominators beyond the single example, confidence intervals for most headline comparisons, complete five-attempt frontier-model details beyond the reported subset, and whether released code independently reproduces the results; the supplied paper states that code is available but does not provide an external reproduction.
