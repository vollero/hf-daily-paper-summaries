# VideoGAIA: A Benchmark for General AI Assistants on Agentic Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14718) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14718)


## One-sentence takeaway

VideoGAIA evaluates whether multimodal agents can combine targeted video inspection with web retrieval and multi-step evidence synthesis, and its best evaluated model reaches only 58.30% pass@1 under a unified tool-using setup.

## Problem and motivation

Conventional video benchmarks primarily use single-turn, closed-world question answering and are reportedly nearing saturation, with leading models approaching 90% accuracy. The authors argue that this protocol does not adequately test capabilities needed by general-purpose assistants: adaptive evidence acquisition, tool use, long-horizon reasoning, context management, and integration of video evidence with external information (Introduction; Figure 1).

VideoGAIA addresses this gap by treating video as one source of evidence in an open-world task, rather than merely the object of a question.

## Approach and contributions

- **New evaluation paradigm:** Multi-turn, agent-centric video understanding in which models inspect videos, search the web, visit pages, and synthesize cross-modal evidence (Sections 1–2.2).
- **Benchmark construction:** A human-model pipeline filters 100,000 public seed videos into 271 open-ended tasks across six domains and two difficulty levels. Tasks must use multiple video anchors, multiple frames, external evidence, and multi-hop reasoning (Sections 3.1–3.3).
- **Quality controls:** Candidates undergo rubric scoring, unimodal and anchor-ablation filtering, tool-necessity tests, safety screening, and independent review by three annotators; majority-approved cases receive further adjudication (Section 3.2).
- **Unified evaluation:** Twenty frontier MLLMs are compared using the same ReAct-style harness, three tools, prompts, frame resolution, and 40-step limit (Section 4.1).
- **Diagnostic analyses:** The paper examines difficulty, tool-use patterns, dominant error causes, trajectory efficiency, and qualitative cases (Section 4.3; Appendix C).

These results establish that the tested models struggle under this particular benchmark and common agent harness. They also provide descriptive evidence that visual grounding and retrieval are frequent failure points. They do **not** establish that every stronger or differently orchestrated agent would remain below 60%, that the error categories are causal, or that VideoGAIA accuracy directly measures real-world productivity.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Multilingual scenarios and audio-dependent tasks are not comprehensively covered (Section D).
- Evaluation uses a simple ReAct-style harness and does not test more sophisticated agent systems with stronger context management or orchestration (Section D).
- Economics contains only six tasks, making its category percentages unstable (Section 4.2).
- The video inspection tool is visual-only, so the benchmark setting cannot evaluate audio reasoning (Section 4.1).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The 271-task test set is modest relative to its six domains and 39 subtasks, potentially limiting fine-grained statistical comparisons; the text supplies no uncertainty intervals or significance tests for model-ranking differences.
- GPT-5.5 participates in task generation, tool-necessity filtering, and primary answer judging while also being evaluated. The paper describes these roles but does not report an ablation using fully independent generators or judges, leaving possible model-family or judge effects unresolved.
- Accuracy is averaged over three runs, but the supplied text does not report run-level variance.
- Error analysis assigns one dominant cause per failed trajectory; overlaps among perception, retrieval, reasoning, and execution failures may therefore be hidden.
- The efficiency estimate excludes several cost components, so it is not a full end-to-end deployment-cost comparison (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does the agent harness constrain performance?** The authors test only one simple ReAct-style loop. A controlled experiment could hold models and tools fixed while comparing ReAct with planning, memory, and verification-oriented harnesses.

2. **Would audio and multilingual evidence change model rankings?** These modalities are explicitly underrepresented. A follow-up benchmark split could add speech-dependent and multilingual tasks and report performance by modality and language.

3. **How robust are scores to the answer judge?** Primary grading relies on GPT-5.5, with DeepSeek-V4-Pro used only as a fallback. Human regrading of a stratified prediction sample, plus agreement estimates across multiple independent judges, could quantify judge sensitivity.

4. **Which intervention most reduces end-to-end failures?** The audit identifies perception, retrieval, reasoning, and execution errors but does not experimentally isolate remedies. Modular interventions—better frame selection, retrieval reranking, evidence verification, or constrained action formatting—could be tested individually and jointly.
