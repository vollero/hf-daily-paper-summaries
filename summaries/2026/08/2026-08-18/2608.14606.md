# Plausible but Not Valid: A Psychometric Audit of LLMs as Synthetic Survey Respondents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14606) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14606)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 37 models, persona-conditioned LLMs often recover qualitative survey relationships but fail to reproduce human joint psychometric structure or downstream validity, making them unsuitable as drop-in replacements for human respondents.

## Problem and motivation

Synthetic survey respondents are usually evaluated through plausible individual answers, item means, or a few demographic effects. The authors argue that these checks miss whether responses preserve the joint distribution, reliability, latent factor structure, mediation pathways, and group effects required for psychometric validity. This matters because synthetic data with realistic marginals but distorted covariance can inflate reliability, create false associations, and invalidate downstream models (Sections 1 and 4.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Open human reference:** A Lithuanian organisational-psychology survey of 263 employees, comprising 68 items and 12 subscales across three validated instruments plus an exploratory scale (Section 3).
- **Large model audit:** Thirty-seven LLMs are evaluated under persona-disclosure, presentation-mode, reasoning-effort, language, demographic-swap, and memorization conditions, yielding roughly 65,000 validated questionnaires (Figure 1; Sections 4.1–4.2).
- **Anchored psychometric evaluation:** The Psychometric Similarity Score evaluates distribution, correlation, reliability, mediation, demographic effects, and—separately from the five-component aggregate—construct fidelity, with five statistical baselines and a held-out human ceiling (Section 4.3).
- **Validity diagnostics:** Counterfactual demographic swaps, permutation-corrected Tucker’s \(\phi\), response-style measures, human-held-out prediction, placebo mediation, and inter-LLM agreement expose failures hidden by plausible responses (Sections 5.3–5.7).
- **Extensible assets:** The authors state that the dataset, generation logs, configurations, baselines, and idempotent evaluation pipeline will be released upon publication (Conclusion; Appendices A–B).

The evidence establishes substantial psychometric mismatch under this Lithuanian organisational-survey protocol, including failures with practical downstream consequences. It does not establish that all LLM survey simulation fails across languages, populations, survey domains, prompts, or open-text responses. Some author language—such as calling education amplification “universal”—should be read within the tested lineup and protocol.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The reference is one convenience sample of 263 Lithuanian employees collected during the first 2020 COVID-19 lockdown; generalization to other countries, constructs, sectors, and periods is untested (Sections 3 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** Human outcomes are self-reported, so human and model social-desirability effects cannot be cleanly separated (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Verbatim memorization is unlikely, but paraphrased exposure to public instruments remains possible (Sections 5.6 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** Headline LLM cells use \(n=100\), one repeat, minimal reasoning, and structured single-call presentation. Top-model differences are comparable to the reported CI width, and presentation materially changes rankings (Sections 4.2, 5.2, and 7).
- **Author-stated:** No human same-respondent Lithuanian–English invariance study was available; the language ablation is LLM-only (Sections 2 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** Provider model versions may drift, and some models have reduced or missing cells because of rate limits or retired deployments (Section 7; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** The five-component PSS depends on chosen transformations and weights; Appendix L shows broad conclusions are robust, but individual ranks and aggregate interpretations remain design-dependent.
- **Analyst inference:** Several headline claims pool heterogeneous models or placebo paths, so they do not imply that every model exhibits every failure.
- **Reproducibility risk:** Code, data, and logs are promised only upon publication; the supplied text therefore does not establish their present availability.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the failures generalize beyond Lithuanian organisational psychology?** External validity is untested. Replicate the complete audit on a non-Indo-European language and a clinical or educational instrument battery with matched human ceilings.
2. **How much apparent fidelity comes from paraphrased instrument exposure?** The recall probe addresses verbatim wording only. Construct a novel preregistered instrument, collect human responses, and compare LLM performance before public release.
3. **Can training interventions repair covariance structure rather than surface style?** Prompt debiasing improved response style but not correlation fidelity (Table 20). Fine-tune or distribution-match a model on a disjoint human survey corpus, then evaluate PSS components and held-out predictive \(R^2\).
4. **How stable are synthetic respondents across time and interacting demographics?** The study tests repeat sampling and one-field swaps, not longitudinal drift or interactions. Re-administer fixed personas across model versions and run factorial two-field swaps with interaction estimates.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experiments because the supplied text includes detailed methods, tables, ablations, and appendices.

Material missing or unresolved information includes peer-review status; currently accessible code and data; independent replication; full raw per-cell outputs and confidence intervals for all reported aggregates; human cross-language measurement invariance; exact effects of model-version drift; and evidence outside this single survey population and domain.
