# Improving the matrix multiplication exponent with modern optimization and AlphaEvolve

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16884) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16884)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors reformulate and scale the non-convex optimization underlying combination loss analysis, then refine it with AlphaEvolve and rigorously certify a new upper bound of \(\omega<2.371177\).

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses the unresolved asymptotic complexity of multiplying two \(n\times n\) matrices, measured by the smallest exponent \(\omega\) permitting \(O(n^{\omega+o(1)})\) arithmetic operations. Current leading bounds rely on combination loss analysis, a refinement of the laser method whose computer-assisted proof requires solving a large non-convex optimization problem.

The authors identify scalability as the central gap: the previous best result, \(\omega<2.371339\), used maximum recursion level \(\ell^*=3\), while increasing to \(\ell^*=4\) raises the parameter count from roughly 25,000 to nearly 7 million and defeats the earlier graph-based SQP formulation (Introduction; Sections 3 and 3.2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Scalable reformulation.** The irregular optimization tree is represented as multidimensional tensors using masked phantom nodes and specialized node groups called stages. This makes the computation hardware-parallelizable, at the cost of up to three times as many optimization parameters (Section 3.2).

2. **Differentiable optimization.** Probability distributions are parameterized through softmax logits. Maximum-entropy distributions are computed using Sinkhorn–Knopp rather than treated as independent variables, with automatic and implicit differentiation followed by Adam updates (Section 3.1).

3. **Larger search setting.** The JAX implementation handles \(q=5,\ell^*=4\) and nearly 7 million parameters, compared with \(\ell^*=3\) and approximately 25,000 parameters for the previous bound (Introduction; Sections 3 and 3.2).

4. **AlphaEvolve refinement.** AlphaEvolve modifies the optimization program, with each candidate run taking approximately five hours on one GPU. Its “evolving constructions” mechanism warm-starts each generation from its parent’s best solution (Section 3.3).

5. **Rigorous certification.** The optimized floating-point solution is rounded to rational values and independently checked using exact rational arithmetic and directionally safe rational bounds on logarithms (Section 4).

The evidence establishes a computer-assisted asymptotic upper bound within the supplied combination-loss framework, with an explicit procedure intended to eliminate floating-point uncertainty. It does not demonstrate faster practical matrix-multiplication software, empirical speedups on finite matrices, or an improvement outside the laser-method framework. The supplied text also omits the displayed content of Equation 11 and the numerical certificate itself, so the certification cannot be independently reconstructed from this text alone.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The authors expect this optimization route may yield further modest gains, but argue that substantially larger improvements probably require new mathematical ideas (Section 5).

- **Resource intensity:** A single AlphaEvolve candidate takes approximately five hours on one GPU, while the complete number of candidates, generations, and total compute expenditure is not reported (Section 3.3).

- **Reproducibility status:** Verification code and the discovered solution are only being prepared for release; they are not included in the supplied text (Section 4).

- **Analyst inference — limited optimizer attribution:** The paper reports gains before and after AlphaEvolve, but supplies no ablation table isolating tensorization, Sinkhorn differentiation, Adam, initialization, warm-starting, or individual evolved code changes. Their separate contributions therefore remain unclear.

- **Analyst inference — narrow evaluated setting:** The reported numerical search fixes \(q=5\) and \(\ell^*=4\); results for other values, random seeds, convergence behavior, or alternative optimizers are not supplied (Section 3).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete audit trail in retrieved text:** Equation 11, Table 1’s entries, optimizer hyperparameters, and the final certificate values are absent or not rendered, limiting independent verification from the provided material.

## Open research questions

1. **Which optimizer components produce the improvement?** This remains open because no component-level ablation is reported. A next experiment could rerun matched-budget searches while individually removing implicit Sinkhorn differentiation, tensor staging, Adam, and AlphaEvolve warm-starting.

2. **How robust is the result across optimization runs?** The text does not report seeds, variance, failure rates, or convergence traces. Multiple independent runs with fixed compute budgets could measure the distribution of certified \(\omega\) bounds.

3. **Can larger recursion levels be made computationally tractable?** The parameter count already rises from about 25,000 at \(\ell^*=3\) to nearly 7 million at \(\ell^*=4\). A plausible experiment would test sparse, symmetry-aware, or hierarchical parameterizations at \(\ell^*>4\), followed by the same exact certification procedure.

4. **Can the optimization framework yield materially larger theoretical gains?** The authors anticipate only modest additional improvements without new mathematics. A next study could combine the optimizer with a genuinely different tensor decomposition or loss analysis and compare certified bounds under matched settings.
