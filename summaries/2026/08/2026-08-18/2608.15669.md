# Large Discovery Models: Empirically-grounded Model-Based Open-Ended Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15669) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15669)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors propose LDM, a recurrent search framework that combines an LLM candidate generator with a continually updated Gaussian-process surrogate and uncertainty-aware acquisition function, reporting gains over LLM-only and Bayesian-optimisation baselines across program, antibody, and molecular design.

## Problem and motivation

Scientific design often requires optimizing unknown objectives over vast structured spaces while evaluations are expensive, noisy, and scarce. The authors argue that LLMs can generate plausible structured candidates but cannot reliably estimate external rewards or calibrated epistemic uncertainty; Bayesian optimisation (BO) supplies uncertainty-aware selection but depends on a fixed representation and reachable candidate set (§1, pp. 2–5).

The identified gap is a search system that both expands its effective hypothesis space and allocates limited evaluations using empirical, uncertainty-aware evidence. The paper formalizes this as sequential inverse design and distinguishes exploration within the represented space from “discovery,” which changes what the search can express or reach (§§1–2, Figures 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Recurrent LDM architecture.** An LLM generates or parameterizes candidates; a Gaussian-process surrogate predicts reward and epistemic uncertainty; an acquisition function directs refinement, computation, and external evaluation. New observations update both the surrogate data and generation context (§3; Figure 5; Algorithms 1–2).

2. **Acquisition-tilted search.** LDM optimizes expected acquisition value subject to KL regularization toward the generative prior. The resulting policy reweights the prior by \(\exp(\eta a_t(x))\), approximated through finite candidate pools and deterministic top-\(b\) or stochastic Gumbel-top-\(b\) selection (§§3.1–3.3, Equations 2–10).

3. **Dynamic search support.** The LLM can directly generate complete designs or indirectly define a parameterized search region; reflection can revise this support after stagnation (§§4.1–4.2).

4. **Regret decomposition.** Under stated GP-calibration and local-regularity assumptions, the analysis separates error from incomplete reservoir coverage, GP-UCB optimisation, and sampling from an acquisition-tilted proposal instead of exactly maximizing acquisition (§5, Theorem 1).

5. **Policy distillation.** High-budget acquisition-guided search traces are converted into weighted actions, optionally with rationales, and used to fine-tune a smaller proposer (§3.4, Figure 26).

These experiments support the authors’ claim that coupling structured generation with an empirical acquisition signal can improve budgeted search across heterogeneous digital benchmarks. They do not establish wet-lab effectiveness, universal superiority over domain-specialized optimizers, or that the proposed “discovery” events constitute novel scientific discoveries in the broader community sense. Several comparisons are trajectory- or benchmark-specific, and overlapping error bands weaken some fine-tuning conclusions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** All three evaluations use in-silico or digital oracles; wet-lab validation remains future work (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Exact Gaussian processes scale cubically with observation count and may suffer from surrogate misspecification or reward exploitation (§6.5).
- **Author-stated:** Memory retrieval and federated discovery are outside the current single-process formulation (§2).
- **Author-stated:** Multi-step prefix search, beam search, and Monte Carlo tree search are proposed but not fully evaluated (§3.3).
- **Author-stated:** LDM is not claimed to dominate the specialized AntBO method (§6.3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence for search-frontier “discovery” partly relies on interpreted plateau-and-revision trajectories; the supplied text does not present an independent measure of novelty or search-support expansion.
- **Analyst inference:** Some numerical claims use few seeds, and several intervals overlap—for example, the molecular fine-tuning comparisons in Figure 17.
- **Analyst inference:** Baselines and candidate-pool construction vary by domain, limiting direct cross-domain conclusions about which component produces the gains.
- **Analyst inference:** The theoretical guarantee depends on calibration, regularity, and reservoir-mass conditions whose empirical validity is not demonstrated in the supplied sections.
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility cannot be fully assessed because the source is truncated and omits part of the paper, including material from the middle of the appendices.

## Open research questions

1. **Does LDM retain its advantage with noisy wet-lab feedback?** All reported evaluations use digital oracles. A next experiment could run a preregistered small-batch antibody or chemistry campaign and compare LDM with matched BO and expert-designed baselines.

2. **How should the surrogate scale beyond exact GPs?** Cubic GP cost is an acknowledged limitation. A controlled comparison could test sparse GPs, ensembles, and neural surrogates at increasing observation counts while measuring calibration, regret, and wall-clock cost.

3. **Is dynamic-support revision genuinely responsible for novel candidate families?** The current evidence is mainly ablation curves and interpreted trajectories. A next experiment could operationalize support expansion using structural-distance or scaffold-novelty metrics and test fixed-support versus adaptive-support variants under identical proposal budgets.

4. **Which parts of acquisition-policy distillation transfer across domains?** Task-level antibody transfer fails on 1H0D_C, suggesting that selection strategy transfers more readily than domain-specific proposal skill. Factorial experiments could separately train candidate-generation and acquisition-response components, then evaluate both candidate quality and selection quality on held-out domains.
