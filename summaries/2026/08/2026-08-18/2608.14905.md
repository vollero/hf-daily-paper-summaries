# How Do Agents Fail on AutoResearch: End-to-End Diagnostic Evaluation on 100 Real-World Frontier Research Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14905) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14905)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 800 full-lifecycle research-agent trajectories, the authors find pervasive evidence–claim, methodological-integrity, and self-correction failures, arguing that these reflect a missing metacognitive loop, although automated annotation error and the evaluation design limit causal attribution.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks where and why autonomous agents fail when conducting an entire scientific investigation rather than an isolated task. The authors identify three gaps in prior evaluation: narrow or simulated tasks, endpoint scores that obscure process quality and reward hacking, and failure analyses that lack systematic coverage of generated artifacts such as code, data, and logs (§1, pp. 3–4).

This matters because a plausible final report or strong metric can conceal invalid methods, unsupported claims, leakage, or failures the agent itself detected but did not correct (§§1, 5.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **AutoResearchEval:** 100 tasks derived from published research across seven domains, comprising 70 open-ended discovery tasks and 30 target-anchored optimization tasks. Each of eight harness–model combinations runs every task through six lifecycle stages, producing 800 complete trajectories (§§2.1–2.2; Fig. 2; Table 1).

- **Artifact-aware evaluation:** A human-calibrated agent judge examines transcripts, code, data, logs, intermediate outputs, and final reports under stage-specific rubrics and evidence requirements (§3).

- **ARFT taxonomy:** An inductively developed taxonomy of 45 failure patterns organized by six lifecycle stages plus a cross-stage layer and four root-cause pillars (§4; Table 3; Appendix A).

- **Empirical diagnosis:** The authors aggregate 12,712 established pattern hits and interpret the three cognitive pillars as manifestations of a missing metacognitive loop (§§5.1–5.2; Fig. 3).

- **Released assets:** The paper states that tasks, trajectories, annotations, judging protocols, and ARFT are publicly released (§6, p. 17).

These results establish that the judge frequently identifies recurring, artifact-supported failure patterns in this particular corpus. They do not establish that the reported frequencies generalize to all research agents, that every attributed hit is correct, or that a metacognitive deficit is uniquely causal. The “model-level” interpretation is an author claim; the supplied design does not clearly isolate model from harness effects for the same backbone across multiple harnesses (Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Eight harness–model combinations and 100 tasks cannot exhaust agent systems, scientific activities, or possible failures (§6).
- Only one rollout is reported per task–system pair, so frequencies are indicative rather than exhaustive (§6).
- Fixed wall-clock and token budgets may contribute to shallow search and premature termination, but incidence is not analyzed against remaining budget (§6).
- Contamination cannot be fully excluded despite de-identification and temporal holdouts (§6).
- Judge validation covers only 50 trajectories and is aggregate; per-pattern and per-pillar error rates are unavailable (§6).
- Cognitive Depth & Adaptability labels require more subjective judgment and are treated as lower-confidence (§3.1).
- Open-ended and target-anchored subsets are compositionally unmatched, preventing direct regime comparisons (§2.1.2).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The automated judge produces most corpus labels, so correlated judge biases could materially affect the 12,712-hit distribution.
- Refining the taxonomy until expert consensus may overstate independent reliability; κ = 0.85 is reported after iterative category refinement (§3.1).
- The paper infers a common metacognitive cause from recurring observational patterns but reports no intervention or causal ablation that adds such a loop.
- Claims that the deficit is located at the model level are not cleanly supported by the combinations shown in Table 1, which largely confound backbone and harness.
- The self-healing judge requires regenerated attempts to contain at least five more issues after failure (Appendix C.8), potentially creating pressure toward over-identification; its effect is not quantified.
- The supplied source is truncated, with 19,659 characters omitted from the middle, so this analysis cannot guarantee coverage of all appendices, implementation details, or qualifications.

## Open research questions

1. **Can an enforced correction gate reduce F.4 failures?** The paper observes self-diagnosed flaws that remain unfixed but tests no intervention. A next experiment could randomize trajectories to ordinary review versus a gate requiring either revised evidence or a retracted claim.

2. **Are the patterns caused primarily by models, harnesses, or their interaction?** Table 1 does not provide a clearly controlled factorial comparison. Running the same backbones under multiple harnesses, with repeated seeds, would permit variance decomposition.

3. **How accurate is attribution for each ARFT pattern?** Only aggregate judge agreement is reported. A larger blinded human study could estimate per-pattern precision, recall, and uncertainty-adjusted prevalence.

4. **How sensitive are failures to resource budgets?** Fixed budgets may induce shallow retrieval or early stopping. Randomizing token, wall-clock, retrieval, and compute allowances would test which patterns diminish with resources and which persist.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains extensive methods, aggregate results, tables, and limitations, but it is explicitly truncated.

Material missing or incomplete includes the omitted appendix content; per-pattern and per-pillar judge agreement; uncertainty intervals for failure frequencies; repeated-run variance; a controlled model-versus-harness analysis; intervention studies targeting the proposed metacognitive loop; detailed task-authoring rater checks; and complete information about how often judge regeneration occurred or changed labels.
