# StateM: Reaching 95.3% Raw Accuracy, or a \$15 Frontier Run, on Terminal-Bench 2.1 via Harness Scaling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15089) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15089)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors claim that externalizing an agent’s procedural state into an enforceable, reusable runbook substantially improves long-horizon task completion without changing model weights, although gains depend on model and task-family fit and the headline 95.3% result is an unadjudicated raw score.

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon agents may possess the capabilities needed for individual steps yet fail to complete an overall task because they lose track of mutable state, forget earlier lessons, omit procedures, or stop before verifying the deliverable. The authors argue that most work targets model scaling, leaving comparatively underexplored how much failure can be addressed by the surrounding execution harness.

They identify two operational pressures: important control signals become diluted in long execution traces, and current state becomes ambiguous when it must be reconstructed from append-only history. Their goal is to improve fixed-model reliability while retaining the autonomy of a general-purpose CLI agent rather than decomposing work into narrowly scripted model calls (Sections 1 and 3; Figures 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Harness scaling:** The paper frames systematic improvement of the runtime, state management, verification, and recovery layer as a capability axis complementary to model scaling (Sections 1 and 4).

- **StateM runtime:** StateM represents coarse execution phases in a human-readable YAML runbook containing state-local instructions, valid transitions, hooks, checks, recovery rules, and durable history. The agent operates this control layer through its ordinary CLI environment, while users can inspect and revise the same artifact (Sections 3.1–3.5; Figure 2).

- **Context-and-contract boundaries:** Entering a state refreshes phase-relevant context; leaving it requires configured transition checks. Host-evaluated commands and predicates can provide stronger evidence than checklists, agent receipts, or LLM review (Sections 3.2–3.3).

- **Failure-driven harness optimization:** Postmortems are converted selectively into versioned prompts, checks, activation rules, state boundaries, and recovery paths, preserving procedural lessons outside model weights (Sections 4.6–4.7).

- **Transfer hierarchy:** The experiments distinguish fixed-model lift, unchanged transfer within the GPT family, adapted transfer to DeepSeek, and task-family generalization on BusinessBench (Sections 4.1–4.5).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that a developed control profile can materially change observed system-level task success and that unchanged transfer works better between nearby models than across providers. It also demonstrates both positive and negative task-family transfer.

It does not isolate the generic state-machine runtime from the prompts, checks, routing rules, and benchmark-adapted procedural content. Nor does it establish that 95.28% is a finalized leaderboard score, that five-trial coverage reflects single-run reliability, or that the mixed-timeout DeepSeek aggregate is directly comparable to a standard full-suite evaluation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The empirical system is the runtime plus an evolved control profile; the experiments do not isolate the state-machine abstraction itself (Sections 3.1 and 4).

- **Author-stated:** The 95.28% score is raw and pre-adjudication. Four rewarded trajectories were judged invalid, and nine were flagged for possible reward hacking; corrected variants are 94.38% and 93.26% (page 4, footnote 1).

- **Author-stated:** Public GPT references are not newly rerun, agent-version-matched controls. Some score and cost comparisons also come from different GPT evaluations (Section 4.2, footnote 3; Section 4.4, footnote 5).

- **Author-stated:** The DeepSeek 88.76% aggregate changes the timeout for one task, so 88.09% is the proper uniform standard-timeout full-suite score (Section 4.4).

- **Author-stated:** BusinessBench has one stochastic trajectory per arm, unequal family sizes, and only its first frozen held-out evaluation is untouched. Later refinements reuse aggregate evaluation feedback (Sections 4.1, 4.5, and 4.7; Table 2).

- **Author-stated:** Checks are not correctness or security oracles. Self-attestations and LLM reviews remain fallible; missing requirements cannot be enforced, arbitrary external effects cannot generally be rolled back, and untrusted hooks require host sandboxing (Sections 3.2–3.6).

- **Author-stated:** The work covers single-agent control only; multi-agent coordination and permission design remain future work (Section 5).

- **Analyst inference:** Causal attribution to individual StateM mechanisms is weak because Table 3 associates controls with improvements but explicitly is not a component ablation.

- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark-driven procedural memory risks learning evaluator conventions absent from visible task specifications, as the paper itself illustrates with video precision defaults and DNA insertion boundaries (Section 4.7).

## Open research questions

1. **Which StateM mechanisms produce the gains?** The paper evaluates bundled profiles and does not isolate state refresh, transition checks, stop hooks, recovery, or procedural memory. A factorial ablation on the same model, harness version, and task trials could estimate each component’s contribution and interaction.

2. **How robust are results under matched, finalized comparisons?** The principal references differ in agent version or evaluation provenance, and the headline submission is unadjudicated. A preregistered matched A/B study with identical model, agent version, compute budget, timeout, and finalized adjudication would reduce this uncertainty.

3. **Can profile adaptation avoid evaluator overfitting?** The paper documents cases where feedback can encode unstated evaluator conventions. A next experiment could develop profiles on one evaluator and test them against independently written task specifications and verifiers.

4. **What predicts successful transfer?** Exact GPT-to-DeepSeek transfer fails, while two BusinessBench families improve strongly and two regress. Measuring task and model characteristics before adaptation, then testing whether they predict gain or negative transfer, could turn the proposed “mechanism match” principle into a validated selection rule.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s reported methods, settings, and numerical results because the supplied text includes the complete retrieved paper, tables, footnotes, and qualification of the headline claims.

Material missing information includes the actual source repository and commit, executable implementation details beyond the example runbook, complete per-trial trajectories, statistical significance or confidence intervals for most comparisons, randomized matched A/B controls, independent replication, final leaderboard adjudication, and component-level ablations.
