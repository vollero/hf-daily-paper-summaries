# AnyTalk: Speech Animation for Arbitrary Characters Leveraging a Video Generation Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16143) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16143)


## One-sentence takeaway

AnyTalk adapts a pretrained talking-head diffusion model to a rigged character using only static blendshape renders, then converts its audio-driven video into 3D blendshape animation, outperforming three baselines on lip-sync metrics while requiring no character animation data.

## Problem and motivation

Audio-driven 3D facial-animation systems commonly require paired audio–animation data for each mesh or blendshape configuration, or demand re-rigging and mesh preprocessing. The authors argue that this limits adoption by independent developers and small studios.

AnyTalk addresses animation-data-free speech animation across varied character rigs. Its key challenge is that pretrained human talking-head generators provide useful speech-motion priors but can distort stylized or rendered characters, undermining subsequent 3D parameter estimation (Introduction; Section 2.2, Figure 3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Two-stage 2D-to-3D pipeline.** A pretrained Hallo audio-driven diffusion model generates a talking-head video; per-frame optimization then fits the character’s blendshape coefficients to that video (Sections 2.1 and 2.4).

2. **Character-specific Fine-tuning (CsF).** Each blendshape is activated and rendered as a frontal still. These renders are paired with zeroed audio embeddings, while only the denoising UNet’s spatial residual blocks are updated. Temporal, audio-attention, and reference modules remain frozen to preserve speech-motion priors (Section 2.2, Figure 4).

3. **Landmark-based 3D fitting.** Ray-casting establishes landmark-to-vertex correspondences. Homography alignment uses relatively expression-invariant landmarks, followed by optimization with talk-landmark, asymmetric mouth-opening, and sparse regularization losses; Gaussian smoothing reduces temporal jitter (Section 2.4, Equations 2–6).

4. **Real-time distillation.** AnyTalk outputs supervise `AnyTalk_RT`, an audio-to-blendshape network trained with feature-matching and reconstruction losses (Section 4.1, Figures 14–15).

These experiments support the authors’ claim that the personalized two-stage method improves measured and perceived lip synchronization on the five tested characters, and that distillation provides a substantial speed–quality trade-off. They do not establish performance on unrigged characters, non-frontal views, poorly designed rigs, broader character collections, or ground-truth 3D motion accuracy. The baseline comparison is also asymmetric because AnyTalk is personalized per character while the principal baselines are not; the main text points to a supplementary non-personalized evaluation, but its results are absent from the supplied content.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Full AnyTalk requires per-frame optimization taking about 3.12 seconds; `AnyTalk_RT` is faster but has worse lip-sync metrics (Section 4.1; Limitations).
- Characters must already have appropriate blendshapes. Missing controls—such as mouth opening—can cause failure (Limitations).
- Sparse 2D landmarks may miss subtle details and high-frequency motion; photometric loss was slower and did not improve the reported results (Section 3.6.2; Limitations).
- The pipeline depends on frontal views. Attempts using independently generated multi-view videos or off-the-shelf reconstruction were unsuccessful because of cross-view inconsistency or character mismatch (Limitations).
- Masks or stylized dental geometry may disrupt landmark-to-vertex ray-casting, requiring component removal or manual vertex assignment (Section 2.4).
- Prioritizing stable 3D geometry and view independence can produce less pronounced lip motion than per-frame 2D-oriented methods (Conclusion).

**Analyst inferences**

- **Limited diversity:** evaluation covers only five characters and LibriSpeech clips, so “arbitrary characters” is not exhaustively validated (Section 3.2, Table 1).
- **Comparison asymmetry:** AnyTalk receives character-specific fine-tuning, whereas the principal baselines do not. The authors acknowledge this, but the referenced supplementary control is unavailable here (Section 3.5).
- **No ground-truth 3D accuracy:** LSE-D and LSE-C assess audio-video synchronization because paired ground-truth 3D animations were unavailable; they cannot directly verify correct blendshape coefficients or geometry (Section 3.5).
- **User-study scope:** participants were 21 people aged 23–35; the text does not report animation expertise or broader demographic sampling (Section 3.7).
- **Reproducibility gap:** code availability is claimed, but the supplied text does not establish model-weight availability, licensing, random seeds, per-character training sample counts beyond the blendshape-rendering procedure, or complete runtime breakdowns.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the method animate unrigged or poorly rigged meshes?** The current optimizer requires suitable predefined blendshapes. A next experiment could compare direct vertex-deformation or learned rig-generation methods against blendshape fitting on meshes with systematically degraded rigs.

2. **Can multi-view consistency be achieved without sacrificing video quality?** Independent diffusion generations were inconsistent across viewpoints. A controlled experiment could jointly generate synchronized front and profile views with shared motion latents and measure cross-view landmark and geometric consistency.

3. **Can fine facial dynamics be recovered efficiently?** Sparse landmarks omit subtle motion, while global photometric loss was ambiguous and 2.96× slower. A next experiment could add localized dense correspondences or neural displacement maps and assess lip-sync, geometric error against captured 3D motion, and runtime.

4. **Can real-time inference retain the teacher’s quality across characters?** `AnyTalk_RT` reaches 110 FPS but degrades both reported lip-sync metrics and was timed on Morphy. A next experiment could evaluate character-conditioned distillation across all five rigs, including unseen rigs, with latency and quality reported per character.
