# Advancing Open and Reproducible Relational Learning: RelArena-α, TabPFN-Rel and RPI

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16319) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16319)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors release a standardized RelBench v1 evaluation framework, a competitive flattening-based TabPFN harness, and a declarative deployment interface, finding at one seed that TabPFN-Rel leads standardized model submissions while the custom-tuned RT-PluRel system leads overall.

## Problem and motivation

Relational-learning results are difficult to reproduce or compare because implementations and tuning procedures are often unavailable, evaluation regimes and data versions differ, and papers use incompatible cross-task aggregation methods. The authors also identify weak coverage of pragmatic feature-engineering baselines and a lack of accessible interfaces for applying research methods to new relational databases (Section 1, Table 1).

These gaps matter because apparent model improvements may instead arise from tuning, validation, timestamp handling, or data-state differences. For example, changing the forecasting evaluation regime reduced performance by more than 10 ROC-AUC points on two `rel-f1` classification tasks and increased MAE by over 40% on its regression task (Section 1.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **RelArena-α:** An open framework covering all 21 entity-level forecasting tasks in RelBench v1. It standardizes data loading, data states, evaluation, model APIs, and model-submission tuning, while separately admitting systems with custom tuning. Its evaluation tooling reports Elo, ranks, normalized scores, win rates, and confidence intervals (Sections 2.1 and 2.2).

- **TabPFN-Rel:** A relational harness that uses deep feature synthesis to flatten foreign-key paths before TabPFN-3 performs in-context prediction. Relative to RDBLearn, it changes the tuning data freeze, backbone and context scale, supports entity-table text, and introduces recency/diversity-aware context selection (Figure 1; Sections 2.2).

- **RPI:** An open, model-agnostic declarative interface that turns CSV or Parquet collections plus YAML database/task specifications into RelArena-compatible problems, without task-specific Python. It includes specifications for all 21 evaluated tasks (Section 2.3).

- **Unified baseline evaluation:** Six model submissions, one system submission, a simple LightGBM baseline, and two constant predictors are evaluated through the same framework (Section 3.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| TabPFN-Rel (API) ranks first among standardized model submissions. | Elo 1821 across 21 RelBench v1 tasks, versus 1706 for TabPFN-Rel OSS, 1658 GraphSAGE, 1575 RelGT, 1548 RDBLearn, and 1506 RelGNN; single seed and standardized tuning. | Section 3.1; Figure 2, left |
| RT-PluRel leads the combined model-and-system board. | Elo 1861 versus 1826 for TabPFN-Rel API, but RT-PluRel uses an internal custom sequential tuning regime, so this is an end-to-end system comparison rather than an isolated model comparison. | Section 3.2; Figure 2, right; Appendix F |
| Text availability materially affects TabPFN-Rel’s aggregate ranking. | Removing text in the OSS variant changes Elo from 1821 to 1706; the authors attribute most of the difference to `rel-event/user-ignore` and `rel-avito/user-clicks`. | Section 3.2; Figure 2; Table 4 |
| Simple entity-history constants remain competitive on some tasks. | `constant-per-entity` beats RelGNN and RelGT on four tasks each; only TabPFN-Rel and RT-PluRel beat it on all 21 tasks. | Section 3.2; Tables 4–5 |
| Tuning benefits differ substantially by method. | Selected configurations beat defaults on 15/21 tasks for RelGNN, 14/21 for GraphSAGE, 9/21 for TabPFN-Rel API, and 7/21 for RelGT. | Section 3.3; Figure 3; Table 6 |
| Reported runtimes omit potentially substantial preprocessing. | Measured mean per-task runtime is 76 minutes for TabPFN-Rel API, 511 minutes for RelGT, and 351 minutes for RT-PluRel; preprocessing is excluded, so these are lower bounds rather than end-to-end costs. | Appendix B, Table 2 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that flattening-based tabular methods are strong baselines under this particular unified evaluation and that benchmark protocol choices materially affect comparisons. It does not establish universal superiority over relational architectures: results cover one task family, one benchmark version, and one seed; model and system Elo values are relative to the included competitors; and some methods use different tuning regimes or timestamp context.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Results use a single seed over 21 entity-level forecasting tasks, so variance across training seeds is not measured (Section 3.1).

- **Author-stated:** Tuning compute is only approximately matched. Runtime varies substantially, preprocessing is cached and untracked, and more expensive methods may be advantaged (Section 3.1; Appendix B).

- **Author-stated:** RelArena lacks several competitive methods and stronger traditional tabular learners coupled with automated feature engineering (Section 4).

- **Author-stated:** Timestamp boundaries remain inconsistent: some GNN and transformer methods can use rows at the entity’s test timestamp, whereas TabPFN-Rel and RDBLearn do not (Section 4).

- **Author-stated:** RPI is restricted to entity-level forecasting and has limited safeguards against task mis-specification; practitioner requirements are not yet well understood (Section 2.3).

- **Author-stated:** The benchmark and TabPFN-Rel share authors, creating a conflict of interest, although all artifacts are opened for independent scrutiny (Section 3.1).

- **Author-stated:** RDBLearn excludes one backbone used in its original sweep, which may modestly disadvantage it; some methods had to be reconstructed or reimplemented (Section 3.2 footnote; Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** The text-feature comparison is not a clean text-only ablation because the API and OSS variants differ in model availability as described in Section 2.2; the isolated contribution of text is therefore not fully established.

- **Analyst inference:** Elo ranks depend on the included methods and task-level pairwise outcomes, so the numerical ratings should not be interpreted as absolute performance measures (Section 3.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can relational tuning budgets be standardized end to end?** Preprocessing costs are currently excluded and runtimes vary by orders of magnitude. A next experiment could record preprocessing, tuning, and inference on fixed hardware and compare methods under matched wall-clock or energy budgets.

2. **How much does timestamp-boundary access change each method’s ranking?** RelArena currently permits inconsistent access at the entity timestamp. A controlled experiment could run every method with both inclusive and exclusive timestamp boundaries on all 21 tasks.

3. **Which TabPFN-Rel components drive its gains over RDBLearn?** Backbone, context size, text handling, context selection, and tuning fixes change together. A factorial ablation could introduce each component separately while holding the data split and compute budget fixed.

4. **Do the conclusions transfer beyond entity-level forecasting?** Recommendation, attribute prediction, foreign-key prediction, and autocomplete are excluded. Extending the unified protocol to representative tasks from each family would test whether flattening remains competitive and whether the model API generalizes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported setup, artifacts, and numerical results because the supplied text includes the experimental protocol, leaderboards, raw task scores, and runtime tables.

Material missing information includes multi-seed uncertainty; complete end-to-end preprocessing time and hardware normalization; component-wise TabPFN-Rel ablations; confidence intervals for the primary Figure 2 values in the retrieved text; performance outside RelBench v1 entity-level forecasting; and independent reproduction of the released benchmark and implementations.
