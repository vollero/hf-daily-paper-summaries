# ENTLORE: A Graph-Grounded Benchmark for Latent Organizational Reasoning in Enterprise Question Answering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10679) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10679)


## One-sentence takeaway

EntLORE evaluates enterprise QA where organizational relations must be inferred rather than retrieved, finding that structured corpus access performs best on average but even gold-document access leaves 30.4% of latent questions unanswered.

## Problem and motivation

Enterprise records are operational by-products, so relations such as project attribution, responsibility, and alias-to-initiative correspondence may remain implicit across heterogeneous documents. The authors argue that existing enterprise and multi-hop QA benchmarks generally materialize the facts along a predefined answer path, testing retrieval and composition more than recovery of an unstated organizational relation (Sections 1–2).

This creates an evaluation gap: realistic questions may lack complete public evidence, depend on private premises, or admit multiple interpretations. A benchmark therefore needs to establish answerability, completeness, and uniqueness without leaking the target relation (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **New capability formulation:** The paper defines *latent organizational reasoning* as recovering organization-specific relations absent from every released document, contrasted with stated lookup (L1) and stated cross-source composition (L2) (Sections 1 and 3.4).
- **Graph-grounded construction:** Routine documents, authoritative tables, and operational traces are reconstructed into a provenance-bearing raw graph. Versioned, human-authorized conventions add certified derived edges to an audited truth graph (Sections 3.1–3.2).
- **Private truth, public evidence:** Systems receive an aligned anonymized corpus, while organizational records, certification rules, target edges, and item-level proofs remain private (Sections 3.1 and 3.3; Figure 2).
- **Executable verification:** Typed graph programs determine answers and proof dependencies; release gates check premise grounding, target absence, completeness, unique denotation, privacy, and replay after anonymization (Section 3.5; Appendix C).
- **Benchmark and evaluation:** EntLORE provides 907 questions over 2,341 documents and evaluates eight models under seven access conditions, totaling 56 model–access configurations (Abstract; Section 4.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Structured and lexical access form the leading overall tier. | Across all questions and eight models, weighted accuracy is 52.6% for BM25, 52.2% for GraphRAG, and 50.9% for LLM-Wiki, versus 36.5% for agentic retrieval and 36.0% for Flat RAG. | Section 4.2; Table 1 |
| GraphRAG leads latent-relation QA on average, but not uniformly. | Mean L3 accuracy: GraphRAG 36.2%, BM25 34.0%, LLM-Wiki 27.7%, Flat RAG 18.9%, agentic retrieval 13.5%; GraphRAG leads for five models and BM25 for three. | Section 4.3 |
| Performance differences depend strongly on relation family. | On 42 department-attribution questions, GraphRAG reaches 53%, BM25 2%, and the gold-document reference 84%. For containment attribution and hierarchy rollup, no deployable method exceeds 34%, against oracle references of 66% and 59%. | Section 4.3; Figure 3a |
| GraphRAG’s relative advantage concentrates where flat retrieval misses the complete evidence set. | Across 1,872 L3 model–question pairs, BM25 leads 54.9% to GraphRAG’s 48.5% when either flat retriever assembles the full gold set; GraphRAG leads 31.0% to BM25’s 25.1% otherwise. | Section 4.3; Figure 3b |
| Iterative dense retrieval underperforms single-pass dense RAG. | L3 accuracy is 13.5% for agentic retrieval versus 18.9% for Flat RAG; with gold document paths, the same agentic loop reaches 69.6%. | Section 4.3 |
| Gold documents do not eliminate the latent-relation difficulty. | Gold-document residuals are 12.6% for L1, 6.2% for L2, and 30.4% for L3. The L3 residual is 4.9 times the L2 residual and 47.7% of the best deployable condition’s remaining L3 error. | Section 4.4; Figure 3c |
| Release fidelity supports coarse group comparisons, not fine ranking. | On 176 aligned items, mean absolute shift is 0.125; rank correlation across eight configurations is ρ=0.71 with exact permutation p=0.06. | Section 4.5; Figure 4 |
| The oracle residual is partly induced by the evaluation instruction. | Among zero-scored oracle answers, explicit “fact not present” responses occur on 19.5% of L3 items versus 4.0% L1 and 3.3% L2. Eight latent count items fail under every condition; the oracle solves 1/64 model–item pairs. | Appendix G |

The results support the authors’ claim that evidence organization and recovery of implicit relations both matter: supplying gold documents substantially improves L3 performance but does not close the gap. They do **not** isolate a pure reasoning-failure rate, establish that graph structure always beats lexical retrieval, or identify which GraphRAG component causes its gains. The release-fidelity test also does not validate fine-grained system rankings.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The gold-document residual includes navigation, interaction, extraction, and prompt effects; it is only an upper bound on latent relation-recovery failures (Sections 4.1 and 4.4).
- **Author-stated:** The oracle instruction discourages asserting unstated facts, directly conflicting with L3. About one-fifth of zero-scored L3 oracle outputs explicitly abstain, and eight count items should be revised or removed (Appendix G).
- **Author-stated:** Release fidelity is tested on only 176 of 258 paired items. The private corpus is Chinese while the release is anonymized and translated, and index construction differs between the two sides (Section 4.5; Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Privacy controls are not a general guarantee against re-identification (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated:** The post-hoc matched analysis has only 23 anchor triples, matches entities rather than premises, and has an underpowered text-grounded arm (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** GraphRAG is evaluated as a complete access system, so the evidence cannot attribute gains to graph induction, relation typing, community reports, or another component; the paper explicitly cautions that the comparison is system-level (Section 4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparability is weakened by condition-dependent reasoning settings: three conditions were rerun without reasoning traces for five open-weight models, while other cells retained reasoning traces (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility cannot yet be verified from the supplied text because the artifacts are promised “upon publication,” and exact model versions and budgets reside in an external evaluation manifest (Appendix G).

## Open research questions

1. **Can latent reasoning be isolated from retrieval and prompt-induced abstention?** The oracle residual mixes several failure sources. A next experiment could supply premise-matched evidence in a neutral prompt and score intermediate inferred relations separately from final answers.

2. **Which structured-access component produces GraphRAG’s gains?** The paper compares complete systems without component ablations. A controlled experiment could add entity linking, typed edges, and community summaries incrementally while holding retrieval and generation budgets fixed.

3. **Do conclusions transfer across enterprises, languages, and organizational conventions?** The benchmark reconstructs one audited enterprise world, while the release also changes language and identity surface forms. A next study could repeat construction across multiple organizations and evaluate both native-language and translated projections.

4. **Can systems learn when an organizational inference is warranted?** L3 requires deriving unstated relations, but unsupported inference must still be avoided. A next experiment could jointly evaluate answerable L3 items and the verified-abstention bank, measuring derivation accuracy and calibrated abstention.
