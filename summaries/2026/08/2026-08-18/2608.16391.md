# Ventor-QTest: Threat-Model-Driven Verification of Vendor-Hosted LLM APIs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16391) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16391)


## One-sentence takeaway

Ventor-QTest audits whether a hosted LLM route behaves like a trusted reference using text-only target queries, separately measuring average within-window divergence (AFL) and extreme run-level deviations (EFL), but its evidence is finite-probe and does not identify causes or establish downstream causality.

## Problem and motivation

Third-party APIs may advertise a particular open-weight model while serving a different checkpoint, precision, inference stack, or routing policy. Clients typically observe only generated text and provider-controlled metadata, creating a verification gap between the advertised service and its actual behavior (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

Existing checks may miss intermittent degradation or require target probabilities. The authors therefore study whether black-box queries can quantify behavioral deviation from a trusted reference while requiring ordinary text generation—but no logits—from the target (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Threat and process model:** The hosted route is modeled as a stochastic process over serving windows, covering operational differences, undisclosed quantization, intermittent degradation, substitution or metadata forgery, and adaptive selective routing (Sections 2.1–2.2).

- **Average fidelity loss (AFL):** Each frozen context is sent repeatedly to the target. Returned strings are mapped into predeclared categories, forming an empirical distribution compared with the reference using a null-bias-corrected, coarsened-KL statistic averaged across contexts (Sections 3.2–3.4).

- **Extreme fidelity loss (EFL):** Independent target-generated sequences are rescored token by token by the reference. The empirical upper tail of complete-run absolute centered-surprisal drift captures unusually large deviations hidden by averages (Sections 3.5 and B.2).

- **Composite reporting:** AFL and EFL are retained as distinct observables because they have different units and neither can be converted directly into task success (Equation 17; Sections 3.1 and 3.5).

- **Evaluation and release:** Seven DeepSeek route snapshots are evaluated using the audit, GPQA-Diamond, and Terminal-Bench; the authors report releasing protocols, counts, per-run measurements, benchmark outcomes, and reproduction artifacts (Sections 4 and F).

The evidence supports finite-context detection of behavioral inconsistency and shows that mean and upper-tail statistics can rank route snapshots differently. It does not establish which checkpoint, quantization, kernel, or routing policy caused a deviation. The logprob comparison is target-controlled and statistically route-limited, while the Terminal-Bench result is observational and temporally unsynchronized with the audit; neither validates fidelity against independent ground truth nor establishes causality.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- AFL covers only declared categorical maps in one observed window for most routes; it does not estimate cross-window mean latent divergence (Table 1; Sections 3.3 and 6).
- EFL is centered-surprisal drift, not KL, and 20 runs cannot estimate precise rare-event probabilities or the latent excess-maximum functional (Sections 3.5 and 6).
- Correlated routing, caching, session affinity, overdispersion, reference uncertainty, and reference drift may make intervals and null tests optimistic (Assumptions A4–A5; Section 6).
- The audit tests text generation, not tool execution, multimodal processing, full API semantics, or agent-environment interaction (Sections 2.1 and 2.3).
- A provider that recognizes finite probes can route audit traffic selectively and evade detection (Sections 2.2, 2.3, and 6).
- Provider region, several sampling/network fields, retry lineage, and request interleaving were not preserved, limiting exact operational reproduction (Section B).
- Terminal-Bench windows were not time-aligned with audit probes, and 47 of DigitalOcean’s 56 failures were agent timeouts, preventing causal attribution to model-output deviation (Sections 6 and C).

**Analyst inference:** Generalizability is narrow because the formal evaluation covers one requested model revision, seven route snapshots, 12 constrained one-token contexts, and a small number of logprob-capable conditions. Sensitivity checks address estimator and pooling choices, but the supplied text reports no controlled interventions that isolate checkpoint substitution, quantization, or routing changes as causal mechanisms.

## Open research questions

1. **Can EFL predict long-horizon failures prospectively?**  
   This remains open because audit and Terminal-Bench windows were unsynchronized. A next experiment could collect time-aligned EFL probes and agent tasks across many independent windows, then test whether pre-task tail measurements predict failures after controlling for timeouts and route conditions.

2. **How should rare intermittent deviations be estimated?**  
   Twenty runs provide only descriptive upper-tail summaries. A larger sequential study could collect hundreds or thousands of independent windows and compare empirical quantiles, extreme-value estimators, and calibrated stopping rules.

3. **How robust is AFL to dependent routing and reference drift?**  
   The null assumes approximately independent within-cell requests and fixed reference vectors. A factorial experiment could vary session identifiers, timing, batching, and reference windows, then compare multinomial calibration with overdispersed or hierarchical alternatives.

4. **Can adaptive providers be audited with less recognizable probes?**  
   Fixed finite sets can be detected and selectively routed. A next experiment could generate hidden, procedurally varied challenges at runtime and evaluate detection power against a controlled probe classifier and mixed-routing adversary.
