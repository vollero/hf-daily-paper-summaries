# When Context Bites: Detecting RAG Poisoning via Document-Level Attention Collapse

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06947) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06947)


## One-sentence takeaway

D-SCAN detects RAG poisoning with a linear classifier over token- and document-level attention statistics, outperforming tested output-, activation-, and LLM-based detectors across three multi-hop QA benchmarks while remaining effective with one generation.

## Problem and motivation

RAG systems can retrieve attacker-injected documents that steer an LLM toward targeted answers, creating serious risks in high-stakes question answering. The authors argue that existing detectors often inspect output uncertainty, consistency, semantics, or undifferentiated internal signals, overlooking the document structure of retrieved context and offering limited mechanistic interpretation (§1–2).

Their empirical motivation is “blind confidence”: poisoned generations can have higher token probabilities and greater cross-sample consistency than clean generations, undermining the assumption that attacks or hallucinations produce uncertainty (§3.1, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Mechanistic finding:** The authors identify “Attention Collapse,” where attention becomes concentrated on poisoned documents and document-level attention entropy falls relative to benign generation (§3.2, Figures 1 and 3).
- **Document-level measurements:** Document Attention Density aggregates attention from generated tokens to each retrieved document and normalizes by document length; normalized densities are then used to calculate document-level entropy (§3.2, Equations 2–3).
- **D-SCAN detector:** A linear classifier uses entropy, variance, and density features derived from token- and document-level attention during inference (§3.3).
- **Outcome-independent detection:** The detector is evaluated separately on successful and failed attacks; the authors report that the signal remains detectable even when poisoning does not change the final answer (§4.2, Figure 4).
- **Released implementation:** The abstract supplies a public code repository, although complete feature definitions and implementation details reside there rather than in the supplied paper text.

The evidence establishes strong detection performance within the specified synthetic poisoning setup and supports the usefulness of combining token- and document-level attention features. It does not establish robustness to other attack generators, retrieval configurations, domains, languages, or inaccessible-attention models. The supplied text also does not quantify runtime overhead, document-localization accuracy, or the successful-versus-failed attack results.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-indicated implementation gap:** Comprehensive feature definitions and further implementation details are deferred to the code (§3.3–4), limiting reproducibility from the paper text alone.
- **Analyst inference — narrow attack coverage:** All reported poisoned samples use PoisonedRAG and replace exactly two of five retrieved documents (§4); generalization to other attack strategies or poison ratios is untested.
- **Analyst inference — white-box requirement:** D-SCAN requires internal attention states (§3.3), so applicability to black-box APIs is not demonstrated.
- **Analyst inference — limited domains and retrieval setting:** Evaluation covers three English multi-hop QA datasets, E5-base-v2 retrieval, and English Wikipedia (2018), not the legal or medical settings cited as motivation (§1, §4).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete model-transfer evidence:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is named for the empirical study (§3), but the supplied text does not clearly describe cross-generator training and testing of D-SCAN.
- **Analyst inference — overhead claim is unquantified:** “Lightweight,” “low computational overhead,” and “real-time” are asserted, but no latency, memory, or throughput measurements are reported (§3.3, §4.3).
- **Analyst inference — mechanism language may be stronger than the evidence:** Attention concentration correlates with poisoned inputs and attack success, but the reported observational analysis does not by itself prove that attention collapse causally produces the manipulated answer (§3.2, §4.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does D-SCAN generalize to unseen poisoning methods and poison ratios?** Only PoisonedRAG with two poisoned documents among five is reported. A next experiment could train on this setup and test without retraining on several unseen attacks and varying numbers of poisoned documents.

2. **Can the signal transfer across generators, layers, and attention implementations?** Cross-model D-SCAN evaluation is not clearly documented. A controlled study could train the classifier on one generator and report zero-shot and lightly calibrated transfer to other architectures and sizes.

3. **How does attention collapse differ from benign evidence concentration?** A legitimate answer may depend heavily on one retrieved document, potentially resembling collapse. An experiment could construct clean single-evidence, redundant-evidence, contradictory-evidence, and poisoned contexts and measure false-positive rates separately.

4. **Can comparable detection work without white-box attention access?** The proposed features require internal states. A next experiment could distill D-SCAN labels into a black-box detector using observable generation or attribution signals and measure the resulting accuracy–cost trade-off.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, experimental setup, and tabulated results; **medium** for broader robustness claims.
