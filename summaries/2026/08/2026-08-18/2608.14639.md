# Valid Per-Field Selective Risk Control for Document Extraction: Three Failure Modes, a Validity Ladder, and When Conditioning Pays

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14639) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14639)


## One-sentence takeaway

Naive field-level risk calibration can fail under document clustering, score-refit leakage, and tied scores; the proposed validity ladder restores increasingly defensible control, but the only assumption-matched document-level certificate achieves little coverage on the evaluated data.

## Problem and motivation

Document extractors need to accept reliable fields automatically and route uncertain ones for review while keeping the accepted-set error rate below a target \(\alpha\). The authors argue that the common procedure—fit a confidence score and select an add-one-calibrated threshold—does not reliably provide this contract on real documents because fields are clustered by document, learned scores may be calibrated on their own fitting data, and discrete score masses can make useful thresholds unreachable (§§1, 3.1, 4).

The identified gap is practical rather than new conformal theory: prior machinery does not diagnose these document-specific failures, state guarantees at the correct exchangeability unit, or characterize when provenance-based group conditioning improves certification (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Failure diagnosis:** The paper isolates document clustering, score-refit leakage, and score tie masses using counterfactual controls on genuine extraction outputs (§4, Table 3, Appendix B).

2. **Validity ladder:** It organizes procedures by guarantee strength (§5, Table 4):

   - Fit/validation splitting with add-one thresholding for expected-risk control.
   - Mondrian Learn-then-Test (LTT), exact binomial tails, and multiplicity correction for field-iid PAC guarantees.
   - A design-effect-adjusted field-level variant.
   - A document-iid tier bounding mean per-document loss.

3. **Provenance conditioning:** It defines ambiguity-penalized grounding support and a pre-specified three-bin taxonomy for groupwise thresholds (§§3.3–3.4).

4. **Two-regime characterization:** The authors claim that covariates should enter a sufficiently expressive learned score, whereas taxonomy-side conditioning can help a weak or frozen score when a pooled threshold cannot certify (§7, Propositions 1–3).

5. **Released artifact:** VerifyDoc includes a library, CLI, review UI, MCP server, and seed-pinned evaluation harness under Apache-2.0 (§10).

These results establish strong in-corpus evidence that the three diagnosed mechanisms matter and that separating score fitting from threshold calibration repairs the learned-score procedure’s mean operating point. They also show that stronger guarantees can sharply reduce coverage.

They do **not** establish per-deployment validity for the practical tiers, population-level significance from the overlapping 40 resplits, general provenance-conditioning gains across extractors or corpora, or useful document-level certification at the present sample size. Tier 3’s nominal PAC interpretation also relies on a field-iid assumption that the measurements contradict.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only CORD with Sonnet yields substantial rigorous, taxonomy-enhanced certification; FUNSD’s taxonomy lift is insignificant and XFUND-de has only 42 documents (§9).

- **Author-stated:** The assumption-matched doc-iid tier is near-vacuous, with 47.5% zero-coverage splits (§§6.1, 9).

- **Author-stated:** The general two-regime law is an empirical pattern over three corpora and two score families. Formal results cover idealized boundary cases, while no quantitative rate is proved for \(\rho>0\) (§§7, 9).

- **Author-stated:** The 40 resplits reuse documents, so permutation tests measure split stability rather than population significance; the scale CORD data also overlap data used to select support-bin (§§3.5, 5.3, 9).

- **Author-stated:** Tiers 2–4 initially refit the LR on data used for LTT testing, formally violating score–threshold independence. The measured effect is small for this five-parameter model, but fully freezing tier 4 lowers coverage from 0.060 to 0.006 (§§5.2, 6.1).

- **Author-stated:** FUNSD free-text labels are protocol-sensitive, with automatic-protocol agreement \(\kappa=0.10\); certificates apply to the supplied labels (§9, Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** Because headline captures consume OCR text rather than page images, the evidence does not establish performance for image-conditioned extraction or visually derived grounding (§3.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Human validation covers 149 accepted fields from the practical configuration, not the PAC tiers, other corpora, or rejected fields; its reassuring result therefore has limited scope (§6.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Can document-level certificates become useful without weakening their estimand?** The honest doc-iid tier frequently certifies nothing. A next experiment could compare cluster-aware empirical-Bernstein or variance-adaptive document bounds with the existing Hoeffding tier on larger document samples.

2. **How stable is the conditioning rule across unseen models and domains?** Support-bin wins on Sonnet CORD but not on weaker-signal models or easier corpora. A frozen prospective study could test the proposed gain-versus-fragmentation criterion across additional extractors, languages, and document families.

3. **What guarantees hold between the \(\rho=0\) boundary and score sufficiency?** The paper does not provide a quantitative result for intermediate within-group score–error dependence. A next study could derive a finite-\(\rho\) bound and test whether it predicts coverage gains more accurately than the current worst-case penalty.

4. **How does label uncertainty affect certified risk?** FUNSD labels are unstable and the CORD audit suggests pessimistic automatic errors. A plausible experiment would repeat calibration and certification using adjudicated human labels, then compare thresholds and achieved human-gold risk with automatic-label results.
