# NaviDC-OCR: Navigating Document Parsing Across Digital and Camera-Captured Documents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12898) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12898)


## One-sentence takeaway

NaviDC-OCR is a 1.2B-parameter decoupled vision-language framework that combines deformation-aware polygonal layout modeling with explicit content–structure separation, achieving leading reported results on digital, camera-captured, and scientific figure-to-table benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation

Document parsers must recover text, layout, reading order, formulas, and tables from both clean digital pages and camera-captured documents. The authors identify two complementary weaknesses: decoupled systems depend on layout detection and therefore propagate errors caused by warped or irregular regions, while end-to-end systems can generate redundant or hallucinated content and struggle with high-resolution structural reasoning (Abstract; §1).

NaviDC-OCR targets a unified solution. Its motivation is that geometric rectification improves two-stage parsing of captured documents, while tables, formulas, and scientific figures require explicit structural modeling beyond character recognition (§1; §3.2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Deformation-aware parsing:** The model learns global document control points and region boundaries from synthetic distortions, replacing rectangular layout boxes with polygonal boundary prediction for captured documents (§3.2.1; §4.2).

2. **Adaptive boundary representation:** Curvature-Guided Douglas–Peucker Sampling prioritizes points with large contour deviation or local curvature, seeking to retain folds and corners within a limited point budget (§3.2.2).

3. **Content–structure decoupling:** Formula grammar labels and content-free OTSL table structures are learned separately from content generation. The same principle is applied to scientific figure-to-table conversion (§4.3).

4. **Automated data engineering:** Multi-node consensus voting selects pseudo-labels from heterogeneous models, while a fine-tuned self-judgement VLM compares source images with rendered predictions and filters or explains errors (§3.1; §3.3; Figure 2).

5. **Progressive optimization:** Four stages cover vision-language alignment, deformation-aware training, structured parsing, and GRPO using task-specific text, table, and formula rewards (§4.1–§4.4; Figure 3).

These results establish strong comparative performance under the paper’s common evaluator settings, including fixed matching rules and timeouts (Appendix D). They do not isolate which proposed component causes each gain: the supplied text contains no quantitative ablation of deformation awareness, CGDP, content–structure decoupling, MCV, self-judgement, or reinforcement learning. The reported **78.41** PureDocBench score in the Abstract is also not reconciled with the three track-level scores in Table 3.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated failure mode:** NaviDC-OCR produced severe repetitive generation on several PureDocBench cases. Six invalid prediction files were removed before scoring and treated as missing, hence assigned zero (§Appendix D; Table 5).

- **Author-stated formula issue:** Many OmniDocBench formula errors are attributed to disagreement between first-stage layout granularity and benchmark annotations, indicating dependence on fine-grained layout localization (§5.1).

- **Analyst inference — attribution is weak:** No component ablations are supplied, so the claimed benefits of CGDP, deformation-aware supervision, structure decoupling, data filtering, and GRPO cannot be separated from training scale or the combined pipeline.

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility is incomplete:** The paper claims release of implementation and configurations, but the supplied text provides no repository link, complete data inventory, consensus thresholds, CGDP hyperparameters, RL configuration, compute budget, or inference cost (§3–§4).

- **Analyst inference — synthetic-to-real validity remains uncertain:** Much deformation and scientific-chart supervision is synthetic (§4.2; Appendix B). Wild OmniDocBench includes physically captured images, but broader deployment populations and capture devices are not described.

- **Analyst inference — some “SOTA” wording needs qualification:** On PureDocBench Digital Degraded, OvisOCR2 has a higher overall score; on Real Degraded, Gemini-3.1-Pro exceeds NaviDC-OCR overall (Table 3).

## Open research questions

1. **Which component produces each performance gain?** This remains open because no quantitative ablation is reported. A next experiment could add deformation supervision, CGDP, structure decoupling, self-judgement filtering, and GRPO incrementally under identical data and training budgets.

2. **How robust is deformation modeling outside the tested capture distributions?** The evaluation covers benchmark-specific degradations, but not an enumerated range of devices or environments. A cross-device study could test unseen cameras, lenses, lighting, paper materials, folds, blur, and occlusion severity.

3. **Can repetitive generation be prevented rather than scored as missing?** Appendix D documents failures but does not analyze their cause. A next experiment could evaluate constrained decoding, repetition penalties, structural validators, or retry policies while reporting failure rates and latency.

4. **Does content–structure separation generalize across chart and table families?** The paper reports aggregate benchmark results without per-structure ablations. A controlled evaluation could stratify performance by merged-cell topology, formula grammar depth, and the 19 listed scientific-figure classes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the architecture, training outline, benchmark values, and stated claims because the retrieved text includes the main sections, tables, and appendices.
