# ClawGym II: Exploring Black-Box RL on Agent Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16798) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16798)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors present a sandboxed black-box RL framework that reconstructs intercepted model calls as prefix trees and adapts PPO/GRPO for opaque agent harnesses, reporting stable gains across OpenClaw, Claude Code, and multiple long-horizon task benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation

Modern agent harnesses manage tools, context, retries, subagents, and environment state, but their opaque control flow prevents RL systems from directly observing clean, complete trajectories. Long-horizon training further introduces concurrent stateful execution, infrastructure failures, fragmented model-call traces, and training–inference mismatch. The authors identify a gap in stable, scalable optimization through heterogeneous production-style harnesses without modifying or reimplementing them (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Isolated rollout infrastructure:** Each task environment and harness runs in a temporary sandbox, with MCP services supplying common or task-specific tools. A serving proxy captures exact input/output tokens, rollout log-probabilities, and metadata without instrumenting the harness (Section 3.1; Figure 1).
- **Trajectory reconstruction:** Captured calls are assembled into rollout-level prefix trees. Retry-induced dead leaves, excessively branched rollouts, and auxiliary subagent or compaction trajectories are filtered before training (Sections 3.2.1–3.2.2).
- **Tree-aware RL:** GRPO assigns a rollout-level relative advantage across the retained tree; PPO independently backs up the terminal reward along each retained branch with \(\gamma=\lambda=1\). Shared-prefix tokens contribute only once per rollout (Section 3.2.3).
- **Training–inference consistency:** The framework trains on the exact generated tokens and applies truncated token-level importance weighting to mitigate probability differences between rollout and training engines (Section 3.2.4).
- **Heterogeneous-harness training:** Mix-harness training combines task–harness groups in one batch while normalizing GRPO advantages separately within each group (Section 3.3).

The results support the authors’ claim that the framework can improve agents within two structurally different harnesses and can train a shared model from both. They also show transfer beyond the main training distribution and limited white-to-black harness transfer.

They do not establish superiority over all black-box RL systems, universal compatibility with arbitrary harnesses, or statistical significance: confidence intervals, repeated-run variance, and hypothesis tests are not reported in the supplied text. Mix-harness results are largely curve-based rather than numerically tabulated.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Auxiliary subagent and context-compaction trajectories are discarded because assigning them the rollout reward creates ambiguous credit assignment; learning from them is deferred (Section 3.2.2 and Conclusion).
- **Author-stated:** PPO treats branches independently with \(\gamma=\lambda=1\), which may increase variance and requires the critic to estimate long-horizon returns; principled tree credit assignment remains open (Section 3.2.3).
- **Author-stated:** Token-level rollout correction trades bias for lower variance and is truncated, rather than using an unbiased sequence-level ratio (Section 3.2.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Evidence for harness generality is limited to OpenClaw and Claude Code, so the claim of applicability to “any” complex harness is broader than the experiments directly support.
- **Analyst inference:** OpenClaw and Claude Code use different initializations in the main experiments, complicating direct attribution of training-dynamic differences to the harness (Sections 4.1.1 and 4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The 30-task, non-multimodal PinchBench subset narrows external-validity coverage, while rubric-based evaluation depends partly on one named judge model (Section 4.1.1).
- **Analyst inference:** No seed counts, uncertainty estimates, significance tests, infrastructure scale, wall-clock cost, preset branching threshold, or exact correction truncation value are supplied, limiting reproducibility and robustness assessment.

## Open research questions

1. **How should rewards be assigned to subagent and compaction branches?** They are currently excluded because the terminal reward poorly identifies their contribution. A next experiment could compare learned branch-level critics, difference rewards, and explicit auxiliary-task rewards against main-trajectory-only training.

2. **Can PPO exploit the actual tree topology more faithfully?** The paper independently backs up rewards on each branch and acknowledges possible variance. A next experiment could implement tree-structured value backup and compare variance, stability, and Pass@1 with the simplified PPO variant.

3. **Does mix-harness training improve transfer to unseen harnesses?** The study evaluates the mixed model on the same two harness families used for training. A next experiment could hold out a compatible third harness and compare single-harness, mixed-harness, and white-box training.

4. **Which consistency and filtering components are essential?** Component-specific ablations are not reported. A next experiment could separately remove exact-token preservation, importance correction, dead-leaf filtering, shared-prefix deduplication, and over-branch filtering while measuring stability and final performance.
