# Gathered, Not Admitted: How Attention Brings a Latent Variable into Verbalizable Form

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15022) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15022)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper argues that task demand makes a latent variable more verbalizable by prompting attention to gather it into the query position within a mid-depth window, rather than by gating access to a representation already present there.

## Problem and motivation

Prior work found that language models represent latent quantities in a verbalizable form more strongly when tasks require flexible reuse, but did not establish how those quantities enter that form. The paper tests an “admission” hypothesis—a gate unmasks information already present at the query position—against a transport hypothesis in which attention moves the information there.

This distinction matters for mechanistic interpretations of latent-variable readouts: visibility under a Jacobian lens need not indicate either local availability or causal use (§1–§2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Demand-controlled benchmark:** JGateBench creates five instruction arms over identical contexts—automatic, report, flexible, control, and supplied—to separate latent-variable inference, operator use, prompt format, and accuracy (§2.2; Appendix A).

2. **Visibility versus availability:** Jacobian-lens visibility is compared across arms, while a jointly trained linear probe tests whether the same representation is decodable even without task demand (§3).

3. **Causal localization:** Activation patching independently varies patch and readout depth, distinguishing installation from survival. Residual, attention, MLP, and head-level interventions test which components transport the variable (§4–§5).

4. **Behavioral validation:** Counterfactual answers, ablations, and projection of concept-aligned lens directions test whether transported content affects task behavior (§5, §7).

5. **Methodological findings:** The paper quantifies sensitivity to donor pairing and shows that similar lens-readout shifts can correspond to substantially different behavioral effects (§6, §8).

Together, these results support the authors’ narrower claim that later lens-readable, donor-substitutable content at the query position is produced predominantly by attention-mediated gathering. They do not locate the variable’s source in the passage, prove that attention alone is sufficient, identify a complete write circuit, or exclude a gate acting on the attention route.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The experiments refute admission only at the query position; a gate controlling the attention route remains compatible with the evidence (§10).
- **Author-stated:** Mechanistic breadth is limited. Visibility effects span five checkpoints and two task families, but attention/MLP routing, mediation, and ablations are primarily tested on Qwen3.6-27B with language identity (§10).
- **Author-stated:** The pre-gather representation is not localized: passage-span patches transported nothing, but the tested intervention was too weak to determine whether the source is distributed (Appendix C; §10).
- **Author-stated:** Attention’s effect is necessary but not sufficient. Joint component patches do not reconstruct the residual-stream effect, and at least half of the counterfactual behavior survives removal of the concept-aligned direction (§5, §7).
- **Author-stated:** The supplied arm is at 1.000 accuracy versus flexible at 0.940 on the primary checkpoint, leaving a ceiling confound in the factorial decomposition (§10).
- **Author-stated:** Head-level results can depend strongly on donor pairing; single pairings report 7–64% more significant components than the four-pairing intersection (§6).
- **Author-stated:** The primary published 27B lens lacks a configuration file and convergence trace, although fitted lenses and artifact-level reproducibility information are supplied for replications (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-architecture evidence establishes similar fractional timing more securely than a shared component mechanism, because the gemma attention/MLP split is limited to one pairing and its lens-side sweep was not run (§5.1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Some layer/head choices were data-selected. Later experiments use larger or held-out instance pools, but the paper acknowledges that this cannot independently validate L39 as the optimal layer (Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **What controls the demand-sensitive gather?** The paper leaves a route-level gate plausible but unidentified (§10). A next experiment could causally intervene on passage-to-query attention edges at the gathering layer while preserving other attention paths.

2. **What is the variable’s pre-gather representation and source location?** Tail-span passage patches transported nothing, but the authors say this intervention could not block whole-passage re-derivation (Appendix C). A next experiment could patch or mask structured passage regions while measuring both nonlinear decodability and downstream transport.

3. **Does the same attention/MLP asymmetry generalize across architectures?** The behavioral window replicates on gemma-4, but the detailed route result does not (§5.1, §10). A next experiment could run matched-distance lens and path-patching sweeps on several dense and hybrid checkpoints with multiple donor pairings.

4. **Can readout changes be calibrated to causal use?** Section 8 shows that similarly sized readout shifts yield 7.4× different behavioral effects. A next experiment could fit and test a calibration mapping across components, layers, concepts, and task families using continuous behavioral margins.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s reported experiments and scoped conclusions because the supplied text includes extensive methods, numerical results, appendices, and reproducibility details.

Material missing or unresolved within the supplied text includes peer-review status, independent replication, the complete causal route from passage to query, a path-patching decomposition of attention versus other contributions, broad cross-architecture mechanism tests, and full provenance/configuration for the published Qwen3.6-27B Jacobian lens.
