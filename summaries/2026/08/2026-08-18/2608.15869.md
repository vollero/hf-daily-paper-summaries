# Beyond Visual CoT: Internalized Visual Thinking for Proactive Video Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15869) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15869)


## One-sentence takeaway

Internalized Visual Thinking jointly trains a video-language model to predict future-frame embeddings and textual events, improving over matched answer-only training in all six evaluated dataset–task settings while retaining direct-answer inference at roughly one-fifth the latency of explicit Visual CoT.

## Problem and motivation

Proactive video reasoning must recognize incomplete ongoing actions or anticipate events that have not begun, often under latency constraints. The authors argue that textual chain-of-thought can be inefficient and poorly grounded, while Visual CoT’s explicit future-frame generation requires expensive image decoding and re-encoding and depends on the fidelity of generated frames (Sections 1 and 3.2).

Their central gap is therefore how to obtain predictive visual representations without paying for visual generation during inference. In the controlled motivation study, generated-frame Visual CoT raised mean latency by 6.2× for early-event prediction and 5.0× for next-event prediction, yet produced inconsistent next-event gains; oracle future frames performed substantially better, suggesting that future visual evidence is useful but difficult and costly to externalize (Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Internalized Visual Thinking (IVT):** During post-training, the model jointly minimizes textual answer loss and a future-frame embedding prediction loss. At inference, it predicts text directly from the observed video prefix and prompt, with no future-frame synthesis (Sections 2.1–2.2; Figure 1).

- **Alternative future-state targets and objectives:** The paper studies Flux-VAE, DINOv2, and adaptive or frozen SigLIP2 representations, using either direct feature regression or rectified-flow matching (Sections 2.2, 3.3, and 3.5).

- **Integration study:** Controlled experiments vary shared Dense versus expert-separated MoE decoders, prediction horizon, understanding-to-prediction batch ratio, and joint versus two-stage curricula (Sections 3.4–3.7).

- **Quality–efficiency evidence:** Against matched BAGEL-7B-based methods, IVT improves every reported metric over Answer-Only SFT in all six combinations of three datasets and two tasks, beats Visual CoT in four of six settings, and has latency close to Answer-Only SFT (Tables 3–5 and 8).

The results establish consistent gains over the matched answer-only baseline within the tested BAGEL-based setup and a substantial measured latency advantage over explicit Visual CoT. They do not establish universal superiority over other model families: the authors characterize external-model comparisons as uncontrolled reference points, and IVT still trails Qwen3-VL-8B in several main-table settings.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The experiments focus on short-horizon anticipation; long-horizon reasoning, which may depend more on goals, procedures, and language priors, is left for future work (Section 3.1).

- **Author-stated comparison caveat:** External models differ in backbone, pretraining data, and optimization, so comparisons with them are not strictly controlled (Section 3.8).

- **Author-demonstrated sensitivity:** Benefits depend on target representation, predictive objective, decoder sharing, horizon, batch mixture, and curriculum. Two-stage training can underperform answer-only training, so future prediction is not automatically beneficial (Sections 3.3–3.7).

- **Analyst inference — domain concentration:** The three main datasets concern human activities, prominently egocentric settings, and all main results are in-domain. The single Charades transfer experiment helps but does not establish generality to broader video domains (Sections 3.1 and Appendix D.1).

- **Analyst inference — latency portability:** Efficiency was measured on one NVIDIA B200 at batch size 1 and bf16. Relative costs may differ across hardware, batching regimes, or deployment stacks (Table 8).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete artifact evidence:** Algorithms and hyperparameters are provided, but the supplied text does not state that code, checkpoints, or processed data were released.

- **Analyst inference — metric validity:** Main quality evaluation relies on reference-overlap and embedding-based text metrics; no human evaluation of plausibility, grounding, or calibration is reported in the supplied text (Section 3.1; Tables 3–5).

## Open research questions

1. **Does IVT help long-horizon anticipation?** The paper explicitly excludes it. A next experiment could vary anticipation gaps and prediction horizons on long-horizon procedural events while separating visual-dynamics gains from language-prior effects.

2. **How well do latent predictions represent multiple plausible futures?** NEP admits several valid outcomes, but the paper evaluates textual top-5 decoding rather than the diversity or calibration of latent forecasts. An experiment could compare deterministic regression, flow matching, and multimodal latent predictors using future plausibility and downstream calibration.

3. **Does IVT transfer beyond human activity video?** Main evaluation is concentrated on three activity datasets, with only Charades as held-out transfer. A next study could post-train once and test without adaptation on driving, sports, or other dynamic domains.

4. **Which properties make a target representation useful?** Flux-VAE outperforms the semantic targets, but the causal role of spatial detail, normalization, dimensionality, and predictability is unresolved. Controlled targets that independently vary these properties could isolate the mechanism.
