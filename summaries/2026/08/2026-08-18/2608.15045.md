# MOSS-VL Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15045) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15045)


## One-sentence takeaway

MOSS-VL combines append-only gated visual cross-attention with timing-aware supervised training to support streaming vision–language interaction, achieving the strongest reported average on three of four streaming benchmarks while true perception-during-generation remains validated only qualitatively.

## Problem and motivation

Most vision–language models process completed videos and answer afterward; even streaming systems typically stop perceiving while generating a reply. The authors target real-time interaction in which a model continuously observes, decides when to speak or remain silent, and can revise an unfolding response when new evidence arrives. They identify the lack of both architectures designed for concurrent perception and generation and quantitative benchmarks for the highest-level behavior—perception during generation (L5) (Introduction, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Streaming-compatible architecture:** Visual tokens remain outside the decoded text sequence and are accessed through 12 zero-initialized, gated cross-attention layers. New frames append keys and values without recomputing prior frames or disrupting decoding (Figure 2; §§2.2, 2.5).
- **Temporal representation:** XRoPE places text and visual patches on a shared three-axis sequence coordinate system, while textual timestamp tokens expose wall-clock time under variable frame rates (Figure 3; §§2.3–2.4).
- **Realtime-SFT:** A 0.56M-sample synthesized and curated interaction corpus teaches frame-by-frame decisions to wait, speak, continue, or self-correct using two state tokens and a class-reweighted next-token loss (§§4.2–4.3; Table 4).
- **Staged training and unified modes:** Four pre-training stages, standard SFT, and a final Realtime-SFT stage build offline and interactive models; one system prompt selects streaming/real-time behavior without architectural changes (Tables 3–4; §4.3).
- **Open system assets:** The authors report releasing five checkpoints, the curriculum, inference code, dialogue template, and an extended FlashAttention-3 backend (§§2.6, 5, 7).

These results support the authors’ claim that MOSS-VL is effective on existing streaming tests—particularly proactive timing—and that separating visual tokens from the decoded sequence improves measured serving latency in the tested setup. They do not quantitatively establish L5 perception during generation: available benchmarks cover only L2–L4, and L5 evidence consists of selected demonstrations. Nor do the results isolate how much each architectural, data, or training component contributes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** No benchmark quantitatively measures L5 perception during generation, so the paper’s central highest-level capability is supported qualitatively rather than by systematic evaluation (§7; Table 1).
- **Author-stated:** The model trails stronger comparable-scale models on MMMU and several document-oriented benchmarks; the authors associate this with the absence of a thinking mode and prioritization of real-time video (§7; Table 5).
- **Author-stated:** MOSS-VL accepts no audio, excluding StreamingBench’s audio-dependent Omni-Source group (§6.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Streaming baseline numbers largely come from separate reports and may use different inference settings; the authors explicitly flag frame-count differences, weakening strict offline comparability (§6.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Apart from the end-token masking experiment, the supplied text provides no component-level ablations for XRoPE, timestamps, gated cross-attention, synthesized data, loss weighting, or curriculum stages. Causal attribution to the full co-design is therefore unresolved.
- **Analyst inference:** Efficiency is measured against one same-backbone comparator on a single H200 under offline serving; hardware generality and latency during actual concurrent frame arrival and generation are not established (Figure 4; §6.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Detailed provenance and composition percentages for much of the large synthesized and collected training corpus are not supplied, limiting independent assessment of coverage, bias, and reconstruction (§§3–4).

## Open research questions

1. **How reliably does MOSS-VL revise or interrupt speech when evidence changes mid-generation?** Existing quantitative benchmarks stop at L4. A next experiment could introduce controlled visual state changes during generated replies and score detection delay, correction accuracy, and unnecessary interruption rate.

2. **Which components produce the proactive-timing gains?** The paper does not report systematic component ablations. A factorial study could remove timestamps, XRoPE, synthetic revision examples, focal reweighting, and gated cache updates while holding training tokens constant.

3. **How robust is interaction timing across stream rates and delays?** Training supports variable sampling, but streaming evaluation is reported at 1 fps. Tests could vary frame rate, network jitter, frame drops, and generation speed while measuring alert latency and false emissions.

4. **Can reasoning and document performance improve without sacrificing real-time latency?** The model underperforms on MMMU and several document benchmarks. A controlled reasoning-oriented post-training stage could measure changes in those suites alongside proactive scores and TTFT.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, training stages, benchmark scores, and stated evaluation boundaries because the supplied text is complete and detailed.

Material missing information includes full training-data provenance and mixture proportions, compute and training-duration costs, broad component ablations, statistical uncertainty for benchmark scores, quantitative L5 evaluation, multilingual robustness, safety evaluation, and latency measurements across additional hardware or genuinely concurrent real-time workloads.
