# A Plug-and-Play 2D Motion Interface for Real-World Motion Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15984) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15984)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors align monocular 2D motion features to the latent space of frozen, 3D-pretrained motion language models, largely matching clean-3D captioning performance and outperforming estimated-3D inputs in their real-video evaluation when paired with a lightweight adapter.

## Problem and motivation

Motion language models (MoLMs) are commonly trained on rich 3D motion representations, but accurately recovering equivalent joint positions, rotations, and global motion from monocular video is difficult. This creates a deployment gap: real applications usually provide noisy monocular observations rather than motion-capture-quality 3D data (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

The authors question whether motion understanding actually requires direct 3D input, while explicitly limiting their claim to a setting where 2D features are aligned to a pretrained 3D latent space—not claiming that 3D-specific information is generally unnecessary (Introduction, p. 2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Plug-and-play 2D interface:** A trainable 2D encoder maps 68-D features from 13 COCO-compatible joints into a frozen MoLM tokenizer’s continuous latent space. An L1 feature-matching loss aligns its output with the corresponding 3D VQ-VAE encoder output; inference reuses the existing codebook and language model without modifying either (Secs. 3.2–3.4; Fig. 2).

2. **View-robust representation:** Training motions are projected orthographically after randomized yaw and pitch, with clip-scale, translation, and dataset-level normalization (Sec. 3.2; Supplement A.2).

3. **Cross-model evaluation:** The interface is tested with TM2T, MotionGPT, and MG-MotionLLM on motion captioning, plus MG-MotionLLM on detailed motion-to-text generation (Sec. 4; Tables 1–2).

4. **Real-video adaptation and evaluation:** The authors collect 132 monocular imitation videos and train a 0.3M-parameter residual MLP adapter on pseudo-real pairs obtained by rendering AMASS motions and running ViTPose. Only the adapter is optimized while the 2D encoder remains frozen (Secs. 5.1–5.2; Fig. 4; Supplement E).

These results support the authors’ claim that a frozen 3D-pretrained latent space can be accessed effectively from clean 2D skeletons and, with adaptation, from their evaluated real-video pipeline. They do not establish that 2D motion universally replaces 3D information, that results transfer to other tokenizer families or motion tasks, or that the real-video gains generalize beyond this small imitation dataset and the selected estimators.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The alignment experiment does not establish that depth, joint rotations, or other 3D-specific information are unnecessary in general (Introduction, p. 2).
- The real-video adapter can smooth away fine-grained trajectories and actions, producing over-general captions; reported failures include losing an S-shaped walking path and confusing hands raised near the face with clapping (Sec. 5.3; Fig. 5).
- Reference 3D clips are prompts for imitation, not ground-truth 3D motion of the recorded performances. Imitation errors therefore weaken comparisons against the reference captions (Secs. 5.1, 5.3).
- Human verification found only 86.4% of video-caption pairs semantically consistent (Sec. 5.1; Supplement D).
- MotionGPT’s 2D-from-scratch baseline was omitted because the authors could not reproduce stable training (Table 1; Supplement A.3).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The real-video evidence is narrow: 132 videos from 10 participants, recorded using one stated phone model and evaluated with specific 2D/3D estimators. Broader camera, environment, occlusion, activity, and population robustness remains untested.
- Statistical significance is not reported. Clean-data results include three-run variability for the proposed method, but comparable variance for 3D and scratch baselines is unavailable.
- Baseline comparability is imperfect: TM2T’s VQ-VAE architecture was modified and retrained, while official checkpoints were used for MotionGPT and MG-MotionLLM (Supplement A.3).
- The adapter-architecture ablation shows mixed metric winners rather than uniform superiority of the chosen MLP; no ablation isolates confidence scores, pseudo-real rendering, or adapter smoothing (Table S2).
- The study evaluates motion-to-text only, so the interface’s suitability for text-to-motion or other MoLM capabilities is not established (Sec. 3.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can fine-grained trajectories survive real-video adaptation?**  
   This remains open because the adapter loses path shape and subtle hand actions. A next experiment could add trajectory- or temporal-detail-preserving losses and evaluate action-part and path-specific annotations against the current L1 adapter.

2. **How well does the interface handle uncontrolled videos?**  
   The collected set is small and based on instructed imitation. Testing on larger naturally occurring videos with varied cameras, occlusion, clothing, crowding, and motion types would measure external validity.

3. **Which 3D cues are irrecoverably absent from the aligned 2D representation?**  
   Higher MPJPE and the authors’ scope caveat leave this unresolved. Controlled paired motions differing only in depth, body orientation, or global trajectory could quantify which distinctions collapse under 2D projection.

4. **Does the interface transfer beyond VQ-VAE motion-to-text models?**  
   The method assumes a discrete tokenizer and evaluates only comprehension. Experiments with alternative continuous or residual tokenizers and generation tasks could test whether plug-and-play alignment is architecture- and task-general.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, settings, and numerical results because the supplied text includes the full main paper and supplement.

Material missing or unclear: statistical significance tests; metric units for MPJPE/PAMPJPE; full per-run baseline variability; whether the new real-video dataset is publicly released and under what terms; detailed participant distribution and recording conditions; and ablations isolating confidence input, view randomization, normalization, pseudo-real data scale, and individual adapter effects.
