# GenRouter: Unified Workflow Routing for Agentic Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16721) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16721)


## One-sentence takeaway

GenRouter selects prompt-specific workflow–generator pairs from a standardized agentic image-generation space, matching heavyweight static pipelines’ quality while substantially reducing agentic execution cost and latency.

## Problem and motivation

Agentic image-generation systems add retrieval, reasoning, layout construction, verification, and refinement to text-to-image generation, but typically use isolated, fixed workflows. The authors identify two consequences: fragmented capabilities that are difficult to combine, and “compute-mismatch,” where simple prompts incur the same expensive processing as complex ones. Their goal is therefore to route each prompt to a workflow that balances visual quality, monetary cost, and latency (Introduction; Eq. 1, §§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **GenCanvas:** An open-source, modular workflow space comprising eight primitives—rewrite, decompose, search, reason, skill, verify, refine, and sketch—and nine bounded templates spanning direct generation, grounding, structural reasoning, and iterative refinement (Eq. 2; §§4.1–4.3; Table 1).

- **GenRouter:** A router over joint workflow–generator plans. It extracts a seven-dimensional prompt-demand signature, prunes incompatible or unnecessarily heavy plans, estimates quality/cost/latency using trajectory and route memories, removes Pareto-dominated plans, and maximizes a weighted utility (Eqs. 3–7; §§5.1–5.3).

- **Experience-driven adaptation:** Execution traces are distilled into instance-level and bucket-level memories, allowing routing estimates to evolve without router retraining. Cold start exhaustively evaluates valid plans for 10 benchmark-sampled calibration prompts (§5.2; Appendix §B.1).

- **Unified evaluation infrastructure:** GenCanvas standardizes primitive traces, model interfaces, benchmark scoring, token/API accounting, and latency measurement (Appendix §§A.1–A.3).

These results support the authors’ claim that prompt-specific workflow routing can preserve or improve benchmark alignment while cutting orchestration overhead relative to fixed agentic pipelines. They do not establish lower total deployment cost or total latency because image-generator inference is explicitly excluded from cost and latency measurements (Appendix §C.1). The experiments also do not establish that the same gains hold with different profiler, primitive, search, or verifier backends.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Evaluation omits terminal generation overhead:** Cost and latency cover routing and primitive execution but exclude image-generator inference, so the reported savings are not end-to-end system savings (Appendix §C.1).

- **Target-benchmark calibration:** Cold start uses 10 prompts sampled from the target benchmark and exhaustively executes valid candidates. This weakens a strict interpretation of fully unseen deployment and adds an unreported initialization expense to practical use (Appendix §B.1).

- **Author claim exceeds direct evidence:** The authors claim generalization “without catastrophic forgetting,” but the supplied experiment reports transfer and accumulated-experience results, not an explicit forgetting evaluation (§6.4).

- **Analyst inference — backend dependence:** Main configurations use Qwen3.5-4B for profiling, Kimi K2.5 for general primitives, and Serper for search. Performance and cost could change materially with other components; no cross-backend primitive study is reported (§6.1).

- **Analyst inference — normalized mixed-set validity:** The 500-prompt mixed set uses hand-specified weights to combine heterogeneous official scores. The text does not explain how these weights were derived or validate that the resulting aggregate utility reflects user preferences (Appendix §C.1, Table 4).

- **Analyst inference — comparison scope:** Baselines share Kimi K2.5 but retain their workflow topologies. This improves backbone comparability, yet it may not reproduce each baseline’s original best configuration (§6.1; Appendix §C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the efficiency gains persist under true end-to-end accounting?** Generator inference is excluded. A follow-up should measure wall-clock latency, energy, and total monetary cost—including all generated or edited images—for every routed plan.

2. **How well does routing transfer without target-benchmark calibration?** Cold start uses target-distribution prompts. An experiment could initialize exclusively from unrelated benchmarks and evaluate a held-out domain with no calibration or online updates.

3. **How robust is routing to component substitutions?** The reported router is tied experimentally to particular profiler, primitive, search, and generator backends. A factorial study could swap these components and measure plan-selection stability, quality, and cost.

4. **Are mixed-set score weights well calibrated to human preferences?** Their derivation is not supplied. A next experiment could learn or validate weights using blinded human pairwise judgments across quality–cost–latency trade-offs.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method structure and reported experiments because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material missing or unclear: derivation of mixed-set weights; exact sample counts for the five headline benchmarks; statistical uncertainty or repeated-run variance; complete numeric component-ablation results; cold-start cost; total generator-inclusive cost and latency; licensing/release details beyond the repository link; and human-evaluation results.
