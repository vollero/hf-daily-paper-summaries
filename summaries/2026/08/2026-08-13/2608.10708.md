# Self-Geometry: GT-Free and Plug-and-Play Test-Time Adaptation for Geometrically Consistent 3D Vision Foundation Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10708) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10708)


## One-sentence takeaway

Self-Geometry uses externally matched 2D correspondences to LoRA-adapt frozen 3D vision foundation models per scene, generally improving pose and geometry estimates across six backbones and four benchmarks, though gains are not universal and adaptation is not real-time.

## Problem and motivation

Feed-forward 3D vision foundation models predict camera pose, depth, and pointmaps efficiently, but their pretraining omits costly explicit multi-view optimization, allowing geometrically inconsistent predictions. Prior test-time methods rely mainly on implicit agreement between model outputs and reportedly offer limited corrections when the pretrained model is inaccurate. The authors seek GT-free, architecture-agnostic test-time supervision that directly constrains multi-view geometry without full bundle adjustment or an external training dataset (Sections I and II).

## Approach and contributions

- **Correspondence-based explicit supervision:** LightGlue matches every input-view pair once, and filtered 2D correspondences serve as pseudo-labels. A point-to-point Multi-View Consistency loss jointly supervises pose and depth, while a depth-independent epipolar loss constrains pose (Section III-B, Eqs. 1–3).
- **Geometric Disentanglement Optimization:** Because the two losses can produce conflicting parameter gradients, the MVC gradient is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the epipolar-loss gradient (Section III-B, Eq. 4).
- **Efficient view sampling:** Frame Angular-Neighbor selects a target and angularly diverse source views using scene-scale-invariant SO(3) geodesic distances and angular bins (Section III-C, Eqs. 5–6).
- **Lightweight per-scene adaptation:** Only LoRA parameters inserted into attention QKV weights are optimized; the backbone remains frozen. Training uses 50 AdamW iterations plus three auxiliary regularizers, robust losses, and dynamic loss weighting (Section III-D; Section IV-A; Supplement S.III–S.V).
- **Breadth of evaluation:** The method is applied to VGGT, π³, and four DA3 sizes on 7Scenes, ETH3D, ScanNet++, and HiRoom (Section IV).

The evidence supports gains across multiple architectures, scales, datasets, and two evaluation tasks, with component studies indicating that loss combination, gradient handling, filtering, and target-view selection matter. It does **not** establish universal improvement: several model–dataset metrics decline, and some mean gains are small. The supplied results also do not provide uncertainty estimates or statistical tests, so the stability of small differences is unknown.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Performance depends on LightGlue correspondences; repetitive textures, textureless surfaces, limited overlap, and difficult wide-baseline changes weaken supervision (Supplement S.VI).
- **Author-stated:** Per-scene adaptation takes minutes and remains far from real time (Supplement S.VI).
- **Analyst inference:** “GT-free” describes adaptation supervision, not the pretrained VFMs or evaluation, which rely on pretrained models and benchmark ground truth (Sections II-D and IV-A).
- **Analyst inference:** TCO is evaluated with VFM-predicted poses rather than GT pose priors and is reimplemented for unsupported backbones; its severe π³ collapse may therefore reflect this altered setting as well as method robustness (Sections IV-A–IV-B).
- **Analyst inference:** Component ablations are limited to VGGT on ETH3D, so their conclusions are not directly validated across the other five backbones or three datasets (Section V).
- **Analyst inference:** Matching all view pairs introduces an initialization dependency that may scale poorly with view count; the supplied text reports timing but no asymptotic or large-view stress test beyond the stated benchmark settings (Section III-B; Table VII).
- **Evidence-quality risk:** Some extracted table cells contain malformed repeated text, and the source is explicitly truncated. The clearly readable rows and narrative claims are usable, but complete verification of every table entry is not possible.

## Open research questions

1. **Can adaptation remain effective when correspondence quality is poor?** This remains open because matcher failure is an acknowledged limitation. A next experiment could stratify results by verified match precision, overlap, and texture level while comparing alternative matchers or confidence-aware objectives.
2. **Can the pipeline approach real-time operation?** Current scene adaptation takes minutes. A next experiment could reduce matching pairs and TTA iterations, then measure the accuracy–latency curve on identical hardware.
3. **Does each component generalize across backbones and domains?** Ablations cover only VGGT on ETH3D. A factorial ablation across at least one small and one large DA3 model on indoor and outdoor benchmarks would test transferability.
4. **How can regressions on already-strong predictions be prevented?** Several VGGT and DA3 settings decline. A next experiment could evaluate a correspondence-quality or checkpoint-improvement gate that retains the frozen prediction when adaptation confidence is low.
