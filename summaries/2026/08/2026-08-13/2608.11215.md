# Poor Man&#x27;s Agentic Modeling: Simulating Large LLM-Agent Societies on a Laptop

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11215) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11215)


## One-sentence takeaway

Low-parameter behavioral clones can reproduce macroscopic LLM-society behavior cheaply when perception and memory admit an appropriate closure, while heterogeneous, local, curved, or long-memory responses require richer surrogates and can impose non-vanishing errors.

## Problem and motivation

Large LLM-agent simulations are costly, especially for studying population-size scaling, although their research questions usually concern aggregate phenomena rather than individual cognition. Existing simulation and coarse-graining work does not provide a validated criterion for when cheap behavioral surrogates preserve those phenomena. The paper argues that agents’ perception structure and memory determine whether averaging is valid and what closure is required (Sections 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- Introduces an interaction-order × memory taxonomy that predicts, before fitting, whether scalar-surrogate error should vanish, plateau, or grow with population size; response curvature and shared-driver fluctuations refine this classification (Section 3, Figure 1).
- Presents a workflow that screens behavior-free observables, elicits genuine LLM decisions, fits 2–12-parameter behavioral clones, estimates error floors and knees, and evaluates aggregate behavior without fitting the target observable (Section 4, Algorithm 1).
- Derives solvable results for community-error floors, curvature-induced knees, exact mean-field commutation, and propagation from one-step closure error to trajectory observables (Section 3.1–3.2, Propositions 1–3, Lemma 1).
- Tests the taxonomy on EconAgent, held-out graphs, genuine LLM perception responses, a measured memory kernel, and several named simulations; it also identifies reasoning-sensitive Phillips behavior and interprets consensus group size as a perception threshold (Sections 5.1–5.8; Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The cloned EconAgent Phillips correlation matches the published target within noise. | Published correlation \(-0.619\); clone \(-0.569\pm0.138\), absolute difference \(0.05\). The aggregate target was not fitted. | Section 5.1 |
| EconAgent’s Okun result is not behavior-identifying. | GDP is an affine-invertible function of worker count with \(R^2=0.996\); a behavior-free Bernoulli(0.5) policy gives \(-0.998\), versus the published \(-0.918\). | Section 5.1 |
| Reasoning, more than wording, drives the cloned Phillips effect. | Without reasoning: \(-0.43\) plain and \(+0.04\) amplified; with reasoning: \(-0.73\) and \(-0.66\). The plain-wording reasoning shift is \(0.30\), with difference SE near \(0.04\). | Section 5.3, Table 2 |
| Held-out graph tests follow the predicted cell ordering. | Mean-field error shrinks with \(N\), community error reaches an \(O(1)\) floor, and local error tracks graph degree structure. | Section 5.5 |

| Near-linear responses retain the predicted averaging behavior. | Consumption-head knee \(N^\ast\approx2.5\times10^4\); global and private errors fall as \(N^{-1/2}\), while community error is flat in \(N\) and falls as \(B^{-1/2}\). | Section 5.6, Figure 2 |
| Core perception predictions mostly transfer across models. | Global averaging, community floors, and block-aware repair meet the stated criterion for 12 of 13 traces; gpt-4o-mini improves but misses the factor-of-two repair criterion by \(N=3200\). | Section 5.6 |
| LLM memory is empirically non-Markovian but short-ranged in the tested probe. | \(K(0)=0.27\); the past-interaction tail is 47% of \(K(0)\) and disappears after the first few lags. | Section 5.8, Figure 5 |
| A compact epidemic closure improves fit and runtime on self-generated data. | Mortality RMSE 22 versus 48 for a well-mixed baseline; transmission and fatality recovered within 13% and 6%; runtime 0.34 s versus roughly 400 s GPU forward time per county. | Appendix E.2, Figure 9 |
| The LLMTraveler clone reaches the reported equilibrium band. | A two-parameter DeepSeek rule converges to DUE at \(+4.7\%\), within the published \(\pm10\%\) band. | Appendix E.6, Figure 12 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that perception and memory are useful predictors of surrogate behavior across several controlled and reconstructed settings. It does not establish exact reproduction of the original simulations under their original models, prompts, networks, and environments; most named-target results instead match mechanisms, trends, or functional forms using newly elicited decisions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- Author-stated: most named-target evaluations do not run the original target models or reproduce their exact published numbers; that stronger test remains open (Section 7; Appendix E.1).
- Author-stated: the commutation-error decomposition is an organizing heuristic rather than a theorem, and its constants are not bounded (Sections 3 and 7).
- Author-stated: the main LLM evidence relies heavily on DeepSeek, while sibling models produce materially different macroscopic values (Sections 5.3 and 7).
- Author-stated: repository history alone cannot prove that preregistrations preceded observation; the external archive timestamps the deposit, not necessarily the prediction (Section 7).
- Author-stated: one-step defect predicts long-horizon observable error cleanly only under non-expanding dynamics; expanding dynamics may amplify errors geometrically (Section 3.2).
- Author-stated: TwinMarket’s stylized facts depend on an unelicited market-impact coupling, showing that cloning agent behavior alone can underdetermine aggregate outcomes (Appendix E.7).
- Analyst inference: breadth is substantial, but heterogeneous reconstruction choices—new models, assumed graphs, and self-generated environments—limit direct cross-target comparability. This follows from Sections 7 and E.1–E.8 rather than a controlled common benchmark.
- Analyst inference: some post-hoc curvature analysis is quantitatively persuasive but is weaker evidence than the preregistered predictions it was introduced to explain (Section 5.6).

## Open research questions

1. Can the method reproduce exact published aggregate values using each target’s original model, prompt, environment, and graph? This remains open by the authors’ account; a next experiment would rerun several targets unchanged and compare surrogate and full-LLM trajectories across \(N\).

2. How reliable is the taxonomy near expanding or critical dynamics? Lemma 1 permits geometric amplification there; a next experiment could estimate local Lipschitz factors and compare one-step, trajectory, and final-observable errors across longer horizons.

3. Can market or environmental mechanisms be inferred jointly with agent policies? TwinMarket shows that agent-only elicitation leaves price-impact strength unidentified; a next experiment could jointly fit behavioral and market parameters using held-out micro- and macro-observables.

4. Does measured LLM memory generalize across models, prompts, and tasks? The reported kernel comes from a controlled DeepSeek attitude-update probe; a next experiment could repeat the lag intervention across model families and named long-memory simulations.

## Confidence and missing information

Confidence: **high** for the paper’s stated methods, numerical claims, and caveats because the supplied text is extensive and includes the main results and appendices.

Material missing information includes full numerical contents of Tables 1–3, detailed uncertainty and replication counts for several figures, exact held-out graph error values, complete prompt and feature specifications for every named target, and independent verification of the released code, traces, and preregistration timing.
