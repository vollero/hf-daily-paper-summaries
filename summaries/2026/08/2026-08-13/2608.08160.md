# Can LLM Agents Stick to the Script? A Benchmark for Long-Horizon Consistency in Interactive Narratives

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08160) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors introduce an auditable benchmark for preserving facts and plot commitments during long interactive narratives, finding that all six tested LLMs remain brittle, with the strongest retaining only 42% of environments without conflict after 20 turns.

## Problem and motivation

LLM narrator agents can produce fluent stories but may contradict established facts, reveal information prematurely, ignore player actions, or bypass mandatory plot events during free-form interaction. The authors argue that existing evaluations emphasize perceived coherence or engagement and do not reproducibly test whether specific logical obligations survive adversarial interventions over many turns (Sections 1 and 2; Appendix A).

They formalize this gap as **Narrative Commitment Preservation (NCP)**: maintaining an explicit world state and satisfying non-optional narrative constraints while responding to unconstrained player actions.

## Approach and contributions

- **Task formalization:** NCP represents each interaction using an evolving fact ledger, fixed narrative commitments, an ordered reference trajectory, and trajectory progress. Any confirmed fact, commitment, or player-input conflict terminates the episode (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Benchmark:** NCP-Bench contains 100 manually curated movie–character environments spanning 18 primary genres. Each includes initial facts, invariant/ordering/achievement commitments, and trajectory nodes generated from a synopsis and manually reviewed (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figure 3).
- **Auditable evaluation:** Fixed-prompt LLM components check conflicts, extract fact updates, track trajectory progress, and evaluate commitments separately from the narrator being tested. A second pass confirms detected conflicts (Section 4.1; Algorithm 1).
- **Empirical study:** Six LLM narrator backbones are tested against an adversarial player, supplemented by auditor-sensitivity, memory-augmentation, natural-player, and one-environment human-baseline analyses (Sections 5.1–5.6; Appendix B).
- **Released assets:** The authors state that data, code, and prompt templates are publicly released (Section 1; Appendix H).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Long-horizon survival is poor | GPT-5.2, the strongest model by survival, retains 42% of the 100 environments without detected conflict after 20 adversarial turns. | Figure 4; Section 5.2 |
| Very few runs remain conflict-free to the cap | Across 600 runs—six models over 100 movies—3.5% reach 100 turns without an explicit conflict; most of these still do not satisfy all commitments. | Section 5.2 |
| GPT-5.2 lasts longest but progresses slowly | GPT-5.2 averages 32.92 turns, with 9.94% trajectory progress and 11.22% satisfied commitments. DeepSeek-V3.2 averages 15.88 turns but reaches the best trajectory progress, 15.40%, and satisfied rate, 13.42%. | Table 1, Part I |
| Fact conflicts dominate | Fact-conflict rates range from 40% for GPT-5.2 to 68% for Qwen3-235B-A22B; GPT-5.2 also has the lowest commitment-conflict rate, 24%. | Table 1, Part II; Section 5.2 |
| Auditor choice changes strictness but preserves broad patterns | For GPT-4o-mini, three auditors report fact-conflict rates of 60%, 64%, and 67%; commitment conflicts of 26%, 32%, and 41%; and pairwise Pearson correlations of 0.9628–0.9866. | Table 2; Section 5.4 |
| Human review found a limited auditor error mode | Experts reviewed 100 GPT-4o-mini final results and disputed four fact-conflict false positives; none reported errors in commitment- or player-input-conflict outputs. | Section 5.4 |
| Memory augmentation trades off failure modes | Under the same GPT-5.4-mini auditor, HiAgent raises average turns from 22.16 to 30.05 and reduces commitment conflicts from 26% to 4%, but raises player-input conflicts from 13% to 38% and reduces all-commitment successes from two to zero. | Table 3; Section 5.5 |
| Adversarial input is not the only source of difficulty | Natural inputs raise GPT-4o-mini’s average length from 22.16 to 46.08 turns and conflict-free runs from 5 to 19, but produce zero all-commitment successes and still leave most interactions ending in conflict. | Table 4; Sections 5.6 and 6 |
| A human succeeds on one showcased environment | On *Iron Man*, the resolve-all human session handles 19 interventions, reaches 13/13 trajectory nodes, and satisfies 7/12 commitments—including all achievement commitments—without conflict. | Table 5; Appendix B |

The evidence establishes that, under this benchmark’s LLM-driven adversarial-player and auditing protocol, the tested narrator models frequently lose logical consistency and rarely complete all required achievements. It also shows that longer survival does not necessarily imply greater plot progress.

It does **not** establish equivalent failure rates for human players, nonlinear stories, other narrative sources, or non-narrative agent domains. The single human environment is evidence of feasibility for that case, not a representative human performance estimate.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** LLM auditors can misjudge ambiguous state transitions despite fixed prompts, structured outputs, and conflict confirmation (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** Provider-side nondeterminism prevents exact output replication under identical decoding settings (Sections 5.1 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** The framework covers single-threaded, chronological narratives rather than branching timelines, flashbacks, or parallel perspectives (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** One adversarial simulator may not reflect the intervention patterns or performance observed with real users (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** Movie-derived specifications may inherit source-material biases, and logical consistency does not measure creativity, emotional resonance, cultural appropriateness, or overall narrative quality (Impact Statement; Section 6).
- **Analyst inference:** The three-auditor study evaluates only GPT-4o-mini, so auditor robustness across the other narrator models is not demonstrated (Section 5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The human baseline uses only the *Iron Man* environment, making broad human–model comparisons unsupported (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Genre estimates for categories with one to three movies are unstable; the authors explicitly caution about these small samples (Table 7).

## Open research questions

1. **Can explicit state management improve consistency without losing player intent?** HiAgent reduces commitment conflicts but sharply increases player-input conflicts. A next experiment could compare memory representations that retain both structured commitments and verbatim recent actions across all 100 environments.

2. **How reliable is evaluation across narrator models and ambiguous transitions?** Auditor sensitivity is tested only on GPT-4o-mini, while human disputes concentrate on nuanced fact changes. A larger blinded human study could validate multiple auditors across all narrator backbones and conflict categories.

3. **Does NCP generalize beyond linear movie narratives?** The current fact and commitment models assume chronological, single-threaded stories. A next benchmark could introduce controlled branching timelines, flashbacks, and conditional commitments.

4. **How do real players change the observed failure profile?** The adversarial and synthetic cooperative players cover only two generated behavior styles. A preregistered study with human players could compare conflict rates, achievement completion, and intervention types against both simulators.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark design and numerical results because the supplied text includes the main methods, tables, appendices, and prompts.

Material missing or unresolved information includes per-environment raw outputs, confidence intervals or repeated-run variance, inter-annotator agreement for human result verification, aggregate human performance beyond one environment, computational cost, and evidence that the benchmark transfers to non-narrative domains.
