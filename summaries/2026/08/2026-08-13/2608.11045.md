# ReRound: Reconstructive Rounding to Resolve Midpoint Ambiguity in Calibration-Free LLM Quantization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11045) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11045)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReRound uses model-specific diffusion reconstructions and weight-only spectral selection to revise a small fraction of ambiguous RTN assignments, consistently improving average zero-shot accuracy for evaluated small LLMs at 3- and 4-bit weight precision without changing inference.

## Problem and motivation

Standard round-to-nearest (RTN) becomes ambiguous when a weight lies near the midpoint between adjacent quantized integers: rounding up and down have nearly equal scalar error, yet the collective choices can alter a layer’s matrix structure. The authors argue that prior PTQ methods improve quantization parameters or identify opportunities to depart from RTN but do not determine alternative assignments using a learned prior from the model’s weights (Sections 1–2).

The goal is therefore to improve the final floor-or-ceiling assignments after scales, zero-points, group size, and quantized layers have already been fixed—without activation samples, calibration text, or downstream labels.

## Approach and contributions

- **Conditional weight reconstruction:** For each LLM, ReRound trains a conditional diffusion model on paired full-precision and stochastically quantized \(64\times64\) weight patches. At inference, deterministic low-bit RTN patches condition reconstructions that serve only as rounding guidance (Sections 3.2 and C; Figures 1–2).
- **Midpoint-aware revision rule:** A reconstructed weight can reverse RTN only if it favors the opposite adjacent integer and its deviation satisfies a position-dependent tolerance. The tolerance is largest around the midpoint and decreases toward interval boundaries (Section 3.3; Equations 12–15; Figure 3).
- **Matrix-level candidate selection:** Sweeping the tolerance produces candidate integer matrices. ReRound selects the dequantized candidate whose leading singular values most closely match the full-precision matrix; RTN is included as the zero-tolerance candidate (Section 3.4; Equations 16–18; Figure 4).
- **Deployment-preserving refinement:** Only integer assignments change; the quantization parameters, low-bit representation, and inference procedure remain fixed. The method can also refine scales produced by another PTQ method (Sections 3.4 and 4.5).

The results establish consistent improvements over a tightly matched RTN baseline in the evaluated small-LLM, W3/W4 settings. The ablation supports the combined design, and SINQ-scale results show compatibility with one alternative parameterization. They do not establish benefits for larger LLMs, other model modalities, activation quantization, hardware latency or energy, or downstream tasks beyond the reported accuracy and perplexity evaluations. No uncertainty intervals or significance tests are reported.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Candidate quality remains constrained by fixed scales and zero-points; joint optimization is left open (Section 5).
- Only small LLMs are evaluated, despite the proposed applicability beyond LLMs (Section 5).
- Every pretrained model requires a separate diffusion model, making many-model scaling expensive (Section 5; Table 6).
- Weight-space spectral selection may not choose the best candidate for every downstream task (Section 5).
- \(64\times64\) patch reconstruction may miss long-range and cross-layer dependencies (Section 5).

**Analyst inferences from the supplied evidence:**

- The principal component ablation uses only OLMo 2 1B, so component necessity across other architectures is not established (Table 4).
- Hyperparameters—including tolerance candidate sets and \(\beta\)—vary by model, but the supplied text does not describe a tuning protocol or held-out selection procedure; this complicates assessment of selection bias (Table 8).
- Diffusion inference uses deterministic RTN conditions whereas training uses stochastic-rounding conditions; the authors explicitly note that the reconstruction is neither exact recovery nor necessarily an exact conditional mean (Section J.2).
- Accuracy evaluation covers four zero-shot tasks, while perplexity is reported only for the SINQ-scale Qwen3 experiment; broader behavioral preservation is untested (Sections 4.1 and 4.5).
- Runtime is reported, but peak memory, energy, end-to-end quantization cost relative to baselines, and deployment throughput measurements are absent.
- Figure 5 discusses eight models, whereas supplemental Table 10 additionally reports Falcon 3-1B-Base; the reason for its exclusion from that figure is not stated.

## Open research questions

1. **Can reconstruction-guided rounding be optimized jointly with scales and zero-points?** Fixed parameters restrict the candidate set (Section 5). A next experiment could jointly alternate scale optimization, reconstruction-guided proposals, and rounding selection against matched RTN and PTQ baselines.

2. **Can one diffusion prior transfer across models?** Per-model training and reconstruction dominate offline cost (Table 6). A direct test could train on one model or model family and evaluate transferred guidance on unseen architectures, comparing accuracy and cost with separately trained priors.

3. **Would broader structural selection outperform leading-singular-value matching?** The spectral criterion is weight-only and may not align with every task (Section 5). An experiment could compare spectral matching with cross-layer criteria or a small calibration-based output criterion while holding candidate assignments fixed.

4. **Does ReRound scale beyond small LLMs and weight-only W3/W4 quantization?** The current evaluation is limited to small LLMs and 16-bit activations. Testing larger models, additional bit widths, and joint weight–activation quantization would measure scalability and scope.
