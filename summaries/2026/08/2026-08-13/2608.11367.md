# Gaze Target Estimation Anywhere with Concepts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11367) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11367)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors introduce promptable gaze target estimation, a dataset of 119,525 concept-annotated samples, and an end-to-end model that jointly identifies a text- or coordinate-specified person and predicts their gaze target without requiring an externally supplied head box.

## Problem and motivation

Gaze-target estimators commonly depend on preprocessing steps that detect a person and localize their head, pose, or depth before estimating gaze. Errors in these stages can cascade, especially in crowded scenes, poor lighting, or images of children (Introduction; Figure 1). Existing end-to-end approaches also cannot select a particular person through a natural-language query (Related Work; Supplementary §8.1).

The proposed Promptable Gaze Target Estimation (PGE) task instead asks a model to infer the gaze target of a person identified by a short text description or spatial coordinate. The intended benefits are flexible subject selection and removal of externally generated localization inputs (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **New task:** PGE maps an RGB image plus a text or coordinate-based prompt directly to a gaze heatmap for the specified subject. Text prompts can describe appearance, location, pose, and action (§3.1).

- **GazeAnywhere model:** Frozen image and text encoders feed projected tokens into a trainable transformer detector. Separate heads predict a \(64\times64\) gaze heatmap, the prompted subject’s head box, and whether the gaze target is in-frame (§3.2; Figure 2). Training combines heatmap BCE, presence focal loss, and head-box L1/GIoU loss (§3.3).

- **Gaze-Co dataset and benchmark:** A human-in-the-loop data engine augments GazeFollow, VAT, and ChildPlay with concept phrases, yielding exactly 119,525 training samples according to the supplement; the main text rounds this to 120K (§4; Supplementary §10.1). Converted public test sets and a private Child-SC out-of-domain set support evaluation (§§4.2, 10.2–10.3).

- **Applied system:** The GazeAnywhere Agent combines speech transcription, an MLLM, GazeAnywhere, and smart-glasses video to answer higher-level questions about gaze shifts and eye contact (§5.5; Figures 5–6).

These results support the authors’ qualitative claim that joint prompt-conditioned localization and gaze estimation is competitive across their selected benchmarks. They do not permit independent assessment of effect sizes, statistical reliability, latency gains, or the magnitude of improvements because the supplied extraction omits all numerical cells from Tables 1–9. The qualitative examples in Figures 4 and 9 illustrate successes but do not establish population-level robustness.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Missing result values:** The retrieved tables contain placeholders rather than cells, preventing verification of the claimed state of the art, “on par” prompting performance, ablation effects, agent MAE, or computational trade-offs.

- **Private OOD evaluation:** Child-SC cannot be inspected or independently reproduced from the supplied material because it is private and IRB-restricted (§10.3).

- **Annotation dependence:** Most concepts were generated and initially verified by Gemini 2.5 Pro, with humans spot-checking passed batches until observed error was at most 1% (§4.1). The paper does not supply sample sizes, confidence intervals, or inter-annotator agreement for this estimate.

- **Analyst inference — demographic validity:** Labels such as man, woman, boy, and girl are perceived visual categories rather than verified attributes (§4.1; §10.1). Their use may introduce annotator or model bias, and the supplied text reports no subgroup performance analysis.

- **Analyst inference — benchmark inheritance:** Gaze-Co draws entirely from three existing gaze datasets after filtering (§4), so its diversity remains bounded by those sources and by filters excluding small, oversized, or blurry heads.

- **Analyst inference — prompt scope:** Although the paper uses “open-vocabulary,” training concepts follow four constrained fields and a restricted set of apparent person categories (§§3.1, 4.1). Robustness to complex, ambiguous, multilingual, adversarial, or nonstandard descriptions is not established.

- **Small applied study:** The AR-agent evaluation uses only 10 videos (§5.5), and the supplied text gives no participant composition, duration, uncertainty, or annotation protocol.

- **Resource demands:** Training used four NVIDIA H100 GPUs, while inference used one L40S (§5.1). Performance on lower-resource hardware is not established.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is PGE to unconstrained or ambiguous language?** The evaluated concepts follow a structured appearance/location/pose/action template. A next experiment could test paraphrases, omitted attributes, multilingual prompts, and intentionally ambiguous descriptions while separately measuring subject-localization and gaze errors.

2. **Does performance remain reliable across demographic and scene subgroups?** The dataset reports apparent-category distributions but no subgroup accuracy. A next experiment could stratify AUC, L2, AP, and head-localization accuracy by perceived age category, crowd density, head size, lighting, and in/out status.

3. **How much of the gain comes from joint localization rather than stronger pretrained encoders?** Loss and encoder ablations are reported, but the supplied text does not expose their values or a controlled parameter-matched decomposition. A next experiment could compare joint and two-stage systems using the same frozen encoders, training data, capacity, and oracle versus predicted head boxes.

4. **Does the system support clinically meaningful longitudinal measurements?** Child-SC and the 10-video agent study show feasibility but not clinical validity. A prospective study could compare gaze-event estimates with blinded expert annotations across participants and sessions, reporting uncertainty and test–retest reliability.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The task, architecture, data pipeline, datasets, metrics, and experimental design are described in substantial detail. Material missing information includes every numerical entry in Tables 1–9, confidence intervals or significance tests, per-baseline performance and latency, agent MAE values, parameter counts, annotation-agreement statistics, subgroup results, detailed Child-SC participant characteristics, and direct evidence supporting clinical utility.
