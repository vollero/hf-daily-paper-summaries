# MBA: Multimodal Benchmark and Agents for Real-World Business Ideation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11616) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11616)


## One-sentence takeaway

MBA introduces a 30K-example image–text benchmark and two 7B multimodal agents whose SFT–GRPO training improves automated business-idea scores—especially novelty, differentiation, and market size—though validation relies primarily on a single MLLM judge rather than humans.

## Problem and motivation

Existing business-ideation systems operate mainly on text, particularly difficult-to-collect patent documents, even though real-world opportunities may depend on visual details that captions omit or cannot adequately express. The authors argue that this modality gap limits idea specificity and originality: caption-only baselines trail multimodal systems across the six evaluation dimensions, while general-purpose multimodal models still tend toward conventional ideas (Introduction, pp. 2–3; Figure 2).

The work therefore addresses two gaps: the absence of a dedicated benchmark for visually grounded business ideation and the absence of specialized multimodal agents trained for both creative and feasible idea generation.

## Approach and contributions

1. **MBA-Bench:** A benchmark containing 30,000 image–caption–question–idea quadruplets derived from 2,000 images across six visual domains. Each image receives three business questions and five GPT-4o-generated reference ideas per question (Section 3.1; Tables 1 and A1).

2. **Retrieval-grounded construction:** GPT-4o extracts a visually grounded search query, DuckDuckGo retrieves market evidence, and GPT-4o synthesizes structured reference ideas containing title, description, implementation, and differentiation fields (Section 3.1; Figure 3; Algorithm 1).

3. **Two deployment-specific agents:** MBA-b targets settings where evaluation criteria are hidden, optimizing creativity and feasibility; MBA-k targets known criteria and additionally optimizes six disclosed business metrics (Section 3.2; Table 2).

4. **Two-stage training:** Both agents adapt Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct using LoRA-based SFT followed by GRPO with group-relative rewards. Feasibility is grounded through the web-sourced MBA-Library rather than scored solely by an MLLM (Sections 3.2 and 4.1; Algorithms 2–3).

The results establish stronger scores under the paper’s fixed InternVL2.5-78B judge and rubric, with paired tests supporting several MBA-k comparisons. They do not establish equivalent human preference, startup success, commercial viability, or generalization beyond the benchmark: direct human evaluation is explicitly left for future work (Section 4.2). Although the paper says it reports means and standard deviations, Table 3 as supplied displays only means.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Inputs are restricted to images and text, excluding audio, olfactory, tactile, and other potentially useful signals (Section 5).
- Static images do not model motion, behavior, or causal context available in video (Section 5).
- Generation and evaluation ignore entrepreneur-specific constraints such as capital, expertise, location, networks, and risk tolerance (Section 5).
- Direct human evaluation on MBA-Bench remains future work (Section 4.2).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- Evaluation validity depends heavily on a single fixed MLLM judge. Separating the training and evaluation judges reduces direct reward-model reuse but does not demonstrate agreement with human experts (Sections 3.2 and A.3).
- All reported experiments use one random seed and a single run, so training variance is unmeasured (Section 4.1; Appendix A.1–A.2).
- MBA-k directly optimizes the same six disclosed dimensions used at test time; its gains demonstrate adaptation to a known rubric but may not transfer to undisclosed criteria (Table 2; Section 3.2).
- Reference ideas and parts of benchmark construction depend on GPT-4o and retrieved web evidence, creating possible synthetic-data and retrieval-quality biases that are not independently audited in the supplied text (Section 3.1).
- The 100-image test set is small and restricted to six curated domains, despite producing 1,500 prompt-level responses (Algorithm 3; Tables 1 and A2).
- The claim that visual inputs cause downstream improvements is supported by modality comparisons, but the supplied text does not show a fully factorial ablation of image, caption, query, evidence, SFT, and each reward component.
- Table 3 claims standard deviations were computed, but they are not shown in the supplied table; the reported percentage improvements also lack an explicit calculation.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the judge-based gains predict human expert preference?**  
   This remains open because MBA receives no direct human evaluation. A next experiment could collect blinded pairwise rankings from entrepreneurs or investors and measure agreement with each automated metric.

2. **Which training component causes each improvement?**  
   The paper does not isolate SFT, creativity reward, feasibility reward, or individual known-criterion rewards in a complete component ablation. A controlled study could add each component incrementally across several seeds.

3. **How well does MBA generalize beyond its six curated domains?**  
   The test set uses 100 held-out images from the same six source families. Evaluation on newly collected images, unseen industries, videos, and different geographic markets would test distributional transfer.

4. **Can ideation be personalized to real entrepreneurial constraints?**  
   Capital, skills, location, networks, and risk tolerance are absent. A plausible experiment would condition generation on structured entrepreneur profiles and have domain experts rate executability for each profile.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark structure, training pipeline, reported judge scores, statistical comparisons, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material missing or unclear information includes human evaluation of MBA outputs, results across multiple training runs, displayed standard deviations for Table 3, the exact formula behind the headline percentage gains, a complete reward/component ablation, detailed audits of retrieved evidence and synthetic references, and evidence of real-world commercial outcomes.
