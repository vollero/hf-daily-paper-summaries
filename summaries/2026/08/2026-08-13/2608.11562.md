# From Synthesis to Removal: Physics-Grounded Reflection Simulation and Diffusion-Based Video Dereflection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11562) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11562)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors combine controllable reflection-video synthesis, a one-step diffusion restoration model, and a dedicated benchmark, reporting stronger video dereflection quality and faster inference than evaluated frame-wise image baselines.

## Problem and motivation

Reflections in videos obscure the transmitted scene, reduce visual quality, and can impair downstream perception. Video dereflection is harder than single-image removal because reflections and scenes move independently, while frame-wise processing can flicker or alter details.

The authors identify three gaps: scarce aligned reflected/clean video pairs, limited temporally coherent learning-based removal methods, and no dedicated benchmark covering both reference-based and real-world perceptual evaluation (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **S2R-Synthesis:** Composes transmission and reflection structure as lineart conditions, applies six clip-consistent, physics-motivated augmentations, and uses a Wan2.1-based diffusion renderer to produce aligned reflected/clean videos (§3; Fig. 2). The controls approximate planar reflection, roughness blur, thickness ghosting, reflectance, partial coverage, and static reflections (§3.3; Table 7).
- **S2R-Removal:** Adapts a pretrained video diffusion backbone in two stages. Stage I uses LoRA, diffusion supervision, and a residual-derived reflection-intensity target; Stage II performs one-step denoising with reconstruction, SSIM, and reflection-masked depth-consistency losses (§4; Fig. 3).
- **S2R-Bench:** Provides 60 paired, virtually animated sequences for full-reference testing and 50 in-the-wild videos for human evaluation (§5.1; Appendix D).
- **Closed-loop evaluation:** The paper separately tests the removal model, the utility of synthesized training data, individual loss additions, image-domain transfer, and downstream driving perception (§5; Appendices G–H).

The results support the authors’ claims of strong performance against the listed frame-wise image baselines, useful synthetic augmentation, and efficient one-step inference. They do not establish superiority over prior dedicated video methods quantitatively, because those methods lacked available code (§5.2), nor do they establish robustness across a large paired corpus of genuinely captured dynamic videos.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Nested, multilayer reflections have an ambiguous target and can cause residual reflections or over-removal (Appendix I; Fig. 14).
- **Author-stated:** The simulator does not model camera-motion-dependent reflection geometry such as viewpoint-dependent parallax (Appendix I).
- **Analyst inference:** S2R-Ref is not captured video: static paired images are converted to video with shared pan/zoom transformations. It therefore tests temporal processing under controlled virtual motion, not the full geometry of independently moving real transmission and reflection layers (Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative comparisons use image methods frame by frame; prior dedicated video methods receive only qualitative comparisons, limiting the scope of the state-of-the-art claim (§5.2; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** S2R-Ref derives from DRR training data and is manually filtered. Although DAI is excluded for leakage, the supplied text does not discuss possible overlap or tuning advantages for other models (Appendix D.1; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported training-set totals conflict: §5.1 says roughly 21k synthesis samples and 38k removal pairs; Table 4 totals 19,932; Appendix B states 38,826 samples while Table 6 totals 37,596. This weakens reproducibility.
- **Analyst inference:** Table 2 adds PGA variants cumulatively, so it does not isolate each primitive’s independent contribution or interactions.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text gives human scoring criteria and participant count but not inter-rater agreement, participant background, randomization, blinding, or uncertainty estimates.
- **Resource/licensing constraint:** Training used eight A100 GPUs, while several inputs are restricted to non-commercial or academic use (Acknowledgements; §5.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the method handle physically coupled motion and parallax?** The simulator omits viewpoint-dependent reflection geometry. A next experiment could train with rendered camera/glass trajectories and evaluate on aligned, genuinely captured moving-camera sequences.

2. **Which PGA primitive contributes independently?** The cumulative ablation cannot separate marginal effects or interactions. A factorial ablation with matched sample counts could test every primitive alone and selected combinations.

3. **How well does the model generalize to multilayer glass?** The authors show both under- and over-removal in nested reflections. A stratified benchmark with annotated glass layers and separate residual/over-removal measures could quantify this failure mode.

4. **Are the perceptual gains statistically reliable?** No uncertainty or inter-rater agreement is reported. A blinded, randomized study with confidence intervals and agreement statistics would test robustness of the human-ranking result.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported tabular results because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material missing or unclear: reconciled training-set counts; code/model/dataset release status; exact clip lengths and most training hyperparameters; inference hardware and batching details; TC’s precise implementation; confidence intervals or repeated-run variance; human-study randomization, blinding, and agreement; full quantitative comparison with prior video-specific methods; and quantitative evaluation on genuinely paired dynamic real videos.
