# NeuPAT: Neuron-aware Plasticity Allocation Tuning for Language-Preserving MLLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08107) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08107)


## One-sentence takeaway

NeuPAT uses small text and vision probing sets to assign neuron-specific update rules during visual instruction tuning, largely restoring language performance while keeping average multimodal performance near vanilla tuning.

## Problem and motivation

Visual instruction tuning can overwrite language capabilities inherited from LLM pretraining: the default experiment’s 11-benchmark language average falls from 48.99 to 43.92 (Table 1). Existing remedies rely on text replay, architectural changes, or post-training merging, which can add data, complexity, or preservation–adaptation trade-offs.

The authors identify a gap in directly controlling the LLM backbone’s internal adaptation. Their central claim is that neurons differ in modality association and overall importance, so uniform freezing, regularization, or updating fails to use the backbone’s heterogeneous plasticity effectively (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Modality-aware probing:** NeuPAT estimates each neuron’s text- and vision-associated importance from activation magnitude and its output-weight norm, using separate forward-only probing sets (Equation 1; Sections 3.1 and 4.1).
- **Layer-adaptive role allocation:** Within each layer, cumulative importance thresholds define text-important and vision-important sets. Their intersection, differences, and complement produce language-associated, multimodal-adaptive, shared, and reserve roles (Equations 5–6).
- **Role-specific tuning:** Language-associated neurons are frozen; multimodal and reserve neurons receive full updates; shared neurons receive input-side L2 and output-side cosine constraints relative to pretrained parameters (Section 4.3; Equation 7).
- **Empirical contribution:** The paper evaluates the method across six language backbones, 11 language benchmarks, and five vision-language benchmarks, with allocation, update-rule, probing-size, and hyperparameter ablations (Tables 1–12).

These results establish strong average language retention under the tested vision-language pipeline and backbones, with little average multimodal loss. They do not establish causal neuron functions, performance on substantially larger models or other modalities, long-term continual-learning stability, or statistical significance. The abstract’s claim of recovering **94.5%** of degradation is not explicitly derived in the supplied text; the default Table 1 averages instead show a 5.14-point recovery against a 5.07-point loss.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Effectiveness beyond the tested model scales—whose largest listed backbone is 14B—remains unverified (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers vision-language expansion only, not audio, video, embodied interaction, or unified multimodal systems (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** The method addresses a single expansion stage rather than sequential or continual modality acquisition (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Neuron roles are response-based functional approximations, not causal explanations (Section 8).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variation, or significance tests are reported, so the reliability of small differences such as +0.07 multimodal points cannot be assessed.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text specifies probing and training data but does not establish release of code, checkpoints, or exact sampled probing instances, limiting exact reproducibility.
- **Analyst inference:** Hyperparameters are assessed one at a time, and the reported default does not maximize every aggregate metric; robustness to joint changes remains unclear (Tables 8–10).

## Open research questions

1. **Does neuron allocation remain stable at larger scales?** Section 7 leaves substantially larger backbones untested. A next experiment could repeat the probing, role-distribution analysis, and full benchmark evaluation on larger backbones while measuring probing and masking overhead.

2. **Can the same importance formulation preserve capabilities when adding audio or video?** Only text and still-image probing are evaluated. A controlled experiment could replace the vision set with audio or video probes and compare shared versus modality-specific allocation rules.

3. **Does NeuPAT prevent cumulative forgetting across sequential expansions?** The study considers one visual expansion stage. A next experiment could add vision, audio, and video sequentially and evaluate retention after every stage against replay and merging baselines.

4. **Are identified neuron roles causally responsible for preservation?** The authors explicitly describe their assignments as functional approximations. Targeted role swapping, suppression, or matched random interventions could test whether manipulating each identified group produces the predicted language–multimodal trade-off.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, reported benchmark values, and stated scope because the supplied text includes the complete paper, tables, appendix, and training configuration.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variance or significance tests; runtime, memory, and probing overhead relative to baselines; code or checkpoint availability; exact probing samples or random seeds; derivation of the abstract’s 94.5% recovery figure; and evaluations on larger backbones, non-visual modalities, continual learning, safety, fairness, and robustness.
