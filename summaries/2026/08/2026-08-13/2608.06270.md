# The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06270) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06270)


## One-sentence takeaway

A three-level causal audit finds that crop-and-zoom tools improve aggregate accuracy for some multimodal models, but useful visual evidence drives those gains mainly in a calibrated minority while many calls are causally inactive or poorly scheduled.

## Problem and motivation

“Thinking with images” lets multimodal LLMs actively crop and zoom, yet these operations can consume substantially more tokens while producing marginal, absent, or negative accuracy gains. Models may also repeatedly inspect irrelevant regions and overturn answers that direct inference gets right (Introduction; Figure 1).

The authors argue that ordinary accuracy comparisons cannot determine whether returned pixels actually influence an answer: improvements could instead travel through an action-induced shortcut, where emitting a tool-call trace changes the prediction regardless of the returned observation. The missing capability is therefore a causal, step-sensitive audit separating genuine visual grounding from merely performing tool use (§§1–3.2; Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Causal formulation:** The paper models each rollout over the image, query, tool actions, returned observations, and answer, separating direct inference, observation-mediated evidence, and action-induced shortcuts (§§3.1–3.2; Figure 2).

2. **Three-level intervention protocol:** It compares tool-enabled with direct inference at the policy level, corrupts every observation online at the trajectory level, and replaces one observation under a fixed prefix at the step level (§3.3; Figure 3).

3. **Visual Evidence Gain (VEG):** VEG subtracts the answer-preference change induced by a counterfactual crop from that induced by the real crop, estimating the local contribution of visual content while holding the prefix and action fixed (§3.3.3, Equations 3–4).

4. **Failure taxonomy and diagnostic:** A deterministic classifier partitions rollouts into No-call, Calling Without Looking (CWL), Looking Without Planning (LWP), and Calibrated groups using confidence, peak VEG, over-extension, and tool-budget exhaustion (§§4.1–4.2; Algorithm 1).

5. **Gain decomposition:** The authors decompose policy-level accuracy change by diagnostic group and report that the Calibrated subset supplies most positive gain (§4.3, Equation 6; Tables 5 and 20).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that visual observations have sharply different causal utility across models and rollouts, and that aggregate accuracy can conceal inactive or incoherently scheduled calls. The interventions do not establish that outcome-only reinforcement learning causes these failures. Trajectory corruption also combines evidence removal with downstream policy reactions, while VEG measures a local, fixed-readout contrast rather than the full causal effect on unconstrained final-answer generation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only open-source thinking-with-images models are evaluated; extension to closed systems is unresolved because intermediate observations and token probabilities cannot be controlled or inspected (Limitations, “Model access constraints”).

- **Author-stated:** Experiments focus on crop-and-zoom. Segmentation, OCR, video-frame selection, external search, and other visual tools could exhibit different calibration behavior (Limitations, “Limited tool set”).

- **Author-stated:** The outcome-only-RL explanation is a hypothesis, not a result of matched training experiments (Discussion; Limitations, “RL trap as a hypothesis”).

- **Author-stated:** MCQ VEG uses an option-restricted next-token readout. Open-ended VEG has a different, unbounded scale and cannot inherit the MCQ thresholds or classifier without calibration (Appendix D).

- **Author-stated:** Online trajectory corruption measures both loss of evidence and induced reactions such as repeated calls or budget exhaustion; the force-answer study separates only the missing-answer component (§3.3.2; Appendix B.4).

- **Analyst inference:** Tool-enabled and direct policies use different system prompts, so their accuracy difference is an intervention on the full prompted policy, not solely the availability of pixels (Appendix A.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Counterfactual quality depends on random alternative crops; although three crops are averaged per VEG estimate and noise/blank ablations are included, an alternative crop can still contain relevant or misleading content (Appendix A.3; Appendix B.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Some central numerical tables, including Tables 1, 3–5, and 14–20, lack their cell contents in the supplied retrieval, limiting independent verification of several summary claims.

## Open research questions

1. **Does outcome-only RL cause policy miscalibration?** The paper offers this only as a hypothesis. A matched training experiment could hold backbone and data fixed while comparing outcome-only, process-aware, and VEG-informed rewards.

2. **Does the taxonomy transfer to other tools?** Crop-and-zoom is the only studied operation. The next experiment could apply tool-specific counterfactuals to OCR, segmentation, localization, or video-frame selection and recalibrate the diagnostic thresholds.

3. **Can inference-time stopping improve both accuracy and efficiency?** POER and Hit-MaxT identify over-extension, but the paper does not report deployment of a stopping controller. A controlled evaluation could stop calls when confidence saturates and measure accuracy, tokens, call count, and CWL/LWP rates.

4. **How well does local VEG predict unconstrained answer changes?** VEG probes immediate answer preference under a fixed marker and prefix. An ablation could compare VEG against paired continuation rollouts from the same checkpoint to test whether local effects forecast final-answer revisions.
