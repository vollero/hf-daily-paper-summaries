# SkillZip: Contract-Preserving Graph Compression for Scalable Agent Skill Libraries

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05604) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05604)


## One-sentence takeaway

SkillZip compresses recurring section-level procedures into reversible graph macros and retrieves dependency-closed, verifier-reachable contexts, improving agent performance while substantially reducing active storage and rendered context.

## Problem and motivation

Growing agent-skill libraries create a mismatch between the unit retrieved, compressed, and executed: existing systems typically retrieve whole skill packages, compress text, and construct execution graphs only afterward. This can load redundant procedures, obscure inputs or guards, detach operations from verifiers, and prevent persistent reuse below the skill level. The authors argue that compression and retrieval should instead operate on contract-bearing procedural subgraphs whose interfaces, dependencies, verification paths, and source provenance remain explicit (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Sec2Graph:** Converts each skill into source-grounded section nodes with execution roles, typed inputs/outputs, resources, guards, verifier conditions, dependencies, and provenance (Section 4.1).
- **MotifZip:** Finds recurring, interface-compatible subgraphs and replaces them with reversible ported macros only when boundary signatures, dependency closure, and verifier reachability are preserved (Section 4.2; Algorithm 3).
- **PathHydrate:** Retrieves query-relevant sections, connects and repairs their execution scaffold, and renders each macro at the lowest sufficient detail level under a context budget (Section 4.3; Algorithm 4).
- **ReZip:** Incrementally incorporates new skills and uses expansion, verifier-failure, and repair signals to increase macro detail, split risky macros, or retire them (Section 4.4; Algorithm 5).
- **Structural guarantee:** The authors prove reversible structural recovery for non-conflicting rewritten occurrences, preserving recorded external dependencies and verifier reachability; they explicitly do not claim semantic equivalence of unrecorded behavior (Proposition 0, Section 4.2).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that section-level, contract-aware compression can improve retrieval, execution outcomes, and measured efficiency under the tested configurations. Ablations strengthen the case that dependency and verifier constraints—not compression alone—drive structural fidelity. It does not establish semantic equivalence for behavior absent from extracted contracts, optimal graph compression, or comparable performance outside the supplied technical and embodied benchmarks.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated boundary:** The lifting result is structural, not semantic; it recovers recorded interfaces, dependencies, verifier paths, and provenance but cannot validate omitted behavior or verifier correctness (Section 4.2).
- **Author-stated optimization limit:** MotifZip uses greedy conflict-aware rewriting and makes no claim of globally optimal macro selection (Section 4.2).
- **Contract dependence:** Extraction reaches 91.6 macro-F1 and 84.6 exact match, but corruption progressively damages reward and structural fidelity; implicit preconditions and guards are the weakest reported fields (Figure 3; Table 5).
- **Residual system failures:** The paper attributes 26% of remaining failures to retrieval, 18% to contract extraction, and 14% to hydration; 33% arise during agent execution and 9% from infrastructure (Figure 7; Appendix B.8).
- **Analyst inference:** The main evaluation covers 87 SkillsBench tasks and 140 ALFWorld episodes, so broader procedural domains and real production skill ecosystems remain insufficiently tested (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** The 100K-skill construction timing excludes fresh LLM-assisted contract extraction and starts from cached records; total deployment preprocessing cost at that scale is therefore unknown (Table 9; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Some baseline disclosure policies use different package limits and budgets rather than identical rendered-token caps, complicating strictly budget-matched end-to-end comparison (Appendix D).
- **Reproducibility risk:** Prompts and hyperparameters are supplied, but the text does not establish public availability of code, processed graphs, annotations, or evaluation artifacts.

## Open research questions

1. **Can semantic correctness be protected beyond recorded graph structure?** The current guarantee does not cover unrecorded behavior or faulty verifiers. A next experiment could compare macro-expanded and original routines using execution traces, state diffs, and independently written tests.

2. **How robust is SkillZip to naturally occurring contract drift?** ReZip is tested with controlled verifier tightening. A longitudinal study on a genuinely evolving skill repository could measure detection delay, false macro revisions, and maintenance cost.

3. **Can contract extraction be improved for implicit guards and preconditions?** These are the weakest annotated fields and contribute to remaining failures. An experiment could compare the current section parser with joint document-level extraction or execution-assisted contract induction.

4. **How should compression and hydration behave under much tighter budgets?** Reward drops sharply at 1,000 tokens on SkillsBench. A targeted study could compare learned budget allocation, verifier-first selection, and adaptive source expansion across task complexity levels.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text contains detailed methods, algorithms, experimental settings, ablations, and appendices.

Material missing information includes confirmed code/data release availability, fresh LLM-extraction time and monetary cost at large scale, full per-task results, detailed composition of the contract-annotation subset, and evidence from real production libraries or domains beyond SkillsBench and ALFWorld.
