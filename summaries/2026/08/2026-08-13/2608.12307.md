# AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12307) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12307)


## One-sentence takeaway

A stronger builder model can substantially improve a fixed weaker model without retraining by compiling reusable task structure into an inference-time harness, raising GPT-5.4-mini’s macro-average Theory-of-Mind accuracy from 0.488 to as high as 0.912.

## Problem and motivation

Most capability-transfer methods modify a weaker model’s parameters through distillation or post-training. The authors instead ask whether capability can be transferred entirely at test time by having a strong builder construct a persistent harness around a fixed target model.

This matters because deployed language models increasingly operate inside systems containing prompts, routing, tools, checks, and preprocessing, yet the field lacks a systematic account of how much automatically constructed harnesses help, why they work, and when they fail. The paper studies this gap on four Theory-of-Mind (ToM) benchmarks, where nested beliefs, hidden information, and goal inference create substantial cognitive load (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Strong-to-weak scaffolding framework.** A builder receives task rules, target-model calling instructions, and a labeled validation subset, then iteratively writes and evaluates an executable harness. The target’s parameters remain fixed, and the final harness is evaluated on a hidden test set without further builder intervention (Section 3; Algorithm 1; Figure 1).

2. **Broad controlled evaluation.** The study reports 72 runs varying builder model, builder platform, reasoning effort, target model, and repeat. It evaluates four text-format ToM datasets using macro-average accuracy and validation-use efficiency (Section 4; Table 6).

3. **Mechanistic analysis.** The authors classify harness techniques and analyze stability, validation efficiency, target headroom, platform effects, builder effort, deterministic offloading, and residual errors (Sections 5.2–5.10).

4. **Design findings.** Successful harnesses commonly combine answer-format enforcement, benchmark routing, deterministic or rule-based offloading, and targeted decomposition. More validation probing and longer target reasoning are not shown to be the main drivers (Sections 5.3–5.9).

The results establish strong accuracy improvements under this particular validation-guided, text-only ToM setting, including transfer from a small validation sample to a hidden split. They also provide correlational evidence that deterministic offloading and explicit task structure are important.

They do not establish that technique-level associations are causal, that the approach generalizes beyond these benchmarks, or that the harness transfers to different target models without rebuilding. Comparisons with larger unscaffolded models also do not control for total construction and inference cost.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limitation:** Evaluation is restricted to four ToM benchmark families; broader symbolic, ambiguous, and open-ended tasks remain untested (Section 7).

- **Author-stated mechanism boundary:** Deep belief recursion, deception, and Bayesian goal inference remain difficult to compile into rules, leaving more work to the weak target (Sections 5.10 and 6).

- **Author-stated instability:** Deterministic solvers can produce large performance swings when a single benchmark-specific rule is wrong; the widest repeat range is 0.201 (Section 5.2).

- **Author-stated target risk:** Scaffolding can interfere with already-correct behavior on stronger targets, as seen in 9/20 Gemini-3.5-flash benchmark-level cases (Section 5.6).

- **Analyst inference — technique attribution is confounded:** Techniques co-occur and were extracted from completed runs. The authors explicitly describe the with/without comparisons as associational, and some contrasts contain fewer than five runs (Section 5.8; Figure 9).

- **Analyst inference — benchmark exploitation complicates interpretation:** BigToM can often be solved through benchmark-specific deterministic rules. Consequently, its near-ceiling performance does not by itself demonstrate improved general ToM reasoning (Sections 5.1, 5.4, and 7).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete cost accounting:** Validation evaluations are counted, but the supplied text does not report builder token use, target-call totals, latency, monetary cost, or full inference overhead. Thus, deployment efficiency relative to training or stronger-model inference is unresolved.

- **Analyst inference — protocol inconsistency:** The main paper repeatedly states a 195-item, 5% validation set, whereas the reproduced builder instruction says “2%” (Appendix A, page 21). The operational split appears to be 5%, but the discrepancy weakens reproducibility.

- **Analyst inference — limited model independence:** Only two target models are tested, and model/platform names are reported without enough configuration detail in the supplied text to independently reproduce their behavior.

## Open research questions

1. **Does strong-to-weak scaffolding generalize beyond structured ToM benchmarks?**  
   This remains open because all reported tasks belong to one reasoning domain. A next experiment could apply the same hidden-test protocol to coding, mathematical reasoning, information extraction, and open-ended planning while holding validation budget constant.

2. **Which harness components cause improvements?**  
   Technique comparisons are observational and confounded by builder quality and co-occurrence. A factorial ablation could add or remove routing, format enforcement, deterministic solvers, and structured extraction from the same scaffold while keeping all other code and target calls fixed.

3. **When should a harness defer to an already-strong target?**  
   Some scaffolds harm Gemini-3.5-flash on near-ceiling tasks. A next experiment could learn a validation-calibrated gate that chooses between the vanilla target, model-mediated scaffolding, and deterministic solving at the subtype or instance level.

4. **Are gains worthwhile after total cost is included?**  
   The paper does not provide end-to-end compute, latency, or monetary comparisons. A next experiment could measure builder construction cost, amortized inference cost, target-call count, and accuracy against both distillation and direct use of a stronger model.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental setup and headline numerical findings, because the supplied text contains the complete method, aggregate results, per-run table, and major analyses.

Material missing or unclear information includes:

- Whether code, generated harnesses, validation splits, and prediction traces are publicly released.
- Exact prompts, model versions, decoding settings, and platform configurations for every run.
- Total token, latency, target-call, and monetary costs.
- Human coding protocol and inter-rater reliability for the 12-technique taxonomy.
- Controlled component ablations establishing causal mechanisms.
- Results outside text-only ToM benchmarks.
- Resolution of the 5% versus 2% validation-language inconsistency.
