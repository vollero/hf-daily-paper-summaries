# Ready Cohorts: Bounding GPU Opportunity and Avoiding Host Round Trips in LLM-Agent Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12123) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12123)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper separates GPU-based LLM-agent control into two prerequisites—enough deadline-feasible, semantically compatible events to form GPU-sized cohorts and enough device-resident control to avoid host redispatch—then measures each prerequisite separately without claiming end-to-end acceleration.

## Problem and motivation

LLM-agent services repeatedly perform small deterministic transitions between model and tool calls, such as updating state and choosing the next route. Individually these operations may be too small for efficient GPU execution, while repeated CPU–GPU crossings can put host orchestration on the critical path.

The authors identify two unresolved questions: whether concurrent sessions produce sufficiently large same-route cohorts before launch deadlines, and whether keeping an intermediate GPU decision on device materially reduces control overhead. Existing workload similarity alone does not answer either question because executable compatibility, minimum profitable cohort size, deadlines, and decision placement all matter (Sections 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Ready-cohort framework:** Defines fixed-window eligibility \(F\), exact offline opportunity \(P^\star\), local upper bound \(U\), and finite-runtime achieved share \(A\), with the ordering \(F\leq P^\star\leq U\) and \(A\leq P^\star\) under stated compatibility conditions (Section 3, Proposition 1).

- **Exact opportunity estimator:** For equal relative launch deadlines, zero service time, and unlimited capacity, derives a dynamic program that exactly maximizes the number of events assigned to cohorts of at least \(K\) (Section 4, Equation 7 and Algorithm 1).

- **Trace-conditioned cohort study:** Applies this estimator to a stationary Poisson replay built from a pinned 851-session public agent-trace panel, comparing fixed windows, exact sliding-deadline packing, and a local overlap bound (Sections 5–6).

- **Decision-placement mechanism study:** Compares a matched host-round-trip CUDA path with a device-resident path that retains a binary routing decision across epochs, plus a non-admissible oracle floor and an earlier negative device-launch control (Sections 7–8).

The evidence establishes offline scheduling headroom under one replay model and a consistent mechanism-level benefit from avoiding one matched host decision round trip on four named GPU placements. It does not establish end-to-end service acceleration, CPU displacement, completion-time SLO improvement, production workload generalization, or that the trace’s route labels represent executable compatibility. The trace and mechanism numbers cannot be combined because \(K=256\) was swept rather than measured for the CUDA mechanism, and its 32-epoch horizon was not inferred from the trace (Sections 9 and 11).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The offline optimum assumes future knowledge, zero service time, unlimited capacity, equal relative deadlines, no upper batch limit, and deadlines on launch rather than completion. \(A\), the achieved share of a finite online runtime, is not measured (Sections 3, 4, and 11).

- **Author-stated:** The trace route key is only a conditioning proxy. It omits state-machine nodes, schemas, arguments, policy context, and identities within multi-tool outcomes, so the analysis cannot prove that grouped events share executable semantics (Sections 2.2, 5.1, and 11).

- **Author-stated:** The replay uses one 851-session customer-service panel and stationary Poisson arrivals. Three seeds measure simulation variation, not independent deployment populations; bursts and correlated arrivals may change the boundary (Sections 5.2–5.3 and 11).

- **Author-stated:** The mechanism uses a regular 16-byte synthetic state and one global binary decision. It omits per-event compaction, heterogeneous routes, ingress and egress, recovery, ordered effects, CPU fallback, and interference with LLM inference (Sections 7 and 11).

- **Author-stated:** Setup, state reset, output copying, and validation are excluded from timing. The metric is batch-average wall time, not invocation P99 or end-to-end task latency (Sections 7.1 and 11).

- **Author-stated:** Hardware sampling is narrow and confounded: two L4s, one H100, and one GTX across differing providers and environments. Rows are technical repetitions rather than independent hardware samples (Sections 7.2 and 11).

- **Author-stated:** The trace arrays omit per-session sequence constraints, while overlapping spans and completion-order inversions may affect feasibility (Section 11).

- **Author-stated:** Protocol deviations include reuse of the development GPU, retaining one additional external placement beyond the frozen first-stage count, and an incomplete native-dispatch placement plan (Section 7.4 and Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** The absence of a tuned CPU baseline for the real transition prevents determining whether either GPU path is preferable to keeping these small operations on the CPU. The paper itself identifies that comparison as a required future baseline (Sections 8.1, 10, and 11).

## Open research questions

1. **How much offline opportunity can a finite online scheduler realize?**  
   \(A\) is defined but unmeasured, and \(P^\star\) uses future knowledge. A next experiment could replay the same event sets through a frozen online compactor with finite queues, measured service times, GPU capacity, and CPU fallback, reporting \(A\), \(R_A\), deadline misses, and P99.

2. **How does verified executable compatibility change cohort supply?**  
   Outcome-derived route keys omit semantic fields required to prove safe fusion. A next experiment could instrument a real agent runtime with implementation version, state-machine node, schema, policy context, and route-body identity, then recompute \(F\), \(P^\star\), and \(U\).

3. **Does device-resident control beat a tuned CPU implementation end to end?**  
   The mechanism study compares two GPU paths, not GPU control with a production CPU transition. A next experiment could benchmark identical real route bodies on CPU, host-mediated GPU, and device-resident GPU while including ingress, egress, validation, CPU core-seconds, energy, cost, and effect commitment.

4. **Can control kernels coexist safely with LLM inference?**  
   The paper does not measure TTFT, TPOT, or model throughput interference. A next experiment could run the online compactor at controlled priorities on a shared inference GPU and measure achieved share and CPU savings subject to frozen TTFT/TPOT guardrails.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated methods, numerical results, and explicit claim boundaries because the supplied text is complete and provides detailed settings and locations.

Material missing information includes the actual contents of Tables 1–6, per-cell raw values beyond those quoted in the prose, route-level opportunity distributions, exact CPU and software configurations for every placement, a tuned CPU baseline, finite-runtime measurements of \(A\), end-to-end latency and cost, production-arrival validation, inference-interference results, and verified executable route identities.
