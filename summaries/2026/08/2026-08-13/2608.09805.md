# Parameter Exploration for RLVR via Variational Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09805) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09805)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that sampling multiple learned weight perturbations during RLVR—especially C3PO’s within-group policy sampling—improves 7B-model math and code performance over GRPO at nearly identical FLOPs, though current implementations take about 1.5× longer.

## Problem and motivation

RLVR learns exclusively from model-generated rollouts, so useful high-reward trajectories may never be sampled. This is particularly harmful for GRPO: when every rollout in a prompt group receives the same reward, all advantages are zero and learning stalls (Sections 1 and 2.1).

The authors argue that common action-space exploration methods are limited. Temperature scaling changes distribution entropy while preserving token ordering and, at high values, can promote malformed outputs (Figure 1). The identified gap is parameter-space exploration for LLM RLVR: sampling nearby policies can reorder token probabilities and potentially discover diverse but still plausible trajectories (Sections 1 and 2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **3PO family:** Perturbed Parameter Policy Optimization samples policies from an approximate diagonal Gaussian posterior learned with IVON. Its variance is controlled by effective sample size \(\lambda\), with smaller \(\lambda\) producing stronger parameter noise (Equations 2–4; Section 3).
- **B3PO:** Uses one sampled weight perturbation for every prompt and rollout group in a gradient step (Section 3.1; Algorithm 1).
- **M3PO:** Averages gradients across \(M\) independently perturbed policies, reducing noise-sample variance; under equal compute, each perturbation receives a smaller rollout group (Section 3.1; Algorithm 1).
- **C3PO:** Divides each \(G\)-rollout group across \(N\) perturbed policies, then computes advantages over the combined group to directly increase within-group diversity (Section 3.2; Algorithm 2).
- **Empirical contribution:** Compares parameter exploration with GRPO and three action-space baselines on two 7B models, six math benchmarks, and a code-generation setting; it also studies zero-advantage rescue, malformed outputs, noise scale, policy-sample count, posterior initialization, isotropic noise, and Seq-MIS (Sections 4–5 and Appendices G–J).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that multi-policy parameter exploration can outperform the tested GRPO and action-space baselines in these 7B-model math and code settings. Ablations also support within-group policy diversity, curvature-scaled noise, and Seq-MIS as important components.

It does not establish improvements at larger model scales, with non-binary or non-verifiable rewards, or across broader language tasks. Faster convergence in reward curves also does not by itself prove lower real-world cost because the current M3PO and C3PO implementations have higher wall-clock time.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Experiments are limited to 7B models because of compute; the expected benefit at larger scales remains a hypothesis (Section 7).
- M3PO and C3PO take about 1.5× longer than GRPO despite similar FLOPs, attributed to inefficient multi-model sampling and unfused IVON infrastructure (Section 7; Appendix A).
- Larger numbers of Monte Carlo samples, rollout budgets, and test-time scaling were not explored (Section 7).
- Structured non-diagonal posteriors and alternatives to Monte Carlo sampling remain untested (Section 7).
- The SFT-derived noise prior was nearly isotropic and showed no clear advantage; longer SFT or pretraining-derived curvature was not tested due to computational constraints (Section 5.4).

**Analyst inferences**

- External validity is narrow: the main evidence covers two 7B checkpoints, predominantly math training, and one OLMo3 code experiment.
- Robustness evidence is incomplete because only GRPO and C3PO on OLMo3 math were rerun across three seeds; other methods, Qwen2.5-Math, and code generation lack comparable multi-seed results (Appendix G).
- The LiveCodeBench result supplies no standard error or statistical comparison, so the certainty of the reported code advantage is unclear.
- C3PO depends strongly on \(\lambda\) and Seq-MIS: one noise setting collapses completely, and removing the correction stalls training (Table 2; Appendix I).
- The claim of “near-identical cost” applies to FLOPs, not present wall-clock execution (Table 3).
- Although Appendix A provides extensive hyperparameters and page 1 indicates a repository, the supplied text does not establish which checkpoints, logs, or evaluation artifacts are actually released.

## Open research questions

1. **Does parameter exploration scale to larger LLMs?** The paper tests only 7B models and explicitly attributes this to compute limits. A next experiment could compare GRPO and C3PO across several model sizes while sweeping \(\lambda\) and holding rollout FLOPs fixed.

2. **Can noise strength be adapted automatically?** Fixed \(\lambda\) is sensitive across methods and model families, with excessive noise causing collapse. A next experiment could compare fixed values against schedules driven by zero-advantage frequency, reward variance, or training stability.

3. **Would structured posterior noise improve directed exploration?** IVON uses a diagonal Hessian approximation, while structured non-diagonal estimators are left untested. A controlled experiment could compare diagonal, block-structured, and isotropic matched-magnitude perturbations under the same rollout budget.

4. **Can C3PO retain its gains without the wall-clock overhead?** Current multi-policy inference reduces batching efficiency. A next experiment could implement fused perturbation and batched multi-policy generation, then compare time-to-target reward and Pass@1—not only FLOPs per step—with GRPO.
