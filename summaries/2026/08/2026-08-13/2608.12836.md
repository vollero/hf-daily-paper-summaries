# From Atomic Evidence to Logical Composition: Structured Compositional Reasoning over Compound Answer Options

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12836) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12836)


## One-sentence takeaway

Separating atomic-answer evaluation from operator-constrained composition raises Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct’s Macro-F1 from 48.3 to 77.0 on Logical-CommonsenseQA-HV and from 47.0 to 75.6 on Logical-SATA, with the largest gains on Neither/Nor options.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses multiple-choice questions whose options combine two atomic answers with And, Or, or Neither/Nor. The authors argue that direct prompting conflates two tasks—judging individual propositions and composing those judgments—and can therefore fail even when the underlying atomic evidence is available. Existing decomposition methods still leave composition to unconstrained generation, while neuro-symbolic methods often require error-prone translation into a formal representation. Here, the operators are already explicit, allowing composition to be isolated and enforced directly (Introduction; Sections 3.1 and 3.6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Atomic decomposition and contrastive scoring:** Each option is deterministically split into two atoms and an operator. Every unique atom is scored once through paired positive/negative hypotheses, using normalized first-token probabilities; the model never evaluates the compound option directly (Sections 3.2–3.4).

- **Constrained composition:** A binary ILP assigns atom statuses, encodes exact And/Or/Neither-Nor semantics, and enforces one valid option while maximizing total atomic evidence (Section 3.6).

- **Relative calibration:** A learned logistic calibrator combines an atom’s raw logit with its within-instance z-score, rank, and distance from the maximum score (Section 3.5).

- **Logical-SATA benchmark:** The authors derive a 5,400-instance compound-answer reading-comprehension benchmark from human-labeled SATA-Bench data (Section 4).

- **Operator-specific evaluation:** The framework is compared with zero- to three-shot and zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting across commonsense and reading-comprehension settings (Section 5.1).

The results establish that, for the tested model and constructed task format, enforcing composition over separately elicited atomic evidence is much stronger than the included direct-prompting baselines, particularly for negated and mixed operators. They do not establish that constrained inference improves the model’s underlying semantic judgments, generalizes to other models or logical forms, or works when the answer structure and operators must first be extracted.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors state that evaluation covers only Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, two benchmarks, pairs of atoms, and three explicit operators. Both datasets guarantee exactly one valid option, unlike tasks permitting multiple or no valid answers. Performance depends on atomic evidence and can inherit commonsense ambiguity, passage-grounding errors, annotation problems, SATA-Bench’s domain and label coverage, prompt choices, calibration data, and the elicitation strategy (Limitations).

The method also requires calibration labels and, for the main paired-MC scorer, access to first-answer-token probabilities (Sections 3.4–3.5). Its deterministic composition cannot correct erroneous atomic judgments; it only forces a logically feasible assignment (Section 5.1; Appendix C.5).

**Analyst inference:** The comparison does not isolate every component’s causal contribution. Tables 1–2 compare direct compound prompting with an entire pipeline, while calibration variants isolate only the calibration stage. No reported ablation separately removes atom sharing, contrastive hypotheses, or the exact-one constraint. Also, Logical-SATA is derived from one source dataset under eligibility and balancing filters, which may limit representativeness (Section 4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the gain generalize across model families and scales?** Only one 8B instruction model is tested. A next experiment could repeat the full operator-stratified evaluation on several model families and sizes with identical prompts and calibration splits.

2. **Which pipeline components produce the improvement?** The paper does not separately ablate shared-atom scoring, positive/negative contrast, and exact-one constrained inference. A factorial ablation could vary each component while holding the atomic scorer fixed.

3. **Can the method handle richer or latent logical structures?** Evaluation is limited to explicit binary And, Or, and Neither/Nor. A next benchmark could introduce implication, exclusive disjunction, nested expressions, and more than two atoms, measuring both parsing and composition errors.

4. **How should uncertainty propagate through composition?** The ILP returns a single discrete assignment, so uncertainty is discarded at inference time. A probabilistic structured model could instead produce option distributions and be compared on predictive performance and calibration.
