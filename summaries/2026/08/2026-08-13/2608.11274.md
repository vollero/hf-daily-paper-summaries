# Agent Safety Should Be a Runtime Contract

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11274) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11274)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors argue that consequential AI agents should be governed at runtime by layered action controls and evidence-gated completion, making the trajectory with verifiable artifacts—not the model alone—the unit of safety.

## Problem and motivation

Model-level alignment methods such as RLHF and DPO produce behavioral tendencies but cannot reliably constrain open-world agents that execute code, alter files or databases, and communicate externally. The authors identify five structural mismatches: statistical proxies versus formal rules, closed training distributions versus open-world deployment, opaque reasoning versus replayable trajectories, plausible outputs versus grounded evidence, and model alignment as a single defensive layer (§3).

Their central gap claim is that deployed systems already use permissions, sandboxes, logging, and validation artifacts, but these mechanisms are not treated as a unified safety contract, especially one that refuses completion without verifiable evidence (§1, §5).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Two-faced runtime contract.** The proposed architecture combines preventive controls—such as permission gates, sandboxing, monitoring, rollback, and least privilege—with evidential controls that accept completion only after deterministic verification of task-specific artifacts (§4.1–§4.2, Fig. 1).

2. **Formal trajectory and evidence model.** The paper defines a hash-chained Agent Trajectory Schema, distinguishes hard evidence verifiable from external state from soft model-dependent evidence, and defines evidence chains and evidence-gated submission (§4.2).

3. **Composition proposition.** Preventive layers are modeled as finite-state monitors and evidential layers as checkers. Under disjoint observation alphabets and independent verifiers, their guarantees compose; shared events require assume-guarantee reasoning (§4.3).

4. **Four empirical audits.** The authors compile 52 reported incidents, 31 non-contested false-completion cases plus one disputed case, an audit of 12 public systems, and a title-level analysis of 28,560 conference papers. They report releasing row-level protocols and coding in supplementary JSON (§5, Table 1).

Together, these results support the authors’ position that runtime controls could address many documented failure patterns, that evidence-gated completion is uncommon despite widespread artifact capture, and that publication attention is training-heavy. They do **not** establish causal effectiveness in prospective deployments: the incident classifications are counterfactual, the product audit covers public documentation rather than internals, and the proceedings analysis is based only on titles and estimated ranges (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The contract constrains actions and submissions, not agent goals or mesa-optimization (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Polynomial composition depends on independent verifiers; general composition can be exponential (§4.3, Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Classifier-based harness components retain model-like fragility, although within narrower domains (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** The incident and false-completion audits oversample English-language coverage, and task evidence schemas currently exist only where correctness criteria are established (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** The trajectory audit measures documented behavior, not undisclosed vendor internals; OSWorld is a benchmark rather than a deployed product (§5, Table 2).
- **Author-stated:** Proceedings counts are title-level estimates using keyword rules, lower bounds, and truncation corrections—not a full-text census (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** The paper does not report a deployed implementation or controlled comparison measuring whether its complete two-faced contract reduces harm, false acceptance, latency, or operational cost.
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that particular harness layers would have prevented historical incidents depends on retrospective counterfactual coding and cannot by itself demonstrate real-world efficacy.
- **Analyst inference:** Hash chains reveal modification but do not ensure that logged events, external reference states, or verifiers are complete and correct; the authors partly acknowledge this through the flaky-test example (§4.3).

## Open research questions

1. **How effective is the complete contract under adversarial deployment?** The paper offers audits and an architectural example but no end-to-end intervention study. A next experiment could compare model-only, preventive-only, evidential-only, and combined harnesses on identical agent tasks with seeded attacks.

2. **How should evidence schemas be derived for partially subjective tasks?** The authors limit current applicability to tasks with established correctness criteria. A next experiment could test human-authored versus automatically proposed schemas on messaging or research tasks, measuring false acceptance and unnecessary rejection.

3. **How robust are evidence gates when verifiers or reference states are flawed?** The paper notes that flaky tests can produce incorrect gates but does not quantify this risk. A next experiment could inject stale citations, incomplete test suites, compromised logs, and inconsistent database snapshots, then evaluate redundant or differential verifiers.

4. **What are the operational costs of runtime contracts?** The authors argue schema definition is largely a one-time engineering cost, but provide no latency, compute, or human-review measurements (§7). A next experiment could instrument a coding-agent workflow and measure completion time, approval burden, and safety gains at different gate strengths.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated framework, audit aggregates, and limitations because the supplied text includes the full retrieved sections, tables, and appendix.

Material missing information includes the supplementary JSON contents, row-level source and coding decisions, detailed proceedings keyword rules, inter-rater agreement, audit sampling procedures beyond the stated criteria, executable schema/verifier implementations, prospective deployment results, and quantitative overhead measurements.
