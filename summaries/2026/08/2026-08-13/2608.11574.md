# Hand Visibility Detector: Per-Keypoint Visibility Estimation for Hands

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-13.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11574) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11574)


## One-sentence takeaway

Hand Visibility Detector treats 21-joint hand visibility as a standalone classification task, using a lightweight head on a frozen pretrained hand-pose backbone to outperform prior visibility estimators on HInt and improve visibility-weighted multi-view triangulation.

## Problem and motivation

Most hand-pose estimators return joint positions without stating whether each joint is directly visible or inferred under occlusion. This limits reliability assessment in AR/VR, human–computer interaction, robotics, and automatic 3D annotation. The authors identify a gap: visibility has usually been an auxiliary pose-estimation signal, so its standalone accuracy and generalization across self-occlusion, object occlusion, and image truncation have not been systematically evaluated (Abstract; Sections 1 and 2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Standalone task formulation:** Per-joint visibility is posed as binary classification for the 21 MANO keypoints; occluded and out-of-frame joints are labeled invisible (Sections 3 and 3.2).
- **Hand Visibility Detector:** A frozen ViT backbone from HaMeR or WiLoR extracts hand features, while a 0.83M-parameter head uses projection layers, a Gated Attention Unit, global average pooling, and sigmoid outputs (Figure 2; Sections 3.1–3.2 and 4).
- **Systematic evaluation:** The method is trained and tested on HInt’s manually annotated, diverse in-the-wild imagery and compared with existing per-joint visibility estimators and multiple pretrained backbones (Section 4; Tables 1–3).
- **Downstream validation and release:** Predicted visibility weights multi-view DLT triangulation on DexYCB, HO3D, and H2O; the authors also report releasing code, a demo, and a ready-to-use package (Abstract; Sections 5.2 and 6).

The results establish strong HInt performance relative to the selected baselines, support the value of frozen hand-specific features and the GAU head, and show consistent reprojection-error improvements in the tested multi-view setting. They do not establish better 3D positional accuracy, performance on unseen visibility-label datasets, temporal consistency, or superiority over every possible confidence-calibration and robust-triangulation method.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The method operates on individual images; temporally consistent video visibility estimation is left for future work (Section 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Primary visibility evaluation uses ground-truth hand boxes, whereas deployment and triangulation use detected boxes. Table 1 therefore does not measure the full detected-crop pipeline (Sections 3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** Standalone visibility accuracy is evaluated only on HInt. Although HInt contains diverse imagery, cross-dataset generalization with independent visibility labels is not demonstrated (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference:** The downstream metric is reprojection error rather than error against ground-truth 3D joints, so improved 3D annotation accuracy is not directly established (Section 5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Figure 5’s supplied text reports only the largest percentage improvement, not exact per-dataset error values, limiting quantitative comparison.
- **Analyst inference:** The severe degradation from fine-tuning WiLoR is shown, but the supplied text does not describe alternative fine-tuning schedules or regularization ablations that could explain whether the failure is intrinsic (Table 2).
- Training requires about 2.5 hours on one NVIDIA H200 and approximately 10GB GPU memory; behavior on lower-resource hardware is not reported (Section 4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does visibility estimation generalize to independently labeled datasets and new capture domains?** HInt is the only standalone visibility benchmark used. A next experiment could evaluate a frozen model on separately annotated egocentric, interaction-heavy, and synthetic test sets without retraining.

2. **Does visibility weighting improve true 3D joint accuracy?** The paper reports reprojection error but not error against trusted 3D ground truth. A next experiment could compare MPJPE or another explicitly defined 3D error across the three weighting schemes.

3. **Can temporal modeling improve consistency without sacrificing frame-level accuracy?** Video extension is explicitly left as future work. A next experiment could add a temporal head and measure both HInt-style classification metrics and visibility flicker on annotated sequences.

4. **Why does backbone fine-tuning collapse performance?** Only one reported fine-tuned WiLoR result is provided. A next experiment could compare learning-rate scales, partial unfreezing, regularization, and parameter-efficient adaptation across identical seeds.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the architecture, HInt comparisons, ablations, and stated downstream trend because the supplied text includes the complete method and result sections.

Material missing information includes exact per-dataset values plotted in Figure 5, ground-truth 3D accuracy, cross-dataset visibility evaluation, temporal evaluation, detailed error breakdowns by occlusion type or joint, calibration metrics, and enough fine-tuning detail to explain the reported collapse.
