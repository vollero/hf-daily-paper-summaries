# Sliding-window beats linear attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28444) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28444)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 1.3B–70B language models, training-free sliding-window attention with four attention sinks generally matches or exceeds post-trained linear-attention conversions, with especially large advantages on the tested long-context reasoning tasks and competitive fixed-memory inference.

## Problem and motivation

Standard self-attention has context-dependent compute and an ever-growing KV cache, making long-context LLM inference costly. Linear attention promises fixed-size recurrent state but can lose information and typically requires post-training. The authors identify a missing baseline: prior linearization studies were generally not compared directly against sliding-window attention (SWA) with attention sinks, even though sink-free SWA can fail catastrophically (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Direct baseline comparison:** The paper compares existing post-trained linear or linear-plus-SWA models with training-free `SWA(w,4)`, which attends to four initial sink tokens and a recent window of 64–512 tokens (Sections 2.2–4).
- **Broad short-context evaluation:** It evaluates models from 1.3B to 70B parameters on MMLU, ARC-C, ARC-E, HellaSwag, PIQA, and WinoGrande (Table 2; Appendix A.1).
- **Long-context evaluation:** It compares SWA with LoLCATs and Liger-GLA on S-NIAH, and with LoLCATs on BABILong, through 4K-token contexts (Tables 3–4).
- **Systems comparison:** It measures decoding throughput and state/KV-cache memory through 256K context on a controlled four-layer Transformer implementation (Figure 2; Section 4.3).
- **Additional conversions:** The authors distill three linear variants on approximately 0.1B tokens and compare them with SWA on three modern model architectures (Table 5).

The evidence supports SWA with sinks as a strong, cheap baseline for the particular converted models, benchmarks, window sizes, and hardware tested. It does not establish that SWA universally beats linear attention trained from scratch, extensively post-trained linear models, hybrid full-attention systems, or linear attention at context lengths beyond the 4K task evaluations. The 256K experiment measures synthetic systems behavior, not task quality at that length.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only training-free SWA is studied; post-training SWA and token-scaling comparisons remain unexplored (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Models mixing SWA or linear attention with full-attention tokens or layers are excluded (Sections 4 and 5).
- **Author-stated:** Extremely large models, agentic tasks, multimodal LLMs, and multidimensional windows for video remain future work (Section 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Long-context quality evidence is narrow: one base model, two benchmark families, and contexts only through 4K (Sections 4.2.1–4.2.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The systems comparison uses a four-layer synthetic Transformer rather than the evaluated production-scale LLMs, and different optimized backends are used for SWA and linear attention; this limits direct generalization of speed rankings (Section 4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Many short-context comparisons reuse scores from existing linearized models with differing post-training budgets and procedures, so the study is not a uniformly controlled head-to-head experiment (Tables 1–2). Statistical uncertainty is not reported.
- **Analyst inference:** Figures 2–4 lack extractable numerical values in the supplied text, preventing precise verification of throughput, memory, and FLOP differences.

## Open research questions

1. **Does post-training SWA preserve or enlarge its advantage?** The paper tests only training-free SWA. A controlled token-scaling study could train SWA and linearized variants on identical data and budgets.
2. **How does the comparison change at genuinely long task contexts?** Accuracy is evaluated only through 4K despite systems measurements through 256K. S-NIAH, BABILong, and natural long-document tasks could be run at 8K–256K.
3. **Which hybrid allocation offers the best quality–memory trade-off?** Models containing full-attention layers are excluded. An ablation could vary the number and placement of full-attention layers while holding memory and compute budgets constant.
4. **Are the speed and memory conclusions stable across architectures and hardware?** The systems test uses one small Transformer and one GPU. Equivalent kernels could be benchmarked on full-scale LLMs across multiple accelerators, batch sizes, precisions, and window sizes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported table values and the paper’s stated scope; **medium** for broad comparative conclusions because experimental controls differ across methods.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty and run counts, exact numerical values underlying Figures 2–4, full implementation and reproducibility details, task-quality measurements beyond 4K, controlled equal-budget training comparisons, and results for models trained from scratch with linear attention.
