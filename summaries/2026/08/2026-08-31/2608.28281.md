# LoopArena: Benchmarking Models as Runtime Controllers for Loop Engineering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28281) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28281)


## One-sentence takeaway

LoopArena isolates a model’s ability to direct a fixed coding agent, finding low full-task success—at best 24.69%—while a paired task-slice evaluation cuts estimated inference cost by 64.4% on average and largely preserves Controller ordering under the primary scoring rule.

## Problem and motivation

Long-running coding agents require repeated decisions about implementation, verification, recovery, and stopping. A successful or failed end-to-end run, however, does not reveal whether the outcome arose from the coding agent’s execution ability or from the surrounding loop’s guidance. The authors identify a gap in existing benchmarks: most evaluate either the complete agent system or isolated process actions, rather than the model controlling a separate, fixed coding Worker (Sections 1 and 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled Controller–Worker protocol:** The evaluated Controller receives structured Evidence Packets and issues Loop Contracts telling a fixed Worker to advance, verify, or stop. Only the Worker can inspect, modify, and test the repository; a temporary read-only Reporter summarizes each completed segment (Sections 1 and 2.2; Figure 1).

- **Three evaluation scopes:** Type I tests a four-way next-contract choice whose answer was established through matched execution replays; Type II runs the loop on a prepared task slice; Type III runs the paired full task from its original state (Sections 2 and 3; Figure 2).

- **Controller-specific comparison:** Worker, Reporter configuration, tools, environment, budgets, evaluator, and interface are held fixed across Controller models, separating loop-control quality from the complete coding stack (Sections 1 and 4.2).

- **Released evaluation asset:** The benchmark includes 90 Type I questions, 27 paired Type II/III tasks, harness and analysis code, construction records, configurations, and evaluation records (Table 1; Sections 9.1 and 16).

These results establish performance for five Controllers paired with one fixed Worker on the selected SCBench and BeyondSWE tasks. They support Type II as a cheaper proxy only for this panel under the primary Core scoring criterion. They do not establish that the ranking generalizes to other Workers, task domains, scoring policies, or Controller panels; Table 13 directly shows criterion sensitivity. The fixed-control comparison is suggestive of a need for adaptive guidance, but it does not isolate which adaptive decisions cause gains.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Coverage is limited to repository-level coding and a structured Controller–Worker organization. Other software domains, Worker families, multi-Worker systems, loop structures, and non-coding domains remain unevaluated (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Cost figures estimate inference under frozen public prices and assume no prompt caching. They exclude repository execution, evaluator compute, storage, and infrastructure, so they are not realized spending measurements (Section 13.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The strong Type II/III ranking agreement is fragile because alternative SCBench scoring rules produce correlations of 0.1481 and −0.2962 (Table 13).

- **Analyst inference:** Generalization across Workers is untested because every executable experiment uses Qwen3.7-Plus as both Worker and Reporter (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Output-limit failures may confound Controller-quality comparisons, especially for GLM 5.2, whose calls reached the limit in 18.70% of Type II and 20.39% of Type III calls (Table 12).

- **Analyst inference:** With only 27 executable tasks and three stochastic runs per task, task-level estimates can be unstable; Table 14 shows numerous tasks with mixed outcomes across repeats. No uncertainty intervals or significance tests are supplied in the retrieved text.

## Open research questions

1. **Do Controller rankings transfer across Worker models?** The paper fixes one Worker, leaving Controller–Worker interaction effects unknown. A next experiment could repeat the paired Type II/III panel with several fixed Workers and measure ranking stability and interaction effects.

2. **Which control actions actually improve outcomes?** Aggregate SSR does not isolate the value of advancing, verifying, redirecting, or stopping. An intervention study could replay matched states while replacing one action category at a time and compare downstream success and cost.

3. **Can a lower-cost setting remain predictive under different scoring definitions?** Table 13 shows that correlation changes sharply with the SCBench criterion. A next experiment could predefine multiple evaluators and test proxy validity across larger task and Controller panels.

4. **How much performance loss comes from protocol limits rather than poor control?** Output-limit events are frequent for some Controllers. Re-running affected cases with matched higher output limits—or a constrained contract decoder—could separate control quality from formatting and termination failures.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes detailed methods, metrics, tables, prompts, and appendices.

Material missing information includes uncertainty intervals or statistical significance tests, results with alternative Worker/Reporter models, broader non-coding or multi-Worker evaluations, and causal ablations of individual control actions or Evidence Packet components. The text lists extensive release artifacts, but their actual availability and reproducibility were not independently verified.
