# StarHarness: Evolving Harnesses with Stratified Search for Enterprise Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24804) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24804)


## One-sentence takeaway

StarHarness improves fixed-model agents on three stateful enterprise benchmarks by evolving their surrounding prompts, tools, interfaces, skills, and execution policies through failure-stratified search with hidden selection and held-out evaluation.

## Problem and motivation

Enterprise agents must navigate large tool surfaces, persistent state, cross-step dependencies, policy constraints, and operational conventions that tool schemas may not express. The authors argue that persistent failures can therefore arise from a mismatch between the model and its harness—not only from model capability—and ask whether a harness can be optimized from a compact task subset without overfitting, then transferred to unseen tasks and other models (Sections 1 and 3.1).

The identified gap is a harness-optimization protocol that keeps model weights fixed while clearly separating proposal data, candidate selection, and held-out evaluation in realistic stateful environments (Sections 1 and 2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Stratified harness evolution:** StarHarness constructs a compact evolution pool using baseline failure mode, task score, and verifier pass rate. It separates proposer-visible search tasks, proposer-hidden selection tasks, and untouched holdout tasks (Sections 3.1–3.2).

- **Executable harness search:** Candidate patches may alter prompts, task framing, tool schemas and preprocessing, skills, MCP providers, subagents, context management, verification, and finish logic while model weights and benchmarks remain fixed (Section 3.1).

- **Guarded selection loop:** Patches undergo scope, leakage, import, smoke-test, and proposer-selected “test flip” checks. Surviving candidates are accepted only when hidden-selection performance improves, with verifier rate as an optional tie-breaker (Figure 1; Sections 3.1 and 3.4).

- **Exploration and exploitation strategies:** The framework supports greedy hill climbing and a tree search that retains, debugs, merges, and refines alternative hypotheses. Their sequential use on EnterpriseOps-Gym is presented as complementary rather than a causal comparison (Section 3.3).

- **Empirical and diagnostic analysis:** The study evaluates held-out task generalization and frozen-harness transfer across GPT and Qwen families, and groups learned adaptations into interface repair, explicit environment conventions, and operational knowledge/search compression (Sections 4.3–5).

The evidence supports an association between harness evolution and higher task scores, lower estimated cost, and improved trajectory properties across the evaluated settings. Hidden selection and holdout separation strengthen the generalization claim, but the supplied text omits the numerical contents of Tables 1–4 and therefore prevents detailed verification of cross-model transfer, held-out gains, and several trajectory comparisons. The comparisons also do not isolate which individual patch or harness component caused each improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The StarHarness–GEPA comparison is descriptive because prompts, tools, execution policies, and harness architectures differ; it does not identify a single causal factor (Section 4.2).

- **Author-stated:** Tree search and hill climbing were sequential stages on EnterpriseOps-Gym, not a causal head-to-head experiment (Section 3.3).

- **Author-stated:** Individual tools and patches cannot be assigned causal contributions from the paired execution records (Section 5.1).

- **Author-stated:** AutomationBench uses a Finance-100 subset and a different harness from the benchmark paper, so its scores are not directly comparable with that paper’s reported results (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The evaluation covers only three enterprise-oriented benchmarks, one evolution model, and environment-specific harnesses; broader domain and proposer-model generality remains uncertain.

- **Analyst inference:** Descriptors used for stratification are computed from a baseline run over all reproducible tasks before partitioning. Although holdout outcomes do not guide later search, this preprocessing uses holdout-task baseline behavior and may differ from a stricter partition-first protocol (Section 3.2).

- **Analyst inference:** No reported ablation separates stratification, hidden selection, the test-flip gate, persistent memory, or particular editable harness components.

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility cannot be fully assessed because the retrieved text does not specify random seeds, search budgets by benchmark, repeated-run variance, exact partitions, or whether evolved artifacts and ledgers are released.

## Open research questions

1. **Which parts of StarHarness produce the gains?** The paired comparisons cannot isolate individual patches or protocol components. A factorial ablation could independently remove stratification, hidden selection, the test-flip gate, ledger memory, and major edit classes.

2. **How stable is evolution across task partitions and proposer runs?** The paper reports no repeated-run variance or alternative partitions. Repeating evolution across several seeds and independently sampled search/selection/holdout splits would measure stability and selection overfitting.

3. **How broadly do evolved harnesses transfer?** Transfer is demonstrated within GPT and Qwen families on the same three environments, but wider domain transfer is not established. A next experiment could freeze each harness and test new enterprise environments, different tool protocols, and additional model families.

4. **Can joint harness and weight optimization outperform harness-only adaptation efficiently?** The conclusion proposes co-evolution with reinforcement learning but does not test it. A controlled experiment could compare fixed-weight harness evolution, weight-only fine-tuning, and joint optimization under matched data and compute budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and several AutomationBench and ITBench results are described clearly, but the retrieved text omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–4 and the plotted values in Figure 2.

Material missing information includes exact per-model transfer scores, held-out gains and partition sizes, detailed EnterpriseOps-Gym results, most ITBench trajectory metrics, uncertainty or run-to-run variance, search budgets by environment, exact accepted-patch contents, random seeds, and artifact-release details.
