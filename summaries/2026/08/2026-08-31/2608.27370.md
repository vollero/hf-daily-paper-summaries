# Puro-2B: Poor Lab&#x27;s Qwen2-1.5B Trained on RTX 5090 within $5090

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27370) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27370)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors present an open recipe for training a 2B-parameter language model on consumer RTX 5090 GPUs, reporting a $4.37K run that exceeds Qwen2-1.5B and a $6.89K canonical run that approaches Qwen2.5-1.5B under their 15-benchmark, accelerator-only cost protocol.

## Problem and motivation

From-scratch language-model pretraining remains inaccessible to small research groups even when weights and recipes are public, because reproduction may still cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. The authors identify a gap between openness and practical affordability: researchers need reconstructible data, code, configurations, checkpoints, and transparent cost accounting on accessible hardware, not merely released weights (Abstract; §1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Integrated low-cost recipe:** A dense decoder-only Transformer of approximately 2B parameters is trained from scratch for about 1.4T tokens using RTX 5090 GPUs, blockwise FP8, MuonH optimization, proxy-guided data selection, and a two-phase schedule (§2.1; Table 1).
- **Curriculum Model Averaging:** Phase 2 orders data by component-local preference ranks, uses a late constant-learning-rate continuation, and averages six checkpoints. The method preserves the cross-component mixture without assuming scores are comparable across sources (§3.4; Figure 7; Appendix H).
- **Cost scaling analysis:** Uniform Phase 2 checkpoints at several budgets are used to fit a recipe-specific relationship between reproduction cost and the 15-benchmark aggregate (§4.3.1; Figure 2b; Tables 16–17).
- **End-to-end controlled comparison:** Uniform and curriculum/CMA pretraining endpoints receive matched supervised adaptation in focused mathematics, scaled mathematics, and broad instruction settings (§§3.5, 5).
- **Released artifacts:** The authors report releasing model variants, intermediate checkpoints, data manifests/components, training code, and preprocessing code, generally under Apache 2.0 subject to upstream data terms (§1).

These results establish competitive performance under the authors’ own fixed evaluation and rental-equivalent accounting protocol, alongside useful controlled evidence for the complete curriculum/CMA endpoint. They do not establish total development cost, universal superiority over other hardware or training recipes, or causal contributions from every component independently.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated accounting boundary:** The headline cost covers only accelerator time for the finalized Phase 1 and Phase 2 production runs. It excludes data processing, proxy experiments, ablations, failed runs, post-training, evaluation, checkpoint averaging, labor, CPU, storage, networking, taxes, and ownership costs (§2.2).
- **Author-stated incomplete factor isolation:** The curriculum, continuation, and averaging experiments do not form a complete factorial design, so their individual gains cannot be identified. Resume points were not tested as matched ablations, and repeated pretraining seeds are absent (Appendix H.4).
- **Possible contamination:** The authors acknowledge that benchmark contamination in later-stage data may explain part of the curriculum advantage (§3.4). Persistence after SFT weakens but does not eliminate this concern.
- **Transfer assumptions:** FP8 and MuonH savings come from TPP=20 scaling ladders, whereas production uses roughly TPP=700; the full-horizon BF16 and Muon counterfactuals were not run (§4.3.1, footnote 7).
- **Hardware reproducibility and safety:** Some throughput depends on unsupported driver modifications enabling P2P and GDR; GDR modification details are withheld because of EULA constraints (§3.1.2).
- **Data licensing:** Several components have restrictive, mixed, missing, or unclear terms, and some raw data cannot be redistributed despite the open-recipe framing (Table 20; §I).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting the best observed continuation branch may introduce configuration-selection bias because matched validation runs and uncertainty across branch choices are unavailable.
- **Analyst inference:** Model-by-model selection of the better cloze or multiple-choice formulation could make aggregate comparisons sensitive to evaluation-selection policy (§4.1.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Which part of curriculum/CMA causes the gain?** The available runs confound ordering, continuation schedule, and averaging. A full factorial experiment crossing uniform/curriculum ordering, fixed/decaying LR, and averaging on/off—with repeated seeds—would isolate effects.

2. **Do the FP8 and MuonH efficiency estimates transfer to the full 1.4T-token regime?** Current estimates derive from shorter TPP=20 ladders. Matched long-horizon BF16 versus FP8 and Muon versus MuonH runs at the production scale would test the transfer directly.

3. **How robust is the recipe across hardware configurations?** Results depend on RTX 5090 topology and partly undisclosed driver modifications. Replications using stock drivers and alternative consumer or data-center GPUs could compare realized cost, stability, and throughput.

4. **How much of the curriculum benefit survives strict decontamination?** The authors identify possible benchmark contamination. Rebuilding Phase 2 with benchmark-near material removed and repeating pretraining and matched SFT would test whether the advantage persists.
