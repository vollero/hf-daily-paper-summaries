# Training, learning and inference: unified dynamics of neural systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20965) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20965)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper proposes provenance-style Generation-Fact Graphs (GFGs) and uses intervention-heavy experiments to argue that training reorganizes state-dependent distributed support, while inference freezes and query-conditionally projects that learned support.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses two linked gaps. First, existing provenance mechanisms are presented as incomplete projections of a richer record needed to explain how a concrete result was generated. Second, standard learning curves and isolated objects such as loss, gradients, or components may not explain how training actions produce, erase, or recover capabilities.

The author argues that a common execution record is needed to study training, learning, and inference as parts of one causal process rather than as separate phenomena (Introduction, pp. 3–5).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Generation-Fact Graph framework.** An atomic generation fact records participating source information, realized transformation, concrete occurrence, outcome or explicit disposition, and relation role. Validated facts are compiled into a graph retaining identities, multiplicities, occurrences, and typed relations (Section 2, pp. 5–8).

2. **Recursive scientific process.** Analyses, interventions, replays, and validations consume existing GFGs and generate further traceable facts for subsequent cycles (Section 3, Fig. 1).

3. **Unified training–learning theory.** Experiments across 13 nanoGPT histories characterize training responses as finite-amplitude, nonlinear, and jointly conditioned by the target boundary state, target-specific update geometry, and parameter–Adam receiving state. Learning is defined as persistent reorganization of distributed functional support (Section 4).

4. **Prospective target-boundary predictor.** A second-order formula predicts four post-update correctness transitions after the actual update is formed but before post-update target outputs are evaluated; it is not a separately trained classifier (Section 5; Methods, TL-P01).

5. **Inference and feedback claims.** Component gating and rollback are used to argue that frozen inference recruits training-formed support in a query-dependent, non-additive manner. Separate controlled feedback experiments show a trade-off between reinforced and unreinforced capabilities (Sections 6.1 and 6.4).

6. **Cross-system evaluation and released evidence.** Related protocols are applied to ResNet-18/CIFAR-100 and DDPM-style U-Nets/CIFAR-10, and the paper reports a public evidence archive, code, validators, checkpoints, and reproduction instructions (Section 7; Methods, pp. 32–38).

These experiments support the author’s claims within the recorded nanoGPT, ResNet/CIFAR, diffusion/CIFAR, and controlled-feedback settings. They do not by themselves establish that the proposed dynamics are universal across architectures, datasets, optimization regimes, capability definitions, or real-world reinforcement-learning environments. Nor do the reported correlations alone establish causality; the stronger causal evidence comes from the matched interventions and rollback experiments.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope qualification:** The reinforcement results are not presented as an argument against reinforcement learning; they concern sustained asymmetric feedback and its potential collateral effects (Section 6.4).

- **Analyst inference — limited external breadth:** The main theory is developed from 13 nanoGPT histories, with cross-system confirmation limited to three seeds each for one ResNet/CIFAR and one diffusion/CIFAR setup. Broader model scales, datasets, optimizers, and modalities are not evaluated in the supplied text.

- **Analyst inference — instrument-dependent support concept:** Distributed support is operationalized through four registered components or routes and their gating coalitions. The text does not establish whether conclusions are invariant to alternative component decompositions or finer-grained interventions (Methods, TL-E06 and cross-system protocols).

- **Analyst inference — predictor dependence on costly information:** Prediction requires the actual parameter update, target-specific derivatives, and exact receiving state. The paper does not report computational cost, scaling behavior, or comparisons with simpler predictive baselines beyond local approximations and factor ablations.

- **Analyst inference — incomplete quantitative cross-system reporting:** Section 7 gives seed counts, qualitative recovery claims, and 504 checked diffusion records, but no cross-system effect sizes or prediction metrics comparable to Table 1.

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility not independently verified here:** The paper states that code, evidence, checkpoints, and validators are public and that fresh reproductions were performed, but the supplied text does not include independent third-party replication results.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the target-boundary predictor scale to substantially larger models?** The paper evaluates 12 nanoGPT runs but reports no runtime or memory cost. A next experiment could measure accuracy and derivative-computation cost across increasing model sizes and compare against first-order and low-rank approximations.

2. **Is “distributed functional support” stable under different component partitions?** The experiments use four predefined residual components or routes. A next experiment could repeat gating and rollback at layer, head, channel, and learned-subnetwork granularity and compare support-allocation conclusions.

3. **How broadly do the dynamics generalize?** Cross-system validation covers CIFAR-based ResNet and diffusion models with three seeds each. A next experiment could preregister the same protocols for larger datasets, other optimizers, and non-CIFAR vision or language architectures.

4. **Can feedback concentration predict capability loss before accuracy declines?** The paper reports support concentration, margin decline, and later capability loss but does not provide an early-warning benchmark. A next experiment could test whether support and margin trajectories forecast held-out losses under varied feedback schedules.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text is complete and unusually detailed about protocols, interventions, metrics, seeds, and artifact availability. However, several broad conclusions rely on author-defined constructs and relatively narrow experimental systems.

Material missing information includes computational cost, model sizes and detailed nanoGPT datasets/configurations, uncertainty intervals for several correlations and effect sizes, full confusion matrices, quantitative cross-system results, results of all factor ablations, comparisons with alternative mechanistic-analysis methods, and independent external replication.
