# Acquire, Repair, Preserve: A Diagnosis-Guided Post-Training Recipe for Small-Model Dialogue Game Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28458) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28458)


## One-sentence takeaway

On Qwen3.5-2B, broad success-only SFT delivered most of the dialogue-game improvement, mechanically targeted turn-local DPO repaired some Wordle protocol failures, and LoRA-delta scaling retained large in-domain gains with nearly unchanged aggregate static score, but transfer beyond related Wordle variants remained weak.

## Problem and motivation

Small language models acting in multi-turn dialogue games must maintain state, interpret feedback, and obey changing action constraints; they can therefore fail through local decisions—such as repeating a rejected guess or emitting an invalid-length word—even when they understand the protocol and produce plausible language (Introduction; Tables 1 and 7).

The authors argue that trajectory-level feedback does not identify which decision should receive supervision. Their gap is thus how to place post-training supervision so that a 2B model improves interactive behavior without sacrificing general static-benchmark performance. This matters because the official 2B baseline scores only 10.67 public clemscore, versus 60.30 for the 27B baseline, while small models are cheaper and more suitable for constrained deployment (Introduction; Table 3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Acquire broad participation:** success-only SFT over 9,522 dialogue rows, capped at 700 examples per game, teaches the model to participate across the game suite (Section 3.1; Tables 5–6).

2. **Repair diagnosed local failures:** Wordle-specific checks create DPO pairs with a fixed dialogue history and one mechanically corrupted response. Two passes target repeated guesses, bad length, invalid words, and duplicate answer fields; a subsequent branch-DPO stage contrasts immediate responses from high- and low-scoring sibling rollouts (Sections 3.2 and A.3.3–A.3.4).

3. **Preserve aggregate static capability:** the learned LoRA delta is scaled by \(s=0.85\), selected to maximize static score subject to an interactive-performance gate, without further gradient updates (Section 3.3; Equation 3).

4. **Empirical supervision-placement evidence:** turn-local preference learning produced a clearer signal than whole-dialogue DPO, which caused protocol violations, although only the first turn-DPO pass improved clemscore consistently across both evaluation rails (Sections 5.1 and A.5–A.6; Tables 2 and 8).

5. **Audited training account:** the authors identify and remove two nominal stages that silently made no weight updates, documenting the implementation failures and corrected reruns (Appendix A.4).

The official endpoint establishes a large improvement over the 2B baseline on public and closed in-domain dialogue games, with aggregate—but not component-wise—static-score preservation. It does not establish broad OOD generalization or causal contributions from every phase: later-stage effects are inconsistent across evaluation rails, and the sequential pipeline was repeatedly selected using a small development set.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Mechanically verified repair is demonstrated only for Wordle and not for errors requiring semantic or strategic judgment; most positive OOD movement comes from related Wordle variants (Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** The method is evaluated on one Qwen3.5-2B base, so applicability across model families and scales is untested (Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** Repair follows a single capped SFT parent. The study does not hold the repair setup fixed while varying acquisition quality, so it cannot determine whether repair complements stronger SFT or compensates for this particular parent (Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** The 67-episode development set is small, reused for phase and scaling decisions, and exhibits measurable noise; adjacent checkpoints therefore do not provide additive causal attribution (Section 2; Appendix A.4; Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** Turn-DPO pass 2 and branch-DPO are sign-inconsistent across evaluation rails, leaving their necessity and magnitude uncertain (Tables 2 and 8; Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** Static preservation is aggregate only; substantial declines in IFEval, EQ-Bench, CLadder, and MMLU-Pro can be offset by the BBH increase (Table 10; Limitations).

- **Author-stated:** The official metadata does not record the exact clembench game-tree revision, weakening exact reproduction of the primary evaluation (Appendix A.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The three-episode public Wordle probe is too small to quantify repair reliability, especially because delta scaling reintroduces bad-length aborts on all three fixed episodes (Appendix C; Table 14).

- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons to other challenge submissions demonstrate competitiveness, not method superiority, because their training data and procedures are not controlled in this paper (Table 3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does turn-local repair remain useful after stronger acquisition?** The paper tests repair after only one capped SFT recipe. A factorial experiment could train several acquisition parents with different coverage or data volumes, then apply the identical repair corpus and DPO configuration to each.

2. **Can local diagnostics extend beyond mechanically checkable Wordle errors?** Semantic and strategic failures are outside the demonstrated scope. A next experiment could define auditable state-based diagnostics for navigation or reference games and compare local DPO with whole-trajectory supervision.

3. **Can preservation prevent component-level regressions?** Aggregate scaling masks substantial benchmark redistribution. A multi-objective selection experiment could impose per-benchmark floors and compare the resulting interactive/static Pareto frontier with scalar delta scaling.

4. **What improves genuinely unrelated OOD participation?** OOD Played falls and most positive movement comes from Wordle-crazy variants. A held-out-family selection suite or broader repair curriculum could test whether diagnostics spanning multiple game families improve OOD Clemscore and Played.
