# LayerRecall: A State-Conditioned Memory Router for Long-Horizon Consistency in Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28460) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28460)


## One-sentence takeaway

LayerRecall improves long-range recall in bounded-context autoregressive video diffusion by retrieving history according to the current state and injecting it only at profiled memory-sensitive layers, achieving the strongest reported MemoBench and MovieBench overall scores while preserving its backbone’s VBench-Long score.

## Problem and motivation

Chunk-autoregressive video generators use bounded recent context for efficient long rollouts, but this evicts evidence needed when subjects, objects, attributes, or scenes disappear and later return. Existing memory mechanisms expose models to older information, yet the authors argue that access alone is insufficient: temporal-attention profiling shows different DiT layers favor current, recent, or distant context, with the relevant layers varying by backbone (Section 1; Figures 1–2 and 9). The identified gap is therefore joint control of both which historical content is retrieved and where in the network it is introduced.

## Approach and contributions

- **Current-conditioned retrieval:** Each layer stores compact summaries of physically resident historical chunks. A learned query conditioned on the current hidden state scores these summaries, and the selected chunks’ full K/V tensors enter attention (Section 3.1; Figure 3).

- **Layer-selective injection:** Retrieved K/V states are injected only into a fixed, profiled, backbone-specific set of memory-sensitive layers. Other layers retain the original sink-plus-sliding-window context to protect local temporal modeling (Section 3.1).

- **Cross-Horizon Prediction Matching (CHPM):** A frozen bounded-memory student learns the router by matching denoised-latent predictions from the same frozen backbone operating as a privileged full-context teacher. This requires neither explicit retrieval labels nor backbone fine-tuning (Section 3.2; Figure 4).

- **Empirical analysis:** The paper reports layer-wise temporal preferences, controlled routing and training ablations, transfer to two other backbones without router retraining, and runtime measurements (Sections 4.3–4.4; Appendix A–C).

The results establish gains on the paper’s held-out reappearance-focused prompt bank and support the individual roles of CHPM and layer selection under the reported controls. They do not establish universal performance across arbitrary video distributions or backbones. No uncertainty intervals or statistical significance tests are reported, and the MemoBench/MovieBench results depend on a VLM evaluator sampling 12 frames per video (Appendix C.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Cross-backbone portability is demonstrated only for LongLive and Self-Forcing; the authors explicitly avoid claiming universal zero-shot transfer (Section 4.4; Appendix A.1 and A.3).

- **Author-stated design limitation:** The insertion policy is fixed and backbone-specific. Adaptive layer activation and compressed memory are left to future work (Section 5).

- **Analyst inference — narrow evaluation distribution:** The main bank contains only 100 held-out three-shot prompts, balanced across four constructed axes. This supports targeted reappearance evaluation but leaves natural, unstructured, or substantially longer narratives untested (Table 5; Appendix C.1).

- **Analyst inference — comparison sensitivity:** Several baselines lack a native 48-block prompt interface and receive adapted single prompts. Although the adaptation policy is documented, different interfaces may affect comparability (Appendix C.3; Table 7).

- **Analyst inference — evaluator validity:** MemoBench-style and MovieBench-style scoring uses `gemini-3-flash-preview` on 12 sampled frames. The text reports no human validation, evaluator agreement, or uncertainty estimates (Appendix C.4).

- **Analyst inference — policy-selection risk:** Candidate layer policies are compared before fixing the final allowlist, but the supplied text does not fully quantify how many policies were tried or the selection variance. This makes the degree of policy-selection tuning unclear (Appendix B.3).

- **Analyst inference — resource demands:** Training requires 128 H100 GPU-hours on 16 H100s, despite updating only the router, which may limit independent reproduction (Table 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Can insertion layers be selected dynamically per chunk or prompt?** The current “where-to-use” policy is fixed per backbone despite current-dependent retrieval. A next experiment could train a sparse per-layer gate and compare it with the fixed allowlist under an equal compute and memory budget.

2. **Does LayerRecall remain effective over much longer and less scripted narratives?** Evaluation uses 384 latent frames and structured reappearance prompts. A next experiment could test several-hourglass horizons with variable return gaps and naturally authored multi-scene stories.

3. **How robust are gains to memory compression and smaller physical caches?** The reported student maintains ten physical chunks and retrieves up to two. A next experiment could sweep cache size, retrieval slots, and quantized or compressed K/V representations while measuring recall, temporal stability, and runtime.

4. **Do automatic memory metrics correspond to human judgments?** The main memory scores come from a VLM-based protocol without reported human validation. A blinded human study could compare identity, attribute, layout, and narrative consistency rankings against MemoBench and MovieBench scores.
