# Video Generative Models as Geometry Learner

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28549) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28549)


## One-sentence takeaway

GeoNeXt repurposes Stable Video Diffusion to jointly generate an image, affine-invariant depth, and surface normals as successive frames, achieving strong zero-shot geometry estimation with 59K synthetic training samples.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses joint monocular depth and surface-normal estimation, an ill-posed problem important for reconstruction, inverse rendering, and autonomous driving. The authors argue that existing diffusion approaches either train separate task-specific models—missing depth–normal correlations—or modify image-diffusion architectures substantially, increasing supervision requirements and weakening transfer from pretraining. They propose exploiting a video model’s temporal dependencies as priors for coherent image–geometry modeling (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Geometry as next frames:** GeoNeXt represents the RGB image, depth map, and normal map as an ordered frame sequence and learns the conditional distribution of geometry given the image (Sec. 3.2, pp. 6–8).
- **Joint reconstruction:** Image, depth, and normals are denoised together; reconstructing the image is intended to preserve appearance–geometry consistency and fine detail (Fig. 2; Secs. 3.2–3.3).
- **Lightweight video-model adaptation:** Starting from Stable Video Diffusion, the method freezes the VAE, disables CLIP cross-attention, and fine-tunes only the denoising U-Net (Fig. 2; Sec. 4, p. 8).
- **Data-efficient unified estimation:** One model predicts both geometric modalities after training on approximately 59K synthetic Hypersim and Virtual KITTI 2 samples (Sec. 4, pp. 8–9).
- **Protocol analysis:** Ablations examine image reconstruction, CLIP conditioning, joint versus separate estimation, and depth/normal frame order (Tables 3–4).

The tables support competitive zero-shot performance, gains over the reported unified generative baseline, and benefits from joint reconstruction and training. They do not isolate whether gains arise from video pretraining itself versus model scale, pretraining data, or other implementation differences. One narrative statement says GeoNeXt surpasses a baseline with “13.1 vs. 5.6” AbsRel on ETH3D, but lower is better and Table 1 assigns 13.1 to DA-V2 and 5.6 to GeoNeXt; the table supports the intended conclusion, while the prose ordering is erroneous (p. 10).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The method predicts affine-invariant rather than absolute metric depth (Sec. 2.1).
- **Author-stated runtime omission:** Table 5 excludes model-loading and GPU I/O overhead, despite discussing it as a deployment cost (Sec. 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited training diversity:** Training uses only two synthetic datasets, with a 9:1 indoor/outdoor sampling ratio; robustness to broader training distributions is therefore not established (Sec. 4).
- **Analyst inference—partial ablation coverage:** Ablations use only NYUv2 and ETH3D for depth and NYUv2 and iBims-N for normals, not the complete ten-dataset evaluation (Sec. 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution remains uncertain:** There is no reported matched comparison between identically sized image- and video-pretrained backbones, so the specific contribution of temporal priors is not isolated.
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility gaps:** The retrieved text does not report training duration, batch size, hardware used for training, random-seed variability, confidence intervals, or complete preprocessing/evaluation protocols.
- **Internal reporting risk:** The CLIP ablation discussion says conditioning is removed, while the table labels the variant “w/ CLIP Embed.” The intended mapping is ambiguous (Table 3 and accompanying text).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gain comes specifically from video pretraining?** The paper lacks a capacity- and data-matched image-diffusion control. A next experiment could initialize the same architecture from image-only and video-pretrained checkpoints and fine-tune both identically.
2. **Does joint learning remain beneficial with more geometric modalities?** Only depth and normals are evaluated. A controlled extension could add segmentation, edges, or camera parameters and measure positive versus negative task transfer.
3. **Can the method produce metric depth without sacrificing cross-camera generalization?** The current output is affine-invariant. An experiment could condition on camera intrinsics and compare metric and affine-invariant performance across unseen cameras.
4. **Can stochastic inference be made cheaper and more stable?** Reported default evaluation uses five denoising steps and five seeds. Distillation or deterministic sampling could be tested against accuracy, variance, and latency at matched NFEs.
