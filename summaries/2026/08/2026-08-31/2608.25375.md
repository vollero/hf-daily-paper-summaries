# GGSS: Geodesic-Gated Spherical Steering for Inference-Time Debiasing of Generative Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.25375) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25375)


## One-sentence takeaway

GGSS reduces measured race- and gender-related output disparities in four frozen generative VLMs by selectively rotating visual-token activations along norm-preserving spherical paths, while MMStar accuracy remains within 0.6 percentage points of the unsteered models.

## Problem and motivation

Generative VLMs can produce different outputs for counterfactual images that vary only in perceived race or gender, creating risks in human-centered applications. The authors argue that existing inference-time debiasers largely target single global embeddings in CLIP-like or language models, whereas generative VLMs expose many visual tokens with heterogeneous demographic signal (§1).

They identify three gaps: Euclidean corrections can distort activation norms and directions; uniform projection can unnecessarily alter low-bias tokens; and a single bias direction cannot represent multi-class attributes such as perceived race (§1). Their goal is therefore a lightweight intervention for frozen models that preserves activation geometry and adapts correction strength token by token.

## Approach and contributions

- **Counterfactual spherical subspace discovery:** GGSS extracts pooled activations from pixel-aligned counterfactual photographs, computes within-context Fréchet means, maps demographic shifts into tangent space, and uses SVD to learn a multi-dimensional protected-attribute basis (§3.1, Eq. 1).

- **Token-adaptive gating:** At inference time, each token’s projection magnitude onto the learned basis is compared with discovery-set statistics. A sigmoid gate steers tokens with larger protected-coordinate magnitudes more strongly (§3.2, Eq. 3).

- **Norm-preserving geodesic steering:** Protected coordinates are removed in tangent space; the token is then rotated toward the resulting target through spherical linear interpolation and restored to its original norm (§3.2, Eqs. 2–5; Proposition 1).

- **Unified evaluation suite:** The authors adapt ten CLIP- and LM-era steering baselines to the same late vision-to-language projection layer, add prompt mitigation and structural ablations, and report released implementations (§4.1, “Baselines”; §1 contributions).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that GGSS is comparatively consistent across the tested models and bias probes, with small measured MMStar changes. It does not establish complete fairness, removal of biased knowledge, robustness under distribution shift, or superiority across arbitrary architectures and bias definitions. Average percentage changes are also not strictly comparable across models because the LLaVA averages exclude their zero-baseline 2AFC task (Table 1 note).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers four VLMs, three bias protocols, perceived race and binary gender, and principally MMStar. It does not establish generalization to other architectures, languages, domains, protected attributes, intersectional groups, or open-ended uses (“Limitations—Scope”).

- **Calibration dependence:** α is selected separately for each method/model from five candidates using bias measurements. The authors acknowledge that this can make absolute reductions optimistic, although cross-fitted results remain negative in every fold (“Limitations—Calibration”; Appendix B.5).

- **Fairness is not guaranteed:** The intervention changes activations but leaves model parameters and biased knowledge intact; behavior under distribution shift is untested (“Limitations—Deployment”; “Residual risk”).

- **Attribute-removal trade-off:** At bias-minimizing strengths, reportability of the targeted demographic attribute can fall sharply, potentially harming tasks where that information is relevant (Appendix B.8, Table 13).

- **Operational labels:** Race and binary gender are curator-assigned perceptions rather than self-identification and do not represent the full diversity or intersectionality of identity (“Ethics—Demographic labels”).

- **Analyst inference—limited geometry evidence:** The strongest evidence that spherical steering is preferable occurs on the single 12B backbone; at 4B–7B, matched-gate geometry differences are within sampling noise, and Qwen3 point estimates favor a Euclidean variant (Appendix B.11, Table 15).

- **Analyst inference—benchmark validity:** The bias probes quantify disparities in narrowly controlled salary, income-comparison, and nurse/doctor questions. Their relationship to harms in open-ended or consequential deployments is not measured.

## Open research questions

1. **Can steering strength be selected without labeled bias evaluation?**  
   α remains model-dependent and is tuned using measured bias. A next experiment could derive α from discovery-set projection statistics and evaluate it prospectively on unseen identities, tasks, and models.

2. **Does GGSS transfer beyond perceived race and binary gender?**  
   Other attributes and intersectional groups are outside the evaluation. A factorial counterfactual study could learn joint or separate subspaces for age, disability, and intersecting attributes and measure both cross-attribute interference and retention.

3. **How robust is steering under natural distribution shift?**  
   Discovery uses aligned, face-only photographs and the paper does not establish robustness in broader scenes. A next experiment could test fixed checkpoints on uncontrolled lighting, occlusion, cultural context, multiple people, and non-face demographic cues.

4. **When does spherical geometry materially outperform chordal or Euclidean updates?**  
   Matched-gate differences are decisive only on Pixtral-12B in the reported study. Scaling experiments across more architectures, layers, activation norms, and aggressive α values could identify predictors of geometric failure.
