# Locate Anything in Videos: Rethinking Efficient Generative Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-31.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28192) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28192)


## One-sentence takeaway

Parallel Tube Decoding generates a video’s temporal interval first and all time-conditioned bounding boxes simultaneously, giving fixed two-round decoding with substantially lower decode latency and stronger reported grounding accuracy than autoregressive tube generation.

## Problem and motivation

Spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) must identify when a language-referred event occurs and localize its target with a bounding box at every grounded time step. Existing generative multimodal language models serialize these boxes autoregressively, so latency grows with tube length and early coordinate errors can influence later boxes. Alternatives often reduce this cost by moving spatial localization into separate detectors, trackers, or specialized decoders, sacrificing a fully native generative interface. The authors target the remaining trajectory-level dependency after token quantization and within-box parallelization (Introduction; Sec. 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Parallel Tube Decoding (PTD):** Predicts one temporal block, then generates every time-conditioned spatial block in parallel. Sequential depth is reduced from \(4+7T\) rounds for quantized token decoding and \(1+T\) for sequential block decoding to \(1+1\), independent of the number of boxes \(T\) (Sec. 3.1).
- **Decoupled Block Attention:** Each spatial block attends to the shared video-query context and predicted temporal block, with bidirectional attention internally but no attention to other spatial blocks (Sec. 3.2; Fig. 3).
- **Dual-formulation training:** Joint next-token and multi-token targets preserve ordinary autoregressive generation while teaching structured block prediction in one forward pass (Sec. 3.2).
- **Localization-aware optimization:** GRPO combines temporal IoU reward with a spatial reward based on generalized IoU and an \(L_1\) coordinate penalty (Sec. 3.4).
- **Efficiency analysis and transfer evaluation:** The paper introduces decode-only Tube Completion Latency and Boxes Per Second measurements, then evaluates in-domain STVG and zero-shot transfer to temporal grounding, grounded VideoQA, and referring video object tracking (Secs. 3.3, 4.2–4.4).

The controlled VidSTG comparison supports the claim that removing sequential dependencies can improve decode-only latency without reducing the reported localization metrics. The broader tables show competitive benchmark performance and zero-shot transfer under the authors’ chosen protocols. They do not establish end-to-end latency improvements because TCL excludes multimodal prefill, nor do they isolate every training difference behind comparisons with independently reported prior systems.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Temporal boundaries remain difficult for short, visually ambiguous state transitions; spatial errors occur for small, fast-moving, or occluded targets (Sec. 5; Fig. 5).
- Standard STVG assumes one continuous event interval and one tube, leaving disjoint occurrences, out-of-view periods, and multiple matching instances largely unexplored (Sec. 5).
- Dense STVG training data is limited, with VidSTG and HC-STVG serving as the primary resources (Sec. 5).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- The efficiency results are decode-only, batch 1, BF16 measurements on one AMD MI210 and exclude video/prompt prefill; end-to-end and cross-hardware speedups remain uncertain (Secs. 3.3, 4.1).
- PTD’s latency advantage up to 64 boxes does not establish scaling beyond the paper’s maximum 64-frame input setting (Sec. 4.1; Fig. 4a).
- The final main model receives two SFT epochs plus GRPO, whereas the controlled decoding variants receive one SFT epoch; Table 1 isolates decoding more cleanly than comparisons involving the final model (Sec. 4.1).
- Tracking requires an external SAM2 or SAM3 segmentation model, so those results do not demonstrate standalone pixel-level tracking by PTD (Sec. 4.4; Table 7).
- No statistical uncertainty or repeated-run variance is reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can PTD handle multiple disjoint event intervals or several valid target instances?** The present formulation emits one interval and one tube. A next experiment could extend the output to multiple temporal and spatial block groups and evaluate on newly annotated multi-event, multi-instance cases.

2. **How does parallel decoding scale for substantially longer videos and denser trajectories?** Testing stops at 64 sampled frames. A useful experiment would vary hundreds or thousands of temporal anchors while reporting memory, prefill time, TCL, and end-to-end latency.

3. **How robustly can identity be recovered after long occlusions?** Figure 5 identifies occlusion as a failure mode, while decoupled boxes cannot directly condition on earlier predicted boxes. An experiment could compare PTD with limited local cross-frame attention under controlled occlusion lengths.

4. **Which gains come from PTD versus extra optimization in the final model?** Table 1 controls decoding strategy, and Table 8 controls rewards, but the final benchmark model also receives longer SFT. A factorial experiment crossing decoding mode, SFT duration, and GRPO would better separate their effects.
