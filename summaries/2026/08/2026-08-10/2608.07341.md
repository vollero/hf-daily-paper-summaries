# Zero Gap Is Not Restoration: Stratified Per-Question Probability Evaluation and Step-wise Mitigation of Benchmark Contamination

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07341) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07341)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors propose a stratified per-question restoration metric that exposes misleading aggregate-score cancellation and a decoding intervention, RailCap, that achieves the lowest reported restoration gap across six simulated-contamination settings.

## Problem and motivation

Public benchmark items may enter language-model training data, allowing memorization to inflate evaluation scores. Decoding-time mitigation aims to recover a contaminated model’s genuine capability, but the prevailing gap-of-aggregate-performance metric (G-AP) can misrepresent restoration because it uses unstable single-response correctness, permits over- and under-suppression to cancel, and ignores how unevenly clean-model solve probabilities are distributed (§§1, 3).

The authors identify a broader methodological gap: earlier mitigation methods used different readouts and had not been compared under one per-question probabilistic metric (§2). Consequently, an aggregate gap near zero could be mistaken for restoration even when individual questions remain substantially distorted.

## Approach and contributions

1. **SA-PPG evaluation metric.** For each question, the method estimates clean and mitigated contaminated-model solve probabilities from repeated samples, computes their absolute per-question gap, groups questions into equal-width strata based on clean-model solve probability, and averages first within strata and then across non-empty strata (§§3.2–3.3).

2. **Metric analysis.** The paper formally shows that aggregate differencing can cancel residual contamination against collateral damage, whereas per-question absolute gaps equal zero only when every question’s solve probability matches the clean reference (§3.2.2).

3. **RailCap mitigation.** RailCap greedily decodes each question once to construct an n-gram trajectory index. During sampling, whenever the generated suffix matches the greedy trajectory, it caps that trajectory’s next-token logit at the current runner-up logit (§4; Algorithm 1).

4. **Controlled evaluation.** SA-PPG and RailCap are tested using simulated contamination across three model families and two contamination domains; RailCap records the lowest SA-PPG in every reported setting (§§5.1, 5.3.1).

These results support the authors’ claims that G-AP can conceal per-question errors and that RailCap performs best under SA-PPG within the controlled experiments. They do not establish recovery of an unobservable “genuine capability” in naturally contaminated deployed models: restoration is operationalized as matching a separately trained clean reference under simulated leakage.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The experiments cover three open base-model families, GSM8K, and a paraphrased GSM8K variant, with contamination introduced through controlled fine-tuning (§5.1). No broader task or natural-contamination evaluation is reported.

- **Analyst inference — clean-reference dependence:** SA-PPG requires a same-domain clean model and its per-question solve probabilities. Such a reference may be unavailable or difficult to certify in real contamination cases (§§3.1, 5.1).

- **Analyst inference — sampling cost and uncertainty:** The protocol generates 50 responses per question from both relevant distributions, while RailCap additionally performs a greedy decode (§§3.2.1, 4, 5.1). The supplied text reports no confidence intervals, repeated training seeds, or sensitivity analysis for the number of samples or 50-bin choice.

- **Analyst inference — narrow ablation:** RailCap’s threshold and suppression-form ablations are confined to Llama-2 × GSM8K (Table 4), so their robustness across other models and PQ is untested here.

- **Analyst inference — metric weighting judgment:** Equal weighting across occupied probability strata intentionally gives rare strata more influence per question (§3.3). The paper demonstrates protection against All-Zero but does not compare alternative stratification schemes or analyze variance in small strata.

- **Analyst inference — proxy validity:** Matching the clean model’s solve probability is treated as restoration. The supplied evidence does not show that probability matching also restores reasoning processes, calibration, output quality, or behavior outside the evaluated prompts.

- **Reproducibility gap:** Training and evaluation hyperparameters are provided, but the text does not establish public release of PQ, code, model checkpoints, generated samples, or exact implementation details for every baseline.

## Open research questions

1. **Does SA-PPG remain reliable without a known clean model?** This paper always uses a same-domain clean reference. A next experiment could estimate references from independently trained models or held-out data and compare those estimates with the controlled clean-model ground truth.

2. **Does RailCap generalize beyond grade-school mathematics?** Only GSM8K and its paraphrased form are evaluated. A next experiment could simulate contamination on knowledge, code, and open-ended generation benchmarks with task-appropriate correctness measures.

3. **How sensitive is SA-PPG to estimation and binning choices?** The study fixes 50 samples and 50 equal-width groups without a reported sensitivity analysis. A next experiment could sweep sample counts and binning rules while measuring ranking stability and confidence intervals.

4. **Does trajectory divergence represent restored reasoning rather than degraded memorization?** RailCap is motivated by dispersion away from the contaminated greedy trajectory, but dispersion alone need not imply clean reasoning. A next experiment could compare reasoning traces, error types, and calibration between clean, contaminated, and mitigated models at matched solve probability.
