# StreamArena: Toward Continuous, Interactive, and Long-Horizon Agentic Streaming Video Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05703) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05703)


## One-sentence takeaway

StreamArena pairs an hour-scale, causally timestamped streaming-video benchmark with StreamMind, a persistent multi-worker architecture that leads evaluated streaming systems across perception, retrospection, tool use, and proactive interaction, though backbone and protocol differences limit architectural attribution.

## Problem and motivation

Autonomous multimodal agents must continuously process audio-visual streams, remember distant events, respond quickly, use external information, and initiate alerts without fresh prompts. The authors argue that existing evaluations usually isolate these abilities, use short clips or multiple-choice questions, and therefore admit language and recency shortcuts—including systems that inspect only the latest few frames (§1; Table 1).

StreamArena targets the missing joint evaluation of continuous causal access, hour-scale memory, open-ended answering, proactive monitoring, and tool-enabled reasoning. This matters for always-on assistants operating in settings where important evidence may occur long before a query or where an intervention must be initiated autonomously (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **StreamArena benchmark:** 243 videos lasting 60–134.2 minutes, averaging 88.8 minutes, with 3,646 open-ended tasks across real-time perception, historical retrospection, multimodal tool use, and proactive interaction (§2.1; Figure 1; Table 6).
- **Causally grounded annotation:** Every task records query and supporting-evidence timestamps. Drafts undergo two independent cross-validations and a third blind audit; approximately 73% are retained (§2.1; Appendix B.1).
- **StreamMind architecture:** A frontend handles immediate responses and independently scheduled monitoring, while asynchronous backend workers construct persistent hierarchical event, entity-relation, and key-frame memory and perform recall and external search (§2.2; Figure 2).
- **Cross-paradigm evaluation:** The paper evaluates offline MLLMs, recent-window systems, text summaries, internal compression, and StreamMind under method-specific causal protocols (§3.1; Table 10).
- **Released assets:** The authors state that annotations, evaluation and baseline adapters, and the StreamMind implementation are released; raw YouTube videos are represented by IDs rather than redistributed (Appendix C.7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| StreamMind leads the evaluated streaming systems on all four capabilities. | Accuracy: 44.5% RTP, 34.9% HR, 56.1% Tool, and 11.6% Proactive. Reported relative gains over the strongest streaming baseline for each capability are 58.4%, 53.7%, 228.1%, and 54.7%, respectively. | Table 3; §3.2 |
| StreamMind’s historical recall deteriorates with distance. | HR accuracy is 31.5% at ≤5 minutes, 46.7% at 5–15 minutes, 34.6% at 15–30 minutes, and 17.1% beyond 30 minutes. | Table 3 |
| Persistent state substantially lowers response latency against a shared-backbone offline system. | Against Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, pooled latency falls from 81.4 to 27.5 seconds, a reported 66.2% reduction, while retaining 89.7% of pooled accuracy; task-specific reductions are 84.6% RTP, 73.9% HR, and 55.3% Tool. | Table 4; §3.3 |
| The benchmark imposes substantial temporal and multi-evidence demands. | HR has a 12.1-minute median evidence-to-query gap; 49 questions exceed one hour; 189/877 HR questions require at least two evidence segments, with a maximum of 15. | §2.1; Tables 2 and 9 |
| RTP benefits from combining vision and speech. | On the 616-question diagnostic subset using Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, RTP accuracy is 4.2% with ASR only, 26.8% with 128 visual frames, and 32.4% with vision plus ASR. | Table 5; §3.4 |
| Human performance also reflects the difficulty of streaming memory. | Human HR accuracy is reported as 80.7% with rewatching versus 63.4% without rewatching; human RTP, Tool, and Proactive scores are 91.8%, 95.2%, and 91.5%. | Table 3; §3.2 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that StreamMind offers broader capability coverage and lower response latency than the evaluated streaming baselines. It does **not** isolate every gain to the architecture: StreamMind uses a much larger backbone than several streaming competitors, baseline harnesses reconstruct causal inputs rather than preserving state, and each result comes from one run without uncertainty estimates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** StreamMind’s HR accuracy falls sharply at long horizons; the authors report a 63.4% decrease from the L2 to L4 strata and argue that future queries make retention decisions difficult (§4).
- **Author-stated:** Comparisons with smaller-backbone streaming systems confound architecture and model capacity. The same-backbone comparison isolates latency and accuracy trade-offs only against an offline system, not against another continuous architecture (§4).
- **Author-stated:** Each reported cell is based on a single run; there are no standard deviations or statistical-significance tests because evaluations are expensive (Appendix C.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Gemini 3.1 Pro is the sole factual-equivalence judge, but the supplied text reports no human–judge agreement study or judge-sensitivity analysis. This leaves metric validity uncertain (§2.1; Appendix C.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Baselines and StreamMind receive materially different ingestion mechanisms, frame budgets, model sizes, and sometimes audio representations. These choices reflect native interfaces but complicate causal claims about memory design (§3.1; Appendices C.1–C.5).
- **Analyst inference:** The dataset is geographically and linguistically narrow: 189 of 243 videos have Chinese audio, and sensitive, inappropriate, and political content is excluded. Generalization to other languages and high-risk domains is therefore unestablished (Appendix B.1 and B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility depends on mutable YouTube content, public preservation copies, Google search results, and hosted model APIs whose behavior is time-dependent (Appendices C.6–C.7).
- **Analyst inference:** No component-level ablation isolates the contributions of key-frame retention, event hierarchy, entity graph, worker separation, or retrieval routing; the supplied Figure 3 diagnostics vary inputs and reasoning mode rather than these architectural components.

## Open research questions

1. **Which StreamMind components actually cause the accuracy gains?** This remains open because no component-level ablation is reported. A next experiment could remove the key frames, entity graph, event hierarchy, monitors, and asynchronous worker separation one at a time under the same backbone and ingestion rate.

2. **Can smaller models obtain similar benefits from persistent multi-worker memory?** The paper explicitly identifies backbone scale as a confound. A controlled model-size sweep could compare StreamMind and continuous-memory baselines using identical backbones, frame rates, search access, and token budgets.

3. **How reliable is LLM-judged open-ended accuracy?** The paper uses one judge without reporting agreement with human raters. A blinded study could compare several judges against expert annotations and report agreement, false-positive rates, and rank stability.

4. **How can distant visual evidence be retained efficiently before its future relevance is known?** HR drops to 17.1% beyond 30 minutes. A plausible experiment would train or adapt retention policies from later retrieval success and evaluate accuracy, storage, and compute across temporal strata.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the main paper, task definitions, results, detailed implementation settings, and reproducibility notes.

Material missing information includes numerical values from the Figure 3 sweeps, human validation of the LLM judge, component-level architectural ablations, repeated-run uncertainty, total compute or energy cost, and direct evidence that all claimed release artifacts remain accessible and executable.
