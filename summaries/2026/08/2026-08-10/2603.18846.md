# Towards Interpretable Foundation Models for Retinal Fundus Images

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.18846) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18846)


## One-sentence takeaway

Dual-IFM combines self-supervised retinal-image representations, intrinsic patch-level evidence maps, and direct 2D dataset visualization while achieving broadly RETFound-comparable classification performance with substantially fewer parameters.

## Problem and motivation

Retinal foundation models can transfer well across clinical tasks, but their predictions generally lack built-in, faithful explanations; post-hoc attribution may not reflect the actual decision process. Their high-dimensional representation spaces are also difficult to inspect globally, while methods such as t-SNE require refitting for new samples. The authors address both gaps for color fundus photographs (CFPs): faithful local evidence for individual predictions and a reusable dataset-level visualization of learned structure (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions

- **Interpretable-by-design encoder:** Dual-IFM uses BagNet-33, whose \(33\times33\)-pixel receptive fields restrict features to local image patches. A \(1\times1\) convolution produces class-specific activation maps, and spatial averaging converts this local evidence directly into class scores (§2.1, §2.3).
- **Joint representation learning and visualization:** Three-stage t-SimCNE pretraining learns high-dimensional downstream representations alongside a parametric 2D projector that can embed new images without refitting (§2.1–§2.2).
- **Large-scale retinal pretraining:** The model is self-supervised on 802,360 CFPs from EyePACS, AREDS, and UK Biobank, then evaluated through linear probing and fine-tuning across in-distribution and out-of-distribution disease-classification datasets (§2.4).
- **Two-level interpretability evaluation:** The paper assesses global disease structure using 2D k-NN AUROC and local lesion correspondence using overlap between the ten most activated patches and IDRiD lesion annotations (§3.2–§3.3).
- **Released assets:** The abstract states that code and pretrained models are available through the authors’ repository.

The results support the authors’ claim that intrinsic local evidence and a direct 2D projection can coexist with competitive retinal classification representations. They do not establish clinical utility, causal correctness of highlighted regions, superiority on every dataset, or parity under fully matched architecture, compute, and pretraining-data conditions. Exact classification scores and uncertainty estimates cannot be assessed because the retrieved text references Tables 2–4 without reproducing their contents.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is restricted to CFPs, so generalization to other imaging modalities is untested (Discussion).
- **Author-stated:** Fine-tuning used a limited hyperparameter search; the authors attribute occasional degradation on PAPILA and FIVES to possible overfitting (§3.1, Discussion).
- **Author-stated:** BagNet’s large spatial feature maps create high memory use despite its low parameter count, and pretraining requires a relatively small batch size (Discussion).
- **Author-stated:** A frozen 2D projector may become unreliable when fine-tuning substantially shifts encoder representations; realignment may then be necessary (§2.3).
- **Author-stated:** Constraining the learned projection to 2D reduces measured embedding quality relative to some post-hoc PCA results (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Localization is demonstrated only for binary DR on IDRiD using the ten most activated patches. This is narrow evidence for the broader claim of clinically meaningful interpretability (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The comparison with RETFound confounds architecture, parameter count, pretraining corpus size, and interpretability mechanism; the supplied text does not report a controlled component-level ablation.
- **Analyst inference:** Sparsity was selected to maximize localization before classification deteriorated, but the supplied text omits the selected coefficient and full trade-off values, limiting reproducibility (Figure 3, §3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Lesion overlap shows spatial correspondence, not necessarily that clinicians find the maps useful or that all prediction-relevant evidence is medically valid.

## Open research questions

1. **Does intrinsic localization remain reliable across diseases and institutions?** Only IDRiD binary DR localization is quantified. A next experiment could evaluate masked lesion annotations for glaucoma, AMD, and multiple external cohorts using the same patch-overlap protocol.

2. **How stable is the 2D projector after downstream adaptation?** The authors warn that encoder drift can degrade a frozen projector but do not provide a systematic drift analysis. A next experiment could vary fine-tuning depth and measure representation drift, k-NN AUROC, and projector-realignment cost.

3. **Which design component creates the best accuracy–interpretability trade-off?** The supplied results do not isolate receptive-field size, sparsity, projector dimensionality, and pretraining objective in a common ablation. A factorial experiment could vary these components under matched compute and data.

4. **Do the explanations improve clinical decisions?** Lesion overlap does not measure human benefit. A reader study could compare diagnostic accuracy, calibration, and review time with Dual-IFM maps, LRP maps, and no explanation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes the full narrative, methods, and several headline measurements, but omits the numeric contents of Tables 1–4. Consequently, per-dataset classification scores, variability or confidence intervals, full computational comparisons, complete embedding baselines, exact sparsity settings, hyperparameter-search ranges, augmentation details, and statistical significance are unavailable. The text also does not report prospective clinical evaluation, clinician assessment, or broader explanation-faithfulness interventions.
