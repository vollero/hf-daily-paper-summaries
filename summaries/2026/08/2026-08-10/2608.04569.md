# Relevant but Incomplete: Referential Dangling as a Paradigm-Level Failure Mode in Hard Prompt Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04569) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04569)


## One-sentence takeaway

Independent relevance-based hard prompt compression frequently retains answer-bearing text while deleting its required bridge or definition, and restoring that support can recover substantial QA accuracy at the same or nearly the same token budget.

## Problem and motivation

Hard prompt compressors typically score tokens, sentences, or chunks independently and retain the highest-scoring units under a budget. The authors argue that relevance alone does not ensure that the retained context remains interpretable: selection may preserve a task-relevant statement but remove its antecedent, definition, or bridge fact, creating **referential dangling** (Sections 1 and 3; Figure 1).

This matters because the answer string may remain visible while the evidence path needed to connect the question to that answer is broken. The identified gap is that prior compressor objectives generally lack an explicit constraint rewarding joint retention of dependent evidence, and it was previously unclear how systematic this failure is, how much downstream accuracy it costs, or whether it can be repaired automatically (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Formalization and structural result.** The paper defines referential dangling for extractive hard compression using task-relevant sentences and inclusion-minimal sufficient support sets. Proposition 1 shows that an additive fragment-selection objective offers no guarantee of referential completeness, even when a complete selection fits the same budget (Section 3).

2. **Cross-dataset and cross-compressor diagnosis.** The authors approximate dangling through directional content-word coverage and evaluate Beaver on three multi-hop QA datasets plus a separate first-mention diagnostic on LongBench-v2. They also compare six hard compressors spanning embedding, syntax, self-information, learned-token, perplexity, and attention-based scoring (Section 4).

3. **Fixed-budget causal intervention.** On detected dangling cases, the omitted annotated supporting paragraph is restored while at least as many tokens are removed from paragraphs not annotated as support. This tests whether different content—not a larger budget—improves QA accuracy (Section 5; Appendix D).

4. **Automatic restoration prototype.** A roughly 110M-parameter BERT classifier ranks omitted candidate sentences using a retained sentence, candidate sentence, and question. The highest-ranked candidates are reinserted without support annotations at inference (Section 6; Appendix F).

5. **Released assets.** The authors report releasing code, per-example results, and the trained restoration model (Section 1).

The results establish that the operational dangling diagnostic is common in the tested hard compressors and that changing retained content can substantially improve accuracy on identified Beaver failures. The fixed-budget gains strongly support the practical importance of content selection.

They do not prove that every flagged case meets the exact formal definition: the coverage test is an approximation, support annotations may be incomplete, and alternative evidence paths are not enumerated. The reselection comparison also changes both the restored paragraph and the removed distractor content, so it does not isolate the restored paragraph’s causal contribution. Automatic restoration improves over Base, but its advantage over matched random insertion was not statistically established (\(p=0.15\); Section 6 and Table 12).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The formal theory covers extractive hard compression and exactly characterizes only nonoverlapping candidate units selected by a modular knapsack objective. Hierarchical or overlapping selectors are evaluated empirically rather than claimed as exact instances (Section 3).

- **Author-stated:** The diagnostic uses content-word overlap rather than exact semantic sufficiency, and it does not enumerate alternative support paths. The LongBench-v2 first-mention measure also uses a different denominator and is not directly comparable with bridge dangling rates (Section 4).

- **Author-stated:** Fixed-budget reselection depends on support annotations and jointly changes restored and removed content. Consequently, it cannot isolate the effect of the missing paragraph from effects of removing other paragraphs (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** Automatic restoration is a diagnostic pipeline rather than a complete compression system. Candidate generation appears limiting, and gains do not clearly transfer beyond Beaver/HotpotQA or across fragmented compressor outputs (Section 6; Appendix H).

- **Author-stated:** Targeted restoration’s +4.7-point gain exceeded random insertion’s +2.0-point gain, but the direct comparison was not significant (\(p=0.15\)); superiority over random addition is therefore unestablished (Section 6; Table 12).

- **Analyst inference:** The main empirical conclusions are concentrated at compression ratio 0.30, bridge-style QA, and Beaver-centered repair experiments. Although prevalence is tested across ratios and document types, evidence for downstream benefits in broader generation, summarization, or non-QA tasks is absent.

- **Analyst inference:** Choosing \(K=3\) because it produced the largest observed Qwen3-8B gain may introduce tuning optimism unless the choice was confirmed on a separate selection set; the supplied text does not establish such a separation (Appendix F).

## Open research questions

1. **Can dependency completeness be optimized during compression rather than repaired afterward?** Proposition 1 exposes the missing interaction term, but no end-to-end dependency-constrained compressor is evaluated. A next experiment could add learned pairwise or graph-based support rewards to the selection objective and compare accuracy, dangling rate, latency, and compression ratio against the six compressors.

2. **Can semantic dangling be measured without gold support annotations or lexical-overlap heuristics?** The current proxy can miss alternative support paths or confuse lexical survival with interpretability. A next experiment could construct a human-labeled benchmark of retained-fragment sufficiency and compare content-word, coreference, entailment, and model-based diagnostics.

3. **Does restoration generalize across tasks and compressor granularities?** Transfer beyond Beaver and HotpotQA remains unresolved. A next experiment could train on multiple compressors and datasets, then test held-out compressors on long-document QA, summarization, and retrieval-augmented generation.

4. **Is targeted restoration reliably better than adding arbitrary context?** The matched random-control contrast was inconclusive. A larger preregistered paired evaluation could compare targeted, random, salience-based, and oracle additions at identical per-example token budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experiments and the paper’s stated scope because the supplied text includes methods, tables, appendices, sample sizes, and statistical tests.

Material missing or unresolved information includes full prompt templates and answer-scoring details; complete candidate-generation rules; confidence intervals for many accuracy comparisons; compute and latency costs of restoration training/inference; independent replication; and downstream evaluations outside QA. The text also does not establish whether the released URL remained accessible or whether every reported artifact was complete.
