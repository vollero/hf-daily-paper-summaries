# SFT Conflicts, RL Coexists: A Theoretical and Empirical Analysis of Multi-Task Learning for LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03573) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03573)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across four reasoning domains, the authors find that sequential SFT causes cross-task forgetting while on-policy RL produces smaller, sparser, nearly orthogonal updates, motivating a parallel-train-and-merge RL framework that largely preserves single-task gains.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks why multi-task LLM post-training commonly mixes data for SFT but can train tasks sequentially with RL. The identified gap is the lack of a systematic empirical and theoretical comparison of SFT and RL under multi-task training, particularly their effects on capabilities not currently being trained (Sections 1–2).

This matters because interference determines whether new reasoning skills can be added without erasing existing ones and whether task training can be distributed or composed modularly.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Empirical paradigm comparison.** The authors compare mixed-data, multi-stage, and single-task SFT/RL across math, science, logic, and code using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen models (Sections 2 and 5).

2. **Parameter-level diagnosis.** They measure update norms, sparsity, and cross-task cosine similarity, finding much smaller and less aligned updates under RL (Section 3; Figure 2).

3. **Theoretical account.** For GRPO-style RL, zero-sum normalized advantages remove the mean score direction; under bounded-score assumptions, the authors derive norm-limited SFT and variance-limited RL interference bounds (Section 4; Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.5). Appendix C proposes an analogous argument for PPO.

4. **Parallel-RL.** Independently trained task updates are merged by averaging, summation, TIES, rank-1 SVD, or light post-merge adaptation. The method also includes preliminary task-compatibility selection (Section 5; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

Authors summarize average relative changes as −23.1% and +24.9%. | Table 1; Section 2.2 |
| Single-task SFT harms untrained tasks, whereas RL generally preserves or improves them. | Authors report mean target-task gains of 4.0% for SFT and 6.8% for RL, with mean untrained-task changes of −5.1% and +2.3%, respectively, across four single-task runs. | Table 2; Section 2.3 |

| The proposed theory bounds interference differently. | Under Assumption 4.4, the authors derive \(|I_{\mathrm{SFT}}|\le M_iM_j\) and \(|I_{\mathrm{RL}}|\le V_iV_j\), where RL’s bound depends on residual-score variance after normalized advantages cancel the mean direction. | Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.5; Appendix C |

The evidence supports a strong association among normalized on-policy RL, low measured update overlap, reduced forgetting, and successful merging in the tested settings. It does not establish that all RL algorithms, task combinations, or model families behave this way, nor does the empirical evidence isolate advantage normalization from on-policy sampling as the unique cause.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope boundary:** Some task pairs interfere substantially; Parallel-RL therefore requires task selection rather than assuming universal orthogonality (Appendix D.1).
- **Author-stated resource limitation:** The temperature/exploration analysis uses LoRA and a data subset because of resource constraints (Appendix D.2).
- **Narrow model and domain coverage:** Experiments use two sizes from one distilled Qwen model family and primarily four reasoning domains (Sections 2.1 and 5.2; Appendix A).
- **Different SFT and RL training data:** Appendix A.1 explicitly uses different datasets for the two paradigms, complicating causal attribution to the optimization objective alone. This is an analyst inference.
- **Theory depends on material assumptions:** The bounds require independent task sampling, normalized advantages, and bounded score norms or residual variance; approximate orthogonality additionally invokes high-dimensional sparsity or weak residual coupling (Section 4; Appendix C.4).
- **Analyst inference—proof reliability:** The retrieved Appendix C.2 contains dimensionally inconsistent or malformed intermediate inequalities, including apparent missing square roots and inconsistent powers of \(V_i\). The final theorem may therefore require checking against a corrected source or implementation.
- **Heuristic compatibility test:** Task selection relies preliminarily on t-SNE separation, which is visualization-dependent and is not validated as a calibrated decision rule (Appendix D.1).
- **Reproducibility gap:** Frameworks, hyperparameters, and hardware are supplied, but the retrieved text does not give exact dataset subset sizes, random-seed protocol, training duration, compute cost, or a usable code-release identifier.

## Open research questions

1. **Which mechanism matters most: on-policy sampling or advantage normalization?** The theory attributes coexistence to both, but no clean factorial ablation is reported. A next experiment could compare on/off-policy sampling with and without group-centered advantages while holding data and update budgets fixed.

2. **Can task compatibility be predicted without expensive training?** The t-SNE heuristic is preliminary and fails to define a threshold. A next experiment could test whether pre-training gradient statistics predict merge retention across many task pairs, evaluated with held-out pairwise merges.

3. **Does Parallel-RL scale to many heterogeneous tasks and model families?** Evidence covers a small number of reasoning tasks and one base-model family. A next experiment could vary task count and include instruction following or other domains while tracking accuracy, interference, and retention.

4. **How tight and robust are the interference bounds?** The theory provides upper bounds but no reported comparison between measured interference and bound tightness. A next experiment could estimate \(M_i\), \(V_i\), and actual cross-task inner products throughout training across GRPO, PPO, and unnormalized variants.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text is extensive and includes main tables, appendices, and experimental settings, but material uncertainties remain: exact training subset sizes, seeds and run counts, statistical-test methodology, wall-clock or GPU-hour efficiency, complete code/release details, reward definitions, and a cleanly rendered or independently checkable version of the theoretical proofs.
