# Addressable Memory for Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07408) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07408)


## One-sentence takeaway

WorldTrace is a training-free, fixed-cache framework that keeps long-horizon video memories retrievable by assigning compressed entries distinct in-distribution RoPE positions, improving both temporal coherence and scene recall.

## Problem and motivation

Autoregressive video world models store prior frames in a KV cache, but visual persistence degrades beyond the training horizon. The authors identify two coupled causes: temporal RoPE offsets become out-of-distribution, making stored content difficult to address, while averaging keys after RoPE rotation can cancel incompatible positional phases and corrupt summaries (Secs. 1–2). Existing approaches are described as addressing position or content separately; the target gap is a bounded, training-free memory that handles both.

## Approach and contributions

- **Addressable cache:** WorldTrace divides the fixed attention window into verbatim recent frames and compressed summary slots. Each summary receives a distinct, slot-rank-based virtual position within the trained temporal-offset range (Sec. 3.1, Def. 1).
- **WorldTrace-Field:** Contiguous historical groups are unrotated into canonical key space, averaged, and re-rotated at their virtual positions; values are averaged directly. This targets temporal coherence while avoiding RoPE phase cancellation (Sec. 3.3, Def. 2).
- **WorldTrace-Landmark:** Canonical-key changes detect scene entries; selected frames are retained verbatim as frozen canonical keys and freshly rotated when read, targeting episodic recall (Sec. 3.4, Eq. 4).
- **Unified interpretation:** The authors formulate cache compression as structured sparse attention, with temporal averaging and one-hot landmark retention as different projection matrices (Sec. 3.2; Appendix B).
- **Evaluation asset:** LoopBench tests whether a model reconstructs a previously generated scene after controlled detours varying topology, duration, orientation, and repeated visits (Sec. 4.1; Appendix F).

These experiments support the authors’ claims that position assignment matters independently of compression, canonical averaging avoids measurable phase-related distortion, and verbatim landmarks improve controlled revisit recall. They do not establish general superiority across arbitrary video generators, natural-world distributions, unrestricted navigation, or downstream control performance. LoopBench compares return frames with the model’s own earlier generation rather than external ground truth, so it measures self-consistency rather than real-world correctness.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The method assumes an autoregressive generator with temporal RoPE on keys and a known local attention window; visual quality, motion, and action following remain limited by the frozen backbone (Appendix H.1).
- WorldTrace-Field is increasingly lossy because each slot averages roughly \(T/N_s\) frames, blurring frame-level details as the horizon grows (Appendix H.1).
- WorldTrace-Landmark succeeds only when the detector selects the relevant scene and can retain at most \(N_s\) landmarks; older scenes are evicted (Appendix H.1).
- The two writers represent only simple projections: Field favors coherence but may blur details, while Landmark depends on recognizing the correct entries (Conclusion).
- The default Field recompute writer has host-memory state that grows by 5.4 MB per latent frame, although a constant-state streaming alternative is evaluated (Appendix E.6–E.7).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Evaluation breadth is limited primarily to MG2, with a smaller cross-architecture PAC study on LingBot-World; broader model and content diversity is not established.
- LoopBench’s self-generated reference avoids needing ground truth but may reward reproducing an initially incorrect or degraded scene.
- The ± values in Table 3 are not defined in the supplied text, preventing a precise interpretation as standard deviations, standard errors, or confidence intervals.
- The landmark threshold \(\tau\) is introduced but its numerical setting is not provided in the supplied text, weakening exact reproducibility.
- Theoretical near-optimality is conditional on constructed query families and a relaxed objective; the authors explicitly do not measure the relevant \(\epsilon_q\) or \(\delta_q\) conditions on the pretrained models (Appendix B.5).

## Open research questions

1. **Can one writer jointly preserve coherence and recall?** Mixed slot allocations were non-monotonic and sometimes below the sliding-window baseline (Table 8). A next experiment could learn a soft-sparse projection that dynamically chooses averaging versus verbatim retention under a fixed budget.

2. **How robust is scene-entry selection in continuous, ambiguous environments?** Recall depends on detecting and retaining the right landmark, while experiments use controlled trajectories. A next experiment could compare cosine-threshold detection with learned, pose-aware, and semantic detectors on longer paths with more than \(N_s\) revisitable scenes.

3. **Can constant total memory preserve the Field variant’s gains?** The default writer uses linearly growing host memory, while the streaming writer is slightly weaker on some metrics (Tables 13–14). A next experiment could compare streaming coresets or adaptive temporal buckets at much longer horizons under matched CPU and GPU memory.

4. **Does self-consistency translate to geometric or task correctness?** LoopBench does not use external ground truth. A next experiment could evaluate matched-pose recall against recorded reference video or measure whether memory improves closed-loop navigation and action success.
