# DCAS: Decoupling CLI Agent Scaffolding to Internalize Planning across Scaffolds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06113) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06113)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that planning-aware supervised fine-tuning on 576 successful trajectories collected under one CLI scaffold improves a 30B coding agent across that scaffold and two unseen scaffolds, although planning is not fully isolated from other effects of successful-trajectory training.

## Problem and motivation

Open coding-agent models are commonly trained and evaluated under the same CLI scaffold, especially OpenHands, but their performance can fall sharply when the deployment scaffold changes. The authors argue that this matters because scaffold choice is driven by practical constraints such as cost, latency, licensing, and privacy rather than by a model’s training environment (Sections 1–2.2).

Their motivating study finds that fine-tuned models exhibit wider cross-scaffold performance spreads than their untrained bases. They hypothesize that fine-tuning installs scaffold-specific planning conventions—both explicit pre-execution plans and implicit execution structure—while noting that tool formats, parsers, context handling, and turn management are additional confounds (Sections 2.2–2.3 and 9.1).

The tooling gap is the lack of a general way to substitute backend models inside otherwise unmodified scaffolds, evaluate one model across scaffolds, and collect trajectories that preserve planning structure (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **DCAS interception layer:** Translates API requests and responses between a CLI scaffold and a substituted backend model without modifying the scaffold, while logging planning and execution trajectories (Section 3.1; Figure 1).

- **Controlled plan-source intervention:** Under fixed Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct, Claude Code 2.0.76, benchmark, and execution budget, the authors compare no plan, self-generated plans, and externally generated plans to estimate the leverage of explicit plan source (Sections 3.2 and 4).

- **Planning-aware fine-tuning:** From 576 successful GLM-4.7 trajectories collected under Claude Code, they construct PlanOnly and Plan+Exec datasets and fully fine-tune Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct (Sections 3.3–3.5).

- **Planning decomposition and transfer tests:** PlanOnly and Plan+Exec are compared to probe explicit versus implicit planning, followed by evaluation on a newer Claude Code release and two unseen scaffolds, OpenCode and mini-swe-agent (Sections 5–6).

- **Released assets:** The paper reports public release of DCAS source and reproduction scripts, trace logs, both fine-tuned models, and the trajectory dataset (Section 12).

The controlled RQ1 intervention establishes that changing the supplied plan can materially affect the fixed executor’s task success. RQ2 and RQ3 show that the selected planning-aware SFT recipe is associated with improved in-scaffold and cross-scaffold Pass@1.

The evidence does not establish that planning alone causes the SFT gains: training also exposes the model to successful in-domain repair trajectories. Nor does it isolate planning from cross-scaffold differences in tool schemas, parsers, context handling, and turn management. The PlanOnly result is consistent with the authors’ implicit-planning interpretation, but does not uniquely prove it.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Planning is only partially isolated. Across scaffolds, it co-varies with tool-call formats, parsers, context windows, and turn management; component-wise ablations are absent (Section 9.1).

- **Author-stated:** There is no execution-only, token-matched, or degraded-plan training control, so planning structure cannot be separated from general exposure to successful issue-resolution trajectories (Section 9.1).

- **Author-stated:** Rejection sampling retains only successful patches and therefore biases training toward easier instances (Section 9.1).

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is limited to SWE-bench Verified and one SFT target model, so transfer to other tasks, repositories, model scales, architectures, or pretraining distributions is unknown (Section 9.2).

- **Author-stated:** Training uses only Claude Code 2.0.76; unseen-scaffold evaluation covers only OpenCode and mini-swe-agent. Codex CLI could not be evaluated, while Gemini CLI, SWE-agent, Agentless, IDE agents, and GUI agents remain outside the evidence (Sections 6.1 and 9.2).

- **Author-stated:** Pass@1 does not measure turn efficiency, cost, or plan quality directly; self-plan gains may partly reflect execution changes (Section 9.3).

- **Analyst inference:** The claim that PlanOnly “installs implicit planning” is underdetermined because no matched non-planning or execution-only SFT condition tests whether comparable gains follow from generic successful-task supervision.

- **Analyst inference:** The newer-version result demonstrates compatibility across two Claude Code versions, but not an improvement over the corresponding base model because Table 6 provides no base-model score under version 2.1.73.

- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty estimates, repeated-run variance, or statistical significance tests are reported in the supplied text, making small differences—such as 53.8% versus 53.2%—difficult to interpret confidently.

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the SFT gain is uniquely attributable to planning structure?**  
   This remains open because execution-only, token-matched, and degraded-plan controls are missing. A next experiment could train matched PlanOnly, ExecOnly, Plan+Exec, shuffled-plan, and token-budget-controlled variants on the same task instances.

2. **Which scaffold differences cause the remaining portability gap?**  
   Planning co-varies with tool schemas, parsing, context management, and turn policies. A factorial intervention could independently normalize each dimension while holding the model, tasks, and planning content fixed.

3. **Does planning transfer across model families and scales?**  
   All SFT results use one 30B Qwen3-Coder target. Repeating the same datasets and evaluation matrix across smaller, larger, reasoning-oriented, and differently pretrained models would test whether the effect is general.

4. **What makes a plan well calibrated to an executor?**  
   Sonnet-generated plans outperform Opus-generated plans despite the authors describing Opus as the stronger general-purpose model. A plausible experiment would score plan specificity, feasibility, adherence, and executor success across multiple planner–executor pairings.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental configurations and point estimates, because the supplied text includes the complete paper, detailed tables, and stated threats to validity.

Material missing information includes run-to-run variance, confidence intervals or significance tests, training compute and collection cost, rejection-sampling acceptance rate, task-level outcomes, direct plan-quality or adherence measurements, matched non-planning SFT controls, base-model results for Claude Code 2.1.73, and successful evaluation on additional frontier or structurally different scaffolds.
