# Round-Trip Consistency: Bidirectional Diffusion Models Can Predict Their Own Rollout Errors

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00675) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00675)


## One-sentence takeaway

A bidirectional latent diffusion model can use forward-then-backward rollout discrepancy as a ground-truth-free proxy for its rollout error, with strong calibration on MHD but effectiveness that varies with system heterogeneity and inverse-map conditioning.

## Problem and motivation

Autoregressive surrogate models accumulate errors by feeding predictions back as inputs, while the true future state needed to measure those errors is unavailable during deployment. Existing uncertainty signals such as ensemble or sample dispersion quantify model disagreement rather than realized trajectory error and may fail under rollout-induced distribution shift. The authors seek a test-time error estimate requiring neither observations, governing equations, ensembles, nor a separately learned inverse (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Bidirectional dynamics model:** One conditional latent diffusion model learns forward and backward transitions through a direction flag, using shared weights and recent-frame context (Section 4, Equations 1–2).
- **Round-trip error signal:** A prediction is rolled forward \(i\) steps and then backward \(i\) steps. The discrepancy between the returned and original seed pair, \(\mathcal C_i\), is used as a measurable proxy for the unavailable rollout error (Section 5, Equation 3).
- **Calibrated error estimation:** A heteroscedastic Gaussian calibrator maps \(\log \mathcal C_i\) to a distribution over \(\log \mathcal E_i\), enabling magnitude prediction and selective deferral (Sections 6.2–6.3).
- **Conditional theoretical guarantee:** A Lipschitz/co-Lipschitz analysis bounds true error using round-trip discrepancy, backward-model residual, and inverse conditioning; it also formalizes cancellation as the key failure mode (Section 5.1, Proposition 1 and Corollary 1).
- **Additional capabilities:** The backward direction functions as an inverse solver, while shared bidirectional training improves both directions over specialists in the reported low-data ablation (Sections 6.4 and 6.7).

The results establish that round-trip consistency carries useful trajectory-specific error information in several evaluated settings, most strongly on MHD and face video. They do not establish universal reliability: the radiative-layer result is marginal, Navier–Stokes gains over depth-only are small at frame level, and the OOD claim rests on one canonical trajectory. The theoretical result is not an unconditional certificate because the required conditioning constants are not reported as certified measurements.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Round-trip agreement is necessary but not sufficient; forward and backward errors can cancel. The guarantee requires a well-conditioned learned inverse and a small backward residual (Sections 5.1 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** The worst-case bound degrades with rollout depth, and MHD depth-conditional miscalibration reaches 0.27 at depths \(i\ge40\), despite pooled MCA of 0.013 (Section 6.2; Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** Strongly dissipative backward dynamics may be ill-posed. Only the studied horizons are supported, while stiff systems and very long horizons remain untested (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** The metric is computed in latent space; physical-field calibration requires additional per-field modeling (Sections 6.2 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** Computing a cycle costs one additional rollout, giving 2× inference overhead per checked depth (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Face-video experiments exclude strongly irreversible content and are presented as a probe rather than a competitive prediction benchmark (Section 6.5).
- **Analyst inference:** OOD evidence is narrow because AUROC is a percentile comparison involving one Orszag–Tang trajectory, not a diverse OOD test population (Section 6.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Generality across architectures is unresolved because experiments use variants of one latent diffusion-transformer pipeline; no non-diffusion dynamics model is tested.
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is incomplete from the supplied text: extensive configurations are given, but code, model weights, random-seed sensitivity for the main MHD calibration, and certified estimates of \(\mu\) and \(L\) are not established.

## Open research questions

1. **Can cancellation be controlled rather than merely calibrated?** The guarantee becomes weak as the backward map collapses or depth grows. A next experiment could train a certified bi-Lipschitz or Jacobian-regularized inverse and compare calibration and false-negative rates with the current model.

2. **How well does OOD detection generalize beyond one canonical case?** The reported MHD OOD result uses a single Orszag–Tang trajectory. A next experiment could test many parameterized OOD families and report AUROC, precision–recall, and detection delay across seeds and shift severities.

3. **Can round-trip signals represent spatially heterogeneous error?** On Navier–Stokes, a frame-level scalar trails learned per-pixel uncertainty. A next experiment could learn a spatial cycle-residual map while keeping calibration data and test data separated.

4. **Does the method extend to strongly irreversible or stochastic dynamics?** Face videos are restricted to mildly time-asymmetric content, and deterministic DDIM defines the main cycle. A next experiment could evaluate pouring, smoke, or stochastic physical systems using repeated cycles and explicitly measure cancellation and sampling variance.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s reported methods and numerical findings because the supplied text includes the main paper, tables, and substantial supplementary material.

Material missing or unresolved includes certified or empirical values of the theoretical \(\mu\) and \(L\) constants; diverse OOD populations; evaluations on strongly irreversible dynamics; non-diffusion model comparisons; code and checkpoint availability; and full random-seed sensitivity for the principal MHD calibration results.
