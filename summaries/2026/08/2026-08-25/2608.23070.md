# From Generation to Simulation: How Far Are World Models from Being True Simulators?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23070) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23070)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors’ audit of 200 works argues that generative world models can substitute for simulators in some scenario-specific interaction and control settings, but still lack formal physical guarantees, rich queryable state, and reproducible long-horizon evolution.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether generative world models can replace physics engines, game engines, and reinforcement-learning environments rather than merely produce plausible future observations. The authors argue that prior surveys classify world models by architecture, function, or application, leaving their functional gap from rigorous simulators insufficiently characterized.

This matters because visually convincing rollouts can still hallucinate objects, drift geometrically, violate contact physics, or respond inconsistently to repeated actions. The paper therefore evaluates models against an external simulator-oriented yardstick rather than treating generation quality as sufficient evidence of simulation capability (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Capability framework:** Defines eight simulator capabilities: asset construction, physics engine, interaction, controllability, stability, state feedback, diversity, and evaluation (Sections 1 and 3.2; Figure 1).
- **Corpus-level evidence map:** Codes 200 works from 2018 through June 2026 by technical route and principal capability contribution; 163 are implementation papers and 37 are benchmarks, surveys, or other contextual records (Sections 2.1–2.3).
- **Cross-route comparison:** Compares latent dynamics/action, autoregressive, diffusion, JEPA, explicit 3D/4D, and occupancy-centric methods (Table 2).
- **State-interface audit:** Applies five labels covering ego information, sensors, rewards/termination, runtime entity or physics annotations, and closed-loop interaction to the 163 implementation papers (Section 2.3).
- **Research roadmap:** Proposes formalized physics, unified action interfaces, first-class state feedback, long-horizon stability, downstream-utility evaluation, and cross-route hybridization (Abstract).

The evidence supports the authors’ conclusion that research activity and selected demonstrations are strongest in control and interaction, while queryable state, physical guarantees, and reproducibility remain poorly documented. It does **not** establish a universal ranking of model families: capability counts measure research attention, benchmarks and thresholds are heterogeneous, and many numerical results are reports from individual surveyed papers rather than results reproduced by this survey.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Source truncation:** The supplied paper text omits 72,583 characters from the middle, so later capability analyses, the complete roadmap, appendices, and parts of the state-feedback audit are unavailable. This digest cannot claim complete coverage.
- **Author-stated scope:** The survey primarily covers generative models that accept actions or conditions and roll out future observations or states; policy-only, representation-only, trajectory-only, and unrelated “world model” work is excluded (Sections 1 and 2.1).
- **Author-stated measurement limit:** Capability labels quantify research attention, not capability attainment, and there is no shared performance threshold across families (Section 2.3; Table 2).
- **Corpus validity:** Searches were relevance-ranked, truncated per query, and manually screened. This supports curation but does not demonstrate exhaustive retrieval (Section 2.1).
- **Evidence maturity:** 128 of 200 records were preprints or other non-formally-published records at the cutoff, concentrating substantial evidence in recent, less-validated work (Section 2.2; Figure 2).
- **Analyst inference:** Paper-level coding can inherit ambiguity from abstracts and inconsistent reporting, although the authors separate “unresolved” from “absent” in the state audit.
- **Analyst inference:** The excerpt does not provide inter-annotator agreement, duplicate-coding procedures, or sensitivity analyses, so annotation reliability cannot be assessed.
- **Analyst inference:** Several headline performance claims come from surveyed systems with different hardware, domains, and protocols; they should not be treated as controlled cross-model comparisons.

## Open research questions

1. **Can learned simulators provide verifiable physical guarantees without losing open-domain visual generation?**  
   The survey finds mostly plausibility constraints rather than formal guarantees. A next experiment could compare a modular differentiable-physics-plus-neural-renderer system against a pure generator on conservation, collision, and out-of-distribution intervention tests.

2. **What should a standardized runtime state-feedback API expose?**  
   Only 6/163 implementation papers reportedly provide queryable entity or physical state. A next experiment could implement B1–B5 across representative model families and measure both controller performance and interface overhead.

3. **Can long-horizon behavior be statistically reproducible rather than merely visually stable?**  
   The reviewed literature provides little evidence on seed control, deterministic execution, tolerance bands, or repeated-run variance. A next experiment could repeat identical action sequences across seeds and report state divergence over increasing horizons.

4. **Do simulator-oriented metrics predict downstream task success?**  
   The paper warns that visual quality and physical plausibility do not necessarily establish functional utility. A next experiment could correlate standardized physics, state, stability, and interaction scores with closed-loop planning or policy-training outcomes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The available pages clearly support the paper’s framing, corpus construction, taxonomy, and several central numerical findings, but the source is substantially truncated.
