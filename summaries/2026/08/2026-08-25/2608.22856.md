# Same Agent, Different Answers: A Repeat-Aware Audit of Corpus-Induced Answer Churn in Retrieval-Augmented QA

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22856) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22856)


## One-sentence takeaway

Holding the generator and retrieval interface fixed, expanding corpus access can change RAG answers substantially beyond ordinary generation variability even when aggregate exact-match accuracy barely changes or moves in either direction.

## Problem and motivation

The paper studies “accuracy-blind answer churn”: instance-level answer changes following a corpus update that aggregate accuracy can conceal because improvements and regressions cancel, or because different incorrect answers receive the same score. One-shot comparisons also confound update-induced changes with normal stochastic generation variation.

The identified gap is an evaluation method that separates compatibility—whether users observe the same answer-level behavior—from average utility while accounting for repeated-call variability (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Repeat-aware compatibility estimand.** The Snapshot Compatibility Audit collects two independent responses per question under each snapshot. It defines excess churn \(\hat D\) as average within-snapshot agreement minus cross-snapshot agreement, using normalized-exact and blinded semantic similarity (Equations 1–4, Section 3.1).

2. **Question-level inference and diagnostics.** The authors bootstrap entire questions and introduce a post-hoc “repeat-stable semantic flip” diagnostic: both responses agree within each snapshot, while every cross-snapshot pair disagrees semantically (Section 3.3–3.4).

3. **Controlled corpus-access audit.** A fixed single-turn retriever-generator is evaluated while a nested FineWeb prefix expands from one to seven shards, with prompts, retrieval depth, rendering, and exposed generation controls held fixed (Section 4.1).

4. **Preregistered and robustness evidence.** The study includes confirmatory Natural Questions results, separately preregistered supportive TriviaQA results, a post-hoc second DeepSeek configuration, retrieval-turnover analysis, and a cross-family semantic-judge audit (Sections 4.2–4.3 and 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| NQ shows excess churn despite a small accuracy change. | On 400 NQ questions, \(n_1\!\rightarrow n_7\) normalized-exact excess churn is 6.438 pp (95% CI [4.188, 8.750]; one-sided LCB 4.563), and semantic excess churn is 10.250 pp ([7.188, 13.438]; LCB 7.688), while EM changes by −1.50 pp. | Table 2; Sections 5.1 and 5.3 |
| TriviaQA gives a smaller supportive effect while EM moves oppositely. | On 200 questions, exact excess churn is 3.000 pp ([0.000, 6.250]; LCB 0.500), semantic excess churn is 2.125 pp ([0.125, 4.500]; LCB 0.375), and EM changes by +1.25 pp. | Table 2; Sections 5.1 and 5.3 |
| A second configuration directionally reproduces churn. | On the post-hoc NQ-100 subset using V4-Pro, exact and semantic excess churn are 7.25 and 8.75 pp; EM rises from 38.00% to 41.00%, a +3.00-pp change with interval [−2.00, 8.50]. | Section 5.2; Figure 2 |
| Aggregate EM hides substantial transition volume. | For NQ’s 800 matched-repeat comparisons, 46 are match→nonmatch and 34 nonmatch→match, yielding −1.50 pp net EM despite 80/800 (10.00%) gross correctness flow; another 155/800 (19.38%) change between semantically different EM nonmatches. | Section 5.3; Figure 3 |
| Strict flips account for nearly all NQ semantic excess churn. | Forty of 400 NQ questions are repeat-stable semantic flips, contributing 10.00 pp of the 10.25-pp effect; 35/40 remain EM nonmatches at both endpoints. TriviaQA has 5/200 flips. | Section 5.4; Equation 7 |
| Retrieved evidence changes heavily, but most answers remain stable. | Mean top-8 document overlap is 1.19/8 for NQ and 1.09/8 for TriviaQA; zero-overlap rates are 27.75% and 29.50%, while cross-scale semantic agreement remains 78.88% and 94.63%. | Section 5.4; Figure 4 |
| Cross-family judging gives similar labels and churn on a small audit. | Across 50 questions and 1,400 pair labels, judges agree on 95.71%, with \(\kappa=0.855\); semantic excess churn is 9.50 pp under the original judge and 11.00 pp under the audit judge. | Section 5.6 |

The evidence establishes a path-specific behavioral difference beyond the measured two-call stochastic baseline, and shows that aggregate EM can miss offsetting or within-nonmatch answer changes. It does not establish that every changed answer is harmful or incorrect, that corpus growth universally causes this churn rate, or which retrieved document caused a particular change.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The corpus treatment follows one fixed nested FineWeb shard path. Scale is therefore confounded with the identities and ranking effects of the added documents; no content-averaged or universal scaling effect is identified (Section 6.2).

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers two English QA benchmarks, one search service, top-eight retrieval, and two configurations from one generator family. The V4-Pro replication changes both generator and serving interface (Section 6.2).

- **Author-stated:** Two responses per state are the minimum needed for a within-state baseline but only coarsely characterize each answer distribution (Section 6.2).

- **Author-stated:** Semantic labels may merge distinct answers or split valid aliases, sometimes lack transitivity, and received only a 50-question cross-family audit—not human validation (Sections 5.6 and 6.2).

- **Author-stated:** Provider-default temperature and top-p values were not recorded, and the cross-family judge’s raw execution trace was not retained (Sections 4.2 and 5.6).

- **Author-stated:** The study does not identify document-level causes, demonstrate retrieval utility, or evaluate multi-step agents, temporal refreshes, retriever replacements, or other proposed applications (Sections 3.1, 6.1, and 6.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Because only two repeats inform each state, concentrated flip counts may be sensitive to sparsely observed answer distributions even though the aggregate estimand is unbiased under the paper’s sampling assumptions.

## Open research questions

1. **How stable is excess churn with more repeats?** Two responses provide only a coarse distributional estimate. A follow-up could collect 5–10 calls per question and compare uncertainty, flip membership, and estimated churn against the two-repeat design.

2. **Does churn generalize across corpus paths and update types?** The paper evaluates one nested shard path. An experiment could randomize multiple equal-size shard additions and separately test refresh, deduplication, chunking, and retriever changes.

3. **Which evidence changes cause answer-regime changes?** Retrieval overlap is only a mechanism hint, and overlap-bin tests are inconclusive. Controlled document addition/removal experiments could estimate which retrieved items trigger particular flips.

4. **Does excess churn predict user-visible harm?** The audit measures compatibility, not factual damage or operational cost. Human annotation and downstream workflow studies could label flip correctness, ambiguity, severity, and application-specific consequences.
