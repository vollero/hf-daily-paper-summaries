# The Mask Is Not the Model: Auditing Prefix Invariance in Attention, State-Space, and Hybrid Sequence Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22876) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22876)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors introduce a two-forward-pass, layer-wise prefix-invariance audit that exactly localizes injected causal leaks and identifies a shared inter-chunk recurrence defect in the reference PyTorch implementations of Zamba2 and Nemotron-H.

## Problem and motivation

Autoregressive models require every prefix representation to be independent of future inputs, but releases rarely verify this property. Inspecting attention masks is insufficient for modern stacks because leakage can arise elsewhere in the computation graph—such as scans, aggregations, convolutions, or normalization—and some state-space and recurrent models have no attention mask at all (§§1.1–1.3).

Such leakage is dangerous because it can improve teacher-forced loss or perplexity while breaking the assumptions behind generation, caching, and model comparisons (§1.2). The authors identify a gap in lightweight, architecture-independent tests that both detect a violation and locate its origin.

## Approach and contributions

- **Prefix-invariance audit:** Run two deterministic, cache-disabled forward passes on sequences differing only at the final token; capture every layer’s output and compare all earlier positions. The first layer whose maximum absolute difference exceeds threshold \(\tau\) is reported (§§2.1–2.3).

- **Graph-level causality criterion:** Formalize causality as invariance of every layer’s prefix representations, explaining why inspection of any single operator or mask is not a sound audit (§§1.4, 2.1).

- **Positive controls and leakage taxonomy:** Define eight executable fault patterns across shift, aggregation, normalization, and reverse-direction leakage classes, injected at multiple depths to test detector liveness and localization (Table 12; §3.2).

- **Audit methodology:** Require a passing positive control on the same loaded checkpoint and test sequences longer than architecture-specific chunk, window, and kernel sizes (§§3.7.2, 3.8.7).

- **Released-code diagnosis:** Combine static code inspection with dynamic auditing to trace one shared wrong-axis inter-chunk recurrence across Zamba2 and Nemotron-H, then verify a reference-orientation patch (§§3.8.2–3.8.5).

The evidence strongly supports the audit’s ability to localize the tested injected faults and supports a specific causal defect in the reference PyTorch chunked-scan path shared by two released models. Patch-to-zero controls strengthen the root-cause attribution.

It does not establish the prevalence of causal leakage across model releases, superiority over gradient localization accuracy, correctness of fused-kernel paths, or universal correctness of models receiving clean verdicts. Several long-sequence clean results also lacked checkpoint-specific positive controls.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The two released-model failures trace to one byte-identical code lineage, so they are an existence proof rather than two independent observations or an ecosystem-wide rate (§4.1).

- **Author-stated:** Logits-only and resampling checks matched the audit’s injected-fault detection rate; the new empirical advantage is localization. Gradient checking matched both detection and localization but costs a backward pass and requires differentiability (§4.2–4.3).

- **Author-stated:** The census covers roughly twenty non-randomly selected checkpoints, chosen for architectural diversity, so it cannot support prevalence estimates (§4.5).

- **Author-stated:** Several models could not be audited because of compiled-kernel dependencies, packaging, access, or degenerate loading. Coverage is therefore systematically weaker for custom-kernel hybrids (Table 5; §§3.7.1–3.7.3, 4.6).

- **Author-stated:** Some clean long-sequence checkpoint results are provisional because architecture-specific return signatures prevented positive-control injection (§§3.8.2, 4.7).

- **Author-stated:** Experiments do not cover rare-input-triggered leakage, training mode, varied batch shapes, distributed or sharded execution, or the fused CUDA implementations of the faulty models (§§3.8.2, 4.9–4.10).

- **Author-stated:** Localization sensitivity is model-dependent; LFM2-1.2B’s downstream localization at weaker injection strengths remains unexplained (§§3.5, 4.8).

- **Analyst inference:** The claim that clean deltas require no calibration is well supported for the tested deterministic float32/float64 paths, but should not automatically be transferred to other numeric formats, stochastic kernels, or nondeterministic hardware execution.

- **Analyst inference:** Static conformity to the chosen Mamba2 reference is predictive in this census but is not, by itself, a proof of full-model causality; other leakage mechanisms could exist outside the inspected recurrence block.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the fused CUDA paths for Zamba2 and Nemotron-H share the defect?**  
   They were unavailable in the authors’ environment (§3.8.2). A next experiment should audit matched checkpoints with and without fused kernels at lengths spanning multiple chunk boundaries and compare per-layer deltas.

2. **Can positive controls be made architecture-independent?**  
   Several clean checkpoints could not be gated because their layer return signatures rejected the generic injection (§4.7). A next experiment could build output adapters for each return type and rerun the full fault grid on every provisional checkpoint.

3. **How well does the audit find data-dependent or execution-dependent leaks?**  
   The paper tests graph-present faults under deterministic evaluation and limited shapes (§4.9–4.10). A next experiment could combine structured input fuzzing with varied batches, training mode, and distributed execution while retaining layer-wise prefix comparisons.

4. **What determines a model’s exact-localization floor?**  
   Floors vary substantially, and LFM2-1.2B remains unexplained (§3.5). A controlled study could vary normalization, residual scaling, depth, precision, and injection site to measure how weak leaks propagate before crossing the threshold.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, injected-fault results, and scoped diagnosis of the two reference PyTorch implementations, because the supplied text includes detailed controls, tables, settings, root-cause code, and patch verification.

Material information not established in the supplied text includes:

- independent third-party reproduction of the released-model defects;
- results for the fused-kernel implementations;
- complete positive controls for every long-sequence clean checkpoint;
- ecosystem-wide leakage prevalence;
- behavior under quantization, mixed precision, training mode, distributed execution, or varied batch shapes;
- a mechanism for the LFM2-1.2B localization anomaly;
- empirical validation outside language-model checkpoints;
- confirmation that all referenced raw logs and artifacts are publicly accessible from the host paths listed in Appendix B.
