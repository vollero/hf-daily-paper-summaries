# ClawProBench: Trace-Aware Evaluation of AI Agents with Runtime Coverage and Frozen Workplace-Style Holdouts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22510) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22510)


## One-sentence takeaway

ClawProBench evaluates complete model–runtime configurations from execution traces and shows that answer-only or one-off-success rankings obscure substantial native-tool, repeated-reliability, execution-status, and runtime sensitivity.

## Problem and motivation

Agent benchmarks often reduce behavior to final task success even though deployed agents operate through runtimes that determine tool routing, evidence collection, safety boundaries, retries, and auditability. The authors argue that the under-measured object is therefore the complete model–runtime–trace system, not live execution or trajectory scoring individually (§1).

This matters because an agent may produce a plausible answer while using the wrong runtime surface, omitting required evidence, acting without approval, wasting calls, or leaving an inadequate audit trail. Existing benchmarks cover subsets of these concerns, but the authors identify a gap in jointly measuring correctness, process, safety, status, native-runtime coverage, and repeated reliability under one protocol (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Two complementary evaluation tracks.** A 102-scenario full profile combines 66 workspace-live tasks with 36 OpenClaw-native tasks across eight runtime surfaces; a frozen 68-scenario closed-world workspace holdout supports repeated-trial and cross-runtime comparisons (§§3.1–3.3; Table 1).

2. **Trace-aware, safety-gated scoring.** Each scenario runs three trials. The trial score combines correctness and bounded process quality, applies a severity-aware safety gate, and penalizes excess tool use. Reports retain execution status and failed-check evidence (§3.5, Equation 1; Table 40).

3. **Explicit runtime-adaptation contract.** Cross-runtime evaluation preserves scenario identity, isolated state, output contracts, normalized trace events, safety labels, deterministic checkers, repeated-trial status, and release provenance. Native OpenClaw tasks are explicitly not claimed portable (§3.4; Table 3).

4. **Frozen and auditable benchmark assets.** Scenario and checker hashes, stable selectors, status labels, repair records, and exposure metadata support reproducibility and contamination auditing (§3.6; Tables 15–23).

5. **Configuration-level empirical diagnosis.** Version and cross-harness studies show that measured performance can change for the same model identity when its declared runtime bundle changes (§4.3; Tables 7–8).

The evidence supports the authors’ central claim that runtime identity, trace quality, execution status, and repeated reliability add information beyond final answers. It does not identify which individual runtime component causes performance changes, prove that native surfaces alone cause the native-task gap, or establish a stable full-profile-versus-holdout ordering.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Results measure complete model–runtime configurations, not harness-independent model intelligence (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The cross-harness study varies wrappers, schemas, routing, safety layers, controllers, and implementation jointly, preventing single-component causal attribution (§4.3; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Native scenarios are OpenClaw-specific; cross-runtime evidence covers only four model identities, three harnesses, and the workspace holdout (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The holdout is a calibrated diagnostic stress set rather than a probability sample, and its public release creates overfitting and contamination risk (§3.3; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Native and workspace tasks may differ in checker density, output format, wording, or construction, so their score gap is descriptive rather than causal (§4.4; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The cross-track rank interval spans zero, making precise ordering claims unsupported (§4.5).
- **Author-stated:** Failure categories are automatically derived from checker details, not independently human-validated prevalence estimates (§4.6).
- **Analyst inference:** Three trials expose instability but may still give imprecise reliability estimates for close configurations; the paper itself notes that more repetitions would improve confidence at additional cost (Table 22).
- **Analyst inference:** Calibration on a six-model panel may shape which tasks appear discriminative for later systems; no alternative calibration-panel ablation is reported in the supplied text (§3.3; Table 12).

## Open research questions

1. **Which runtime components cause the observed configuration sensitivity?** The current harnesses vary several mechanisms jointly. A next experiment could swap wrappers, routing policies, tool schemas, and safety layers one at a time while holding all other components fixed.

2. **Does the native-task gap persist under matched task contracts?** Native and workspace slices may differ beyond surface usage. A paired benchmark could implement semantically equivalent tasks in workspace-only and native-surface forms with matched graders and output schemas.

3. **How stable are reliability estimates beyond three trials?** The paper establishes a large occasional-versus-strict gap but uses three runs per scenario. Running selected configurations for 10–20 trials could estimate per-task success distributions and confidence intervals more precisely.

4. **How well does the frozen holdout generalize after public exposure?** The authors acknowledge contamination risk. A plausible experiment is to compare public-holdout performance against a delayed-release, hash-committed task rotation with explicit submission-exposure labels.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text is extensive and includes the benchmark definition, scoring formula, experimental settings, principal tables, uncertainty analyses, and stated limitations.
