# ReWorld: An Interactive World Model with Long-Horizon Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23565) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23565)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReWorld combines window-split training, pose-retrieved bounded memory, and four-step adapter distillation to generate action-controlled 720p video while retaining revisited scene content over minute-long rollouts with constant on-device KV-cache size.

## Problem and motivation

Interactive world models must simultaneously follow current camera actions, reproduce previously visited places, and stream indefinitely at interactive speed. The authors identify a structural conflict: direct action conditioning improves control but can weaken long-range recall, while memory training requires long attention histories that cannot be retained under a fixed deployment budget. Standard contiguous-history training also mismatches the sparse, non-contiguous cache available after compression (Introduction; §§2.2–2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Window-split training:** Of 24 attention heads, 18 use a short 12-frame window and six see the full causal history. A pool of 12 random head partitions rotates the global role across heads, preventing control or memory from becoming tied to deployment-incompatible specialist heads (§2.2; Fig. 3).
- **Complementary conditioning:** Direct Plücker-ray action injection specifies the current camera command, while MRoPE indexes cached content by relative camera pose for spatial retrieval (§2.2).
- **Bounded long-term memory:** A 12-chunk working cache contains one sink, five recent chunks, and six pose-nearest landmarks retrieved from a host-memory bank capped at 30 chunks. Distance-based admission and redundancy-based eviction preserve diverse full-resolution landmarks (§2.3; Fig. 4).
- **Sparse-history training:** Chunk drop retains the sink and randomly selected historical chunks, exposing the model to incomplete histories resembling inference caches (§2.3).
- **Data and deployment pipeline:** The authors assemble 220,724 pose-annotated clips from eight synthetic, real, and game sources, align translation scales, and add palindrome trajectories for revisit supervision. A rank-128 LoRA distills generation to four denoising steps while leaving the backbone and control path unchanged (§§2.4, 3; Fig. 6).

These results support the authors’ claim that routing mitigates the observed control–memory interference and that pose-retrieved landmarks retain useful revisit information under a fixed device cache. They do not establish reliable memory for arbitrary dynamic state, unrestricted interaction, or rollouts beyond the tested trajectories. The baseline recall comparison is also not uniformly controlled for executed path length.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** Memory is keyed only by camera pose; dynamic scenes and richer non-navigational interactions remain unsupported (§6).
- **Author-acknowledged confound:** Revisit similarity can reward low-motion generation. HY-WorldPlay’s stronger 96-latent scores accompany a much shorter path, so those scores do not isolate memory quality (§4.3; Table 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Palindromic out-and-back trajectories closely match the palindrome augmentation used in training, so generalization to loop closures, changed viewpoints, or non-retraced returns is not established (§§3.4, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The central long-rollout cache ablation cannot be quantitatively assessed because the retrieved text gives its design and conclusions but omits Table 6’s numerical cells.
- **Analyst inference:** “Real time” is insufficiently substantiated in the supplied text because no latency, throughput, hardware, or frames-per-second result is provided.
- **Analyst inference:** Main benchmark methods receive intent through different native interfaces, and trajectory registration absorbs scale and coordinate conventions; this improves comparability of trajectory shape but does not test absolute motion magnitude (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No statistical uncertainty or repeated-seed analysis is reported for the presented metrics.

## Open research questions

1. **Can pose-indexed consolidation retain dynamic object state?** The current memory key is camera pose alone (§6). A next experiment could revisit identical poses after controlled object movements and compare pose-only retrieval with pose-plus-content or object-state keys.
2. **Does memory generalize beyond exact palindrome returns?** Training augmentation and the principal recall benchmark both use retraced paths. Testing loop closures with different return routes, viewing angles, and lighting would measure broader spatial recall.
3. **How should memory quality be separated from motion magnitude?** Table 4 shows that shorter executed paths can inflate revisit similarity. A follow-up could condition scoring on matched optical-flow path length or replay measured trajectories with calibrated absolute displacement.
4. **What is the quality–latency–memory trade-off under different budgets?** The paper fixes a 12-chunk cache and four denoising steps for deployment. Sweeping cache size, landmark-bank capacity, retrieval count, and denoising steps while reporting memory, quality, latency, and host-transfer cost would expose the operating frontier.
