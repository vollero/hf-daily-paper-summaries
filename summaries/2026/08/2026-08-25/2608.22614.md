# What AstroPT knows about galaxies, and what that can teach us about LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22614) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22614)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across model sizes and two reconstruction objectives, AstroPT’s linear decodability follows an astronomy-defined difficulty ordering and its probe directions reproduce known galaxy-property relationships, supporting astronomy as a controlled calibration setting for interpretability rather than directly validating conclusions about language models.

## Problem and motivation

Interpretability studies often ask when concepts emerge and whether linear probes recover meaningful structure, but language lacks an agreed ground-truth ordering of concept difficulty or relationships. The authors use galaxy data—where some properties are close to pixel statistics and others require increasingly indirect inference—to test whether probe behavior corresponds to known structure rather than arbitrary correlations (Sections 1–2, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Developmental probing:** AstroPT transformers process galaxy images as patch sequences and learn by either GPT-like autoregressive reconstruction or BERT-like masked autoencoding. Linear ridge probes are fitted to frozen representations at every saved checkpoint and layer, with held-out \(R^2\) measuring accessibility (Section 2, pp. 2–3).
- **Controlled scaling comparison:** The study compares 1M, 21M, and 100M-parameter models trained for one epoch on 8.7M galaxy images, testing whether objective or capacity changes the order of property emergence (Section 2, p. 3).
- **Representation-geometry analysis:** Cosine similarities between normalized probe weights test whether hidden-space directions preserve known signs among luminosity, stellar mass, specific star-formation rate, and redshift (Section 2; Appendix A).
- **Residual and intervention analysis:** A mass-at-fixed-luminosity residual probes information beyond the dominant mass–luminosity relation; an exploratory activation-patching experiment tests whether transferring matched activations selectively shifts this residual readout (Appendix B, pp. 12–13).

The evidence supports robust qualitative ordering and relationship signs within the tested galaxy models, objectives, scales, and one-epoch regime. It does not show that AstroPT uses the linearly decoded properties during pre-training, that the proposed difficulty ladder is uniquely determined, or that analogous probe results in language models are valid. The intervention result is narrower than the main sweep because the text reports it only for the AR setting.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors state that the principal analysis is correlational, labels are inferred from catalogs, training covers only one epoch and three relatively small capacities, and linear probes expose only linearly accessible information. They also emphasize that galaxy patches are not discrete language tokens and make no claim that astronomical data is useful for LLM pre-training (Limitations, p. 4).

Analyst inferences:

- The claim that emergence order is objective-invariant is limited to AR and MAE patch-reconstruction objectives.
- The capacity claim is stronger for ordering than for score magnitude: Table 1 shows non-monotonic MAE performance, particularly between 1M and 21M.
- Catalog correlations and selection effects help define the expected geometry, but may also let probes reproduce dataset structure without establishing a learned physical mechanism.
- The residual activation-patching analysis lacks exact numerical results and is reported only for AR in the supplied text, limiting assessment and generalization.
- The supplied text does not establish the probe sample sizes, ridge hyperparameters, train/test construction, image tokenization details, or checkpoint schedule, constraining reproducibility.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the ordering persist with longer and larger training?** One epoch and a 100M-parameter maximum leave sSFR comparatively weak. Train larger models for multiple epochs and test whether sSFR eventually becomes robustly decodable without changing the sequence.

2. **Does decodability correspond to functional model use?** The authors explicitly note that linear accessibility does not prove utilization. Apply targeted ablation, mediation, or activation-patching tests to each property across layers and checkpoints.

3. **How much geometry reflects catalog selection rather than physical representation?** Redshift–mass alignment is partly attributed to flux-limited selection. Reweight or construct samples that control mass, luminosity, and selection effects, then repeat the probe-direction analysis.

4. **Does the calibration transfer to language-model interpretability?** The paper motivates this connection but does not test language models. Create synthetic or scientific text tasks with known concept ordering and compare probe-emergence and intervention results against the astronomical testbed.
