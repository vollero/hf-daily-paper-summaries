# Industrial-Instruction: An End-to-End Framework for Building Instruction-Tuning and Benchmark Datasets from Industrial Technical Reports

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22817) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22817)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors present a reproducible pipeline and two synthetic industrial QA datasets showing that full fine-tuning improves a 4B model on matched Panasonic benchmarks, although performance transfers inconsistently and remains completely brittle to rephrased FailureSensorIQ questions.

## Problem and motivation

Industrial reports contain valuable maintenance, troubleshooting, and engineering knowledge distributed across prose and tables, but their heterogeneous PDF layouts impede extraction, retrieval, and reasoning. The authors identify a shortage of public instruction-tuning and benchmark data derived from real industrial documentation, particularly for deployable language models below 10B parameters (Abstract; Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Two dataset versions:** QA data and held-out benchmarks derived from 906 public Panasonic documents spanning 7,525 pages, generated separately with Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct and Claude-Opus-4.6 (Sections 1 and 3.5).
- **End-to-end construction pipeline:** Dots.OCR performs layout-aware PDF extraction; text and Markdown tables are retained; EmbeddingGemma and FAISS provide semantic retrieval; generator models synthesize multiple-choice QA; rule-based filters enforce option and answer formats (Sections 3.2–3.4).
- **Five retrieval relationships:** Samples represent irrelevant retrieval, single- and multi-document support, and single- and multi-document explicit answers, targeting retrieval-noise robustness and evidence integration (Section 3.4; Figure 3).
- **Generator comparison:** The study compares an open-weight and a closed API generator on filtering rate, cost, downstream fine-tuning gains, and MMLU retention (Sections 3.5, 4.4, and 5.1).
- **Small-model evaluation:** Qwen3-4B-Instruct is evaluated before and after LoRA or full fine-tuning, with and without basic RAG, on Panasonic, FailureSensorIQ, and MMLU (Sections 4–5).

These results establish strong gains on benchmarks generated from the same Panasonic corpus and pipeline, plus materially different cost and formatting reliability between generators. They do not establish that Claude data is strictly superior in a controlled head-to-head evaluation: its and Qwen’s held-out splits differ, as the authors acknowledge in Section 4.4. Nor do they demonstrate broad industrial generalization; FailureSensorIQ results are inconsistent and perturbation accuracy is zero.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The study covers one company’s documentation, uses a basic RAG implementation, evaluates a limited model set, and excludes images and diagrams. The authors propose broader sources, architectures, advanced RAG, and multimodal processing (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** Both fine-tuned Qwen variants score 0% on perturbed FailureSensorIQ, and the training pipeline contains no paraphrased or adversarial variants (Sections 5.2.3 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** The generator comparison uses different held-out splits, so improvement magnitudes are indicative rather than a controlled head-to-head result (Section 4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Filtering mainly verifies schema and option structure; the supplied text reports no expert audit of factual correctness, grounding, ambiguity, or answer validity. A low rejection rate therefore establishes formatting compliance more directly than semantic quality.
- **Analyst inference:** The OCR pipeline’s 291 problematic pages and removal of all images may omit knowledge needed to answer visually grounded industrial questions (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that each released dataset contains approximately 13.6k QA pairs conflicts with Section 3.5, which reports 26,395 Claude samples and only 143 removals. The retrieved text does not reconcile these counts.
- **Analyst inference:** LoRA conclusions are weakly supported because the numerical contents of Table 7 are absent from the supplied rendering and no comparison against a fully specified hyperparameter search is available.

## Open research questions

1. **Does generator quality improve factual grounding, not merely formatting?** The filtering results chiefly measure schema compliance. A blinded expert audit comparing correctness, evidence support, ambiguity, and distractor quality across generators would test this directly.
2. **Can perturbation-aware generation eliminate the 0% robustness result?** The current data contains no explicit paraphrase or adversarial augmentation. A next experiment could add controlled rephrasings to every scenario and evaluate AccPerIBM before and after training.
3. **Do gains transfer beyond Panasonic documentation?** Evaluation is dominated by a single-company corpus, while FailureSensorIQ transfer is mixed. A leave-one-manufacturer-out study across several industrial document collections would measure cross-source generalization.
4. **Which pipeline stages drive downstream gains?** The paper does not report ablations for OCR choice, table retention, retrieval scenarios, instruction simulation, or filtering. A factorial ablation using a common test set could isolate each component’s contribution.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methodology and many narrative result values, but several rendered tables omit their cells. Materially missing or ambiguous information includes complete dataset counts and split sizes, full Tables 3–17, exact fine-tuning hyperparameters beyond Appendix B, retrieval settings and retrieval-quality metrics, per-scenario QA results, human validation of generated samples, statistical uncertainty or significance tests, and a reconciliation of the approximately 13.6k-per-dataset claim with the reported Claude sample count.
