# LongRCA Bench: Diagnosing Responsible Roles and Root Causes in Long-Horizon Agent Failures

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15242) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15242)


## One-sentence takeaway

LongRCA Bench evaluates who was responsible and where the earliest decisive error occurred in 1,140 naturally failed, long-horizon agent trajectories, while RCTA improves exact root-step accuracy from the strongest baseline’s 13.2% to 24.1% under matched evaluation conditions.

## Problem and motivation

Outcome-level evaluation shows that an agent failed but generally does not identify the responsible workflow role or the earliest recorded step that introduced the unrepaired, decisive error. This matters in long executions because hundreds of later steps may implement, propagate, or reveal an earlier mistake.

The authors identify a benchmark gap: prior failure-attribution resources generally use shorter histories or different targets, such as failure modes, spans, stages, or injected errors. LongRCA Bench instead scores responsible-role attribution and exact root-step localization independently on completed, naturally failed trajectories (Sections 1–3; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **LongRCA Bench:** 1,140 evaluator-confirmed, non-injected failures from five sources, normalized into step-indexed histories and labeled with a responsible role, earliest decisive root step, and unscored rationale (Sections 3.1–3.3; Tables 2–3).
- **Long-horizon diagnostic regime:** 178,137 recorded steps, with median trajectory length 145 and median root-to-end distance 48; some roots precede the end by hundreds of steps (Section 4; Figure 4).
- **Independent prediction targets:** Role and root step are predicted and scored separately; the role is not inferred from the selected step’s emitter (Sections 3.1 and 6.1).
- **RCTA:** A training-free pipeline that segments histories, summarizes each segment to recall candidate errors, constructs a trajectory outline, retrieves earlier handoff instructions, and compares original logged evidence before final attribution (Section 5; Figure 5).
- **Matched evaluation:** RCTA and five baselines use DeepSeek-V4-Flash, the same 1,140 trajectories, and identical scoring rules (Section 6.1; Table 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The benchmark contains 1,140 natural failed trajectories and 178,137 steps. | Five source benchmarks; no synthetic error injection; 156.3 mean, 145 median, and 728 maximum steps per trajectory. | Sections 3.2, 4.2; Tables 1–2; Figure 4 |
| Roots can substantially precede visible failure. | Median root-to-end distance is 48 steps, P90 is 183, and maximum is 605; 28.4% of trajectories have more than 100 subsequent steps. | Section 4.2; Figure 4 |
| RCTA leads every reported aggregate metric. | On all 1,140 trajectories with DeepSeek-V4-Flash: 51.1% role accuracy, 24.1% root exact, 37.4% root ±5, and 38.6 source-weighted valid-output MAE. | Table 4; Sections 6.1–6.2 |
| RCTA substantially outperforms the strongest baseline, ECHO. | ECHO obtains 27.5% role, 13.2% exact, 24.7% ±5, and 50.4 MAE; RCTA gains 23.6, 10.9, and 12.7 percentage points and lowers MAE by 11.8 steps. | Table 4; Section 6.2 |
| Exact event localization remains much harder than role attribution. | RCTA’s 24.1% exact root accuracy is less than half its 51.1% role accuracy under independent scoring. | Table 4; Sections 6.2 and 7 |
| Length-stratified performance is non-monotonic. | RCTA exact accuracy is 30.3%, 20.3%, 20.2%, and 31.0% for ≤100, 101–200, 201–400, and >400 steps; the last bin contains only 42 cases. | Figure 6; Table 7 |
| Root-to-end-distance results are also non-monotonic. | RCTA scores 21.5%, 27.1%, 20.9%, and 25.6% across ≤10, 11–50, 51–100, and >100-step bins. | Figure 6; Table 8 |
| Human exact localization is intrinsically ambiguous. | On redundantly annotated cases, pairwise exact agreement is 65.9% for role, 39.5% for step, and 38.4% jointly. | Section 3.3; Table 3 |

The evidence establishes that RCTA outperforms the five implemented baselines under one shared backbone and protocol, and that exact localization remains difficult. It does not establish model-independent superiority, causal effects of trajectory length or root distance, or accurate reconstruction of complete failure chains.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Labels require substantial human judgment; each trajectory ultimately has only one finalized role and root-step reference (Section 7).
- Intermediate causal chains are unscored, so the benchmark does not measure full causal-chain reconstruction (Section 7).
- Evaluation is offline and cannot support claims about early warning or intervention (Sections 3.1 and 7).
- All methods use one backbone; absolute rankings or performance may change with other models (Section 7).
- Difficulty bins confound source, workflow, role structure, length, and root position, precluding causal interpretation (Sections 6.3 and 7).
- Component ablations for candidate recall, handoff tracing, instruction checks, and validation are not reported (Section 7).

**Analyst inferences**

- Annotation reliability is a material validity risk because exact root-step agreement is only 39.5% on the redundantly annotated subset, while evaluation uses a single resolved reference (Section 3.3; Table 3).
- Source balance is narrow: TravelPlanner supplies 60.1% of instances, so aggregate results may be disproportionately shaped by that domain (Section 4.1; Figure 3).
- MAE is calculated only over valid numeric outputs within each source; invalid predictions count against accuracy but contribute no direct MAE term, complicating interpretation across methods with different invalid-output rates (Section 6.1).
- RCTA’s computational cost is potentially substantial: a trajectory with \(m\) segments requires \(m+2\) initial LLM calls, but latency, token use, and monetary cost are not reported (Appendix E).
- The phase-building fallback is imperfectly auditable: 308 outputs fall outside the requested 3–8 phases, yet fallback flags were not retained (Appendix E).
- The supplied text reports no statistical significance tests for aggregate method comparisons; Wilson intervals are shown only for stratified plots (Figure 6).

## Open research questions

1. **Which RCTA component produces the improvement?**  
   This remains open because no component ablations are reported. A next experiment could remove segmentation summaries, handoff retrieval, backward instruction comparison, and validation individually while preserving the backbone and prompts otherwise.

2. **How robust are results to inference backbones?**  
   Every evaluated method uses DeepSeek-V4-Flash, so model-independent performance is unknown. A factorial evaluation across several backbones, with identical trajectory inputs and scoring, could test ranking stability.

3. **Can localization account for legitimate label ambiguity?**  
   Low exact step agreement suggests that some failures admit multiple defensible boundaries. An experiment could retain all independently supported annotations and compare exact scoring with set-valued or adjudicated tolerance-based metrics.

4. **Can the method diagnose failures online?**  
   The benchmark provides complete failed histories only. A prefix-based evaluation could reveal trajectories incrementally and measure detection accuracy, lead time, and whether an intervention prevents the evaluator-confirmed failure.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design, reported metrics, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and implementation appendices.

Material missing information includes per-source method results, invalid-output and abstention rates, aggregate significance tests, inference cost and latency, component ablations, cross-backbone experiments, detailed adjudication outcomes, and evaluation of rationale or full causal-chain quality.
