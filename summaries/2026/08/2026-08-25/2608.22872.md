# Better Retrieval, Worse Robustness:How Multi-hop RAG Amplifies Upstream ASR Errors

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22872) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22872)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across three multi-hop QA benchmarks, entity-graph linking and iterative reformulation usually improve absolute F1 but enlarge the clean-text-to-accented-speech performance gap, chiefly when ASR corrupts query entities.

## Problem and motivation

Voice-driven RAG systems receive ASR transcripts rather than original questions, making recognition errors a fixed upstream constraint. Multi-hop systems may be especially vulnerable because graph linking and iterative query reformulation introduce additional dependencies on entity surface forms.

The authors identify a gap in prior spoken-QA research: it largely studies single-hop reading or retrieval, leaving unclear whether structurally richer multi-hop RAG absorbs or amplifies ASR errors and which errors drive failures (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled evaluation suite:** The authors synthesize 1,000 questions from each of HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue using four English-accented TTS voices, then transcribe them with Whisper-large-v3. Together, these form 12,000 spoken queries, with clean text used as a per-method oracle (Sections 3.1 and 4.1, pp. 3–4).
- **Architecture comparison:** Four configurations isolate dense retrieval, entity-graph linking, iterative reformulation, and their combination: Naive RAG, HippoRAG2, IRCoT+Naive, and IRCoT+HippoRAG2 (Section 3.2, pp. 3–4).
- **Mechanism analysis:** Rule-based transcription-difference labels test whether named-entity corruption dominates degradation cases; an IRCoT step-count experiment examines cumulative reformulation effects (Sections 5.2–5.3, pp. 6–8).
- **Diagnostic mitigations:** Five-temperature N-best decoding and phonetic entity correction test how much of the gap simple surface-form recovery can close (Section 3.3 and Appendix A, pp. 4, 12–13).
- **Sensitivity checks:** The paper compares synthetic speech with 500 real Nigerian-accented utterances and reruns one condition with SeamlessM4T-v2-large (Section 5.5 and Appendices E–F, pp. 8–9, 14–15).
- **Released asset:** Code and data are reported as released under Apache-2.0, with underlying licenses documented separately (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| ASR input consistently lowers QA performance. | Across every dataset–method cell, accented input has lower F1 than clean text. Oracle–Nigerian gaps range from **0.043** (Naive RAG, MuSiQue) to **0.195** (IRCoT+HippoRAG2, 2WikiMultiHopQA); all such gaps have **p < 0.001**. | Section 5.1; Table 1, pp. 5–6 |
| Downstream degradation tracks upstream error rate. | Across cells, mean WER and oracle–accent F1 gap correlate at **Pearson r = 0.88**. For Nigerian 2WikiMultiHopQA questions with WER above 20%, degradation rates are **25.2%** for Naive RAG and **37.8%** for IRCoT+HippoRAG2; this bin contains **413 questions**. | Section 5.1; Figure 3, pp. 5–6 |
| Combining both structural extensions produces the largest robustness gap. | Relative to Naive RAG, IRCoT+HippoRAG2 enlarges the oracle–Nigerian F1 gap by **36.5%** on HotpotQA (0.104→0.142), **42.3%** on 2WikiMultiHopQA (0.137→0.195), and **67.4%** on MuSiQue (0.043→0.072); combined-method amplification has **p ≤ 0.007**. | Section 5.2; Table 1, p. 6 |
| Worse robustness does not imply worse absolute performance. | On Nigerian 2WikiMultiHopQA, IRCoT+HippoRAG2 has a larger gap than Naive RAG (**0.195 vs. 0.137**) but higher ASR-input F1 (**0.450 vs. 0.331**). | Section 5.2; Table 1, p. 6 |
| Iterative amplification is not monotonic. | On HotpotQA, one IRCoT step reduces the Naive gap from **0.104 to 0.096**, while three steps increase it to **0.115**; HippoRAG2 peaks at **0.142** after three steps and falls to **0.131** at five partly because oracle performance decreases. | Section 5.2; Figure 4, pp. 6–7 |
| Entity corruption dominates identified degradation cases. | Across all configurations, entity corruption accounts for **67–82%** of HotpotQA, **87–96%** of 2WikiMultiHopQA, and **54–78%** of MuSiQue degradation cases. Labels are automated, rule-based, and can overlap. | Section 5.3; Table 8, pp. 7–8, 15 |
| Lightweight mitigations recover little of the lost performance. | On Nigerian 2WikiMultiHopQA, N-best recovery ranges from **−2.2% to +2.5%** and phonetic correction from **+4.4% to +11.1%** of the oracle–ASR gap; no configuration exceeds 12% recovery. | Section 5.4; Table 3, p. 8 |
| The method ordering survives a second ASR system. | SeamlessM4T has **28.0% WER** versus Whisper’s **17.1%** on Nigerian 2WikiMultiHopQA, while preserving the gap ordering; the largest gap changes from **0.195 to 0.214**. | Section 5.5; Table 9, pp. 8–9, 15 |
| Real speech exhibits substantial entity corruption, but is not evaluated end to end. | Among 282 real Nigerian utterances containing entities, **51.8%** contain at least one mistranscribed entity; **40.7% of 607 entity instances** are altered. Mean WER is **28.9%**, but these are general-purpose utterances rather than QA queries. | Section 5.5; Appendix E, pp. 8–9, 14–15 |

The evidence supports an association between added retrieval structure and larger within-method clean-to-ASR gaps in this controlled pipeline. It also supports entity corruption as the most frequent rule-derived label among degradation cases. It does **not** establish demographic fairness effects, causal generalization to real spoken multi-hop QA, or that every additional structural component necessarily reduces robustness in other models, languages, or retrieval settings.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- The main experiment uses synthetic speech and only one female TTS voice per accent, so it does not represent within-accent or demographic variation (Limitations and Ethical Considerations, p. 10).
- Real speech is used only to compare ASR error patterns, not for end-to-end multi-hop QA; the alternative-ASR experiment covers only Nigerian 2WikiMultiHopQA (Sections 5.5 and Limitations, pp. 8–10).
- Evaluation is English-only; code-switched and tonal languages may produce different errors (Limitations, p. 10).
- Entity corruption is identified through a capitalization-based, rule-driven proxy without reported human validation (Section 5.3, Appendix B, and Limitations, pp. 7, 10, 13).
- Answer generation uses only gpt-4o-mini, which may limit generalization to larger or open-source generators (Limitations, p. 10).

**Analyst inferences**

- The experiments compare four configurations, but not alternative graph retrievers or iterative frameworks; the claim is therefore strongest for the implemented Naive RAG, HippoRAG2, and IRCoT designs.
- Mitigation hyperparameters were chosen by inspecting representative errors before evaluation, without a separately described development set. Although no tuning used the 1,000 evaluation samples, selection bias cannot be fully assessed from the supplied text (Appendix A.2, p. 13).
- N-best decoding is applied only to the initial transcription for IRCoT, so Table 3 does not test correction of errors arising or persisting at later reformulation steps (Appendix A.1, p. 12).
- The real and synthetic speech sets differ in content and recording conditions; similar entity-error rates alone cannot validate equivalent downstream behavior, a boundary the authors also acknowledge.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the amplification persist on real spoken multi-hop questions from diverse speakers?**  
   This remains open because the real-speech set contains general-purpose utterances and receives no end-to-end RAG evaluation. A next experiment could record the same multi-hop questions from multiple speakers per accent and rerun all four configurations.

2. **Can entity correction at every retrieval hop prevent cumulative reformulation errors?**  
   The reported phonetic correction operates before retrieval, while IRCoT-generated follow-up queries remain uncorrected. A next experiment could apply ASR-aware entity grounding and uncertainty tracking before every iterative retrieval step.

3. **Which graph or iterative design choices cause amplification?**  
   The step-count experiment shows non-monotonic behavior but does not isolate graph seeding, query rewriting, evidence accumulation, and stopping policy individually. A factorial ablation could corrupt controlled entity spans while toggling each mechanism.

4. **Does uncertainty-aware routing improve the accuracy–robustness trade-off?**  
   Complex methods retain higher absolute F1 but larger oracle gaps, and the paper does not test dynamic method selection. A next experiment could route low-confidence ASR queries to dense retrieval or multiple retrievers and measure F1, gap, latency, and calibration.
