# Towards a Densing Law for User Representation Learning at Billion-Scale Capacity

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23392) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23392)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors argue that industrial user-representation scaling is limited by redundant behavioral data and propose a power-law recipe for selecting tokenization capacity plus ALGN, an adaptive residual tokenizer that improves classification while using less representational capacity on Alipay data.

## Problem and motivation

Industrial user models are commonly improved by adding users, extending behavioral histories, or enlarging encoders. The authors find that these strategies eventually yield diminishing downstream returns because additional records are often repetitive or weakly informative; larger models may continue lowering pretraining loss without improving representation quality (Sections 4.1–4.3).

They identify two gaps: insufficient understanding of whether discrete tokenization can overcome this “raw behavioral scaling wall,” and no quantitative rule relating behavioral-data scale to the minimum sufficient tokenizer capacity (Introduction; Sections 1 and 6).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Scaling diagnosis:** The paper varies user population, history length, and encoder size on Alipay behavioral data, comparing raw and RQ-VAE-tokenized inputs under matched data and compute settings (Sections 4–5).

2. **Behavioral Densing Law:** Tokenizer configurations are selected by finding the least capacity whose utility lies within an unspecified tolerance \(\epsilon\) of the best observed utility. The resulting capacity trajectory is modeled as \(\ln C^*(s)=\beta+\alpha\ln(s/s_0)\), generalized to multiple scale dimensions (Equations 13 and 19–21).

3. **Cross-setting validation:** The claimed log-linear capacity relationship is evaluated across PayBill, SPM, and MiniProgram data; RQ-VAE, VQ-VAE, and SARQ tokenizers; and classification, text-retrieval, and user-to-user retrieval tasks (Section 6.4; Figure 9).

4. **Adaptive tokenization:** ALGN chooses whether to activate another residual-quantization level using residual magnitude, code uncertainty, and marginal cost, with a prior over output lengths discouraging universal maximum-depth activation (Section 7.1, Equations 24–29).

The evidence supports saturation and favorable tokenized-model trade-offs within the evaluated Alipay settings. It does not establish a universal law beyond these data, prove that the fitted relationship is causal, or demonstrate generalization to public datasets, other platforms, or other modalities. Also, Table 3’s raw values show ALGN beating SARQ by 1.07, 2.03, and 0.36 percentage points; the paper’s wording uses percent signs, leaving the intended unit ambiguous.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation focuses on a single behavioral modality, with video consumption explicitly left untested (Section 8).
- **Author-stated:** Robustness beyond data from one platform has not yet been established; public-benchmark evaluation is ongoing (Section 8).
- **Author-stated:** Fixed-length tokenization is suboptimal for heterogeneous behavior, motivating ALGN (Section 5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The precise dataset construction, labels, train/test splits, and scenario-level results are absent; only dataset counts, per-set user counts, and averages are supplied (Table 2; Section 3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is limited because the configuration search space, tolerance \(\epsilon\), capacity coefficient \(\eta\), training budget details, uncertainty estimates, and statistical variation are not reported in the retrieved text (Section 6.2; Equations 11–13).
- **Analyst inference:** Claims of matched compute are difficult to audit because exact budgets and tokenizer-training costs are not tabulated (Sections 5.2–5.3; Figure 8).
- **Analyst inference:** The proposed theoretical law depends on an assumed power-law scale term and a local diminishing-return utility approximation; the experiments provide empirical fits rather than proof of universality (Sections 6.3–6.4).
- **Analyst inference:** ALGN’s main comparison is restricted to PayBill classification because the authors infer similar task trends from earlier experiments; this does not directly establish ALGN’s advantage on both retrieval tasks or other sources (Section 7.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No confidence intervals, repeated-run variance, or significance tests are reported, making small differences—such as 0.05 percentage points in accuracy—hard to interpret (Tables 3–4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the Densing Law transfer across platforms and modalities?** It remains open because evaluation uses one platform and the authors acknowledge single-modality coverage. A next experiment could fit the law independently on public recommendation datasets and video-consumption logs, then test coefficient transfer without recalibration.

2. **How stable are fitted coefficients and saturation thresholds?** The paper reports point estimates without uncertainty. Repeated training across seeds and bootstrapped Pareto-frontier fitting could provide confidence intervals for thresholds, slopes, and intercepts.

3. **Does ALGN improve retrieval and cross-source performance as consistently as classification?** Its detailed comparison is limited to PayBill classification. The same baseline and ablation suite could be run on text-to-user and U2U retrieval for all three data sources.

4. **Which capacity definition best predicts deployment cost?** The paper combines discrete and embedding capacity analytically but does not fully connect it to end-to-end latency, memory, energy, or tokenizer-training expense. A controlled deployment study could compare capacity estimates against measured resource use at equal downstream utility.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated methods and reported results, because the supplied text includes the complete retrieved paper, equations, tables, and figure captions.

Material missing information includes exact dataset labels and splits, scenario-level rather than averaged results, privacy/data-access details, configuration-search grids, \(\epsilon\), \(\eta\), full optimizer and hardware settings, tokenizer-training cost, random-seed variance, significance tests, public code/model/data availability, and public or cross-platform validation.
