# Hybrid Quantum-inspired Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks for Privacy-Aware Federated Biosignal Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13914) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13914)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under matched FedAvg training, HQKAN outperformed an MLP across reported aggregate metrics on two federated ECG benchmarks while reducing trainable parameters by 37.35–44.81% and per-round communication by 24.89–36.41%.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses arrhythmia classification when privacy-sensitive ECG recordings remain distributed across hospitals or devices. Federated learning avoids centralizing raw signals, but limited local samples, severe class imbalance, non-IID client distributions, and communication costs can impair performance. The authors identify a need for a compact classifier that remains effective on minority classes and under cross-client label skew (Abstract; Sections I and III-A).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hybrid classifier:** HQKAN combines fully connected encoder and decoder components with a quantum-inspired KAN latent processor whose learnable edge activations use data re-uploading activation functions (Section III-C).
- **Federated comparison:** HQKAN and an MLP are compared under matched FedAvg budgets, partitions, and inputs on MIT-BIH and INCART, using 8, 16, or 32 fully participating clients under IID and Dirichlet non-IID partitions (Sections III-B–III-C).
- **Efficiency evaluation:** The study reports trainable-parameter and full-model communication reductions for HQKAN (Section III-C).
- **Heterogeneity analysis:** At 32 clients, robustness is evaluated across five Dirichlet concentrations using Hellinger distance and aggregate AUROC/AUPRC (Section IV-C; Figures 3–4).

The evidence supports the authors’ comparative claim that HQKAN is more accurate and compact than this particular MLP under the tested simulations. It does not establish superiority over stronger ECG architectures or federated optimizers, formal privacy protection, real institutional deployment, or statistical significance of the differences.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** FedAvg is the only aggregation method, and experiments cover two ECG datasets, 8–32 clients, full participation, and simulated IID or Dirichlet label partitions (Sections III-A–III-B).
- **Analyst inference—narrow baseline:** The sole classifier baseline is an MLP; the paper does not test CNNs, recurrent models, ResNets, or other compact architectures, so the source of the advantage is not isolated.
- **Analyst inference—missing ablations:** No reported ablation separates the effects of the encoder/decoder, QKAN latent processor, activation design, parameter count, or GPU implementation.
- **Analyst inference—privacy scope:** Keeping raw data local provides data-locality benefits, but the supplied text reports no differential privacy, secure aggregation, attack evaluation, or leakage analysis.
- **Analyst inference—simulated federation:** Dirichlet label skew represents only one form of heterogeneity; patient-, device-, hospital-, or acquisition-specific shifts are not evaluated.
- **Analyst inference—uncertain generalization:** INCART is described as “cross-dataset evaluation,” but the text does not establish transfer from a MIT-BIH-trained model; it appears to be a separate dataset experiment.
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility gaps:** The retrieved text does not provide complete layer widths, QKAN configuration, client sample counts after partitioning, or a release link for this study’s code and checkpoints.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Five-seed means and standard deviations are reported, but no hypothesis tests or confidence intervals establish statistical significance.

## Open research questions

1. **Does HQKAN outperform equally sized conventional networks?** Parameter count may confound architecture and efficiency. Compare HQKAN with parameter-matched MLPs, CNNs, and compact ECG models under identical federated partitions.
2. **Which HQKAN component produces the gains?** The paper provides no component ablation. Remove or replace the QKAN processor, encoder/decoder, and DARUAN functions individually while holding parameter count fixed.
3. **Does robustness transfer to realistic institutional heterogeneity?** Only IID and synthetic Dirichlet label skew are tested. Partition data by patient, recording source, device, or hospital and evaluate unseen-site generalization.
4. **Can the method retain its gains with stronger privacy and participation constraints?** Formal privacy and client dropout are not evaluated. Add secure aggregation or differential privacy and test partial, asynchronous, or unreliable client participation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental comparisons because the retrieved text includes methods, tables, and five-seed summaries.

Materially missing information includes complete architecture specifications, exact communication-cost calculation and units, exact heterogeneity-sweep values, statistical-significance tests, patient-level partition details, runtime and memory measurements, formal privacy analysis, stronger baselines, ablations, and an explicit release of this study’s code or trained models.
