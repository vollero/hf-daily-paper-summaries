# EchoWM: Open and Enterable Omnimodal World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23189) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23189)


## One-sentence takeaway

EchoWM unifies first- and third-person navigation through calibrated relative 6-DoF camera trajectories while jointly generating 720p video, environmental audio, music, and speech, achieving leading visual-quality and control results on WBench Navigation and SANA-WM-Bench but remaining limited to camera-representable actions and imperfect long-horizon geometry.

## Problem and motivation

Interactive world models are typically silent visual simulators or rely on domain- and subject-specific controls, while joint audio-video generators generally lack continuous geometric interaction. The authors target an “enterable” generated world that users can navigate continuously while receiving synchronized visual, environmental-sound, music, and speech output (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

The central gap is the difficulty of jointly handling heterogeneous control semantics and metric scales, precise camera motion, rich audio-visual generation, and sustained continuation across both first- and third-person scenes (Introduction, p. 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified camera-intent interface:** Discrete commands and metric pose sequences are converted into the same relative 6-DoF trajectory. In first person this represents observer motion; in third person, camera–character coupling is learned from data rather than specified by a separate controller or rig (Sections 4.2.1–4.2.3; Figure 7).

- **Dataset-level scale calibration:** Translation is normalized by one robust training-set scale—the 90th percentile of per-clip maximum displacement—while rotations remain unchanged. Out-of-range trajectories are filtered rather than clipped (Section 4.2.2, Equations 4–5).

- **Relative geometric conditioning:** A lightweight relative-UCPE branch injects pairwise ray geometry into video self-attention; audio receives trajectory information only indirectly through cross-modal coupling (Section 4.2.3; Figure 6).

- **Capability-aligned data engine:** Collected gameplay, Internet gameplay, Unreal Engine simulation, and general Internet video are processed into AV-rich, control-clean, and balanced mixtures, using recovered or ground-truth metric poses where available (Section 3; Figures 4–5).

- **Progressive and autoregressive training:** AV continued pretraining, trajectory-only fine-tuning with a frozen backbone, joint fine-tuning, and teacher-forcing/self-gradient-forcing post-training progressively add control and long-horizon few-step streaming (Sections 4.3–4.4).

These results establish competitive control and visual quality under the specified benchmark protocols, including 961-frame continuation. They do not establish deterministic simulation, arbitrary actor control, metrically correct reconstructed 3D worlds, or quantitatively verified audio synchronization. Comparisons are reported by the authors; the supplied text does not provide independent replication.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Control is limited to navigation and viewpoint changes representable by relative 6-DoF camera motion; jumping, attacking, manipulation, and robot commands are not modeled explicitly (Section 7).
- EchoWM does not reproduce game-engine rules, collision state, or deterministic transitions (Section 7).
- It lacks explicit persistent 3D memory, allowing geometry, identity, state, and audio to drift over repeated turns (Section 7).
- Generalization beyond the retained trajectory range—to larger translations, higher speeds, or unusual rotations—is unestablished (Section 7).
- Recovered poses may remain erroneous and may correlate with scene content or motion blur (Section 7).
- The trajectory reaches audio only through cross-modal coupling; the authors explicitly do not claim separately supervised action-to-sound control (Discussion, p. 38).

**Analyst inferences from the supplied evidence:**

- Audio and speech capabilities are demonstrated qualitatively in Figure 20, but no objective or human audio-synchronization metric is reported.
- Section 5.5.1 says speed-control correlations, slopes, scale errors, and boundary changes are measured, yet the retrieved text supplies no corresponding numerical table or response curves; the quantitative scale-control claim therefore cannot be checked.
- The ablations do not isolate the numerical contribution of UCPE, global calibration, each data source, each training stage, or autoregressive post-training.
- The game-video data are heavily third-person and mostly lack speech (Figure 3), which may limit conclusions about balanced viewpoint and speech performance.
- WMB is self-built; annotator counts are given only for the reported 99-case scene subset, while recruitment, assignment, blinding, uncertainty, and significance testing are not described (Section 5.3; Table 8).
- The qualitative unseen-domain result covers 2D/2.5D games and robots (Figure 17), but no quantitative out-of-domain evaluation is provided.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the interface represent semantic actor actions?** It currently covers only effects expressible as camera motion. A next experiment could add explicit actor-action trajectories and compare navigation, action success, and cross-domain transfer against the camera-only model.

2. **How much does each training component contribute?** The text lacks quantitative component ablations. A factorial study could remove global scale calibration, UCPE, each fine-tuning stage, and long-horizon SGF while measuring WBench, SANA-WM, audio synchronization, and drift.

3. **Can persistent memory reduce long-horizon drift?** The authors identify the absence of explicit 3D memory as a limitation. A controlled experiment could add pose-indexed or learned scene memory and test revisit PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS and trajectory error beyond 961 frames.

4. **Does navigation causally produce appropriate sound?** Existing evidence is qualitative and trajectory conditioning is not applied directly to audio. A matched-trajectory study could vary motion while fixing scene and text, then measure event timing, semantic sound correctness, and human-rated synchronization.
