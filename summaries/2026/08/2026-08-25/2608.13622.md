# ARC: Fair Relative Advantage Comparison in Open-Ended Real-World Interaction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13622) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13622)


## One-sentence takeaway

ARC conditions group-based reinforcement-learning rollouts on interaction strategy during training, improving fair comparison and tool-use performance—especially with GRPO—while INTER3 enables responsive, strategy-annotated agent interaction.

## Problem and motivation

Open-ended interactions can admit several appropriate next actions, such as answering directly, clarifying, confirming intent, or reporting progress. Standard group-relative RL may compare these different strategies in one rollout group, allowing reward-model preferences for style or length to contaminate advantages and favor a behavioral mode rather than higher-quality execution. The authors call this the “reward fairness problem” and argue that existing agent interfaces also inadequately expose responsive, interruptible interaction for training (Introduction; Sections 3 and 5.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **ARC:** Attach a strategy instruction during training, sample rollouts within that conditioned class, compute within-strategy relative advantages, and optimize with a hybrid reward plus entropy regularization. Instructions are removed at inference (Sections 5.2–5.5).
- **Estimator analysis:** Under a stylized reward decomposition and perfect strategy compliance, conditioning removes the between-strategy mean-shift term from centered-advantage variance. This is not a convergence guarantee (Theorems 5.1–5.3; Appendix A).
- **INTER3:** A streaming framework separating visible `<answer>` spans from latent reasoning and structured tool calls, supporting progress updates, interruptions, and steering (Section 3).
- **INTER3-86K:** A strategy-annotated corpus of 86,796 examples assembled from deployment traces, public data, and teacher-generated or rewritten trajectories (Section 4; Table 2).

The evidence supports a targeted benefit for tool-using interactive agents, particularly under GRPO and PPO. Component ablations and rollout diagnostics are consistent with the proposed comparison mechanism. It does not establish universal improvement: reasoning results are mixed, DAPO gains vanish across training seeds, and the theoretical results depend on stylized assumptions rather than describing full RL convergence.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The four-family taxonomy is coarse relative to real, context-dependent behavior (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Strongest evidence is confined to open-ended tool-use; generalization to other domains is untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Results depend on normalized traces and strategy annotations that may encode deployment or annotator biases (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The variance and sample-efficiency results are estimator-level idealizations, not convergence theory (Section 5.2; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The empirical variance confidence intervals are wide because few prompt groups contain multiple realized strategies (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Dataset composition is imbalanced—Progress Update is 75.3% of RL data and tool use is 72.8% overall—so claims about minority strategies have limited support (Figure 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Some diagnostics use only one training seed, including the 50%-label-corruption experiment, limiting robustness conclusions (Appendix F; Table 11).
- **Analyst inference:** The reward relies partly on a Qwen3-235B answer judge, and Table 8 shows meaningful sensitivity to judge choice; this may limit reproducibility or portability.
- **Analyst inference:** The text says code and data “will be released,” but does not establish their availability or licensing.

## Open research questions

1. **Can conditioning use a richer or learned strategy representation?** The four-family taxonomy is explicitly coarse. A next experiment could compare fixed families with hierarchical, multi-label, or learned strategy clusters while controlling the training data.
2. **Does ARC help beyond tool-centric interaction?** The strongest evidence comes from τ/τ2 and a tool-heavy corpus. A next experiment could evaluate strategy-conditioned grouping on open-ended writing, negotiation, or multimodal interaction using the same backbone and compute.
3. **How robust is ARC to realistic annotation errors?** Only uniform 50% label replacement is tested, using one seed. A next experiment could sweep corruption levels and compare systematic confusion between similar strategies across multiple training seeds.
4. **Can fair comparison be achieved without explicit labels?** ARC requires annotated strategy instructions and introduces a train–inference mismatch. A next experiment could infer latent strategies online, cluster rollouts before advantage normalization, and compare against clean-label ARC.
