# EXPL-FR: Explaining Face Recognition Models via Vision-Language Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21486) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21486)


## One-sentence takeaway

EXPL-FR maps text prompts into a frozen face-recognition model’s embedding space through an image-only-trained adapter, enabling semantic explanations and label-free audits, though its conclusions are bounded by the vocabulary, adapter, and vision-language model’s concept recognition.

## Problem and motivation

High-performing face-recognition (FR) systems output similarity scores without revealing which human-interpretable attributes influenced their embeddings. Existing explainability methods mainly localize influential pixels, require predefined attribute labels or generators, or explain a separate vision-language model rather than the deployed matcher.

The authors target the missing post-hoc connection between an arbitrary textual vocabulary and a frozen, potentially black-box FR encoder. This matters for understanding individual decisions and auditing demographic or attribute-dependent behavior without retraining the matcher or annotating every audit dataset (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Image-only cross-model alignment:** A four-layer MLP adapter maps normalized embeddings from a frozen VLM image encoder into a frozen FR embedding space. It is trained on paired embeddings from face images using cosine alignment; neither encoder is updated, and no text or attribute labels are used (Sections 3.1–3.2, pp. 4–5).

- **Zero-shot text transfer:** The same adapter is applied to VLM text embeddings, producing candidate semantic anchors in FR space. A face’s cosine similarities to these anchors form a vocabulary projection (Section 3.3, Eqs. 3–4).

- **Model-specific semantic signatures:** A label-free, pseudo-label-based detectability score ranks which prompts remain reachable and separable after mapping. The top 100 of 978 prompts define each model’s signature (Section 3.3; Figure 2; Table 4).

- **Three explanation levels:** The signature supports per-image profiles, identity-level averages, and pairwise differences for genuine, imposter, and morph comparisons (Section 3.4; Figures 1 and 7–9).

- **Auditing at three supervision levels:** The paper compares axes built from ground-truth labels, VLM-ranked unlabeled images, and prompts alone. These axes support dependence, profile, sensitivity, and attribution analyses validated against verification behavior (Section 3.5; Figure 5).

The results establish that the adapter provides a useful, measurable bridge between frozen VLM and FR spaces and that selected prompt coordinates retain meaningful identity and audit signals across the tested models. They do not establish that individual coordinates are causal drivers of a match, that prompts are disentangled, or that high pseudo-label detectability proves a concept is correctly grounded. RFW model-ranking evidence is also based on only four models, while several explanation examples are qualitative.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- The 978 prompt directions leave a 25.78-point verification gap from the FR upper bound; distributed facial geometry may not be compactly nameable (Section 5; Supplementary Section C).
- Prompts combine correlated properties, so coordinates are directional rather than disentangled attribute measurements (Sections 3.1 and C).
- Every construction inherits VLM blind spots. Pose, illumination, negated phrasing, and extreme low resolution can yield plausible-looking but invalid audit values (Section 5; Supplementary Sections C and E).
- Detectability uses VLM-generated pseudo-labels and adapter-mapped embeddings. Its values are not absolute evidence of concept presence or invariance; the authors rely mainly on rankings (Sections 3.3, 4.3, and C).
- GAN-Control EER is near the floor for most attributes, and its age traversal partly changes identity, limiting model comparison and making age sensitivity an upper bound (Supplementary Section C).
- RFW partly overlaps the source domain of the MS1MV2 target, potentially affecting its apparent advantage (Supplementary Section C).
- Model-ranking evaluations use only four FR models, producing noisy Kendall statistics (Section 4.4; Supplementary Section C).
- All adapters use WebFace4M alignment data, so the weaker MS1MV2 result may partly reflect alignment-data mismatch rather than FR geometry (Supplementary Section C).

**Analyst inferences**

- The qualitative identity and morph examples do not establish broad human interpretability because no reader study or explanation-quality annotation is reported.
- The adapter architecture is held fixed, but the supplied text reports no comparison with linear, shallower, or substantially larger mappings; its architectural necessity is therefore unclear.
- Validation spans four AdaFace targets across ViT and ResNet backbones, but not unrelated FR objectives or deployment pipelines, limiting claims of universal black-box applicability.
- The claim that an attribute “drives rejection” should be treated cautiously: the method measures projection differences and associations, not interventions on the deployed matcher.

## Open research questions

1. **Can VLM blind spots be detected without attribute labels?** The internal two-space check fails to reliably flag pose. A next experiment could compare several VLMs and require agreement between independent image-based concept estimators before reporting an audit.

2. **Can semantic axes be made more disentangled?** The paper acknowledges that prompts mix correlated visual factors. A next experiment could orthogonalize or causally validate prompt axes using controlled, identity-preserving edits and measure cross-attribute leakage.

3. **How sensitive are findings to adapter design and alignment data?** Only one four-layer MLP recipe and WebFace4M alignment source are reported. A factorial study could vary linear versus nonlinear adapters, capacity, dataset, and dataset size while holding FR/VLM pairs fixed.

4. **Do semantic explanations help practitioners make better decisions?** The paper evaluates representation faithfulness rather than human utility. A blinded study could test whether signatures improve error detection, morph-attack review, or bias diagnosis over similarity scores and saliency maps.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the complete main paper and supplementary material, with methods, datasets, quantitative tables, and explicit limitations.

Material information not established includes a human evaluation of explanation usefulness, detailed adapter-architecture ablations, sensitivity to prompt paraphrases at vocabulary scale, compute time and hardware, uncertainty intervals for most reported metrics, and validation on FR systems outside the four AdaFace targets.
