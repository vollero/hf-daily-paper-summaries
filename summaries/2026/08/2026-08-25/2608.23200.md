# LongWoF-Bench: Evaluating EvoMap Genes for Verifiable Long-Workflow Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23200) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23200)


## One-sentence takeaway

On 252 tasks with verifier-confirmed Claude Opus trajectories, evolved EvoMap Genes improved strict pass rates over procedural Skills across seven consumer models, but unmatched reference-distilled results show only an association—not a clean causal effect—between verified provenance and utility.

## Problem and motivation

Complex workflows require models to preserve interdependent constraints across multiple steps and produce artifacts that pass strict end-to-end verification. Successful execution experience is usually discarded, forcing later runs to rediscover strategies and failure modes. The authors identify an evaluation gap around guidance provenance: existing work does not adequately establish whether guidance distilled from verifier-confirmed execution is more useful than static procedural instructions under controlled task and verifier conditions (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** LongWoF-Bench contains 778 machine-verifiable tasks: 341 code-generation, 127 agent-environment-synthesis, 151 mathematical-reasoning, and 159 rule-following tasks (Section 2.2; Figure 1, p. 3).
- **Reusable experience:** A producer model iteratively attempts a task using sanitized verifier feedback. After a successful trajectory is found, execution-critical strategies, prerequisite checks, boundary conditions, and corrections are distilled into a structured Gene (Section 3.1, p. 5).
- **Controlled comparison:** No Context, Skill, and Gene conditions share task specifications, runtimes, decoding configurations, and private verifiers; only auxiliary guidance changes (Section 4, p. 5).
- **Provenance and transfer analyses:** The study compares evolved and reference-distilled Genes and evaluates Genes across seven consumers from four model families, including consumers different from the producer (Sections 4 and 5.2).
- **Efficiency analysis:** It measures model calls, solve-time tokens, and tokens per passed task for one-shot reuse and multi-round discovery (Section 4; Tables 3 and 9).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Evolved Genes outperform Skills across all seven consumers. | On the same 252 Opus-evolved tasks, Gene-minus-Skill gains are 8.73–15.48 percentage points; paired bootstrap 95% CIs exclude zero and exact McNemar tests report \(p<0.05\) or better for every model. | Tables 4–5, p. 12 |
| Mean performance rises across guidance conditions. | Unweighted seven-model strict pass rate averages 41.0% for No Context, 51.2% for Skill, and 62.9% for Gene on the 252-task evolved subset. | Figure 3 and Section 5.1, p. 6 |
| The producer model also benefits from reuse. | Claude Opus passes 63.9% with Skill and 79.4% with Gene on the 252 evolved tasks, a 15.48-point increase. | Tables 4–5 |
| Reference-distilled Genes show the opposite pattern. | On the separate 526-task subset, Genes trail Skills for every consumer by 3.3–11.3 points. | Table 2, pp. 6–7; Table 6, p. 12 |
| Gene quality varies with the producer. | On 180 tasks where both producers found verified trajectories, Opus-authored Genes outperform Gemini-authored Genes for all consumers by 4.4–11.7 points. | Figure 4, p. 7; Table 7, p. 13 |
| One-shot Gene reuse improves Opus effectiveness and efficiency relative to Skill. | Across 252 tasks, Gene passes 200 using 723,480 tokens versus Skill’s 161 using 803,099: 39 more passes and 9.9% fewer tokens. Tokens per pass are 3,617.4 versus 4,988.2. | Section 5.3; Table 9, p. 13 |
| Reuse is cheaper than original discovery but has lower success. | Discovery uses 404 calls and 1,333,968 tokens to pass 252/252; one-shot reuse uses 252 calls and 723,480 tokens to pass 200/252, a 45.8% token reduction under unequal-success settings. | Figure 5; Tables 3 and 9 |
| Benefits differ by workflow family. | Across seven models, Gene-minus-Skill gains are uniformly positive for agent environments (15.6–29.7 points) and rule following (2.1–22.9), but code and mathematics include ties or regressions. | Figure 6, pp. 8–9; Table 10, p. 14 |

The evidence establishes a consistent paired advantage for evolved Genes over Skills on the selected 252-task subset and supports cross-family consumer reuse. The task-matched producer comparison also indicates that downstream utility depends on the producing process. It does not establish that verified provenance alone causes the evolved-versus-reference reversal, because those provenance groups contain different tasks with different selection difficulty. Nor does the discovery comparison establish equal-quality replacement: discovery passes all 252 tasks, while one-shot reuse passes 200.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated qualification:** The evolved and reference-distilled provenance groups contain different tasks. The reference subset specifically comprises tasks Opus could not solve within its evolution budget, so the reversal is provenance-associated rather than a same-task causal ablation (Section 5.2).
- **Author-stated qualification:** The matched producer experiment does not isolate whether Opus’s advantage arises from exploration quality, selected experience, or the Gene’s final expression (Section 5.2).
- **Author-stated qualification:** Discovery and reuse have unequal success rates, making the 45.8% token reduction an amortized-cost comparison rather than an equal-success comparison (Section 5.3).
- **Author-stated accounting boundary:** Token totals exclude the one-time cost of Gene distillation and auditing (Section 5.3; Table 9).
- **Analyst inference:** One recorded trial per task-condition pair cannot quantify variability across repeated hosted-model runs; the reported uncertainty is over tasks instead (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference:** The strongest claims apply to the 252 tasks for which Opus successfully produced trajectories, not automatically to all 778 benchmark tasks.
- **Analyst inference:** Family-level evidence is heterogeneous: mathematical reasoning and code generation contain several ties or regressions, limiting claims of universal benefit (Table 10).

## Open research questions

1. **Does verified provenance cause better guidance on the same tasks?** The current provenance subsets differ in task composition. A next experiment could construct evolved and reference-distilled Genes for an identical task set while controlling representation length and producer.

2. **Which stage of production determines Gene quality?** The matched Opus–Gemini comparison does not separate exploration, experience selection, and final wording. A factorial experiment could swap trajectories, selection procedures, and distillers across producers.

3. **How reliable are gains across repeated executions?** Each task-condition pair has one recorded trial. Repeating trials with controlled decoding seeds or multiple API runs could estimate run-level variance and robustness.

4. **When do Genes harm coding or mathematical performance?** Table 10 contains model-dependent regressions and ties. A targeted error analysis could stratify tasks by constraint type, required computation, and guidance content, then ablate individual Gene components.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported benchmark composition, protocols, and numerical results because the supplied text includes detailed tables and qualifications.

Material missing information includes the full task-generation and contamination-control procedure, exact evolution rollout budget beyond the three observed passing rounds, prompts and formatting for Skills and Genes, Gene lengths, distillation and auditing costs, repeated-run variance, verifier implementation details, and detailed results for the full 778-task benchmark.
