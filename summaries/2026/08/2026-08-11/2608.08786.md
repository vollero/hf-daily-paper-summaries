# SymDiag: Explainable Diagnosis for LLM Reasoning via Neuro-Symbolic Verification

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08786) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08786)


## One-sentence takeaway

SymDiag converts LLM chains of thought into dual Prolog encodings, uses self-audited step-level symbolic checks to separate translation artifacts from reasoning errors, and returns localized evidence that improves faithfulness detection and iterative repair.

## Problem and motivation

Correct final answers can conceal invalid intermediate reasoning, including unsupported assumptions, inconsistent steps, and non-entailing transformations. Existing answer matching cannot inspect the process, while LLM judges and scalar reward models provide feedback that the authors characterize as subjective, difficult to audit, or insufficiently localized. Neuro-symbolic verification could provide checkable evidence, but translation from natural language into symbolic form can itself introduce errors; the identified gap is therefore diagnosis that both localizes reasoning failures and distinguishes them from formalization noise (§1, §2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Diagnosis-centric verification:** SymDiag reports per-step pass/fail verdicts, failing indices, error labels, and symbolic evidence rather than only a score (§3.1).
- **Dual symbolic compilation:** A formal-translation branch and a critical-restatement branch independently encode the problem and chain of thought as Prolog programs (§3.2.1–§3.2.3).
- **Self-audited attribution:** Cross-encoding consistency checks and canonical rewrites classify apparent failures as `TranslationError` or `ReasoningError` before verification (§3.2.4).
- **Symbolic diagnosis and repair:** Satisfiability and local-entailment checks produce counterexamples, inconsistency witnesses, or missing-premise indicators; these guide either a local patch or a rewrite from the earliest failing step (§3.2.5–§3.3).
- **Evaluation asset:** The authors construct a 437,792-instance corpus and a manually verified 240-instance gold set spanning mathematical, logical, scientific, and general reasoning (§3.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated direction:** The conclusion calls for stronger or hybrid symbolic backends and better handling of underspecified language, indicating current dependence on Prolog expressibility and imperfect natural-language formalization (§7).
- **Analyst inference — narrow gold evaluation:** Core claims are evaluated on only 240 manually audited instances across four domains, although a much larger automatically constructed corpus exists (§3.4, §4.1.1).
- **Analyst inference — potentially biased corpus labels:** Incorrect answers are automatically labeled unfaithful, while correct-answer traces are retained through multi-judge voting before manual gold-set verification. The supplied text does not report annotation agreement or adjudication details (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference — model dependence:** SymDiag normally uses GPTOSS-120B for translation, attribution, and feedback; Figure 8 confirms lower performance with GPTOSS-20B, so results partly depend on a strong neural component (§4.1.3, §6.4).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete quantitative reporting:** Table 1 and Figures 4–6 and 8 are present only as images or prose summaries; exact baseline, per-dataset, ablation, and repair values cannot be audited from the retrieved text.
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility unclear:** Prompts, solver programs, inference cost, latency, voting counts, annotation protocol, and artifact-release details are not supplied.

## Open research questions

1. **How well does diagnosis generalize beyond the four evaluated reasoning domains?** The gold set is limited to mathematics, logic, science, and general reasoning; a next experiment could evaluate fixed SymDiag configurations on independently annotated legal, clinical, or code-reasoning traces.

2. **How accurate is `TranslationError` versus `ReasoningError` attribution?** Figure 7 reports reductions in pipeline errors but no independently labeled attribution accuracy; a next experiment could create a human-audited test set with deliberately injected translation and reasoning defects and report class-wise precision and recall.

3. **Which evidence type actually drives successful repair?** The paper ablates broad components but does not isolate counterexamples, unsat cores, and missing-premise indicators; a controlled repair study could remove or randomize each evidence type while holding the base model and token budget fixed.

4. **Can comparable performance be achieved with less computation?** Dual generation, auditing, solver execution, and up to four repairs may be costly, but resource measurements are absent; a next experiment could report latency, tokens, solver calls, and accuracy under single-branch, early-exit, and smaller-model configurations.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text clearly describes the method, evaluation setup, headline F1, and Self-Auditor statistics, but it omits exact contents of several image-rendered tables and figures.
