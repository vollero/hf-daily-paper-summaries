# Factorized Hypothesis Search for Evidence-to-Taxonomy Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06614) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06614)


## One-sentence takeaway

Factorized Hypothesis Search improves top-ranked taxonomy retrieval by generating multiple structured interpretations of indirect evidence and verifying retrieved candidates against their explicit semantic dimensions.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses evidence-to-taxonomy retrieval: mapping a located fact—such as a numeric table cell or clinical mention in context—to one concept in a large taxonomy. Unlike conventional retrieval, neither the fact nor its broader context necessarily states the target concept directly, creating what the authors call a “retrieval readiness gap” (Sections 1 and 3.1–3.3).

The authors argue that this gap is mainly interpretive and rank-oriented: raw evidence often retrieves the correct concept only deep in the list, while an oracle query containing the gold label and definition retrieves it reliably. Single-query rewriting is inadequate because the evidence may support several plausible semantic interpretations rather than one merely under-specified wording (Sections 1, 3.2, and 3.3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Task formulation:** The authors formalize evidence-to-taxonomy retrieval, where a grounding function must turn a located fact and its context into a retrieval-ready query before taxonomy search (Section 3.1).

2. **Factorized Hypothesis Search:** FHS samples multiple partial interpretations over named semantic dimensions, explicitly leaving unsupported dimensions unresolved (Sections 3.4 and 4.1).

3. **Shared structured pipeline:** Each hypothesis produces a generated definition-form query and, for tabular evidence, a programmatically rendered label-form query. Retrieved rankings are fused with reciprocal rank fusion, then reranked by an LLM verifier that evaluates candidate support dimension by dimension (Sections 4.2–4.4; Algorithm 1).

4. **Cross-domain evaluation:** The method is instantiated for financial tagging against US-GAAP and clinical diagnosis coding against ICD-10-CM/CodiEsp (Section 5.1).

5. **Controlled analyses:** Ablations examine ensembling, query rendering, verification, and fusion; a sequential variant tests whether iterative revision improves upon the initial parallel hypothesis round (Sections 5.5 and 4.5; Tables 6 and 20).

These results establish consistent improvements in shallow retrieval and, more modestly, downstream top-1 accuracy under the paper’s two task instantiations and controlled backbone. They do not establish universal superiority at deeper cutoffs: retrieval-feedback refinement had substantially higher Recall@50 on financial data. They also do not isolate factorization alone, because the free-text ablation simultaneously removes dimension-level verification, or establish effectiveness across different LLM families.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** FHS requires a small semantic-dimension schema derived from taxonomy structure and metadata. Inventories without such structure would require manual definition or automated schema induction (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** All LLM components use Qwen3-32B, so robustness across model families and scales—and the associated cost trade-off—is unknown (Limitations).
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation assumes the target fact or mention has already been located; upstream extraction is not modeled (Section 5.1).
- **Author-stated scope:** The study is zero-shot and offline; methods requiring task-specific training or interactive clarification are excluded (Section 2).
- **Author-stated validity caution:** The financial narrative subset contains only 168 facts, so modality-specific comparisons there are indicative rather than decisive (Appendix E.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-domain evidence is limited to two taxonomy-alignment settings, both with substantial curated taxonomy metadata; this weakens the claim of a fully general framework.
- **Analyst inference:** The largest representation ablation confounds factorization with verification, preventing a clean causal estimate of factorization alone (Sections 4.1 and 5.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Baselines were adapted because they normally assume explicit queries, so conclusions compare the authors’ implementations of those adaptations rather than unmodified prior systems (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** CodiEsp evaluation excludes 287 facts that failed exact English mention relocation; performance on those excluded cases is not established (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** FHS was not call- or compute-matched to the parallel baselines and was materially more expensive, although the paper reports this difference explicitly (Appendices C.2 and E.6).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the semantic dimensions be induced automatically?** This remains open because both schemas were derived from existing taxonomy metadata. A next experiment could compare manual schemas with schemas induced from labels, definitions, and hierarchy structure on inventories with varying metadata quality.

2. **Which gains come from factorization independently of verification?** The free-text ablation removes both. A matched experiment could verify free-text hypotheses using an equivalent candidate-scoring budget or apply a non-dimensional verifier to both representations.

3. **How robust is FHS across backbone models and scales?** Only Qwen3-32B was tested. A factorial evaluation across several model families and sizes could measure accuracy, calibration, latency, and cost while holding retrieval fixed.

4. **Can specialist hypothesis generators improve coverage without degrading plausibility?** Forced-diversity prompting reduced development coverage, but functionally specialized generators were not tested. A matched-budget experiment could assign interpretable specialties, reporting each specialist’s solo recall, selection frequency, and ensemble gain.
