# Don&#x27;t Scroll Back: Missing-Evidence Memory for Streaming Dialogue Summarization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09043) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09043)


## One-sentence takeaway

ReMEMBER detects unresolved dependencies in a recent dialogue window, retrieves and refines historical evidence for them under a fixed budget, and raises memory recall from the strongest baseline’s 0.5412 to 0.6984 on the authors’ 900-instance benchmark.

## Problem and motivation

The paper studies streaming dialogue summarization: generating a self-contained summary of a recent window while the preceding dialogue grows indefinitely. Recent utterances can omit referents, attributes, rationales, or causal links that are only recoverable from history.

The authors argue that closed-dialogue and global-summary methods are poorly matched to this setting. Local-only summaries leave dependencies unresolved; full-history input is costly and can bury useful evidence; incremental or hierarchical compression can discard fine-grained facts; and conventional retrieval searches for history resembling what the window says rather than evidence for what it leaves unstated (Sections 1 and 3.1; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Task and benchmark.** The authors formalize streaming dialogue summarization and construct 900 window-level instances from 35 chit-chat and workplace dialogues. Each instance links salient current-window utterances to historical evidence for referential, attribute, or relational gaps (Sections 3.1–3.2; Table 5).

2. **Gap-conditioned retrieval.** ReMEMBER uses a compact LLM to identify summary-worthy, history-dependent utterances and formulate evidence-seeking queries without hypothesizing their answers. Sparse BM25 and dense embedding retrieval are fused to obtain candidate history chunks (Section 4.1).

3. **Evidence refinement.** Within each retrieved 128-token chunk, individual turns are ranked against the gap query. A round-robin allocator then selects evidence across gaps under a 1,024-token memory budget, preventing one dependency from consuming the budget (Section 4.2).

4. **Separated evaluation.** The benchmark independently measures evidence recovery and whether recovered evidence reaches the summary, alongside current-window coverage, conciseness, and faithfulness (Section 3.3; Appendix B).

These results establish that, within this benchmark, model suite, and fixed-budget setup, targeting missing evidence improves both evidence recovery and its inclusion in summaries relative to the tested bounded-memory baselines. They do not establish superiority on other dialogue domains, languages, window or budget sizes, human-authored reference summaries, or non-LLM evaluation. They also show that retrieval is not the only bottleneck: even Ideal Memory reflected only 80.34%, 73.73%, and 72.96% of referential, attribute, and relational gaps in summaries (Table 3).

## Limitations and validity risks

No dedicated author-stated limitations section appears in the supplied text. Several validity boundaries are nevertheless explicit:

- **Narrow source coverage:** only 35 source dialogues and two domains underpin the 900 sampled windows; workplace examples comprise 600 of the 900 instances (Section 3.2; Table 5).
- **LLM-mediated ground truth and scoring:** benchmark construction uses majority agreement among LLMs, while all reported metrics are judged by Qwen3.6-27B. No human validation results are supplied (Sections 3.2 and 5.1; Appendix A.3).
- **Single runs:** all results come from one experimental run, so variance or statistical significance is unavailable (Appendix D).
- **Resource dependence:** experiments use open-weight models on a single H200 at FP16; ReMEMBER also depends on an LLM detector and embedding model (Table 10; Appendix D).
- **Restricted gap representation:** evaluation assigns at most one representative gap to each history-dependent salient utterance, although an utterance may contain several missing dependencies (Appendix A.2–A.3).
- **Full-memory comparison is imperfect:** histories beyond the generator’s context limit are truncated, and faithfulness for full-context methods is verified through top-50 BM25 chunks rather than directly against all history (Section 5.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference — evaluator coupling risk:** using related LLM-centric procedures for annotation, generation, and judging may favor phenomena those models recognize consistently; the absence of human evaluation leaves the magnitude of this risk unresolved.
- **Analyst inference — limited budget sensitivity:** the main comparisons fix both window and memory at 1,024 tokens. The supplied results do not show whether rankings persist across substantially smaller or larger budgets.

## Open research questions

1. **Can summary generation use retrieved evidence more reliably?** Ideal Memory still falls well short of complete gap reflection (Table 3). A next experiment could train or prompt generators to explicitly align every retrieved gap with a summary claim and compare coverage without reducing faithfulness.

2. **How robust is the benchmark to human judgment?** Both annotations and metrics are LLM-mediated, and no human agreement study is reported. A stratified human audit across gap types and domains could measure annotation precision, evidence sufficiency, and evaluator correlation.

3. **How does ReMEMBER scale across memory and window budgets?** The main setup fixes both at 1,024 tokens. A budget sweep could measure recall, latency, and summary quality from very small memories through larger contexts while holding retrievers and generators fixed.

4. **Does missing-evidence retrieval generalize beyond the two evaluated domains?** The benchmark covers chit-chat and workplace dialogue only. Testing multilingual conversations, customer support, meetings, or technical incident streams would reveal whether the gap taxonomy and retrieval gains transfer.
