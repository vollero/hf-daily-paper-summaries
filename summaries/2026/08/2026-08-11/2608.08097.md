# OasisKV: Scaling In-Decode KV Cache Beyond HBM with Lookahead Sparse Prefetching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08097) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08097)


## One-sentence takeaway

OasisKV keeps full decode-time KV caches outside GPU HBM and uses speculative-decoding lookahead to prefetch a sparse working set, yielding up to 2.3× dense-vLLM throughput in the tested settings while generally remaining close to full-attention accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Long-context LLM decoding is constrained by KV-cache capacity and traffic: the cache grows linearly with batch size and context length, limiting how many requests fit in HBM (§2.1). Sparse attention reduces computation and reads but ordinarily retains the full cache in HBM; hierarchical retrieval moves KV storage off-GPU but puts transfers on the decode critical path; existing prefetch approaches remain limited by cross-tier bandwidth and memory management (§1, §2.2).

The authors target production-scale serving where optimized decode steps leave little time to hide PCIe or network transfers, particularly under multi-GPU and prefill–decode disaggregation. In disaggregated systems, transferring and staging every request’s full cache additionally increases admission latency and decode-node DRAM consumption (§3.2–§3.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Lookahead sparse prediction:** OasisKV reuses draft tokens from an EAGLE-3-style speculative-decoding component as a training-free-from-the-deployer’s-perspective signal. Draft queries scan compact Quest-style per-block key summaries to predict the next step’s head-wise top-K KV blocks (§4.2.1).
- **Asynchronous prefetch pipeline:** Per-layer prediction, selection, and CPU-to-GPU transfer run concurrently on separate CUDA streams, with layer-local synchronization intended to keep retrieval one step ahead of attention (§4.2.2, Fig. 8).
- **Bandwidth-bounded cache replacement:** Delta selection preserves already-resident blocks, evicts least-recently-selected blocks, and caps newly fetched blocks per head and step (§4.2.3).
- **Production-engine integration:** The vLLM prototype adds a bounded head-wise logical mapping over existing GPU and CPU page tables, supports transitions from dense to sparse decoding, and operates with tensor parallelism (§4.3, §5.1).
- **Remote partial fetching:** In disaggregated serving, only the initial sparse set, key summaries, and draft state are transferred at admission; later misses are fetched once, asynchronously, from the prefill-side full cache (§4.4, Fig. 10).

These results support the authors’ claim that sparse residency can trade saved HBM capacity for greater concurrency and throughput on the evaluated H100 systems. They also demonstrate that prediction and transfer can often be overlapped effectively. They do not establish equivalent gains on other accelerators, interconnects, models without suitable draft heads, short-context workloads, or production traffic distributions. Accuracy comparisons with Quest and FreeKV are not direct end-to-end system comparisons because those methods use a different serving stack and stack-specific full-attention anchors (§5.2, §5.4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Draft tokens are used only for prefetch prediction and forcibly rejected; joint speculative decoding is future work (§5.3.1).
- **Author-stated:** The prototype lacks prefix caching. Its reported prefix-cache TTFT gains are analytical estimates, not measured system results (§5.5.2, Fig. 15).
- **Author-stated:** Each full request cache remains in prefill-node host DRAM until completion; extension to remote memory servers or SSD is proposed but not evaluated (§4.4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Hardware coverage is narrow: all measurements use H100 nodes with PCIe Gen5, and disaggregation uses 400-Gbps RoCE (§5.2). Transfer hiding and throughput gains may differ elsewhere.
- **Analyst inference:** The approach depends on a compatible draft model or MTP artifact. Although deployment requires no predictor-specific training, the paper evaluates only models with named public EAGLE-3 heads (§3.1 footnote, §5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Performance comparisons against ShadowKV, InfiniGen, and FreeKV use each system’s own framework; unsupported or OOM points are omitted, and no cross-GPU runs exist for those baselines (§5.2–§5.3.1). This limits attribution of gains solely to the proposed mechanisms.
- **Analyst inference:** LongBench v2 uses one seed, while some per-split changes are several points despite small overall changes; uncertainty intervals are not reported (Table 1, §5.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text gives no public code or artifact-release statement, limiting independent reproducibility despite substantial implementation detail.

## Open research questions

1. **Can accepted draft tokens jointly amortize prediction overhead and accelerate generation?** The prototype rejects every draft token (§5.3.1). A next experiment should compare forced rejection against integrated speculative acceptance across batch sizes, reporting throughput, TPOT, acceptance rate, traffic, and accuracy.

2. **How robust is lookahead selection without a high-quality public draft head?** All evaluated models use named EAGLE-3 heads (§5.2). A next experiment could vary draft-model size and quality, including previous-query and lightweight learned predictors, while measuring top-K agreement, accuracy, and total system cost.

3. **Does remote partial fetching remain effective with real prefix caching and production arrivals?** Prefix-cache TTFT results are analytical, while measured disaggregation uses a fixed synthetic request sweep (§5.3.2, §5.5.2). A next experiment should implement prefix caching and replay bursty traces across cache-hit rates and network contention levels.

4. **How should fetch cap and top-K be adapted online?** Fixed settings expose large accuracy–bandwidth–throughput trade-offs (§5.3.3, §5.5.1). A next experiment could compare fixed caps with per-request or per-layer controllers under a target accuracy or TPOT constraint.
