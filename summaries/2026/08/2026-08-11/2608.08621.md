# Business Arena: Benchmarking LLM Agents in a Realistic Marketplace

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08621) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08621)


## One-sentence takeaway

Business Arena evaluates LLM agents as autonomous cross-border shop operators and finds large, reliably measured performance differences, but even the strongest model substantially trails an expert-designed strategy and sometimes fails basic profitability or compliance requirements.

## Problem and motivation

Existing agent benchmarks mostly test bounded workflows or fixed environments with prompt feedback and verifiable solutions, while business operation requires coordinated decisions under partial information, delayed consequences, changing conditions, and persistent obligations. Existing business benchmarks also cover only portions of the physical-commerce cycle.

The authors address this gap with a controlled, reproducible marketplace intended to test whether agents can operate an entire business without risking real capital, customers, or regulatory violations (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **End-to-end business benchmark:** Business Arena simulates a 30-day cross-border B2B shop spanning research, sourcing, inventory, logistics, pricing, advertising, sales, service, compliance, and finance. It includes more than 60 agent-facing tools and continuous autonomous operation (Sections 3.1–3.4; Appendix A).

2. **Data-grounded, dynamic marketplace:** Supplier offers derive from Alibaba.com data, while demand, tariffs, seasons, and commercial events are calibrated from cited public sources. Hidden demand, evolving suppliers, NPC competitors, delayed shipping, financial frictions, and compliance constraints create partial observability and non-stationarity (Section 3.2; Appendices B–F).

3. **Diagnostic evaluation framework:** Final net worth is complemented by expert-designed reference strategies, capability-level economic metrics, action-to-outcome attribution, and save–fork–load state restoration for controlled continuations (Section 4; Appendices J–K).

4. **Benchmark validation and model study:** The authors evaluate 15 frontier models across 150 runs, analyze behavioral styles and failures, and use mechanism ablations to test whether intended evidence-based behavior beats neglect or simulator-oriented shortcuts (Sections 5–6; Appendices G–H).

These results establish stable differences among the tested models under one fixed leaderboard world and show that the tested competent policies outperform selected neglect and misuse controls. They do not establish performance in live commerce, robustness across different market seeds, superiority over human business operators, or that the expert-designed reference is an optimal policy.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The arena abstracts external systems behind structured tools, so it does not test safe execution in live storefronts, third-party platforms, inboxes, or GUIs (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Coverage is restricted to cross-border B2B commerce and does not represent the full range of businesses or industries (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Leaderboard reliability is conditional on a single shared world; robustness across market realizations requires crossing models with multiple world seeds (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** The compressed mapping of one real-world year into 30 simulated days may alter planning and timing demands relative to real operations (Appendices A and C).
- **Analyst inference:** Mechanism ablations validate selected hand-designed policies, not every strategy an LLM might discover; compliance and financial mechanisms receive no policy ablation because the authors regard their consequences as directly observable and auditable (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Public demand signals were post-processed to correlate with oracle demand, so conclusions about market inference depend partly on that calibration choice; its sensitivity is not reported (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text does not provide complete implementation, licensing, model-cost, or prompt details sufficient to independently assess full reproducibility.

## Open research questions

1. **Do model rankings persist across market realizations?** The leaderboard uses repeated launches of one shared world. A next experiment should cross every model with multiple world seeds and report model-by-world interactions.

2. **How well does simulated competence transfer to production interfaces?** Structured tools omit live storefront, inbox, and GUI execution. A next experiment could replay matched business decisions through a sandboxed production-like interface and measure execution errors and unsafe side effects.

3. **Which components causally improve agent performance?** The paper diagnoses behaviors but does not report model-level interventions based on those diagnoses. A next experiment could train or prompt agents with action-linked feedback and compare profitability, compliance, and generalization against trajectory-level feedback.

4. **How sensitive are conclusions to simulator calibration?** Demand signals, NPC populations, salvage rules, and financial frictions are designed choices. A next experiment could vary these parameters systematically and test whether rankings and operating styles remain stable.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes detailed methods, appendices, metrics, ablations, and repeated-run statistics.

Material missing information includes the complete code and data release terms, exact prompts and runtime configurations, per-run inference cost, sensitivity to multiple world seeds, full results for every skill metric, calibration sensitivity, and any evaluation against actual human business operators.
