# The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam Does

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05136) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05136)


## One-sentence takeaway

The author argues that optimizer equivariance to latent-basis rotations governs access to gradient flow’s low-rank bias in factored models, while a separate spectral schedule determines performance within the equivariant class.

## Problem and motivation

Factored models \(W=UV^\top\) admit many parameterizations related by \((U,V)\mapsto(UQ,VQ)\) for orthogonal \(Q\), all representing the same matrix and loss. The paper asks why optimizers starting from gauge-equivalent factorizations can nevertheless select different interpolating solutions, and whether a general criterion predicts preservation of gradient descent’s low-rank implicit bias.

The identified gap is a connection between optimizer basis dependence and interpolant selection in factored models, together with a classification covering commonly deployed optimizers rather than gradient flow alone (§1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Equivariance criterion and theory.** Gauge equivariance is defined for stateful optimizers (§3, Definition 3.1). The paper classifies GD, momentum, shared-scalar Adam, Muon, and Shampoo as equivariant, while Adam, RMSProp, signSGD, Lion, and Adafactor break the symmetry (§4, Propositions 4.2–4.3).

2. **Structural characterization.** For full-column-rank gradients, every memoryless equivariant rule is characterized as a Gram-determined left preconditioner,
   \(\Phi(G)=H(GG^\top)G\) (§4, Theorem 4.5).

3. **Path-transfer result.** A common-scalar continuous flow follows the same parameter-space path as gradient flow under time reparameterization, subject to integrability and convergence conditions (§4, Theorem 4.6). This does not cover scalar-Adam’s momentum state.

4. **Controlled optimizer experiments.** Nine update rules are compared after interpolation on planted matrix sensing (§5, Table 2), supplemented by an Adam anisotropy dial that continuously moves from coordinate-wise to shared-scalar preconditioning (§7, Figure 3).

5. **Within-class spectral analysis and broader tests.** A spectral-tail sweep studies when Muon’s equal-rate schedule helps or hurts (§8, Table 4); gauge-equivalent transformer twins test basis sensitivity in attention (§6, Table 3); and two hyperspectral completion datasets test GD and Adam at matched training loss (§9, Table 5).

6. **Constructive repair.** Replacing coordinate-wise clipping in FlowAdam with global-norm clipping restores equivariance of the injected velocity, while full optimizer equivariance additionally requires replacing Adam’s diagonal denominator (§10).

The experiments support the author’s claim that gauge equivariance predicts access to a useful low-rank mechanism under the studied tasks and budgets. They also show that equivariance alone does not determine recovery: spectral schedule, learning rate, initialization, tail energy, and training horizon matter. The evidence does not establish that equivariant optimizers always generalize better, that Adam cannot find low-rank solutions, or that the observed rankings hold asymptotically; long-anneal signum and untuned Shampoo are explicit exceptions (§11, Appendix D.10).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The transfer theorem covers memoryless common-scalar continuous flows, not scalar-Adam’s momentum dynamics or general stateful equivariant optimizers (§4, Remark 4.7).

- **Author-stated:** Equivariance is neither necessary nor sufficient for low-rank recovery: ScaledGD is equivariant but performs poorly, while long-anneal signum can recover despite being non-equivariant (§4, §11, Appendix D.10).

- **Author-stated:** Experiments are modest in scale—matrices up to \(2000\times200\), transformers up to six layers—and emphasize controlled mechanism tests rather than state-of-the-art benchmarks (§11).

- **Author-stated:** Results depend on finite budgets and optimizer-specific learning-rate coverage. Shampoo’s damping was not retuned across sizes, and some grids were undersampled (§9, §11).

- **Author-stated:** The hyperspectral 43–44% gap depends on a train-only learning-rate selection rule; test-informed per-method selection reduces the reported Indian Pines advantage to 13%, though the ordering remains (§9, Appendix D.6).

- **Author-stated:** Late transformer-twin divergence can reflect finite-precision or dynamical instability rather than symmetry violation; the paper treats step-1 drift as the cleaner diagnostic (§6).

- **Analyst inference:** The main optimizer-zoo table reports three-seed means without dispersion, so uncertainty for its exact numerical gaps must be assessed from the separate ten-seed ladder rather than Table 2 alone.

- **Analyst inference:** The paper’s model-merging consequence is indirect: it measures disagreement in a gauge-invariant product but does not perform end-to-end merging (§6).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the spectral-tail crossover be predicted from full coupled dynamics?** Proposition 8.1 uses a decoupled two-timescale idealization, leaving the empirical boundary near 4% tail energy without a full theorem. A next experiment could vary measurement density, initialization, spectrum shape, and budget jointly and test a derived boundary predictor.

2. **Does momentum preserve the limiting interpolant of common-scalar flow?** The transfer theorem excludes first-moment EMA, so scalar-Adam’s agreement with gradient flow is empirical. A next study could combine a momentum-invariance proof attempt with decreasing-step simulations against continuous gradient flow.

3. **What causes long-anneal signum’s low-rank recovery?** Appendix D.10 rules out a simple rank-one-update explanation but provides no mechanism. Logging singular-mode growth across learning rates, annealing lengths, and spectral-tail levels could distinguish max-margin, threshold, and discrete-time explanations.

4. **Does early gauge dependence harm downstream reuse?** The paper infers consequences for model merging but does not test merging directly. A next experiment could train gauge twins, perform optimal per-head alignment, merge them, and measure loss and functional disagreement.
