# What to Edit Next: Visually Aligned Image-Editing Follow-Up Suggestions in Conversational Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07565) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07565)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors combine supervised slate construction, click-aligned reinforcement learning, and image-first edit verification to improve follow-up image-edit suggestions while reducing visual inconsistency from 3.7% to 0.9% and increasing several online engagement metrics.

## Problem and motivation

The paper studies how a conversational image-creation system should recommend the user’s next edits. Unlike text-only query suggestion, this task requires suggestions that are appealing and diverse but also executable on the latest image.

The authors identify two gaps: prior systems do not jointly learn from behavioral preferences and verify recommendations against the current visual state, and click feedback alone can favor attractive suggestions that assume nonexistent objects or request changes already present. Their audit classifies 80.1% of 100,000 follow-up queries as image-dependent, supporting the need for multimodal grounding (§1; Appendix A.5).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Task-specific supervised learning:** A human-reviewed taxonomy of 61 editing intents constrains a vision-language teacher that generates candidate suggestions from real image, query, and intent contexts. Validation produces 39.5K six-suggestion training slates for LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen3-VL-8B (§3.3; Figure 3).

2. **Behavioral preference alignment:** The authors construct 173,071 click-preference pairs, comparing a clicked suggestion only with unclicked suggestions displayed above it to reduce position bias. An 8B vision-language reward model and multi-objective GRPO optimize click preference alongside format, SFT proximity, length, and diversity (§3.4).

3. **Visual-consistency supervision:** An image-first verifier separates each recommendation into required sources and a target state, then checks for missing sources and already-satisfied targets. Its grounding score becomes a sixth GRPO reward but is not required during serving (§3.5; Figure 4).

4. **Industrial evaluation:** The cumulative system is evaluated offline on real Qwen App sessions and online through a 14-day, user-randomized experiment involving millions of users per arm (§4.1; Appendix F).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that direct visual-consistency supervision complements click optimization in this deployment. The randomized online experiment provides evidence of product-level effects relative to the concurrent prompt-engineered policy, while cumulative offline ablations associate the grounding reward with fewer visual errors.

It does not establish generalization beyond Qwen App, other languages or recommendation interfaces, or comparison against independently developed multimodal recommendation systems. The supplied text also omits the numeric contents of several displayed tables, limiting inspection of all arms and uncertainty estimates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Position-aware pairing reduces but does not eliminate display-position bias; no propensity correction is used (§2, §3.4.1).
- **Author-stated:** The verifier fails open on uncertain, unparseable, or failed cases, so subtle visual errors can go undetected (§3.5.2–§3.5.3; Appendix E).
- **Author-stated:** The calibration set is not sampled from production prevalence, and some inconsistent examples are semantically constructed. Its recall and false-rejection estimates transfer more readily than precision (§4.5; Appendix B.2–B.3).
- **Author-stated:** The verifier ablation changes image-first observation, source–target decomposition, and post-parsing guards together, so their individual contributions are not isolated (Appendix B.3).
- **Author-stated:** Preference data lack usable user IDs, preventing a user-level training/validation split; 22 paired impressions also have empty session IDs (Appendix A.1).
- **Author-stated:** Serving-stack seeds are not reproducible across runs, although arms are regenerated and scored together (§A.7).
- **Author-stated:** Production prompts, lexicons, and exact post-processing triggers are omitted, limiting full reproduction (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** The evidence is strongly product-specific: it uses internal traffic, a Chinese recommendation rail, proprietary logs, and Qwen-family policies, so external validity remains uncertain.
- **Analyst inference:** The online gains are against a prompt-engineered version of the same backbone. The text does not report online comparisons with alternative learned multimodal recommenders.
- **Analyst inference:** Reported \(p<0.05\) establishes significance under the authors’ test, but effect uncertainty cannot be assessed because confidence intervals and exact user counts are absent.

## Open research questions

1. **Would explicit propensity correction outperform the above-click pairing rule?** Residual display bias remains acknowledged. A next experiment could train matched reward models using the current rule versus estimated examination propensities and compare held-out preference calibration and downstream A/B metrics.

2. **Which verifier component produces the gain?** Observation ordering, source–target decomposition, and post-processing guards change jointly. A factorial ablation could add each component separately and measure error-type recall, false rejection, and resulting policy inconsistency.

3. **Can fail-open errors be reduced without suppressing valid creativity?** The verifier misses uncertain and subtle states, while high false rejection could encourage generic edits. A next experiment could use calibrated uncertainty or multi-verifier agreement and trace the quality–inconsistency frontier during RL.

4. **Does the framework transfer across products, languages, and image-editing models?** All reported deployment evidence comes from one Qwen App surface. Replication on other interfaces and model families, with identical offline and randomized online metrics, would test generality.
