# VectraYX-Vision-1B: A Sub-2B Spanish/LATAM Cybersecurity Vision-Language Model with Structured Visual Reasoning and Native Tool Use

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-11.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08477) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08477)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper delivers an offline Spanish cybersecurity VLM stack, corpus, and benchmarks, but its valid checkpoints show almost no visual grounding, leaving the proposed NoPE–vision interaction and remediation untested.

## Problem and motivation

Security analysts rely on text-dense screenshots from tools such as IDA, Ghidra, Wireshark, Nmap, and Volatility, while text-only models cannot inspect those images and general-purpose VLMs may lack cybersecurity specialization, technical Spanish, or a sufficiently small offline footprint. The authors target Spanish-speaking teams working on air-gapped or data-sovereign systems, where uploading sensitive screenshots to cloud models may be prohibited (§1, §1.1).

The identified gap is a compact, locally deployable VLM combining Spanish cybersecurity understanding, visual reasoning, and guarded tool invocation. The paper also raises an architectural question: whether periodic layers without positional encoding help or hinder attention over an injected visual-token block (§1.2, §3.4, §8.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Model architecture:** A frozen SigLIP-so400m encoder produces 729 visual tokens, which a two-layer MLP maps into a 1.04B-parameter Spanish cybersecurity decoder. The decoder alternates three RoPE layers with one NoPE layer and supports native reasoning and MCP tool-call tokens (§3, Figure 1).

- **Vision curriculum:** A fourth training phase extends the existing language curriculum through projector-only alignment, joint visual instruction tuning with text replay, and reasoning/tool supervision (§4.2, Table 1).

- **Data and evaluation assets:** The authors construct 14,596 multimodal QA pairs across ten security domains and introduce B6_vision and B7_think, with a deterministic synthetic-image renderer (§5, Table 2; §6.2–§6.3).

- **Deployment:** Separate decoder and multimodal-projector GGUF artifacts support offline llama.cpp inference (§3.6, §7.5).

- **Negative result and diagnostic:** Valid checkpoints remain near zero on visual grounding. The paper also identifies an inference loader bug—failure to strip an `llm.` checkpoint prefix—that invalidated earlier measurements by leaving the decoder randomly initialized (§7.3, §9).

- **Unresolved architectural contribution:** The released V0/V1/V2 design compares periodic NoPE, all-RoPE, and NoPE plus learned 2D visual positions; no results are yet reported (§7.4, Table 6; §8.2).

The evidence establishes a functional, compact inference pipeline and a small positive difference between the best image-conditioned checkpoint and the text-only control. It does **not** establish useful visual competence, effective native visual reasoning, safe tool use, superiority over another VLM, or any benefit or harm from NoPE. The numerical results are single-run, and no uncertainty intervals are actually presented in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

Author-stated limitations include:

- Visual grounding remains insufficient; the remediated training run and V0/V1/V2 ablation results are pending (§9).
- The largely LLM-generated QA corpus may contain answer leakage and factual errors, and no proposed expert audit of at least 200 items has been completed (§5.4, §9).
- Synthetic screenshots may not transfer to real tool interfaces; the real-image subset is small and the gap is unquantified (§9).
- Some Spanish data are machine-translated, while the LATAM-specific subset contains only 120 pairs (§5.5, §9).
- The frozen encoder may impose a ceiling on out-of-distribution security imagery; the frozen-versus-unfrozen experiment is unrun (§9).
- Training uses SFT without preference optimization, so reasoning traces may be unfaithful (§9).
- Evaluation is single-seed, single-image, and single-turn (§6.6, §9).
- Visual prompt injection and tool-call over-triggering are recognized but unevaluated (§8.4, §9).
- Model and benchmark construction by the same author creates an independence and benchmark-overfitting risk (§9).

**Analyst inference:** The reported gain from 0.02 to 0.08 tool identification corresponds to only a few additional correct items in a 50-item test, so its reliability cannot be assessed without the promised confidence interval or repeated runs. Likewise, the claim that unfreezing token embeddings *caused* tool-use recovery is suggestive rather than conclusive because Table 4 does not show a controlled run differing only in that setting.

## Open research questions

1. **Do periodic NoPE layers help visual grounding?**  
   This remains open because V0/V1/V2 results are pending. Train all three variants under the same remediated schedule and compare paired B6 results separately for spatial panes and linear terminal output.

2. **Is inadequate training volume the main cause of failure?**  
   The paper attributes near-zero scores to limited vision SFT but does not test a scaling curve. Run matched checkpoints over increasing step and annotation budgets, reporting B6/B7 trajectories and text-retention metrics.

3. **Does adapting SigLIP improve security-screen understanding?**  
   The encoder remains frozen and the planned comparison is unrun. Compare frozen, partially unfrozen, and fully unfrozen encoders on both synthetic and independently collected real screenshots.

4. **Can image-conditioned tool calls be made safe?**  
   Visual injection and over-triggering have not been measured. Evaluate benign, adversarial, and irrelevant screenshots with tool-call precision, false-trigger rate, and runtime guardrails enabled.
