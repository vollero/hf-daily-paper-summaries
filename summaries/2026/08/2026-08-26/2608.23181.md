# CyberFactory: Scaling Cyber Security Capabilities with Instances from the Wild

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23181) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23181)


## One-sentence takeaway

CyberFactory converts public vulnerability evidence into verified, tool-interactive training trajectories, producing OpenAegis, which achieves 58.1% Pass@1 on CyberGym under a one-hour budget versus 29.6% for its Qwen 3.5 base model.

## Problem and motivation

The authors address the lack of a unified, reproducible way to train open-weight LLMs for cybersecurity. Existing open efforts either release capable models without reproducible training recipes, specialize in isolated tasks, lack scalable agentic data, or rely on rollouts without sufficiently strong security-domain procedures. CyberFactory aims to connect real-world vulnerability artifacts, executable verification, trajectory synthesis, and model training across PoC generation, vulnerability patching, and cybersecurity QA (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified training framework:** CyberFactory links source adaptation, executable-instance reconstruction, difficulty calibration, trajectory synthesis, and multi-task model training (Figure 2, p. 4).
- **Real-world, verifiable instances:** It derives tasks from ARVO, OSS-Fuzz, and wild CVEs, reconstructing pre- and post-patch environments and using differential execution to verify PoCs (Sections 3.1 and 4.1, pp. 4–5).
- **Skill-guided agentic supervision:** A reusable vulnerability-analysis skill directs a teacher toward source inspection, domain-guided exploration, validation, and revision using environment feedback. Only successful, task-verified trajectories are retained (Section 3.3, p. 5).
- **Grounded CyberQA construction:** Answers are obtained first from execution, program structure, or authoritative reports; generated questions then undergo traceability, leakage, and uniqueness checks (Section 3.2, pp. 4–5).
- **Released model and instances:** OpenAegis is obtained by full-parameter supervised fine-tuning of Qwen 3.5-397B-A17B. The paper says synthesized instances are released and that the model does not require the analysis skill at inference time (pp. 3–4; Section 4.2, p. 6).

The results establish a substantial CyberGym association between CyberFactory training and improved verified PoC generation, including a controlled same-backbone comparison. Behavioral measurements are consistent with the intended procedure being learned. They do not establish that the skill alone causes the complete model-level gain, because the paper explicitly says the aggregate result does not isolate individual pipeline components, and the teacher skill comparison is not compute-matched. They also do not establish comparable gains for patch generation or CyberQA.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is bounded by the available CVE artifacts, CyberGym coverage, and a fixed one-hour execution budget (Conclusion, p. 10).
- **Author-stated:** Some targets benefit from manual input construction rather than a fuzzing-first strategy; broader vulnerability, project, and programming-language coverage remains future work (Conclusion, p. 10).
- **Author-stated:** Patch generation and CyberQA have not yet received equally rigorous evaluation (Conclusion, p. 10).
- **Author-stated:** The skill-guided GLM 5.2 comparison uses five 15-minute attempts versus one 60-minute attempt and therefore is not compute-matched (Section 5.2, p. 7).
- **Author-stated:** Skill-injected GLM 5.2 can become over-dependent on the supplied domain prior (Section 5.3, p. 8).
- **Analyst inference:** The same-backbone comparison does not separate the effects of instance selection, skill guidance, successful-trajectory filtering, multi-task data, and context compaction; no component-wise training ablation is reported.
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility may be resource-constrained: full-parameter training uses 256 GPUs, and inference allows up to one hour per task (Sections 4.1–4.2, pp. 5–6).
- **Analyst inference:** Behavioral internalization is supported by directional similarity and event statistics, but these measurements do not prove that the model learned the exact causal procedure encoded by the skill.

## Open research questions

1. **Which CyberFactory component drives the CyberGym improvement?** The paper does not isolate skill guidance, verification filtering, source mixture, or multi-task supervision. A factorial training ablation could remove each component while holding the backbone and trajectory count fixed.

2. **Do gains transfer to patching and CyberQA?** Those tasks are included in training, but the authors say rigorous evaluation remains incomplete. A next experiment could compare OpenAegis and its base model on executable patch correctness, regression preservation, and source-grounded QA accuracy.

3. **How robust is the method beyond the current vulnerability distribution?** Coverage is limited by available CVEs, projects, languages, and sanitizer-based environments. A held-out evaluation stratified by project, language, CWE class, and vulnerability source could test distributional transfer.

4. **Can adaptive strategy selection outperform a fixed vulnerability-analysis workflow?** Some targets favor manual construction, while others may favor fuzzing. A routing experiment could choose among manual analysis, fuzzing, symbolic techniques, and hybrid workflows under the same wall-clock budget.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported CyberGym results, training configuration, and stated pipeline because the supplied text includes the complete methodology, tables, and conclusions.

Materially missing or insufficiently specified information includes dataset and split sizes; counts contributed by ARVO, OSS-Fuzz, and wild CVEs; overlap checks between training data and CyberGym; detailed patch-generation and CyberQA results; the exact text and implementation of the vulnerability-analysis skill; sampling details for trajectory analysis; uncertainty estimates or repeated-run variance for Table 1; training cost beyond GPU count; and safety evaluation of OpenAegis.
