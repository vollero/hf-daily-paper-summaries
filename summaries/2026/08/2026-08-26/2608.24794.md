# CAFE: Self-Improving Search Agents Need Co-Evolving Feedback

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24794) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24794)


## One-sentence takeaway

CAFE jointly updates a search agent’s feedback-seeking policy and its shared-model critic, outperforming the strongest evaluated RL search baseline by 2.1 average EM and 1.3 average F1 across seven SearchQA benchmarks while reducing hallucinations relative to outcome-only GRPO.

## Problem and motivation

Outcome-supervised search agents receive terminal rewards that neither locate intermediate mistakes nor correct an active trajectory before errors compound. Existing fine-grained rewards remain primarily evaluative, while prompted or post-hoc critiques do not learn when intervention is useful. The authors identify a coupled problem: the agent must learn when and how to use feedback, the critic must learn corrections without direct feedback labels, and the critic must remain aligned as policy updates change the agent’s visited states and failure patterns (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Shared agent–critic model:** One role-conditioned model acts as both search agent and critic; the agent may request corrective feedback during a trajectory (Section 2.1, pp. 3–4).
- **Failure-centered initialization:** SFT demonstrations preserve a base-agent failure prefix, insert teacher-generated feedback, and retain only continuations reaching the correct answer (Section 2.1; Algorithm 1, pp. 4, 20).
- **Feedback-aware online RL:** Comparative Feedback Estimate (CFE) shapes rewards using the prompt-level success gap between feedback-calling and feedback-skipping rollouts. Advantage shaping reduces credit on pre-feedback tokens and increases it on the recovery continuation (Section 2.2, pp. 4–5).
- **Co-evolving offline feedback training:** RDPO prefers feedback from successful recoveries over feedback from unsuccessful, prefix-matched rollouts mined from recent on-policy data. Online agent updates and offline critic updates alternate while sharing parameters (Section 2.3, p. 5).
- **Empirical contribution:** The paper evaluates accuracy, hallucination, component ablations, alternation schedules, agent–critic cross-play, model scale, and a deep-research benchmark (Sections 3–4; Appendix D).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that coupled, alternating feedback optimization improves these search-QA systems under their protocol. Component, one-sided-training, cross-play, scale, and schedule analyses strengthen the attribution to co-evolution, but they do not establish that CAFE will generalize to arbitrary tools, non-QA tasks, other model families, or live search environments.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-indicated scope constraints:** The main evaluation uses one in-domain dataset, six out-of-domain SearchQA datasets, fixed local retrieval, and a 30-tool-call limit; BrowseComp-Plus also uses controlled rather than live search (Section 3; Appendices A.1 and D.4).
- **Author-indicated reward confounding:** Final success depends on the prefix, feedback, and subsequent actions, so successful feedback is not necessarily useful by itself. RDPO mitigates this with prefix matching but does not eliminate the problem (Sections 2.3 and 4.1).
- **Analyst inference — comparison heterogeneity:** Baseline evidence mixes newly evaluated checkpoints, originally reported results, and a reproduction initialized from CAFE’s SFT checkpoint, limiting perfectly controlled ranking claims (Section 3 and Table 1 notes).
- **Analyst inference — external supervision remains:** Initialization requires Kimi-K2.5 to locate errors and generate repairs, and RDPO filtering uses an unspecified LLM judge; the contribution is therefore not fully self-bootstrapped from environment rewards alone (Section 2.1; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference — resource and architecture scope:** Experiments cover only Qwen2.5 3B and 7B backbones; the 7B training uses eight A100 GPUs for about two days. Efficiency relative to alternatives is not reported (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference — hallucination measurement ambiguity:** The unsupported-claim criterion is defined, but the supplied text does not identify the annotator or judge, agreement, or adjudication procedure (Section 4.4).
- **Analyst inference — theoretical condition is not empirically verified:** Theorem 1 guarantees positive task-update alignment only under a norm-bound condition; the paper does not report checking that condition during training (Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can CAFE work without a stronger external teacher?** The bootstrap stage depends on Kimi-K2.5-generated diagnoses and repairs. A next experiment could compare this setup with self-generated repairs, smaller teachers, and teacher-free rejection sampling while controlling the SFT-data budget.

2. **How robust is co-evolution beyond fixed-corpus search QA?** Most evidence comes from short-answer QA with a local retriever. A next experiment could evaluate live search, multiple tool types, and non-QA tasks under identical tool budgets.

3. **Does prefix matching sufficiently isolate feedback quality?** RDPO uses token-set Jaccard similarity of at least 0.7 plus an LLM judge, but residual trajectory confounding may remain. A next experiment could compare this pairing with exact state matching, causal intervention on feedback, or repeated continuations from the same prefix.

4. **What determines the optimal alternation frequency?** Among three tested schedules, 100×5 performs best, but the mechanism is unresolved. A next experiment could sweep more frequencies and relate performance to measured policy–critic distribution drift.
