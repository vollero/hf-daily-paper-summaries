# On-policy Distillation with Verifiable Reward

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24696) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24696)


## One-sentence takeaway

OPDVR gates sampled-token distillation updates by verified trajectory correctness, improving average mathematical-reasoning accuracy over standard OPD in both tested architecture settings without introducing a balancing hyperparameter.

## Problem and motivation

RL with verifiable rewards supplies objective but sparse trajectory-level feedback, while on-policy distillation supplies dense token-level guidance but can reinforce incorrect trajectories or penalize correct ones because its updates depend only on teacher–student probability ratios. Existing combinations reportedly require objective weights or heuristic switching. The authors seek a unified formulation that preserves dense teacher guidance while making every retained update direction consistent with verified task outcomes (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **OPDVR:** Reinterprets the sampled-token OPD log-probability ratio as an implicit token reward, then applies a correctness-conditioned ReLU gate. Correct trajectories retain only non-negative reinforcement; incorrect trajectories retain only non-positive updates (Sections 4.1–4.3).
- **Conflict-removal analysis:** Shows that the gate removes precisely the component whose projection opposes the verifier gradient; a simplified binary-token example demonstrates conditions under which OPDVR can remain better than a suboptimal teacher (Appendix A).
- **GRPD:** Replaces binary correctness with the sign of a GRPO-style group-relative advantage, enabling a group-relative policy-distillation variant (Section 4.4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Tests same- and cross-architecture distillation on six mathematical-reasoning benchmarks, including an inverse-gating ablation and training-dynamics analysis (Section 5).

The tables establish average gains over the tested OPD baselines and strong evidence that gate orientation matters. They do **not** support the paper’s textual claim that OPDVR beats sampled-token OPD on every benchmark: in the same-architecture setting, OPDVR is lower on MATH500 (84.7 versus 85.5), although its six-benchmark average is higher (Table 1). The results also do not establish statistical significance, broader-domain generalization, or superiority across model families.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied text contains no dedicated author-stated limitations section.

Clearly labeled analyst inferences:

- **Narrow domain and model family:** Evaluation is limited to mathematical reasoning with Qwen3 students and teachers, so broader language tasks and other model families remain untested (Section 5.1).
- **No uncertainty estimates:** Results use avg@16 accuracy, but no confidence intervals, run-to-run variance, or significance tests are reported in the supplied text (Tables 1–4).
- **Incomplete comparison breadth:** Main OPDVR experiments compare only sampled-token and top-64 OPD; GRPD is compared with GRPO and OPD, but not with the combination methods discussed in related work (Sections 2 and 5).
- **Claim–table inconsistency:** “Across all six benchmarks” is contradicted by MATH500 in the same-architecture comparison (Section 5.2; Table 1).
- **Resource reporting is incomplete:** The paper identifies RTX 5090 GPUs but does not state GPU count, training time, or total compute (Appendix C).
- **Verifier dependence:** The approach assumes reliable trajectory-level correctness labels; sensitivity to verifier errors is not evaluated (Sections 3.1 and 4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does OPDVR generalize beyond mathematical reasoning?** Only verifier-backed math tasks are evaluated. A next experiment could test compilable-code generation or another domain with deterministic outcome checks.

2. **How robust is gating to noisy verifiers?** The analysis assumes correctness labels determine the proper update direction, but verifier errors are not studied. A controlled experiment could flip increasing fractions of verifier labels and measure accuracy and gating behavior.

3. **Does the method remain effective across model families and larger teacher–student gaps?** Both settings use Qwen3 models. A factorial study across unrelated architectures, parameter ratios, and teacher quality levels could test this boundary.

4. **Which gated token types drive the gains?** The inverse-gate ablation validates orientation but does not independently ablate the correct-trajectory and incorrect-trajectory masks. Separate one-sided gates would isolate their contributions.
