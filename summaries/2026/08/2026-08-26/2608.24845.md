# LAION-BVD: A 10-Million-Hour Open Video Dataset for Multimodal Pre-training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24845) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24845)


## One-sentence takeaway

LAION-BVD releases a minimally filtered, web-sourced corpus of 80M videos spanning 10M hours, plus synthetically captioned video, audio, and frame subsets that support competitive contrastive multimodal pre-training but inherit caption, bias, and evaluation-scope limitations.

## Problem and motivation

Open video-language resources remain much smaller than text and image corpora because downloading, storing, segmenting, and annotating web video requires substantial infrastructure, while hosting platforms may restrict access. This limits reproducible multimodal pre-training despite video offering both temporal visual content and aligned audio (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

The authors address this gap with an openly documented collection and annotation pipeline operating at substantially larger duration than the datasets compared in Table 1. They also investigate whether video-derived frames provide image-text supervision outside the usual web-image distribution.

## Approach and contributions

- **Large video corpus:** From 4.7B candidate URLs found in CommonCrawl dumps, the pipeline retains 1.3B links from YouTube, Vimeo, and Dailymotion. Approximately 80M of 130M attempted downloads succeeded, producing 10M video hours (Section 3.1, Figure 2, p. 5).

- **Multimodal annotation pipeline:** Videos are filtered by duration, split using content-aware scene detection, and checked for visual change. Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct generates short video captions, Audio Flamingo 3 captions aligned audio, and DeepSeek-VL2-tiny captions scene-changing frames (Sections 3.1–3.2, pp. 5–6).

- **Released derived subsets:** The authors report 55M captioned scene-level clips from 2.4M sampled videos and 300M captioned scene-changing frames, alongside the larger raw-video and URL releases (Introduction, p. 2; Figure 2).

- **Cross-modal validation:** ViCLIP, CLAP, and CLIP experiments test the data for video-text, audio-text, and image-text contrastive learning across multiple data and model scales (Section 4, pp. 8–12).

The results establish that these subsets can train competitive contrastive encoders and that retrieval performance generally scales with data or model size. They do not establish performance for generative models, unified audio-visual representations, or the entire 10M-hour corpus: the main training experiments use captioned subsets derived from roughly 2.4M videos. Nor does benchmark performance alone establish comprehensive caption accuracy, safety, or representativeness.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Captions are automatically generated, intentionally short, and produced by relatively small models, limiting descriptive richness and potentially importing systematic errors or biases (Section 5, p. 12).

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers contrastive ViCLIP, CLAP, and CLIP models, not video-language generation or diffusion-based paradigms (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** Audio, video, and frames are evaluated separately; the paper does not test unified audio-visual learning or synchronization-sensitive tasks (Section 5).

- **Author-stated:** Minimal filtering of platform content may leave stereotypes and uneven coverage across languages, regions, and topics. Reliance on platform moderation substitutes for additional collection-stage safety filtering (Sections 3.1 and 5).

- **Analyst inference:** Claims about the complete 10M-hour resource rely on experiments using much smaller captioned subsets, so performance may not extrapolate to uncatalogued portions of the raw corpus.

- **Analyst inference:** The audio captioner had seen 356 of 975 AudioCaps test video IDs, although not their evaluation captions. The authors’ controlled re-evaluation argues against this explaining BVD gains, but the overlap remains a validity consideration (Appendix A.2.4, pp. 28–29).

- **Analyst inference:** Some headline InternVid comparisons mix different unique-dataset sizes, samples seen, filtering, and checkpoint-merging choices. The five-seed fixed-compute comparison is stronger evidence, but it finds only a 0.58-point aggregate gain and no clear improvement on several individual metrics (Tables 6 and 12).

- **Analyst inference:** Caption audits contain only 134 samples per modality, which is very small relative to tens of millions of captions and cannot characterize long-tail errors.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the corpus support genuinely joint audio-video-language learning?** Separate encoders do not test cross-modal synchronization. A next experiment could train a unified contrastive encoder on aligned clips, audio, and captions, then evaluate audio-visual event localization and retrieval.

2. **How does stronger captioning affect classification without sacrificing retrieval?** BVD frames have far fewer ImageNet class-name mentions than DataComp and weaker ImageNet accuracy (Appendix B, pp. 31–33). A controlled experiment could recaption matched frames with event-, object-, and class-oriented prompts and compare classification and retrieval.

3. **Do generative models benefit from the dataset at similar scale?** The authors explicitly omit video-language and diffusion evaluations. A next experiment could train matched generative models on BVD and an established video corpus under equal compute, then test captioning, question answering, or video generation.

4. **How representative and safe is the full release?** The paper reports broad platform-level statistics but no comprehensive harmful-content or regional audit. A stratified multilingual audit across platforms, dates, topics, and regions could quantify safety failures and representation gaps.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported pipeline, dataset counts, benchmark results, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the complete paper and appendices.

Material gaps remain: the supplied paper does not provide a comprehensive safety audit, licensing analysis for individual videos, full demographic or geographic statistics, failure rates by source platform, caption audits beyond 134 examples per modality, or evaluation of generative and unified audio-visual models. Release terms are mentioned but not reproduced, and the exact amount of raw video practically accessible to non-research users is not established.
