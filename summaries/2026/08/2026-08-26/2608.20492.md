# Annotations as Rollouts: Efficient and Scalable Reinforcement Learning for Video MLLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20492) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20492)


## One-sentence takeaway

OraRL turns each annotation into an oracle rollout while excluding it from the on-policy advantage baseline, improving unified video perception over matched GRPO and SFT baselines with lower training and inference costs.

## Problem and motivation

RL post-training for video MLLMs is sample-inefficient because sampled rollout groups often lack precise positive examples for intervals, boxes, masks, or trajectories; CoT makes rollouts longer without reliably fixing this scarcity. Although annotations provide correct targets, conventional methods use them only to score sampled outputs.

Naively adding an annotation to the normalized rollout group introduces “advantage inversion”: the high-reward oracle raises the baseline, so some better-than-average policy rollouts receive negative advantages and are suppressed (Sections 1 and 3.2; Table 14). The authors seek scalable supervision that preserves on-policy exploration, works across heterogeneous tasks, and avoids CoT overhead.

## Approach and contributions

- **Annotation-as-rollout:** Serialize every task annotation into the model’s answer format and append it as an oracle rollout without replacing any policy sample (Section 3.2, Eq. 5).
- **Decoupled advantage estimation:** Compute policy-relative advantages using only on-policy rewards; separately encode the oracle-policy gap through an asymmetric directional gain and a bounded, detached oracle advantage (Section 3.2, Eqs. 7–10).
- **Efficient rollout selection:** Retain the oracle plus strong positive and negative policy rollouts, then correct the selected advantages’ mean and scale (Section 3.2, Eqs. 11–13).
- **Unified models and assets:** Train Video-ORA at 0.8B–9B across seven task families and provide project, data, model, and source-code links in the supplied front matter.
- **Empirical diagnosis:** Quantify advantage inversion and compare OraRL against SFT, GRPO variants, naive oracle injection, reward shaping, and off-policy guidance under matched protocols (Tables 12–16).

The matched controlled experiments support the authors’ claim that oracle integration must be separated from the on-policy baseline and that the resulting method improves the tested backbones and tasks. They do not establish robustness to noisy supervision, generalization beyond two backbone families, or superiority for every spatial-reasoning subtype. Benchmark-leading comparisons also do not isolate OraRL alone where models differ in architecture, data, or evaluation conditions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** OraRL assumes each annotation can be serialized as a valid oracle response and evaluated with a scalar reward; ambiguous, partial, noisy, or learned oracles were not tested (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers seven task families but only two backbone families, and complex spatial reasoning still trails some proprietary models (Sections 4.6 and 5).
- **Author-stated:** ReVSI exposes weak robustness: Video-ORA-9B drops 14.9 points relative to VSI-Bench, and backward relative-direction accuracy is only 8.3% at 128 frames (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported video-to-spatial transfer is confounded by compute: adding 9,000 video prompts also doubles RL updates. The authors explicitly acknowledge the expanded training budget (Section 4.5; Table 9).
- **Analyst inference:** Some cross-model “best reported” comparisons have heterogeneous frame settings or externally reported values; Appendix F explicitly flags this for ReVSI, limiting causal attribution.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text gives extensive component removals, but no evaluation under corrupted annotation levels or alternative oracle-reward calibration, leaving sensitivity to supervision quality unresolved.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is OraRL to noisy or ambiguous annotations?** This regime is explicitly unevaluated. A next experiment could inject controlled label, boundary, box, and answer noise and measure inversion rates and downstream scores by noise level.
2. **Can oracle guidance work with partial or learned supervision?** The method currently requires a serializable response and scalar reward. Compare complete annotations with partial labels, pseudo-labels, and learned reward models under matched compute.
3. **Does video supervision intrinsically improve spatial intelligence?** Table 9 also increases the number of updates. A compute-matched study could compare video prompts against duplicated spatial prompts and equal-update mixed-task curricula.
4. **Can directional and backward spatial reasoning be balanced?** ReVSI shows strong forward but very weak backward performance. Add backward-oriented training targets and report forward/backward accuracy separately without changing the total data budget.
