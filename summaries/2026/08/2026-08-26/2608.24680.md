# Game2World Engine: Unlocking In-the-Wild Gameplay Videos for World Model Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24680) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24680)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors introduce a taxonomy, synthetic-data engine, benchmark, and mask-free video editor for removing gameplay interfaces, reporting improved proxy world-model training on clean footage and substantially stronger UI-removal/background-preservation trade-offs than evaluated baselines.

## Problem and motivation

Internet gameplay videos could provide diverse, scalable observations for world-model training, but they combine the game world with HUDs, menus, notifications, watermarks, and streaming overlays whose screen-space dynamics are unrelated to the simulated environment. The authors argue that prior gameplay world models generally treat these composite frames as direct world observations, while conventional video inpainting targets physical objects rather than structured, temporally varying interfaces (Introduction; Sections 2.1–2.2).

A controlled study supports the concern: otherwise identical text-to-video models trained on clean versus synthetically UI-overlaid versions of 5,442 clips favored the clean data on overall VideoReward, motion quality, aesthetics, and motion consistency (Section 3; Tables 1–2). This is proxy evidence, however, because the experiment does not train an action-conditioned world model.

## Approach and contributions

1. **GameUI-Taxonomy:** A 21-label taxonomy organized by interface function and rendering behavior, intended to standardize UI annotation, grounding, synthesis, and removal (Section 4.1; Figure 2; Appendix A, Table 7).

2. **G2WEngine:** A pipeline that extracts verified transparent UI assets from real gameplay, curates clean five-second clips, and synthesizes category-aware, temporally animated overlays. Each synthetic example includes aligned clean and overlaid videos, masks, boxes, labels, and rendering metadata (Sections 4.2–4.4; Appendix F–G).

3. **Game2World dataset and evaluation protocol:** Game2World-S provides 96K synthetic pairs; Game2World-W provides 1,079 authentic five-second clips from 303 games without clean targets. Evaluation uses element-level removal, artifact, trajectory, and background-preservation judgments (Section 5; Sections 7.1; Appendix J).

4. **GameCleaner:** A mask-free editor based on Kiwi-Edit, coupling a frozen multimodal encoder with a video diffusion transformer. Semantic conditioning comes from cross-attention over learned queries, source-video latents preserve structure, and LoRA post-training uses paired overlaid/clean videos with a flow-matching objective (Sections 6–7.1; Figure 5; Equations 1–3).

5. **Data-utility pilot:** The paper isolates the effect of synthetic UI contamination on a text-to-video training proxy using matched clean and overlaid clips (Section 3).

The evidence establishes strong performance on the paper’s synthetic and curated wild UI-removal benchmarks, plus a controlled benefit for a text-to-video training proxy. It does not establish improved action-conditioned interaction, planning, or control, nor does it show that arbitrary cleaned Internet footage can directly train a complete world model. Wild evaluation also depends on an MLLM judge rather than unavailable clean ground truth.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Game2World does not cover the full diversity of games, interfaces, or recording conditions. Large opaque interfaces, rapidly changing overlays, and UI visually entangled with the scene remain difficult (Section 8).

- **Author-stated:** The downstream study uses text-conditioned video generation as a proxy; action-conditioned world models additionally require recovering heterogeneous player actions, for which the paper says sufficiently general methods are unavailable (Section 8).

- **Author-stated:** The observed data-scaling trend is not a formal scaling law, and the current study is limited to roughly 100K pairs (Section 8).

- **Author-stated:** GameCleaner uses a full generation trajectory; few-step distillation and GPU-level optimization remain future work (Section 8).

- **Author-stated validity risk:** Judge scores change with evaluation resolution, so resolution must be standardized. The protocol uses one stochastic judgment per video (Table 6; Appendix J.6).

- **Analyst inference:** Wild results lack pixel-level clean references and therefore inherit errors and residual variability from the MLLM judge. Human validation is encouraging but not perfect, particularly for preservation agreement at 78.00% (Section 7.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The strongest GameCleaner result uses an optional clean reference image, whose origin and availability in a real Internet-cleaning pipeline are not clearly specified. The reference-free results are therefore the more directly applicable comparison for fully automatic processing.

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility details conflict: Section 7.1 reports 8 H100 GPUs and 3,000 steps, whereas Appendix H reports 8 H200 GPUs and 1,400 steps. The supplied text does not resolve whether these describe different runs.

- **Analyst inference:** Baseline inference settings beyond resolution are not reported in the main comparison, limiting assessment of compute and tuning fairness. Release availability is promised but not demonstrated in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Do cleaned videos improve an action-conditioned world model?** The pilot evaluates only a text-to-video proxy. A next experiment could recover or collect actions for matched gameplay clips, train otherwise identical action-conditioned models on clean and overlaid observations, and compare rollout fidelity and controllability.

2. **How well does removal generalize beyond the represented games and recording conditions?** The authors acknowledge incomplete coverage. A held-out benchmark stratified by unseen game, genre, UI style, opacity, overlay size, and capture artifact could measure out-of-distribution performance.

3. **Can reference-free removal close the wild-data gap?** Wild AAR falls from 80.05 with a reference to 51.60 without one for the reference-post-trained variant. Experiments could vary reference-drop curricula or add temporal/multi-clip context while preserving a strictly reference-free test protocol.

4. **Can evaluation become less judge-dependent?** Resolution sensitivity and imperfect human agreement leave uncertainty. A plausible next study would compare multiple frozen judges, repeated judgments, and larger blinded human evaluations under a preregistered aggregation protocol.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated method, dataset composition, and tabulated results; **medium** for reproducibility and practical deployment conclusions.

Material missing or ambiguous information includes the train/validation/test split construction, explicit game-level leakage controls, the number of valid games used for UI-statistics aggregation, baseline inference compute and tuning, the source and realistic availability of inference reference images, downstream action-conditioned results, resolved training hardware/step counts, and confirmation that the promised code, dataset, and model were actually released.
