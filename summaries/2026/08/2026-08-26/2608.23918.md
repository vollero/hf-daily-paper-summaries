# MARS: Multi-Specialist LLM Relay System for Competitive Programming

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-26.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23918) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23918)


## One-sentence takeaway

MARS routes competitive-programming problems through a small relay of retrieval-grounded algorithm specialists and uses public-test execution at each turn, achieving a \(0.624\pm0.006\) pass rate on 165 CodeContests tasks—14.4 percentage points above direct prompting, though below heavier-search systems.

## Problem and motivation

Competitive-programming code generation requires both implementation skill and topic-specific algorithmic knowledge, often across several areas. The authors argue that existing multi-agent pipelines divide work into generic planner, coder, and debugger roles, leaving algorithm selection to the backbone model rather than explicitly supplying relevant expertise. MARS addresses this gap with dynamically selected, algorithm-topic specialists grounded in an algorithm-theory corpus and coordinated through execution-aware handoffs (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Topic-specialist relay:** Eleven agents represent algorithmic topics such as dynamic programming, graphs, strings, and geometry. Self-assessment and retrieval signals select at most three specialists and elect a starter (§3; Figure 1).
- **Theory-grounded specialization:** Each specialist retrieves from its topic-filtered portion of the cp-algorithms corpus rather than retrieving code or API documentation (§2–§3).
- **Execution-aware refinement:** Every specialist drafts code, observes its performance on public examples, and then keeps, repairs, or hands off the shared program. Repairs are accepted only if they compile and do not reduce the number of passing public tests relative to the same-turn draft (§3; Figures 6–7, 9).
- **Bounded, prompt-only coordination:** The relay permits at most three unique specialists and eight steps, with explicit termination and no-progress rules; a final fixer is restricted to infrastructure-level issues (§3).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The paper compares MARS with direct prompting, retrieval, ensemble, relay, CodeSIM, and PairCoder variants across three backbones and C++17/Python settings (§4).

These results support the authors’ claim that the complete MARS protocol improves over direct prompting, single-specialist RAG, a parallel specialist ensemble, and a simpler relay under the reported evaluation. They also show that MARS does not achieve the best absolute accuracy: CodeSIM and PairCoder remain ahead while using heavier search or greater wall-clock cost. The evidence does not isolate the individual effects of topic specialization, public-test feedback, subtask tracking, and final infrastructure handling.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is limited to 165 CodeContests tasks, three backbones, two languages, one retrieval corpus, and Codeforces tags. Transfer beyond C++17 and Python is untested.
- **Author-stated:** The repair gate protects only against same-turn public-test regressions. It cannot detect hidden-test failures or prevent a later specialist from replacing better code.
- **Author-stated:** Generated programs require sandboxed execution, adding an infrastructure and safety requirement.
- **Author-stated:** CodeSIM is the only stage-aligned comparison, while PairCoder is evaluated only in Python; several published systems could not be ported faithfully.
- **Analyst inference:** The Base-relay comparison changes public-test self-check, subtask tracking, and infrastructure handling together, so its 7.2-point gap cannot attribute gains to any single component (§4.1, §4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The generalist comparison simultaneously removes specialization and RAG, preventing a clean estimate of specialization’s contribution (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Public examples influence generation and repair, so improvements may depend on their availability and diagnostic quality; no evaluation without usable public tests is reported.
- **Analyst inference:** Model- and method-specific decoding settings differ in the transfer study, limiting strict causal comparison across backbones and methods (§3, §4.2).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does topic specialization contribute independently of retrieval?** The generalist ablation also disables RAG. A factorial experiment crossing specialist/generalist roles with RAG/no-RAG would separate these effects.
2. **Which execution-aware component produces the gain over Base relay?** Several protocol changes are bundled. Ablating self-check, subtask tracking, no-progress routing, and infrastructure handling one at a time would identify their marginal effects.
3. **Can the gate protect progress across specialists and hidden cases?** It compares only a repair with its same-turn draft on public examples. A next experiment could retain prior candidates and evaluate cross-specialist changes using generated or held-out tests.
4. **Does topic routing generalize beyond the current taxonomy and corpus?** The study uses Codeforces tags and cp-algorithms. Testing alternative taxonomies, corpora, contest sources, and additional programming languages would assess robustness.

## Confidence and missing information
