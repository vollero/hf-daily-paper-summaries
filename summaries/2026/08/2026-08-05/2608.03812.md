# OmniPack: Unified Token Compression for Efficient Omni-modal Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03812) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03812)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmniPack is a training-free, two-stage audio-visual token-compression method that preserves 95.6% of Qwen2.5-Omni-7B’s average benchmark performance at 10.0% of its estimated FLOPs by combining structure-aware pre-LLM compression with query-conditioned inner-LLM refinement.

## Problem and motivation

Omni-LLMs produce long visual and audio token sequences, making inference computationally and memory intensive. The authors argue that existing methods deteriorate under aggressive compression because pre-LLM methods may miss globally distributed or transient evidence, while inner-LLM methods insufficiently exploit query-conditioned audio-visual collaboration. OmniPack addresses this gap by assigning structural compression and semantic refinement to different stages of inference (Introduction; Sections 3.2–3.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Structure-aware pre-LLM compression:** Independently selects audio and video tokens using encoder-attention importance, modality-specific temporal or spatial variation, and DPC-KNN coverage over feature and positional distances (Section 3.3).
- **Information-preserving merging:** Transfers unselected tokens into retained representatives using feature similarity, positional proximity, and importance-weighted aggregation rather than simply discarding them (Equations 9–10).
- **Query-conditioned inner-LLM compression:** After several Transformer blocks, scores tokens using textual relevance, the other modality’s prototype, and within-modality representativeness; relevance-diversity selection and merging then reduce the sequence further (Section 3.4, Equations 11–12).
- **Training-free integration:** The framework modifies inference without additional alignment or compression training and is evaluated across three Omni-LLM backbones and five benchmarks (Sections 3–4).

The evidence supports a favorable measured trade-off across several token budgets, three backbones, and five benchmarks. It also supports complementary contributions from the pre-LLM components, although the isolated benefit of audio-visual collaboration is modest in Table 5.

It does not establish end-to-end savings for the entire system: the FLOP calculation excludes modality encoders, projectors, text tokens, token-selection operations, and the language-model head (Appendix D). Nor do scores above 100% relative performance prove general improvement over the uncompressed model; they are benchmark averages under compression and may reflect redundancy removal or evaluation variation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope constraints:** FLOP estimates exclude several system components, including encoders and compression-selection operations (Appendix D). SEATS is not evaluated on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B because its official schedule targets 28-layer architectures (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete latency coverage:** Wall-clock prefill latency is reported only for Qwen2.5-Omni-7B on an NVIDIA H20, so deployment gains across other hardware and backbones remain uncertain (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference — limited task scope:** Evaluation covers audio-visual understanding and multiple-choice QA, not multimodal generation, streaming interaction, or text-heavy workloads (Section 4.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference — comparison qualification:** OmniSIFT is evaluated without its alignment training, and some baselines use different modality allocations or cannot operate at every budget. Although these choices are documented, they complicate direct claims of universal superiority (Section 4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference — ablation breadth:** Component ablations use only Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and selected benchmarks; cross-backbone ablations of individual mechanisms are not supplied (Section 4.4).
- **Analyst inference — uncertainty reporting:** The supplied results provide point estimates but no repeated-run variation, confidence intervals, or significance tests.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the efficiency gains survive full end-to-end accounting?** Encoder, selection, projector, text-token, and output-head costs are excluded. A next experiment should profile total latency, peak memory, energy, and throughput on several hardware platforms.

2. **How robust is each compression component across architectures?** Ablations are confined to Qwen2.5-Omni-7B. Repeating the component and compression-layer ablations on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B and MiniCPM-o-2.6 would test whether the design choices generalize.

3. **Does compression preserve open-ended generation quality?** The reported benchmarks primarily use multiple-choice QA. A plausible experiment would compare uncompressed and compressed models on captioning, conversational, and free-form temporal-reasoning tasks with task-specific and human evaluation.

4. **What causes compressed MiniCPM-o-2.6 to exceed its uncompressed average?** The paper reports relative performance above 100% but does not establish the mechanism. Repeated evaluations, per-example error analysis, and controlled tests of redundancy removal could distinguish a systematic benefit from evaluation variance.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported benchmark results because the supplied text includes the main paper, appendices, tables, and implementation details.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty and run-to-run variance; full end-to-end compute, memory, and energy measurements; latency on hardware other than NVIDIA H20; cross-backbone component ablations; results on open-ended generation or streaming; and explicit licensing or release details beyond the repository link.
