# PosterMELD: Multi-Agent Paper-to-Poster Generation for Controllable Design Diversity with Editable Print-Ready Outputs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02218) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02218)


## One-sentence takeaway

PosterMELD combines template-first capacity planning, specialized agents, deterministic validation, and bounded repair to generate editable scientific posters with an 81.3% request-level print-ready rate at a reported mean cost of $0.38 per request.

## Problem and motivation

Scientific poster generation must compress a long, multimodal paper into a readable canvas while preserving factual content, visual assets, layout constraints, and post-generation editability. The authors argue that prior evaluations often score only completed artifacts, thereby obscuring execution and validity failures such as overlap, overflow, missing assets, or illegible text. They also identify a practical gap between non-editable direct image generation and expensive coding-agent workflows: neither jointly offers request-level validity, native element editability, explicit design controls, and measured cost (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions

- **Template-first generation:** Poster structure is selected before final writing. A library of 24 validated layout topologies exposes slot-level character, bullet, visual-footprint, compatibility, prominence, and reading-order constraints (Method, “Template Library Construction”).

- **Five-agent pipeline:** Content, Template, Layout, and Visual agents construct a structured poster state; a Review Agent renders drafts, applies deterministic and VLM-based checks, and routes localized failures to bounded rewrite, reflow, resize, or rerender actions (Method, “Agent-Skill Generation Pipeline”; Figure 2).

- **Editable, controlled artifacts:** Passing requests export a native PPTX, matching PNG, and provenance manifest. Templates, styles, densities, generated assets, and seeds can be varied across independent requests; the discrete controls provide 864 combinations before seed variation (Method).

- **Validity-centered evaluation:** The authors introduce request-level Print-Ready Rate (PRR) and evaluate six generated methods plus human references across 621 papers, alongside conditional aesthetics, content diagnostics, editability, and request-level cost (Benchmark and Evaluation; Table 1).

The results support the authors’ claim that their system substantially improves request-level validity over the evaluated open-source poster pipelines while retaining native editability and low reported cost. They do not establish superior PRR to GPT-Image-2 or Codex+Skill, nor do they quantify design diversity: the diversity evidence is qualitative. CHE is conditional on successful, print-ready outputs and therefore cannot be interpreted independently of PRR. Moreover, benchmark-wide PRR and CHE rely on a calibrated VLM rather than exhaustive human assessment.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- External foundation models and parsers create dependency and error-propagation risks, particularly for papers with unusual layouts.
- Validation gates certify geometry and legibility, not complete scientific correctness; authors must perform final factual verification.
- The frozen evaluator may share biases with the generation models despite human calibration.
- The template library represents common academic structures rather than all design cultures, and portrait layouts are less mature than landscape layouts.
- Generated teaser or background assets may hallucinate despite provenance marking and restrictions against measured values.
- CHE excludes non-print-ready outputs and must be interpreted jointly with PRR.
- Design diversity is demonstrated qualitatively, not with a scalar diversity measure (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The supplied text reports no component ablation isolating the effects of template-first planning, deterministic gates, VLM review, individual agents, or bounded repair; their separate causal contributions therefore remain unclear.
- Comparability is imperfect because Codex+Skill uses GPT-5.4, the other agentic systems use GPT-4o, and GPT-Image-2 receives a parsed representation rather than the native PDF (“Baselines and Fair Protocol”).
- The selected judge’s agreement with human labels is substantial but imperfect—84.0% PRR accuracy and CHE correlation of 0.592—so close benchmark differences may be sensitive to evaluator error (Table 3).
- The benchmark covers many publication sources and domains, but all inputs remain scientific papers with paired academic-poster references; validity outside this artifact class is not established.

## Open research questions

1. **Which pipeline components drive the PRR improvement?** The supplied experiments contain no component ablation. A next experiment could remove slot contracts, deterministic gates, VLM review, and bounded repair one at a time while measuring completion, PRR, CHE, repair count, and cost.

2. **Can controllable diversity be quantified without rewarding defects or content omission?** The paper shows same-paper variants qualitatively but reports no diversity metric. A controlled study could compare layout, palette, content-allocation, and perceptual variation while holding keypoint coverage, PRR, and factual accuracy fixed.

3. **How reliable is scientific-content verification?** Current gates do not certify full scientific correctness. A next experiment could add expert annotation of claims, numerical values, figure-caption consistency, and omitted qualifications, then compare deterministic, VLM, and human verification.

4. **How well does the approach generalize beyond its template distribution?** The authors note incomplete design-culture coverage and weaker portrait templates. Evaluation on deliberately out-of-distribution poster styles, languages, orientations, and unusual paper layouts could measure failure rates and adaptation requirements.

## Confidence and missing information
