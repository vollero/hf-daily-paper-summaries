# When Agents Learn to Be You: Benchmarking Privacy Leakage, Impersonation Risk, and Defenses in Persona Skills

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03700) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03700)


## One-sentence takeaway

AntiSkillBench shows that distilling synthetic personal dialogue histories into reusable agent skills preserves substantial personal and behavioral signals, while tested sanitization, obfuscation, and provenance defenses remain incomplete and dependent on the model and distillation protocol.

## Problem and motivation

Persona skills compress interaction histories into portable, executable artifacts for personalized agents. The authors argue that this creates three distinctive hazards: fragmented signals become concentrated in an inspectable artifact, portability amplifies opportunities for misuse, and cross-trace inference can bypass record-level anonymization or retrieval filtering (§1).

Existing privacy and impersonation benchmarks primarily evaluate direct histories, retrieved memories, prompting, or fine-tuning—not the full trace-to-skill-to-agent pipeline. The paper therefore targets both static privacy leakage from skill artifacts and downstream disclosure or behavioral impersonation by skill-equipped agents (§§1, 2.2, 4.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **End-to-end benchmark:** AntiSkillBench contains 50 synthetic profiles, 2,500 three-round dialogue traces, and 7,500 persona-grounded user turns spanning general assistance, tool design, and mathematical calculation (§4.1).
- **Two-level risk formulation:** It evaluates skill-level privacy leakage and agent-level attribute disclosure or behavioral impersonation (§4.2).
- **Three evaluation metrics:** Skill Coverage measures retained profile properties; Field QA Accuracy tests direct recovery of attributes; VocabGain measures movement toward persona-specific language relative to no-persona and oracle-profile conditions (§4.3).
- **Cross-protocol evaluation:** The benchmark compares Direct Distill, a newly developed three-stage procedure, and a COLLEAGUE.SKILL-derived persona pipeline on three agent backbones (§5.1).
- **Defense suite:** Privacy Sanitization and Adversarial Obfuscation actively alter traces, while online and post-hoc Semantic-level Backdoor Injection provide provenance signals (§4.4).
- **Empirical characterization:** The authors report persistent leakage across backbones and protocols, stronger retention of communication and personality signals than demographics, and defense effectiveness that varies by protocol and model (§§5.2–5.3, Appendix E.2).

These results establish leakage and impersonation signals within the paper’s controlled, synthetic benchmark and automatic-evaluation design. They do not establish how frequently real users would be harmed, whether outputs would successfully deceive human recipients, or how the defenses perform against adaptive adversaries and other skill formats.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Personal traces are instantiated as simulated user–assistant dialogues, although the conceptual pipeline allows documents, messages, interviews, and multimedia transcripts (§3; §4.1).
- **Synthetic construction:** Profiles and dialogues are LLM-generated and intentionally person-revealing. This provides control but may overstate leakage relative to less persona-saturated real histories, or miss real-world irregularities (analyst inference from §4.1 and Appendix A.2).
- **Automatic evaluation:** GPT 5.4 judges Skill Coverage and Field QA across all backbones. The supplied text reports auditable rationales but no human-validation agreement, creating possible judge-model bias (§5.1; Appendix D.1).
- **Single stochastic runs:** Seeds were not fixed, and each configuration was run once per character. Aggregating 50 profiles reduces character-level sensitivity but does not quantify run-to-run variance (Appendix F).
- **Defense capability constraint:** Defenders may modify only traces, not the distiller, deployed skill, agent, or interface; conclusions therefore do not cover broader system-level defenses (§4.4).
- **Narrow behavioral proxy:** VocabGain measures embedding-matched lexical/discourse markers, not human judgments of identity confusion, intent attribution, or deceptive success (§4.3; Appendix D.3).
- **Selective defense coverage:** The main defense table uses GPT 5.4; Claude receives supplementary evaluation, but behavioral SBD ASR is absent there, and the supplied text gives no corresponding full defense table for Gemini (Tables 2 and 7).
- **Limited ablation scale:** Dialogue quantity, sanitization strength, and backdoor coverage ablations use only five profiles with GPT 5.4 three-stage Distill, limiting generalization (Fig. 5; Appendix E.1).
- **Availability:** Data and code are promised only upon formal publication, so immediate independent reproducibility is not established (Appendix G).

## Open research questions

1. **Do these risks persist in naturally occurring histories?** The benchmark uses synthetic, deliberately persona-grounded English dialogues. A next experiment could distill consented real interaction histories and compare leakage against synthetic histories matched for length and task mix.

2. **Can defenses jointly protect privacy, behavioral authenticity, and utility?** PS suppresses communication cues more than personality/background information, while SBD detects reuse without preventing it. A next experiment could optimize a combined artifact- and behavior-level defense and measure task utility alongside all leakage and ASR metrics.

3. **How well do the metrics predict human-perceived impersonation?** VocabGain captures marker similarity but not whether people attribute an output to the target. A blinded human study could compare VocabGain with identification, misattribution, and perceived-authenticity rates.

4. **Can provenance survive adaptive or heterogeneous distillation?** SBD performance varies sharply by protocol and backbone, and adaptive attackers are not evaluated. A next experiment could let distillers paraphrase, prune, merge, or adversarially remove suspected watermarks before measuring static and behavioral ASR.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported table values; **medium** for broader claims about structural real-world risk because the evaluation is synthetic and largely model-judged.

Material missing information includes human validation of the automatic judges and impersonation metric, uncertainty estimates or repeated-run variance, complete Gemini defense results, behavioral SBD results for Claude, utility effects of defenses, real-user evaluation, adaptive-adversary tests, and currently available code/data rather than a future release plan.
