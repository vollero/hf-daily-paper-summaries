# Video-DeepResearch: Towards the Next-Generation Multimodal Deepresearch Agent

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03979) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03979)


## One-sentence takeaway

Video-DeepResearch trains video agents to ground entities across frames before searching the web, achieving a reported 64.0% average accuracy while introducing a benchmark intended to require both visual and external-knowledge retrieval.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses multi-hop question answering over continuous video plus open-web evidence. The authors argue that image-centric research agents and conventional video VQA do not adequately support long-horizon spatiotemporal grounding, external knowledge retrieval, or evaluation of tool-mediated reasoning (§1).

A preliminary study identifies two motivating failures: agents rarely use visual tools, and existing questions may be answerable from memorized knowledge without inspecting the video. For example, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B averaged 0.10 visual versus 1.27 text-tool calls, while GPT-5 reportedly scored 57 with 0.00 visual and 0.12 text calls per task (Table 1; §2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Vision-first agent workflow:** The agent selects keyframes, crops and visually searches salient entities, and only then gains access to textual `Search` and `Visit` tools. This stage-wise unlocking is designed to prevent premature text-only answering (§2; §3.2; Figure 3; Table 6).

- **Data-generation pipeline:** Videos undergo rule- and model-based filtering, CLIP-assisted keyframe selection, entity localization, visual-search verification, and single- or multi-entity question generation. Questions answered in any of four tool-free rollouts are removed. The resulting resources contain 30K VQA pairs and 7K successful trajectories (§3.1–§3.2; Figure 3).

- **Two-stage training:** Both model variants receive SFT on video-research trajectories plus text-only research examples, followed by GRPO on 2K moderate-difficulty instances using a binary answer-correctness reward (§3.3).

- **VideoDR-Bench:** The authors construct a human–AI-annotated, multi-domain benchmark whose questions are intended to require visual grounding and external knowledge reasoning (§3.4; Figure 2). The supplied text conflicts on whether it contains 100 or 200 instances.

These results support the authors’ claim that the complete training pipeline improves judged answer accuracy and substantially changes tool-use behavior under their evaluation setup. The staged ablation also associates each added training phase with incremental gains.

They do not establish that increased tool use itself causes higher accuracy, that the agents’ retrieved evidence is faithful, or that the method generalizes beyond the two reported benchmarks. “State of the art” is limited to the evaluated systems and judge-based protocol. The benchmark-size contradiction also makes the precise scope of the reported evaluation uncertain.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors state that data synthesis and training require substantial GPU resources and dynamic web-search operations. Appendix B reports SFT on 32 80-GB GPUs with an 80,000-token context. They also acknowledge that meticulous human annotation restricts benchmark scalability (Limitation; Appendix B).

Analyst inferences supported by the text:

- **Benchmark-size inconsistency:** The abstract, introduction, and conclusion call VideoDR-Bench a 200-instance benchmark, whereas §4.1 says it comprises 100 VQA pairs. The evaluated sample size therefore cannot be resolved from the supplied text.
- **Judge dependence:** Correctness and RL rewards rely on Qwen-family model judges, but no human agreement, judge calibration, or robustness comparison is reported (§3.3; §4.1).
- **Incomplete table retrieval:** Captions and narrative results are present, but the numerical bodies of Tables 1–5 are absent. Several comparisons therefore cannot be independently reconstructed.
- **Causal ambiguity:** Tool-call frequency correlates with performance, but the paper reports no intervention holding model and training fixed while varying tool-use requirements (§4.3).
- **Dynamic-web validity:** Results depend on external search and visited pages, yet the supplied text does not specify a frozen web snapshot or evidence-retrieval reproducibility procedure (Table 6).
- **Potential benchmark-construction coupling:** Similar filtering, tools, keyframe preprocessing, and model families appear in data generation, training, judging, and evaluation. The text does not report evaluations with independently constructed tools or judges (§3; §4.1; Appendix C).
- **Domain brittleness:** The 35B model’s 41.7% News score trails the 30B model’s 58.3%, which the authors themselves describe as a possible transfer challenge (§4.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does forced vision-first ordering improve evidence fidelity, or merely increase tool calls?** The reported evidence is correlational. A next experiment could randomize tool availability and ordering while holding the model, prompts, and tool budget fixed, then score both answers and citation-grounded evidence.

2. **How robust are results to judge choice?** Training rewards and final accuracy rely on model judges without reported human calibration. A blinded human evaluation plus multiple independent judge models could measure agreement and ranking stability.

3. **Can the method handle temporally changing web knowledge?** The weak and non-monotonic News results leave this unresolved. A time-stamped benchmark with frozen search snapshots and questions divided by evidence recency could test temporal robustness.

4. **How much computation can be removed without losing performance?** The pipeline uses large teacher models, web operations, long contexts, and 32 high-memory GPUs for SFT. Experiments varying keyframe caps, teacher size, context length, and trajectory volume could map accuracy against training and inference cost.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text describes the method and headline results in detail, but the bodies of the main numerical tables are missing. Material unavailable or inconsistent information includes the definitive VideoDR-Bench size, complete per-model and per-category scores, uncertainty estimates or repeated-run variance, benchmark video-length statistics, exact train/test overlap controls, judge accuracy and human agreement, GRPO hyperparameters beyond the stated sampling and reward design, inference tool budgets, web snapshot details, and confirmed release status or licensing for the models and datasets.
