# When Attention Goes Blind: Numerical Failure in ALiBi Positional Encodings

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03994) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03994)


## One-sentence takeaway

ALiBi’s linearly growing positional bias inevitably causes distant attention weights to underflow to zero, impairing long-range retrieval even though standard language-model benchmarks reveal little degradation; log-scaled distances best improve passkey retrieval but not needle-in-a-haystack retrieval.

## Problem and motivation

ALiBi is intended to support efficient length extrapolation by adding a distance-proportional bias to attention logits. The authors identify a previously unreported numerical failure: at sufficiently large distances, this negative bias crosses the floating-point underflow threshold, forcing attention weights to exactly zero and making individual heads blind to distant tokens.

This matters because it contradicts ALiBi’s formal unbounded-context behavior, wastes computation on weights later discarded, and may prevent gradients from teaching steep-slope heads to use distant information. Prior work had observed sliding-window-like behavior, but the authors state that it had not traced this behavior to floating-point underflow or established its downstream impact (Sections 1, 3.2–3.3, and 5.1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Analytical characterization:** The paper derives a per-head “blindness distance” from the attention logit, ALiBi slope, and floating-point underflow threshold. It distinguishes partial blindness, full blindness, and the resulting redistribution of attention weight (Sections 3.2–3.3).

2. **Empirical confirmation in pretrained models:** Perplexity probes instrument BLOOM, Falcon-RW, and MPT to measure actual underflow fractions at contexts up to 8,192 tokens (Section 4.1).

3. **Controlled training study:** The authors train 148M-parameter causal decoders on 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens, varying slope schedules to change when blindness should begin. Configurations are evaluated over three seeds on perplexity, passkey retrieval, needle-in-a-haystack retrieval, and grouped zero-shot decoder benchmarks (Section 4.2; Table 3).

4. **Mitigation study:** Four training-time interventions—bias clamping, blindness-distance-aware slopes, logarithmic distance scaling, and attention-logit soft capping—are tested separately and in combinations (Sections 3.4 and 4.3).

5. **Practical guidance:** The authors recommend clamping or an explicit hard window when sliding-window behavior is intended, while identifying logarithmic scaling as promising for passkey extrapolation but not uniformly superior (Section 5.3).

The evidence establishes that ALiBi underflow is mathematically inevitable under the analyzed finite-precision computation and empirically occurs in inspected pretrained models. Controlled training also shows that slope selection and mitigations materially affect associative retrieval.

It does **not** establish that underflow alone causes the observed pretrained-model perplexity spikes, that eliminating underflow universally improves long-context behavior, or that results transfer to larger models. In particular, the opposing passkey and NIHS outcomes show that “retrieval improvement” is task-dependent.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Training experiments use only 148M-parameter decoders, so behavioral conclusions may not transfer to larger models (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Architecture and training corpus are held fixed; interactions with other architectures and corpora are untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Promising configurations did not receive a systematic hyperparameter sweep because of compute cost (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Some clamping-related configurations have high variance because one of three seeds performs substantially worse (Section 4.3.2).
- **Author-stated:** Log scaling and soft capping appear to learn more slowly, so equal-duration training may disadvantage them (Section 5.3).
- **Author-stated:** Passkey repeats its target twice whereas NIHS presents it once, complicating direct comparison between probes (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The mitigation evidence does not isolate forward-pass zeroing from backward-pass gradient loss; the paper explicitly leaves that proposed mechanism for future work (Section 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is incomplete at retrieval time because code is promised only upon publication, although implementation, environment, and broad training details are documented (Abstract; Appendices C and E).

## Open research questions

1. **Does underflow primarily damage learning through lost gradients rather than inference through zero-valued weights?**  
   The paper proposes this mechanism but does not test it. A next experiment could train matched models with identical forward attention values while selectively preserving or blocking gradients beyond the blindness distance.

2. **Do the mitigation rankings hold at larger model scales?**  
   The controlled study is limited to 148M parameters. Training otherwise matched models at several sizes could test whether passkey gains and NIHS losses persist or change with scale.

3. **Why does log scaling improve passkey retrieval but degrade NIHS?**  
   The paper demonstrates the divergence without identifying its cause. Attention-head attribution and controlled probes varying target repetitions, distractor density, and insertion position could separate positional attenuation from content selection.

4. **Can an adaptive bias retain ALiBi’s strong NIHS behavior while avoiding numerical blindness and wasted computation?**  
   No tested strategy dominates both retrieval tasks. A plausible experiment would learn or schedule per-head clamping/log-scaling parameters and compare them with explicit sparse sliding windows under matched compute.
