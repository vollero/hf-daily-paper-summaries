# Multi-Task Multi-Frame Visual Piano Transcription

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03419) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03419)


## One-sentence takeaway

V2N jointly models one second of video to recover piano-note onset, physical key release, key-hold state, and velocity, sharply improving offset-aware transcription while exposing severe failure under unseen keyboard geometry.

## Problem and motivation

Audio transcription cannot reliably recover physical key-release times when the sustain pedal is active because the sound continues after release. Video directly observes key mechanics, but prior visual piano transcription systems use at most 0.2 seconds of context, emphasize onset detection, perform much worse on offsets, and lack note-level velocity evaluation. The authors target complete video-only MIDI transcription with accurate physical offsets and dynamics (§1; §2.2; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multi-task prediction:** A shared temporal representation feeds separate onset, offset, key-hold, and velocity heads. Training supervises every frame, with velocity loss masked to ground-truth onsets (§3.1–§3.3; Figure 2).
- **Longer temporal modeling:** A frame-wise ResNet-18 extractor and three convolutional Conformer modules process 25-frame, one-second clips; four parallel BiLSTM heads produce per-key outputs (§3.2).
- **Offset-guided decoding:** Notes end at an offset-head peak or, as fallback, an earlier key-hold termination (§3.2; §4.3).
- **Empirical contributions:** Ablations attribute gains to multi-frame supervision, sequence modeling, multi-task heads, and offset-guided decoding (§5.2; Tables 2–3). The authors also release code, checkpoints, predicted MIDI, and detected R3 synchronization offsets (§1; §6).
- **Robustness finding:** Cross-dataset onset performance collapses because fixed-geometry preprocessing does not align keyboard positions across datasets (§5.3; Table 4).

These results support the authors’ claim that explicit offset supervision, multi-task learning, temporal modeling, and offset-guided decoding improve in-dataset video transcription—especially physical key-release accuracy. They do not establish robustness to unseen camera geometry, pedal transcription, or broader instruments and viewpoints. The retrieved text omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–4, so most complete baseline comparisons and several ablation values cannot be independently inspected here.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Cross-dataset transfer fails almost completely because the learned pixel-to-key mapping is tied to training-camera geometry (§5.3; §6).
- **Author-stated:** V2N does not estimate sustain-pedal control changes (§4.3; §6).
- **Author-stated:** R3 contains synchronization offsets exceeding 200 ms in 70 of 895 files. Keeping affected files in the official split substantially lowers measured performance (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is narrow: evaluation covers two piano-video datasets, both warped to the same nominal image shape and restricted to keyboard-focused views (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline velocity comparisons are weak because systems without velocity heads receive constant-velocity predictions, and Li et al.’s video-only branch has degenerate near-zero-duration offsets (Table 1 caption; Figure 3).
- **Analyst inference:** The claim that one-second context is “decisive” on R3 is not directly isolated by an R3 short-versus-long-context ablation in the supplied text; the explicit window-length ablation is reported on PianoVAM (§5.2; Table 3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can visual transcription generalize across camera and keyboard geometry?** Transfer collapses despite perspective normalization (§5.3). A next experiment could train a key-localization or geometry-normalized representation and evaluate zero-shot transfer between PianoVAM and R3.

2. **How much would synchronization-aware training improve R3 performance?** Removing affected test files yields large gains, but the model is not evaluated with learned or corrected alignment (§6). A next experiment could jointly estimate video–MIDI lag during training and test on both original and corrected splits.

3. **Can pedal state be recovered jointly with physical and acoustic note duration?** V2N intentionally predicts physical release but omits CC64 (§4.3; §6). A next experiment could add a pedal head and compare physical-offset, pedal-extended-offset, and complete-MIDI metrics.

4. **Which temporal component matters most under heterogeneous conditions?** The supplied R3 evidence does not isolate context length, convolutional backbone, and recurrent heads. A factorial ablation on R3s and R3x could vary these components while holding supervision and decoding fixed.
