# PAST-Bench: Benchmarking the Foundations of Recursive Self-Improvement in Personal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04003) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003)


## One-sentence takeaway

PAST-Bench evaluates whether persistent agents genuinely improve across fresh sessions, finding positive but capability- and model-dependent gains while showing that outcome improvements alone do not establish use of the intended persistence pathway.

## Problem and motivation

Persistent personal agents can retain memories, procedures, histories, and user-specific state, but retaining experience does not guarantee better future behavior: agents may save the wrong information, fail to retrieve it, reuse stale state, or succeed through unrelated mechanisms. Existing one-shot, memory, and skill benchmarks do not jointly compare later-task performance with and without retained state or diagnose the responsible pathway. The authors therefore frame online self-evolution as a performance-attribution problem: determine whether cross-session gains arise from persistence while holding the model, task, runtime interface, and grader fixed (Sections 1 and 3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **PAST-Bench:** A trajectory-level benchmark containing 26 synthetic task families and 204 episodes across Memory, Procedural Reuse, Information Gathering, and Update. Episodes run in fresh sessions and include matched persistence-on/off evaluations plus shortcut, distractor, stale-state, and wrong-mechanism controls (Sections 3.1–3.2; Table 7).
- **Two-axis evaluation:** The self-evolution gap, \(\Delta\), measures the family-balanced difference between persistence-on and persistence-off task scores. A separate mechanism-evidence score measures consistency with the expected write, retrieve, apply, and update pathway using artifacts and runtime telemetry (Section 3.2; Appendix B).
- **Controlled diagnostic study:** The evaluation varies seven base models under Hermes and four frameworks under a fixed MiniMax-M2.7 model, separating model-side from framework-side variation (Section 4.2).
- **Hermes+:** Five independently ablatable runtime interventions—Plan, Render, Route, Gate, and Close—target failures in consultation, structured memory rendering, skill routing, pre-action retrieval, and correction persistence (Section 4.3; Figure 3).

The evidence supports the authors’ central claim that retained state can improve later-session performance and that capability-level and pathway-level analysis reveals differences hidden by an overall score. It does not establish that persistence is causally necessary in every successful episode: the authors explicitly describe the matched comparison as a strong control rather than causal proof, and Mech as pathway consistency rather than causal attribution.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** All tasks are synthetic, isolated, and contain no real-user data, limiting ecological validity and long-horizon conclusions (Section 6; Appendix A.2).
- **Author-stated:** The benchmark covers four foundations of online self-evolution, not stronger recursive improvement such as modifying learning mechanisms, architectures, or model parameters (Sections 1 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** Mech encodes an expected pathway but does not prove causal necessity and may underrepresent alternative valid persistence encodings (Section 6; Appendix B.5).
- **Author-stated:** Hermes+ effects vary by capability and model; its +0.02 aggregate improvement is smaller than run-to-run variation (Sections 4.4–5).
- **Author-stated:** Absolute cross-system scores retain differences in native context management, and framework adapters preserve different native loops rather than creating byte-identical deployments (Appendices C.1 and E.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark construction and checking may share systematic biases because models generated the prompts, graders, and expected artifacts, while author review covered each family but not necessarily independent review by multiple authors (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Judge validation is limited to 48 samples, uses one unchanged MiniMax-M2.7 judge, and reports that judge–human agreement is imperfect; the supplied text omits Table 8’s actual agreement values (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Agent-Zero’s 1,200-second task budget versus 300 seconds for Hermes/Hermes+ complicates direct framework comparisons, although the authors present these as design-point characterization rather than winner selection (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Do the measured persistence gains survive realistic, long-running user interactions?** The present families are synthetic and isolated. A next experiment could evaluate human-authored task streams lasting weeks, with cross-family transfer and interference measurements.

2. **Is the expected artifact causally necessary for success?** Mech only records pathway consistency. Artifact deletion, replacement, or corruption interventions before evaluation episodes could measure the behavioral effect of specific retained state.

3. **How should agents dynamically choose among memory, skills, histories, and structured artifacts?** Hermes+ uses fixed mechanisms whose interactions can reverse component benefits. A next experiment could learn a routing policy and compare it with fixed routing under matched accuracy, latency, and token budgets.

4. **Can self-evolution extend beyond state reuse to improving the learning process itself?** PAST-Bench does not test acquiring new tool strategies or revising storage and retrieval policies. A follow-up benchmark could require agents to modify these policies and test transfer to unseen task families.
