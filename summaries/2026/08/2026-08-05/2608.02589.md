# CAPEval: A Decoupled Caption Evaluation across Understanding and Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02589) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02589)


## One-sentence takeaway

CAPEval separates caption completeness from factual reliability and, across ten captioners in controlled training pipelines, finds that Coverage is more strongly associated with general vision-language understanding while Precision is more strongly associated with text-to-image generation.

## Problem and motivation

Caption quality is commonly reduced to one score, conflating how much visual content a caption mentions with how often its claims are correct. The authors argue that this prevents task-specific data selection and leaves unclear how intrinsic caption properties affect models trained on those captions. They address two gaps: explicit separation of coverage and reliability, and end-to-end evaluation of captions as training supervision rather than only as standalone outputs (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Decoupled metrics:** CAPEval defines Coverage as the percentage of ground-truth checklist facts mentioned, whether correctly or incorrectly, and Precision as the percentage of mentioned facts judged correct (§3.3, Equations 1–2).

2. **Human-verified evaluation set:** The benchmark contains 300 varied-resolution images, human-written captions, and 14,965 verified atomic facts across eight semantic dimensions (§3.2; Appendix A.3, Table 2).

3. **Model-based checklist scoring:** Qwen2.5-72B labels each ground-truth item as correctly described, incorrectly described, or unmentioned using the candidate caption without seeing the image (§3.1, §3.3).

4. **Downstream-utility protocol:** Captions from ten captioners spanning four model families are substituted into otherwise fixed training pipelines: two VLM pipelines and two text-to-image pipelines (§3.4).

5. **Task-dependent analysis:** OLS regressions relate Coverage and Precision to downstream scores, supplemented by within-family comparisons of InternVL3.5 captioners (§4.1–4.2).

The experiments support robust conditional associations across the two tested backbones per downstream category, and the controlled training design reduces confounding from images, architectures, schedules, and optimization settings. They do **not** establish that independently manipulating Coverage or Precision causes the observed differences: caption source changes may introduce other correlated textual properties, and each regression contains only ten captioners.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Analyst inference — small regression sample:** Each OLS model uses ten captioners with two predictors, making coefficient estimates and p-values potentially sensitive to individual models (§3.4, §4.2). No confidence intervals, robustness checks, or leave-one-family-out analyses are reported in the supplied text.

- **Analyst inference — association versus causal attribution:** Training variables are controlled except for caption source, but Coverage and Precision themselves are not independently manipulated. Other caption characteristics could covary with them, so the regression supports association rather than an isolated causal effect (§3.4, Equation 3).

- **Analyst inference — judge validity:** Qwen2.5-72B evaluates checklist items from caption text without image access. The supplied text gives no judge-versus-human agreement, alternative-judge comparison, or scoring ablation (§3.1, §3.3).

- **Analyst inference — limited benchmark scope:** Intrinsic scoring uses 300 images, and the text does not report sampling procedures sufficient to establish representativeness across broader image distributions (§3.2; Table 2).

- **Analyst inference — hallucination conclusion is weak:** Precision’s \(p=0.124\) is lower than Coverage’s \(p=0.914\), but it is not statistically significant at 0.05; describing hallucination as “dominated” by Precision therefore exceeds the reported statistical evidence (§4.2, Figure 7).

- **Analyst inference — metric edge case:** Equation 2 is undefined when a caption mentions no checklist items, and the retrieved text does not state how this case is handled (§3.3).

- **Reproducibility gap:** Training configurations are detailed in Appendix C, but the supplied text does not establish public availability of annotations, generated training captions, code, judge prompts, random seeds, or per-run variance.

## Open research questions

1. **Are Coverage and Precision causal drivers or proxies for other caption properties?** The regressions do not manipulate them independently. A next experiment could generate matched caption pairs that vary only in omitted facts or injected factual errors and train otherwise identical models.

2. **How stable are the results across captioners and data distributions?** Only ten captioners enter the regressions, while intrinsic evaluation uses 300 images. A larger study could add model families, captioning styles, languages, and image domains, then report family-held-out estimates and confidence intervals.

3. **How reliable is the checklist judge?** No human-agreement or alternative-judge evidence is supplied. A next experiment could compare multiple blinded judges with human labels, broken down by all eight semantic dimensions.

4. **Can a single caption policy jointly improve understanding, generation, and hallucination robustness?** The reported associations indicate different preferred profiles, while the hallucination evidence is inconclusive. A controlled Pareto-frontier experiment could vary caption breadth and factuality and evaluate all three objectives together.
