# Are the Financial Reasoning from LLMs Credible? A Real World Test over Long-Horizon Statements

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28661) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28661)


## One-sentence takeaway

FININDICES shows that leading LLMs’ exact-match accuracy on long-context financial calculations falls sharply without formula hints and under multi-value table generation, while supervised fine-tuning partially recovers performance.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether LLMs reliably apply structural financial reasoning rather than surface-level pattern matching. This matters because real financial workflows require auditable extraction, temporal alignment, accounting-taxonomy judgments, caliber reconciliation, and calculation across full statements—not merely a correct isolated number.

The authors argue that existing benchmarks emphasize multiple-choice knowledge, short cropped tables, single-hop answers, visual perception, or end-to-end tool use, leaving intermediate numerical-table reliability under realistic long contexts insufficiently measured (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **FININDICES benchmark:** Two paradigms—Single-Index computation and Table-Index tabulation—cover domain understanding, caliber alignment, temporal-plus-caliber reasoning, and temporal-plus-domain reasoning (Sections 2.1–2.2; Table 1).
- **Realistic long-context construction:** An automated pipeline extracts authentic corporate statements, randomizes table representations, aligns periods, introduces distractors and missing-information traps, and programmatically calculates answers from canonical formulas (Section 2.1; Figure 2).
- **Strict evaluation:** Table answers receive credit only when every required value is numerically equivalent and appears within the requested structural format (Section 2.2.2, Equations 1–2).
- **Failure analysis:** Controlled indicator-level cases attribute errors to missing domain knowledge and structure-induced temporal or aggregation shortcuts (Appendices C–D; Tables 13–16).
- **Training intervention:** Supervised fine-tuning on 6,301 distilled reasoning trajectories tests whether structured reasoning can be improved without reducing performance on the reported auxiliary benchmarks (Section 3; Table 4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

These results establish substantial sensitivity to formula availability and output structure under the benchmark’s strict end-to-end scoring. They do not independently prove that models lack “genuine” reasoning: exact-match failures may combine calculation, extraction, formatting, and reasoning errors. The controlled cases support the proposed structural-bottleneck mechanism, but the supplied text reports only a small qualitative set rather than its prevalence across the full benchmark.

There is also a textual inconsistency: the prose reports Gemini’s hinted Table-Index result as **70.07%**, whereas Table 3 reports **70.70%** (Section 3 versus Table 3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** FININDICES is restricted mainly to balance sheets, income statements, and cash-flow statements; it omits market, macroeconomic, and internal operational data (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** It focuses on fundamental accounting analysis and does not evaluate areas such as earnings-call sentiment or cross-asset correlation modeling (Section 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond Chinese corporate statements and accounting conventions is uncertain; multiple cases explicitly require Chinese accounting taxonomy (Appendix A; Tables 5 and 9).
- **Analyst inference:** The all-or-nothing table metric confounds reasoning accuracy with structural compliance and gives no partial credit, although this strictness reflects the authors’ auditability objective (Section 2.2.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The structural-bottleneck evidence relies on a limited set of controlled cases; no benchmark-wide decomposition quantifies formatting-only versus reasoning-only failures (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility details are incomplete in the supplied text, including training hyperparameters, decoding settings, prompts, inference budgets, expert backgrounds, and detailed split policy.
- **Analyst inference:** The experiment description says models were evaluated on 13,299 instances sampled from the training split, while Table 3 also reports development and test results; how these evaluation sets relate is not fully explained (Section 3; Table 3).

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the Table-Index deficit comes from reasoning rather than serialization?** The strict metric combines both. A next experiment could score extracted cell values independently of format, then separately measure schema compliance.

2. **Does structural load itself cause errors after controlling for the number of calculations and context length?** Appendix D gives illustrative matched cases but not a broad causal estimate. A factorial experiment could vary output width, number of indicators, context length, and format while holding formulas and source values fixed.

3. **Can tool-assisted or modular pipelines overcome the identified bottlenecks?** FININDICES evaluates end-to-end model outputs, leaving decomposition strategies untested. A follow-up could compare direct generation with explicit extraction, calculation, validation, and formatting stages on identical examples.

4. **Do the findings transfer to other accounting regimes and financial sources?** The current benchmark is centered on Chinese corporate statements. A multilingual, multi-standard extension could compare performance across accounting frameworks while keeping indicator logic aligned.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design, reported scores, and stated scope because the supplied text includes methods, tables, examples, and limitations.

Material missing information includes complete prompts and templates, model/API versions and decoding configurations, compute and inference budgets, SFT hyperparameters, contamination controls, expert qualifications and disagreement categories, benchmark licensing and access details, statistical significance tests, and a full quantitative error decomposition.
