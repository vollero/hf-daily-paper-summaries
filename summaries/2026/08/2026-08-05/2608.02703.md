# ARCHead: Activation-Metric Residual Correction for Large Language Model Output Heads

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02703) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02703)


## One-sentence takeaway

ARCHead compresses dense LLM output heads to roughly one quarter of BF16 storage while largely preserving perplexity by correcting a quantized core’s residual error in a metric derived from calibration activations.

## Problem and motivation

Post-training quantization pipelines may quantize transformer blocks while retaining the final LM head in BF16/FP16; in the inspected Qwen3-8B-Base AWQ and bitsandbytes checkpoints, this left a 1.18 GB dense projection. Because LM-head errors directly perturb vocabulary logits without subsequent normalization or learned correction, naive weight-space INT4 quantization can cause substantial quality degradation. The authors identify a gap for a deployable, packed head representation that accounts for the anisotropic distribution of final hidden states and does not retain a dense BF16 copy (§1, §3.3, §5.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Activation-metric objective:** ARCHead estimates a damped covariance from final hidden-state calibration activations, weighting reconstruction errors according to their expected effect on logits (§3.1, Equations 1–2).
- **Quantized core plus residual repair:** The head is represented by quantized low-rank core factors, a group-wise signed-INT4 residual, and a separate low-rank correction fitted via truncated SVD after transforming the remaining error by the activation metric (§3.2, Equations 3–7; Figure 1).
- **Conditional theoretical result:** For a fixed core and invertible metric transform, the unquantized truncated-SVD correction is the best correction of its rank in the induced metric. This guarantee precedes factor quantization and is not global end-to-end optimality (§3.2, Equations 8–9).
- **Realized packed implementation:** Only packed values, factors, scales, and metadata are serialized; the dense source head is discarded. Storage is measured from instantiated tensors rather than nominal bit widths (§3.3, Tables 1–2).
- **Complementary integration:** ARCHead can replace the BF16 output projection left by AWQ or bitsandbytes without changing their transformer-block representation (§3.3, §5.2).

The evidence supports a strong persistent-storage/quality trade-off for the evaluated output heads and shows that the correction branch—not merely the quantized core—accounts for most of the recovery. It also supports compatibility with the two tested block-quantization backends. It does not establish equivalent reductions in total peak GPU memory, universal superiority over GPTQ or naive INT4, statistically significant downstream gains, or preservation of all capabilities and safety properties.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** ARCHead targets output heads, not attention or MLP weights; it offers less advantage when naive INT4 already works well or when a backend already quantizes the head successfully (Limitations; Appendix K).
- **Author-stated:** Quality evaluation covers three model heads, while storage is checked across five; broader architectures, languages, contexts, and calibration domains remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Persistent parameter savings cannot be interpreted as equal reductions in peak VRAM because logits, activations, workspaces, allocator state, and KV caches may dominate (§3.3; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The three downstream tasks are only a sanity check and do not establish comprehensive capability or safety preservation (§5.7; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Conditional optimality applies only to the unquantized correction for a fixed core; factor quantization and other design choices fall outside the guarantee (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Hardware generality is uncertain because the reported deployment timing and preliminary fused-kernel evidence use one NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 (§4; Appendix K–L).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text reports no isolated ablation comparing activation-metric correction against an equal-rank unweighted correction, so the specific benefit of the metric—separate from adding a correction branch—is not numerically established here.
- **Analyst inference:** Several referenced tables are present only as captions in the retrieved text, preventing verification of their complete numerical rows, uncertainty estimates, and downstream task scores.

## Open research questions

1. **How much does activation weighting help beyond low-rank residual correction alone?** The supplied ablation removes the entire correction branch rather than changing its metric. An equal-rank, equal-storage comparison of \(p=0\), \(p=0.5\), selected \(p\), and ordinary residual SVD would isolate this effect.

2. **Does the method generalize across calibration domains and languages?** Calibration uses WikiText-103, and the authors explicitly leave broader domains and languages untested. A cross-domain matrix fitting on one corpus and evaluating perplexity and logit fidelity on several others would test robustness.

3. **Are capabilities and safety behavior preserved more broadly?** Only three downstream benchmarks are treated as a sanity check. A larger evaluation spanning generation, multilingual ability, long context, calibration, and safety—ideally with statistical uncertainty—would test behavioral preservation.

4. **What are the end-to-end memory and latency trade-offs across hardware?** The central claim concerns persistent head storage, while kernel evidence is preliminary and single-accelerator. Benchmarking peak memory, latency, throughput, and energy across GPUs and production backends would determine deployment value.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text is extensive and clearly describes the method, protocol, major findings, and limitations. However, it omits the numerical contents of Tables 1–19 beyond values repeated in surrounding prose. Consequently, exact dense perplexities and CE values, complete baseline results, standard deviations, downstream accuracies, per-model byte counts, construction times, and detailed peak-memory measurements are unavailable. The text also does not provide an isolated metric-versus-unweighted-correction ablation or broad multi-hardware validation.
