# TurnSight: Turn-Level Hindsight Self-Distillation for Tool-Integrated Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-05.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04007) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04007)


## One-sentence takeaway

TurnSight improves multi-turn tool-use training by turning the consequences of an agent’s own tool executions into turn-level, multi-horizon hindsight signals that rescale—but never reverse—reinforcement-learning advantages.

## Problem and motivation

Outcome-based reinforcement learning assigns the same trajectory-level credit to tool interactions with potentially very different effects, making long-horizon credit assignment imprecise. Existing fine-grained methods may depend on reference trajectories that cease to be appropriate after the policy visits different states, while on-policy self-distillation often uses global privileged information and token-level signals that can conflict within one tool interaction. The authors therefore identify two requirements: supervision should align with states actually visited by the policy and should be coherent at the level of complete interaction turns (Introduction; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Execution-conditioned hindsight:** For each on-policy turn, a frozen reference model re-scores the student’s sampled response while seeing its subsequent tool calls and execution results. The resulting teacher–student log-probability gaps measure how execution consequences affect confidence in the chosen action (Execution-Conditioned Hindsight Construction).

- **Turn-level supervision:** Token-level gaps are averaged across all policy tokens in a turn, treating reasoning, tool selection, and argument construction as one decision unit (Turn-Level Aggregation).

- **Multi-horizon selection:** Teachers receive one, two, or three turns of lookahead. Majority voting determines the signal direction, after which the strongest signal agreeing with that direction is selected (Multi-Lookahead Teacher Selection).

- **Direction-preserving RL integration:** Selected signals are normalized across sibling rollouts and converted into bounded weights. These weights change the magnitude, but not the sign, of the base advantage; no auxiliary imitation loss is introduced (Group-Normalized Weight Construction; Policy Optimization).

- **Empirical contribution:** The method is evaluated using two Qwen3 model sizes on an in-domain benchmark and two out-of-domain benchmarks, with component, context, temporal-coverage, and hyperparameter analyses (Experiments; Figures 3–6).

The results support the authors’ claim that TurnSight outperforms the listed baselines under a shared protocol across two backbone sizes and three benchmarks. The out-of-domain BFCL and ToolHop gains provide evidence of transfer beyond the FTRL training environment.

They do not isolate whether improvements arise purely from TurnSight rather than its MatchTIR RL backbone: the paper states that both RLSD and TurnSight use MatchTIR as their backbone, whereas several other comparisons use different objectives. The evidence also does not establish generalization to other model families, larger models, longer trajectories, or settings without executable feedback. Statistical uncertainty is not reported in the supplied text.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-discussed sensitivity:** Excessive hindsight weight can weaken global outcome guidance or amplify noisy teacher–student gaps; performance peaks at intermediate \(\lambda\) and \(\epsilon_w\) values (Figure 6).

- **Author-discussed horizon noise:** Longer lookahead can introduce unrelated interactions and dilute turn-specific evidence; fixed depths beyond one performed worse in the reported comparison (Figure 4).

- **Analyst inference — narrow model coverage:** Evaluation uses only Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, so architectural and scale generality remain untested (Experimental Setups).

- **Analyst inference — bounded interaction regime:** Training allows at most 10 turns and hindsight only spans depths 1–3; effectiveness for substantially longer delayed dependencies is unknown.

- **Analyst inference — compute and deployment asymmetry:** Training uses 16 rollouts per query, three teacher views, and 8 NVIDIA A800 80 GB GPUs. Deployment needs only the learned policy, but the paper does not quantify TurnSight’s extra training cost against baselines (Experimental Setups; Appendix B).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete uncertainty reporting:** No confidence intervals, repeated-seed variance, or significance tests are supplied, so the stability of performance differences cannot be assessed.

- **Analyst inference — incomplete retrieved ablation evidence:** Table 2’s numerical contents and the plotted values in Figures 3, 4, and 6 are absent, limiting independent assessment of component effect sizes.

- **Analyst inference — external-service dependency:** BFCL Web Search uses the Serper API’s Google Search endpoint, which may introduce result variability; no temporal-control details are provided (Appendix B).

## Open research questions

1. **Does TurnSight scale to substantially longer interactions?** The experiments cap trajectories at 10 turns and look ahead at most three turns. A next experiment could evaluate 20–50-turn tasks while varying the maximum horizon and measuring both accuracy and credit-assignment quality by turn position.

2. **How much improvement comes from TurnSight versus the MatchTIR backbone?** The comparison design does not present a clearly quantified factorial separation in the supplied text. A controlled experiment could apply identical TurnSight modulation to GRPO and MatchTIR, with matched rewards, data, decoding, and compute.

3. **Can the method work when tool feedback is noisy, partial, or non-verifiable?** The training data provides executable environments and programmatically verifiable feedback. A next experiment could corrupt or delay tool observations and measure degradation against outcome-only and reference-trajectory supervision.

4. **What is the accuracy–compute tradeoff of multiple hindsight teachers?** Training evaluates three frozen teacher views, but overhead is not quantified. A next experiment could compare one, two, and three views using wall-clock time, memory, teacher forward passes, and benchmark accuracy.
