# InSight-doc: Agentic Visual Perception for Long-Document Understanding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10628) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10628)


## One-sentence takeaway

InSight-doc trains an 8B multimodal agent to inspect long documents at low resolution and selectively zoom into high-resolution regions, improving document-VQA accuracy and evidence localization while reducing token and latency costs relative to its Qwen3-VL-8B base model.

## Problem and motivation

Long, visually rich documents require fine-grained evidence from many pages. Feeding every page at high resolution creates long visual-token sequences, costly attention computation, and performance degradation attributed by the authors to context rot; aggressive downsampling, however, can erase crucial details. Existing parsing pipelines risk cascading errors, visual-retrieval systems depend on external retrievers, and prior coarse-to-fine methods primarily fetch whole pages rather than acquiring sub-page evidence during reasoning (§1; §2.1; Appendix A.1).

The authors target document VQA in which answers must be grounded in the supplied document and unsupported questions should elicit abstention (§3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Retriever-free active perception:** The agent begins with downsampled page images, interleaves reasoning with `zoom_in` calls specifying an image, region description, and bounding box, and appends crops recovered from the high-resolution source to its context until answering or reaching the tool limit (§3.1; Figure 2).

- **Region-level, iterative evidence acquisition:** Unlike page-fetching approaches, it can inspect sub-page regions, recursively zoom, combine evidence across pages, and revise its search within a unified multimodal reasoning trace (§2.1; §3.1).

- **Active-perception training corpus:** The authors construct 17,913 SFT trajectories and 19,236 RL prompts from six source families, including multi-page, multi-hop, answerable, and unanswerable examples (§4.1–§4.3; Table 8).

- **SFT followed by RL:** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is fully fine-tuned on generated zoom trajectories, then optimized with GRPO using a binary accuracy reward judged by GPT-5-nano (§5.1; Tables 13–14).

- **Released artifacts:** The abstract states that code, datasets, and the trained model are released.

These results establish consistent gains over the authors’ base-model controls, including a zoom-enabled baseline, across several resolutions and datasets. The SFT-versus-SFT+RL comparison supports the value of the RL stage, while trajectory metrics provide evidence that gains coincide with better localization and fewer failed searches.

They do not isolate every causal ingredient of the data pipeline or reward design. Comparisons with unrelated published systems are not controlled for backbone, data, preprocessing, metrics, or budgets (Tables 5 and 17). The claim that the method “mitigates context rot” is therefore an author interpretation of improved accuracy under shorter contexts, not a direct measurement of the proposed causes of context rot.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct was trained with the proposed SFT+RL procedure, so transfer to other backbones and providers is untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The study does not evaluate advanced RL algorithms or alternative reward designs (Limitations).
- **Author-stated comparison caveat:** Cross-paper results differ in backbones, training data, resolutions, page budgets, protocols, and sometimes metrics; the closest method, Doc-V⋆, lacked released artifacts for a controlled reproduction (Table 17; Appendix F.3–F.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The main Table 2 imposes a 40-page limit on the two long-document benchmarks, potentially understating full-document difficulty; uncapped results are lower, especially on LongDocURL (Table 2; Table 16; Appendix F.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Accuracy and RL reward rely on GPT-5-nano judging. Although a 150-example stress set yields 94.7% judge accuracy, it is explicitly not distribution-matched and retains two false positives and six false negatives (Table 12; Appendix E.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Training-data construction depends heavily on several model-based generators, filters, verifiers, and answer rewriters. Their individual effects are not ablated in the supplied text (§4; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Zooming assumes access to high-resolution source images; performance when only compressed or degraded pages exist is not established (§3).
- **Analyst inference:** Efficiency measurements use a particular vLLM configuration with prefix caching and controlled concurrency, so hardware and serving-stack generality is unknown (Table 15).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the policy transfer across multimodal backbones?** Only Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct was trained. A next experiment could repeat matched SFT+RL training on multiple model families and sizes while holding data and tool budgets fixed.

2. **Which training component produces the gains?** The paper compares SFT with SFT+RL, but does not isolate multi-hop synthesis, unanswerable add-ons, difficulty filtering, weighted sampling, or trajectory generation. A factorial ablation could remove each component and measure accuracy, abstention F1, and evidence coverage.

3. **How robust is active zooming to imperfect source images?** The method relies on high-resolution originals to recover details. A next experiment could progressively degrade scan resolution, compression, skew, and OCR legibility while measuring localization failures and abstention calibration.

4. **Can reward design improve precision without expanding crop area?** RL reduces redundancy but increases crop-union area to 22.5–28.5% of a page (Table 6). A plausible experiment would add localization-efficiency or crop-cost rewards and trace the accuracy–coverage–area Pareto frontier.
