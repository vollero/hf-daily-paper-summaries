# DistilVDR: A Compact End-to-End Visual Document Retriever via Dual-Student Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10636) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10636)


## One-sentence takeaway

DistilVDR distils both sides of an 8.1B visual-document retriever into a 524M single-vector system that reaches 61.74 average NDCG@5 while substantially reducing indexing and storage costs on the evaluated ViDoRe suite.

## Problem and motivation

Visual document retrieval ranks page images for text queries while preserving visual information such as layout, tables, and figures without an OCR pipeline. Existing high-performing systems are costly multi-billion-parameter models, while smaller alternatives either retain expensive multi-vector indexes and MaxSim scoring or compress only the query encoder, leaving document indexing dependent on the teacher. The authors target the missing combination: a compact, end-to-end, single-vector retriever with small query and document towers (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Bilateral distillation:** Independent document and query students reproduce cached, L2-normalized 4096-dimensional embeddings from one frozen 8B teacher using pointwise cosine loss. Student training uses no relevance labels, negatives, or contrastive term, although the teacher itself was relevance-supervised (Sections 3.2 and 3.5; Figure 1).
- **Asymmetric architecture:** A 454M image-document tower combines InternViT-300M, a projection layer, and ModernBERT-base; a separate 70M DistilBERT text tower encodes queries. Deployment discards the teacher and scores one vector per side by dot product (Sections 3.1–3.4).
- **Two visual-budget variants:** Fast permits two image tiles plus a thumbnail; HiRes permits six plus a thumbnail. They otherwise share the training recipe and encoders (Section 4.1).
- **Unified comparison:** The paper evaluates twelve released retrievers plus the teacher through one retrieval and efficiency pipeline, reporting quality, latency, throughput, VRAM, and index footprint (Sections 1 and 4.1; Tables 1 and 3).
- **Released artifact:** The authors state that they release both model variants and code (Abstract and Section 1).

The evidence establishes a favorable quality–deployment-cost trade-off relative to the released systems reproduced under this paper’s pipeline. It does not isolate whether the gains arise from distillation, architecture, teacher choice, or training data, because the baselines were not retrained under matched conditions. It also does not establish that pointwise distillation is superior to from-scratch contrastive training: the contrastive experiment only refines an already-distilled checkpoint.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Baselines use their official releases rather than matched data, architecture, and compute, so the source of DistilVDR’s margin cannot be isolated (Limitations, page 8).
- The key control—a contrastively trained version of the same 524M architecture using the same mixture and comparable compute—was not run (Limitations, page 8).
- Only one teacher family and scale were tested, leaving teacher dependence unresolved (Limitations, pages 8–9).
- Quality remains 7–10 points below the strongest reproduced 4–8B multi-vector systems, and the experiments confound model scale with scoring mechanism (Limitations, page 8).
- Retrieval quality is evaluated only on ViDoRe; enterprise layouts, degraded scans, and production query distributions are absent (Limitations, pages 8–9).
- Students inherit the frozen teacher’s weaknesses, while independent tower training omits explicit cross-modal coupling (Limitations, page 9).
- Tile allocation depends on aspect ratio rather than page content, and its maximum is fixed at release time (Limitations, page 9).
- The text-only query tower excludes image-conditioned queries and non-Latin scripts such as Chinese, Japanese, and Arabic (Limitations, page 9).
- Only dimensional truncation is tested for index compression; scalar/product quantization and binary hashing remain unevaluated (Limitations, page 9).

**Analyst inference:** Efficiency measurements use one H200, a synthetic 100-page throughput corpus, and synthetic 10,000-candidate CPU scoring data. Although the protocol is controlled, the supplied evidence does not demonstrate the same throughput or latency on other hardware or production corpora (Appendix G).

**Analyst inference:** Perceptual-hash deduplication at distance zero removes only exact visual duplicates, so near-duplicate leakage is not ruled out by the described procedure (Appendix B).

## Open research questions

1. **Does distillation itself outperform matched contrastive training?** This remains open because no from-scratch control used the same architecture, data, and compute. Train matched distilled and hard-negative contrastive systems and compare both quality and convergence cost.

2. **How sensitive is the student to teacher scale and family?** Only one 8B teacher was evaluated. Cache embeddings from several teachers and train otherwise identical students to measure retained quality and teacher-specific error transfer.

3. **Can content-adaptive tiling improve the quality–throughput frontier?** Fast and HiRes apply fixed caps regardless of page complexity. A next experiment could predict tile budgets from a cheap page-complexity signal and compare against both fixed variants at matched average token cost.

4. **Can richer distillation close the remaining gap?** Independent pointwise alignment leaves cross-modal relations unused and bounds students to the teacher embedding space. Compare pointwise alignment with joint query-document distillation from relevance scores or multi-vector teacher signals.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the method, full principal tables, ablations, stated limitations, training composition, and efficiency protocol.
