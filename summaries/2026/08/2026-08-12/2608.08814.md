# 360CityArena: A Realistic Virtual Urban Navigation Benchmark for Embodied Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08814) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08814)


## One-sentence takeaway
360CityArena evaluates embodied agents on 175 urban navigation and spatial-reasoning tasks in a video-derived virtual Akihabara, where the best tested LMM reached 17.1% overall versus 77.3% for locally familiar humans.

## Problem and motivation
Existing outdoor embodied-AI benchmarks do not jointly provide photorealistic, dynamic, ground-level observations; extended street-network exploration; and varied diagnostic tasks. According to the authors, 3D simulators lack sufficient visual realism or structural complexity, Street View environments are discontinuous and static, and prior video-based environments are too short for district-scale exploration (Introduction, pp. 2–3; Related Work, pp. 5–6). This limits evaluation of agents intended to perceive, plan, and reason in realistic cities.

## Approach and contributions

- **Urban benchmark:** A navigable pose graph of Akihabara constructed from 602 prerecorded 360° video segments covering 85 streets, approximately 750 m × 650 m (Section 3.1, pp. 6–7).
- **Multi-task evaluation:** 175 manually constructed tasks spanning localization, language- and image-goal landmark search, map navigation, vision-language navigation, object counting, and relational spatial reasoning, grouped into three categories and three difficulty levels (Sections 3.2–3.3, pp. 7–8).
- **LMM baselines and diagnostics:** Six proprietary or open vision-language models are evaluated against a five-participant local-expert human baseline, with additional analyses of input modality, explicit location information, difficulty, and verified failure categories (Sections 4.2, 5.1–5.2, pp. 10–13).
- **Controlled modality comparison:** Language- and image-based landmark search use identical settings and landmarks apart from goal modality (Section 3.2, p. 7).

The results establish that the tested agent implementations struggle on this benchmark and that goal modality, difficulty, and location presentation correlate with different outcomes. They do not establish real-world navigation safety, generalization beyond Akihabara, or that LMM reasoning alone causes the failures: the authors explicitly note that controller and prompting brittleness contribute to integrated performance (Section 5.2, p. 13).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Motion is restricted to captured trajectories, boundaries may be visually discontinuous, and physical interaction is unavailable (Section 6, p. 14). Boundary transitions comprise 11.3% of actions and were not overrepresented in Action or Explore failures, but this does not rule out subtler effects.
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers only one district, limiting urban diversity and evidence for cross-regional generalization (Section 6, p. 14).
- **Author-stated:** Difficulty labels involve annotator judgment despite monotonic supporting statistics (Section 3.3, p. 8).
- **Analyst inference:** The five-person human baseline is a locally familiar, in-domain reference rather than a general human estimate, as the authors acknowledge; comparisons may therefore mix capability and local knowledge (Section 5.1, p. 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Each subtask has only 25 examples, so differences of a few cases can cause sizable percentage changes. The text reports no confidence intervals or repeated-run variance for model scores (Sections 3.2 and 5.1, pp. 7, 10–12).
- **Analyst inference:** The location-information study reports only GPT-5 and offers no statistical uncertainty; it cannot establish a model-general causal effect (Table 3, p. 12).
- **Analyst inference:** Failure attribution reduces each failed episode to one dominant cause and depends initially on another model’s interpretation, although humans verify the labels (Section 5.2, p. 13).
- **Reproducibility risk:** Prompts, stopping rules, software, and some inference settings are supplied, but the retrieved text does not state benchmark licensing, artifact availability, model API snapshots for every baseline, or repeated-trial procedures (Supplement A–B, pp. 20–29).

## Open research questions

1. **Does performance transfer across cities and visual cultures?** Only Akihabara is evaluated. A next experiment could construct matched pose-graph benchmarks in several cities and test zero-shot versus city-adapted agents.
2. **How can agents align maps with egocentric observations reliably?** Location priors sometimes hurt GPT-5, and the authors attribute this to possible map-to-view alignment difficulty. A controlled experiment could separately score orientation estimation, map registration, and route execution with progressively richer location cues.
3. **Would learned navigation control improve reasoning-task results?** Action and exploration failures indicate that the current discrete controller and prompting affect scores. A next experiment could hold the perception/reasoning model fixed while comparing the ReAct controller with a learned or hierarchical policy.
4. **How much do video boundaries and constrained motion affect validity?** Aggregate logs found no clear overrepresentation of boundary-related failures, but trajectory constraints remain. A paired study could reproduce identical routes in a freely navigable reconstruction and compare behavior near and away from boundaries.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes the full methods, tables, limitations, and supplement.

Material missing or unclear: public release and license status; statistical uncertainty and run-to-run variance; exact aggregation used to obtain the 17.1% and 77.3% overall scores; per-difficulty sample counts; full numerical values behind Figure 4 and Figure 5; demographic details beyond the human participants’ student status and local familiarity; and evidence from cities outside Akihabara.
