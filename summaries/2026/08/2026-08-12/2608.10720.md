# Ex-Omni-2D: Expressive Omni-Modal Dialogue Models with Native Visual Presence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10720) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10720)


## One-sentence takeaway

Ex-Omni-2D turns multimodal dialogue into coordinated text, personalized speech, and reference-conditioned avatar video through a structured visual plan and shared speech units, while its distilled streaming variant enables incremental—but not end-to-end real-time—generation.

## Problem and motivation

Current omni-modal dialogue models can understand text, speech, and images and produce spoken answers, but generally lack a synchronized visual presence. Conversely, avatar systems typically animate completed or externally supplied speech without deriving visual intent from the dialogue itself.

The authors target three gaps: dialogue-conditioned planning of scene, affect, and motion; a compact interface that coordinates speech and video without requiring large-scale fully paired query–text–speech–video data; and an incremental alternative to slow full-sequence video diffusion (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Dialogue-native visual planning:** A Qwen3-8B-based dialogue model emits a private, five-field Visual Thought Plan (VTP)—first-frame scene, overall scene, emotion, movement style, and detailed motion—before the user-facing response (Sections 3.2 and 3.6).

- **Shared acoustic-temporal interface:** A Qwen3-TTS-initialized generator produces 16-codebook speech units at 12.5 Hz. These units are decoded into personalized speech and repeated to align with video at 25 FPS, connecting vocal content and avatar motion without waveform re-encoding (Section 3.3).

- **Heterogeneous staged training:** Speech, dialogue, and avatar-video pathways use their respective datasets; VTPs and speech units reconnect them at inference. Training uses roughly 800K ASR examples, 1M TTS examples, InstructS2S-200K and OmniCharacter, and about 140K filtered SpeakerVid clips rather than fully paired dialogue-video records (Sections 3.6 and L.1–L.2).

- **Quality and streaming realizations:** A bidirectional, full-sequence 1.3B Video Teacher provides the primary visual output. It is distilled into a block-causal Student whose Prefix Streaming carries the preceding clean latent into each new window to limit accumulated subject drift (Sections 3.4–3.5).

The evidence supports a functioning coordinated response pipeline, a measurable quality–efficiency trade-off, and improved late-chunk stability from the selected Prefix-Streaming system. It does not establish strict architectural superiority over renderers with different interfaces, true single-request real-time interaction, independent causal control by VTP, or uniformly better dialogue/language ability than the underlying backbone.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Reference-speaker similarity remains limited; the Teacher’s reported SIM is 0.417 (Limitations; Table 1).
- VTP is not an independently sufficient control signal because speech units jointly influence video. Fixed text/audio guidance scales do not work equally well across responses (Section 4.6; Limitations).
- Sharing the autoregressive channel with VTP harms speech QA and reasoning: CommonEval falls by 0.11 and BBH by 2.40 points in the reported ablation (Table 7).
- Long-horizon evaluation covers temporal subject stability and facial-motion regularity rather than broader semantic, behavioral, or interaction quality (Limitations).
- The Teacher is computationally expensive; the Student has lower IQ, DD, and Sync-C and remains above E2E RTF 1 in the highlighted four-GPU setting (Table 4; Limitations).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Table 1 is capability-oriented across mismatched interfaces, so renderer rankings are confounded by supported inputs and upstream response sources; the authors explicitly caution against treating it as a same-interface ranking.
- The prefix comparison uses independently trained and validation-selected systems, leaving an inference-time toggle or tighter controlled ablation unavailable (Section 4.7).
- VTP realization scores are based on a third-party multimodal judge and co-generated plan, speech, and video. They show observational agreement, not independent controllability; first-frame grounding is only 43.00% (Table 15).
- Much of the main audio-video evaluation uses 200 CommonEval queries and held-out SpeakerVid references, so generalization to other identities, languages, visual settings, and longer conversations is not established.
- The supplied text gives extensive training settings but does not establish the release or availability of weights, code, processed records, or evaluation outputs.

## Open research questions

1. **Can visual planning be isolated without reducing dialogue and reasoning quality?** VTP supervision lowers CommonEval and BBH scores. A next experiment could compare a separate planner, adapter, or parallel prediction head against the shared autoregressive protocol under matched training data.

2. **How independently controllable is the VTP?** Existing VTP scores evaluate naturally co-generated speech and video. A factorial intervention that holds speech units fixed while swapping individual VTP fields could measure causal control over emotion, scene, and motion.

3. **Can streaming reach genuinely interactive latency without losing Teacher-level quality?** The four-step pipeline’s first video chunk takes 3.142 seconds and E2E RTF is 1.293. Future work could test a larger causal backbone, speculative planning, or faster speech-unit generation while reporting both time-to-first-output and complete-request RTF.

4. **Does Prefix Streaming preserve identity and motion over substantially longer and more diverse responses?** Evaluation spans 16 chunks and focuses on subject consistency and facial dynamics. Longer-duration, multilingual, multi-speaker, and gesture-semantic evaluations with human judgments would test whether drift reduction generalizes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the architecture, reported evaluations, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.
