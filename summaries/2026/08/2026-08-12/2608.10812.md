# Reference-Free Post-Training of Open Large Language Models for Multilingual Machine Translation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10812) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10812)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors claim that language-gated, reference-free GRPO followed by SFT–RL checkpoint interpolation improves neural translation-quality metrics across 46 languages and three model sizes, while partly recovering the spBLEU lost during RL.

## Problem and motivation

Multilingual translation post-training is limited by scarce and uneven parallel data, especially for low-resource languages and non-English directions, whereas source-only text is easier to obtain. The authors target this gap by optimizing translations without reference targets. They also address two risks of reference-free reinforcement learning: reward hacking through wrong-language outputs and drift away from behavior learned during supervised fine-tuning (Introduction; Section 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Reference-free RL:** Starting from MiLMMT-46-v0.1, the method applies GRPO using the average of XCOMET2 and COMETKiwi3 as reward, set to zero when OpenLID-v3 detects the wrong target language (Section 2, “Reward design”).
- **Filtered source-only training set:** References are removed from MiLMMT SFT examples, and rollout statistics filter 263,982 candidates down to 31,572 instances across 192 directions: 30,572 training and 1,000 validation examples (Section 2, “RL data”; Appendix A, Table 3).
- **Checkpoint interpolation:** SFT and RL parameters are linearly interpolated; the released MiLMMT-46-v1.0 checkpoints use an SFT weight of \(\alpha=0.5\) at 1B, 4B, and 12B scales (Sections 2–3).
- **Post-training comparison:** The paper compares independently trained RL models with on-policy distillation from the 12B model, finding that distillation approaches but does not exceed the RL–interpolation frontier (Section 5, Tables 2 and 16).
- **Released assets:** The authors state that models and code are released, but the supplied text does not include the release location or license details (Abstract; Conclusion).

These results establish consistent gains on the paper’s learned evaluators and show an empirical trade-off with spBLEU under the tested benchmarks, languages, and checkpoints. They do not establish improved human-rated translation quality: no human evaluation is reported in the supplied text, and the reward uses the same QE model families employed in evaluation. Comparisons with external systems are observational benchmark comparisons, not controlled comparisons of training data or compute.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Source truncation:** The collector omitted 16,317 characters from the middle of the appendices. Consequently, this digest cannot claim complete coverage, particularly of detailed reference-based results.
- **Author-stated limitation:** Pure RL lowers spBLEU even as learned quality metrics improve; interpolation mitigates but does not eliminate this trade-off (Sections 4.1–4.2).
- **Author-stated limitation:** OPD transfers much of the teacher’s performance but does not extend the frontier produced by RL plus interpolation (Section 5).
- **Analyst inference — evaluator coupling:** Training rewards use XCOMET and COMETKiwi, while evaluation also uses XCOMET and COMETKiwi. Gains may therefore partly reflect optimization toward the evaluators; the supplied text reports no human assessment or independent learned-metric family to resolve this risk.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete reward ablation:** The supplied text does not report isolated ablations of the two QE components, the language-ID gate, or the data-filtering thresholds, so their individual contributions are not established.
- **Analyst inference — subset comparability:** Baselines with different language coverage are compared only on shared subsets. This is reasonable but means rankings across the 21-, 26-, 28-, 31-, and 46-language blocks are not directly interchangeable (Section 3; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — uneven training coverage:** Appendix A shows large differences in retained examples per direction, including directions with very few instances. Aggregate results may conceal direction-specific instability.
- **Reproducibility gap:** Hyperparameters are supplied in Table 5, but the excerpt does not provide model/code URLs, hardware, total compute, random-seed variation, or statistical uncertainty.

## Open research questions

1. **Do gains survive independent human evaluation?** This remains open because the training reward and primary evaluation share QE model families. A next experiment could compare v0.1, pure RL, and several interpolation weights using blinded human adequacy and fluency judgments across resource levels.

2. **Which component drives the improvement?** The individual effects of dual-QE averaging, language gating, rollout filtering, and interpolation are not isolated. A factorial ablation could remove or replace each component while holding data and training budget fixed.

3. **How robust is the method for sparsely represented directions?** Training counts vary sharply across the 192 directions, while results are largely aggregated. A next experiment could stratify gains and wrong-language rates by retained-example count and language resource class from Table 4.

4. **Can reward models reduce the spBLEU–XCOMET trade-off without overfitting evaluators?** The authors identify more robust, human-aligned rewards as future work. A plausible experiment would train with a held-out reward model and evaluate with human judgments plus metric families excluded from training.
