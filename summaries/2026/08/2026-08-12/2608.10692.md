# SPIEval: Evaluating Large Language Models as Mobile Assistants over Scattered Personal Information

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10692) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10692)


## One-sentence takeaway

SPIEval shows that current LLM mobile assistants remain unreliable when completing underspecified tasks from information scattered across apps, with the best tested configuration reaching 57.3% exact accuracy and information localization emerging as the main bottleneck.

## Problem and motivation

Mobile assistants often receive brief instructions whose required entities, preferences, and action parameters must be recovered from multiple applications. The authors argue that prior benchmarks mainly provide parameters directly, expose missing information through preceding tool calls, or limit personalization to single-document retrieval, leaving proactive cross-app localization insufficiently evaluated (Sections 1–2).

This matters because selecting plausible but incorrect personal information can cause an assistant to execute the wrong call, payment, or other action. SPIEval targets this gap through underspecified instructions, scattered structured records, proactive retrieval, and executable, automatically verifiable outcomes.

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** SPIEval contains 250 manually constructed tasks—50 for each of reasoning, disambiguation, integration, preference inference, and multi-intent decomposition—grounded in 4,335 fictional records across 10 simulated apps (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Interactive environment:** Models receive 11 retrieval tools and 10 execution tools. Retrieval supports pagination and app-specific substring, regular-expression, fuzzy, field-targeted, and case-sensitive searches (Section 3.3; Appendix C).
- **Verifiable evaluation:** Final execution calls are checked against human-authored gold tools and parameters using binary exact accuracy; order is ignored for the 76 multi-execution tasks, and explicitly annotated alternative or unconstrained values are supported (Sections 3.4 and 4).
- **Behavioral analysis:** Nine LLMs are evaluated at their highest and lowest reasoning-effort settings, including controlled full-result and no-search conditions plus analyses of failure types, search methods, retrieval counts, and efficiency (Sections 4–6).
- **Released asset:** The abstract reports that benchmark data and code are available through the linked Hugging Face dataset.

The controlled no-search improvement supports the authors’ claim that retrieval and localization are major obstacles in this environment. The evidence does not isolate query formulation from every other retrieval-related behavior, prove that the same bottleneck dominates real phones, or establish that more retrieval causally improves accuracy: the successful-versus-failed retrieval comparison is observational.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied text does not include a dedicated author-stated limitations section.

- **Analyst inference — synthetic scope:** All records are fictional, apps are simulated, schemas are structured, and tools execute deterministically. This supports reproducibility but leaves transfer to noisy real devices, interfaces, and personal histories untested (Sections 3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference — narrow user population:** Every task uses one unified user profile, so robustness across users, cultures, preferences, and record distributions is not established (Section 3.3; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference — manually intended solution paths:** Tasks are accepted when two researchers converge through the intended retrieval process. This improves consistency but may favor curator-anticipated strategies (Section 3.3).
- **Analyst inference — strict metric:** Binary exact accuracy gives no partial credit when some tools or parameters are correct, potentially concealing differences in near-successful behavior (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference — default-setting comparability:** Models use their provider-specific highest and lowest reasoning levels while other hyperparameters remain at defaults; equal computational budgets across models are not established (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference — missing statistical detail:** Three-run means and standard deviations are promised, but the retrieved Table 2 contains no visible cells, so per-model uncertainty and significance cannot be inspected.
- **Analyst inference — ambiguous figure references:** Sections 5–6 appear to mismatch some claims with the supplied Figure 5–8 captions, weakening traceability without the underlying figures.
- **Analyst inference — limited ablation:** Full-result and no-search controls are provided, but the text reports no interventions that separately train or enforce query reformulation, verification, field targeting, fuzzy search, or stopping criteria.

## Open research questions

1. **Can explicit evidence-sufficiency checks prevent premature execution?** Failed tasks contain fewer retrievals and wrong parameters dominate reported failures, but causality is not tested. A next experiment could require a verification step or calibrated confidence threshold before execution and measure exact accuracy, abstention, and retrieval cost.

2. **Would teaching structured search improve localization?** Regex, fuzzy matching, and field targeting are rarely used, but the paper does not test whether greater use improves outcomes. A controlled intervention could compare tool-use demonstrations or search-policy training against the unchanged baseline.

3. **How well does SPIEval performance transfer to realistic devices and diverse users?** The benchmark uses one fictional profile and deterministic structured tools. A follow-up could evaluate the same capability categories across multiple profiles and noisy GUI- or API-mediated app environments.

4. **Can multi-intent planning improve independently of retrieval?** Multi-intent accuracy remains 45.7% under no-search. An experiment could provide an oracle decomposition, an oracle execution plan, or both to separate decomposition, coordination, and parameter-inference errors.
