# Articulated Object Reconstruction from Rest-State Observation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27749) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27749)


## One-sentence takeaway
Rest2Art reconstructs part-level meshes and prismatic or revolute joints from a single closed object state by cross-validating VLM and segmentation outputs on an explicit mesh, then fitting mesh-constrained joints to motion hypotheses generated by video diffusion.

## Problem and motivation
Interactive digital twins require both 3D part geometry and kinematic structure, but most reconstruction methods depend on visible motion, multiple articulation states, part annotations, or known part counts. The authors target the harder and inherently ambiguous case of openable objects observed only in a closed rest state, which is also easier to capture at scale from product imagery and scene datasets (Introduction, pp. 2–3). Their identified gap is that pretrained models provide useful semantic and motion priors but can independently miss, duplicate, or hallucinate parts (Fig. 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Rest-state formulation:** Rest2Art accepts a rest-state mesh and automatically returns part-wise meshes and joint parameters without a known part count or observed motion (Sec. 3.1).

- **Cross-model part recovery:** A VLM proposes a hierarchy and semantic part counts, while SAM3 produces view-level masks. Count disagreements trigger up to two rounds of mutual correction; validated evidence is fused onto the mesh using confidence-weighted voting and graph-based label propagation (Secs. 3.2, A.1–A.3).

- **Motion hypotheses with geometric verification:** A video diffusion model synthesizes candidate articulation, CoTracker3 extracts trajectories, and rigid prismatic or revolute models are fitted by robust reprojection optimization. Mesh collisions and adjacent-boundary geometry constrain otherwise ambiguous axes (Secs. 3.3, A.4–A.5).

- **Simulation-ready geometry:** Surface patches are solidified into watertight parts; drawers receive a parametric interior tray because their true interiors are unobserved (Secs. 3.4, A.6).

- **Input flexibility:** The same mesh-centered pipeline is demonstrated with multi-view reconstructions, single-image 3D generation, and scanned or existing meshes (Sec. 4.5; Figs. 9, F–G).

The evidence supports strong segmentation performance and competitive rest-state joint recovery on the reported synthetic and real-world evaluations. It does not establish universally superior joint estimation: some baselines have lower angular or positional error, different methods receive different input conditions, and those errors exclude incorrectly classified joint types. The MultiScan comparison is also conditioned on method-specific evaluable coverage.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The main formulation covers rigid parts connected by prismatic or revolute joints, primarily openable furniture. More complex kinematic structures and non-planar joints remain future work (Secs. 3.1, 5, D.3).

- **Author-stated observability limit:** True interior geometry cannot be recovered from closed observations. Drawer interiors are therefore plausible parametric constructions rather than reconstructed ground truth (Sec. A.6).

- **Author-stated failure modes:** Two of 69 generated videos had corrupted geometry that prevented tracking; subtle or short-range motion can yield insufficient displacement; joint accuracy depends on segmentation and mesh quality (Sec. C.4).

- **Author-stated convergence issue:** With two refinement rounds, 84.1% of ACD scenes reach agreement; 7.2% oscillate between two decompositions. Raising the cap to 20 reaches 92.8%, so some ambiguity persists (Sec. C.1).

- **Analyst inference — comparison validity:** Two-state ACD baselines receive synthetically generated articulated views rather than their native real-motion inputs. Only 46 of 69 videos survive manual verification, and the authors explicitly describe direct comparison as infeasible; Table 2 therefore should not be read as a clean comparison under equal or native input conditions (Secs. 4.3, B.1).

- **Analyst inference — real-world protocol:** For single-state methods on MultiScan, both available states are processed and the lower-reprojection-error result is reported. This differs from a strict single-observation deployment and may make the reported real-world result optimistic for that setting (Sec. 4.3).

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility and resources:** The system relies on several large pretrained components, prompting, random video generation, and up to 86.34 GB of GPU memory. The supplied text does not establish a public code release or quantify variability across random seeds (Secs. 4.1, A.3–A.7).

## Open research questions

1. **Can joint recovery extend beyond revolute and prismatic motion?**  
   This remains open because the current rigid model excludes non-planar and more complex mechanisms. A next experiment could add screw, spherical, or learned constrained-motion models and evaluate them on annotated non-furniture categories.

2. **Can hidden geometry be inferred with calibrated uncertainty?**  
   Drawer interiors are currently generated from fixed geometric rules because they are invisible at rest. A next experiment could compare multiple completion hypotheses against objects scanned after opening and measure both geometric error and uncertainty calibration.

3. **How much does video generation actually contribute under controlled conditions?**  
   The paper provides qualitative design ablations and artifact analysis, but the supplied text does not report a full joint-metric ablation replacing generated trajectories with alternative priors. A next experiment could compare video diffusion, direct VLM regression, category priors, and oracle real trajectories under identical meshes and metrics.

4. **Can comparable accuracy be achieved with lower computation?**  
   The reported system takes about 277 seconds and peaks at 86.34 GB on an H200, with video generation as the main bottleneck. A next experiment could reduce video resolution, frames, seeds, or model size and trace accuracy–latency–memory trade-offs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported results because the supplied text includes the full main paper and substantial supplementary material.

Material missing or unclear: the complete quantitative segmentation breakdown referenced in the main text beyond Table C; sample counts per ACD subset and MultiScan evaluation; uncertainty or confidence intervals; sensitivity to random video seeds; exact definitions or thresholds for several motion-classification and robust-fitting decisions; quantitative ground-truth evaluation of mesh completion; and whether code, prompts, processed data, or pretrained artifacts will be publicly released.
