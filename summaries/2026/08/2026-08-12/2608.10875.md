# VibeLifeBench: Can Your Life Agent Be Proactive and Persistent in a Living World?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10875) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10875)


## One-sentence takeaway

VibeLifeBench evaluates agents on 200 simulated multi-week life tasks and finds that all seven tested models struggle with proactive monitoring, persistent state maintenance, and adapting plans to silent world changes.

## Problem and motivation

Existing agent benchmarks largely evaluate explicit, short-lived requests in static coding, office, or tool-use environments. The authors argue that everyday assistance instead requires agents to act without fresh prompting, detect independently occurring changes, preserve implicit constraints and authorization boundaries, and maintain coherent plans over weeks.

The identified gap is the lack of a benchmark combining all three properties: proactivity, a dynamic “living” world, and long-horizon coherence within one continuous task (Introduction; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Living-world task formulation.** Each task is a scripted timeline on a virtual clock. User messages, world observations, and notifications create turns, while silent mutations alter backend state without alerting the agent (Sections 2.2.1–2.2.3; Table 2).

2. **Broad life-assistance benchmark.** VibeLifeBench contains 200 tasks across ten domains, using 22 mock services, 288 tool interfaces, and 7,453 events. Tasks have a median horizon of 29 simulated days and 24 stages (Sections 2.4; Tables 4).

3. **Artifact-based, stage-aware evaluation.** A total of 12,261 weighted deterministic checks evaluate timely conduct, cross-stage constraints, and final state using tool calls, backend state, persistent artifacts, and replies—not hidden reasoning (Sections 2.2.5 and 2.4; Table 3).

4. **Frontier-model evaluation.** Seven models were evaluated three times on every task under a common tool-using harness. The best reported avg@3 was 32.5/100 (Sections 3–4; Table 5).

These results establish poor performance under VibeLifeBench’s particular scripted, offline environment and artifact-weighted scoring system. They do not by themselves establish equivalent failure rates in real deployments, isolate which benchmark mechanism causes each score difference, or show that more costly inference causally improves performance.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated qualification:** Capability-axis labels in Table 7 are assigned through keyword matching over check names and are described as indicative rather than exact; 41.8% of failed checks fall outside the named categories (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference — simulation validity:** The benchmark uses scripted timelines and mock, offline services. This supports reproducibility but leaves uncertain how well results transfer to noisy real services, unscripted users, and genuinely asynchronous deployments (Sections 2.1, 2.2.2, and 3.3).

- **Analyst inference — causal attribution:** The evaluation does not report ablations that remove silent mutations, implicit constraints, or long horizons. Consequently, the contribution of each design feature to model failure is not isolated.

- **Analyst inference — comparison control:** Models share a harness and use their “strongest reasoning setting,” but the supplied text does not specify all model configuration, sampling, prompt, or provider-version details needed to assess strict comparability (Sections 3.3–4).

- **Analyst inference — scoring dependence:** Uneven, manually authored weights deliberately emphasize safety-critical failures. This is defensible, but the paper does not report sensitivity to alternative weighting schemes (Sections 2.2.5 and 2.4).

- **Analyst inference — asset availability:** The text alternates between calling the 200 tasks a “released evaluation set” and saying the assets “will” be open-sourced; current public availability and licensing are not established by the supplied content.

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the deficit is specifically caused by silent mutations?** The paper reports combined benchmark results but no mutation ablation. A next experiment could compare identical timelines with silent mutations, explicit notifications, and no mutations.

2. **Can structured persistent memory prevent late-task decay?** Durable artifacts rarely pass, and all models deteriorate in the final third. A controlled experiment could compare the common harness against agents equipped with validated ledgers, constraint stores, and scheduled reconciliation.

3. **How robust are rankings to scoring design?** Safety and final-state checks receive disproportionate weight, but weighting sensitivity is not reported. Recomputing results under preregistered alternative weight schemes would test rank stability.

4. **Do simulated results predict real-world assistant behavior?** The environment is deterministic and offline. A plausible follow-up would replay a subset of tasks against sandboxed real service APIs with human-generated, unscripted interventions and compare failure patterns.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes the complete methods, statistics, and main tables.

Material missing or unresolved information includes exact prompts and model configuration parameters, task-author and checker validation procedures, inter-annotator or audit statistics, weighting-sensitivity analyses, component ablations, real-world external-validity studies, contamination checks, and confirmed release URLs, licensing, or asset availability.
