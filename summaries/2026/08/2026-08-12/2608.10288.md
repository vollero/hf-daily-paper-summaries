# Power law graph attention: exact generalization of scaled dot-product attention, empirical collapse at inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-12.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10288) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10288)


## One-sentence takeaway

PLGA exactly contains scaled dot-product attention as the \(G_{\mathrm{LM}}=I\) case, while one audited PLDR-LLM checkpoint learns an almost input-invariant operator that can be cached without changing tested decisions, though this collapse is empirical and not established across models or training runs.

## Problem and motivation

Scaled dot-product attention fixes the query–key comparison to a Euclidean bilinear form. The authors study whether this form can instead be generated from each input through a learned nonlinear operator, and whether the resulting extra training-time machinery remains necessary at inference.

The identified gap is both mathematical and empirical: prior PLDR-LLM work lacked a consolidated formal account separating proved containment and cacheability results from measured invariance, criticality interpretations, and conjectures. The paper also examines PLGA’s unusual global-Gram semantics, under which historical rows in a block may depend on later supplied rows even though final-row online generation remains causal (Sections 1, 4.3–4.5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Power Law Graph Attention:** PLGA constructs a query Gram matrix \(D_Q=Q^\top Q\), processes it with a shared row-wise residual learner, forms a strictly positive tensor \(A_{\mathrm{LM}}\), applies learned elementwise powers, and maps the result to a bilinear score operator \(G_{\mathrm{LM}}\) used inside ordinary softmax attention (Definition 3.2).

- **Exact relationship to SDPA:** The paper proves that setting \(G_{\mathrm{LM}}=I\) recovers scaled dot-product attention exactly. Under exact input invariance, the generator can be removed at inference and replaced by constant operators (Theorem 5.4).

- **Structural analysis:** It proves positivity and Perron–Frobenius properties, characterizes rank-one generator collapse, explains cross-head identity through the shared row map, analyzes the NOTEARS-style regularizer, and characterizes when a constant operator preserves RoPE’s relative-position dependence (Propositions 3.7–3.11, 4.1; Theorem 4.4).

- **Conditional collapse mechanism:** The proposed three-stage account combines partial rotary averaging, idealized statistical concentration, and learned row-map contraction. Only parts are proved; key statistical and contraction assumptions are conditional or sampled empirically (Section 5.4).

- **Reproducibility assets:** Selected proof cores are checked in Lean 4, and a numerical audit evaluates released checkpoints, including cache fidelity, operator spectra, scoring semantics, contraction diagnostics, and validity limits (Appendices B–D).

The evidence establishes exact algebraic containment and conditional cacheability, plus unusually strong collapse and scoring agreement for the audited workloads. It does not establish that training reliably produces collapse across seeds, scales, datasets, or architectures; that PLGA outperforms matched SDPA; that the worst-case stability bound guarantees cached decoding; or that the proposed self-organized-criticality interpretation is causal.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Source limitation:** The supplied paper text is truncated, with 43,264 characters omitted from the middle. This analysis therefore cannot claim complete coverage, particularly of Sections 7–9 and parts of the appendices.

- **Author-stated empirical scope:** The main audit centers on one 110M checkpoint, eight prompts, one software stack, and sampled benchmark subsets; a second checkpoint is used for selected contrasts. Generalization across seeds, scales, datasets, devices, and training trajectories is untested (Appendix D.13).

- **Conditional mechanism:** Independence and common-second-moment assumptions do not match contextual transformer queries exactly, and the rotary-twirl bound is vacuous at the reference context length. The audit finds only about 10% median off-commutant suppression (Proposition 5.10; Appendix D.4).

- **No uniform contraction certificate:** Jacobians and contraction ratios are sampled at visited points. Individual residual units are not all contractive, so the strong per-unit assumption in Proposition 5.12 is false for the audited checkpoint even though the composition appears highly contractive (Appendix D.6).

- **Training/evaluation semantics:** Global-Gram block training exposes intermediate historical-row predictions to later tokens, including their targets. Block loss is therefore not generally an autoregressive chain-rule likelihood, although sampled aggregate differences are small after training (Sections 4.3–4.5; Appendix D.12).

- **SOC interpretation:** The paper labels self-organized criticality as phenomenological. It lacks multi-seed uncertainty, finite-size scaling, susceptibility, avalanche statistics, and an identified self-tuning mechanism (Section 6.3).

- **Analyst inference:** The evaluation is sufficient to demonstrate a checkpoint phenomenon, but too narrow to support a general systems claim that PLGA generators can routinely be discarded after training.

## Open research questions

1. **How reliably does operator collapse emerge?**  
   It remains open because the audit covers very few checkpoints and no multi-seed training study. A next experiment would train matched PLGA models across seeds, scales, datasets, and learning-rate schedules while measuring operator variance throughout training.

2. **Does PLGA improve outcomes over a resource-matched SDPA model?**  
   Exact containment does not prove strict function-class separation or performance superiority (Remark 5.5). A next experiment would compare matched parameter, compute, data, optimizer, and positional-encoding budgets, including identity, random, learned-constant, and input-generated \(G\) ablations.

3. **Is target exposure in blockwise training beneficial or exploitable?**  
   The global Gram allows intermediate rows to depend on their targets, but its training-time effect was not measured. A next experiment would compare global-Gram training with sequential-prefix or cumulative-Gram training and track both block loss and true sequential NLL during training.

4. **Can cache fidelity receive a useful certificate?**  
   The factorwise norm-product bound misses the observed decoding margin by roughly 45 orders of magnitude. A next experiment would test margin-aware propagation, interval bounds, or decision-direction sensitivity along complete decoding trajectories.
