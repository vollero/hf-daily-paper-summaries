# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-08-28

## Executive summary

- Agent adaptation is a central theme: several papers move reusable knowledge into mutable harnesses, wikis, memories, or skill graphs rather than model weights (2608.15763, 2608.26530, 2608.27454, 2608.25500), while 2608.27260 offers a conceptual framework for judging the data behind such systems.
- Closed-loop world–action models span games and robotics. GameWAM (2608.26200) jointly models video and native controls; Zero-WAM (2608.26103) uses human videos as task specifications; TacForcing (2608.25798) refreshes tactile evidence during action generation.
- Evaluation exposes major gaps between plausible local output and reliable behavior. PAWBench (2608.27345) finds probability and support mismatch in repeated video rollouts; UrbanGround (2608.27456) finds near-zero long-route, closure, and pedestrian-safe success despite much stronger local perception.
- Model-generated supervision is used in several distinct settings: mathematical test-time optimization (2608.27448), evolution strategies for reasoning coverage (2608.27351), weak-model critique retrieval (2608.27455), and teacher-free flow-model alignment (2608.26872).
- Long-video editing advances along two operational axes: shot-aware offline editing (2608.26809) and causal live-stream character editing (2608.27123). Both report improved consistency, but rely heavily on author-created evaluations.
- Explicit intermediates help selectively. CaRGo-T (2608.23172) uses symbolic cause–effect graphs; Aphanta (2608.26993) finds useful headroom from edited images but weak realization on exact structural transformations and external transfer.
- Production-oriented 3D work emphasizes explicit structure: Procedura (2608.26238) generates editable mechanical assemblies, while Luce (2608.23943) generates geometry and PBR material channels for relighting.
- The day also includes a methodological warning about evaluation evidence: 2608.19269 finds that 110 of 124 eligible Inspect Evals units could not reach deterministic claim analysis because required evidence or semantic bindings were missing.

## Main results worth noting

- **2608.27345 — probability alignment:** Across 11 generators, 25 calibration scenes, and 50 rollouts per model–scene pair, observed conditional TVD averaged 31.2, compared with 8.33 for matched samples from the reference distributions. Because TVD conditions on readable, in-schema outputs, scene pass rate must be reported alongside it.

- **2608.27456 — composition failure in city navigation:** Ten MLLMs achieved 75.0%–93.8% on four-choice local recognition but only 0.0%–3.8% on long navigation under a 100-step limit. Road-closure success was 0.0%–3.3%, and pedestrian collision rates were 76.3%–90.0%.

- **2608.27448 — label-free math adaptation:** On three test-time-training benchmarks using Avg@12, TTPO improved Qwen3-1.7B from 38.0 to 45.2, 4B from 57.4 to 61.1, and 8B from 60.7 to 65.3. These results use \(K=64\) rollouts and do not establish efficiency at smaller budgets.

- **2608.27351 — optimizer trade-off rather than universal winner:** In the hard mathematical setting, GRPO led Pass@1 at 52.9 versus ES at 49.9, while ES led Pass@16/32 at 75.0/78.9 versus 74.7/78.0. The result supports retaining sample-count-qualified metrics.

- **2608.27455 — small but efficient inference gains:** Qwen2.5-72B CritICL-static reached 59.2% macro Pass@1 across five math benchmarks versus 59.0% for Consistency@5. Only the MATH and macro differences were significant at \(p<.05\); the static method used one generation and 3,768 tokens on the reported MATH cost comparison.

- **2608.26872 — teacher-free image alignment:** Mixed Self-OPD reported 0.9521 strict GenEval and 0.9597 OCR versus DiffusionOPD’s 0.9150 and 0.9464. On a separate aesthetic set, however, Self-OPD’s 23.39 PickScore and 0.3415 HPSv2 trailed DiffusionOPD’s 23.95 and 0.3729.

- **2608.25518 — trace value under scarce labels:** With eight labeled target examples per engine, UWDP protocol features reached 0.719±0.094 Spearman versus 0.159±0.168 for final-snapshot metadata, aggregated over eight seeds and two target engines. This is stronger evidence for trace usefulness than for broad world-model scaling.

- **2608.15763 — harness adaptation:** On 978 real interactions with augmented harnesses, HAT scored 94.6 average versus 88.2 for fixed-harness SFT. On official IFEval, HAT reached 83.5/88.7 IFE-P/IFE-I versus 73.8/82.4 after fixed-harness SFT.

- **2608.26530 and 2608.27454 — persistent procedural knowledge:** PILOT’s best observed Terminal-Bench 2.0 result rose from 66.3% to 80.9% over 20 repeated sweeps for GLM-5.1, while WikiSkill led the five-task average for all five tested models by 3.3–12.0 points over each model’s strongest skill baseline. PILOT does not yet establish held-out transfer; WikiSkill shows both positive and severely negative cross-model transfer.

- **2608.25500 — scalable retrieval:** Across six models, CaSKG averaged 86.79% ALFWorld success versus 80.01% for Graph-of-Skills and used fewer environment steps in every model–benchmark pairing. The evaluation does not measure token, latency, or graph-construction costs.

- **2608.26200 — world supervision for game control:** GameWAM reached 50.7% MCU Mini average success; removing future-video supervision reduced it to 35.7%, with combat falling from 39.0% to 13.0%. External leaderboard comparisons remain confounded, but the internal supervision ablation is directly informative.

- **2608.26103 — video-conditioned robot task transfer:** Across seven task-held-out RoboTwin tasks, Zero-WAM achieved 46.95%±0.72 macro success versus 17.45%±1.40 for LingBot-VA. Removing in-context future prediction reduced the average to 28.55%.

- **2608.25798 — execution-time touch:** TacForcing averaged 65% success across six simulated tasks and 69% across three real tasks. Adding its execution-aware tactile attention raised the reported ablation averages from 51% to 60% in simulation and 48% to 69% in the real world.

- **2608.27168 and 2608.27123 — streaming systems:** Magpie generated regular 20-frame chunks in about 620 ms on one H100, but action-aligned feedback began after about 1.55 s. EditaLive achieved 14.47 FPS with 0.829-s inter-chunk latency; replacing Align Forcing with Self Forcing reduced long-video ID-SIM from 0.492 to 0.252 and success from 0.817 to 0.367.

- **2608.26809 — multi-shot editing:** MMLVE-Agent scored 81.84 overall versus 79.47 for Seedance 2.0 on 25 approximately one-minute videos. It led consistency and instruction execution but trailed Seedance slightly on preservation, 81.68 versus 82.25.

- **2608.23172 and 2608.26993 — intermediates:** CaRGo-T improved automatic humor metrics, but GPT-4o detection accuracy remained below 50% on the two reported detection datasets. Aphanta improved a selected positive-task subset from 0.343 direct to 0.445 with actual edits, while ideal references reached 0.558; all tested actual-edit pipelines hurt BabyVision.

- **2608.26238 and 2608.23943 — explicit 3D assets:** Procedura scored 0.590±0.009 on the 203-case P3D-Bench assembly judge versus 0.524±0.012 for the strongest full-coverage non-Procedura entry. Luce reported Toys4K rendered-view FID 20.99 versus 29.22 for TRELLIS 2, while TRELLIS 2 remained better on some material-reconstruction metrics.

- **2608.19269 — evaluation replayability:** At the pinned Inspect Evals commit, only 14 of 124 eligible units reached deterministic claim analysis. Among 103 outcome-blind census-review stops, 47 stopped at evidence binding and 35 at comparative observations. Completed cases also showed that winners may remain stable even when exact values or complete rankings do not.

- **2608.27260 — conceptual rather than empirical result:** ACE argues that validity should gate admission, complexity should be defined relative to a declared learner and execution configuration, and diversity should measure valid behavioral coverage. No controlled ACE-selection experiment is reported.

## Open research directions

1. **Test whether probabilistic alignment predicts planning quality** (2608.27345). Compare planners using generators with different TVD, coverage, and scene-pass profiles under asymmetric outcome costs; measure decision regret rather than video plausibility alone.

2. **Add memory to city-scale agents without introducing stale-state hazards** (2608.27456). Factorially vary landmark/pose memory, read-time conflict filtering, route waypoints, and recovery feedback; report LongNav completion, endpoint regression, collision rate, and stale-memory errors.

3. **Disentangle visual prediction from data and compute in world–action models** (2608.26200, 2608.26103). Hold trajectories, token exposure, architecture capacity, and optimization budget fixed while varying future-video objectives, prompt modality, and action-only training.

4. **Evaluate persistent skills on genuinely held-out tasks and growing libraries** (2608.26530, 2608.27454, 2608.25500). Evolve skills on one partition, freeze the harness, and test retrieval and execution on unseen tasks under fixed context and construction budgets.

5. **Measure test-time optimization on an accuracy–compute frontier** (2608.27448, 2608.27351, 2608.27455). Compare TTPO, ES/GRPO schedules, CritICL, and sampling baselines at matched generated tokens, wall time, and hardware; retain Pass@1 and Pass@K separately.

6. **Validate model-derived rewards with independent human or held-out evaluators** (2608.26872, 2608.26809, 2608.26238, 2608.23172). Use blinded human judgments and scorers excluded from training and pipeline control; test whether rankings survive evaluator substitution.

7. **Identify when visual intermediates causally help** (2608.26993, 2608.23172). Compare actual edits or graphs with matched second-pass reasoning, sham intermediates, identity transforms, and hidden-return controls; then test a learned router on preregistered unseen tasks.

8. **Reduce streaming latency while preserving long-term identity and structure** (2608.27168, 2608.27123). Compare chunked and frame-streamed generation with overlapped encoding, richer engine buffers, and persistent 3D or identity memory at matched resolution and quality.

9. **Establish cross-sensor and cross-embodiment tactile robustness** (2608.25798). Sweep block size, tactile delay, noise, dropped updates, sensor type, and robot morphology while reporting success, control frequency, latency, and compute.

10. **Prospectively test evaluation-release contracts** (2608.19269, with relevance to all empirical papers). Randomize new evaluations to legacy versus claim-replay-complete releases and measure replay completion, audit time, stable claims, and disagreements among independent auditors.

Low-confidence or particularly qualified conclusions include Self-OPD’s internally inconsistent headline/configuration values, Aphanta’s post-selected aggregate, Magpie’s mainly qualitative quality evidence, and broad causal interpretations of CaSKG’s textual counterfactual edges.

Historical evidence was service-cached and often truncated. The retained cross-day edges use only relationships still supported by the visible evidence at both endpoints; none relies solely on a listing summary. Historical links are therefore editorial comparisons or follow-up opportunities, not claims of citation, chronology, influence, or dependency.
