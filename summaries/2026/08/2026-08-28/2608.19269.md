# What Does an Evaluation License? A Commit-Bound Census of Claim-Relative Inference in Inspect Evals

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19269) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19269)


## One-sentence takeaway

In a pinned 124-unit Inspect Evals census, 110 evaluations lacked evidence or semantic bindings needed to replay their claims, while executable cases showed that winners can remain stable even when exact values or complete rankings do not.

## Problem and motivation

Evaluation artifacts specify what was run and scored, but may not preserve enough historical evidence or semantic context to determine whether an associated value, winner, or ranking survives other grounded interpretations. The authors identify a missing “claim-replay” layer connecting task/scorer artifacts to historical observations, admissible evaluation meanings, and a typed claim query. This matters because treating unavailable evidence as a score, or collapsing every interpretation into one sensitivity set, can obscure both non-replayable claims and conclusions that remain stable at a coarser resolution (Sections 1–2; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Claim-relative formalization:** The paper represents an audit through a frozen historical substrate \(D\), a grounded specification family \(F\), and a claim query \(q\). The identified set contains the claim outcomes produced by all admitted specifications. It separately models a primary family and a broader review envelope containing disputed specifications (Section 2.1, Equations 1–3).

2. **Commit-bound census:** It applies a mechanical eligibility rule to Inspect Evals at commit `32b79a2`, producing 124 eligible units from 129 candidates and assigning every eligible unit either a completed audit or a typed `STOP` with the first missing object and minimum unblock (Section 3.1; Appendix B.1).

3. **Resolution-aware audit output:** For executable cases, the audit reports exact values where commensurate, directions, winners, complete weak orders, pairwise stability, and two-specification instability witnesses instead of one robust/non-robust label (Sections 2.1 and 5.1).

4. **Executable release contract:** The proposed schema binds claims, evidence, candidate admission, support and aggregation decisions, exposure/freeze information, and either identified-set outputs or actionable stop records. Arithmetic after human admission decisions is deterministic and independently recomputed (Section 5; Table 18; Figure 11).

5. **Reproducibility artifact:** The authors report an ancillary archive containing census summaries, audit records, source-table recomputations, a contract implementation, checksums, and result-profile verification (page 2).

The evidence establishes terminal auditability accounting within the pinned frame and case-specific claim behavior for explicitly enumerated families. It also demonstrates that coarse conclusions can survive finer ranking instability.

It does **not** establish ecosystem-wide prevalence, complete or uniquely correct semantic families, benchmark validity, independent replication, or a causal benefit from adopting the proposed contract. The purposive and outcome-exposed cases cannot be pooled with the frozen random draw (Sections 3.2, 6.2; Table 6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated—commit-bound scope:** The 124-unit result applies only to the mechanically eligible frame at one repository commit, not to Inspect Evals generally or the broader evaluation ecosystem (Section 6.2).

- **Author-stated—finite-family relativity:** Every identified set is conditional on the specifications that auditors enumerated and admitted; the method does not certify semantic truth or family completeness (Sections 5 and 6.2).

- **Author-stated—selection licenses differ:** The three deep audits are purposive, and eight exploratory cases were selected after outcome exposure. They explain mechanisms but do not support prevalence estimates or independent replication (Sections 3.2 and 6.2).

- **Author-stated—historical precision:** When the substrate is a published table, “exact” refers to those frozen displayed values rather than latent unrounded results (Section 2.1; Appendix A.1).

- **Author-stated—no prospective utility evidence:** The retrospective census does not show how many stops the contract would prevent, how much audit time it would save, or whether it would improve decisions (Section 6.1; Appendix K).

- **Author-stated—human semantic boundary:** Auditors choose endpoint types, comparable support, and A/D/X admission. Deterministic execution verifies consequences of those choices but does not remove judgment from family construction (Section 5).

- **Analyst inference—adjudication sensitivity remains:** Because admission decisions determine the inferential domain, alternative qualified auditors could plausibly form different primary or review families. The supplied text defines rationale fields but reports no inter-rater agreement study.

- **Analyst inference—raw-data verification is uneven:** Some completed audits rely on frozen summary vectors or displayed tables rather than raw outputs or trajectories, limiting checks of sample-level facts and unrounded calculations; the paper explicitly records such residual missing material in Appendices E and G.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the contract improve prospective replayability?** The paper supplies only retrospective stop accounting. A preregistered trial could compare new contract-assisted and legacy releases on replay completion, time-to-audit, and downstream decision changes, as proposed in Figure 12.

2. **How reproducible is semantic admission across auditors?** Human A/D/X judgments define the primary and review families, but inter-rater consistency is not reported. A blinded multi-auditor study could measure agreement and compare resulting identified sets.

3. **How much semantic-family coverage is sufficient?** Results are relative to enumerated candidates, and completeness is explicitly not certified. A next experiment could use staged independent candidate elicitation and measure how often claim images or stable backbones change as new specifications are added.

4. **Which release artifacts most efficiently prevent typed stops?** The census identifies missing evidence categories but not intervention effects. A factorial release study could add logs, judge traces, state manifests, support ledgers, or semantic registries separately and measure their marginal effect on replay completion.
