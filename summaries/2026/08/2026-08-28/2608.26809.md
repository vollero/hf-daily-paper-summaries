# Thinking on Shots: Consistent Multi-Shot Video Editing with Agentic Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26809) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26809)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors introduce an agentic, shot-aware pipeline for applying multiple instructions to minute-long videos, reporting better overall benchmark and human-preference results than fixed-duration chunking baselines while relying heavily on proprietary components and VLM-based judging.

## Problem and motivation

Existing video editors primarily handle short, single-shot clips with simple instructions. Applying them to long videos by fixed-duration chunking can edit shots where the target is absent, produce inconsistent entity appearances across cuts, and disrupt backgrounds or chronology.

The authors formalize Multi-Instruction Multi-Shot Long-Video Editing (MMLVE) around three requirements: cross-shot editing consistency (CSEC), independent execution of multiple instructions (MID), and preservation of non-target content and temporal structure (ZDSS) (Introduction; Task Definition; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task formulation:** MMLVE maps heterogeneous instructions to sparsely occurring entities in physical shots, subject to CSEC, MID, and ZDSS constraints (Task Definition).
- **Shot-aware agentic pipeline:** PyDetect segments physical shots; an LLM parses instructions; and a VLM analyzes shots, retrieves target occurrences through three-way voting, and triggers editing only when at least two votes are positive (Overall Architecture; Figure 2).
- **Global Memory Card:** Six retrieved keyframes are used to produce a before/after visual reference that conditions edits across shots, providing a common appearance anchor (Global Memory Card Making).
- **Pos-Neg Editing Feedback:** Image- and video-level VLM evaluators generate both preservation and correction prompts for up to three attempts (P-NEF; Equations 1–2; Figures 3 and 6).
- **Evaluation assets:** MMLVE-Bench contains 25 approximately one-minute videos, about five instructions per video, and tailored CSEC, MID, and ZDSS scoring criteria (MMLVE-Bench; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best overall automated score | MMLVE-Agent scores **81.84**, versus Seedance 2.0 **79.47**, Kling o3 **70.57**, and HappyHorse 1.0 **69.61**, on MMLVE-Bench using Gemini 3.5 Flash as judge. | Table 1; Quantitative Evaluation |
| Highest CSEC and MID | MMLVE-Agent obtains **84.80 CSEC** and **79.04 MID**; the strongest baseline scores are **77.58 CSEC** and **78.58 MID**, both from Seedance 2.0. | Table 1 |
| Not best on ZDSS | MMLVE-Agent scores **81.68 ZDSS**, below Seedance 2.0 at **82.25**, but above Kling o3 at **69.13** and HappyHorse 1.0 at **66.88**. The authors attribute Seedance’s result to conservative missed edits. | Table 1; Quantitative Evaluation |
| Strong expert preference | Across **45 rankings** from nine expert evaluators, MMLVE-Agent is ranked first in **75.6%**, top-two in **93.3%**, and has average rank **1.36**, versus baseline ranks of **2.63–3.16**. | Appendix A; Table 2 |
| High pairwise preference | Reported head-to-head preference is **88.4%** over Seedance 2.0, **80.5%** over Kling o3, and **93.3%** over HappyHorse; paired tests are reported as Bonferroni-corrected **p<0.001**. | Appendix A, Results and Analysis |
| Positive-feedback ablation is qualitative | Figures show P-NEF preserving already-correct attributes better than negative-only feedback; no numerical effect size or attempt counts are reported. | Figures 3 and 6; Ablation Study |
| Performance varies substantially by case | MMLVE-Agent’s per-case scores range as low as **41 CSEC, 36 MID, and 30 ZDSS** on `sub_025`, despite several perfect cases. | Appendix D; Table 3 |

The evidence supports superior average performance under the paper’s benchmark, judge, and baseline adaptation, reinforced by a small blinded expert study. It does not establish superiority on broader datasets, against purpose-built open systems, or under an evaluator independent of the VLM family used inside the pipeline. The claim that editing hallucinations are “eliminated” is stronger than the reported evidence: low per-scene MID and ZDSS scores show remaining failures.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Analyst inference — narrow benchmark:** Evaluation uses 25 approximately one-minute clips derived from UniVA-Bench, limiting evidence about other durations, genres, resolutions, or instruction distributions (MMLVE-Bench).
- **Analyst inference — evaluator coupling:** Gemini 3.5 Flash serves both as several pipeline agents and as the automated judge, creating a possible shared-model bias; no alternative automated judge is reported (Implementation Details; Quantitative Evaluation).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablations:** P-NEF is compared only qualitatively with negative-only feedback. The text provides no quantitative ablations isolating shot segmentation, voting, Global Memory Card, image-level feedback, or video-level feedback (Figures 3 and 6).
- **Analyst inference — baseline adaptation may disadvantage competitors:** Every baseline receives the full prompt on fixed-duration chunks, whereas MMLVE-Agent gets instruction parsing, entity retrieval, and shot-aware routing (Baselines).
- **Analyst inference — missing cases affect comparability:** Safety-rejected baseline cases are excluded from metric means, and the exact number differs by method; this complicates direct aggregate comparisons (Table 1; Table 4).
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility constraints:** Core components—Gemini 3.5 Flash, Nano Banana 2, HappyHorse, Seedance, and Kling—are described as closed or externally hosted models, while prompts, costs, API versions, and runtime are not supplied.
- **Analyst inference — small human study:** Nine experts each evaluated five cases, producing 45 rankings; the supplied text does not report inter-rater agreement or whether cases received equal numbers of ratings (Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **Which modules produce the gains?** The paper lacks quantitative component ablations. A factorial study could remove instruction parsing, voting, the Global Memory Card, and each P-NEF loop separately while measuring all three metrics and edit-attempt counts.

2. **Does the method generalize beyond the 25 curated clips?** Broader validity remains untested. A next experiment could evaluate longer videos and new genres with varied shot density, entity sparsity, and instruction counts.

3. **How robust are the scores to judge choice?** The pipeline and automated evaluation share Gemini 3.5 Flash. Re-scoring with multiple independent VLMs and human annotations could measure judge agreement and ranking stability.

4. **Can consistency improve without sacrificing preservation?** MMLVE-Agent leads CSEC and MID but trails Seedance 2.0 slightly on ZDSS. A controlled experiment could vary retrieval thresholds and feedback acceptance criteria to estimate the execution–preservation trade-off.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, dataset size, benchmark scores, and user-study results because the supplied text includes detailed methodology and appendices.

Material missing information includes public-release and licensing status; exact prompts and API configurations; cost, latency, and token usage; independent judge validation; inter-rater agreement; quantitative component ablations; complete runtime comparisons; dataset demographics and licensing; and evaluation beyond the 25 curated cases.
