# Procedura: Agentic 3D Modeling with Procedural Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26238) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26238)


## One-sentence takeaway

Procedura uses a frozen LLM to construct editable, part-structured CSG assemblies through typed mates and verified part-by-part generation, achieving the highest judged quality reported on MechBench-36 and P3D-Bench under the paper’s shared evaluation harness.

## Problem and motivation

Existing native 3D generators can produce visually convincing meshes, but the authors argue that their geometry remains unsuitable for many mechanical assets: edges are rounded, semantic parts are absent, and dimensions are not directly editable. Existing program-generating agents offer parametric output but tend to produce simplified objects because they write many parts and placements at once, often using guessed transforms without explicit relationships between parts (Introduction; Sec. 2).

The paper targets text-driven generation of precise, detailed mechanical objects that are simultaneously editable, meaningfully decomposed, connected, and suitable for materials and articulation.

## Approach and contributions

- **Procedural assembly representation:** Each object is a parametric CSG program with named part modules connected by typed, machine-checkable mates. Static mates determine placement, while kinematic mates encode intended motion (Sec. 3.1; Fig. 4).
- **Plan-and-build agent:** A frozen LLM first creates an ordered assembly graph, then writes one part per call. Placement is solved from mate frames rather than emitted as a raw transform (Secs. 3.2–3.3; Eq. 1).
- **Deterministic verification:** Compile, mate-registration, penetration, and connectivity checks guard part commits. A span-based terminal gate detects visually significant disconnected components (Secs. 3.3–3.4; Eqs. 2–4).
- **Decoupled refinement:** A separate vision-critic call diagnoses defects, and a code-enforced latch permits exactly one edit per fresh diagnosis (Sec. 3.4).
- **Production-oriented outputs:** The assembly graph supports per-part PBR materials and exports articulated OpenUSD and URDF assets validated in Isaac Sim (Secs. 3.5–3.6).
- **Evaluation asset:** The authors introduce MechBench-36, a fixed benchmark of 36 many-part hard-surface objects (Sec. 4.1).

These results support the authors’ claim that the complete pipeline improves judged hard-surface quality over the evaluated alternatives under one controlled harness. They also show that the clean refinement ablation contributes to quality and that many generated articulations survive simulator checks.

They do not establish superiority for organic objects, independent human preference, production deployment, or evaluation outside the two benchmarks. P3D-Bench gains over published systems also partly reflect stronger base models, as the authors explicitly acknowledge in Sec. 4.3.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Render-only perception can miss occluded interiors and fine contact geometry; the authors suggest depth maps, cross-sections, or mesh queries (Sec. 5).
- CSG is less natural for organic and freeform surfaces than for mechanical hard-surface objects (Sec. 5).
- The mate vocabulary covers only rigid joints, and undeclared mates cannot be verified (Sec. 5).
- The refinement case study reaches its cycle limit before a final diagnosis, illustrating linear progress under the one-diagnosis/one-edit rule (Sec. 4.4; Fig. 11).
- Material quality has no quantitative evaluation because no ground-truth material map is available (Sec. 4.5).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The vision judge uses Gemini 3.7 Flash, which is also the default generation model. Although judging is blinded, the text does not provide human-judge validation or an analysis of model-family bias (Sec. 4.1).
- MechBench-36 is small and author-created, with no ground-truth geometry; its results may not generalize beyond its 36 mechanical prompts (Secs. 4.1–4.2).
- The planning and 3D-feedback ablations also omit refinement, so their independent causal contributions cannot be extracted from Table 3.
- Only 18 objects were articulated, rather than the full 36-object benchmark, and the selection procedure is not given (Sec. 4.5).
- The source names an in-house LLM harness but does not provide code-release, prompt, runtime, cost, or repeated-run variance details, limiting reproducibility assessment (Sec. 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the agent detect defects hidden from rendered views?** This remains open because the authors identify occluded interiors and fine contacts as blind spots. A next experiment could compare RGB-only feedback with depth maps, cross-sections, and direct mesh-query tools on objects with annotated internal defects.

2. **How well does the representation extend beyond hard-surface objects?** CSG is acknowledged to fit organic geometry poorly. A plausible experiment would add a hybrid CSG/freeform representation and evaluate it on matched mechanical and organic prompt sets.

3. **What is each pipeline component’s independent contribution?** Table 3 cleanly isolates refinement but confounds planning and 3D-feedback removal with the absence of refinement. A factorial ablation retaining refinement across all combinations would separate these effects.

4. **Can articulation reliability scale to the full benchmark and richer joint types?** Only 18 assets were articulated, and the mate vocabulary covers rigid joints. Testing every MechBench-36 object plus deformable or compliant mechanisms would measure broader validity.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported within-paper results, because the supplied text includes the full method, evaluation protocol, tables, ablations, and limitations.

Material missing or not established includes public availability of code and MechBench-36, exact prompts and full benchmark cases, compute cost and runtime, stochastic repeated-run variance, human evaluation, benchmark-selection rationale beyond the stated criteria, articulation subset selection, and quantitative material-quality evaluation.
