# CritICL: Inference-Time Weak-to-Strong Generalization from Small Language Model Failure Modes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27455) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27455)


## One-sentence takeaway

CritICL retrieves offline critiques aligned with failure patterns observed in smaller same-family models, improving stronger models’ reasoning with one target-model generation for its static variant and two for its dynamic variant.

## Problem and motivation

Inference-time reasoning methods often depend on repeated sampling, refinement, or external judging, increasing generation and token costs. Existing weak-to-strong inference approaches may likewise require weak-model supervision for every query and rely on the quality of direct weak-model outputs. The authors identify an underused signal: weaker and stronger models within a family exhibit similar, structured failure-mode distributions, which may provide reusable guidance without parameter updates or repeated online supervision (Introduction; Sections 4.1 and B.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **CritBank:** An offline repository built from incorrect chain-of-thought responses generated by small models. Each entry contains a question, incorrect response, failure-mode labels, and natural-language critique; labels are clustered, and GPT-4o-mini supplies the labels and critiques by default (Section 2.1).
- **CritICL-dynamic:** The target model predicts up to five likely failure modes for each query, after which up to five aligned critique examples are retrieved and used as in-context guidance. This requires two model generations (Sections 2.2 and G.3).
- **CritICL-static:** An aggregated, model-family-specific failure profile determines which critiques to retrieve. It is query-independent and needs only one target-model generation (Sections 2.2 and G.4).
- **Empirical contribution:** The paper quantifies strong within-family similarity between weak- and strong-model failure distributions, evaluates math and GPQA reasoning, and provides retrieval, taxonomy, annotation, transfer, source-of-gain, cost, and statistical analyses (Sections B–E).

The evidence supports improved accuracy over standard ICL and favorable per-query efficiency relative to multi-generation baselines in the tested models and benchmarks. It also supports an association between within-family failure-profile similarity and successful retrieval. It does **not** establish that shared architecture or training choices causally produce transferable failures, nor that CritICL always beats the strongest baseline on every benchmark. The cost tables exclude a consolidated end-to-end accounting of CritBank’s offline construction expense.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Same-family transfer is the primary setting; cross-family profiles correlate less strongly and yield smaller improvements (Section D.1; Tables 4 and 10).
- **Author-stated uncertainty:** Only the MATH and macro-average improvements reach \(p<.05\); GSM8K, AMC23, and both AIME differences do not. Small AIME sets produce wide confidence intervals (Section C.3; Table 9).
- **Author-stated mechanism uncertainty:** Shared inductive biases are proposed only as a hypothesis, not a causal or mechanistic explanation (Section B.2).
- **Offline expense:** CritBank requires weak-model generations plus critique and label annotation. The authors argue that this can be amortized, but do not provide a complete construction-cost figure in the supplied text (Section F.1).
- **Annotation dependence:** Most labels and critiques come from GPT-4o-mini. The validation covers only 300 independently LLM-annotated and 100 human-annotated examples, so reliability over the full bank is not directly measured (Sections 2.1 and B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation covers two model families and primarily structured reasoning benchmarks. This does not establish effectiveness for open-ended generation, multilingual use, interactive agents, or modalities beyond text.
- **Analyst inference:** Greedy decoding makes results reproducible for fixed prompts but leaves sensitivity to prompt wording, retrieval ties, and alternative decoding policies insufficiently characterized.
- **Analyst inference:** The static method’s benefit assumes that historical weak-model failure frequencies remain relevant to the target model and deployment distribution; distribution drift is not evaluated.

## Open research questions

1. **What causally creates shared failure structures across scale?** Section B.2 offers shared architecture and training choices only as a hypothesis. A next experiment could compare controlled model pairs that vary one factor at a time—such as tokenizer, instruction tuning, or architecture—and measure Table 4’s correlation statistics.

2. **How robust is CritICL under deployment-domain drift?** Domain-specific CritBanks outperform cross-domain ones, while mixed banks remain close (Table 11). A next experiment could progressively shift the test distribution and measure when static profiles become stale relative to dynamic retrieval.

3. **Can annotation and construction costs be reduced without losing accuracy?** CritBank currently requires multiple weak-model responses and frontier-model annotations. A next experiment could vary responses per question, annotation-model strength, and bank size while reporting accuracy alongside total offline and amortized cost.

4. **When should static and dynamic retrieval be combined?** Static is cheaper, while dynamic is query-adaptive, but the paper does not establish a routing rule. A next experiment could train or calibrate a confidence-based router and compare accuracy, generations, and total tokens against both fixed variants.
