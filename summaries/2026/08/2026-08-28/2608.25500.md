# CaSKG: Counterfactual-Causal Skill Graphs for Scalable Agent Skill Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.25500) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25500)


## One-sentence takeaway

CaSKG improves agent skill retrieval by validating candidate graph edges with textual counterfactual probes before graph propagation, outperforming three retrieval alternatives across six LLMs on two interactive benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation

Large reusable skill libraries create a retrieval tradeoff: exposing the full library is costly and noisy, vector retrieval misses procedural dependencies, and graph retrieval can propagate relevance through unreliable associations. The authors identify edge reliability as the key unresolved bottleneck: exhaustive validation is quadratic, but publishing weak edges can introduce irrelevant or operationally unsuitable skills (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Budgeted edge calibration:** The paper formulates skill-graph construction as deciding which candidate relations to assess and how their confidence should govern propagation (Section 1).
- **Multi-signal candidate induction:** CaSKG constructs a directed, high-recall graph from lexical, semantic, input/output, structural, and available repair evidence; an optional LLM judge refines scores (Section 3.2).
- **Counterfactual probing:** Selected edges undergo source removal, dissimilar-source substitution, and order reversal tests intended to measure necessity, specificity, and directionality (Section 3.3).
- **Bayesian state-gated publication:** Beta-smoothed probe evidence assigns edges to confirmed, uncertain, rejected, or unvalidated states; only suitably weighted edges and a bounded scaffold enter the published graph (Section 3.4).
- **Policy-independent retrieval:** At runtime, lexical and semantic seeds initialize personalized PageRank over the frozen graph, without altering the downstream policy or task interface (Section 3.5).

These results establish consistent performance and observed interaction-step advantages under the reported fixed-cohort benchmark protocol. The ablations support contributions from multi-signal induction, judge-assisted scoring, and selective publication, but do not isolate every counterfactual probe or prove that the learned edges represent real causal relations. The evidence also does not establish improvements in token cost, latency, construction cost, or real-world deployment.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments are confined to simulated ALFWorld and ScienceWorld environments; safeguards would still be required when connecting agents to external tools or real environments (Ethics and Privacy Statement).
- **Author-stated measurement boundary:** `Steps` measures environment interactions, not tokens, wall-clock time, retrieval latency, or offline graph-construction cost (Section 4.1).
- **Author-stated scale caveat:** The scale study is descriptive; MiniMax’s validation budget changes from 500 relations at 200–1,000 skills to 2,000 at the 2,000-skill setting, and archived Qwen GoS aggregates are not episode directories (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference — causal validity:** The “causal” evidence consists of LLM judgments over textual perturbations rather than interventions in the environment or comparison with ground-truth dependency edges. Operational causality is therefore not independently validated.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablation:** Table 3 does not separately remove the removal, substitution, or reordering probes, nor compare alternative smoothing and publication thresholds. Their individual contributions remain unclear.
- **Analyst inference — uncertainty:** The supplied text reports aggregate outcomes but no repeated-run variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests, limiting assessment of statistical stability.
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility:** Equations describe the framework, but the supplied text does not enumerate all concrete signal weights, thresholds, scaffold rules, prompts, or model settings needed to reconstruct the reported graph from the paper alone.

## Open research questions

1. **Do textual probe scores correspond to genuine operational dependency?** No edge-level ground truth or environment intervention is reported. A next experiment could compare calibrated edge states against expert-labeled dependencies and controlled execution where source skills are actually removed or reordered.

2. **Which counterfactual probe contributes most?** The component study evaluates the combined correction pipeline but not individual probe types. A factorial ablation could test removal, substitution, and reordering alone and in combinations under an identical validation budget.

3. **How does performance scale under a fixed validation budget?** The 2,000-skill MiniMax experiment increases the assessed-edge budget, confounding library scale with validation effort. A next study could sweep library size while holding both the absolute and proportional budgets fixed.

4. **Does CaSKG improve total system efficiency?** Only environment interactions are measured. Profiling offline graph construction, LLM-judge calls, retrieval latency, context tokens, and end-to-end cost would test whether step savings outweigh calibration overhead.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, benchmark results, and stated evaluation scope because the supplied text includes the complete method, tables, and experimental discussion.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty or significance tests, ground-truth edge-quality evaluation, per-probe ablations, exact operational prompts and all hyperparameter values, token and latency costs, offline construction expense, and evidence from real-world agents or environments.
