# Training Agents to Evolve with Their Harness: TaoLive Digital Avatar Agent Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15763) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15763)


## One-sentence takeaway

Harness-Aware Training exposes a compact live-commerce agent to changing skills, tools, prompts, and hooks during SFT and RL, improving adaptation to evaluated harness edits while preserving instruction following and meeting low-concurrency production latency targets.

## Problem and motivation

Live-streaming digital-avatar agents must answer accurately, use commerce tools, adapt quickly to changing business rules, and respond with low latency. The authors argue that large general models can interpret changed runtime configurations but are too slow, whereas compact models trained on a fixed configuration memorize identifiers and templates and fail after harness updates. Fixed-Harness SFT also reduced IFEval prompt-level accuracy by 7.7 points in their experiments. Test-time adaptation and retraining after every edit are presented as incompatible with the application’s latency and update frequency (Introduction; §3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evolvable Harness:** The policy model is separated from independently versioned Skills, tool definitions, prompt assembly, and lifecycle Hooks. A developer-reviewed diagnose–edit–evaluate loop changes these modules without updating model weights (§2; Figs. 1–2).

- **Harness-State Augmentation:** HSA applies task-preserving changes to skill identifiers and content, tool definitions, prompt structure and constraints, and Hook behavior, intended to prevent surface-form memorization (§3.2; Fig. 3).

- **Three-stage Harness-Aware Training:** HSA-SFT learns from filtered teacher trajectories across augmented environments; General On-Policy Distillation uses the base model and Tulu3 data to restore general capabilities; HSA-RL trains on policy-generated trajectories in a production-informed simulator with tool, skill, prompt, and Hook variation (§§3.3–3.4; Fig. 4).

- **Systems and evaluation assets:** The authors construct real and synthetic evaluations for live-stream response quality, harness variation, tool/prompt robustness, instruction following, judge calibration, and complete-agent latency, followed by human and production tests (§4; Table 2).

These results support the authors’ claim that HAT improves quality and adaptation within the tested live-commerce harness-change envelope while retaining general instruction following. They do not establish robustness to arbitrary harness changes, universal serving gains, or that HAT alone caused the production uplift: the A/B comparison changes complete systems, and its statistical details are unavailable.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Training curves and MTP adaptation traces are single-run diagnostics, not evidence of cross-seed stability, training efficiency, or causal latency improvement (§5.4; Figs. 5–7).

- **Author-stated:** MTP is beneficial mainly in the low-concurrency range; at concurrency 4–8, P95 does not improve (§5.5; Table 7).

- **Author-stated:** The online dashboard exposes neither its statistical test nor confidence intervals and records only one positively significant day per metric. Long-term effects therefore are not established (§5.8; Table 10 note).

- **Author-stated:** The production A/B test compares complete system versions, so it cannot isolate the policy, Harness, or routing configuration (§5.8).

- **Analyst inference:** Most offline domain scores depend on a proprietary Harness-based judge. Human calibration helps, but selected agreement is imperfect—90.46% for Accuracy and 82.2% for Effectiveness—and one final voter is also the SFT teacher (Appendix B.4 and C.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Synthetic robustness cases are LLM-generated from in-domain seeds, so they provide broader in-domain coverage but limited evidence for unrelated domains or fundamentally different tool ecosystems (Appendix C.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Public release of the training data, simulator, harness variants, model checkpoint, and evaluation sets is not established, limiting independent reproduction.

- **Analyst inference:** Table 4 reports cumulative, apparently single-path stage comparisons without retraining variance; causal attribution among HSA-SFT, OPD, and HSA-RL is therefore suggestive rather than fully isolated.

## Open research questions

1. **How far outside the declared augmentation envelope does adaptation persist?** The paper evaluates controlled, in-family changes. A next experiment could introduce unseen structural edits—new tool compositions, conflicting Skills, or qualitatively different Hook logic—and measure degradation by edit distance.

2. **Which HSA dimensions contribute most?** Skill, tool, prompt, and Hook perturbations are combined without a component-wise ablation. A factorial ablation could remove each augmentation family at SFT and RL stages and evaluate T2, prompt robustness, and held-out edit compliance.

3. **Are the training gains stable across seeds and compact model families?** Reported trajectories do not establish cross-seed or cross-architecture reliability. Repeating each pipeline with several seeds and multiple compact policies would quantify variance and portability.

4. **What portion of production uplift comes from HAT itself?** The A/B test changes the complete system. A multi-arm production experiment could separately vary policy training, modular Harness runtime, and routing while retaining identical traffic allocation and reporting confidence intervals.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported design and within-paper comparisons because the supplied text includes detailed methods, evaluation definitions, ablations, and deployment settings.

Material missing information includes public artifact availability; exact HSA sampling probabilities and augmentation ranges; most optimization hyperparameters and compute costs; training-seed variance; raw human-annotation protocol details such as annotator count for the blind test; complete production A/B statistical methodology, duration, confidence intervals, and daily stability; and evidence beyond live-streaming commerce or outside the specified harness-change envelope.
