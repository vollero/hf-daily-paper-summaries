# PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27345) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27345)


## One-sentence takeaway

PAWBench finds that eleven video generators can produce plausible futures but do not reliably recover both the valid outcomes and their scene-conditioned probabilities under repeated, fixed-condition rollouts.

## Problem and motivation

Video generators are increasingly treated as world models, yet existing evaluations mainly judge individual outputs for plausibility, coherence, or instruction following. The authors argue that stochastic world modeling instead requires matching the conditional distribution of futures under the same initial observation and action—both its support and the probability assigned to each outcome. This “probabilistic alignment” matters for planning because decisions depend on the likelihoods of possible consequences, not merely whether one plausible consequence can be rendered (Introduction, pp. 1–2; Fig. 1).

The identified gap is the lack of evaluation that repeatedly samples an unchanged condition and tests the resulting outcome distribution rather than isolated videos (Introduction, p. 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Formal criterion:** The paper defines a model-induced terminal-outcome distribution \(p_M(y\mid x,a)\). Probability-mass alignment requires equality with a known reference distribution; support alignment requires recovering the complete valid outcome set (§2, p. 3).

- **PAWBench:** A manually curated benchmark of 50 fixed-image, fixed-action scenarios across eight physical-mechanism groups. PAW-Calibration contains 25 scenarios with analytically or symmetry-derived probabilities; PAW-Coverage contains 25 scenarios with enumerable outcomes but no defensible probabilities (§3.1–3.2, pp. 3–5; Fig. 2).

- **PAWEval:** A rubric-based protocol that uses repeated rollouts and a Gemini 3.5 Flash judge to map readable, in-schema videos to terminal outcomes. It reports conditional total variation distance, support coverage, and a separate scene pass rate for outcome readability (§3.3–4.1, p. 5; Appendix B.1–B.3).

- **Empirical diagnosis and interventions:** Eleven generators are evaluated with 50 rollouts per scene. Additional experiments test causal versus non-causal changes, prompt steering, marginal-preserving coupled noise, and LoRA training mixtures (§4–5).

The evidence establishes a substantial distributional mismatch on PAWBench’s controlled terminal-outcome tests and indicates that neither finite sampling nor clear-case evaluator disagreement alone explains the reported calibration gap. It does not establish trajectory-level physical fidelity, performance in interactive or long-horizon environments, or universal failure across all video generators. Conditional TVD and Coverage also exclude unreadable or out-of-schema rollouts, so they must be interpreted alongside SPR.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Terminal outcomes omit intermediate dynamics and trajectory-level behavior (§7, pp. 10–11).

- **Author-stated:** Distribution estimates use finite rollouts; larger budgets improve estimation at increased cost, although they do not by themselves correct bias (§7).

- **Author-stated:** The scenarios are controlled and visually parseable, leaving longer-horizon, interactive, and embodied environments unevaluated (§7).

- **Analyst inference:** Conditional metrics can present favorable alignment among readable outcomes while many rollouts fail the gate. The paper addresses this with SPR, but any comparison based on TVD or Coverage alone would be incomplete (Appendix B.1–B.3).

- **Analyst inference:** PAWEval’s 81.3% agreement applies only to 888 mutually clear cases from a deliberately constructed 1,500-video study frame, not all benchmark outputs or model rankings (Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** The auxiliary trustworthiness audit does not affect TVD, Coverage, or SPR; consequently, a physically implausible trajectory can still contribute a valid endpoint label (Appendix B.1–B.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The training intervention covers one base model, two pencil states, and binary left/right outcomes. It supports a coarse training-frequency effect but not a general account of how training data shape stochastic video distributions (§5.3; Appendix D.3).

- **Analyst inference:** The text does not report an ablation over alternative automated judges or outcome-rubric designs, so evaluator dependence beyond the human comparison is unresolved.

## Open research questions

1. **Can alignment be evaluated over trajectories rather than terminal labels?** Terminal categories discard intermediate dynamics (§7). A next experiment could define time-indexed physical states and compare trajectory distributions while retaining a separate readability gate.

2. **Can training learn state-conditional probabilities rather than a global outcome prior?** The pencil LoRA intervention shifts both physical states in the same direction (§5.3). A next experiment could train on multiple initial tilts with independently varied target distributions and test held-out tilts.

3. **Can rollout-efficient estimators preserve benchmark conclusions?** Fifty samples per scene are costly, while fewer samples weaken support estimation (§4.3; §7). A next experiment could compare adaptive stopping or confidence-interval estimators against the \(K=50\) and \(K=100\) results.

4. **How does probabilistic alignment affect actual planning quality?** Planning motivates the criterion, but no downstream decision task is evaluated. A next experiment could compare agents planning with generators of different PAWBench profiles under asymmetric outcome costs.
