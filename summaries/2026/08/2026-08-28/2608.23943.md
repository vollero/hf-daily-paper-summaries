# Luce: Relightable Gaussians for 3D Asset Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23943) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23943)


## One-sentence takeaway

Luce generates relightable 3D assets from one image by jointly modeling geometry, albedo, metallic-roughness, and normals as voxelized Gaussian modalities, achieving lower Toys4K FID and higher alignment on the authors’ synthetic-image benchmark than the reported baselines.

## Problem and motivation

Single-image 3D generators must recover both geometry and appearance, but many representations bake illumination into color, omit explicit materials, smooth fine details, or depend on meshes. This limits relighting and compatibility with standard production renderers. The authors target this gap with a representation that jointly captures detailed geometry and explicit PBR materials while remaining compact enough for latent generative modeling (Abstract; Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multimodal PBR Gaussians:** Each occupied voxel contains geometrically independent Gaussian sets for albedo, metallic-roughness, and normals. Their rendered buffers are combined using deferred Cook–Torrance image-based lighting (Section 3.1, Equations 1–2).
- **Unified material-aware latent:** A sparse-transformer VAE compresses all three modalities together. A two-stage rectified-flow pipeline first generates the voxel structure and then jointly generates geometry and material latents from one image (Sections 3.2–3.3; Figure 3).
- **Multi-layer conditioning:** Features from DINOv2 layers 6, 12, 18, and 24 combine fine spatial cues with deeper semantics, targeting text, logos, and inscriptions (Section 3.3; Figure 4; Appendix B).
- **Dual asset decoding:** The latent produces directly renderable PBR Gaussians or an optional FlexiCubes mesh with albedo, metallic, roughness, and tangent-space normal textures (Sections 3.3–3.4).
- **Normal-detail transfer:** Decoded normal Gaussians are baked into tangent-space maps, preserving sub-voxel surface detail without increasing polygon count (Section 3.4; Figure 8).

The results support the authors’ claims of stronger rendered-view generation metrics, effective multi-layer conditioning, and improved mesh normals from normal-map transfer in the tested settings. They do not establish superior underlying 3D geometry independently of rendering, performance on natural photographs or scenes, broad material realism beyond the modeled PBR channels, or equal-compute superiority: methods use different encoders and input resolutions.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Fixed voxel resolution can under-resolve features that occupy only a few voxels.
- The material model omits subsurface scattering, anisotropy, translucency, thin-film effects, and strongly view-dependent reflectance.
- Mesh export is limited by the inherited FlexiCubes decoder and could benefit from higher-resolution or UV-aware extraction.
- The pipeline targets object-centric assets rather than complete scenes (Section 5, “Limitations and future work”).

**Analyst inferences from the reported setup:**

- Baseline compute is not strictly matched: Luce uses 1036×1036 DINOv2 conditioning, while listed baselines use different encoders and resolutions (Section 4, “Baselines”).
- The 130-image benchmark is generated by one image model and intentionally prompted for rich detail; its representativeness and release status are not established.
- Evaluation lighting can favor explicit-material methods: on Toys4K, Luce and other PBR systems relight to the evaluation environment, while baked-appearance methods retain the conditioning illumination (Appendix D).
- LiTo’s generated coordinate frame is not pose-matched to other methods for the four-view alignment evaluation, complicating direct comparison (Appendix F).
- Only two principal ablations are reported; the supplied text does not isolate the value of independent per-modality Gaussian geometry, unified versus separate modality latents, or inherited sparse-structure generation.
- The supplied tables omit their numerical bodies, so many metric values and complete baseline rankings cannot be independently summarized from the retrieved text.

## Open research questions

1. **Would adaptive spatial resolution improve thin and highly detailed objects?** Fixed voxels are an acknowledged bottleneck. A next experiment could compare the current 128³-to-64³ design against cascaded or octree decoding, stratified by feature size.

2. **How much does each representation choice contribute?** Independent Gaussian layouts and a unified multimodal latent are central claims but lack isolated ablations in the supplied text. Experiments could compare shared versus modality-specific Gaussians and joint versus separate modality latents under matched capacity.

3. **Does the method generalize to photographs and complex materials?** Evaluation covers Toys4K renders and AI-generated images, while the reflectance model excludes several material effects. A next study could use real photographs paired with scanned geometry/materials and category-specific tests for translucent, anisotropic, and view-dependent surfaces.

4. **Can comparable quality be achieved with lower inference cost?** The GS path uses 4.5B parameters and approximately 42 seconds on an H100, with mesh export substantially slower. Distillation, fewer flow steps, or faster texture baking could be evaluated against generation and per-modality reconstruction metrics.
