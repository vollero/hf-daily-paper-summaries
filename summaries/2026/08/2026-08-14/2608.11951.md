# TailBooster: A Dual-Layer Generative Framework for Extreme Value Augmentation with Operational Validity Enforcement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11951) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11951)


## One-sentence takeaway

TailBooster combines tail-specific tabular generation with data-driven validity filtering, reducing extreme-value regression MAE versus conventional synthetic training by 47–49% for air time and 29–57% for arrival delay on one month of New York State flight data.

## Problem and motivation

Extreme flight delays and abnormal air times are operationally costly but rare, leaving regression models with little training signal in distribution tails. Conventional generators both under-represent those tails and may create infeasible feature combinations, while prior extreme-value generators largely assume homogeneous continuous data and domain-constrained methods require hand-crafted rules. The authors target the unsupported combination of mixed-type tabular tail augmentation and fully data-driven operational-validity enforcement (Sections 1 and 2.5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Tail-focused generation:** IQR/Tukey fences isolate an extreme subset for each user-selected continuous target. Separate TVAE models learn the full dataset and each tail subset, concentrating training signal on rare values (Sections 3.2B–C).
- **Validity enforcement:** Generated records first pass an observed-route filter and then target-specific autoencoders reject samples exceeding the 99th percentile of reconstruction error on selected operational features (Sections 3.2D–E).
- **Two deployment outputs:** Cleaned nominal and extreme samples form an Augmented Synthetic dataset; cleaned extremes can instead enrich the real records to form Augmented Real (Section 3.2F).
- **Multi-dimensional evaluation:** The authors assess diversity, statistical similarity, fidelity/memorization, operational validity, and extreme-only regression utility, using six regressors for the utility tests (Sections 3.3 and 4).

These results establish improvements over the paper’s own naïve TVAE baseline on one flight dataset, with gains recurring across six regressors. They do not establish superiority over alternative tail-augmentation methods such as EVT-based models, zGAN, SMOTE, or ADASYN. Operational validity is supported primarily by visual pairwise plots and autoencoder rejection, not an independent quantitative or physics-based validity test. Augmented Real also contains the real records by construction, so its similarity and coverage advantages are partly structural rather than purely generative.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors identify three principal limitations (Section 5):

- Evaluation covers only New York State arrivals and departures from January 2023, so seasonal, geographic, airline, and network generalization is unknown.
- Operational validity is assessed visually rather than with a quantitative metric, limiting objective comparison and hyperparameter optimization.
- Tail-specific training sets are modest—3,726 air-time extremes and 5,470 arrival-delay extremes—which may restrict generated diversity and may not represent the full tail.

Additional analyst inferences supported by the text:

- **No component ablation:** The naïve-versus-augmented comparison changes both tail augmentation and autoencoder cleaning, so Table 8 does not isolate their individual causal contributions (Section 4 introduction).
- **Narrow generator comparison:** TVAE is the only demonstrated generator despite claims of model agnosticism; interchangeability with GANs or diffusion models is architectural, not empirically validated (Sections 3.2C and 5).
- **Threshold sensitivity is unresolved:** The fixed 1.5×IQR tail rule, 99th-percentile cleaning threshold, and 1.2 sampling ratio are not sensitivity-tested here (Sections 3.2B–E and 6).
- **Reported utility variability is incomplete:** Table 8 gives point MAEs without folds, uncertainty intervals, or significance tests; the supplied text does not establish statistical significance.
- **Reproducibility is prospective:** Code and data are said to be released “upon publication,” so their actual availability is not established by the paper text (page 18).

## Open research questions

1. **Does TailBooster generalize across seasons and airport networks?** The study uses one month and one U.S. region. A next experiment could train and test across multiple months, regions, airlines, and held-out airport networks.

2. **Which pipeline component produces each gain?** Tail-specific generation and operational cleaning change together in the main comparison. A factorial ablation could evaluate naïve generation, tail generation alone, cleaning alone, and their combination under identical sample budgets.

3. **How robust are the fixed thresholds?** Sensitivity to the IQR multiplier and autoencoder percentile is unreported. A grid or multi-objective study could measure utility, rejection rate, diversity, and quantitative validity across both thresholds.

4. **What works when tail data are much scarcer?** The authors note that deep tail generators may fail with very small subsets. A controlled downsampling experiment could compare tail-specific TVAE against SMOTE, ADASYN, resampling, and alternative generators at progressively lower extreme counts.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported within-dataset results because the supplied text includes the complete methodology, tables, and limitations.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates or significance tests; a quantitative operational-validity score; component and generator ablations; threshold sensitivity; detailed train/test split proportions; random seeds and repeated-run variability; tuned hyperparameters selected for each model; and confirmed public availability of the promised code and data.
