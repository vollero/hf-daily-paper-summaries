# Spatial Memory Agent: Experience-Grounded Procedure Memory for Spatial Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12743) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12743)


## One-sentence takeaway

SMA improves frozen vision-language models’ spatial reasoning by converting verifier-scored experience into reusable procedural lessons and ranking them by empirically calibrated transfer reliability, without weight updates or expert spatial tools at deployment.

## Problem and motivation

Current spatial VLM improvement mainly relies on post-training or external spatial tools. The authors identify a less explored alternative: letting a frozen model improve through an external memory of verified experience, while avoiding both parameter updates and expert tools at inference (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

The central difficulty is transfer rather than memorization. A useful system must distill general procedures from solved examples and distinguish lessons that merely resemble a new task from those that have repeatedly helped on later tasks.

## Approach and contributions

- **Experience-grounded procedural memory:** In a verifiable environment, a frozen VLM answers spatial problems and receives scalar rewards. A reflection model—implemented using the same frozen VLM—uses the rollout, reward, and verified target to produce a task summary and transferable lesson, subject to answer-leakage restrictions (Sections 3.2.1–3.2.2, pp. 4–5; Section 4.1.2, p. 6).

- **Transfer Reliability Score (TRS):** Every lesson begins with the same prior score. When retrieved during later environment visits, its visit count and cumulative downstream reward update a shrinkage estimator of transfer reliability; the default prior is \(v_0=0.5\) with strength \(\lambda=2\) (Section 3.2.4, p. 5; Appendix B.1, pp. 17–18).

- **Reliability-aware retrieval:** Task-embedding similarity first filters candidates; a weighted combination of normalized similarity and TRS then selects the top-\(k\) lessons. Only the task, summary, and lesson are shown to the solver (Sections 3.1 and 3.2.3, pp. 4–5).

- **Read-only deployment:** After memory acquisition, model parameters, memory contents, and reliability state remain fixed (Section 3.3, p. 5).

- **Empirical contribution:** The method is evaluated primarily on five benchmark slices and four frozen VLMs, with two further benchmarks in the appendix; component, transfer, reliability, memory-scaling, and ability-level analyses are also reported (Sections 4.1–4.6, pp. 6–11; Appendix A.1, p. 16).

The evidence supports consistent accuracy gains over the evaluated memory baselines, useful transfer in the reported probes, and contributions from both structured memory and retrieval calibration. It does not establish causal credit for individual retrieved cards, statistical significance, generalization beyond the tested Qwen models and benchmark splits, or superiority to training- and tool-based methods broadly; the SpatialEvo comparison contains only one released baseline under the paper’s evaluation setup.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated—coarse credit assignment:** Task-level reward cannot determine whether success came from writing, reflection, retrieval, filtering, or the solver’s use of memory (Appendix D.1, p. 40).

- **Author-stated—no complete memory lifecycle:** SMA does not delete, merge, compress, expire, or rewrite stale, conflicting, or redundant memories under storage or latency constraints (Appendix D.2, pp. 40–41).

- **Author-stated—visual evidence can be insufficient:** Some benchmark questions depend on ambiguous depth, metric scale, hidden simulator geometry, or unspecified viewpoints; memory also cannot compensate for failures in visual grounding (Appendix E.2, pp. 41 and 56–63).

- **Author-stated—TRS analysis is correlational:** The pooled score–accuracy relationship may partly reflect benchmark difficulty or question composition (Section 4.6.1, pp. 8–9).

- **Analyst inference—checkpoint-selection optimism:** Main SMA results use the best checkpoint from a ten-pass run, and benchmark/model-specific selected pass values differ in Tables 28–31. The supplied text says the same selection rule is used for all SMA rows, but does not report a separate validation criterion or uncertainty across checkpoints.

- **Analyst inference—limited breadth of causal ablations:** Component ablations are reported only for Qwen3.6-27B on RoboSpatial and Omni3D, so their consistency across the other models and benchmarks is untested (Tables 2 and 8).

- **Analyst inference—attribution confounding in TRS:** Every retrieved card receives the same final-answer reward, despite guidance sets containing three cards by default; this makes individual reliability estimates vulnerable to co-retrieval effects (Section 3.2.4, p. 5; Tables 28–31).

- **Analyst inference—evaluation reporting inconsistency:** The abstract and main claims emphasize five benchmarks and 20 evaluations, while Figure 1 and Appendix A.1 include seven benchmarks. This is reconcilable as main versus extended evaluation, but the headline scope is not consistently phrased (pp. 1–2; Table 7, p. 16).

## Open research questions

1. **Can individual memories receive accurate causal credit?** Task-level reward is shared across all retrieved cards, leaving their separate effects unresolved. A next experiment could compare the current update with leave-one-memory-out reruns or localized attribution on identical guidance sets.

2. **How should SMA maintain memory over long deployments?** The paper evaluates fixed or repeatedly calibrated banks but has no deletion, merging, or expiry policy. A next experiment could impose storage and latency budgets and compare TRS-only retention with learned merge/delete policies.

3. **Does TRS remain useful under distribution shift?** Selected cross-model and cross-benchmark probes are positive, but the score may encode source-specific difficulty and co-retrieval patterns. A next experiment could freeze banks and calibration before evaluating systematically chosen low-similarity target domains, reporting calibration error as well as accuracy.

4. **How robust are gains to checkpoint selection and randomness?** Results use deterministic decoding and fixed data splits, while the main table selects the best pass. A next experiment could use repeated split seeds and a validation-only checkpoint rule, then report variance and confidence intervals on held-out deployment accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported table values because the supplied text includes the complete method, experiments, appendices, prompts, and limitations.

Material missing or unclear:

- Statistical uncertainty, significance tests, and repeated-seed results.
- A validation-only justification for selecting each benchmark’s best pass.
- Full numerical data behind several plotted sensitivity and diagnostic curves.
- Exhaustive cross-model and cross-benchmark transfer matrices.
- Storage, inference-latency, embedding-API, and reflection-compute costs by method.
- Confirmation that code, trained memory banks, or processed splits are publicly released.
