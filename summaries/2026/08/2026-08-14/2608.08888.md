# Full-bandwidth transformer

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08888) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08888)


## One-sentence takeaway

A 1B-parameter transformer that feeds each previous top-layer hidden state back through a gated next-token input improves language, math, and coding performance with under 1% extra decoding cost, though evidence is limited to one model scale and heuristic recurrence scheduling.

## Problem and motivation

Standard autoregressive transformers have broad attention over earlier tokens but a narrow inter-step feedback channel: only the sampled token returns to the bottom of the network, while the top-layer state is discarded and other cached states remain “depth-frozen.” The authors argue that this forces non-verbalized computation either to be expressed as tokens or recomputed, motivating a continuous feedback channel that gives prior computation a renewed depth budget (Sections 1–2; Figure 1).

The training gap is that token-wise latent recurrence would eliminate parallel teacher forcing, while a conventionally pretrained model has never encountered hidden states as inputs (Section 3.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Latent-feedback decoding:** The previous top-layer state is fused with the sampled token embedding using a gated linear unit, with the state as value and token embedding as gate, then supplied as the next input. This preserves ordinary textual generation, transformer blocks, and KV-cache layout (Equations 3–4; Section 3.1).
- **Parallel multi-pass training:** Each pass shifts the preceding pass’s hidden states one position, fuses them with token embeddings, and evaluates all positions in parallel. Next-token loss is applied at every pass without detaching earlier states (Equations 9–12; Figure 2).
- **Progressive recurrence schedule:** Feedback passes are introduced late, with a small fraction of deeper-pass batches. In the reported 200B-token run, adding 3% three-pass batches stabilizes iterations well beyond the trained depth (Section 3.3; Figures 3 and 10).
- **Prompt-boundary and stability mechanisms:** Random plain prefixes approximate the prompt-to-generation transition; depth scaling, RMS normalization, tied input/output weights, and hidden-state noise regularize long feedback horizons (Sections 3.3–3.4).
- **Empirical contribution:** The authors evaluate models pretrained for 10B–400B tokens on language-model benchmarks, math, code, instruction-tuned generation, reasoning length, and synthetic state accessibility (Section 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Deeper-pass mixing stabilizes recurrence | For a 1B model trained on 200B tokens, changing the mixture from 75% one-pass/25% two-pass to 75% one-pass/22% two-pass/3% three-pass kept validation loss flat through 30 feedback steps; stability was also tested to 1,000 passes. | Section 3.3; Figures 3 and 10 |
| Fused prefill improves data efficiency | With two feedback passes, the 100B-token model reached the 200B-token standard baseline, and the 200B-token model reached the 400B-token baseline, using validation loss and ten-task 5-shot LM Eval accuracy. | Section 4.1; Figure 4 |
| Latent decoding improves base-model math | On MATH-500, the 200B-token base model improved from 0.27 under STANDARD to 0.37 under SOFT, using 0-shot Pass@1; the authors state this exceeded their 1T-token standard baseline. | Section 4.2; Figure 5 |
| Base-model coding improves with fused decoding | At 200B tokens, HumanEval rose from 0.31 to 0.34 and MBPP from 0.38 to 0.40 under FUSED; both use 0-shot/3-shot Pass@3 respectively, estimated from 10 rollouts with per-method temperature selection. | Section 4.2; Figure 5 |
| Gains survive instruction tuning | For the instruction-tuned 200B model, GSM8K Pass@1 rose from 64.52% STANDARD to 67.93% SOFT; HumanEval Pass@3 rose from 42.54% to 45.92% under FUSED. Evaluation used no few-shot examples; coding used 10 rollouts and selected the best temperature from 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7. | Table 1; Section 4.2 |
| Larger instruction-tuned model shows mixed best modes | At 400B tokens, GSM8K rose from 67.90% STANDARD to 71.80% FUSED, while MATH-500 was 46.00% STANDARD, 45.40% SOFT, and 48.40% FUSED. | Table 1 |
| Shallow states expose tracked information | In synthetic completion and delayed-memory tasks, one-step recurrent prefilling yielded layer-0 probe accuracy of 99.6% and 100%, respectively, versus standard layer-0 probes described as near chance. | Section 4.4; Figure 7a |
| Decoding overhead is small | The fusion adds two \(D\times D\) matrix multiplications and is reported as under 1% extra cost per generated token, independent of context length and depth; FUSED additionally doubles prefill cost. | Sections 3.1 and 4.2 |

Together, these results support the authors’ claim that latent feedback can improve the tested 1B models and expose global state earlier in the residual stream. They do not establish gains at larger parameter scales, isolate every component of the training recipe, or prove that the feedback map is mathematically contractive. Probe decodability establishes information availability, not causal use; the paper explicitly makes this distinction in Section 4.4.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Experiments are restricted to 1B-parameter models, so scaling to larger models is unverified (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** Feedback-pass scheduling is heuristic; recurrence-phase length and the number of training passes lack rigorous ablation or principled selection (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** The authors lacked resources to compare alternative hidden-state injection methods at equivalent large-scale settings, leaving the best injection design unclear (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Shorter reasoning traces disappear after instruction tuning; the proposed explanation—off-policy traces encouraging standard verbosity—is an attribution rather than a demonstrated causal result (Section 4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Coding temperatures are selected separately for each method, so those Pass@3 comparisons include method-specific tuning rather than a single fixed decoding configuration (Sections 4.2; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported stability tests are empirical proxies based on repeated prefill passes; they do not prove indefinite stability during autoregressive generation (Section 3.3; Figures 3 and 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Later-pass losses backpropagate through earlier passes and increase memory use, but the supplied text does not quantify peak training memory, wall-clock cost, or end-to-end serving latency (Section 3.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does latent feedback continue to help at larger parameter scales?** Only 1B models were tested. A next experiment could train matched standard and full-bandwidth models at several larger sizes with fixed token and compute budgets.
2. **Which training components are necessary?** The schedule, prefix mixin, depth scaling, normalization, weight tying, and noise are used together without complete component ablations. A factorial ablation could measure accuracy, loss, and long-horizon stability for each component.
3. **Can recurrence scheduling be selected automatically?** The current pass mixture is heuristic. A next experiment could adapt pass depth or mixture weights using held-out convergence measurements such as hidden-state change and validation-loss stability.
4. **Can post-training preserve concise latent reasoning?** Conciseness disappears after instruction tuning. Comparing standard off-policy instruction traces with on-policy latent-feedback traces could test whether the shorter-output behavior can be retained without sacrificing accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described mechanism, experimental settings, and explicitly tabulated results; **medium** for broad efficiency conclusions because only one parameter scale is evaluated.

Material missing information includes the full numeric values underlying Figures 3–6, quantified training memory and wall-clock overhead, end-to-end latency measurements, comprehensive recipe ablations, larger-model results, release details for code or checkpoints, and statistical significance tests for most generation comparisons.
