# Massive Activations in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models: Pre-Attention Spikes and Inter-Spike Plateaus

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12149) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12149)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors find that massive activations in layer-interleaved hybrid LLMs spike immediately before full-attention layers and increasingly persist between those spikes as full attention becomes denser, a pattern they interpret through the timing of outlier cancellation.

## Problem and motivation

Hybrid linear-attention (HLA) LLMs combine fixed-state, linear-time sequence mixers with full attention, but how this interleaving reorganizes internal activations is poorly understood. Existing massive-activation research focused mainly on full-attention Transformers, where extreme hidden-state values are associated with attention sinks; it did not establish how such activations behave in hybrid models. Understanding these dynamics matters both as a probe of hybrid computation and because extreme activations can create difficult numerical regimes for low-bit inference (Sections 1–2; Appendix A.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Sink-guided tracing:** The authors identify a consensus attention-sink token by aggregating received attention across full-attention layers and heads, then track that fixed token’s maximum absolute hidden-state activation through depth (Section 3.2, Equation 5).

- **Two activation morphologies:** They introduce **pre-attention spikes (PAS)**—local activation maxima immediately before full-attention layers—and **inter-spike plateaus (ISP)**—persistent elevated activation between adjacent spikes (Sections 3.3–3.4).

- **Quantitative metrics:** Sink–spike alignment measures whether the sink activation peaks at the last linear layer before full attention; inter-spike retention measures activation persistence relative to adjacent spikes (Equations 6–7).

- **Broad empirical characterization:** The analysis covers five linear-attention backbones, four hybrid ratios plus limiting configurations, two controlled-model scales, five input domains, and 12 additional hybrid checkpoints ranging from 1.2B to 397B parameters (Sections 3.3–3.5; Appendices B–C).

- **Controlled interventions and mechanistic account:** From-scratch GDN experiments track PAS and ISP during training and vary output gating. Signed fixed-coordinate analysis motivates a **write–sink–cancel** account for PAS and delayed cancellation for ISP (Sections 3.6 and 4; Appendix E.1).

The evidence strongly establishes an association between full-attention placement and the layerwise location or persistence of sink-associated extreme activations within the evaluated models. Controlled placement and gating experiments strengthen this architectural interpretation.

It does not establish that PAS or ISP causes better language modeling or retrieval, nor that cancellation timing is the unique underlying mechanism. The fixed-coordinate mechanistic verification is presented for one representative PAS; broader evidence often selects the dominant feature independently at each layer and therefore demonstrates recurring signed patterns rather than coordinate-level continuity (Appendix E.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated/open limitation:** What regulates cancellation timing and whether PAS and ISP have distinct computational roles remain unresolved (Section 4.2).

- **Author-noted metric caveat:** ISR is normalized by adjacent spikes, so cross-variant values do not directly measure absolute plateau magnitude and should be interpreted cautiously (Table 10; Appendix D.5).

- **Analyst inference—causality:** The gating interventions alter both activation morphology and some downstream scores, but the experiments do not isolate PAS/ISP themselves; consequently, they cannot show that these activation forms cause performance differences.

- **Analyst inference—mechanistic generality:** Fixed-coordinate write–sink–cancel evidence is detailed for a representative 1.3B GDN 12:1 case. Cross-model decompositions choose each layer’s dominant feature independently, limiting claims that exactly the same feature persists across layers (Appendix E.2).

- **Analyst inference—token and input coverage:** Quantitative controlled results use 500 inputs across five domains, while many large-model results use one representative input per domain and first-token trajectories. Appendix B.2 examines all tokens only for the short running example, so broader token-position generality is less firmly established.

- **Analyst inference—scaling comparisons:** The 340M and 1.3B controlled models use different token budgets, batch sizes, and tracing intervals; the authors explicitly avoid treating the ISR difference as a controlled scaling effect (Appendices D.1 and D.4).

- **Reproducibility risk:** Checkpoints are stated as available, but Section 7 describes analysis code and configurations as forthcoming; the supplied text does not verify their actual availability or provide the omitted repository artifacts.

## Open research questions

1. **What architectural component determines cancellation timing?**  
   The paper associates denser full attention with delayed cancellation but does not isolate the responsible computation. A next experiment could intervene separately on attention, FFN, normalization, and residual updates at selected boundaries while tracing the same token–feature coordinate.

2. **Do PAS or ISP perform a functional computation?**  
   The reported evidence is correlational with respect to downstream behavior. A plausible experiment would selectively clamp, rescale, or patch PAS/ISP coordinates during inference and measure changes in language modeling, recall, and attention-sink behavior against matched control coordinates.

3. **How general is the lifecycle across tokens and sequences?**  
   Much of the large-model evidence follows the first token, while comprehensive token analysis is limited. A next study could perform fixed-coordinate lifecycle tracking for every consensus-sink position over a larger, length-stratified corpus and report prevalence and uncertainty.

4. **Can activation morphology guide numerical optimization?**  
   The paper motivates concern about extreme-value quantization but does not evaluate quantized HLA models. A next experiment could compare uniform and PAS/ISP-aware activation or KV-cache quantization at matched bit widths, reporting accuracy, error, memory, and latency.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported activation-localization and retention patterns, because the supplied text includes metric definitions, controlled comparisons, uncertainty analyses, and numerical tables. **Confidence is medium** for the proposed cancellation-timing mechanism because its strongest fixed-coordinate evidence is a representative case and the broader analyses do not preserve feature identity across layers.

Material missing or unavailable from the supplied text includes raw per-input measurements, exact bootstrap intervals, complete numeric gating-effect magnitudes, all appendix figure values, seeds and run-to-run variance, compute cost, detailed model hyperparameters beyond the summarized recipes, and direct verification that the promised code/configuration release is accessible.
