# PlayWorld: Benchmarking World Models with Agent Players over Long-Horizon Objectives

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13552) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13552)


## One-sentence takeaway

PlayWorld replaces fixed control trajectories with an observation-conditioned Agent Player and finds that nine evaluated video world models remain unreliable on long-horizon objectives, especially persistent state evolution and global spatial consistency.

## Problem and motivation

Interactive video world models differ in action granularity and response speed, so identical predefined controls may lead them to different states and make cross-model scores incomparable. Existing benchmarks largely use fixed low-level trajectories and may therefore conflate trajectory failure with failures of geometry, interaction, or memory. The authors seek to better match human evaluation, where a player adapts actions while pursuing a high-level objective over tens of seconds (Introduction; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Closed-loop Agent Player:** A replaceable multimodal model observes frames, action history, scene description, and objective, then chooses `Keep`, `Stop`, `Extend`, `Correct`, or `End`. A human-annotated basic action sequence provides a common initial reference (Section 3.1).
- **PlayWorld benchmark:** 171 manually curated scenarios span 50 action patterns, four evaluation dimensions, and approximately 10–60-second rollouts. Evaluating nine models produced more than 1,400 videos assessed by over 820 sample-specific questions (Figures 2–3).
- **VQA rubric verifier:** Gemini 3.1 Pro applies dimension-specific validity gates and weighted binary rubrics, producing 1–5 scores for geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, out-of-sight evolution, and insight evolution (Section 3.3; Appendix A).
- **Complementary diagnostics:** The benchmark also measures seven video-quality properties and translation/rotation controllability, separating basic generation quality from objective-level world-model capability (Section 3.4; Table 6).
- **Empirical comparison:** Nine open- and closed-source systems are evaluated under the same initial worlds, objectives, action references, and Agent Player protocol (Section 4.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Long-horizon state evolution was weak across systems. | The best insight-evolution score was 1.95 from LingBot-World2; the best out-of-sight score was 1.81 from Genie 3. | Adaptive preset-guided control improved objective trajectories. | On the same 25 cases, Preset + Agent beat Preset Only in trajectory score and human preference for Genie 3 (1.08 vs. 0.92; 65.6% vs. 39.6%) and HappyOyster (1.12 vs. 1.00; 67.4% vs. 40.8%). | Tables 4; Section 4.2 |
| Agent-model choice had modest but non-identical effects. | On Genie 3 with Preset + Agent, trajectory scores ranged from 1.08 to 1.24; preference from 45.3% to 57.8%; latency from 3.83 to 6.21 seconds/call. | Table 5 |
| Trajectory success and world simulation quality diverged. | SANA-WM had an 80.4% overall validation pass rate but only a 1.48 overall rubric score, supporting the authors’ claim that reaching the target region does not ensure consistent or plausible evolution. | Tables 2–3; Section 4.3 |
| Basic metrics did not identify the strongest long-horizon model. | HappyOyster had the highest Basic Ability Score, 76.4%, while Genie 3 had the highest rubric overall score, 2.12. | Tables 2 and 6 |
| VQA rankings aligned strongly with human preferences. | From 600 judgments over 120 pairs, Spearman correlation was 0.933 overall; dimension correlations ranged from 0.745 to 0.983, all with \(p<0.05\). Majority agreement was 95.8% and overall Fleiss’ κ was 0.434. | Figure 6; Table 7; Appendix D |
| One-pass VQA scoring was stable at aggregate level in a limited repeat test. | Two independent scoring passes yielded mean per-model sample variance of 0.0112 across nine models. | Appendix B |

The evidence supports the benchmark’s ability to expose failures that conventional video-quality and control metrics miss, and supports the usefulness of preset-guided online adaptation on two tested models. It does not establish that PlayWorld measures every aspect of world modeling, that its rankings generalize beyond the nine systems and curated scenarios, or that the VQA verifier is unbiased.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Reported results use one VQA scoring pass per rollout. The two-pass robustness analysis is not a precise uncertainty estimate; multiple passes are recommended when resources allow (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** Agent latency depends on provider infrastructure and network conditions, limiting portability of the latency comparison (Section 4.2).
- **Author-stated scope mismatch:** HY-World2 skips incompatible initial images, while Hunyuan-GameCraft-2 cannot always execute passive or `WAIT`-only insight cases as intended. Their aggregates therefore do not cover exactly the same cases or protocol (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** The key control-strategy ablation covers only 25 cases and two closed-source models, so its generality across the full benchmark and open-source systems remains untested (Section 4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Gemini 3.1 Pro helps generate captions, scores trajectories in an ablation, and serves as the VQA verifier. Human alignment is strong, but dependence on one proprietary verifier may introduce correlated judgment biases not measured by rank correlation alone.
- **Analyst inference:** Candidate images come from Pexels and Google Images and are manually screened. The text provides category counts but no detailed analysis of geographic, cultural, visual-style, or source-distribution bias (Section 3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Invalid trajectories receive the minimum capability score of 1. This intentionally penalizes failure to reach the evaluation state, but can entangle controllability with the capability being scored (Section 4.1; Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **Does adaptive control improve evaluation fairness across all model types?** The control ablation covers only Genie 3 and HappyOyster. A next experiment could repeat the three control strategies across all nine models and each evaluation dimension.

2. **How verifier-dependent are the rankings?** Only Gemini 3.1 Pro is reported as the rubric verifier. A next experiment could compare several independent multimodal verifiers against the same human judgments and measure rank stability and calibration.

3. **Can trajectory failure and world-capability failure be disentangled more cleanly?** Invalid trajectories automatically receive a score of 1. A next experiment could report conditional capability scores on valid rollouts alongside separate success probabilities and a prespecified joint metric.

4. **Do the findings persist beyond 60 seconds?** Rollouts are limited to approximately 10–60 seconds and 40 interaction steps. Longer trials with repeated revisitation and hidden-state changes could test whether errors compound beyond the current horizon.
