# RibAssist 3D: Biplanar Rib-Fracture Detection, Addressing, and Selective 3D Localization from CT-Derived Projections

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06914) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06914)


## One-sentence takeaway

RibAssist 3D shows that CT-derived biplanar triangulation can localize correctly paired rib fractures accurately, but cross-view confidence remains the bottleneck, limiting sealed-cohort end-to-end recall to 2.50% under a conservative false-output policy.

## Problem and motivation

Localizing and assigning anatomical rib levels to every fracture in a CT volume is laborious, while dense volumetric detectors require 3D inference and annotations. The authors investigate a lower-dimensional alternative: detect fractures independently in orthogonal AP and lateral projections, match detections across views, and triangulate accepted pairs into 3D.

The identified gap is reliable cross-view correspondence at a controlled false-output rate. The study specifically tests this mechanism using deterministic CT-derived projections, not independently acquired radiographs (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Staged diagnostic pipeline:** Two independent U-Nets detect fracture peaks in 256×256 AP and lateral projections. SI-axis agreement generates candidate pairs; one-to-one assignment with abstention accepts confident pairs for orthographic triangulation (Sections 3.1–3.5; Figures 1–3).
- **Failure-stage isolation:** Oracle-to-model substitutions separately evaluate geometry, peak localization, candidate availability, correspondence scoring, and detector quality (Section 3.5).
- **Detector intervention:** The lateral detector is retrained with positive-branch strengthening and hard-negative mining, enabling nonzero controlled-budget reconstruction where extraction-policy tuning and learned local-appearance matching did not (Sections 3.6 and 4.3–4.4).
- **Selective assistive workflow:** Uncommitted detections remain available as 2D or candidate findings rather than being discarded; only confident pairs receive 3D locations (Sections 4.6–4.8).
- **Sealed confirmation and provenance:** A fixed policy is evaluated once on 55 untouched cases, with hash-checked inputs and case-bootstrap uncertainty estimates (Sections 3.6 and 4.5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Ideal geometry is exact. | Correct paired GT centers produced 0.0 mm median 3D error and 98.6% rib-exactness; transform round-trip error was 0.0 mm. | Section 4.1, Stage A |
| Detector localization is accurate conditional on oracle correspondence. | Median error 4.0 mm; 88% within 10 mm; 93.6% rib-exact and 100% within ±1 rib. | Section 4.1, Stage B |
| Frozen-detector correspondence fails at the controlled budget. | Assignment with abstention reconstructed 0% at ≤1 false 3D point/case; the local-appearance scorer achieved AUROC 0.57 on hard positive-versus-cross pairs. | Section 4.2, Stages D1–D2; Table 2 |
| Extraction recalibration reduces clutter but not controlled-budget recall. | Changing lateral NMS radius from 5 to 3 and score floor from 0.05 to 0.10 reduced the candidate field about fourfold, while recall remained 0% across tested policies. | Section 4.3, Stage L1; Table 3 |
| Lateral retraining is the only tested intervention yielding nonzero controlled-budget recall. | Development dual-view availability rose from 0.52 to 0.76, and recall within 10 mm rose from 0% to 2.44% at ≤1 false point/case. | Section 4.4, Stages L2–L3; Table 4 and Figure 4 |
| Much of the sealed candidate space is recoverable in principle. | On 55 cases and 601 fractures, dual-view availability was 61.1% and a correct candidate pair existed for 58.4% at 10 mm. | Section 4.5; Table 5 and Figure 5 |
| Final selective coverage is low but nonzero. | The fixed retrained policy correctly localized 15/601 fractures within 10 mm: 2.50% recall, case-bootstrap 95% CI [0.69%, 4.48%], at 0.436 false points/case. The frozen selected policy emitted no predictions. | Section 4.5; Table 5 and Figure 5 |
| Correctly committed sealed pairs are spatially accurate. | Conditional median error was 1.49 mm, with 93% rib-exactness and 100% within ±1 rib. | Section 4.5; Table 5 and Figure 5 |

The evidence supports exact reconstruction geometry, good localization conditional on correct pairing, and nonzero selective performance after improving the lateral detector. Within the tested 2×2 factorial, the operational gain occurs only with the retrained detector plus deterministic confidence assignment.

It does not establish comprehensive reconstruction, superiority through a paired hypothesis test, clinical benefit, safety on fracture-negative scans, or transfer to real biplanar radiographs. The 2.50% figure is an end-to-end yield across several gates, not an isolated detector or triangulation score.

## Limitations and validity risks

Author-stated limitations:

- AP and lateral images are deterministic CT renderings, so real-radiograph scatter, overlap, and calibration errors are absent (Section 5).
- The sealed cohort is small—55 cases—and its confidence interval is wide (Section 5).
- It contains no fracture-negative studies, leaving false-output safety on negative scans unmeasured (Sections 3.1 and 5).
- Development-based selection confirms the effect’s direction on sealed data but not an unbiased effect size (Section 5).
- Retraining jointly changes availability, confidence, prevalence, and candidate ceiling, so the individual causal mediator is unresolved (Section 5).
- Evaluation omits fracture type, displacement, acute-versus-healed status, demographics, fairness, usability, and clinical outcomes (Sections 5, 6, and 4.7–4.8).
- Trained checkpoints are not redistributed (Code Availability).

Analyst inferences:

- Because only deterministic confidence matching and a small local-crop scorer are tested, failure should not be generalized to global, anatomical, graph-based, or jointly learned correspondence; the authors explicitly leave these designs open (Section 7).
- Standalone addressing performance cannot be assessed because no addressing metrics are reported (Section 3.3).
- The absence of independently acquired radiographs and negative cases materially narrows external and safety validity, even though the sealed split strengthens internal confirmation.

## Open research questions

1. **Can global anatomy solve the remaining correspondence bottleneck?** Local appearance reached only AUROC 0.57–0.71 and did not improve controlled-budget recall. A next experiment could compare rib-order-constrained graph matching or full-view feature matching against deterministic confidence under the same sealed protocol.

2. **Does performance transfer to real biplanar acquisition?** CT-derived orthographic views omit acquisition noise, overlap, and calibration error. A prospective or retrospective experiment could evaluate calibrated paired radiographs with independently annotated 3D reference locations.

3. **Can recall rise without violating the false-output budget?** Although 58.4% of sealed fractures had a correct candidate pair, only 2.50% were committed correctly. A next experiment could jointly train detection and correspondence, then compare its recall–false-output frontier with the staged system.

4. **Is the workflow clinically useful and safe?** No reader study or fracture-negative sealed evaluation was conducted. A blinded multi-reader study could measure review time, sensitivity, false positives, and rib-level documentation accuracy on mixed positive and negative cases.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported methodology, numerical results, and stated scope because the supplied text includes detailed methods, staged results, and limitations.
