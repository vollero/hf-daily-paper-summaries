# How Can Rhetoric Reward-Hack AI Reviewers? Dissecting Rhetorical Sensitivity in AI-Based Peer Review

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08975) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08975)


## One-sentence takeaway

In controlled full-paper rewrites, AI-review scores were most sensitive to evidence, novelty, and scope framing, but effects varied substantially by rewriter, reviewer, protocol, and the reviewer’s initial score.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether content-preserving rhetorical changes can alter AI reviewers’ judgments of scientific merit. This matters because LLMs increasingly participate in both manuscript revision and evaluation, creating an opportunity to improve scores through presentation rather than better science.

The authors argue that prior comparisons across naturally occurring papers confound rhetoric with paper quality and that earlier rewriting studies emphasize successful score increases without systematically isolating rhetorical dimensions, directions, and evaluation conditions (Introduction, pp. 1–3; Related Work, p. 4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled counterfactual corpus:** The authors derive 4,080 rewrites from 120 anonymized ICLR 2026 submissions, producing 4,200 full manuscripts. Six rhetorical dimensions are independently varied in positive and negative directions (Table 1, p. 4; Table 2, p. 5).
- **Full-project rewriting system:** GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8 rewrite complete LaTeX projects, with structural checks, compilation, and repair intended to preserve methods, settings, reported values, comparisons, and scientific meaning (Sections 3.1–3.2, pp. 5–6).
- **Multi-condition AI evaluation:** Five LLM reviewers score matched originals and rewrites using standard and strict prompts; primary outcomes are overall assessment and weak-accept probability (Sections 3.4–3.5, pp. 8–9).
- **Enhanced workflow analysis:** The study compares single-dimension, joint, recursive, and reviewer-guided rewriting, separating the roles of rewriters, reviewers, and review protocols (Sections 3.3 and 5–6, pp. 6–14).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence, novelty, and scope produced the clearest directional effects. | Across both rewriters, five reviewers, and both protocols, positive-minus-negative OA contrasts were **0.592** for evidence (`+0.289` vs. `−0.303`), **0.540** for novelty (`+0.187` vs. `−0.353`), and **0.425** for scope (`+0.136` vs. `−0.289`). | Table 3, p. 6 |
| Decision-level effects followed the same hierarchy. | Averaged across configurations, positive-minus-negative weak-accept changes were **13.0 pp** for evidence, **12.0 pp** for novelty, and **9.0 pp** for scope; weak accept means OA ≥6. | Section 4.1, p. 9; Table 16, p. 26 |
| Directional effects were broadly distributed across papers. | Positive variants beat negative variants for evidence on **116/120** papers, novelty on **117/120**, and scope on **113/120**, after averaging rewriters, prompts, and reviewers within paper. | Table 15, p. 25 |
| Starting AI score strongly conditioned score movement. | Under standard evaluation, pooled positive-evidence rewrites changed OA by **+1.05** for original scores `[1,3]` but **−0.19** for `[8,10]`; authors caution that scale bounds and regression to the mean may contribute. | Section 4.3, p. 10; Appendix C.7, p. 30 |
| Joint rewriting was strongly rewriter-dependent. | Reviewer-averaged joint-rewrite OA gains were **+0.289 standard / +0.463 strict** for Opus 4.8, versus **+0.021 / +0.045** for GPT-5.5. | Table 6, p. 10; Section 5.1, p. 11 |
| Reviewer feedback added no reliable benefit over repetition. | Guided-minus-unguided second-pass contrasts were negative in all four rewriter–prompt averages: **−0.035, −0.042, −0.008, and −0.067 OA**. The comparison used independently generated first passes and is descriptive. | Section 5.2, p. 12; Table 6, p. 10 |
| Recursive gains diminished and depended on configuration. | At round 3, pooled OA changes were **+0.076 standard / +0.157 strict** for GPT-5.5 and **+0.438 / +0.631** for Opus 4.8; most Opus improvement occurred by round 2. | Figure 3 and Section 5.3, p. 12; Table 33, p. 49 |
| Strict review changed severity more than robustness. | Mean rewritten OA fell from **6.296 to 4.934** (`−1.361`), and **95.0%** of papers scored lower; baseline-adjusted mean rewrite effects changed only from **+0.003 to +0.020**, with cross-protocol effect correlation **ρ=.081**. | Table 8 and Section 6.2, p. 14 |
| A repeated-review audit supported aggregate stability for one reviewer. | For GPT-5 mini, single-review versus three-review-mean estimates had Pearson **r=.995** across 12 effects; mean absolute difference was **0.040 OA**. | Appendix F, Table 40, p. 54 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that rhetorical sensitivity is structured and configuration-dependent rather than a universal score-increasing trick. Because the interventions are full-document rewrites under soft preservation constraints, however, the results do not isolate causal effects of individual linguistic features or independently prove that every rewrite preserved all scientific meaning. They also do not establish effects on human reviewers or conference decisions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The corpus is limited to 120 ICLR 2026 submissions with recoverable source and public review metadata, restricting venue and field generalization (Limitations, p. 15).
- **Author-stated:** The six rhetorical dimensions are not orthogonal; full-paper effects should not be read as independent coefficients for individual features (Limitations, p. 15).
- **Author-stated:** Most configurations contain one review per manuscript. The repeated-review audit covers only GPT-5 mini and therefore does not establish sampling stability for all reviewers (Limitations, p. 15; Appendix F, p. 54).
- **Author-stated:** Of 42,480 planned reviews, 84 lacked valid structured records; failures may be non-random because some manuscripts apparently triggered safeguards (Appendix B.2, p. 21).
- **Author-stated:** Initial-score subgroup patterns may partly reflect bounded scales and regression to the mean, with sparse extreme cells (Section 4.3, p. 10; Appendix C.7, p. 30).
- **Analyst inference:** Structural checks protect LaTeX anchors, while scientific-content preservation relies substantially on prompts and soft constraints. The supplied text does not report an independent semantic or expert audit of all 4,080 rewrites.
- **Analyst inference:** Reviewer-guided versus unguided rewriting is not a randomized comparison from an identical first-pass manuscript; the authors appropriately label it descriptive (Section 5.2, p. 12).
- **Analyst inference:** No human-review counterfactual experiment is reported, so the findings characterize the tested AI-review pipeline only.

## Open research questions

1. **Do human reviewers show the same hierarchy of rhetorical sensitivity?** This paper evaluates only AI reviewers. A next experiment could randomly assign matched manuscript variants to blinded human reviewers and compare OA and rubric-score changes.

2. **How completely do the rewrites preserve scientific content?** Structural validation does not establish semantic equivalence. Independent domain experts could audit stratified rewrite samples for altered claims, assumptions, comparisons, or evidence boundaries.

3. **Can reviewer training or prompting improve rhetorical invariance without degrading valid quality discrimination?** The strict prompt lowered scores but did not consistently reduce sensitivity. A follow-up could compare robustness-aware prompts or calibrated reviewer models on both rhetorical counterfactuals and genuine scientific-quality perturbations.

4. **Which local textual changes mediate the full-paper effects?** The six interventions are broad and non-orthogonal. Factorial or localized edits could isolate claim verbs, evidence placement, captions, contribution signposting, and scope qualifiers while holding the rest of each manuscript fixed.
