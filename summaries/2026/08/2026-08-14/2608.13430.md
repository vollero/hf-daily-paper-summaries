# Are You Sure You&#x27;re Sure? On the Impact of Instruction Tuning on Confidence and Lexical Diversity

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13430) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13430)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across three matched model pairs and three English multiple-choice benchmarks, instruction tuning consistently increased confidence and reduced cross-rationale variability without consistently improving accuracy, calibration, or within-rationale lexical richness.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether instruction-tuning-induced changes in language-model confidence are accompanied by changes in the lexical diversity of supporting rationales. This matters because confidence may be mistaken for reliability, particularly in high-stakes question answering, while repeated but homogeneous rationales may provide less independent evidence than their number suggests. The authors identify a gap in prior work: confidence, calibration, and output diversity had been studied separately, but their relationship under instruction tuning had not been jointly evaluated (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Paired evaluation:** The authors compare matched base and instruction-tuned variants of Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Llama-3.1-8B on ARC-Easy, MMLU, and CommonsenseQA (§3).
- **Complementary measurements:** They evaluate accuracy, normalized candidate-choice entropy, separately elicited verbalized confidence, Expected Calibration Error, Unique-2, and cross-rationale variability measured as \(1-\)Self-BLEU (§3; Appendix B–C).
- **Repeated rationale sampling:** Five chain-of-thought rationales are sampled per question at temperature 0.7, nucleus-sampling \(p=1.0\), and a 100-token maximum (Appendix B).
- **Controlled comparison:** On CommonsenseQA, they restrict analysis to cases where both variants select the same answer, match rationale counts, and truncate paired rationales to equal token length (Table 3; Appendix B).
- **Empirical contribution:** They show that confidence and cross-rationale diversity shift consistently after instruction tuning, but accuracy, calibration, and Unique-2 do not exhibit corresponding uniform changes (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction tuning increased confidence in every evaluated model–benchmark pairing. | Across all nine pairings, choice entropy fell and verbalized confidence rose; for example, Qwen/MMLU entropy fell from 0.430 to 0.131, while Llama/CSQA verbalized confidence rose from 46.8% to 91.1%. | Table 1; §4 |
| Accuracy did not improve correspondingly. | Llama/ARC-Easy remained at 82.2% despite entropy falling from 0.188 to 0.173 and verbalized confidence rising from 49.2% to 90.4%. Qwen/CSQA accuracy declined from 85.2% to 82.5%. | Table 1; §4 |
| Cross-rationale variability consistently decreased. | \(1-\)Self-BLEU declined in all nine pairings; the largest reported decrease was Mistral/ARC-Easy, 0.813 to 0.626. | Table 1; §4 |
| Unique-2 changed heterogeneously. | Mistral/ARC-Easy fell from 0.701 to 0.673, whereas Mistral/CSQA rose from 0.719 to 0.750. | Table 1; §4 |
| Lower uncertainty did not imply lower lexical diversity. | On CSQA, Mistral combined lower entropy with higher Unique-2 on 61.8% of questions, but lower entropy with lower \(1-\)Self-BLEU on 77.6%. | Table 2; §4 |
| Diversity shifts survived answer and length controls. | On controlled CSQA subsets, \(\Delta\)Unique-2 was −0.001, +0.050, and +0.053 for Qwen, Mistral, and Llama; corresponding \(\Delta(1-\mathrm{SB})\) was −0.036, −0.069, and −0.012. All but Qwen Unique-2 were significant at \(p<0.01\). Sample sizes were 1,104, 904, and 1,065. | Table 3; Appendix B |
| Likelihood calibration generally worsened, while verbalized calibration was mixed. | Likelihood ECE increased in all nine pairings—for example, Qwen/MMLU rose from 4.4% to 21.3%. Verbalized ECE sometimes improved (Qwen/ARC-Easy: 35.3% to 22.8%) and sometimes worsened (Llama/MMLU: 16.6% to 23.7%). | Table 7; Appendix C |
| Instruction-tuned rationales were longer before length control. | Mean length increased in every pairing, by +0.8 to +5.3 tokens across the reported settings. | Figure 2; Appendix C |

The evidence supports a robust association, within these model pairs and benchmarks, between instruction tuning and higher confidence plus lower cross-rationale variability. It also shows that Unique-2 and \(1-\)Self-BLEU capture different behavior. It does not establish that instruction tuning causally produces these effects across other tuning recipes, model scales, languages, task formats, or decoding settings, nor that reduced lexical diversity entails reduced semantic diversity.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ limitations:** The evaluation covers only three English multiple-choice benchmarks, three model families, and lexical diversity measures; semantic, syntactic, multilingual, safety-sensitive, and demographic-sensitive behavior is not evaluated (Limitations; Ethical Considerations).
- **Authors’ limitation:** The consequences of reduced rationale diversity are not validated against human rationales or human judgments (Ethical Considerations).
- **Analyst inference:** The controlled same-answer and length-matched analysis is reported only for CommonsenseQA, so its generality to ARC-Easy and MMLU remains untested (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference:** All rationales use one fixed sampling configuration and five samples, leaving sensitivity to temperature, sampling method, token budget, and sample count unresolved (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Paired \(t\)-tests are reported at \(p<0.01\), but the supplied text does not mention correction for the many model–benchmark–metric comparisons or report effect-size uncertainty.
- **Analyst inference:** The text supplies model references and implementation tools but does not state that code or generated outputs were released.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the observed shifts extend to semantic and syntactic diversity?** The paper measures token-level diversity only. A next experiment could score the same rationale sets with semantic clustering and syntactic-diversity measures while retaining the same-answer and length controls.

2. **How sensitive are the findings to decoding?** Only one temperature, nucleus setting, token limit, and rationale count are tested. A factorial experiment varying temperature, top-\(p\), sample count, and length budget could measure whether instruction tuning still reduces cross-rationale variability.

3. **Do the controlled results generalize beyond CommonsenseQA?** The strongest controls are applied only there. Repeating the matched-answer, matched-count, and matched-length analysis on ARC-Easy, MMLU, other languages, and non-multiple-choice tasks would test scope.

4. **Can confidence be recalibrated without further collapsing rationale diversity?** The conclusion poses this tradeoff but does not test an intervention. A plausible experiment would apply post-training calibration or decoding interventions and jointly track ECE, accuracy, Unique-2, \(1-\)Self-BLEU, and semantic diversity.
