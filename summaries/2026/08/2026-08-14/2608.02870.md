# Maglev: Sliding Recurrent Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02870) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02870)


## One-sentence takeaway

Maglev trains a bounded-memory, token-recurrent Transformer in two parallel passes and, at 435M scale, improves FineWeb-Edu validation BPB and average downstream accuracy over matched sliding-window and latent-recurrent baselines, though only in one pretraining regime.

## Problem and motivation

Full-attention Transformers retain the entire prefix but incur growing attention and KV-cache costs; sliding-window models bound those costs but discard distant information. Existing recurrent alternatives either propagate small states sequentially, update memory at coarse block boundaries, or constrain updates to structured linear or affine forms.

The authors seek a language model with nonlinear memory rewritten after every token, fixed inference memory, and sequence-parallel pretraining. The central gap is that direct training of nonlinear recurrence requires sequentially generating the latent memory trajectory (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Token-wise recurrent Transformer:** The decoder writes one latent state per token and injects shifted memories into gated key/value features within ordinary sliding-window attention. Its token and memory windows remain fixed at inference (§2.2–2.3, Eqs. 6–10).

- **Lifted parallel training:** A stronger causal prefiller \(Q\) constructs memory targets for all positions in parallel. Decoder \(P\) consumes their one-step-shifted trajectory, predicts tokens, and learns to match the targets through a consistency loss (§2.1, Eqs. 3–5).

- **Decoder-only recurrent deployment:** Training uses two sequence-parallel Transformer passes, but inference discards \(Q\) and recurrently feeds \(P\)’s own memories back through the same channel (Figure 1; §2.3).

- **Parameter sharing:** The prefiller and decoder can share Transformer blocks, with separate decoder residual-scaling parameters. A separate-prefiller variant is also evaluated (§2.2).

These results establish improvements within the nanochat d20 setup under a shared tokenizer, pipeline, optimizer family, sequence length, and evaluation scripts. They do not establish scaling behavior, statistical significance, actual deployment throughput, or superior retention over contexts substantially longer than the 2,048-token training sequences.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The experiments were compute-constrained and are described as preliminary rather than a definitive exploration (§5).

- **Author-stated:** The tradeoff between prefiller strength and decoder capacity, the best parameter-sharing pattern, efficient deployment kernels, and alternative memory-injection mechanisms remain unresolved (§5).

- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation uses one 20-layer, width-1,280 configuration trained for 43.52B tokens, so generalization across scales and training budgets is untested (§4).

- **Analyst inference:** Although the architecture targets persistent long-term memory, the reported evaluation contains no explicit long-range retrieval, copying, or context-length extrapolation study; the training context is 2,048 tokens (§4).

- **Analyst inference:** Training uses prefiller memories while inference uses decoder-generated memories (§2.1–2.3), but the supplied results do not separately quantify error accumulation during long recurrent rollouts.

- **Analyst inference:** Table 1 gives single scores without seeds, uncertainty intervals, or significance tests, making the robustness of small differences unclear.

- **Analyst inference:** The paper claims matched inference cache and attention cost, but reports no measured latency, throughput, memory use, or training-cost comparison. Artifact availability is also not established.

## Open research questions

1. **How well does the recurrent memory preserve information over much longer sequences?** The paper reports no controlled retrieval or extrapolation experiment beyond its 2,048-token training setting. A next experiment could test recall and language-model loss as relevant information is placed progressively farther beyond the 512-token window.

2. **Does decoder-memory drift accumulate during deployment?** Training conditions \(P\) on prefiller states, whereas inference feeds back \(P\)’s own states. Compare teacher-forced and recurrent rollouts over increasing lengths while measuring memory-consistency error and next-token loss.

3. **Which prefiller–decoder sharing pattern gives the best quality–memory tradeoff?** Only mostly shared and fully separate stacks are evaluated. An ablation could independently share embeddings, attention projections, MLPs, or selected layers while recording quality and training parameter memory.

4. **What is the real systems cost of recurrent K/V injection?** The paper provides asymptotic cache claims but no deployment measurements. Benchmark optimized Maglev and SWA kernels at matched batch sizes and sequence lengths for latency, throughput, cache memory, and energy.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described architecture and reported Table 1 comparisons because the supplied text includes the method equations, experimental setup, and complete result table.

Material missing information includes random seeds and uncertainty, training and inference wall-clock costs, hardware, measured cache usage and throughput, long-context stress tests, recurrent-drift analysis, detailed ablations of gates and consistency loss, scaling experiments, and any confirmed release of code or checkpoints.
