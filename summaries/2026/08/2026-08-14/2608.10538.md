# SKILLER: Language-Level Reinforcement Learning for Reusable Skill Extraction in Small Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10538) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10538)


## One-sentence takeaway

SKILLER uses verifier-grounded natural-language policy iteration to produce reusable, executor-specific skills that improve small Qwen agent models without updating their weights, although the retrieved text omits most underlying table values.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses the mismatch between agent skills written for frontier models and the behavior of compact language/vision-language models. The authors argue that smaller executors may skip verification, hallucinate tool arguments, or fail under complex branching instructions, making direct skill transfer unreliable despite its potential to lower inference costs.

Existing skill-generation methods are described as primarily optimized for frontier models rather than the constraints of compact executors. The identified gap is therefore automatic generation of procedural skills tailored to a particular small model’s observed failures (Introduction; Related Work, “Skill acquisition and evolution”).

## Approach and contributions

- **Textual skills as policies:** SKILLER optimizes the natural-language skill bundle instead of model parameters. A frozen compact executor interacts with benchmark tools and an official verifier (Method, Eqs. 1–4).
- **Language-level actor–critic loop:** A frontier-model critic diagnoses causal failures using the task, current rollout, successful reference trajectory, verifier evidence, and replay history. A frontier-model actor applies bounded `Insert`, `Replace`, `Create`, or `Delete` operations (Method, Eqs. 5–7; Figure 2).
- **Reusable executable artifacts:** Updates may create deterministic task-local helper scripts, while replay memory and snapshot rollback protect previously successful behavior (Method, “Replay Memory” and “Actor”; Appendix E).
- **Compact-model evaluation:** The method is evaluated with Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-4B on five benchmark families against no-skill execution, human skills where available, three open-source methods, and Manus (Experimental Setup).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Restricted benchmark coverage:** SkillsBench uses only 26 single-skill tasks. SWE-Skills-Bench retains 10 tasks where the original benchmark found that skills changed performance; the authors explicitly warn that this is not representative of the full benchmark (Appendix D).
- **Within-benchmark generalization:** GAIA and EarthBench held-out samples come from stratified splits of the same benchmarks. This is narrower than cross-domain or cross-benchmark transfer (Appendix D).
- **Nonmonotonic optimization:** Later edits can reduce transfer performance; step 2 is best on the reported SkillLearnBench trajectory. Snapshot rollback mitigates regressions but indicates that a fixed final iteration is not reliably optimal (Appendices E and G).
- **Limited executor coverage:** Experiments use only Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-4B in OpenCode, so applicability to other compact models and harnesses is unestablished (Experimental Setup).
- **Dependence on privileged optimization evidence:** Successful reference trajectories and official verifier feedback are used during skill construction, which may not exist for real-world tasks (Problem Definition).
- **Author-stated method boundary:** The critic can reshape information-seeking procedure but cannot supply facts that were never retrieved (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference — evaluation uncertainty:** Three-run means are reported, but the supplied text gives no variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete cost comparison:** Strong-model generation costs exclude downstream executor tokens, and Manus token use is unavailable; the complete end-to-end cost advantage therefore cannot be assessed (Experimental Setup; Appendix G).

## Open research questions

1. **Does SKILLER transfer across executor families?** Only two sizes of Qwen3.5 are tested. A next experiment could generate skills for one compact-model family and evaluate both direct transfer and re-optimization on several unrelated executors.

2. **How much privileged evidence is necessary?** The full method uses reference trajectories and verifier diagnostics, while ablations show both matter. A controlled study could vary reference quality, verifier sparsity, and availability of partial credit across identical tasks.

3. **Can stopping and rollback be selected without repeated test evaluation?** SkillLearnBench performance peaks before the last update. A next experiment could compare validation-based early stopping, uncertainty-aware edit acceptance, and the current snapshot policy on held-out tasks.

4. **Do generated skills remain reusable under larger distribution shifts?** Current zero-shot evaluation holds out samples from the same two benchmarks. A stronger test could train a shared skill on one benchmark or environment version and evaluate it on new domains, tools, and output contracts.
