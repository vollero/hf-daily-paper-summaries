# Alaya-EVOKE: From Linear-Scaling Supervision to Endless World

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13546) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13546)


## One-sentence takeaway

Evoke combines bounded, camera-indexed geometric memory with long-horizon teacher distillation to produce a three-step interactive video world model whose per-chunk context and computation do not grow with session duration, though recall remains finite and coarse.

## Problem and motivation

Interactive world models need persistent scene memory, prompt and camera responsiveness, and long-duration generation. Existing approaches often retain history in denoiser context or key-value caches, making per-step cost grow or forcing information eviction; few-step students are additionally limited by the temporal horizon and conditioning behavior of their teachers (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

The authors identify two distinct failure modes: gradual temporal/content drift requires long-horizon supervision, while inconsistency on revisiting an observed location requires explicit retrieval of that particular spatial state. They argue that neither longer supervision nor stored geometry alone solves both problems (Sec. 3.2, p. 7).

## Approach and contributions

- **Bounded recurrent generation:** Each 1.5-second chunk uses fixed local history, current text and camera controls, and a view-aligned render retrieved from an external world-state bank. The bank has bounded read, write, retention, and eviction, so denoiser context and positional span are independent of elapsed session time (Secs. 3.1, 3.4–3.5).

- **Long-horizon interactive teacher:** A Wan2.2 A14B-based teacher uses chunk-wise sparse attention over local, compressed nearby, selected distant, and linear global-state information. It also accepts chunk-level text conditions. The authors claim approximately linear rather than quadratic attention growth with sequence length (Sec. 3.3; Fig. 5).

- **Long-window distillation:** Full-window distribution matching scores a roughly 31.4-second self-forced trajectory while detaching history between chunks, separating the supervision horizon from the backpropagation horizon. This transfers supervision to a three-step, classifier-free-guidance-free student (Sec. 3.3, Eq. 3).

- **Pose-addressed geometric memory:** Generated frames are lifted using monocular depth and known cameras, stored as world-space geometry, and rendered when camera co-visibility indicates a revisit. Visibility masks suppress unsupported geometry (Sec. 3.4).

- **Timed interaction:** Per-chunk prompts allow elements to be introduced or withdrawn during a rollout, although the paper does not causally isolate this ability to the long-horizon teacher (Secs. 3.3, 4.4; Fig. 8).

These results support bounded per-call computation, useful but limited geometric recall, and improved photometric stability from the long-horizon teacher. They do not establish permanent scene identity, pixel-faithful memory, real-time end-to-end generation, or a uniform long-range-consistency benefit. Small benchmark margins and protocol mismatches also weaken ranking claims.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The geometric memory primarily preserves coarse scene structure; fine object identity, appearance, and detail remain limited. It also lacks an explicit representation of evolving dynamic state (Conclusion, p. 14).

- **Author-stated:** Further acceleration is needed for genuinely real-time interaction (Conclusion). A full 1.5-second chunk takes 4.01 seconds in the shipped instrumented configuration (Fig. 10).

- **Author-stated:** Recall is limited to retained coverage rather than every previously visited location. Hour-scale experiments keep only 90 seconds of geometry (Sec. 3.4).

- **Author-stated:** Long-horizon teacher improvements are demonstrated for photometric stability, not content-descriptor consistency or sharpness; the final continuation checkpoint adds no measured drift advantage (Sec. 4.3; Appendix C).

- **Author-stated:** Timed-control comparisons do not significantly distinguish short- and long-teacher variants, so per-chunk conditioning is an interface rather than an isolated causal explanation (Sec. 4.4; Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark ranks should be treated cautiously because sampling budgets are unmatched, VBench protocols deviate from official settings, and the 0.05–0.1 margins are described by the authors as too small to over-interpret (Appendix E; Table 7).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is constrained by unspecified internal training data and absent details in the supplied text about released weights, training compute beyond the distillation configuration, and complete hyperparameters (Sec. 4.1; Appendix A).

## Open research questions

1. **Can memory preserve fine-grained identity rather than coarse geometry?** The revisit plateau is only 15.4–17.8 dB, and the authors acknowledge weak detail preservation. A next experiment could compare the current point-based bank with object-centric or semantic memory on long-delay identity and texture metrics.

2. **How can the state bank represent dynamic objects and state changes?** Its current formulation writes and retrieves geometry but does not explicitly model persistent motion or evolving object state. A controlled benchmark could revisit moving or manipulated objects after varying delays and test a temporally updateable memory.

3. **Which teacher component causes the stability gain?** Window-length detectability sweeps and timed-conditioning comparisons do not isolate a simple causal factor. A factorial ablation could independently vary attention horizon, self-forced rollout length, prompt granularity, and full-window scoring under matched compute.

4. **Can Evoke reach real-time end-to-end latency without weakening recall?** The full geometric path substantially exceeds generated duration. Experiments could jointly vary VAE compression, retrieved-view count, token pruning, and denoising architecture while tracking latency, revisit PSNR, and WBench scores.
