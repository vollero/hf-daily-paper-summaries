# RetrievalRouter: Joint Modality and Architecture Selection for Document Retrieval

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.25625) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25625)


## One-sentence takeaway

RetrievalRouter uses query text to choose among sparse, dense, and reranked text or multimodal retrievers, producing accuracy–latency trade-offs that outperform every evaluated static neural pipeline on both dimensions.

## Problem and motivation

Document-retrieval pipelines differ in modality—extracted text versus rendered page images—and architecture—sparse, dense, late-interaction, or reranking. No fixed configuration works best for every query: visually complex documents favor multimodal retrieval, nuanced textual queries may favor text retrieval, and fine-grained interaction improves difficult cases but costs more latency. The authors identify the missing capability as query-level joint selection of modality and architecture over a single corpus, rather than committing all queries to one pipeline (Sections 1 and 4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Joint query-aware routing:** RetrievalRouter selects one of five arms—BM25, text-dense, text-rerank, multimodal-dense, or multimodal-rerank—from query text alone. Pure late-interaction pipelines remain static baselines but are excluded as near-redundant routing actions (Section 4.1).

- **Accuracy–efficiency objective:** Per-query rewards combine nDCG@5 and normalized wall-clock latency, controlled by \(\lambda\). Separate routers trained at different \(\lambda\) settings trace the accuracy–latency frontier (Equation 1, Section 4.1).

- **Soft-label training:** A frozen Qwen3-0.6B encoder with LoRA and a linear decision head learns from temperature-scaled distributions over all arms’ rewards, minimizing KL divergence rather than using potentially arbitrary hard winners (Sections 4.2–4.3; Equations 2–3).

- **Comparative pipeline analysis and released assets:** The study evaluates seven static pipelines across 11 datasets and releases code, training scripts, and per-query pipeline labels for more than 80,000 queries (Section 1; Data and Reproducibility).

These results establish an in-domain accuracy–latency advantage over the evaluated static systems and the authors’ five-arm adaptation of one prior strategy-selection method. They do not establish zero-shot cross-domain performance, multilingual robustness, superiority over every adaptive-retrieval method, or production efficiency on hardware other than the reported H100 setup.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated—storage and memory:** RetrievalRouter maintains four vector indices plus BM25. The multimodal late-interaction index is approximately 39 GB, over \(13\times\) the approximately 3 GB text-dense index, and simultaneously maintaining the pipelines requires 40 GB of VRAM (Limitations; Table 3).

- **Author-stated—in-domain evaluation:** Every dataset uses an 80/10/10 intra-dataset split. The router may exploit domain-specific lexical cues rather than generalizable indicators of retrieval difficulty (Limitations; Appendix A).

- **Author-stated—query-only ambiguity:** Queries may not reveal the target document’s layout, leaving routing underdetermined when the optimal choice depends on document structure (Limitations).

- **Author-stated—restricted scope:** Evaluation covers English financial, scientific, and open-domain documents, and the action space excludes generative LLM rerankers and downstream generator selection (Limitations; Ethical Considerations).

- **Analyst inference—limited run-level reliability:** Test results come from one training run per \(\lambda\) with seed 42, so sensitivity to initialization is not measured (Appendix A, Hyperparameters).

- **Analyst inference—baseline adaptation uncertainty:** The closest adaptive baseline was extended by the authors from sparse-versus-dense selection to five pipelines. The supplied text does not provide evidence that this extension is optimal or equally favorable to the baseline (Section 5.2).

- **Analyst inference—missing component ablations:** The supplied text reports no controlled ablations of soft versus hard targets within the same router, query-encoder size, LoRA, temperature beyond validation selection, or individual modality/architecture arms. Thus, the contribution of each design choice is not isolated.

## Open research questions

1. **Can routing generalize to unseen domains rather than known query distributions?** This remains open because evaluation uses intra-dataset splits. A next experiment could train on selected benchmark families and test zero-shot on held-out datasets and domains.

2. **Can inexpensive document-side signals close the query-only oracle gap?** Query text cannot reveal all target-layout properties. A next experiment could compare query-only routing with routing augmented by metadata or a low-cost exploratory retrieval pass, measuring both accuracy gains and added latency.

3. **Can storage-efficient cascades match the router’s frontier?** The method requires several large indices and 40 GB of VRAM, while the authors explicitly identify cascades as an alternative. A next experiment could compare routing and cascading under equal storage, memory, and latency budgets.

4. **Which training choices actually drive routing quality?** Soft targets are motivated qualitatively, but key ablations are absent from the supplied text. A next experiment could hold the encoder and arms fixed while comparing hard labels, soft rewards, temperatures, and reward normalizations across multiple seeds.
