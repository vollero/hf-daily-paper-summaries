# GUI-Primitives: Diagnosing Spatial Reasoning Failures in Vision-Language GUI Grounding

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21832) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21832)


## One-sentence takeaway

GUI-PRIMITIVES shows that current vision-language models’ GUI grounding failures arise predominantly from locating the relevant candidate elements, while relational discrimination remains especially weak for synthetic containment and occlusion cases.

## Problem and motivation

Computer-use agents must translate relational instructions such as “left of Save” into click coordinates, but existing end-to-end and grounding benchmarks combine spatial understanding with planning, perception, and action or do not distinguish individual spatial primitives. The authors address this diagnostic gap by testing whether models bind a changed spatial expression to the corresponding GUI element while holding the screenshot and anchor fixed (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled benchmark:** GUI-PRIMITIVES contains 994 items—497 contrastive pairs—covering horizontal and vertical position, containment, alignment, proximity, list ordinal, and occlusion. Each pair fixes the screenshot and anchor while changing the relation and target (§3; Table 1).
- **Mixed-source evaluation:** It combines 290 items mined from real UI-Vision screenshots with 704 procedurally rendered items; containment and occlusion are synthetic-only (§3.3; Table 6).
- **Human-verified core:** Five annotators assessed 196 items, retaining a 185-item clean subset with well-formedness κ=0.942 and target-selection κ=0.787 (§3.4; Appendix F).
- **Failure decomposition:** Predictions from 19 VLMs are classified as landing in the target, distractor, neither candidate, or being invalid, separating candidate localization from relation-conditioned selection (§4; Appendix R).
- **Intervention and external-comparison study:** The authors test oracle two-candidate Set-of-Mark prompting, chain-of-thought prompting, and activation steering, and compare model-level performance with ScreenSpot-Pro (§§6–7).
- **Released assets:** The benchmark, predictions, analysis code, and parser tests are reported as publicly released (Abstract; §1; Appendix L).

| Shortcut controls show dependence on matched visual input | Qwen2.5-VL falls from 22.0% to 6.5% with a blank canvas, 14.8% with shuffled screenshots, and 16.7% with σ=12 blur; the blur drop is significant but below the preregistered ten-point criterion. | Figure 3; §§5.1, N.1 |

The results establish severe coordinate-grounding failures under this benchmark and support the authors’ decomposition that off-candidate localization dominates aggregate error. They do not establish that candidate localization is the sole cause: containment and occlusion show no measurable relation signal, alignment is sensitive to the classification rule, and pair consistency also indicates dependence on relation wording. The ScreenSpot-Pro result is an association across only ten models, not evidence that primitive competence causes downstream performance. SoM supplies the gold candidate set and therefore measures candidate discrimination under an oracle scaffold rather than improvement on the original task.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Containment and occlusion are synthetic-only, limiting external validity for those relation-specific findings (§9; Appendix V).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation stops at static, pre-action English click grounding; drag actions, other writing directions and languages, and end-to-end OSWorld success are untested (§9).
- **Author-stated:** SoM uses only two oracle-designated marks and may not generalize to crowded interfaces or imperfect detector-generated candidates (§§8–9).
- **Author-stated:** Point-in-box and loose accuracy do not characterize structured errors such as direction inversion or anchor collapse (§9).
- **Author-stated:** The ScreenSpot-Pro correlation uses ten models, has a wide bootstrap interval of [0.11, 0.98], and becomes less significant in some leave-one-out analyses; per-primitive regression coefficients are explicitly treated as fragile (§6; Appendix O).
- **Author-stated:** Closed APIs can change, and provider-side image downsampling required substantial coordinate rescaling; future runs may differ (Appendices E and V).
- **Analyst inference:** The human/model gap is not a direct matched comparison because humans selected between two candidates while models generated unconstrained coordinates; the authors explicitly acknowledge this protocol mismatch (§5.2; Appendix R).
- **Analyst inference:** The synthetic generator reuses a limited number of anchor slots for some primitives. Clustered intervals preserve the reported below-reference findings, but stimulus diversity remains narrower than the item count alone suggests (Appendix L/R).
- **Text ambiguity:** Appendix J.2 says list ordinal is synthetic-only, whereas §3.3, Table 6, and Appendix V report 24 real list-ordinal items; the latter account is better supported but the inconsistency is unresolved in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Which perceptual component causes off-candidate predictions?** Blur cannot separate OCR, icon recognition, spatial encoding, and cross-modal binding (§5.1; Appendix S). A next experiment could independently mask text, replace text with matched random strings, and perturb icons while preserving layout.

2. **Do containment and occlusion failures transfer to real GUIs?** Their absence of measurable relation-word signal is demonstrated only on synthetic screenshots (§9). A next experiment could construct a human-annotated real-GUI subset with parent–child and overlay–target metadata.

3. **Can candidate proposals help without oracle boxes?** SoM’s gains rely on marking exactly the target and distractor (§7.1). A next experiment could replace oracle marks with accessibility-tree or detector proposals and sweep candidate-set size, recall, and clutter.

4. **Does primitive competence predict complete agent success?** The paper compares only with ScreenSpot-Pro, not planning and execution (§9). A next experiment could measure whether per-model primitive profiles predict success or failure on a controlled OSWorld pre-action and end-to-end task slice.
