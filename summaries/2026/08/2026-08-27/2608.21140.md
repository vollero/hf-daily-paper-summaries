# A Modular Agent for Reliable and Auditable Spatial Relation Verification in CT Scans

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21140) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21140)


## One-sentence takeaway

A modular agent that parses spatial questions, detects anatomy, and applies deterministic centroid rules achieves 94.1% accuracy on held-out axial-CT questions while exposing where errors occur.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses binary verification of pairwise anatomical spatial relations in axial CT slices. This capability matters because radiological reporting depends on accurate anatomical localization, while spatial errors can have clinical consequences.

The authors identify two gaps: end-to-end medical VLMs perform near chance on controlled spatial reasoning, and their answers generally lack traceable image evidence. Existing medical agents do not specifically evaluate anatomical spatial relations as controlled, image-grounded, auditable intermediate facts (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Modular agent architecture:** A VLM-based controller retains a conversational interface but routes spatial questions to specialized tools through LangChain (Method, pp. 3–4; Fig. 1).
- **Explicit verification pipeline:** The system extracts a relation tuple, maps entities to detector classes, selects the highest-confidence YOLO bounding box for each class, and determines the answer from horizontal or vertical ordering of box centroids (Method, pp. 4–5).
- **Auditable outputs:** It records the prompt, parsed query, ontology match, detections, geometric comparison, result, and audit information, enabling attribution to the earliest failing stage (Method, p. 5).
- **Controlled comparison:** Direct MedGemma 4b and Qwen2-VL 7b prompting are compared with hybrid versions under the same prompt interface on a held-out MIRP RQ1 test set (Experimental Setup, pp. 5–6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best hybrid result | Qwen2-VL + hybrid: 94.1% accuracy and 94.2% F1 on 938 held-out MIRP RQ1 image-question pairs | Table 1, p. 7; Experimental Setup, p. 5 |
| Gain over matched direct baseline | Qwen2-VL direct: 51.6% accuracy and 56.8% F1; hybrid improves accuracy by 42.5 percentage points under the same prompt interface | Results, p. 6; Table 1, p. 7 |
| Improvement with the medical-domain VLM | MedGemma direct: 51.8% accuracy and 67.2% F1; MedGemma + hybrid: 91.6% accuracy and 91.3% F1 | Table 1, p. 7 |
| No invalid binary outputs | Invalid-output rate was 0 for evaluated systems under strict scoring, which treats invalid, failed, or non-binary outputs as incorrect | Experimental Setup and Results, p. 6 |
| Residual errors are localized to three stages | Of 55 Qwen2-VL hybrid failures: 28 imprecise localizations (50.9%), 15 parsing/query errors (27.3%), and 12 missing detections (21.8%) | Table 2, p. 8 |
| Detector scale and isolation | Detector covers 61 anatomical classes, was trained on 421,023 instances, and excludes test patients from detector training | Experimental Setup, p. 5 |

The evidence establishes a large improvement over two directly evaluated VLM baselines under a shared prompt protocol and demonstrates operational stage-wise auditing. It does not establish clinical effectiveness, full-volume spatial reasoning, or superiority over leaderboard systems: their published results use different evaluation data and are explicitly described as non-paired comparisons (Table 1, p. 7).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation is limited to pairwise 2D relations in axial CT slices. It excludes volumetric reasoning, distance-sensitive relations, containment, overlap, and multi-structure consistency (Discussion, pp. 7–8).
- **Author-stated dependency:** Performance is bounded by component quality; detection and localization account for 40 of the 55 remaining errors in the best configuration (Discussion, p. 7; Table 2).
- **Comparison limitation:** Leaderboard systems were not re-evaluated, and their data differ from the held-out test set, so those comparisons are not strictly paired (Table 1, p. 7).
- **Analyst inference:** The evidence does not isolate the contribution of each architectural choice because no ablations compare centroid rules, detector variants, parser-only configurations, or alternative box-selection policies.
- **Analyst inference:** Failure attribution depends on identifying the earliest erroneous intermediate state. The paper notes that some LLM-fallback cases lacked a stored raw pre-mapping query and were conservatively assigned to parsing, introducing uncertainty into category counts (Table 2, p. 8).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond MIRP RQ1, the covered 61 anatomy classes, and the supplied dataset mixture is untested.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the approach generalize to 3D spatial reasoning?** Only individual axial slices are evaluated. A next experiment could use patient-isolated CT volumes with relations defined across slices and compare 2D centroid rules against 3D localization.
2. **Which modules drive the performance gain?** The paper provides failure attribution but no component ablation. A factorial experiment could vary parser, ontology mapping, detector, box-selection rule, and geometric verifier independently.
3. **Can localization improvements reduce the dominant error source?** Imprecise localization causes 28 of 55 failures. A next experiment could compare bounding-box centers with segmentation centroids or uncertainty-aware geometric decisions on the same test set.
4. **How robust is parsing to unconstrained clinical language?** Fifteen failures are assigned to parsing/query extraction, while evaluation uses a standardized prompt. Testing paraphrases, multi-clause questions, and report-derived queries would measure robustness beyond the benchmark wording.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, evaluation setting, and numerical results because the supplied text includes the complete method description and result tables.
