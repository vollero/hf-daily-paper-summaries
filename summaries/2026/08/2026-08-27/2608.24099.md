# Are Android GUI Agents Robust Against Runtime Anomalies? AnTrap: Evaluating Agents in Dynamic Adversarial Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24099) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24099)


## One-sentence takeaway

ANTRAP shows that 16 Android GUI agents consistently lose task success under dynamically injected runtime anomalies, while trap-specific GRPO improves mostly single-step state/action recovery but offers little benefit for multi-step contextual failures.

## Problem and motivation

Android GUI agents operate in environments where pop-ups, rendering delays, unintended actions, hallucinated UI elements, and execution loops can disrupt otherwise valid plans. Recovery is necessary for real-world deployment because anomalies arise both externally from the environment and internally from agent errors.

The authors argue that existing mobile benchmarks mainly test clean execution, static perturbations, or limited anomaly types, leaving no systematic dynamic evaluation of recovery across the agent’s complete state–thinking–action trajectory (Introduction; Sections 2.1–2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Runtime-anomaly taxonomy:** The STAR taxonomy organizes anomalies into State, Thinking, Action, and Round layers, further divided into ten subcategories derived from a pilot study of real-device trajectories (Section 3.2; Table 2; Appendix B).

- **Dynamic benchmark:** ANTRAP extends AndroidWorld from 116 to 236 tasks and injects anomalies stochastically during live execution while aiming to preserve task solvability. Injection type, timing, frequency, and content are configurable (Sections 3.3–3.4; Figure 2; Appendix A.1).

- **Broad model evaluation:** The benchmark evaluates 16 proprietary and open-weight GUI-capable models using rule-based task success and Pass@3, comparing trapped execution with the clean environment (Sections 4.1–4.2; Table 3).

- **Training-based diagnosis:** Original-environment and trap-specific GRPO experiments on GUI-Owl-7B and UI-TARS-7B distinguish anomalies that respond to adversarial exposure from multi-step contextual failures that remain difficult (Section 5; Table 4).

The evidence establishes degradation under the paper’s Android emulator, task suite, trap schedule, prompts, and Pass@3 protocol. It also supports a distinction between anomaly categories that respond strongly or weakly to category-specific GRPO for two trained models.

It does not establish robustness on other operating systems, natural anomaly frequencies, unseen trap implementations, or whether the observed training pattern holds broadly across all 16 evaluated models. Claims that contextual failures reflect inadequate history integration are author hypotheses rather than directly isolated causal findings (Sections 4.2 and 5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The 236 tasks may not represent the global range of Android GUI operations because online task initialization and validation are expensive (Limitations).
- Adversarial supervised fine-tuning was not tested because of budget and trajectory-annotation constraints (Limitations).
- ANTRAP is primarily diagnostic; the paper does not offer a scalable general-purpose robustness-training solution (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- Evaluation uses controlled synthetic interventions and exactly one trap event per reported episode, so it does not establish behavior under naturally occurring or compounded anomalies (Appendix A.1–A.2).
- Trap-specific GRPO trains and evaluates on the same subcategory. Thus, its strong gains demonstrate within-category adaptation, not transfer to unseen anomaly categories or mechanisms (Section 5.3).
- The training diagnosis covers only GUI-Owl-7B and UI-TARS-7B, limiting generalization of the “environment-learnable versus reasoning-bottlenecked” distinction (Section 5.1).
- No uncertainty intervals, significance tests, or run-to-run variance are reported in the supplied text, making the reliability of small differences unclear.
- GPT models exclude Grounding Error because they use Set of Marks, so coverage is not identical across all models (Appendix A.2).
- The reported 91% human-validation figure lacks per-category agreement statistics in the supplied text; failures were revised, but post-revision validation results are not separately quantified (Section 3.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Do learned recovery strategies transfer to unseen anomaly mechanisms?**  
   Trap-specific training and evaluation use the same subcategory. A next experiment could train on selected operator variants—such as template pop-ups or fixed coordinate offsets—and test on held-out content, parameters, and implementations within and across categories.

2. **Can explicit memory or self-monitoring solve round-layer traps?**  
   Round-layer gains remain below three points after GRPO, but the claimed history-integration bottleneck is not causally isolated. Compare the same base model with trajectory summarization, action-effect verification, loop detection, and controlled history ablations.

3. **How do agents handle multiple interacting anomalies?**  
   Reported evaluation episodes contain exactly one trap event, whereas deployment may involve overlapping failures. Evaluate controlled sequences and combinations of state, action, and round traps while varying order and frequency.

4. **Can adversarial SFT complement online RL?**  
   The authors did not test adversarial SFT. A direct comparison among SFT, GRPO, and combined training on matched data and compute could measure clean-task retention, within-category recovery, and held-out-trap generalization.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes the main paper, tables, and implementation appendix.

Material information unavailable or insufficiently reported includes statistical uncertainty and repeated-run variance; detailed Pass@3 aggregation; per-category human agreement and post-revision validation rates; complete per-task results; generalization to unseen trap operators; and evidence that the announced code and assets are publicly accessible.
