# Is Next-Chunk Reasoning RL Really Better than SFT? Revisiting Training Strategies under no-CoT Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23256) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23256)


## One-sentence takeaway

In a controlled Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base study with identical downstream RLVR, jointly fine-tuning on no-CoT and long-CoT data achieved higher final reasoning accuracy than next-token or next-sentence reasoning RL while using over 60× less training compute.

## Problem and motivation

Next-chunk reasoning RL attempts to exploit worked solutions and derivations that contain useful knowledge but lack explicit chain-of-thought traces: a model generates an implicit trace and receives a reward for predicting subsequent text. Prior evaluations mainly compared this approach with SFT on no-CoT data alone, which can disrupt the long-CoT output format needed by later reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR).

The authors argue that this leaves the central comparison unresolved: whether improvements arise from the RL objective itself or simply from better exposure to no-CoT data. They therefore evaluate the complete post-training pipeline and introduce Mixed SFT—joint training on no-CoT and long-CoT examples—as the missing baseline (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled comparison:** Starting from Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, the study compares long-CoT-only Reasoning SFT, Sequential SFT, Mixed SFT, next-token reasoning (NTR/RPT), and next-sentence reasoning (NSR/RLPT). Every initialization receives the same GRPO-based RLVR stage on DAPO-Math-17K (Section 2; Appendix B).

- **Mixed SFT baseline:** Mixed SFT uses the union of 421K no-CoT AoPS solutions and 152K teacher-generated, correctness-filtered long-CoT trajectories in one supervised stage. Sequential SFT processes these sources in separate stages (Section 2; Appendix B).

- **Pipeline-level evaluation:** Models are measured before and after RLVR on six competition-math benchmarks and three designated OOD benchmarks, with post-RLVR accuracy treated as primary (Section 2).

- **Mechanistic analysis:** The authors test NTR’s entropy-based target selection, inspect reconstruction traces, introduce an entropy-controlled NTR variant, add NTR/NSR after Mixed SFT, analyze Mixed SFT’s format recovery, and use a 100-problem retention probe to compare Mixed and Sequential SFT (Section 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed SFT produced the best mean in-domain result after RLVR. | Across six ID benchmarks, Mixed SFT averaged **67.4**, versus **64.2** for NTR and **63.6** for NSR: gains of **3.1** and **3.7 points**. | Section 3.1; Table 2 |
| Mixed SFT led both NCR variants on all three designated OOD benchmarks. | Post-RLVR scores were **9.24 HLE pass@1**, **60.98 GPQA-Diamond avg@4**, and **75.84 MMLU-Pro pass@1**. NTR scored 7.68/57.70/74.03 and NSR 7.72/56.31/74.89. | Table 2; Section 3.1 |
| Mixed SFT was much cheaper than NCR on the same no-CoT data. | SFT consumed **65.6 GPU-hours**, compared with **4,283.7** for NSR (**65×**) and **4,608.1** for NTR (**70×**), measured at NCR’s 160-step peak pre-RLVR checkpoint. | Figure 2 |
| Low intermediate accuracy did not imply a low final ceiling. | Across all nine benchmarks, Mixed SFT rose from **27.5 pre-RLVR** to **61.1 post-RLVR**; other strategies began at 45.9–48.8 and improved by only 3.3–10.3 points. | Figure 3; Section 3.3 |
| Adding NCR after full Mixed-SFT exposure did not help. | Mixed SFT+RLVR outperformed Mixed SFT+NTR+RLVR and Mixed SFT+NSR+RLVR on every listed benchmark—for example, GPQA-Diamond was **60.98** versus **56.44** and **56.36**. | Table 8; Observation 4 |
| Entropy control prevented NTR’s collapse but lowered final accuracy. | The controlled variant maintained entropy near **0.52** and response length near **640 tokens**, yet underperformed vanilla NTR on all nine benchmarks; AIME25 was **83.71 vs. 84.38**, for example. | Figure 6; Table 9 |
| Mixed SFT retained more no-CoT training signal than Sequential SFT. | On a randomly sampled 100-problem training-corpus probe, post-RLVR avg@8 was **68.63** for Mixed SFT and **59.19** for Sequential SFT. | Table 4; Observation 6 |

The controlled initialization and shared RLVR stage support the authors’ claim that Mixed SFT is a stronger initialization than the tested NTR and NSR implementations in this setup. The diagnostic experiments also weaken explanations based solely on token coverage or entropy collapse.

They do not establish that Mixed SFT universally dominates all possible next-chunk RL algorithms, model scales, data mixtures, domains, or RLVR recipes. Claims that generated traces are not “genuine reasoning” rely partly on behavioral proxies and example inspection rather than a definitive measure of internal reasoning.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only one fixed no-CoT/long-CoT mixing ratio was tested, leaving its effect on format disruption, knowledge absorption, and post-RLVR performance unknown (Appendix A).

- **Author-stated:** Transfer to code generation, multilingual tasks, and other forms of no-CoT data was not studied (Appendix A). The appendix says all evaluation benchmarks are mathematical, but the experimental description designates HLE, GPQA-Diamond, and MMLU-Pro as OOD cross-domain or scientific benchmarks; this inconsistency makes the precise domain-coverage limitation ambiguous.

- **Analyst inference:** Results come from one base checkpoint, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, so model-family and scale robustness remain untested (Section 2).

- **Analyst inference:** The long-CoT corpus was generated by one teacher and filtered only by final-answer correctness; the supplied text reports no comparison across teacher sources or analysis of incorrect intermediate reasoning (Section 2; Appendix B).

- **Analyst inference:** The 100-problem retention probe is sampled from training data. It supports differential retention but does not independently demonstrate generalization or prove that forgetting is the sole cause of Sequential SFT’s lower benchmark scores (Observation 6).

- **Analyst inference:** NSR depends on gpt-oss-120b as a generative reward model, whereas NTR uses deterministic prefix matching and SFT uses likelihood training. Thus the comparison is pipeline-level, not a clean isolation of optimization paradigm from reward-model quality (Appendix B).

- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty intervals, multiple training seeds, or statistical significance tests are reported in the supplied text, limiting confidence in small score differences.

- **Reproducibility:** Hyperparameters, hardware, prompts, and decoding settings are supplied, but release of code, trained checkpoints, and the constructed corpora is not established.

## Open research questions

1. **What no-CoT/long-CoT mixture maximizes post-RLVR performance?** Only one ratio was tested. A next experiment should sweep ratios, including data-scarce regimes, while tracking pre-RLVR format compliance, retention, final accuracy, and compute.

2. **Does Mixed SFT remain superior outside math-centric training?** The training corpus is entirely AoPS-derived, and transfer to code, multilingual, and broader scientific data was not tested. The comparison should be repeated with domain-specific no-CoT corpora and corresponding verifiable evaluations.

3. **Do the findings hold across model families and scales?** All reported experiments use one Qwen3 checkpoint. A factorial study across dense and mixture-of-experts models at several scales could test whether capacity or prior formatting behavior changes the ranking.

4. **Can an NCR objective target genuinely long-horizon dependencies?** The tested entropy filter admitted many locally predictable tokens, while sentence prediction also showed local-completion behavior. A plausible experiment would select targets using controlled context removal or measured long-range dependency, then compare against Mixed SFT under matched token exposure and compute.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported comparisons within the paper’s exact model, data, and RLVR setup; **medium** for the proposed mechanistic explanations.

Material missing information includes training variance across seeds, statistical uncertainty, the exact Mixed SFT sampling ratio, comprehensive SFT optimization details beyond one epoch and maximum sequence length, public availability of code/checkpoints/constructed corpora, and evidence across additional model families or non-mathematical training domains.
