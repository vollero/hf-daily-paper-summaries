# The Handoff Tax: Continuing Non-Native Trajectories in LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24358) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24358)


## One-sentence takeaway

Mid-task model switching is directionally asymmetric: full-trajectory escalation recovers less than half the stronger model’s coding-quality advantage, while downshifting preserves a useful cost–quality compromise, and reducing transferred context helps escalation but hurts downshift.

## Problem and motivation

Long-running coding agents may switch from a low-cost/lower-capability model (LC) to a high-cost/higher-capability model (HC), or vice versa. The receiver must continue a trajectory—including reasoning, tool use, observations, and code edits—that another model created. The authors argue that conventional routing work does not adequately address the resulting “handoff tax”: the quality and monetary penalty associated with continuing this non-native trajectory.

The identified gap is a systematic evaluation of how handoff direction, timing, and transferred information jointly affect end-to-end quality and cost in long-horizon agents (Introduction; Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Controlled handoff framework.** The study varies direction (LC→HC escalation or HC→LC downshift), seven difficulty-calibrated switch percentiles, and four interfaces: full trajectory (`Raw`), sender-written summary (`Compactpre`), receiver-written summary (`Compactsuf`), or no trajectory (`Traj-drop`). Every interface preserves the edited working tree (Sections 3.1–3.3; Figure 2).

2. **Large coding-agent evaluation.** Experiments cover two commercial model families on all 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks: 58 configurations per family, 58,000 runs, 2 million API calls, and 36 billion processed tokens (Sections 3.4 and 4).

3. **Normalized cost–quality measures.** Quality Recovery (QRec) measures the fraction of the LC–HC pass-rate gap recovered; Cost-Savings Retention (CSRet) measures the fraction of LC’s cost advantage retained (Section 3.4, Equations 1–2).

4. **Direction-dependent interface finding.** Removing LC trajectory information improves escalation quality, whereas removing HC trajectory information reduces downshift quality across both tested families (Sections 4.1–4.2; Table 8).

5. **Extension beyond coding.** Raw handoffs are also evaluated under incrementally revealed requirements in LiC and progressive evidence collection in BrowseComp, showing that handoff value depends on when task-relevant information becomes available (Section 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Raw escalation recovered less than half the LC–HC coding-quality gap. | On matched SWE-bench switched subsets averaged over seven switch points, QRec was 47% for Claude and 36% for GPT. | Table 1; Section 4.1 |
| Claude Raw escalation was dominated by restarting HC. | Raw achieved 69.2% pass at $1.61; `Abort + HC fresh` achieved the matched HC outcome, 79.2%, at $0.90. Even `LC-full + HC-full` cost $1.12. | Table 1; Section 4.1 |
| Dropping the LC trajectory improved escalation quality. | Relative to Raw, Traj-drop raised QRec from 47% to 64% for Claude and from 36% to 84% for GPT, while preserving working-tree edits. Its CSRet was negative in both cases (−30% and −8%), so it cost more than HC-only. | Table 1; Section 4.1 |
| The Traj-drop escalation gain over Raw was statistically consistent across families. | Pass-rate differences were +3.1 points for Claude (95% CI +1.3 to +5.0) and +12.2 for GPT (+9.2 to +15.4), using 10,000 task-clustered bootstrap resamples. | Table 8; Appendix A.3 |
| Raw downshift produced an intermediate cost–quality point. | Claude rose from 54.6% LC-only pass at $0.41 to 65.6% at $0.51, with QRec 50% and CSRet 80%. GPT Raw achieved 81.0% at $0.41, with QRec 79% and CSRet 14%, versus HC-only at 85.8% and $0.47. | Table 1; Section 4.2 |
| Removing HC trajectory harmed downshift quality. | Traj-drop versus Raw reduced pass rate by 4.7 points for Claude (95% CI −7.3 to −2.1) and 5.6 for GPT (−8.6 to −2.6). | Table 8; Appendix A.3 |
| Interface costs arose through different receiver-side patterns. | Relative to Compactpre, Raw escalation made each post-handoff HC step 2.2× costlier for Claude and 1.6× for GPT; Traj-drop downshift required 1.6× and 2.0× as many post-handoff LC steps, respectively. | Section 4.3; Figures 4 and 6 |
| Information dynamics changed escalation outcomes. | On LiC, Claude Raw escalation achieved QRec 86% and CSRet 36%, versus downshift at 31% and 53%. On BrowseComp, GPT Raw escalation achieved QRec 95.8% but CSRet −30.0%; downshift achieved 56.7% and 76.8%. | Tables 2–3; Section 5 |

The evidence establishes a reproducible directional reversal within the two tested model families: less inherited trajectory benefits HC receivers in coding escalation, while retaining HC context benefits LC receivers in downshift. It also shows that this pattern’s economics depend on the model pair, task difficulty, and information dynamics.

It does not establish a universal causal explanation for why trajectories help or hurt. The cost-per-step and extra-step decompositions are descriptive, and the authors explicitly characterize their reconstruction/anchoring explanations as interpretations rather than experimentally isolated mechanisms.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Only two LC/HC model pairs are tested, and the main interface study uses one coding benchmark, SWE-bench Verified (Limitations, pages 8–9).
- **Author-stated:** LiC and BrowseComp test only Raw transfer, so the interface reversal is established mainly for coding (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Switches are fixed, not adaptively triggered by detected progress or failure (Section 6; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Each SWE-bench task/configuration has one episode, so within-task generation variability is not estimated; LiC and BrowseComp also use one successful generation per task-policy cell (Limitations; Appendices B–C).
- **Author-stated:** Hard-task SWE-bench cells are small, approximately 24–27 instances, making difficulty-conditioned findings exploratory (Section 4.1; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Cost findings depend on provider prices and cache rates; cost and mechanism analyses remain descriptive (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** BrowseComp answers use Claude Opus 4.7 as judge, and the browsing cohort’s prior screen used Claude models, so it is not claimed to be specifically decontaminated for the evaluated GPT models (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Because commercial model defaults differ—temperature 0 for Haiku, provider defaults for the others, and different reasoning-effort settings—some family differences cannot be cleanly attributed solely to capability or price (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Restart controls are composed from matched observed runs rather than executed as contiguous restart trajectories; they provide accounting comparisons but do not measure behavioral effects of an actual restart event (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The text mentions an accompanying experimental artifact but provides no artifact URL or complete contents, limiting reproducibility assessment from the supplied paper alone (Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can an adaptive policy detect when escalation or downshift is worthwhile?** Fixed percentile switches isolate handoff effects but do not model realistic switching decisions. A next experiment could train or evaluate a progress-aware policy that jointly chooses whether, when, and in which direction to switch.

2. **Which trajectory components cause the directional reversal?** The study compares full context, summaries, and total removal but does not separately ablate hypotheses, tool observations, failed commands, or verified edits. A factorial experiment could selectively retain each component while holding the working tree fixed.

3. **Do the findings survive repeated rollouts and broader model pairs?** Single episodes and two families leave stochastic stability and capability-gap effects unresolved. Repeating each condition across seeds and several pricing/capability ratios would estimate variance and interaction effects.

4. **Can structured handoffs outperform both compaction and removal outside coding?** Alternative interfaces were not tested on LiC or BrowseComp. A follow-up could compare structured state records, evidence ledgers, and requirement summaries against Raw transfer across all three information-dynamic settings.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental design and aggregate findings because the supplied text includes the full retrieved paper, detailed protocols, tables, and uncertainty estimates.

Material missing or unresolved:

- No accessible artifact URL or released code/configuration contents are supplied.
- Aggregate retry-attempt counts were not retained.
- Repeated-run variance for identical task/configuration cells is unavailable.
- Statistical intervals are supplied for interface pass-rate contrasts, but not for most cost, difficulty, LiC, or BrowseComp results.
- The paper does not experimentally isolate the semantic trajectory features responsible for anchoring or rework.
