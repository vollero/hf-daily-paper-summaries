# Agent-G^2: Gaussian Guidance for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23318) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23318)


## One-sentence takeaway

Agent-G² samples expert-prefix depth from an online, cluster-conditioned Gaussian, improving long-horizon agent training while avoiding the extra rollouts required to probe for a single task-specific depth.

## Problem and motivation

Hint-based reinforcement learning combats sparse terminal rewards by starting rollouts after part of an expert trajectory, but performance depends strongly on prefix depth: insufficient guidance rarely yields success, whereas excessive guidance removes the reward variation needed for GRPO.

The authors identify two gaps. Shared-depth schedules ignore substantial task-level difficulty variation, while per-sample searches require noisy, expensive probe rollouts. More fundamentally, both assume one optimal depth per task; the paper argues instead that useful depths form an informative neighborhood (Sections 1–2, Figures 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Gaussian depth guidance:** Agent-G² represents guidance ratio as a Gaussian rather than a deterministic scalar and samples one ratio per task (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Online global-local adaptation:** A global baseline follows overall policy progress, while expert-trajectory-length clusters adjust the mean using cluster success and the spread using within-cluster success-rate variance (Equations 1–5).
- **No probing overhead:** The schedule is updated from the same terminal rewards used for GRPO, requiring neither additional depth-search rollouts nor a learned depth predictor (Sections 3.4 and 4.3).
- **Joint learning objective:** Training combines GRPO on post-prefix rollouts with teacher-forced supervision over the sampled expert prefix (Equations 6–7).
- **Empirical characterization:** The authors report that aligned depth informativeness is approximately Gaussian and remains well fitted across several ALFWorld training windows (Figure 3; Table 6).

These results support the authors’ claim that stochastic, cluster-aware guidance can outperform the tested deterministic schedules and non-probing alternatives on the two evaluated benchmarks. They do not establish that Gaussian guidance is universally appropriate, that trajectory length reliably measures difficulty in other domains, or that the method remains advantageous without expert demonstrations. Statistical significance is explicitly discussed only for the \(K=3\) versus \(K=5\) clustering comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Every training task requires an expert trajectory, limiting applicability when demonstrations are unavailable or expensive (Limitations).
- The Gaussian assumption was validated only on the two evaluated benchmarks; multimodal or skewed depth profiles may need richer distributions (Limitations).
- Difficulty clusters are fixed offline using expert-trajectory length and do not adapt as the policy’s relative task difficulty changes (Limitations).

**Analyst inferences**

- The detailed Gaussian-profile diagnostic and most ablations are confined to Qwen2.5-1.5B on ALFWorld, so their transfer to WebShop and the 7B model is not directly demonstrated.
- Bernoulli reward variance is a proxy for training informativeness; the paper does not directly prove that it corresponds to gradient quality or downstream learning benefit.
- Agent-G² is slower per step than every tested shared-depth scheduler, although the authors report faster convergence and lower cost than probing methods (Figure 6, Table 2).
- The method includes several schedule hyperparameters and weak-prior initializations. Values are reported in Table 5, but a broad sensitivity analysis is not supplied.
- Code and training scripts are promised upon publication rather than demonstrated as available in the supplied text, limiting present reproducibility assessment.

## Open research questions

1. **Can guidance work without complete expert trajectories?** The framework currently requires one trajectory per task. Test suboptimal demonstrations, sparse language hints, or partially observed successful traces while holding rollout budget constant.

2. **Would richer adaptive distributions help on non-Gaussian depth profiles?** The paper acknowledges possible skewed or multimodal cases. Compare Gaussian guidance with learned mixtures or nonparametric samplers on tasks whose depth profiles are first measured diagnostically.

3. **Can clusters track changing task difficulty online?** Fixed length bins may become stale as learning progresses. Compare them with online clustering based on rollout success histories, evaluating accuracy, stability, and estimation cost.

4. **Does the Gaussian diagnostic generalize beyond ALFWorld?** The strongest profile analysis is reported for one backbone and benchmark. Repeat the depth sweep on WebShop, the 7B backbone, and additional long-horizon environments using the same fit and mismatch protocol.
