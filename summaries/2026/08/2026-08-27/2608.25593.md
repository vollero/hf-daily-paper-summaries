# JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.25593) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25593)


## One-sentence takeaway

JIT-Agent learns to generate, repair, and evolve task-specific executable agent harnesses, improving fixed LLM backbones across the paper’s nine agent benchmarks while often reducing inference cost relative to fixed harnesses.

## Problem and motivation

Agent performance depends not only on model weights but also on the surrounding harness—memory, planning, action control, and tool/skill orchestration. The authors argue that current harness engineering is largely manual or ahead-of-time (AOT): it optimizes a durable scaffold before the exact future task is known, which may be poorly suited to heterogeneous or instance-specific demands (Section 1; Table 1).

The proposed gap is a learned “model-as-a-harness” system that constructs an executable scaffold just in time for each task, repairs faulty generations, and reuses execution feedback to improve future harnesses without modifying the executor model (Sections 1 and 4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Structured harness synthesis.** JIT-Agent represents each harness as four protocol-constrained modules—memory, planning, action, and capability orchestration—and generates task-specific executable implementations rather than unrestricted agent programs (Sections 3.1–3.2).

2. **HarnessFactory.** The authors reimplement 13 existing scaffold designs under a shared execution protocol, forming the initial seed bank used as synthesis references and as the basis of an evolving harness archive (Table 2; Section 3.2).

3. **Three-stage training.**
   - Stage I learns task-conditioned synthesis from validated teacher-generated harnesses and preferences based jointly on reward, latency, and monetary cost.
   - Stage II learns bounded repair from compiler, interface, tool-call, and runtime diagnostics; training retains failures repaired within at most two rounds.
   - Stage III introduces Evo-GDPO, which rewards candidates for exceeding an archive incumbent while separately normalizing reward, latency, and cost signals (Sections 4.1–4.3; Figure 3).

4. **Static and streaming inference.** Static inference generates several candidates but executes only the selected harness; streaming inference additionally retains frontier-improving harnesses as references for later tasks while keeping model parameters frozen (Section 5).

These experiments support the authors’ claim that task-conditioned harnesses can improve several fixed backbones and offer favorable performance–cost trade-offs within the evaluated systems. They do not isolate which generated module causes each gain, establish statistical significance, or show that the method generalizes beyond the reported benchmarks, model families, protocol, and execution infrastructure.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limitation:** The four-module protocol is deliberately smaller than production runtimes such as Codex or Claude Code. The authors present it as a starting point rather than a reproduction of full production harness capabilities (Section 3.1, “Position”).

- **Author-stated structural assumption:** The method assumes every considered harness can be represented as memory, planning, action, and capability-orchestration modules. Harnesses outside this factorization are not evaluated (Section 3.1, Equation 2).

- **Repair coverage:** Stage-II data includes only failures that a teacher repairs within two rounds, so reliability claims apply to short-horizon, locally recoverable defects rather than arbitrary synthesis failures (Sections 4.2 and 5).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete ablation evidence:** The supplied text does not report ablations separating Stage-I preference learning, Stage-II repair, Evo-GDPO, archive retrieval, candidate count, or individual harness modules. Their independent contributions therefore remain unclear.

- **Analyst inference — evaluation uncertainty:** Repeated-rollout averages are part of the method, but the supplied experimental text gives no sample counts for most main evaluations, variance estimates, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Figure 5 additionally uses benchmark subsets.

- **Analyst inference — streaming attribution:** Figure 6 shows higher streaming endpoint accuracy, but no exact values or controlled ablation distinguishes archive accumulation from ordering effects, retrieval quality, or stochastic generation.

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility gaps:** The supplied text does not state training-data sizes, teacher identity, compute budget, most hyperparameter values, candidate count \(N\), group size, pricing assumptions, or detailed harness-selection procedure.

## Open research questions

1. **Which training stage and harness module produces the gains?**  
   This remains open because no component-wise ablation is reported. A next experiment could remove Stage-II repair and Stage-III Evo-GDPO separately, then freeze or replace each of the four modules across the same fixed-backbone evaluations.

2. **How robust is generated harness code under unfamiliar protocols and failure modes?**  
   Training restricts repair supervision to defects fixed within two rounds. An experiment could introduce held-out interface changes, unavailable tools, malformed observations, and longer repair chains, reporting initial validity, recovery rate, and repair cost.

3. **Does streaming evolution transfer across task distributions rather than merely within a stream?**  
   Streaming results cover three benchmark streams without exact endpoint statistics. A next experiment could evolve the archive on one task family and evaluate it, without further updates, on held-out families and reordered streams.

4. **How does JIT synthesis compare with selecting or composing existing harnesses under equal compute?**  
   The paper compares generated harnesses with fixed alternatives, but does not report a retrieval/selection-only control over the same seed bank. Such a control could equalize generation, validation, and rollout budgets to test whether novel synthesis is necessary.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported table values, because the supplied text includes the complete method, benchmark definitions, and principal results.

Material missing information includes training-set sizes and composition, teacher and filtering details, hyperparameters, training and inference compute, candidate-selection specifics, evaluation seeds and rollout counts, uncertainty estimates, failure/repair success rates, full streaming endpoint numbers, ablations, and licensing or release conditions for the reported assets.
