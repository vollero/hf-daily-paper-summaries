# VBVR-Pro: A Scalable and Verifiable Suite for Native Visual Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-27.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26105) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26105)


## One-sentence takeaway

VBVR-Pro combines 300 procedural visual-reasoning tasks, deterministic task-specific scorers, and multi-task reinforcement learning, with experiments indicating transferable gains and a larger role for intermediate visual states than textual rationales.

## Problem and motivation

Native visual reasoning uses generated images or videos as the reasoning medium, but the authors identify three obstacles: insufficient scalable training tasks, unreliable VLM-based evaluation and reward signals, and a lack of controlled comparisons among image, video, and interleaved text-image generators. Existing resources are described as mostly evaluation-only, limited in task coverage, or dependent on difficult-to-reproduce model judges (Introduction; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Scalable task suite:** 300 procedural generators span perception, spatiality, transformation, abstraction, and knowledge. Of these, 150 revise VBVR tasks and 150 are newly designed; aligned image, video, and interleaved outputs derive from the same solved instances (Sections 2.1–2.3).
- **Verifiable evaluation:** Dedicated scorers for all 100 benchmark tasks extract semantic attributes with classical vision methods and apply deterministic, task-specific checks rather than relying on raw-pixel matching or VLM judgment (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Controlled modality study:** More than 30 generators are compared under a shared task distribution, with training and intervention experiments examining image, video, and interleaved visual trajectories (Sections 4.1–4.3).
- **Reward-based optimization:** A multi-task RL baseline uses the scorers as verifiable rewards and Coefficients-Preserving Sampling for exploration; it is compared with additional SFT and VLM-reward training under a shared 50,000-instance budget (Sections 5.1–5.3).
- **Released assets:** The authors state that all data, models, scorers, and code are released (Abstract, page 2).

The results support the authors’ claims that broad procedural supervision transfers beyond its training tasks, deterministic scorers can provide useful RL feedback, and intermediate visual states materially affect these models. They do not establish that visual trajectories are universally superior to language-based reasoning: the causal interventions concern one trained interleaved model, modality comparisons involve different architectures and scales, and external transfer is reported only for the strongest fine-tuned video model.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Even the strongest trained model remains below human performance, leaving substantial capability headroom (Section 4.1.2).
- **Author-stated evaluator boundary:** Scorers use human-designed, calibrated weights and task-specific logic; they therefore require engineering for each supported task rather than offering a general evaluator for arbitrary visual reasoning (Section 3.2).
- **Analyst inference — architectural confounding:** Image, video, and interleaved comparisons span different model families, parameter counts, representations, and fine-tuning strategies. The shared data distribution improves control but does not isolate output modality alone (Tables 3–4 and 9).
- **Analyst inference — limited intervention generality:** The counterfactual and intermediate-state experiments use only VBVR-Pro-SenseNova-U1, so the visual-state conclusion is not causally demonstrated across all model families (Section 4.2).
- **Analyst inference — external-transfer selection:** Only the strongest fine-tuned model is evaluated on the seven external benchmarks, leaving uncertainty about whether transfer is equally consistent across architectures and modalities (Section 4.4).
- **Analyst inference — annotation dependence:** Interleaved reasoning text is generated by Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview and checked on five annotations per task, which may leave unmeasured annotation errors or stylistic regularities (Section C.2).
- **Analyst inference — statistical uncertainty:** The supplied results do not report confidence intervals or significance tests for model-score differences, human alignment, or transfer gains.

## Open research questions

1. **Does modality itself cause the video advantage on transition-heavy tasks?** Existing comparisons also vary architecture and capacity. A matched-backbone experiment producing single images, sparse keyframes, and dense video would better isolate representation effects.

2. **Do visual-state interventions generalize across model families?** They are reported for one interleaved model. Repeating removal, corruption, and state-replacement interventions across several interleaved and video generators would test robustness.

3. **How well can verifiable scoring extend beyond hand-engineered tasks?** The current scorers are task-specific. A next experiment could measure scorer-development effort and human alignment on newly authored tasks whose evaluators were not designed during benchmark construction.

4. **Which aspects of task diversity drive external transfer?** The paper contrasts broader task coverage with narrower instance scaling but does not provide a detailed faculty- or task-family attribution. Controlled curricula varying coverage, instance count, and cognitive faculty could identify the transferable components.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the main paper and substantial appendix material with dataset construction, evaluator protocols, training settings, ablations, and numerical tables.

Material omissions or ambiguities include statistical uncertainty for most comparisons, complete human-annotator demographics and recruitment details, per-task scorer implementation effort, full compute totals for all supervised fine-tuning runs, and cross-model replication of the causal intervention experiments.
