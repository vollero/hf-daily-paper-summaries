# OPD-V: Visual On-Policy Self-Distillation with Modality Balance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05131) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05131)


## One-sentence takeaway

OPD-V filters and weights visual on-policy self-distillation using agreement differences between zoomed-image and masked-image teachers, improving reported multimodal reasoning accuracy while reducing measured training latency.

## Problem and motivation

Multimodal models may rely disproportionately on textual context even when answering requires visual evidence, a phenomenon the authors call “Modality Imbalance.” Existing on-policy self-distillation methods provide privileged textual or visual information to a teacher but do not determine whether its predictions actually reflect stronger use of visual evidence. The authors argue that this can make otherwise useful privileged information underused and limit post-training effectiveness (Sections 1 and 2.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Modality balance as privileged information:** The authors compare visual-to-textual attention under three matched conditions—an original image for the student, an evidence-centered zoomed image for a Positive Teacher, and a randomly masked version of that crop for a Negative Teacher (Figure 1; Sections 3.1–3.2).

- **Token-level trust region:** For every student-generated token, OPD-V subtracts its Negative Teacher log probability from its Positive Teacher log probability. Tokens with a positive margin form the Modality-Balance Trust Region (Equations 7–8).

- **Selective, weighted self-distillation:** Inside that region, the positive margin weights Jensen–Shannon divergence between the Positive Teacher and student distributions; both teachers are detached EMA copies of the student (Equation 9; Algorithm 1).

- **Memory-conscious implementation:** Distillation uses the student’s top 100 tokens plus a tail-probability bucket rather than the full vocabulary (Section 7.1).

- **Empirical scope:** The method is tested on four Qwen-family backbones and six benchmark evaluations, with component and image-operation ablations plus step-time measurements (Sections 4.1–4.4).

The results establish consistent gains within the reported Qwen-family models, training data, benchmark suite, and non-thinking evaluation protocol. The component ablations support the usefulness of combining two teacher views, while timing logs show lower step latency in the measured setup.

They do not establish that attention ratios causally measure reliance on visual evidence, that the method generalizes beyond the tested model families or crop-equipped data, or that its accuracy and efficiency advantages hold under other hardware, rollout lengths, or evaluation protocols. Comparisons with unrelated large models are also less controlled than the matched post-training comparison.

## Limitations and validity risks

No explicit limitations section is supplied.

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- OPD-V requires an evidence-centered crop during training, taken from the Vision-OPD dataset’s bounding-box field. Its applicability when such localization supervision is unavailable is not evaluated (Sections 7.2 and 9).
- All four backbones belong to two Qwen families, so architectural diversity remains limited despite covering 4B–9B scales (Section 4.1.1).
- Training uses one 6,241-sample synthetic visual-reasoning dataset for one epoch; performance under larger, natural, or differently distributed training sets is not established (Sections 4.1.1 and 9).
- The evidence links attention ratios, teacher-logit margins, and correctness observationally, but no supplied experiment demonstrates that changing the ratio itself causes improved visual grounding (Figure 1).
- Transformation ablations vary one operation at a time and cover a small candidate set; other crop, masking, or learned perturbation strategies remain untested (Figure 6).
- Efficiency results come from four H200 GPUs and are partly attributed to shorter generated responses. The paper does not isolate method overhead at matched response lengths (Figure 5; Tables 3–4).
- Evaluation uses deterministic matching followed by an unspecified Yes/No judge for unresolved cases; the supplied text does not identify the judge or report judge reliability (Section 10).
- Code, trained checkpoints, random seeds, variance across runs, and statistical significance are not established in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can modality-balanced supervision work without annotated evidence crops?** Crop availability is a material training assumption. A next experiment could compare detector-generated, attention-derived, random, and ground-truth crops while holding all other training settings fixed.

2. **Does the logit margin measure genuine visual grounding or merely sensitivity to perturbation?** Figure 1 reports an association rather than causal validation. A next experiment could evaluate selected tokens against token-level visual-evidence annotations and controlled counterfactual images.

3. **How much of the latency gain comes from the objective versus shorter rollouts?** Current timings combine several effects. A next experiment could cap both OPSD and OPD-V at matched generated-token counts and separately profile rollout, preprocessing, teacher forward, and backward stages.

4. **Does OPD-V generalize beyond Qwen-family models and high-resolution VQA-style evaluation?** The tested architectures and tasks remain concentrated. A next experiment could use unrelated MLLM families and benchmarks emphasizing diagrams, documents, temporal media, or open-ended generation.
