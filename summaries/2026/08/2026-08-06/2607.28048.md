# SKILL-KD: Contrastive Skill Distillation for LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28048) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28048)


## One-sentence takeaway

SKILL-KD improves frozen LLM agents by converting same-task teacher–student trajectory differences into textual skill patches, validating them through student reruns, and consolidating them with trace-linked edit histories.

## Problem and motivation

Skill-based prompting can adapt agents without changing model weights, but existing acquisition methods often summarize either a student’s experience or a teacher demonstration. The authors argue that a failed student trajectory may not reveal the missing strategy, while a successful teacher trajectory may be too implicit or poorly aligned with the student’s behavior. Repeated local fixes can also cause “skill drift” through over-specific, redundant, or destructive edits. SKILL-KD targets both the transfer gap and long-term maintenance of the skill library (Introduction; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Contrastive skill distillation:** A consolidation agent compares student and teacher trajectories on the same task and expresses their actionable discrepancy as a textual patch for a frozen student (Method: Contrastive Skill Distillation).

- **Adaptive behavioral validation:** Each patch is tested by rerunning the student. Failed patches can be refined for up to \(n\) rounds; only patches associated with successful reruns enter the persistent library (Method: Adaptive Skill Distillation).

- **Drift-aware consolidation:** A trace-linked history records add, modify, delete, and skip decisions. The consolidation agent can retrieve supporting trajectories before deciding how to incorporate a patch (Method: Drift-Aware Skill Consolidation).

- **Broad empirical evaluation:** The authors evaluate two teacher–student configurations on five agent benchmarks, including a cross-model-family setting (Experiments; Table 1).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that adaptive validation can turn trajectory contrast into useful prompt-time guidance and that historical consolidation can reduce skill bloat. The supplied rendering omits Table 1’s numerical cells and Table 3’s per-benchmark values, so the claimed across-the-board advantage over every baseline cannot be independently quantified here. Scores also come from single evaluation runs, leaving variance and statistical significance unknown.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Explicit scope boundary:** The method supplies the entire benchmark-specific skill file in the prompt and does not study retrieval or routing for large libraries (Appendix A).

- **Explicit implementation constraint:** Skill construction uses at most three adaptive rounds, and separate libraries are trained for each benchmark; cross-benchmark transfer is therefore not evaluated (Appendix A).

- **Analyst inference—limited replication evidence:** Each score is based on one complete evaluation run, with no reported uncertainty or significance testing in the supplied text (Appendix A).

- **Analyst inference—incomplete numeric comparison:** The retrieved text includes captions but not the cells of Tables 1, 3, 5, or 6. Most benchmark-level baseline differences, split sizes, and consolidation-ablation magnitudes are consequently unavailable.

- **Analyst inference—component entanglement:** Teacher and consolidation roles use the same teacher model within each group. The supplied experiments do not establish how performance changes with a weaker or independently chosen consolidator.

- **Analyst inference—teacher access and cost:** Training requires teacher calls, consolidation calls, evaluator feedback, and repeated student executions. Hardware is stated, but total tokens, calls, latency, and monetary cost are not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can one skill library transfer across benchmarks or interfaces?** The paper trains a separate library for each benchmark. A next experiment could train on selected task families and evaluate unchanged skills on a held-out benchmark with compatible procedures.

2. **How does retrieval affect scaling?** Every current prompt receives the full skill file, so behavior with much larger repositories is unknown. A next experiment could compare full-context prompting with learned and similarity-based retrieval while measuring accuracy, tokens, and routing errors.

3. **How sensitive is SKILL-KD to the consolidation model?** The teacher also serves as consolidator, leaving their contributions entangled. A factorial experiment could vary teacher and consolidator strength independently while holding student trajectories fixed.

4. **Are the gains stable across repeated runs?** Only one evaluation run is reported per score. Repeating skill construction and testing across seeds could quantify variance in performance, patch acceptance, and library size.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and several diagnostic results, but material information is missing: numerical contents of the main-results and consolidation tables, benchmark split sizes, per-benchmark adaptive-round rates, uncertainty estimates, total inference cost, complete prompts, evaluator details, and any artifact-release or code-availability statement.
