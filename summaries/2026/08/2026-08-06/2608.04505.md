# K-EXAONE 2.0 Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04505) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04505)


## One-sentence takeaway

K-EXAONE 2.0 upcycles its predecessor into an open-weight 750B-parameter sparse MoE model with 37B active parameters, improving especially in agentic coding, long-context retrieval, and Korean-context safety while remaining behind stronger baselines on many reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation

The authors target a practical and locally grounded frontier-scale language model that can be built, operated, evaluated, and improved without discarding the substantial investment embodied in K-EXAONE. They identify gaps in its capacity, agentic coding, long-context understanding, multilingual coverage, and safety evaluation grounded in Korean sociocultural conditions (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Sparse-MoE upcycling:** The model expands K-EXAONE from 48 to 78 layers and from 128 to 256 experts per MoE layer, using block-level depth expansion and norm-preserving random rotation noise to differentiate duplicated experts. It reaches 750B total and 37B active parameters (Sections 2.1–2.2; Table 1).

- **Training and stability:** Continued pre-training includes a healing stage followed by 8T additional tokens. The final 16 MoE layers use Clamped SwiGLU to control extreme activations, while mid-training progressively extends context from 8K to 64K and then 256K (Sections 2.2, 3, and 4).

- **Capability-directed training:** Difficulty-focused mid-training and 350B-token SFT emphasize advanced reasoning, repository-level coding, tool workflows, multilingual data, and both thinking and non-thinking operation. Preference learning uses GROUPER for general capability and safety alignment (Sections 4–5).

- **Efficient inference:** The model offers two self-speculative decoding paths: a jointly trained MTP head and a post hoc five-layer DSpark drafter with block size seven (Section 2.3; Table 2).

- **Broader language and safety scope:** Coverage expands from six to ten languages. K-AUT-V2 expands the Korean-context safety taxonomy from 226 to 296 risk areas using a structured consultation with 46 teachers (Section 3.3; Appendix F, Table 10).

- **Open release:** Model weights are released under Apache 2.0 (pp. 15, 17).

The evidence establishes broad benchmark improvement over K-EXAONE, particularly on agentic coding, long-context retrieval, multilingual mathematics, and the reported safety evaluations. It does not establish universal frontier leadership: Table 6 shows K-EXAONE 2.0 trailing at least one comparison model on most listed benchmarks. Nor does perfect NIAH retrieval alone demonstrate reasoning over 256K tokens, and the main model comparisons do not isolate the effects of architecture scale, data, or individual training stages.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The model can produce harmful, personal, biased, semantically incorrect, outdated, false, or contradictory responses (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** K-EXAONE 2.0 does not lead every benchmark, and further work is needed in scale, data, training, inference efficiency, safety, and evaluation (Conclusion, pp. 14–15).

- **Author-stated:** Red teaming based on established attacks is weak at discovering previously unidentified risks (Appendix F, p. 21).

- **Analyst inference:** Attribution is limited because the full model is changed along many dimensions simultaneously. The report provides targeted small-scale or component studies, but no comprehensive full-scale ablation separating upcycling, additional data, mid-training, SFT, preference learning, and parameter growth.

- **Analyst inference:** Some comparisons mix official baseline scores with internally reproduced scores, and recommended inference settings can differ by model (Section 6.1; Table 6). This weakens strict head-to-head interpretation.

- **Analyst inference:** The OpenAI-MRCR result does not test the full 256K window—it stops at 128K—while the 256K claim is supported separately by synthetic NIAH retrieval (Appendix C; Figure 5).

- **Analyst inference:** KGC-Safety and Ko-LongBench are in-house evaluations, and the supplied report does not provide their complete instances. Independent reproducibility is therefore uncertain despite the released model.

- **Analyst inference:** Safety evaluation is culturally deliberate but partly adjudicated by the team that designed the criteria, while model-based judging can transfer judge bias; the authors acknowledge the latter risk (Appendix F.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Which intervention causes the agentic gains?** Scale, repository-level data, preserved thinking, and post-training all change together. A factorial ablation on SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench could isolate each contribution.

2. **Does long-context retrieval translate into compositional reasoning at 256K?** NIAH tests retrieval, while OpenAI-MRCR stops at 128K. Controlled multi-hop reasoning tests from 128K through 256K would probe this distinction.

3. **How robust are the safety gains outside the designers’ evaluation framework?** The strongest score is on in-house KGC-Safety. Independent multilingual red teams using unseen risk taxonomies and attack methods could test transfer.

4. **What is the quality–latency–cost trade-off of the two drafters in production?** Table 2 reports speedups on 8×H200 but not output-quality changes, memory costs, or other hardware. Deployment studies could measure these jointly across workloads and accelerators.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported architecture, training stages, benchmark scores, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the complete report and relevant tables.

Material missing information includes detailed training-data composition and provenance, total training compute and hardware, full contamination statistics, full-scale ablations, statistical uncertainty or repeated-run variance, per-benchmark prompt details not included in Appendix C, production memory and throughput costs, and independently reproduced results for the in-house evaluations.
