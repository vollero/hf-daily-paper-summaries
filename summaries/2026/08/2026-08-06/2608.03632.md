# When Teachers Mislead: Spurious-Signal-Aware On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03632) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03632)


## One-sentence takeaway

SA-OPD improves language and vision-language on-policy distillation by filtering token-level teacher signals that combine weak prompt dependence with large teacher–student divergence.

## Problem and motivation

On-policy distillation supervises student-generated trajectories with dense teacher feedback, reducing the distribution mismatch of offline distillation. Existing selective methods prioritize signals using uncertainty, confidence, divergence, or learnability, but do not test whether the teacher’s judgment actually depends on the task input.

The authors argue that input-agnostic language priors, formatting preferences, and reasoning templates can therefore generate large but poorly task-aligned updates. This matters because such updates may consume substantial gradient energy and transfer teacher biases without improving the downstream task (§1, §3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **New failure-mode formulation:** The paper defines spurious OPD signals as tokens with both low input-groundedness and high optimization impact. A conditional-mutual-information formulation motivates the criterion (§3.1, Eq. 11).

- **Input-grounding proxy:** For each student-generated token, SA-OPD compares teacher–student divergence under the original prompt with divergence after removing the prompt while retaining the response prefix. Their absolute difference is the Input-Grounding Gap (§3.2, Eqs. 12–14).

- **Selective filtering:** Tokens in the intersection of the lowest grounding gaps and largest absolute divergences are excluded from the reverse-KL loss (§3.2, Eqs. 15–19).

- **Adaptive loss-mass control:** The selection ratios are adjusted so filtering stays within a bound on the filtered loss-mass ratio, intended to avoid removing excessive useful supervision (§3.2; Appendix C).

- **Empirical contribution:** The method is evaluated across mathematical reasoning, visual understanding, and visual reasoning, including multiple teacher–student scales (§4).

These results establish consistent improvements over the tested OPD baselines under the reported Qwen-family settings, and Table 4 supports the value of combining both filtering axes. They do not establish statistical significance, broader architectural generality, or that prompt-insensitive tokens are causally harmful in every case. The downstream gains validate the filter operationally, but do not directly measure the claimed alignment with an ideal task gradient.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-acknowledged trade-off:** Aggressive filtering may remove valid perceptual supervision, motivating the dynamic loss-mass constraint (§3.2, §4.4).

- **Author-stated theoretical assumptions:** The weak-alignment result requires the score direction to be only weakly input-dependent after conditioning on response context (Appendix A.1). The parameter-drift result assumes nuisance gradients form a finite-variance martingale-difference sequence (Appendix A.2).

- **Analyst inference — proxy validity:** Removing the entire prompt is only an indirect proxy for input-groundedness. The text supplies downstream ablations but no external labels or direct causal measurement showing that every selected token is genuinely spurious.

- **Analyst inference — narrow model scope:** All experiments use non-thinking Qwen3/Qwen3.5 students and teachers or a Qwen-based DeepSeek teacher (§4.1), so transfer to other model families is untested.

- **Analyst inference — uncertainty reporting:** The supplied results contain no confidence intervals, repeated-run variance, or significance tests.

- **Analyst inference — incomplete comparison coverage:** Token Teachability and Entropy-Aware OPD are discussed as related selective methods but are not included among the experimental baselines (§2.2, §4.1).

- **Analyst inference — reporting ambiguity:** MathVista is 72.2 in Table 1 but 72.1 in Table 4, presumably from different experiments; the supplied text does not explain the discrepancy. The units or normalization of the reported \(\beta=1.8\) FLMR bound are also not fully explicit.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the Input-Grounding Gap identify causally harmful supervision?**  
   This remains open because evaluation is based mainly on downstream scores and token characteristics. A next experiment could manually or automatically annotate whether filtered judgments are supported by the original input, then measure precision and recall against competing proxies.

2. **Would partial or matched input perturbations be more faithful than complete prompt removal?**  
   Full removal changes context substantially and may confound grounding with distribution shift. An experiment could compare no-prompt scoring with masked evidence, shuffled inputs, and semantically matched counterfactual prompts.

3. **How robust is SA-OPD outside the tested Qwen-centered model families?**  
   Architectural generality is not demonstrated. A factorial study across unrelated teacher and student families, including tokenizer-mismatched pairs, would test whether the gains persist.

4. **Can the extra scoring cost be reduced without losing accuracy?**  
   SA-OPD requires additional teacher and student evaluations and adds up to 7.53% training time. A plausible experiment would estimate grounding gaps for sampled tokens or with a lightweight proxy and compare quality–cost curves.
