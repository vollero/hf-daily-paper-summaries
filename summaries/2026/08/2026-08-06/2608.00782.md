# Distill Where You Fail: Recovering Learning Signals of Negative RL-Groups from Adaptive Teacher Guidance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00782) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00782)


## One-sentence takeaway

RSTG recovers otherwise absent gradients on all-failed GRPO prompt groups by selectively combining confidence-weighted, token-filtered on-policy distillation with supervised learning from correct teacher trajectories.

## Problem and motivation

GRPO assigns group-relative outcome advantages, so prompts whose sampled responses are uniformly incorrect receive zero advantage and no learning signal. Although on-policy distillation supplies dense token-level teacher feedback, the authors find that naïvely adding it to GRPO can hurt performance because teacher guidance varies in quality, rapid imitation restricts exploration, and most OPD token advantages are negative; student-generated erroneous prefixes can also make that guidance locally unreliable (§1).

The gap is therefore not merely a lack of supervision, but the absence of a mechanism deciding when, how strongly, and at which tokens teacher supervision should supplement GRPO.

## Approach and contributions

- **Prompt-level routing:** RSTG activates teacher-guided training only for negative zero-variance prompts—groups in which every student rollout fails—and otherwise retains standard GRPO (§4; Figure 2).
- **Confidence weighting:** It scales each prompt’s OPD advantage by the teacher’s `mean@8` success rate, used as a proficiency proxy (§4.1, Eq. 9).
- **Token selection:** It updates only the top-ranked tokens under a Soft-OR score combining normalized student entropy and absolute teacher–student OPD advantage (§4.2, Eqs. 10–13).
- **Positive global supervision:** For the same failed groups, it adds SFT on the shortest correct trajectory among eight teacher samples, counteracting predominantly negative OPD signals and erroneous-prefix locality (§4.3, Eq. 14; §5.1).
- **Empirical diagnosis:** The paper analyzes OPD’s applicable sample scope, advantage asymmetry, convergence toward the teacher, and response-length inflation across math and code (§§4.1, 5.3–5.5).

The results support the authors’ claim that adaptive placement of teacher guidance is more effective than their selected naïve hybrid baseline within the tested Qwen math/code settings. The ablation and diagnostic evidence also associates RSTG with slower teacher convergence and better advantage behavior, but does not isolate every component with readable numerical results because Tables 1–3 and several figures are supplied only as images. It does not establish effectiveness outside these model families, domains, reward structures, or scales, nor prove that preserving lower teacher overlap causally produces higher task accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation covers only mathematics and code; agentic settings remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Scale is constrained by computation and by OPD’s need for a meaningful teacher–student capability gap; effectiveness at larger scales is conjectured rather than demonstrated (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** All three pairs come from Qwen2.5 or Qwen3, limiting evidence for cross-family generalization (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The “naïve GRPO+OPD” comparator is the best of three routing variants chosen separately for each pair. Although favorable to that baseline, this selection complicates interpretation of what “naïve” consistently denotes (Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Teacher `mean@8` is treated as confidence, but the supplied text does not report calibration analysis or comparison against alternative proficiency estimators (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Several key values, uncertainty estimates, run counts, significance tests, token-selection fraction, and full ablation cells are unavailable from the retrieved image-only tables and figures.
- **Analyst inference:** The APPS evaluation uses only 500 of 10,000 items; the sampling procedure is described as uniform and balanced by difficulty, but sampling variability is not reported (Appendix A.3).
- **Reproducibility risk:** Hardware, schedules, rollout counts, and core equations are provided, but the supplied text does not establish a public code release, random seeds, or complete hyperparameters.

## Open research questions

1. **Does RSTG transfer beyond Qwen and beyond math/code?** Evidence is restricted to two Qwen families and two domains. A next experiment could test matched teacher–student pairs from several unrelated model families on tool-use or agentic tasks with verifiable rewards.

2. **How sensitive is routing to the teacher-confidence estimator?** The paper uses teacher `mean@8` without reported calibration comparisons. A controlled study could compare `mean@8`, sequence likelihood, verifier confidence, and uncertainty-aware weighting at equal teacher-query cost.

3. **Which token-selection signal matters most, and what is the best selection budget?** The retrieved text defines entropy, divergence, and their Soft-OR combination but does not expose quantitative component-by-component or top-\(k\) sensitivity results. An experiment could sweep token fractions and separately ablate each score across model pairs.

4. **Does slower teacher alignment cause better exploration and final accuracy?** Lower top-16 overlap is correlated with RSTG at one training point, not shown to be causal. A next experiment could vary token masking to produce controlled alignment rates and measure exploration diversity and downstream accuracy over time.
