# SIGNPOST-Bench: Benchmarking Text-Vision Conflict Resolution in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04244) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04244)


## One-sentence takeaway

SIGNPOST-Bench shows that all 20 evaluated MLLMs are systematically redirected by geographically conflicting scene text, with median geolocation error rising 4.8-fold and model predictions shifting toward injected targets.

## Problem and motivation

MLLMs combine scene appearance and readable text, but these cues can indicate different locations. Existing OCR, VQA, typographic-robustness, and geolocation benchmarks generally evaluate discrete final outcomes and do not measure how far—or in which direction—a prediction moves when text conflicts with visual evidence. The authors argue that clean-input accuracy therefore does not establish reliable multimodal conflict resolution (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Continuous conflict diagnostic:** The authors formulate scene-text arbitration as coordinate prediction, enabling measurement of both degradation relative to the true location and movement toward an injected geographic target (§3; §5).
- **Paired five-condition benchmark:** Each scene becomes an Original, Blank, Similar, Random, and Adversarial quintuplet, intended to isolate native-text use, text removal, compatible replacement, unrelated text, and geographic conflict (§3; Figure 1).
- **Evaluation asset:** SIGNPOST-Bench comprises 5,111 groups, 25,555 images, and 10,084 selected text spans drawn from four image sources (§4).
- **Metrics and model study:** The paper evaluates 20 MLLMs from seven providers using WLA, TBS, TFR, TDR, and the composite MCRS across 511,100 model–image evaluations (§5).
- **Diagnostic extensions:** Two models receive structured conflict probes, conflict-aware prompts, and cross-task tests to examine whether explicit conflict awareness prevents prediction failure (§6; Figure 4).

The evidence establishes a consistent failure mode for the tested models, datasets, prompts, and synthetic interventions. Paired Blank-to-edited comparisons and target-distance metrics support the authors’ claim that the effect is not merely generic image degradation.

It does not establish equivalent behavior for other multimodal tasks, naturally occurring misinformation, deployed systems, or models outside the evaluated suite. Positive mean TDR also does not mean most individual samples move targetward: some models have positive-pair rates below 50%, indicating that a smaller number of large shifts can dominate the mean.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limitation:** Probing, defense, and cross-task experiments cover only Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-4o-mini and should not be generalized to all 20 models (Appendix E, Table 9).
- **Author-stated coverage limitation:** TFR and TDR use only 1,732 of 5,111 groups (33.9%) whose injected targets were geocoded successfully (§6; Table 6).
- **Author-stated release constraint:** Synthesized images are not redistributed because of third-party restrictions; reproduction depends on source identifiers and deterministic reconstruction instructions (Data and Code Availability).
- **Analyst inference:** Because replacements and images are produced by Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite and Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, results may partly depend on those generators’ text selection, wording, and visual-edit distributions. The 120-image audit indicates generally good quality but is small relative to 15,333 text-bearing edited variants (§4; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** The benchmark is dominated by T2 Cultural groups (75.3%), while T1 constitutes 6.8%; aggregate conclusions may therefore reflect the T2 distribution more strongly (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** MCRS incorporates chosen weights, exponents, clipping, and normalization anchors. Reported sensitivity is reassuring—minimum Kendall τ=0.905—but validates ranking stability only over the tested alternatives, not the score’s normative weighting (§5; Appendix C–D).
- **Analyst inference:** Each result uses one accepted temperature-zero response, so run-to-run or provider-side nondeterminism is not quantified (Appendix B, “Model registration”).
- **Analyst inference:** Human audits assess taxonomy agreement and edit quality, but the supplied text reports no human validation that every Adversarial replacement is genuinely misleading while every Similar replacement remains geographically compatible.

## Open research questions

1. **Do these failures transfer to naturally occurring text–vision conflicts?** The benchmark uses synthetic interventions, so a next experiment could evaluate matched real scenes containing misleading business names, reused place names, or temporary signage.
2. **Which intervention properties drive target following?** The paper does not isolate font, placement, text specificity, legibility, or trap distance. A factorial ablation could vary these properties while holding the source scene and target fixed.
3. **Can training improve arbitration without sacrificing useful OCR cues?** Native and Similar text improve localization, while conflicting text harms it. A next experiment could fine-tune on labeled agreement/conflict pairs and measure both clean WLA and conflict robustness.
4. **Does explicit conflict reasoning generalize across models and tasks?** Prompt-based diagnostics cover only two models and yield mixed results. A broader preregistered evaluation could test the same defense across all 20 models, coordinate prediction, country classification, and non-geographic multimodal tasks.
