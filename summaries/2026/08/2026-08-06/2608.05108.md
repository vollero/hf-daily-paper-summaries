# Agent Against Agent: An Agentic System for Automatic Prompt Injection Red Teaming

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05108) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05108)


## One-sentence takeaway

PIMiner turns prior prompt-injection attempts into a routed hierarchical memory, enabling a search-based red-teaming agent to transfer attacks to unseen target LLMs with up to 10 queries per sample and performance often comparable to target-specific RL attackers.

## Problem and motivation

Prompt injection can cause tool-using LLM agents to follow malicious instructions embedded in webpages, retrieved documents, or tool outputs. Automated red-teaming is important both for measuring this vulnerability and for collecting attacks that could train defenses (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

The authors identify a tradeoff in existing methods: RL-based attackers can be effective but require many target-agent queries and transfer poorly to new target models, while search-based attacks avoid attacker training but restart from scratch for each sample and therefore fail to accumulate reusable attack knowledge (Sections 1 and 2.1, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hierarchical attack memory:** PIMiner combines a long-term cross-dataset strategy library, compact intra-dataset experience, and the current sample’s attack history (Sections 4.1–4.2; Figure 1, pp. 4–5).

- **Selective strategy routing:** A router chooses the top \(K\) relevant strategies—\(K=3\) by default—so the attacker need not load the full, growing library (Sections 4.2 and 5.1, pp. 4–6).

- **Iterative adaptation:** For up to 10 attempts per sample, the attacker diagnoses previous failures and revises the injection using all three memory levels (Section 4.2, Equations 2–4, p. 5).

- **Continual strategy discovery:** A digester converts successful and failed trajectories into human-readable strategy files, broadening or narrowing their documented scope and creating new strategies when needed (Section 4.2, pp. 5–6; Appendix E, pp. 19–20).

- **Transfer evaluation:** The learned library is tested on held-out target LLMs and with attacker backbones outside the Claude family; the paper also compares PIMiner with static, search-based, and RL-based attacks (Sections 5.2–5.5).

These results establish that memory-augmented search can be effective across the tested benchmark samples and model configurations, and that both routing and memory components contribute under the reported ablations. They do not establish robustness or attack effectiveness on live deployments, private data, external defense stacks, arbitrary agent architectures, or injection modalities the target cannot read. Comparisons with RL methods are also not fully controlled: the paper imports RL-baseline results from PISmith rather than rerunning every method in a common pipeline (Section 5.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments use public benchmarks and models in controlled, sandboxed environments; no real-world systems, users, or private data are tested (Ethical Considerations, p. 10).

- **Author-stated implementation dependence:** Although the pipeline is described as model-compatible, the main implementation primarily uses Claude Code models, with Claude-Opus-4.7 as the default training and testing backbone (Section 6; Section 5.1).

- **Author-stated cost qualification:** The approximately $20 training cost excludes Claude Code subscription usage. Without that subscription, the authors estimate attacker-model queries would dominate cost (Section 6; Appendix G).

- **Author-stated attack scope:** The evaluation targets intrinsic LLM alignment rather than external detectors, sanitizers, or policy-based defenses (Appendix A, pp. 15–16).

- **Analyst inference—small, sampled evaluations:** The main test sets contain only 21 IPIArena and 30 AgentDojo samples, randomly selected according to the paper. This limits precision for per-model ASRs and may make results sensitive to sample composition (Section 5.1; Table 3).

- **Analyst inference—comparison confounding:** RL baselines are taken from PISmith because rerunning them is expensive. The supplied text does not establish identical attacker backbones, training resources, or execution conditions across all methods (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference—limited ablation coverage:** Memory ablations average four held-out targets and deliberately exclude Claude-Opus-4.5 due to low ASR; therefore they do not show that the same gains hold for the most robust reported target (Section 5.4).

- **Analyst inference—ordering effects:** Intra-dataset memory is available only from already completed samples, and rolling parallelization exposes later samples to more experience than earlier ones. No order-sensitivity experiment is reported in the supplied text (Appendix D, p. 19).

- **Analyst inference—dual-use exposure:** The released strategy files contain actionable attack patterns and examples. The authors acknowledge dual-use risk, but the described mitigation is documentation encouraging controlled research use rather than a technical access control (Ethical Considerations).

## Open research questions

1. **How robust are the gains to dataset order and parallel scheduling?**  
   Later samples receive more intra-dataset memory, but order sensitivity is not measured. A next experiment could repeat each benchmark under multiple random orders and attacker-pool sizes, reporting ASR variance by sample position.

2. **Does PIMiner remain effective against deployed defense stacks?**  
   The paper evaluates intrinsic model alignment, not external detectors or sanitizers. A next experiment could test the frozen library against representative defended agents while measuring both ASR and benign-task utility.

3. **How much of transfer comes from strategy semantics versus stored examples?**  
   The ablations remove whole memory levels but do not separately isolate templates, scope metadata, examples, and failure conditions. A factorial ablation over these strategy-file fields could measure each component’s contribution.

4. **Can comparable results be obtained with cheaper training backbones?**  
   Strategy discovery uses an expensive Claude-Opus-4.7 backbone and substantial token volume. A next experiment could train identical libraries with smaller backbones under fixed token and target-query budgets, then evaluate them on the same held-out targets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported results because the supplied text includes the main paper, figures, tables, and relevant appendices.

Material missing or insufficiently specified includes uncertainty estimates across random samples or repeated runs; full per-run variance; a controlled, rerun comparison with RL baselines; sensitivity to training-pair order; isolated ablations of individual strategy-file fields and the digester; detailed evaluation against external defenses; and evidence from live or production agent deployments.
