# ToolArtist: Tool-Using Unified Multimodal Models for Agentic Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04436) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04436)


## One-sentence takeaway

ToolArtist post-trains an Emu3.5 unified multimodal model to autonomously interleave reasoning, text/image search, and native image generation, achieving stronger reported open-world generation scores than prior non-proprietary agentic systems.

## Problem and motivation

Open-world image requests may depend on long-tail, factual, geographic, historical, or time-sensitive knowledge absent from both the prompt and a model’s parameters. Existing agentic systems either follow fixed search-to-generation workflows or use an agent only to prepare a prompt for a separate generator, leaving the complete reasoning–search–drawing process outside a single learned policy (Introduction; Figure 2).

The authors argue that generation itself should be an autonomous action: one policy should identify knowledge gaps, retrieve evidence, decide when to draw, inspect intermediate images, and optionally search or draw again.

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified agent policy:** ToolArtist uses Emu3.5 to represent reasoning, tool calls, retrieved text/images, generation captions, and generated images in one autoregressive multimodal trajectory. It autonomously selects `TextSearch`, `ImageSearch`, or native drawing actions (Sections 2.2–2.3).

- **Trajectory synthesis and conversion:** A teacher agent creates trajectories using search tools and an external image generator. The conversion stage hides the external generator call but retains its prompt as a visual-caption span and its output as visual tokens, teaching the UMM to generate natively (Section 3.1; Figure 3).

- **Two-stage post-training:** SFT uses 7,132 converted trajectories and masks objectives to policy-generated reasoning and action tokens. RAD-GRPO then applies trajectory-level feedback across reasoning, search, and drawing (Sections 3.2–3.3).

- **Dual-reward RL:** Intent reward evaluates the final generation description; quality reward evaluates faithfulness, visual correctness, text accuracy, and aesthetics. Auxiliary rewards enforce valid formatting, encourage drawing, and discourage excessive length or search-only behavior (Section 3.3).

- **Released assets:** The authors state that they release the training data and complete SFT/RL infrastructure (Abstract; Introduction).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that ToolArtist performs competitively among the reported non-proprietary and agentic baselines on two world-knowledge image-generation benchmarks. The source-summary ablation provides direct evidence for one tool-design choice.

It does not isolate the effects of SFT, RAD-GRPO, individual rewards, autonomous action ordering, or native generation. It also does not establish superiority over the strongest proprietary systems, which remain ahead on several WISE categories.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-shown failure modes:** The qualitative checklists reveal missing details such as architecture, vegetation, clothing, tools, signage, and landscape features (Section 5.3; Appendix A.2).

- **Analyst inference — limited benchmark scope:** Quantitative evaluation covers only WISE and the Humanities split of WorldGenBench, so generalization to other image tasks is not established (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference — judge dependence:** WiScore uses an LLM judge, KCS uses checklist judgments, and RL uses a world-knowledge reward model. Their possible biases or agreement with humans are not analyzed in the supplied text (Sections 3.3 and 4.1).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete component attribution:** Only source-aware image summaries receive a reported ablation. The text provides no separate ablations for RL versus SFT, intent versus quality reward, auxiliary rewards, search modalities, or fixed versus learned action ordering.

- **Analyst inference — reproducibility gaps:** Although infrastructure and data release are claimed, the supplied text does not specify model scale, training compute, RL group size, clipping/KL coefficients, reward-model identity, or complete optimization hyperparameters.

- **Analyst inference — external-system dependence:** Data synthesis relies on Google Search, an LLM Reader, and `gemini-3-pro-image-preview`; inference requires accessible search tools. Sensitivity to tool failures, changing search results, or evidence quality is not quantified (Section 3.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which part of RAD-GRPO causes the reported improvement?** The paper does not separately ablate intent reward, quality reward, or auxiliary signals. A factorial reward-component ablation on both benchmarks could measure each contribution.

2. **Does autonomous action selection outperform a matched fixed workflow?** The central claim concerns full policy control, but the supplied text reports no controlled comparison holding backbone, tools, data, and compute constant. A matched-policy experiment with forced versus learned action schedules would test it.

3. **How robust is ToolArtist to noisy or unavailable retrieval?** Search reliability is assumed, while only source-summary removal is tested. Experiments could corrupt results, remove one search modality, or vary retrieval recall and measure generation degradation.

4. **Can the approach generalize beyond knowledge-heavy static images?** Evaluation is limited to two benchmarks. Testing editing, iterative user interaction, current-event prompts, or other WorldGenBench splits would probe broader transfer.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text describes the method, datasets, headline results, one ablation, and several qualitative cases clearly. However, the actual numerical contents of Tables 1–3 are not reproduced in full, and important information is unavailable: model size, compute budget, full hyperparameters, statistical uncertainty, number of evaluation samples per reported comparison beyond benchmark totals, reward-model identity and validation, human-evaluation details, complete baseline configurations, and controlled ablations of the main training and agency components.
