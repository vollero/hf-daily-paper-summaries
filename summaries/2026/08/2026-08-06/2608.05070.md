# HelloWorld: Enabling Socially Interactive Characters in Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05070) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05070)


## One-sentence takeaway

HelloWorld combines self-distillation with inference-time attention masking to generate camera-controlled videos whose characters perform viewer-directed actions within user-specified time windows, achieving the best timing and camera-control scores on the authors’ benchmark.

## Problem and motivation

Existing interactive video world models primarily expose camera, keyboard, skill, or event controls; they do not explicitly support social interaction between users and in-world characters. Characters may remain static or exhibit ambient motion without responding toward the viewer (Introduction; §2.1).

This matters for interactive games, simulations, and filmmaking where characters should acknowledge users through gaze, gestures, actions, facial expressions, or short speech. The authors target three coupled requirements: performing the requested interaction, directing it toward the viewer, and performing it at the requested time (§1; §4.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Socially interactive video world model:** HelloWorld consumes a first-frame image, text prompt, camera trajectory, and interaction window, then generates a video in which the character responds to the viewer while the camera follows the trajectory (§3.1).

- **Self-distillation for camera control without losing interactions:** A frozen base model generates 156 interaction-rich training videos. Off-the-shelf Pi3X recovers geometry and camera poses; re-rendered “warp videos” encode camera motion. A rank-32 LoRA is trained for 2,000 steps to reconstruct the generated clips while excluding camera descriptions from the text condition (§3.2; §5.2).

- **Training-free temporal localization:** At inference, a temporal cross-attention mask prevents video and audio tokens outside the button-press window from attending to interaction-related prompt tokens (§3.3; Fig. 3).

- **HelloWorldBench:** The authors introduce 400 evaluation samples derived from 120 images, 264 character instances, 101 interaction types, four camera trajectories, and three timing regions, along with metrics for action correctness, timing, and viewer-directed gaze (§4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best interaction timing and camera control | On HelloWorldBench, HelloWorld scores 81.7 TimeAcc and 82.9 CamCtrl; the strongest baseline values are 52.6 and 70.0, respectively. Results average three seeds on 10-second clips with matched prompts and camera inputs. | Table 1; §5.2–§5.3 |
| Viewer-directed gaze improves over world-model baselines, but not the base generator | HelloWorld obtains 40.2° GazeDev versus 52.8°–77.2° for the five world-model baselines; LTX-2.3 obtains 38.1°. GazeDev is computed on 217 human samples, assigning 90° when no face is detected. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Action accuracy is not the overall best | HelloWorld reaches 41.4 ActAcc, below LingBot-World at 50.5 and LTX-2.3 at 42.5, but above the other four world-model baselines (8.0–38.5). | Table 1 |
| Video quality remains competitive | HelloWorld records 96.9 BgCons and 5.27 Aesthetic, the highest Table 1 values; Warp-as-History has the next-best BgCons at 95.3, while LingBot-World has the next-best Aesthetic at 5.21. | Table 1; §5.3 |
| Self-generated interaction data improves action and gaze metrics | Replacing real-video training with the full self-generated set changes ActAcc from 36.4 to 41.4 and GazeDev from 51.3° to 40.2°, while TimeAcc, quality, and camera scores remain close. | Table 2; §5.4 |
| Temporal masking drives timing control | No mask gives 36.7 TimeAcc; video masking gives 80.9; video-plus-audio masking gives 81.7. SpeechInWin rises from 52.5 to 69.1, while ActAcc decreases from 42.5 to 41.4. | Table 3; §5.4 |
| Camera conditioning adds computation | Relative to LTX-2.3, HelloWorld increases 10-second inference time from 50.3 to 60.2 seconds and FLOPs from 6.9 to 9.4 × 10¹⁵ at 1280×704/241 frames. | Table 4; §5.4 |
| Human raters consistently prefer HelloWorld | Across 41 benchmark samples and 30 raters, preference rates against four competitors range from 77.3%–90.9% for action naturalness, 71.7%–88.5% for interaction, and 82.6%–91.2% for scene quality; all reported 95% confidence intervals exceed 66%. | Table 5; §5.5 |

The evidence supports strong temporal localization, camera following, perceptual preference, and competitive visual quality within HelloWorldBench. It does **not** establish universally superior action recognition or gaze: LingBot-World has higher ActAcc, and the uncontrolled base generator has slightly lower GazeDev. It also does not establish real-time, long-horizon, or unscripted interaction.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** HelloWorld is not real-time because of the base model and computational cost (§6). Table 4 reports 60.2 seconds to generate a 10-second clip.
- **Author-stated:** Camera paths and interaction scripts must be specified in advance; the system does not provide open-ended multi-round interaction (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Long-video generation, persistent character identity, and sustained interactions remain unsupported (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** TimeAcc excludes videos judged to contain no interaction, so it does not jointly measure whether an interaction happened and whether it was timed correctly (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** ActAcc and TimeAcc rely on a single named VLM judge, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B; no judge-validation or alternative-judge analysis is reported (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** GazeDev covers only 217 human samples, leaving viewer-directedness for animals, robots, and toys without an equivalent reported gaze measure (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The benchmark contains only 400 samples from 120 Unsplash images, while the user study uses 41 samples; broader generalization is therefore not established (§4.1; §5.5).
- **Reproducibility risk:** The text describes training settings and data synthesis, but does not establish public availability of code, weights, synthesized training clips, benchmark annotations, or exact prompts.

## Open research questions

1. **Can interaction become genuinely real-time?** The current system takes 60.2 seconds per 10-second clip and uses predetermined controls. A next experiment could compare autoregressive or streaming variants on latency, interaction timing, and visual consistency.

2. **Can characters sustain identity and social state over multiple turns?** The paper explicitly leaves persistent identities and multi-round interaction open. A next experiment could evaluate repeated user actions across long sequences using identity, memory, and response-consistency measures.

3. **Does temporal control generalize beyond three coarse timing regions?** TimeAcc evaluates early, middle, or late segments and excludes “no interaction” judgments. A next experiment could test arbitrary-duration windows using frame-level onset/offset error and an interaction-detection metric that retains failures.

4. **How robust are the benchmark’s automated judges?** The principal social metrics depend on a VLM and a human-only gaze estimator. A next experiment could compare multiple judges against frame-level human annotations across humans and non-human characters.
