# Towards Physics of Multimodal Pretraining: Knowledge Flow, Modality Synergy, Early Unification, and Recipes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05000) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05000)


## One-sentence takeaway

Controlled experiments suggest that unified text–image models work best with early joint training, shared attention and normalization but modality-specific FFNs, and an asymmetric language/understanding/generation mix that preserves generation quality with only 5% generative tokens.

## Problem and motivation

Unified models must jointly support language modeling, visual understanding, and visual generation, but their training design—how information transfers, which parameters to share, how to mix data, and when to introduce vision—has largely been guided by heuristics. The authors argue that retrofitting vision onto pretrained language models obscures these interactions and may constrain visual learning. They seek controlled empirical principles that remain useful at realistic scale (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Knowledge-flow experiments:** Mixture sweeps on DCLM and roughly 350M Shutterstock image–text pairs measure transfer among language, image understanding, and image generation. Concept-removal experiments on an extended CLEVR environment isolate transfer for color, shape, spatial relation, size, and count (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Synergy and competition analysis:** Controlled task-complexity progressions and Transformer component-sharing ablations identify when modalities help or interfere. The authors find that simple auxiliary tasks can be beneficial, while complex tasks compete for capacity; shared attention and normalization with modality-specific FFNs performs best in their probes (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Tokenizer generalization:** The interaction patterns are tested with RAE, raw-pixel, CLIP+VAE, and autoregressive UniTok representations (§4.3; Appendix B).
- **Training-timing analysis:** Fixed-budget comparisons vary vision’s introduction time and all six sequential modality orders. Internal activation, embedding, and attention probes motivate the proposed “vision laziness” mechanism for late alignment (§5).
- **Scalable recipe:** A 1T-token mixing-ratio search selects a 70/25/5 language/understanding/generation allocation, followed by controlled evaluations using 13.5B-parameter MoE models trained on 2T tokens (§6; Tables 1–2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge transfer is asymmetric and concept-dependent. | On real data, increasing language from 0% to 80% while holding 50B vision tokens fixed monotonically improves reported visual axes; understanding improves generation, whereas generation causes only minor fluctuations elsewhere. In controlled CLEVR tests, low-level color and shape do not transfer zero-shot in either direction, while understanding helps zero-shot generation of relation, size, and count. | Figures 1–5; §§3.1–3.2 |
| Generation can leave latent low-level priors despite weak zero-shot transfer. | After 2,000 fine-tuning steps, prior generative exposure accelerates color/shape understanding, with reported mean-accuracy AUC differences of +0.133 to +0.273; understanding gives approximately zero benefit for color generation and only marginal benefit for shapes. | Figure 6; §3.2.3 |
| Simple auxiliary tasks produce synergy, while complex streams can induce competition. | With 100B total tokens split 50B/50B, background and noise image tasks improve language perplexity by 0.211 and 0.183, respectively; video and SSTK worsen it by 0.052 and 0.075. Simple language lowers conditional and unconditional generation loss by 0.0153 and 0.0168. | Figure 8; §4.1 |
| Modality-specific FFNs combined with shared attention and normalization perform best in the architectural probe. | Against unimodal baselines, fully shared parameters worsen language perplexity by 0.272 and diffusion loss by 0.0537. The evidence supports comparative design guidance within the authors’ controlled text–image setup, including several single-variable scaled comparisons. It does not establish universal causal laws for other architectures or modalities. The “vision laziness” explanation is supported by correlated internal probes, but those probes do not alone prove that reduced visual activity causes the downstream degradation. Likewise, “5% of compute” refers to the generation-token share in the reported recipe, not a five-percent total training budget relative to the complete model.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Most experiments cover text and static images; video, audio, 3D, and actions are not empirically validated. The authors also caution that modality interactions may change beyond the tested 13.5B/2T-token scale, particularly near trillion-parameter regimes (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** The proposed complexity ordering is broad rather than absolute or strictly monotonic (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** All runs use seed 0, so the text supplies no across-seed variance or statistical uncertainty (§2.1). Small differences in Table 2 therefore have unclear robustness.
- **Analyst inference:** The scaled architecture comparison is not parameter-matched: the MoE has 13.5B total/1.5B active parameters per token, while the dense baseline has 3.5B parameters. It isolates the prescribed systems under matched data and training settings, but not total parameter count (§6.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The synthetic generation evaluation depends on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as an automatic judge over 100 prompts per concept; human validation and judge reliability are not reported (§3.2.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Shutterstock is the exclusive real-world vision source, limiting evidence about data-domain robustness (§2.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Code, trained checkpoints, exact dataset release status, and confidence intervals are not established in the supplied text, constraining independent reproduction.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the transfer asymmetries persist for temporal or embodied modalities?** The paper tests mainly static images. A next experiment could repeat matched concept-removal and mixture sweeps for audio, video, or action streams while controlling token budget and temporal complexity.

2. **Where is the true transition from synergy to capacity competition?** The paper gives several complexity rungs but no calibrated complexity measure or scaling curve. A next experiment could vary dataset entropy and model capacity factorially, with multiple seeds, to estimate the transition boundary.

3. **Can late-aligned models be prevented from becoming visually “lazy”?** Reduced visual activation and attention are observed, but causal interventions are absent. A next experiment could regularize image attention or activation, reinitialize selected shared components, or vary vision-specific learning rates and test whether visual performance recovers.

4. **Is 70/25/5 stable across scales and training phases?** It is selected by a 1T-token search and validated at one larger scale, while the authors note that later generation-heavy training may help. A next experiment could optimize time-varying mixtures across model sizes and compare them with a fixed 70/25/5 allocation under equal compute.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental settings and tabled comparisons because the supplied text is extensive and includes the full retrieved paper.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates and multi-seed results; exact numerical values for several plotted-only findings; code, checkpoint, and dataset-release details; human validation of the synthetic VLM judge; a parameter-matched MoE/dense comparison; and empirical tests beyond text and predominantly static images.
