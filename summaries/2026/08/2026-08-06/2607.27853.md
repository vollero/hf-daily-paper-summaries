# FinanceHarness: Autonomous Financial Deep Research Framework

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27853) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27853)


## One-sentence takeaway

FinanceHarness couples finance-specific agent workflows with a leakage-controlled benchmark, raising a fixed Qwen3.6-27B backbone’s FinanceGym score from 25.3% to 32.4%, while all evaluated systems remain below 40%.

## Problem and motivation

General-purpose deep-research agents lack the financial tools and workflows needed to connect entities, historical evidence, quantitative analysis, and forward-looking risks in analyst-style reports. Existing finance benchmarks mostly test isolated skills, while deep-research benchmarks generally lack per-question publication cutoffs and separate evaluation of historical evidence retrieval from future-outcome anticipation. This makes them inadequate for reproducible financial research evaluation without future-information leakage (Introduction; §§2–3.3; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Point-in-time search sandbox:** A dense retrieval environment over 100+ million dated web articles that blocks documents published after each question’s cutoff (§3.1).
- **FinanceGym benchmark:** 400 expert-annotated questions with 2,464 rubric items, generated from mined financial situations and balanced across topics, sectors, reasoning types, and 12 cutoff-month buckets. Separate pre-cutoff and post-cutoff criteria assess evidence synthesis and outcome anticipation (§3.2; Figures 2–4).
- **FinanceHarness agent framework:** A layered runtime combining point-in-time search, document reading, citation handling, structured financial tools, calculations, reusable workflow skills, robustness policies, and rubric-based rewards (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Controlled evaluation and optimization:** Seventeen baselines share the same retriever and evaluation protocol; a fixed-backbone ablation isolates the contribution of progressively richer harnessing and GRPO training (§§5.2–6.2; Tables 2 and 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| FinanceGym is difficult for evaluated systems. | Across 17 baselines on 400 expert-annotated questions, every overall normalized rubric score is below 40%; the best is Claude-Opus-4.7 at 34.1% ± 0.8 SE. | Table 2; §§5.3 and 6 |
| FinanceHarness leads the reported open-weight systems. | Qwen3.6-27B FinanceHarness scores 32.4% overall, 45.7% pre-cutoff, and 11.8% post-cutoff; the strongest listed open-weight alternative, GLM-5 with search, scores 30.4%, 44.7%, and 8.5%. | Table 2 |
| The harness contributes substantially at fixed backbone. | With Qwen3.6-27B, search-only Vanilla scores 25.3%; Naive harness 29.6%; full Harness 32.4%. Corresponding pre-/post-cutoff scores rise from 36.1%/8.7% to 45.7%/11.8%. | Table 4; §6.2 |
| GRPO adds little beyond the untrained harness. | Training on 172 separate machine-curated instances increases total score from 32.4% to 32.8%, pre-cutoff from 45.7% to 46.2%, and post-cutoff from 11.8% to 12.1%. | Table 4; §§6.2–7 |
| Forward-looking reasoning remains much harder. | Across Table 2, post-cutoff scores range from 5.2% to 12.8%, versus 27.8% to 50.1% pre-cutoff; FinanceHarness records 11.8% versus 45.7%. | Table 2; §§6.1 and C.2 |
| Expert curation rejects a meaningful fraction of generated material. | Of 500 balanced questions sent for professional annotation, 411 pass the quality bar (82%); the final release contains 400 questions and 2,464 rubric items. | §3.2; Figure 3 |
| Document-ID validation sharply reduces failed visits. | Disabling URL pre-fetch validation raises visit errors from 2.1% to 39.4%; the authors state that final score changes little but execution becomes costlier. | §4.1, “Operational guardrails” |
| Cross-backbone component evaluation favors GPT-5.5 but shows competitive Qwen grounding. | On 11 tasks with three runs per cell and dual-LLM judging, GPT-5.5 scores 4.36/4.47 and Qwen3.6-27B 4.02/4.20 for faithfulness/grounding on a 1–5 scale. | Appendix B; Table 9 |

These results support the authors’ claims that the layered harness improves one fixed backbone and that FinanceGym exposes a large pre-/post-cutoff capability gap. They do not establish that the harness universally outperforms proprietary models, that its reports yield profitable investment decisions, or that post-cutoff scores directly measure real-world forecasting accuracy. The cross-backbone appendix evaluates component execution rather than the headline PIT benchmark.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The sandbox uses a self-collected, English-language 2025 web corpus with limited coverage of non-English sources, paywalled professional data, and structured filings (Limitations, page 14).
- **Author-stated:** Models trained with live-web tools face an out-of-distribution corpus-backed interface, so their results measure transfer under the PIT setup rather than general capability (Limitations; §C.1).
- **Author-stated:** The corpus is not released, and grading uses private rubrics, limiting independent reproduction of the reported benchmark scores (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** All agents are run once, so within-system run-to-run variance is unmeasured even though bootstrap errors quantify variation over questions (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The primary scores depend on a single Gemini-3.5-Flash rubric judge; the supplied text gives no human–judge agreement study or sensitivity to alternative judges (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The fixed-backbone progression compares bundled configurations. The supplied text does not isolate the individual effects of citation handling, finance tools, skills, recovery policies, or prompt modes (Table 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Post-cutoff criteria are professionally validated but derive from later outcomes, so high scores may reward correspondence with selected outcomes rather than calibrated forecasts across all plausible futures (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The appendix’s live-web case-study claims are explicitly not independently verified and therefore demonstrate workflow behavior, not financial correctness (Appendix D, page 22).

## Open research questions

1. **Which harness components produce the 7.1-point improvement over search-only operation?** Table 4 changes several features together. A factorial ablation could independently remove citation repair, structured finance tools, skills, grounding review, and recovery policies.

2. **Does the benchmark ranking remain stable across judges?** The headline evaluation uses Gemini-3.5-Flash without a reported judge-agreement analysis. A next experiment could compare multiple blinded LLM judges with criterion-level expert rescoring.

3. **Can training materially improve post-cutoff reasoning?** GRPO adds only 0.3 points to the post-cutoff score, from 11.8% to 12.1%. A larger training sweep could vary reward composition and explicitly optimize forecast calibration on a temporally disjoint training set.

4. **How well does the method generalize beyond the English public web?** The corpus omits much non-English, paywalled, and structured financial evidence. A matched evaluation could add filings, professional databases, or multilingual corpora while preserving identical cutoff dates and questions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the full method, evaluation protocol, main tables, appendices, and explicit limitations.

Material missing information includes the underlying corpus, private rubrics, complete prompts and tool schemas, criterion-level human–judge agreement, repeated-run variance, detailed computational costs, statistical significance tests for pairwise system differences, and fine-grained ablations of individual harness components.
