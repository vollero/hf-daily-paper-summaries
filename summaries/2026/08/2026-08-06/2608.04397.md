# NOLLI: A Difficulty-Calibrated Puzzle Benchmark for Diagnosing the English-Korean Performance Gap

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04397) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04397)


## One-sentence takeaway

NOLLI finds that matched English–Korean presentation produces little performance difference, while tasks requiring multi-step Hangul-jamo manipulation and Korean kinship terminology expose substantial, model-dependent deficits.

## Problem and motivation

Multilingual benchmarks often report a single performance gap without distinguishing whether it comes from presentation language, writing-system operations, cultural knowledge, or task difficulty. Static benchmarks also risk contamination, while structural measures such as input size may poorly represent empirical difficulty. NOLLI addresses these problems with procedurally generated, behaviorally calibrated English–Korean puzzles designed to localize—though not causally isolate—different sources of failure (Sections 1, 2, and 3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Procedural benchmark:** 15 puzzle types yielding 25 language-specific tasks and 7,500 items. Instances are seed-regenerable, uniquely solvable, and evaluated deterministically by exact match; generators, instances, evaluators, and solver traces are released (Sections 3.1 and 3.4).
- **Three-level diagnostic design:** Eight matched direct-translation types estimate presentation-language differences; two script-adapted types contrast Roman-letter and Hangul-jamo procedures; five Korean-only types test cultural systems or orthography (Section 3.2; Figure 1).
- **Behavioral difficulty calibration:** Generator parameters are tuned against Gemini 3 Flash to target 75±10%, 50±10%, and 25±10% accuracy for Easy, Medium, and Hard, with adjacent tiers separated by at least 10 percentage points (Section 3.5).
- **Broad model evaluation:** Fifteen frontier, international open-weight, and Korean-developed models are evaluated; gap analyses retain the 12 models above 3% overall accuracy (Sections 4 and 5).
- **Difficulty taxonomy:** The authors identify size scaling, constraint/information structure, distractor density, and algorithmic/task-type depth as distinct escalation mechanisms (Section 5.3.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Matched presentation-language performance is equivalent within the benchmark’s resolution. | Across 12 non-floor models on eight direct-translation types, EN−KO gaps range from −2.7 to +5.0 pp; Holm-corrected TOST supports equivalence within ±10 pp for every model. Paired testing on three seed-matched tasks also supports equivalence. | Section 5.2; Appendix E, Table 7 |
| Korean Cipher exposes large deficits not consistently seen in jamo Cryptarithmetic. | DeepSeek-V4-Flash scores 74.3% EN versus 5.7% KO on Cipher, a +68.7 pp gap. Cryptarithmetic has no systematic Korean penalty: 6 of 12 non-floor models score higher in Korean. | Section 5.2; Table 2 |
| Jamo Composition performance strongly predicts Korean Cipher performance. | Across 12 non-floor models, \(R^2=0.91\); controlling for Direct-KO accuracy gives partial \(r=0.87\), \(p<.01\). The authors interpret this as consistent with multi-step sub-syllabic difficulty, not causal proof. | Section 5.2 |
| Kinship remains below each model’s Korean direct-task baseline. | The deficit is positive for all 12 non-floor models and does not narrow significantly with overall performance (\(r=0.16\), \(p=.62\), \(n=12\)). GPT-5.5 has a +36.7 pp deficit, while Gemini 3.1 has +4.7 pp. | Section 5.2 |
| Difficulty calibration largely meets its targets. | 72 of 75 task–tier cells fall within their bands; all 51 directly calibrated cells are in-band, while three mirrored Korean cells exceed their upper boundaries by 2–7 pp. | Section 3.5; Appendix C, Table 5 |
| Calibrated ordering transfers beyond the reference model. | Accuracy decreases Easy→Hard in 255/300 non-floor model–task pairs (85%), or 94% when ties count as preserving order. | Figure 3; Section 5.1 |
| Regeneration preserves approximate difficulty in the tested subset. | On six types/eight language-specific tasks, all eight preserve Easy > Medium > Hard; 20/24 cells (83%) remain in-band, with misses no larger than 7 pp. | Section 3.5; Appendix D, Table 6 |
| Structural size is an unreliable difficulty proxy. | A salient size measure fails to increase Easy→Hard in 7/15 types. For English Cipher, text length decreases while reference accuracy falls 52 pp; Saju drops 79%→23% and Time 77%→19% despite invariant instance length. | Section 5.3; Table 3 |
| Frontier models lead overall benchmark accuracy. | Macro averages are GPT-5.5 84.9%, Opus 4.8 78.3%, and Gemini 3.1 73.6%, across all 25 tasks and difficulty tiers. | Section 5.1; Table 2 |

The evidence supports NOLLI as a reproducible diagnostic benchmark and shows that matched presentation language alone is unlikely to explain the observed Korean failures at a ±10 pp resolution. It also establishes strong associations between jamo competence and Korean Cipher behavior. It does **not** isolate tokenization, sub-syllabic processing, cultural knowledge, or any other mechanism causally, because the script-adapted and Korean-only tasks differ in content and procedure.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Difficulty is calibrated against one model at one reasoning-effort setting; absolute tier boundaries need not transfer to other models. Full-suite regeneration stability was not tested.
- **Author-stated:** Direct-language conclusions assume semantic equivalence of fixed, author-written English and Korean templates; template validation cannot eliminate phrasing or interpretation differences.
- **Author-stated:** The jamo evidence is correlational. Both Jamo Composition and Korean Cipher involve jamo, and there is no English character-level control or direct measurement of model-specific jamo merging.
- **Author-stated:** Korean-only comparisons are confounded by task family, answer space, context length, and procedural demands. Kinship is the sole cultural-lexicon task.
- **Author-stated:** Results cover one language pair, exact-match puzzle solving, the reported inference configurations, and a ±10 pp equivalence margin; equivalence does not mean identical accuracy.
- **Analyst inference:** Model comparisons may partly reflect heterogeneous reasoning modes, temperatures, backends, and output budgets rather than model capability alone (Appendix F, Table 8).
- **Analyst inference:** Excluding models below the 3% floor is reasonable for diagnostic gaps but limits conclusions about very weak models; the text does not report a sensitivity analysis for alternative thresholds.
- **Analyst inference:** Calibration and evaluation rely heavily on the same reference-model family and fixed target bands; the supplied text does not include calibration against multiple independent reference models.

## Open research questions

1. **Is the Cipher deficit Korean-specific or a general subword-manipulation weakness?** The current correlation cannot separate these explanations. A next experiment could add matched English and other-script character decomposition, arithmetic, and recomposition controls with equal procedural depth.

2. **How stable is behavioral calibration across reference models?** Only Gemini 3 Flash defines the tiers. Recalibrating the generators independently against several model families and comparing tier assignments would test reference-model dependence.

3. **Does the Kinship deficit generalize across Korean cultural knowledge?** Kinship is the only cultural-lexicon task. A follow-up could introduce several procedurally verifiable Korean lexical systems while matching answer-space size, context length, and reasoning depth.

4. **Can targeted jamo representations improve multi-step execution?** The paper diagnoses an association but evaluates no intervention. Controlled fine-tuning or prompting with explicit jamo decomposition, followed by held-out Cipher and Jamo Composition evaluation, could test whether gains transfer.
