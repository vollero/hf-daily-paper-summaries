# FocusMem: Factorizing Content, Readout, and Trust in Latent GUI Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04530) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04530)


## One-sentence takeaway

FocusMem improves a frozen GUI agent by separating latent memory into role-aware content retention, decision-conditioned readout, and block-level trust, reaching 48.3% mean success across five web benchmarks versus 33.7% for a matched action-only fixed-memory baseline.

## Problem and motivation

GUI agents need episodic memory from completed tasks and working memory for unfinished current-task progress, but replaying full multimodal trajectories consumes substantial context. Existing latent-memory approaches compress each trajectory into a fixed block trained mainly through next-action supervision, leaving three gaps: important evidence may not survive compression, one representation must serve every decision stage, and irrelevant retrieved memories may still mislead the policy (§1, Fig. 1).

The authors argue that retention, decision-time exposure, and trust are distinct responsibilities that should be learned separately within a bounded latent interface.

## Approach and contributions

- **Role-aware content basis:** Episodic and working items share an encoder and Q-Former-style compressor but use role-specific base queries. Semantic distribution matching preserves general trajectory content, while role-specific functional pseudo-targets emphasize reusable experience for episodic memory and progress or remaining constraints for working memory (§3.3, Eqs. 3–7).
- **State-conditioned readout:** A lightweight adapter uses the frozen policy’s current-state representation and an item summary to modify the base queries, allowing one stored trajectory to yield different latent blocks at different decision states (§3.3, Eqs. 8–9).
- **Evidence trust gate:** A block-level gate removes memory whose state-conditioned representation appears irrelevant. It learns from the action objective plus deliberately injected unrelated episodic negatives (§3.3, Eqs. 10–12).
- **Frozen-policy, two-stage training:** Stage A trains fixed role-aware content; Stage B enables dynamic readout and trust. The Qwen3-VL-8B GUI policy and UGround-V1-7B grounding model remain frozen (§3.4; §4.1).
- **Targeted diagnostics:** The study separately tests content recoverability, robustness to expanding trajectory context, dependence on retrieved values, and resistance to irrelevant-memory contamination (§§4.4–4.6).

These results support the authors’ claim that all three components can contribute under the tested frozen-policy web setting. The matched primary comparison and controlled diagnostics provide stronger mechanism evidence than comparisons with adapted prior methods.

They do not establish statistical reliability across repeated runs, superiority for trainable policies or other backbones, or robustness to naturally occurring subtle and stale memories. The oracle-centered experiment tests compression/readout with manually localized supporting evidence, not retrieval quality (§D.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation primarily uses one frozen Qwen3-VL-8B policy on web benchmarks; other backbones and mobile or desktop environments remain untested (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Trust experiments inject clearly irrelevant episodic trajectories and do not examine subtle incompatibilities or stale working memory (§5).
- **Author-stated:** All reported success and diagnostic judgments rely on Gemini-3.1-Pro. Fixed method-blind prompts mitigate, but cannot eliminate, judge bias (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Main results and diagnostics use one run, with no confidence intervals or variance estimates, so the stability of the reported differences is unknown (§D.4; Table 9).
- **Analyst inference:** The cumulative ablation does not test every component independently or interaction order; it shows incremental value in one prescribed sequence rather than a complete factorial decomposition (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference:** Prior methods are “-style” unified-harness adaptations rather than guaranteed exact reproductions, which weakens direct claims about the original systems (§4.1, Baselines).
- **Analyst inference:** Live-web tasks were screened using a reference-policy trajectory and an LLM auditor. Although the resulting subsets were frozen across methods, this additional selection step may affect representativeness (§C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproduction is resource-intensive: the reported server has eight 96 GB GPUs, and the trainable compression pathway contains roughly 174.59M LoRA parameters plus 244.47M Q-Former/adapter parameters (Tables 4 and 11).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the factorization transfer across policies and GUI platforms?** Only Qwen3-VL-8B and web environments receive the full evaluation. A next experiment could repeat the matched fixed-versus-FocusMem study with multiple policy families on mobile and desktop benchmarks.

2. **Can the trust gate detect realistic ambiguity and staleness?** Training and diagnostics emphasize clearly unrelated episodic negatives. A next experiment could construct graded conflicts—outdated page states, partially applicable procedures, and stale working chunks—and measure calibration as well as task success.

3. **How stable are the gains across training and rollout randomness?** Every reported result comes from one run. Repeating training and interactive evaluation across several seeds would permit confidence intervals and paired significance tests.

4. **Which interactions among content, readout, and gate are essential?** The paper reports a cumulative sequence only. A full factorial ablation could remove each component from the complete system and test whether readout or gating still helps without role-aware content supervision.
