# Poly-OPD: Heterogeneous Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Capability-Selectable Flow Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-06.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04349) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04349)


## One-sentence takeaway

Poly-OPD distills complementary aesthetic and compositional abilities from latent-incompatible text-to-image teachers into selectable adapters on one 2.5B flow model, improving both preference and prompt-following metrics.

## Problem and motivation

Specialized text-to-image models have complementary strengths, but their different autoencoders, latent spaces, denoisers, and noise schedules prevent conventional latent- or trajectory-level distillation. Deploying each specialist separately adds memory, latency, and maintenance costs. The authors target a single compact student that matches multiple heterogeneous teachers, avoids cross-capability interference, and permits inexpensive capability selection at inference (Sections 1 and 3.1).

They identify three gaps: existing distillation generally assumes compatible teacher–student coordinates; training only on teacher-generated images is off-policy relative to the student’s inference trajectory; and uniform multi-capability training ignores both gradient conflicts and unequal progress across compositional subskills (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Heterogeneous on-policy distillation:** After an off-policy warm start, the student generates its own image, which is decoded to pixels, re-encoded by the selected teacher, re-noised at a magnitude-aligned point, and refined by that teacher. The student is trained toward this correction using cosine similarity in frozen DINOv2 feature space (Section 3.2, Eqs. 1–5).

2. **Measured adapter sharing:** A gradient-cosine diagnostic finds aligned attention updates but conflicts in some FFN blocks. Poly-OPD consequently shares attention LoRA parameters while keeping mode-specific FFN adapters, allowing preference or composition behavior to be selected by an adapter swap (Section 3.3, Figure 2, Eqs. 6–7).

3. **Gap-aware curriculum:** Compositional categories are sampled according to smoothed positive teacher–student score gaps on held-out probes, shifting training toward subskills with remaining teachable margin (Section 3.4, Eq. 8).

4. **Empirical consolidation:** The authors distill FLUX.1-dev and Z-Image into SD3.5-Medium and report simultaneous gains in aesthetic preference and compositional instruction following (Sections 4.1–4.2).

The evidence establishes improvements for one student, two teachers, two capability modes, and three automated benchmark suites under the reported sampling protocols. Component ablations support the importance of warm start, adapter isolation, gap-aware sampling, refinement depth, and DINOv2 features within this configuration. It does not establish broad generalization to other architectures, modalities, teacher counts, resolutions, or human preference evaluations; nor does surpassing a teacher by itself prove that on-policy training is the unique cause.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The experiments cover a 2.5B SD3.5-Medium student, FLUX.1-dev and Z-Image teachers, and two capabilities. Generality beyond this configuration is not demonstrated (Sections 4.1–4.2).

- **Author-stated dependency:** OPD without warm start performs poorly, so the method relies on first moving the student toward the teacher distributions (Sections 3.2 and 4.3).

- **Analyst inference — automated evaluation:** Results rely on learned reward models and compositional benchmarks; the supplied text reports no direct human evaluation, uncertainty intervals, or significance tests (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference — comparison control:** Teachers use official sampling settings while student variants use the SD3.5-Medium protocol. This is documented, but the text does not establish equal inference compute across model comparisons (Section 4.1).

- **Analyst inference — incomplete reproducibility evidence:** The retrieved text says full sampler, guidance, and schedule settings are in a supplement, but that supplement and exact table contents are absent. Section 3.2 also retains placeholder notation `RMIN`/`RMAX`, although Section 4.1 reports the experimental range as [15,20].

- **Analyst inference — gradient diagnostic coverage:** Compatibility is measured at warm-start initialization using 1,000 paired samples and four sampled blocks of a 24-layer student; stability across training, all blocks, more capabilities, or other architectures is not established (Figure 2).

- **Analyst inference — efficiency claim:** Adapter switching is described as negligible and per-step training costs are characterized structurally, but measured memory, latency, parameter overhead, and training compute are not supplied.

## Open research questions

1. **Does Poly-OPD scale beyond two teachers and capabilities?** The paper evaluates only preference and composition modes. A next experiment could add several heterogeneous specialists and measure quality, adapter growth, gradient conflicts, and mode-switching overhead.

2. **How architecture-dependent is the attention/FFN sharing boundary?** The diagnostic covers four blocks of SD3.5-Medium at one training stage. Repeating it across all layers, checkpoints, and student architectures could test whether the observed split is stable.

3. **Can warm-start dependence be reduced?** Direct OPD loses 24.1 GenEval points in the reported setup. Experiments could anneal refinement depth, use confidence-filtered teacher corrections, or gradually transition from teacher-generated to student-generated states.

4. **Do automated gains correspond to human judgments and deployment efficiency?** No direct human study or measured runtime comparison is supplied. A blinded preference evaluation paired with latency, memory, and training-cost measurements would test practical value.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, protocols, headline results, and prose ablation summaries, but omits the numerical bodies of Tables 1–6, the supplement, uncertainty estimates, statistical tests, hardware and total compute, adapter parameter counts, measured inference costs, code/model release information, direct human evaluation, and broader teacher/student configurations.
