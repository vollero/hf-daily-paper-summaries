# Thinking in a Low-Resource Language: What SFT Builds, What RL Fixes, What Accuracy Cannot See

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17744) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17744)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across four sparse-MoE checkpoints, Greek reasoning SFT reliably changed trace language and budget behavior without a defensible accuracy gain, while controlled RLVR substantially repaired format and channel defects without erasing Greek-language fidelity.

## Problem and motivation

Low-resource-language reasoning fine-tunes are usually judged by aggregate accuracy, which does not reveal whether reasoning is accessible in the user’s language, computationally economical, responsive to difficulty, or obtained at the expense of other capabilities. The authors show that this omission is consequential: the evaluated base models produced no strongly Greek traces despite Greek questions, while single-run accuracy comparisons were dominated by training-seed variation.

The identified gap is therefore both methodological and practical: existing Greek benchmarks assess final answers but not long-form reasoning behavior, and standard evaluation can confuse capability with formatting, contamination, translation register, or scorer defects (Introduction; §§3–9).

## Approach and contributions

- **Cross-family Greek reasoning adaptation:** The authors apply rank-32 LoRA SFT to four checkpoints from three sparse-MoE families at a similar active-parameter budget, using 59,107 reasoning rows and 58,985 direct-instruction rows (§2; Table 1).

- **Language-matched training:** Greek question–trace pairs are retained alongside English counterparts to make the default reasoning language follow the question rather than become unidirectionally locked (§10; Figure 9).

- **Multidimensional evaluation:** Six dimensions cover correctness, trace-language fidelity, reasoning budget, termination, intermediate reasoning steps, and item-conditioned budget overrun; candidate behavioral metrics correlated at \(|r|\geq0.6\) with length are rejected or redesigned (§§3, 9).

- **Controls for evaluation validity:** The study measures seed variance and documents six instrument failures involving answer extraction, output format, contamination, translation provenance, locale-sensitive numbers, and generation-lane defaults (§§4, 8).

- **Pre-registered RLVR refinement:** Four controlled GRPO arms vary deterministic rewards for correctness, formatting, termination, language consistency, and language-override obedience, including a random-reward control (§15).

- **Released assets:** Four SFT checkpoints and one RLVR-refined Qwen checkpoint are released; the paper also presents reusable probes, metrics, controls, and decision rules (Availability).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Single-run accuracy is unreliable at this scale. | Three otherwise identical Qwen reasoning-only runs had accuracy SD 4.4 percentage points and a 7.7-point range; the authors emphasize that this estimate comes from one family and only three seeds. | §4.2; Table 4; Figures 2–3 |
| Trace-language behavior changes sharply under SFT. | On the 5,156-item Greek benchmark, the measured bases had 0/5,156 traces with Greek-character ratio \(\geq0.9\); four language-matched SFT releases reached 97.4–98.1% fidelity. | §5.1; Table 7; Figure 9 |
| Language-matched training preserves question-following defaults but does not guarantee override steerability. | All evaluated releases produced English traces for English questions, but compliance with “reason in English” on Greek questions split 62.5%, 44.8%, 0%, and 0% across the four SFT releases. | §10.1; Tables 11–12 |
| Serving-cost effects depend on model family. | Across the same 5,156 items, median trace tokens changed from 1,788 to 586 for Qwen (3.0× fewer), 681 to 638 for NemotronH (near parity), and 396 to 640 for Gpt-OSS (1.6× more). | §5.2; Figure 5 |
| Fine-tunes exhibit stronger item-conditioned budget discipline. | The base overran the item-relative word budget on 98.0% of easy items, versus 0.2–12% for fine-tuned arms; hard-item think-mode comparisons are limited by truncation. | §5.3; Figure 6 |
| Trace-structure selection did not beat random sampling. | Selecting 15,607 high-scoring traces produced a −0.5-point result relative to an equally sized random sample, contrary to the pre-registered \(>2\)-point prediction. | §4.3; Table 5 |
| Reasoning-only training outperformed two-phase training in the historical Qwen runs, but corpus-version confounding remains. | Six reasoning-only arms averaged 73.6 versus 66.7 for nine two-phase arms; exact permutation \(p=0.0008\). A corpus-stratified test had only 16 permutations and \(p=0.125\). | §7.2; Figure 7 |
| General ability was mostly retained, with a family-specific residual. | Against each base on the Titan-1 suite, Qwen was approximately flat, Nano gained 3.8 Greek points, Lightning gained 1.7 Greek and lost 1.1 English points, while repaired Gpt-OSS retained a −3.2-point Greek residual and gained 1.0 English point. | §11; Tables 13–14; Figure 10 |
| RLVR repaired format and channel defects under a random-reward control. | On the held-out 5,156-item Greek lane, fallback fell 24.1%→2.5% and answer-channel leak 3.53%→0.00%; the random-reward arm remained at 22.1% and 3.61%. Greek fidelity remained above the pre-registered 97% gate. | §15.1; Table 16 |
| Override obedience responded to RLVR but missed the pre-registered success bar. | English-override compliance on 1,000 Greek questions rose 44.8%→53.9% (+9.1 points), with reverse compliance at 85.7% and fidelity at 98.27%; the required threshold was at least 60% and a 15-point gain. | §15.1; Table 16 |
| Accuracy-only RL did not erase Greek fidelity at the tested scale. | The arm without language protection ended at 98.22% Greek fidelity with zero within-question switches after 16,000 sampled completions. | §15.1 |

The evidence strongly supports the authors’ claim that trace language, output compliance, and budget behavior can change substantially while aggregate accuracy remains inconclusive. The random-reward arm also strengthens the causal interpretation of the reported RLVR repairs within this particular Qwen experiment.

It does not establish that Greek-language traces are more faithful, useful, or auditable to humans; fidelity is chiefly script-based. Nor does it establish accuracy equivalence in general, universal RLVR effectiveness, transfer to dense models or genuinely low-resource languages, or causality between shorter reasoning and correctness.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Every trained model is a sparse MoE adapted with LoRA. Dense models, full fine-tuning, other LoRA ranks and targets, and alternative training durations were not tested (Limitations).

- **Limited replication:** Most configurations use one seed; the principal 7.7-point noise estimate uses three Qwen runs and is assumed—not measured—to transfer to other families (§4.2; Limitations). The RLVR experiment likewise uses one family and one seed per arm (§15.1).

- **Benchmark validity:** All three reasoning axes are Greek versions of English benchmarks. Surface 13-gram checks cannot detect paraphrased contamination, and shared translator/training-generator provenance measurably affected one comparison (§8; Limitations).

- **Trace quality is not established:** Automatic character ratios measure script identity rather than fluency, terminology, usefulness, or faithfulness to internal computation. The switching sample had one annotator and no agreement measurement (Limitations).

- **Narrow language setting:** Greek has dedicated models and resources and is described as mid-resource; transfer to languages lacking a strong base or language-directed corpus remains untested (Limitations).

- **Missing cheaper baselines:** The study did not compare post-hoc translation of English traces or few-shot Greek reasoning examples; its prompting evidence concerns bare instructions only (Limitations).

- **Recipe comparison confounding:** The 15-arm reasoning-only versus two-phase result comes from historical runs with incompletely balanced corpus versions; the stratified test has little resolution (§7.2).

- **Metric assumptions:** Zero code-switching is treated as desirable even though Greek technical language commonly borrows English terminology, and item-conditioned length analyses remain endogenous (§5.2; Limitations).

- **Analyst inference:** Releasing metrics and controls improves reproducibility conceptually, but the supplied text does not clearly state whether all training data, evaluation code, reward implementation, fuzz cases, and exact preregistration artifacts are publicly available alongside the checkpoints.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the seed-instability result persist for dense models?**  
   It remains open because all tested models are sparse MoEs and the principal variance estimate comes from Qwen. A next experiment would compare matched dense and MoE models under identical LoRA recipes across enough seeds to estimate both variance distributions.

2. **Do language-matched traces improve human auditability?**  
   The paper measures script identity, not whether Greek-speaking users can understand, verify, or correct the reasoning. A next experiment would use multiple Greek-speaking annotators to score fluency, terminology, logical followability, and error-detection utility, with agreement reported.

3. **Why is explicit reasoning-language override family-dependent?**  
   Identical matched SFT yielded English-override compliance of 62.5%, 44.8%, 0%, and 0%, and the paper offers no mechanism. A next experiment could apply the same controlled override-reward training to every family while measuring representation and channel behavior before and after training.

4. **Will RLVR repairs survive broader training scales and domains?**  
   The reported result is one Qwen round with 2,000 prompts and one seed per arm. A next experiment would vary seeds, prompt-pool size, domains, and training duration while retaining random-reward controls and the preregistered fidelity gates.
