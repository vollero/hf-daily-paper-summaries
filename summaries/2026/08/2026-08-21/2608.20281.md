# Inject, Align, Recover: Staged Post-Training for Retrieval-Free Document Knowledge Internalization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20281) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20281)


## One-sentence takeaway

IAR separates structured document exposure, answer-only QA alignment, and weight-space recovery, producing stronger domain–general trade-offs than Vanilla SFT in 7 of 8 corpus–model settings, though results depend on the corpus, model, and selected operating point.

## Problem and motivation

The paper studies retrieval-free document knowledge internalization: adapting an instruction-tuned language model to answer held-out questions about a fixed corpus without receiving documents or retrieved passages at inference time (Task Definition, p. 3).

The authors identify three linked problems:

- QA-only SFT sparsely covers document facts.
- Continued pretraining exposes full documents but does not directly teach QA behavior.
- Domain adaptation can degrade instruction following and broad benchmark performance through catastrophic forgetting.

The gap is a controlled framework that treats document exposure, QA accessibility, and general-capability recovery as separate interventions rather than collapsing them into one training recipe (Why Three Stages?, p. 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Inject:** Converts documents into supervised continuation, rewrite-from-compressed-representation, and instruction-conditioned full-document reconstruction tasks. Loss applies only to assistant targets, distinguishing it from raw next-token CPT (Methodology, pp. 3–4; Table 7).
- **Align:** Fine-tunes the injected checkpoint on document-derived QA pairs using answer-only supervision, making acquired information accessible through a question-answer interface (p. 3).
- **Recover:** Merges the Inject+Align checkpoint with the original instruction model using a fixed grid of SLERP, task arithmetic, TIES, and DARE candidates. A validation-only, domain-primary rule selects an operating point with general-capability guardrails (p. 4; Tables 15–16).
- **Empirical decomposition:** Evaluates domain acquisition and general retention separately across two corpora and four main model families, with token-matched QA-only training and several adaptation/recovery baselines (pp. 4–7).
- **Clarified novelty:** The authors explicitly do not claim new reconstruction losses or merge algorithms; the contribution is the staged decomposition, controlled evaluation, and operating-point selection (Related Work, pp. 2–3).

The evidence supports the authors’ narrower claim that staged document exposure and recovery often yield a stronger domain-primary operating point than direct SFT, and that pre-recovery document training contributes measurable domain gains.

It does not establish universal dominance: extended baselines sometimes win general metrics, Phi–CCI is mixed, and CC Llama remains a domain–general trade-off against BudgetMatch. Nor does it establish repeated-run robustness, causal memorization, a scaling law, or performance outside the two supplied corpora.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Every checkpoint is based on a single training run; fixed seeds do not establish distributed-training determinism or robustness across seeds (Supplementary Section C, p. 12).
- Domain generation and judge APIs lack explicit sampler seeds, so evaluation is stochastic despite fixed data-order seeds (pp. 4–5; Supplementary Section D).
- LLM judging is imperfect. Bootstrap intervals quantify evaluation-sample uncertainty, not training-seed uncertainty (Supplementary Section D; Table 13).
- The larger Qwen3 scaling runs cover only CC, use the same selected TIES density, and lack archived Inject/Align training configurations; they do not establish a scaling law or cross-corpus generality (RQ4, p. 7; Supplementary Section C).
- Base-initialized CPT+SFT is not initialization-matched to IAR, while Instruct-initialized CPT diagnostics cover only three settings; no uniform comparison between Inject and raw CPT is supported (Baselines, p. 4; Table 23).
- The best Inject mixture varies by model and corpus, so the work does not identify a universal recipe (RQ3; Tables 26–27).
- QA-construction checks are not a substitute for human validation, and some candidates were retained under a fail-open parsing rule (Supplementary Section B, p. 10).

**Analyst inferences**

- The evidence for broad general-capability recovery is limited to IFEval, MMLU, and a fixed 200-example MSBench subset; transfer to other capabilities is untested.
- Because selection explicitly optimizes a validation domain–general frontier, results depend on the chosen tolerance, guardrails, and metric set; no sensitivity analysis of that selection rule is reported.
- The fixed 12-candidate Recover grid compares several merge families but does not isolate which merge design properties cause recovery.
- CC and CCI QA pairs are generated from source documents rather than independently authored, human-validated questions, creating a possible validity gap between benchmark accuracy and practical document use.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is IAR across training and inference seeds?**  
   Only one training run and one stochastic domain-generation pass are reported. A next experiment should repeat representative settings across several training seeds, generation seeds, and judge panels.

2. **Does structured Inject outperform raw CPT under fully matched initialization, tokens, and optimization?**  
   The main CPT baseline starts from Base checkpoints, and the Instruct-controlled diagnostics are incomplete. A factorial comparison could hold initialization, token volume, epochs, and QA alignment constant while varying only the document objective.

3. **How should the Inject mixture be selected without extensive per-model tuning?**  
   Different models and corpora prefer different mixtures. A next experiment could learn mixture weights on training-derived validation data and test whether the selection transfers across corpora or model sizes.

4. **Does recovery preserve internalized knowledge beyond generated QA benchmarks?**  
   Evaluation uses held-out document-derived questions but no human validation or broader knowledge probes. A next experiment could add human-written questions, adversarial paraphrases, multi-hop questions, and calibrated exact-evidence checks.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental design, tabulated comparisons, and stated qualifications because the supplied text includes the main paper and extensive supplementary material.

Material information still unavailable or incomplete:

- Multi-seed training and repeated stochastic-inference results.
- Training configurations and hardware provenance for Qwen3-8B/14B/32B.
- Full human validation of generated QA data.
- Sensitivity of Recover selection to its 1-point tolerance and guardrail definition.
- Complete initialization-matched CPT comparisons across all models and corpora.
- Clear confirmation in the supplied text of whether code, checkpoints, or datasets are publicly released, beyond references to a “code package” and repository artifacts.
