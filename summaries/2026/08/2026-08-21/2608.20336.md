# WithEveryone: Unified Planning and Identity Grounding for Group Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20336) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20336)


## One-sentence takeaway

WithEveryone jointly plans identity-to-person assignments, layouts, and poses while spatially grounding identity supervision, improving preservation and coverage for group images containing five to ten referenced people.

## Problem and motivation

Identity-preserving generation becomes unreliable as more referenced people share an image: identity signals weaken, faces may be duplicated or merged, and references can be bound to the wrong people or locations. Existing identity losses generally support only one or two people and infer correspondence by matching noisy predicted-face embeddings, which the authors argue becomes unstable for larger groups. Larger groups also increase layout, overlap, and pose complexity. The paper targets coherent generation from five to ten identities and identifies the lack of scalable identity supervision coupled with explicit multi-person planning as the central gap (Introduction, pp. 3–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified identity and layout planning:** A multimodal model selects participating references, converts each ArcFace embedding into an addressed ID token, and autoregressively predicts identity–layout bindings, face and body boxes, and pose keypoints. A deterministic renderer converts this plan into a visual condition (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figures 3–4, pp. 5–6).

- **ID Representation Forcing:** Before image synthesis, the model predicts a continuous representation for every selected identity and aligns it with the corresponding target-face embedding through a cosine loss, intended to keep identities accessible in the long shared context (Section 3.2, Equation 1, p. 5).

- **Layout-Grounded ID Loss:** Rather than matching noisy generated faces to references during training, the loss uses annotated face regions and landmarks to establish correspondence, then applies ArcFace cosine supervision to matching predicted and target crops. It operates on one-step clean-image estimates for timesteps \(t\leq0.85\) (Sections 3.3 and B.5, pp. 6–7, 22–23).

- **Evaluation assets:** The authors construct an identity-disjoint benchmark of 210 examples spanning five to ten references and train on 400K in-house group-image samples (Sections 4.1 and C.2, pp. 7, 24).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that their complete system performs strongly on this particular identity-disjoint, five-to-ten-person benchmark, and that LG-ID Loss is the largest isolated contributor among the tested additions. It does not establish generalization outside this reference-count range, causal superiority of every component, or a controlled resolution advantage over all competing systems. Copy-Paste is an embedding-derived proxy rather than direct proof that a model copied pixels.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Conclusions come from one 210-example benchmark, with progressively fewer examples at larger group sizes; per-size results are therefore trends rather than precise estimates (Section E, p. 27).

- **Author-stated:** A single target layout is an imperfect standard when prompts permit several valid compositions (Section E, p. 27).

- **Author-stated:** Face-detection and recognition metrics may vary across demographic groups, but demographic-stratified results are not supplied (Section E, p. 27).

- **Author-stated:** Compared systems differ in maximum resolution and autonomous planning capability. ID-Patch and WithAnyone receive ground-truth layouts, further complicating uniform comparison (Sections 4.1 and E, pp. 7, 27).

- **Author-stated:** The full model changes several components and adds text-to-layout data simultaneously, so its margin over LG-ID Loss alone cannot be assigned to a specific addition (Section 4.3; Table 5, pp. 10, 24).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is limited because training uses 400K in-house samples and an internal HunyuanImage 3.5-preview initialization; the supplied text does not establish public availability of the data, checkpoint, or complete model (Section C.2, p. 24).

- **Analyst inference:** LG-ID Loss assumes that a generated face lands in its annotated region. The authors provide convergence evidence for plan adherence, but failures early in training or under severe occlusion may still weaken crop-based supervision (Sections 3.3 and 4.4, pp. 6, 11).

- **Author-stated risk:** Generating identifiable people can enable non-consensual depiction or impersonation; the authors recommend consent and provenance signaling (Section E, p. 27).

## Open research questions

1. **Can planning quality improve without oracle layouts?** Ground-truth layouts substantially outperform predicted layouts, leaving plan prediction as a reported error source. A next experiment could train a stronger planner while holding the generator, identity losses, and training data fixed, then evaluate RLS, Plan IoU, and identity coverage.

2. **How robust is the method outside five-to-ten-person group photos?** The benchmark establishes only that range. Testing smaller and larger groups, stronger occlusion, non-frontal faces, and non-photographic styles with fixed protocols would measure extrapolation.

3. **Does Representation Forcing causally create identity-specific routing?** Attention is diagonally concentrated in three examples, but the paper lacks an attention comparison against a no-forcing model. A controlled layer-wise routing analysis across the full benchmark could compare matched-ID attention and generated-face attribution with and without the loss.

4. **How fair and stable are the identity metrics across populations?** The authors note demographic sensitivity but provide no stratified analysis. A balanced, consented benchmark could report detection failure, Sim(Ref), Sim(Tgt), Coverage, and Dup by demographic and imaging-condition strata.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, experiments, and within-benchmark conclusions because the supplied text includes detailed objectives, protocols, ablations, and appendices.

Materially unavailable or unresolved information includes public release status and licensing of the model and benchmark; full composition and demographic distribution of the in-house training data and evaluation set; per-group uncertainty for five through ten identities; human evaluation of identity, layout, and copying; detailed safety mitigations beyond recommendations; and controlled component-level attribution for the complete model’s gains.
