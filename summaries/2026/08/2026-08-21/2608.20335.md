# 4DAnyone: Create Anyone in 4D from a Casual Monocular Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20335) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20335)


## One-sentence takeaway

4DAnyone converts an uncalibrated monocular human video into multiview-consistent videos for 4D Gaussian Splatting by combining depth-aware skeleton guidance with fixed-size reference packing and cross-group denoising context routing.

## Problem and motivation

High-quality 4D human reconstruction typically depends on synchronized, calibrated multicamera capture, while monocular methods struggle with occluded anatomy and unseen appearance. Generating many novel-view videos before reconstruction is a promising alternative, but existing video-diffusion systems lose consistency when target views must be split across multiple bounded-attention forward passes. The authors identify two coupled gaps: reference context grows linearly with generated views, and separately denoised target groups cannot exchange structural information (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Generation-to-reconstruction pipeline:** A human-motion-recovery model estimates a 3D skeleton sequence from a monocular video. Depth-buffered skeleton renderings condition a video diffusion transformer, whose outputs are lifted into a 4D Gaussian representation with FreeTimeGS (§3.1, Fig. 2).
- **Reference Context Packing (RCP):** Mixed-resolution patchification compresses prior generated views into fixed token slots, changing reference-context complexity from \(O(N)\) to \(O(1)\) while retaining coarse coverage and selected detail (§3.3).
- **Target Context Routing (TCR):** Four-view groups are cyclically rearranged during high-noise denoising to communicate global structure, then fixed into adjacent groups at low noise for detail refinement (§3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **3D-aware skeleton conditioning:** Pixelwise depth buffering encodes which body parts occlude others without adding input channels, addressing front–back ambiguity in ordinary 2D skeletons (§3.2).
- **Training asset:** The authors construct MVGameHuman, containing 38,000 synchronized videos of 318 actors, rendered from 24 virtual cameras at \(2560\times1440\) (§4.1; Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Skeletons do not constrain loose garments far from the body, producing inconsistent views and degraded reconstruction (Appendix H, Fig. 10).
- **Author-stated:** HMR errors propagate directly into every generated view; the paper shows an en-pointe pose incorrectly reconstructed as flat feet (Appendix H, Fig. 10).
- **Author-stated scope:** Monocular training sequences with large camera motion are filtered (§4.1), limiting evidence for highly mobile capture.
- **Analyst inference:** Quantitative evaluation is narrow—13 scenes overall, with ablations on eight DNA-Rendering scenes—and in-the-wild evidence is qualitative (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline comparability is imperfect: ReCamMaster is fine-tuned with the authors’ data and RCP/TCR, whereas MV-Performer and TrajectoryCrafter use released weights zero-shot; MV-Performer additionally receives ground-truth camera parameters during depth preparation (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility and resource accessibility may be difficult: training uses a 5B-parameter model and 128 H20-3E GPUs, while MVGameHuman comes from an in-house engine and its release status is not established (§4.1; Appendices B–C).
- **Analyst inference:** Missing numerical contents of Tables 1–5 and 7 prevent verification of reported margins, dataset composition, and several ablation claims from the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can loose clothing be modeled without dense, unreliable geometry?** Skeleton guidance fails for fabric moving independently of the body. A next experiment could add garment keypoints, optical-flow cues, or a learned cloth representation and evaluate consistency specifically on loose-clothing sequences.

2. **How robust is the pipeline to HMR uncertainty?** The current generator follows erroneous skeletons faithfully. A plausible experiment would propagate multiple pose hypotheses or confidence-weighted skeletons and measure reconstruction quality under controlled keypoint perturbations.

3. **Does routing scale beyond 16–48 cameras and four-view groups?** The paper describes several camera configurations but provides no reported scaling curve. Testing view count, group size, memory, runtime, and consistency jointly would establish the practical limit of RCP and TCR.

4. **How well does the method handle realistic moving cameras?** Large-camera-motion sequences are filtered. A targeted benchmark varying camera translation, rotation, blur, and occlusion could compare the present preprocessing with camera-motion-aware HMR or stabilization.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The retrieved text provides detailed method, protocol, resource, and failure-case descriptions, but the numerical contents of the main dataset, comparison, ablation, training-configuration, inference-configuration, and routing-sweep tables are missing. Also unavailable are exact quantitative margins, MVGameHuman release terms, code/checkpoint contents, detailed in-the-wild sampling criteria, and the full diversity of failure frequencies.
