# FlowEvo: Self-Evolving Agents through the Co-Evolution of Workflows and Executable Skills

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21596) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21596)


## One-sentence takeaway

FlowEvo is a training-free agent framework that turns verifier-passed workflows into a persistent, curated bank of executable skills, improving accuracy across five benchmarks primarily through direct reuse and skill-conditioned generation.

## Problem and motivation

Workflow-based LLM agents often discover useful procedures during inference but discard them after each episode, forcing later tasks to rediscover similar solutions and incur additional token cost and variance. Text memories preserve descriptions but cannot be directly invoked or validated, while executable skill libraries are generally built offline and separately from the agent’s own problem-solving trajectory. The authors address the gap of continuously converting successful workflows into reusable, executable capabilities without changing model parameters (Sections 1 and 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Online workflow-to-skill compilation:** Successful, verifier-passed traces are converted into records containing an executable body, callable interface, replay tests, metadata, and lifecycle state (Sections 3.1–3.4).
- **Tiered reuse:** Retrieval and routing select among dynamic generation, direct execution of a compatible skill, and generation conditioned on compact structured skill context; failed or unsafe direct reuse can fall back to conditioning (Sections 3.3–3.4; Table 13).
- **Admission and lifecycle governance:** Candidates undergo duplicate, interface, functional, and safety checks. Downstream utility is tracked through guided-versus-withheld comparisons, allowing persistently harmful skills to be restricted or suppressed (Section 3.5).
- **Training-free persistent adaptation:** The base model remains fixed; only the executable bank, auxiliary memory, and routing state evolve over the task stream (Equation 1; Algorithm 1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest reported accuracy on all five benchmarks under GPT-4o-mini | FlowEvo scores 85.6% on ALFWorld, 95.1% HumanEval pass@1, 79.6% MBPP pass@1, 97.1% GSM8K solve rate, and 75.9% MATH-500 solve rate; all exceed the eight reported baselines. | Tables 1–2; Section 4.2 |
| Large ALFWorld accuracy and cost advantage | On 134 out-of-distribution evaluation tasks, FlowEvo reaches 85.6% ± 3.8 over three seeds and 9,329 tokens/task, versus the strongest baseline accuracy of 59.2% for AFlow at 30,137 tokens/task: +26.4 points. | Table 1; Sections 4.1–4.2 |
| Gains extend across model families and scales | Against ExpeL, FlowEvo wins 49 of 50 model–dataset cells across 10 models, with mean gain +11.0 and median +7.0 points. The exception is Qwen3-8B on MATH-500 at −10.0 points. | Table 3; Section 4.3 |
| Skill feedback is the largest ablated component | On ALFWorld over three seeds: ReAct 33.6%; compilation without reuse 38.8% (+5.2); adding direct and conditioned feedback 80.6% (+41.8); adding curation 85.6% (+5.0). | Table 4; Section 4.4 |
| Direct reuse is highly effective in a representative ALFWorld run | The coarse direct-reuse route is used in 101/134 episodes and succeeds in 99/101 (98%); overall run success is 111/134 (83%). | Figure 2; Tables 15–16; Section 4.5 and Appendix G |
| Curation catches at least one harmful skill | In one seed, `pick_two_obj_and_place` achieves 2/14 guided success versus 1/3 withheld, a −0.19 delta below the −0.1 suppression threshold, triggering suppression. | Section 4.4; Tables 12 and 14 |
| Exact executable transfer improves efficiency on MBPP | On 71 held-out fixed-bank exact-transfer cases, executable reuse scores 85.9% at 204.4 tokens versus dynamic generation at 81.7% and 224.2 tokens. Text memory scores 87.3% but uses 377.5 tokens. | Appendix A.1; Table 5 |
| Admission is moderately robust to simulated label noise | On ALFWorld over three seeds, removing trace-success gating lowers accuracy from 85.6% to 84.1% (McNemar \(p=0.125\)); flipping 20% of labels yields 83.8% (\(p=0.227\)). | Table 10; Section 4.4 |
| Router variants are statistically indistinguishable on full-split MBPP | All six fixed-bank router-pair differences are below 0.6 points, their 95% confidence intervals cross zero, and McNemar \(p>0.5\). | Appendix A.2; Table 8 |

The results support the authors’ claim that persistent executable reuse can improve benchmark accuracy and token efficiency without weight updates, with the ALFWorld ablation identifying skill feedback as the main contributor. They do not establish performance on unverifiable or signal-free tasks, cross-benchmark skill transfer, indefinitely growing banks, or universal superiority to text memory: on MBPP exact transfer, text memory is 1.4 points more accurate but substantially more expensive.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation depends on correctness signals from environment feedback or tests; free-form dialogue and long-form writing are outside scope, while delayed, partial, or adversarial feedback may be harder (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** The loop requires enough base capability to generate admissible skills. Qwen3-8B on MATH-500 produces a sparse bank and trails ExpeL by 10.0 points (Table 3; Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** Long-running maintenance—including merging, revising, retiring, and preventing retrieval interference—remains unresolved (Section 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-domain reuse is untested because every benchmark starts from an empty bank and prohibits skill or memory transfer (Section 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Scalability evidence is narrow: the detailed online study contains only 134 ALFWorld episodes and saturates at seven templates plus 18 exemplars, which does not establish behavior for large, heterogeneous banks (Section 4.4; Table 11).
- **Analyst inference:** The sequential component ablation does not test all combinations independently, so interactions and ordering effects among compilation, feedback, and curation remain ambiguous (Table 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Several accumulation and routing statistics come from representative single runs, whereas the headline ALFWorld accuracy uses three seeds; those diagnostics should not automatically be interpreted as population-level estimates (Figures 2–3; Tables 15–16).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline execution uses official default configurations and matched budgets, but the supplied text does not provide complete per-baseline hyperparameters or all run variances, limiting independent assessment of comparison sensitivity (Section 4.1 and Appendix B.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the loop work without reliable automatic verification?** This remains open because all evaluated domains provide environment feedback or executable tests. A next experiment could introduce calibrated partial, delayed, and adversarial verifier signals and measure bank quality, negative transfer, and recovery.

2. **Do accumulated skills transfer across domains?** Benchmark-local banks explicitly exclude cross-benchmark transfer. A next experiment could build a bank on one code or math dataset, freeze it, and evaluate direct and conditioned reuse on a distinct held-out benchmark.

3. **How does lifecycle governance scale to large, nonstationary banks?** The observed ALFWorld bank is small and quickly saturates. A next experiment could run a long task stream with distribution shifts while comparing suppression-only governance against merging, revision, and retirement policies.

4. **Which reuse mechanism matters on unique code and math problems?** The paper attributes gains mainly to conditioned generation and adaptive escalation, but full-split MBPP router variants do not separate statistically. A factorial experiment could independently disable compilation, conditioning, and escalation across all four code/math benchmarks.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported benchmark results because the supplied text includes the full main paper and appendices with protocols, tables, thresholds, and prompts.

Material missing or insufficiently reported includes complete per-baseline hyperparameters, variance or confidence intervals for many main-table entries, full statistical tests for every baseline comparison, computational latency and monetary cost, behavior under cross-domain transfer, and evaluation on long-running or signal-free task streams.
