# ForgeWM: Progressive Causal Training for Few-Step Action-Conditioned Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14022) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14022)


## One-sentence takeaway

ForgeWM progressively converts a bidirectional video generator into budget-specialized causal world models that preserve frame-aligned game controls, achieving the strongest overall quality–control profile among the evaluated Minecraft systems while supporting faster interaction and draft-preserving offline refinement.

## Problem and motivation

Interactive video world models must generate causally and quickly while responding reliably to discrete keyboard inputs and continuous mouse motion. Few-step sampling magnifies errors in generated history, action alignment, and key–value caches, causing degradation across autoregressive chunks. The authors identify a gap in adapting causal distillation to game-native controls whose frame-rate signals must remain synchronized with temporally compressed latent chunks throughout training and rollout (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Progressive causal training:** Four stages move from bidirectional domain adaptation to teacher-forced causal flow matching, online causal consistency distillation, and on-policy distribution matching over self-generated histories (Table 1; Sections 3.3 and A.1).

- **Aligned game-native control:** Separate pathways and caches preserve discrete keyboard states and continuous mouse motion. Four video frames map to one latent frame, and each three-latent chunk aligns with 12 video frames of controls (Section 3.2).

- **Budget specialization:** Separate students target steady-state budgets of 1, 2, or 4 denoising updates; the 1- and 2-step variants retain four updates for their initial chunk (Sections 3.3–3.4).

- **Dual-path deployment:** Online interaction uses a low-step student, while optional replay re-noises and refines saved chunks using the same student, recorded actions, and progressively refined causal prefix (Section 4.3; Figure 6).

- **Control-space transfer:** The same four objectives are applied to FPS gamepad data after widening the continuous-control interface from two to four channels; the backbone and action-module topology remain unchanged (Sections 4.4 and C.2).

These results establish strong relative performance within the paper’s controlled Minecraft comparison and show that its training sequence can produce a causal FPS checkpoint. They do not establish broad out-of-distribution generalization, indefinite rollout stability, or superiority under a common native control interface: HY-WorldPlay required an adapter, and CrossFPS used a separate evaluation protocol.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Quantitative evaluation covers only 77 frames. Rollouts approximately 3.5 times longer show gradual loss of block structure and spreading color artifacts, so indefinite stability is not claimed (Figure 9; Appendix D).

- **Author-stated:** CrossFPS motion is systematically too strong, with a macro-average generated/reference flow ratio of 1.45 (Table C1; Appendix D).

- **Author-stated:** Stage 3’s value is metric-dependent at four steps: it improves Imaging Quality but worsens paired LPIPS relative to Stage 2, and its incremental effect at one and two steps is not isolated (Table A2; Appendix D).

- **Author-stated:** Broader out-of-distribution generalization is outside the study’s scope. CrossFPS is complementary rather than directly comparable with Minecraft, and its combined evaluation split cannot be compared directly with partition-specific benchmark results (Section 4.6; Appendix C.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Baseline fairness is imperfect because HY-WorldPlay uses a different action representation and chunk length, with motion scale influenced by a deterministic adapter (Sections 4.1 and 4.6).

- **Analyst inference:** The human study’s student-only volunteer pool, forced choice without ties, and five comparisons per criterion per participant limit population-level conclusions (Appendix B.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Compute reporting omits VAE decoding and other pipeline costs, so the reported FPS does not establish complete end-to-end interactive latency (Sections 4.1 and B.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Can long-horizon drift be reduced without sacrificing low-step latency?** The extended rollouts still lose structure and accumulate color artifacts. A next experiment could compare longer training rollouts or expanded causal memory across substantially longer, quantitatively scored sequences.

2. **Does on-policy distribution matching help at one- and two-step budgets?** The stage ablation isolates Stage 3 only under four-step inference. Training matched Stage 2 and Stage 3 checkpoints for each lower budget would reveal whether its benefit depends on sampling budget.

3. **How can FPS motion magnitude be calibrated?** CrossFPS preserves enough motion to roll out but over-responds relative to references. A plausible experiment would add magnitude-aware control or flow losses and report per-action direction and magnitude errors separately.

4. **How robust is control transfer beyond the evaluated games and interfaces?** The paper tests Minecraft and one seven-title FPS corpus but excludes broader OOD generalization. A next experiment could hold out entire games or introduce unseen controller mappings while retaining matched-action evaluation.
