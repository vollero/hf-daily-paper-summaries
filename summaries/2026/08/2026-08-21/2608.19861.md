# PolicyGuide: From Guarding One Action to Guiding the Whole Workflow for Policy-Compliant LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19861) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19861)


## One-sentence takeaway

PolicyGuide converts natural-language policies into persistent workflow graphs and uses an external verifier to guide multi-turn LLM agents, improving reliable policy-constrained outcomes most strongly where compliance depends on ordered procedures.

## Problem and motivation

Customer-service agents can violate policy either by taking forbidden actions or by omitting or misordering required steps such as identification, eligibility checks, consent, and post-action verification. Existing action-local guards intervene mainly around risky tool calls, so they may detect procedural failures too late or miss workflows without agent-side mutations; workflow agents, meanwhile, primarily optimize faithful process completion rather than externally safeguarding a general-purpose agent (§1–§2.3).

The authors’ policy analysis finds that ordered workflow requirements are concentrated in Telecom—54.0% of its requirements, versus 4.7% for Airline and 3.6% for Retail—motivating persistent workflow tracking rather than final-action checking alone (Appendix B, Table 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Compiled policy representation:** An offline, multi-stage pipeline converts each domain’s policy and tool registry into a validated workflow graph containing shared procedures, request-specific subflows, decision branches, tool authorizations, and explicit satisfaction conditions (§3.2–§3.3; Figure 3).

- **Proactive external verifier:** Before each response to a user turn, the verifier reconciles open requests, resumes each from code-persisted graph state, traverses satisfied nodes, and returns remediation for the first unmet requirement (§3.4; Algorithm 1).

- **Advisory intervention:** Guidance steers non-mutating behavior, while the first unauthorized mutating call in a user-turn region is intercepted and followed by corrective verification; an immediate retry is then allowed (§3.4).

- **Conceptual contribution:** The paper frames policy compliance as jointly requiring safeguarding and workflow enforcement, and formalizes complete intervention coverage under ideal binding-verifier assumptions (Appendix A, Theorem 1 and Corollaries 1–2).

- **Empirical contribution:** It evaluates outcome reliability, graph/runtime ablations, a matched workflow controller, cross-model transfer, adversarial users, procedural traces, cost, and latency (§4; Appendices C–D).

The evidence establishes improved outcome reliability in the tested synthetic customer-service settings, particularly Telecom, and provides diagnostic support for external graph tracking. It does not establish guaranteed compliance, live-user effectiveness, exhaustive procedural validity, cross-domain adversarial robustness, or transfer across workflow-authoring models. The ablations also remove multiple runtime components together, so they do not isolate the causal effect of persistence, remediation, or interception individually (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Evaluation covers only three English τ²-bench domains, simulated users, and four trials per multi-trial cell; Retail contains only 10 policy-violation tasks (Limitations: “Evaluation scope”).
- CRAFT evaluation uses only a 20-task Airline split, with neither cross-domain nor adaptive-attack robustness established (Limitations: “Benchmark coverage”).
- The Telecom trace rubric is author-designed, uses deterministic ordering and text matching, depends on gold task actions, and lacks a second-annotator agreement estimate; it is not an exhaustive compliance oracle (§4.7; Limitations).
- Only one GPT-5.4-authored workflow per domain is tested, so workflow-author generalization and seed sensitivity remain unknown (Limitations: “Workflow generation and faithfulness”).
- The verifier fires at user-turn boundaries rather than before every policy-relevant action; between-trigger deviations fall outside the unconditional coverage result (Appendix A, Corollary 2; Limitations).
- Enforcement is probabilistic and mostly advisory, exceptions fail open, and the one-shot mutation gate permits an immediate retry; the system is not a formal compliance guarantee (Limitations: “Probabilistic enforcement”).
- Guide calls add approximately $0.40 per conversation in the summarized configuration and increase observed wall-clock time by more than fivefold (Limitations; Appendix D).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The outcome gains cannot be attributed solely to graph compilation because PolicyGuide-Self removes several external-runtime mechanisms together, while PolicyGuide-Raw also lacks persistent graph position (§3.5; §4.3).
- Manual verification of one frozen graph per domain supports those artifacts but provides limited evidence about the reliability of automated graph generation at larger scale or under policy changes (Appendix E).
- Comparisons are uneven across baselines: ToolGuard is Airline-only, FlowAgent is evaluated only on Telecom’s 40-task test split, and agent-family transfer is tested only on Airline (§4.1; Tables 3–4).

## Open research questions

1. **Which runtime component produces the improvement?** The current variants remove state persistence, remediation, interception, and external verification in bundles. A factorial ablation should independently toggle graph representation, code-owned state, per-turn remediation, and mutation interception.

2. **How robust is workflow generation across authors and policy changes?** Only one GPT-5.4-authored workflow per domain is evaluated. A next experiment could generate multiple workflows using several model families and seeds, then measure policy coverage, unsupported constraints, and downstream Pass 4.

3. **Can broader intervention coverage be achieved efficiently?** User-turn firing leaves some between-trigger deviations uncovered, while the current system already adds substantial cost and latency. An experiment could compare adaptive event-triggered verification with per-action and user-turn schedules on compliance, calls, cost, and wall-clock time.

4. **Does the method generalize beyond synthetic English customer service?** Live users, other languages, policy regimes, and adaptive attackers are absent. A next study could evaluate reviewed workflows in multilingual or human-in-the-loop settings using an independently annotated temporal-compliance rubric.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated method, experimental settings, reported results, and acknowledged limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material missing or unresolved:

- No confirmed repository or artifact-release location; release is stated prospectively.
- No cross-workflow-author, multi-seed authoring evaluation.
- No complete factorial runtime ablation.
- No independent validation or inter-annotator agreement for the Telecom trace rubric.
- No live-user, multilingual, cross-domain CRAFT, adaptive-attack, or high-stakes deployment evidence.
- The supplied text says pooled tests favor PolicyGuide, but detailed paired per-task outputs are not included.
