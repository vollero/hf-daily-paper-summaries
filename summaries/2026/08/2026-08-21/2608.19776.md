# CoToGrasp: Contact-Topology-Conditioned Dexterous Grasp Synthesis via Canonical Workspace Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19776) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19776)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoToGrasp learns contact-topology templates entirely from gripper geometry and transfers them through a canonical workspace to unseen objects, improving topology compliance and simulated stability over Dexonomy while requiring no object meshes during training.

## Problem and motivation

Dexterous grasp planners commonly optimize physical stability but collapse toward enveloping power grasps, limiting their usefulness for tasks requiring specific precision or tool-oriented contacts. Existing taxonomy-conditioned planners address functional intent but typically couple grasp types to particular object meshes or rigid joint templates, creating expensive annotation requirements and weak generalization to new geometries (Introduction and Related Work, pp. 2–4).

The authors seek to generate stable grasps that obey a requested hand-contact topology while separating that semantic specification from object identity.

## Approach and contributions

- **Object-agnostic training:** CoToGrasp trains on 10,000 kinematically valid hand configurations paired with 21 contact templates—210,000 examples in total—without object meshes or physics simulation (Sec. 4.1, p. 10).
- **Canonical feature workspace:** Local point-cloud features from a static-graph DGCNN are projected by aligned-distance-weighted kNN onto 8,192 fixed, gripper-centric workspace points. The same representation accepts gripper geometry during training and transformed object geometry during inference (Secs. 3.2–3.3; Table 4).
- **Topology-conditioned generation:** A topology embedding is attached to every workspace token; Transformer and Set Transformer modules capture distributed contact structure, and a CVAE generates probabilistic contact-zone masks (Sec. 3.3; Fig. 2).
- **Validated grasp synthesis:** Predictions pass label-consistency and approximate force-closure checks before energy optimization enforces contact alignment, collision avoidance, joint limits, and separation of inactive hand regions from the object (Sec. 3.4; Supplement D).
- **Semantic evaluation:** The paper introduces topology compliance and normalized semantic/stability entropy metrics, using an asymmetric Tversky classifier to penalize unintended contacts (Secs. 4.1–4.2; Supplement F).

The evidence supports improved performance over the sole taxonomy-aware baseline selected by the authors, better balance across requested grasp types, and qualitative physical feasibility. It does not establish real-world success rates, robustness during downstream manipulation, universal zero-shot performance, or superiority over taxonomy-aware methods beyond Dexonomy. Taxonomy-aware averages also exclude object–topology pairs with 0% success across all attempts; reported coverage was similar but incomplete—80.14% for CoToGrasp and 81.05% for Dexonomy (Supplement H).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The 6D grasp pose is an external prior rather than predicted end-to-end; autonomous experiments rely on topology-specific sampling heuristics (Sec. 4.1; Supplement E).
- **Author-stated:** The approximate force-closure check ignores gravity and object mass and cannot guarantee final simulated stability (Supplement D and G).
- **Author-stated:** Strict contact classification can reject functionally acceptable grasps after small physics-induced shifts or incidental phalanx contacts (Supplement I; Fig. 10).
- **Author-stated:** Position-controlled real-world execution causes asynchronous contacts and topology drift; the authors propose force/position or tactile-reactive control as future directions (Supplement J).
- **Author-stated:** Tabletop evaluation can favor grasps viable only under constrained presentation or bimanual support (Supplement J).
- **Analyst inference:** Physical validation is qualitative: the text provides successful examples but no number of trials, failures, objects, or measured real-world success rate.
- **Analyst inference:** Manual transfer of anatomical zones to each gripper may limit scalability and introduce subjective design choices (Supplement A).
- **Analyst inference:** The sole taxonomy-aware comparison and author-defined asymmetric topology metric narrow the basis for the state-of-the-art claim, even though the metric is applied to both methods.
- **Analyst inference:** Excluding every object–topology pair with 0% success may hide performance on the hardest requests; similar retained coverage reduces, but does not eliminate, this validity concern.
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated-run variability are reported for the main numerical comparisons.

## Open research questions

1. **Can CoToGrasp infer globally suitable approach poses?** Pose selection remains external and heuristic. A next experiment could jointly learn pose proposals and contact templates, then compare SR and TC against the current heuristic under the same attempt budget.

2. **Can feedback control preserve topology during physical contact?** The hardware study reports asynchronous contact and contact drift. A controlled comparison of position control, hybrid force/position control, and tactile-reactive policies could measure real-world topology compliance and lift success.

3. **How well does the representation transfer across substantially different hands?** Shadow and Allegro examples are shown, but manual zone mapping is required and no quantitative cross-embodiment evaluation is reported. A leave-one-hand-out experiment across several gripper morphologies would test transfer and remapping cost.

4. **Can evaluation distinguish harmful extra contacts from benign incidental ones?** The asymmetric Tversky metric sometimes reclassifies physically stable grasps despite achieving intended contacts. Human task judgments or downstream manipulation success could be compared with the current classifier to calibrate a more function-sensitive metric.

## Confidence and missing information
