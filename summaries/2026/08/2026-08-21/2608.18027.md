# Chain-of-Experience for Continual LLM Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18027) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18027)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that retaining full prior attempts and feedback during inference can improve LLM accuracy across math, coding, and knowledge tasks, although the gains are contextual rather than persistent parameter learning and weaken when tasks require unavailable external knowledge.

## Problem and motivation

Conventional LLM inference treats each attempt as isolated, discarding feedback and intermediate experience. Existing search, voting, self-refinement, and memory methods either collapse multiple attempts into one answer, operate through shallow refinement loops, or compress experience across tasks. The authors ask whether an LLM can instead improve during test time by conditioning each new attempt on its complete history of attempts and feedback (§1–§3.1).

This matters because such improvement requires no parameter updates and could turn interactions with verifiers, execution environments, or the model itself into useful inference-time experience.

## Approach and contributions

- **Chain-of-Experience (CoE):** At iteration \(t\), the model generates an answer conditioned on the question and all earlier experience tuples \((a_i,f_i)\), retaining the full trajectory rather than a compressed memory (§3.1; Figures 1–2).
- **Unified feedback spectrum:** The study compares no feedback, execution feedback, model-generated feedback, and binary correctness feedback. Correctness is explicitly treated as an oracle-like upper-bound setting (§3.2).
- **Broad empirical comparison:** CoE is evaluated over six principal benchmarks spanning math, code, and knowledge, with three-run aggregation and comparisons against ICL, Dynamic CheatSheet, ACE, and configurable reasoning effort (§4.1; Figure 3; Table 3).
- **Behavioral analyses:** The paper studies feedback combinations, misleading feedback, feedback-provider strength, iteration count, trajectory compression, and categories of incorrect-to-correct transitions (§5; Appendices F and J).
- **Efficiency analysis:** Accuracy is compared against API cost and aggregate token consumption rather than accuracy alone (§4.2; Figures 4 and 12; Table 5).

The results establish that, in these evaluations, iterative access to feedback and complete trajectories often improves best-observed accuracy and can improve accuracy per token or API cost. They do not establish durable learning, since weights are unchanged, or universal benefit: Gemini 2.5 Pro is an efficiency exception, dual feedback does not win on OmniMath, and self feedback generally declines on BrowseComp-Plus. Because many headline results use the best score across 20 attempts, they also do not directly measure the accuracy of a predetermined stopping policy.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation centers on controlled math, knowledge, and coding tasks rather than interaction-heavy, long-horizon environments (Appendix L).
- **Author-stated:** CoE does not update model parameters; its “learning” is contextual reuse and does not persist beyond the active context (Appendix L).
- **Author-stated:** Binary correctness is oracle-like and may be expensive or unavailable in real applications (§3.2).
- **Author-stated:** Self feedback is ineffective when required knowledge lies outside the model, as observed on BrowseComp-Plus (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Reporting the best accuracy over 20 iterations creates an optimistic selection setting unless a practical method can identify the best round without hidden labels (Tables 1–3).
- **Analyst inference:** The paper reports three-run means and some standard-deviation plots, but the supplied text gives no significance tests or confidence intervals for headline comparisons.
- **Analyst inference:** The setup says “eight” models but explicitly names only seven in §4.1, leaving one evaluated model unidentified in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference:** API-cost comparisons may depend on model pricing and output verbosity; the supplied text does not provide a normalized pricing protocol or enough per-model data to reconstruct the claimed overall 19% reduction.
- **Analyst inference:** Improvement-category attribution is based on GPT-5 judgments. Validation reaches \(\kappa=0.768\) on 100 stratified trajectories, but agreement is weaker for “Random” (\(0.63\)) and “Self Reflection” (\(0.71\)); this limits causal interpretation (Table 4).

## Open research questions

1. **Can CoE improve real long-horizon agents?** The evaluation is mostly controlled QA and code generation. A next experiment could compare full-history CoE against compressed-memory agents on a long-horizon interactive benchmark with task completion, recovery, and cost metrics.

2. **Can the best iteration be selected without ground truth?** Main results often report the maximum over 20 rounds. A next experiment could preregister stopping rules based on answer stability, calibrated self-confidence, or verifier agreement and compare them with oracle best-round selection.

3. **How can experience become persistent?** No weights are updated, so gains disappear with the context. A next experiment could distill successful CoE trajectories into parameter updates and measure retention, transfer, and catastrophic forgetting on held-out tasks.

4. **When is self feedback trustworthy?** It helps on many in-distribution reasoning tasks but hurts on BrowseComp-Plus, while verifier strength also affects results. A next experiment could vary verifier accuracy and knowledge access systematically to estimate when self, external-model, execution, or retrieval feedback should be selected.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains extensive methods, tables, appendices, and numerical results, but several material details remain unavailable or ambiguous: the identity of the eighth model; full per-model values behind Figures 3–5 and 10–12; exact aggregation for the headline 5.6% improvement and 19% cost reduction; statistical significance; complete prompt templates; precise stopping and answer-selection procedures; per-model API pricing assumptions; and any code or artifact release information.
