# The Embedder&#x27;s Dilemma: LLMs Are Better, but at What Cost?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12875) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12875)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 37 text tasks, the best prompted LLM statistically ties the best embedding pipeline overall but costs over three orders of magnitude more, with its clearest advantage confined to reasoning-heavy retrieval.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether practitioners should replace specialized text-embedding pipelines with generative LLMs. Existing comparisons do not jointly measure quality, monetary cost, and same-hardware throughput across a broad set of embedding tasks. This matters because similar aggregate accuracy can conceal substantial differences in task suitability and deployment expense (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled cross-paradigm evaluation:** The authors compare 10 LLMs from six families with 26 embedding models on identical subsets of 37 MTEB tasks spanning classification, STS, clustering, pair classification, and retrieval (§3.1–3.2).
- **MTEB(LLM):** They introduce and release fixed, MTEB-compatible evaluation subsets, prompts, code, datasets, and raw results (§1; §3.2; Reproducibility Statement, p. 11).
- **Cost and throughput accounting:** API token usage determines LLM cost, while measured H100 throughput determines embedding cost; two open LLMs and embedding models are also compared on the same H100 (§3.3–3.4).
- **Deployment analyses:** Cost–quality Pareto frontiers, retrieve-then-rerank experiments, reduced-reasoning ablations, and a five-shot classification ablation identify where hybrid pipelines may be preferable (§4.3–4.7).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best systems are statistically tied overall | Gemini 3.1 Pro scores 77.6 and Octen-8B 77.2 across 37 equally weighted tasks. Paired task-level bootstrap: Δ = +0.3, 95% CI [−2.4, +3.1], p = 0.85. The rounded score difference is 0.4, while the bootstrap reports 0.3. | Table 1; §4.1–4.2; Table 2 |
| LLMs lead on retrieval | Pro scores 64.5 Recall@1 mean versus Octen-8B’s 56.0: Δ = +8.5, 95% CI [+0.2, +16.8], p < 0.05; Pro leads on five of six tasks. This result does not survive the appendix’s stated Bonferroni threshold of α = 0.01. | §4.2; Tables 2, 4, and 11 |
| Embeddings lead on classification | SFR-2 scores 90.8 accuracy mean versus Pro’s 85.2: Δ = −5.6, 95% CI [−9.2, −2.4], p < 0.01. Embeddings use kNN over the full labeled training split, whereas the LLM is zero-shot. | §3.2; §4.2; Tables 2 and 4 |
| Other categories show no significant difference | Pro versus the best embedding: clustering Δ = −0.2, p = 0.96; STS Δ = −0.3, p = 0.75; pair classification Δ = −3.9, p = 0.50. | Table 2 |
| Comparable LLM quality is much more expensive | One benchmark pass costs $154.14 for Pro and $0.108 for Octen-8B, a reported 1,431× ratio under H100 spot pricing. Across alternative assumptions, the ratio is 338–2,424×. | §4.4; Tables 3 and 19 |
| Embeddings have much higher same-GPU throughput | On one H100, two fitting open LLMs sustain roughly 5,400–5,900 tokens/s; embedding models span 14,700–4.3M tokens/s, yielding a reported 2.5–736× advantage. | §4.5; Figure 7; Table 18 |
| Default reasoning is often costly without improving retrieval | Reasoning accounts for 28–81% of inference cost. Reduced reasoning cuts generated tokens by 54–96% and preserves or improves mean retrieval for four of six models; for Gemini 3 Flash specifically, all six retrieval scores improve under low reasoning. | Figures 4a–b; §4.6; Tables 14 and 17 |
| Hybrid reranking helps reasoning-heavy retrieval | On BRIGHT, reranking Qwen3-E-8B with Qwen3.6-27B raises average nDCG@10 from 22.3 to 35.1. On BEIR, the embedding alone scores 63.1, above its best reranked result of 60.3. | §4.3; Figure 3; Table 16 |

The evidence establishes a task-dependent cost–quality trade-off for the evaluated models, subsets, prompts, metrics, and pricing assumptions. It does not establish that LLMs and embeddings have equal intrinsic representational ability: the evaluated deployment pipelines receive different supervision, retrieval corpora are unusually small, and aggregate results depend on equal task weighting. The retrieval p-value is nominally significant at 0.05 but not under the paper’s stated Bonferroni threshold.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The 10 LLMs are a time-specific sample; untested systems may have different cost–capability profiles (Appendix G.i).
- **Author-stated:** Corpus-in-context retrieval uses only 82–415 documents, benefits from full-corpus visibility and prompt caching, and was not tested across corpus sizes. Production retrieval generally requires an indexed first stage (Appendix G.iii).
- **Author-stated:** Classification supervision is asymmetric: embeddings use the full labeled training set through kNN, while LLMs receive zero or five examples (§5; Appendix G.ii).
- **Author-stated:** Pair-classification embeddings receive post-hoc test-set threshold optimization without an LLM analogue (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Reasoning controls differ across providers, and token accounting is provider-dependent (Appendix G.iv).
- **Author-stated:** Same-hardware throughput covers only two open LLMs that fit on one H100, limiting generalization to other architectures and serving configurations (Appendix G.v).
- **Author-stated:** Equal task weighting may change the aggregate ordering relative to dataset-size, family-balanced, normalized, or rank-based aggregation (Appendix G.vi).
- **Analyst inference:** The five-shot result does not justify the stronger suggestion that additional examples are unlikely to help: only four classification tasks and one five-shot construction were tested (Table 15).
- **Analyst inference:** Retrieval’s category-level advantage is uncertain after correction for five planned comparisons, so the task-specific conclusion is stronger descriptively than inferentially (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Cost comparisons mix contemporary API prices for LLMs with modeled GPU-rental costs for embeddings; the sensitivity analysis helps, but operational costs beyond inference are not measured (§3.3; Table 19).

## Open research questions

1. **How does corpus-in-context quality and cost scale with corpus size?** The paper tests only 82–415 documents and explicitly does not vary size. A next experiment could sweep corpus sizes and context occupancy while comparing full-context retrieval, bi-encoders, and fixed-shortlist rerankers.

2. **Would supervision-matched LLM classification close the 5.6-point gap?** The current comparison is full-training-set kNN versus zero/five-shot prompting. A next experiment could give both paradigms the same labeled examples or compare task-adapted versions under matched training budgets.

3. **Can reasoning budgets be selected per query?** Reduced reasoning helps most tested models, but provider controls differ and averages may hide query-level variation. A next experiment could learn a query router that selects no, low, or high reasoning and evaluate its quality–cost Pareto frontier.

4. **How stable are conclusions under alternative aggregation and deployment prices?** Equal task weighting and dated API/GPU prices materially define the frontier. A next experiment could report family-balanced, dataset-weighted, and rank-based scores while periodically refreshing prices and hardware throughput.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experiments because the supplied text includes the full methods, principal tables, appendices, and reproducibility description.

Material missing or unresolved: results for untested frontier LLMs; behavior on large production corpora; matched-supervision classification; same-hardware throughput for closed and multi-GPU LLMs; environmental measurements; systematic frequencies for the qualitative error patterns; and empirical results under alternative task-weighting schemes.
