# SkillEvo: Self-Renewing Evolution Gradients from Multi-Turn Interaction Feedback

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13120) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13120)


## One-sentence takeaway

SkillEvo turns failures from simulated multi-turn support dialogues into evidence-bounded updates to textual agent Skills, while an independent governance layer limits factual regression and structural bloat, reaching 81.8% task success on a held-out Tencent Cloud support set.

## Problem and motivation

Agent Skills are typically hand-maintained or updated from self-reflection and single-turn evaluation. The authors argue that single-turn feedback exposes mainly opening-query gaps, so its useful revision signal decays after early rounds; it also misses defects revealed through clarification and follow-up questions. Existing scalar acceptance gates can detect a worse candidate but cannot localize or repair knowledge loss, broken references, or document bloat (Sections 1–2).

This matters for customer support because failed interactions contain potentially reusable product knowledge, yet manual consolidation is costly and slow. The target gap is therefore a closed-loop process that converts multi-turn failures into trustworthy, repairable feedback while preserving the integrity of a multi-file Skill knowledge base.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Trustworthy multi-turn feedback.** SkillEvo reconstructs constrained users from real, human-handled tickets and runs multi-turn conversations against a Skill-equipped service agent. An intent state machine checks whether all key intents were raised; separate simulator- and agent-side evaluation prevents omitted intents from being blamed on the Skill (Sections 3.1–3.2).

2. **Repairability-aware attribution.** Failures are classified as `Knowledge Gap`, `Capability Limit`, or `Evaluation Noise`. Only verified knowledge gaps are merged across semantically similar cases and passed to the editor, restricting updates to evidence-supported, reusable facts (Sections 3.2.2–3.2.3).

3. **Bounded editing and active governance.** Revisions are anchored to both the original production Skill and the preceding round. A hard fact-consistency check rejects knowledge loss, newly introduced errors, and contradictions; softer graph-structural governance recommends repairs for bloat, broken references, and over-generalized facts (Sections 3.3.1–3.3.2).

4. **Production-oriented evaluation.** The framework is evaluated over four rounds on 2,000 human-escalated tickets covering 9 production Skills, 98 reference files, and six Tencent Cloud service categories, with chronological development/evaluation splits and human confirmation before rollout (Section 4.1.1; Appendix A, Table 7; Appendix G).

The controlled feedback-source ablation supports the authors’ claim that multi-turn feedback accounts for the final 15.4-point advantage over the selected single-turn baseline under their pipeline. The results also show that governance limits line-count growth, although its causal effect on regression is less firmly isolated because Table 5 reports no without-governance RegR comparator. The evidence does not establish generalization beyond private Tencent Cloud support failures, superiority to every cited skill-evolution method, or safe fully automatic deployment.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The production tickets cannot be released because of privacy and commercial-confidentiality constraints, limiting external reproduction (Reproducibility Statement).
- **Author-stated:** The method requires multi-turn consultation logs with human reference solutions and separate generator/evaluator model families (Reproducibility Statement).
- **Author-stated:** Hyperparameters were inherited from the deployed pipeline without a sensitivity sweep (Appendix F, Table 8).
- **Author-stated:** Automatic knowledge write-back can propagate erroneous revisions; candidates therefore require human confirmation before production deployment (Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference:** The dataset is a failure set of escalated cloud-support tickets rather than a representative sample of all support traffic, so reported TSR and gains may not transfer to routine or already-solved cases (Section 4.1.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation is confined to one organization, one domain, and two named model families; cross-domain and cross-model robustness are untested (Appendix A; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** The ablation isolates multi-turn versus single-turn feedback, but the paper compares only one representative single-turn implementation and does not experimentally compare the other cited paradigms (Section 4.1.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Verifier validation is incomplete: the random subset size, annotator protocol, chance-corrected agreement, and uncertainty intervals are absent (Section 4.1.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Version selection uses development-set TSR and average score, making monotonic evaluation curves easier to obtain than reporting each raw round candidate; no confidence intervals or significance tests are supplied (Section 4.2; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** The governance claim for RegR lacks a direct without-governance comparison, while bloat is measured only through line-count growth and may not capture all structural quality changes (Section 4.4.2; Tables 5–6).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the approach transfer beyond cloud support?** The evidence covers only Tencent Cloud technical-support Skills. A next experiment could apply the same pipeline to a separate multi-turn domain with human reference resolutions and report TSR, regression, and attribution accuracy without changing the method.

2. **How much does each trustworthiness component contribute?** The paper jointly uses the intent state machine, dual-sided evaluation, and collective attribution but does not report component-wise ablations. A factorial ablation could remove each component separately and measure false feedback admission, TSR, and regression.

3. **How robust are results to evaluator and threshold choices?** Only two model families and one deployed parameter configuration are tested, with no sensitivity sweep. A next experiment could rotate generator/evaluator families and vary pass, intent-weight, and early-stopping thresholds.

4. **Can governance directly prevent regressions rather than merely correlate with declining RegR?** Table 5 lacks a no-governance comparator. The next experiment should report per-transition RegR for full SkillEvo and otherwise identical no-governance variants, alongside factual-error and reference-breakage counts.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains the complete reported method, appendices, metrics, baselines, and aggregate results, but material gaps remain: the private ticket data and production prompts are unavailable; exact development/evaluation counts per Skill are not shown after splitting; the verifier-validation subset size and agreement calculation are unspecified; uncertainty estimates and statistical tests are absent; raw no-governance RegR values are missing; and no implementation or public artifact release is established.
