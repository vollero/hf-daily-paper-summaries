# GOAG: Generative and Object-Agnostic Grasp Planner for Dexterous Robotic Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-21.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19759) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19759)


## One-sentence takeaway

GOAG learns gripper-specific contact priors without object geometry during training, then transfers them to novel object point clouds at inference, achieving a reported 86.93% average MultiDex grasp-success rate across three dexterous hands.

## Problem and motivation

Data-driven multifinger grasp planners commonly train on costly databases pairing particular objects with validated grasps, creating object-geometry bias and weakening generalization to unseen shapes. Analytical planners can avoid this dependence but may be too slow for real-time use with high-DoF hands. The authors address this gap by making training gripper-centric: the model learns feasible contact patterns from hand geometry, kinematics, and a grasp taxonomy before seeing any object (§I; §II-A–C).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Object-agnostic contact learning.** GOAG procedurally samples valid hand configurations and taxonomy-constrained contact regions using only a gripper’s geometry and kinematics. A CVAE learns their distribution in a fixed gripper-workspace BPS representation (§III-A–B; Figs. 2–3).

2. **Cross-geometry inference.** At inference, an object point cloud with surface normals is transformed into the gripper frame and encoded with the same BPS. Latent sampling produces candidate contact fields, while a PointNet++ mapper assigns contacts to gripper links (§III-B–C).

3. **Physically informed grasp generation.** Candidates undergo a simplified force-closure test with resampling, followed by joint optimization penalizing contact distance, object penetration, self-collision, and joint-limit violations (§III-C, Eqs. 8–9).

4. **Multi-hand and cross-dataset evaluation.** The authors test Barrett, Allegro, and Shadow hands on MultiDex, evaluate a Shadow-hand model across five benchmarks, and demonstrate Allegro-hand grasps on 11 physical YCB objects (§IV-D–F).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope limitation:** Simple convex-hull pose sampling works best when an object’s volume fits within the gripper workspace and is less effective for larger objects (§IV-E).

- **Author-stated approximation:** Force closure uses one barycentric contact per phalanx, assumes μ=0.3, and ignores gravity and object weight. Passing this check does not guarantee kinematic reachability or final physical stability (§III-C; §IV-C).

- **Author-stated domain shift:** The shared BPS encoder processes gripper geometry during training but object geometry during testing, creating an inherent cross-domain transfer problem (§III-B).

- **Author-stated diversity dependency:** Grasp-pose diversity largely depends on external object-pose sampling, although latent sampling can vary finger configurations at a fixed pose (§IV-E).

- **Analyst inference:** Real-world evidence is narrow: 11 successful YCB-object demonstrations are reported without total attempts, failures, uncertainty, or comparisons (§IV-F).

- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text reports strong baseline comparisons but omits Tables II–III’s values, preventing verification of margins, efficiency claims, diversity results, or statistical variability.

- **Analyst inference:** No explicit ablation results are supplied for the taxonomy prior, BPS design, links mapper, force-closure filter, latent dimension, or optimization terms. The prose mentions performance without force-closure estimation, but provides no recoverable number (§IV-E).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is incomplete because training uses an unspecified number of A100 GPUs, and several thresholds or gripper-specific preprocessing choices are not fully enumerated (§IV-A).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does each pipeline component contribute?** No numerical ablation is available. A next experiment could remove the taxonomy prior, link mapper, force-closure filter, and individual optimization penalties one at a time while measuring success, runtime, and collision rates.

2. **Can the gripper-to-object representation shift be reduced?** The authors explicitly identify a train–test geometry shift. A controlled experiment could compare the current shared BPS representation with domain-aligned or jointly regularized encoders on held-out object families.

3. **How robust is GOAG under realistic physical variation?** Force closure ignores gravity and fixes friction at μ=0.3, while real trials lack aggregate statistics. Repeated physical trials could vary mass, friction, pose-estimation noise, and partial point-cloud visibility and report confidence intervals.

4. **Can pose sampling handle large or cluttered objects?** The current convex-hull strategy is acknowledged to be weak for large objects. A plausible experiment would compare learned, task-conditioned, and collision-aware pose proposals under identical grasp-generation budgets.
