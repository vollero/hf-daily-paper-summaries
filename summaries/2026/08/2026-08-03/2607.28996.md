# SULAND v2: A Refined RGB Dataset and Deep Learning Object Detection Benchmark for UAV/UGV-Based SUrface LANDmine Detection Under Domain Shift

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28996) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28996)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors refine SULAND’s RGB surface-mine annotations and show that benchmark corrections substantially change measured accuracy, while all 35 evaluated detector configurations still degrade under an Italy-to-USA domain shift.

## Problem and motivation

RGB cameras offer inexpensive, lightweight survey support for visible surface mines, but safety-critical deployment requires detectors that generalize beyond familiar acquisition conditions. The authors identify two linked gaps: scarce public RGB datasets with explicit IID/OOD splits, and defects in SULAND_v1—including missing or false labels, inaccurate boxes, inconsistent visibility criteria, artifacts, temporal inconsistencies, and reversed OOD class IDs—that confound detector training and evaluation. Existing work also provides limited cross-family comparison under a common domain-shift protocol (Sections I and II-D).

## Approach and contributions

- **Dataset audit and refinement:** The authors inspect every released image and annotation frame-by-frame, establish seven error categories, and manually re-annotate the complete dataset. Three reviewers verify changed boxes by majority vote (Sections III-C and IV-A).
- **SULAND_v2 asset:** Original images, filenames, and IID/OOD splits are retained, while missing labels are added, invalid labels removed, boxes tightened, and the PFM-1/PMA-2 class convention harmonized (Section IV-A).
- **Version-effect analysis:** Same-version and cross-version YOLOv8 experiments separate effects from training labels, evaluation ground truth, and the inverted OOD class mapping (Sections IV-C–IV-D).
- **Detector benchmark:** Thirty-five configurations from nine one-stage, two-stage, transformer, and vision–language/open-vocabulary detector families are fine-tuned under a common protocol and evaluated for IID/OOD accuracy, precision, recall, size, and speed (Sections V-A–V-C).
- **Robustness analysis:** The study compares ranking changes, class-specific behavior, generalization gaps, and accuracy–speed tradeoffs rather than treating IID performance as deployment evidence (Sections VI-B–VI-D).

The evidence establishes that annotation conventions can substantially alter both learned detectors and reported scores, and that this benchmark’s OOD split changes architecture rankings. It does not establish operational safety, robustness to arbitrary real minefields, or a causal architectural explanation for RF-DETR-Large’s stronger retention. The supplied text also omits the numerical bodies of several referenced tables, preventing reconstruction of every configuration’s result.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The dataset covers only two surface-laid surrogate or inert target classes and one principal Italy-to-USA transfer; it excludes much of the variation in mine types, burial, weathering, seasons, dense layouts, camera systems, altitudes, and viewing geometry (Section VII-D).
- **Author-stated:** RGB detection depends on visible surface evidence and cannot address fully buried, heavily occluded, or visually indistinguishable hazards; it is not an autonomous basis for declaring land safe (Sections VII-C–VII-D).
- **Author-stated:** Manual annotation remains subjective for small, blurred, occluded, or low-contrast targets, and residual errors may remain despite three-reviewer quality control. Inter-annotator agreement is not quantified (Section VII-D).
- **Author-stated:** Geographic, terrain, vegetation, illumination, viewpoint, and target-presentation changes are entangled. The t-SNE plot is qualitative and cannot attribute the generalization gap to individual factors (Section VII-D; Figure 2).
- **Author-stated:** A common training protocol may not be optimal for every family; speed is specific to the hardware, implementation, resolution, and measurement procedure (Section VII-D).
- **Author-stated:** Only one random seed is reported, and detector-specific tuning was outside scope (Section V-B; Supplement S3).
- **Analyst inference:** Architecture-level causal claims are unsupported because no controlled component ablations isolate representation, proposal, multiscale, or query mechanisms; the authors explicitly acknowledge this in Section VII-B.
- **Analyst inference:** Operational risk cannot be inferred from F1-selected thresholds alone because false alarms per surveyed area, confidence calibration, and recall-oriented operating points were not evaluated (Sections V-C and VII-C).
- **Analyst inference:** The dataset/code availability section is empty in the retrieved main text. A dataset URL and release claim appear in the supplement, but code contents, licenses, exact evaluator implementation, and artifact completeness are not documented here.

## Open research questions

1. **Which shift factors drive the OOD loss?** Geography, terrain, illumination, viewpoint, and target appearance change together, so their effects remain unidentified. A next experiment could construct factorized test sets that vary one condition at a time.

2. **Do detector rankings persist across independent domains?** The benchmark uses one main Italy-to-USA transfer and two target classes. Cross-dataset testing across additional regions, seasons, cameras, altitudes, and mine/UXO types could measure ranking stability.

3. **Can adaptation or uncertainty methods improve recall safely?** OOD recall drops materially, but domain adaptation, test-time adaptation, calibration, and uncertainty-aware detection are proposed rather than tested. A next experiment could compare these methods using recall-oriented thresholds and false alarms per surveyed area.

4. **How much performance is attributable to specific architectural choices?** Family rankings differ, but model scale alone does not explain robustness and no component ablations were run. Matched-capacity ablations of backbones, proposal mechanisms, multiscale features, and query-based heads could test causal hypotheses.
