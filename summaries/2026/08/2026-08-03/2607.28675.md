# Meshy T2: Fast Native Mesh Generation with Flow Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28675) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28675)


## One-sentence takeaway
Meshy T2 directly generates compact, topology-aware meshes from one image in a median 6 seconds by combining a nearly lossless per-vertex mesh VAE with a two-stage voxel-to-mesh flow-matching cascade.

## Problem and motivation
Production 3D pipelines need explicit meshes that are compact, geometrically faithful, valid, and easy to edit. Implicit or volumetric generators require surface extraction and often yield excessively dense, irregular meshes, while autoregressive native-mesh methods serialize an unordered structure into long sequences, causing slow inference and error accumulation (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

The authors identify two remaining gaps in parallel diffusion/flow approaches: existing latent representations can lose geometry or topology through quantization and heuristic face reconstruction, and most methods address retopology from dense point clouds rather than direct image-to-mesh generation (Introduction, pp. 3–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Nearly lossless vertex-set mesh VAE:** Assigns one continuous latent token to each vertex and jointly reconstructs continuous coordinates, undirected edges, and oriented faces in one decoding pass. Distinct coincident vertices are retained, and a NULL-augmented halfedge permutation represents open boundaries (Sec. 2.1, pp. 4–7).

- **Coarse-to-fine flow cascade:** An image-conditioned flow generates a \(64^3\) occupancy scaffold through a voxel-VAE latent space; a second flow generates per-vertex mesh latents conditioned jointly on the image, scaffold, and requested vertex budget (Secs. 2.2–2.3, pp. 7–10).

- **Optimal-transport positional assignment:** Ground-truth vertices are matched to deterministic Sobol points during training, giving unordered mesh tokens stable 3D positional encodings that remain available without ground-truth geometry at inference (Sec. 2.3, p. 10).

- **Controllable complexity and multipart output:** Requested latent slots provide range-based vertex-count control, while explicitly predicted connectivity allows connected components to emerge without separate part generation or stitching (Secs. 2.1 and 2.3, pp. 7, 10).

The evidence supports strong relative geometry, runtime, and robustness on the paper’s curated benchmark, as well as a clear benefit from Sobol OT in the reported VAE ablation. It does not establish universal state of the art across other datasets, hardware environments, camera distributions, mesh complexities, or production workflows. Image-to-mesh superiority is metric-dependent: Meshy T2 leads DINOv2 FD but not Inception FD.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitations:** Exact vertex-count compliance is not learned because the training data cover limited vertex counts; the method instead promises an output range of \([N/(1+p),N]\) for \(N\) requested slots (Sec. 2.3, p. 10).

- **Author-stated limitations:** The face representation assumes manifold meshes. Non-manifold training meshes are repaired beforehand by splitting offending edges and vertices, so fidelity to unrepaired irregular topology is not demonstrated (Sec. 2.1, p. 6).

- **Author-stated future needs:** The authors identify robustness on highly irregular source meshes, richer material and part controls, and scene-level generation under shared budgets as future work (Conclusion, p. 14).

- **Analyst inference:** The 115-asset benchmark is curated, and its 100k-triangle references are generated by Meshy 6 rather than being independently captured ground truth. Generalization beyond this construction is therefore uncertain (Sec. 3, p. 11).

- **Analyst inference:** Retopology comparisons do not provide perfectly uniform inputs or output densities: Meshy T2 and MeshFlow output roughly 4,000 faces, while some autoregressive outputs are denser. The paper itself warns that absolute topology counts are then not directly comparable (Table 2 and Sec. 3.2, pp. 12–13).

- **Analyst inference:** Only the positional-encoding choice receives a reported ablation. The supplied text does not isolate the contribution of the voxel scaffold, image/voxel/count conditioning, NULL boundary representation, or two-stage design.

- **Analyst inference:** Hardware used for latency measurements, training dataset scale/composition, training compute, flow solver, inference step count, and guidance settings are absent from the supplied text, limiting reproducibility and runtime portability.

## Open research questions

1. **How much does the voxel scaffold contribute relative to a single-stage mesh flow?** The paper says a direct single flow performed poorly but provides no quantitative comparison (Sec. 2.2). A controlled ablation could train matched-capacity one- and two-stage systems and compare geometry, topology, latency, and convergence.

2. **Can face budgets be controlled more precisely across diverse shapes?** Current control only targets a vertex-count range because training counts are limited (Sec. 2.3). A next experiment could balance training examples across count levels and measure requested-versus-realized vertices and faces per object category.

3. **How robust is decoding on genuinely non-manifold or highly irregular assets?** Training meshes are repaired before encoding, and the conclusion names irregular topology as future work (Sec. 2.1; Conclusion). Evaluation on an unrepaired topology stress test could report validity, repair frequency, and geometric distortion.

4. **Does the reported advantage transfer to broader image and asset distributions?** Evaluation uses 115 curated assets and fixed front-facing renders (Sec. 3; Table 3). A larger cross-source benchmark with varied viewpoints, occlusion, backgrounds, and real artist meshes would test generalization.
