# Scaling Properties of Text Conditioning in Visual Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.29679) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29679)


## One-sentence takeaway

Under a fixed diffusion-training recipe, the authors find that measured image-grounded caption information—not prompt length—predicts converged loss, and use structured prompts plus a trained LLM prompter to improve text-to-image generation.

## Problem and motivation

Text-to-image scaling has emphasized model size, data, and compute, while treating captions mainly as text sequences. The authors argue that captions can omit or ambiguously bind visual content, limiting the conditioning signal available from image–caption pairs. Their gap is the lack of a measured relationship between image-grounded caption information and diffusion learning, especially for dense prompts involving objects, layouts, relations, and visual coherence (Introduction, pp. 3–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Information-based scaling analysis:** The authors adapt Grounded Perplexity Gain (GPG), a white-box VLM likelihood measure, and Effective Detailness (ED), a black-box attribute precision/recall measure. They relate both to matched-budget converged diffusion MSE across 15 controlled caption configurations (§3.2; Fig. 7; Table 7).

- **Structured prompts for diffusability:** They introduce a typed JSON representation covering global scene context, per-element attributes and geometry, and cross-element relations. A VLM plus frozen pose, depth, and segmentation experts produces these annotations (§§3.1, 3.3; Figs. 5 and 8).

- **Trained prompter for promptability:** A Qwen3.5-based LLM prompter maps user requests to structured prompts using SFT, cold-start distillation, and verifier-gated on-policy self-distillation; a refine–render–judge loop optionally revises failed fields (§3.4; Figs. 10 and 14).

- **End-to-end matched evaluation:** The structured system is compared with existing generators and with a natural-language system matched on Qwen-Image architecture, images, training stages, and budgets (§4.1; Table 2; Appendix C.2).

The evidence supports a strong empirical association between the proposed information measures and matched-budget loss within the tested BAGEL recipe, plus downstream advantages for the complete structured Qwen-Image system. It does not establish a universal scaling law, causally isolate JSON syntax from richer organized content, provide seed-level uncertainty, or refit the quantitative loss relationship on Qwen-Image (§3.2; Appendix A.6).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** GPG requires an image-conditioned VLM at scoring time, while ED requires offline image-side tuple extraction; both can shift with the chosen judge or extractor (§5).

- **Author-stated:** The fitted relationship is recipe- and backbone-specific. It is calibrated on BAGEL, not quantitatively refitted for the Qwen-Image end-to-end system (§3.2; Appendix A.6).

- **Author-stated:** Each caption configuration is trained once. Reported resampling bands are sensitivity analyses over selected settings, not run-to-run confidence intervals or seed-level uncertainty (§3.2; Appendix A.5).

- **Author-stated:** Structured prompts jointly change information content and organization, so the experiments do not isolate JSON syntax itself (§3.2, “From the properties to two factors”).

- **Author-stated:** The schema is hand-designed, presently image-specific, and adds prompter latency; automatic schema discovery and video/3D extensions remain open (§5).

- **Analyst inference:** Much evaluation depends on model judges, including GPT-5.4 scores and GSB preferences. Separate online and offline judges reduce direct reuse in the refinement experiment, but the supplied text does not provide human-evaluation validation for the headline system comparisons.

- **Analyst inference:** The source contains a duplicated, inconsistent passage around p. 13 that mentions different GPG pool sizes and ED caption-side backends. The main §3.2 and appendices consistently report a 30,000-image pool and GPT-5.4, but the extraction inconsistency weakens certainty about that duplicated text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the information–loss relationship transfer across backbones and scales?** It is fitted only on BAGEL. A next experiment would repeat the same 15-condition, multi-seed sweep on Qwen-Image and at least one substantially different diffusion architecture.

2. **How much gain comes from structure rather than additional factual content?** The authors explicitly state that these factors are jointly varied. A next experiment could compare JSON, canonical prose, and alternative typed serializations with exactly matched facts and tokenized field values.

3. **Can the schema be learned automatically?** The current schema is hand-designed. A next experiment could learn candidate field decompositions from image–caption data and compare their GPG/ED, matched-budget loss, editability, and downstream generation.

4. **How well do model-judge gains track human preference?** Several key evaluations use VLM judges. A preregistered blinded human comparison on the same 150-prompt sets could measure agreement with structure, alignment, and GSB outcomes.
