# ExtractBench: A Benchmark for Schema-Guided Enterprise Document Extraction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.29677) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29677)


## One-sentence takeaway

ExtractBench jointly evaluates schema-guided extraction accuracy, record completeness, source grounding, challenge-specific robustness, and cost across 370 enterprise documents, finding that long-list recall and exact evidence localization remain major weaknesses.

## Problem and motivation

Enterprise extraction systems must turn varied born-digital and scanned documents into schema-valid JSON while returning every requested value, representing absent fields correctly, and linking values to source evidence. Existing benchmarks reportedly cover only fragments of this problem—such as fixed ontologies, complex schemas, or long arrays—and do not jointly score completeness, scans and handwriting, grounding, and measured cost (Introduction; Table 1).

This matters because failures such as truncated schedules, confused identifiers, and overfilled blank form fields can disrupt downstream workflows, while grounding supports human correction and per-page cost determines production viability (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Challenge-tagged benchmark:** 370 documents and 4,869 pages spanning 67 document types and eight business domains, tagged along task, perception, table-structure, length, and domain axes (Sections 2.2 and A.1; Figures 2 and 5; Table 4).

- **Schema-guided task formulation:** Each document type shares one user-authored JSON Schema; systems must return schema-valid JSON plus page and bounding-box evidence for extracted values (Section 2.1).

- **Scalable curation pipeline:** Real-document labels use agreement among independent extraction systems plus adjudication; synthetic long lists use known pre-render values and derived boxes; scanned forms receive field-level human verification (Section 2.3; Figure 3).

- **Deterministic evaluation:** Order-insensitive unified value F1 aligns array records with the Hungarian algorithm; grounding is scored at page and word-box levels, with word boxes requiring IoU ≥ 0.5 and a correct value (Section 2.4; Appendix B).

- **Broad system comparison:** Fourteen systems from commercial and open VLMs, coding agents, and specialized extraction APIs are compared without benchmark-specific tuning on accuracy, grounding, challenge slices, and measured cost (Section 3.1).

They do not establish superiority on all enterprise data, isolate which architectural component causes each difference, or demonstrate that the authors’ explanation—context limitations causing truncation—is causal rather than correlational (Section 3.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied text does not present a dedicated author-stated limitations section. Relevant constraints and analyst inferences are:

- **Ground-truth coverage:** Real-document values are primarily established through cross-system agreement with human review focused on contested cells; exhaustive manual checking is reserved for scanned forms. Real documents also lack word boxes (Section 2.3; Figure 3). **Analyst inference:** shared model errors could survive agreement and make some labels less secure than fully human-audited labels.

- **Synthetic validity:** Very long lists are rendered from known records using layouts modeled after real filings (Sections 2.3 and A.3.2). **Analyst inference:** these stress completeness reliably, but may not reproduce the full irregularity of naturally occurring long documents.

- **Limited long-document sample:** Only 20 of 370 documents exceed 50 pages, although they account for 1,816 pages (Table 4). Slice-level generalization across document families is therefore uncertain.

- **Grounding eligibility:** Documents without verified boxes emit no grounding score, and only 237 documents carry the value-box grounding tag (Section B.4; Table 5). Grounding rankings therefore apply to a subset.

- **Configuration sensitivity:** Systems use different native interfaces and constraints, and coding agents depend on rendering, tools, runtime, and a 1,200-second timeout (Sections 4.2 and C.1). Results may change under other configurations.

- **Cost validity:** Costs use July 1, 2026 public rates and exclude volume or enterprise discounts; self-hosted systems are omitted from cost comparisons (Section C.2).

- **Potential conflict of interest:** The authors are affiliated with the organization whose LlamaExtract systems rank highest, and those systems are labeled “ours” (page 1; Figure 4). **Analyst inference:** independent replication would strengthen confidence.

## Open research questions

1. **What specifically causes long-document truncation?** Recall declines strongly with length, but the paper’s context-limit explanation is an author attribution rather than a controlled causal result. A next experiment could vary document length and record count independently while holding layout and schema fixed.

2. **Can exact grounding improve without reducing value accuracy?** Word-level grounding remains below 50% even when page grounding is much higher (Table 3). A next experiment could compare joint extraction-and-grounding against a two-stage value extraction plus evidence-localization pipeline.

3. **How reliable is ensemble-derived ground truth?** Agreement reduces dependence on one extractor, but most real-document cells are not exhaustively human-verified. A blinded human audit of random agreed cells could estimate residual label error and family-correlated mistakes.

4. **How well do rankings transfer beyond the benchmark’s domain mix?** Finance and energy constitute most documents, while legal and real estate have only 10 and 6 documents respectively (Table 4). A next experiment could add balanced, independently sourced test families and report confidence intervals by domain.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported results because the supplied text includes detailed corpus, metric, configuration, and result tables.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty or significance tests, independent replication, random-audit error rates for agreed ground truth, annotator agreement, complete per-system failure logs, and evaluation on private production distributions. The supplied text also does not report training-data contamination checks or a controlled causal ablation of document length, schema size, tool access, and record count.
