# DataSpace: Benchmarking Data Agents for Verifiable Analytics over Heterogeneous Workspaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03451) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03451)


## One-sentence takeaway

DataSpace evaluates whether tool-using agents can discover and combine cross-language evidence from heterogeneous workspaces into exactly verifiable tables, with the best tested configuration reaching 66.34% accuracy and showing persistent weaknesses on multimodal and join-heavy tasks.

## Problem and motivation

Real organizational analytics often requires finding relevant evidence among databases, files, long documents, videos, and distractors, then reconciling schemas, languages, entities, and units before producing a complete result. The authors argue that prior benchmarks separately test structured querying, retrieval, or open-ended analysis but do not jointly provide heterogeneous source discovery, a complete-table output contract, and deterministic evaluation tolerant of equivalent representations (Section 1, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **DataSpace benchmark:** 410 financial, macroeconomic, and healthcare tasks containing 7,439 artifacts across CSV, JSON, SQLite, Markdown, PDF, and video. Questions and workspaces can mix Chinese and English (Sections 1 and 3.2; Table 2).
- **DataSpace-Builder:** A four-stage, execution-grounded pipeline transforms EHRSQL and BULL Text-to-SQL instances through joint cross-language migration, constraint-aware database sampling, modality routing/rendering, and review by 11 domain experts (Sections 4.1–4.4; Figure 2).
- **Deterministic evaluation:** Complete CSV outputs undergo header-independent column alignment, type- and precision-aware normalization, and ordered-sequence or unordered-multiset row comparison (Section 4.5; Equations 8–9).
- **Controlled empirical study:** Six multimodal backbones are compared under one agent, while five harnesses are compared with MiMo-V2.5 fixed, separating backbone and harness effects (Sections 6.1–6.2; Table 3).
- **Released evaluation assets:** All task inputs, evaluator, baselines, configurations, and documentation are public; gold answers and configurations are public for 60 tasks, while 350 are withheld for official evaluation (Appendix B).

The results establish sizable performance differences under the authors’ controlled backbone and harness settings and identify descriptive associations between task characteristics and accuracy. They do not establish causal effects of multimodality, joins, language, or workspace size because these task groups overlap and were not experimentally randomized. They also do not show generalization beyond DataSpace’s construction domains, languages, output contract, or runtime.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** Execution equivalence verifies database–SQL consistency but cannot prove that a translated question preserves the original intent; an LLM judge is therefore used before expert review (Section 4.1).
- **Author-stated limitation:** Characteristic-level accuracy gaps are descriptive rather than causal (Section 6.4).
- **Author-stated comparison boundary:** Several specialized systems are excluded because their implementations, video support, or output interfaces do not satisfy the protocol, limiting comparison with purpose-built data agents (Section 6.1; Appendix A.7.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Because 363/410 tasks originate from BULL and only 47 from EHRSQL, conclusions may be dominated by finance-oriented source structure rather than organizational analytics broadly (Section 5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Tasks are transformed from two Text-to-SQL corpora, so their analytical logic inherits an executable relational-query foundation even when evidence is rendered as documents or video (Sections 4.1–4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Document and video generation uses proprietary models, and construction costs exclude expert review and infrastructure; reproducing the complete benchmark-building process may therefore require unavailable services and unreported total resources (Table 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Only 60 reference answers are publicly released, preventing independent full-benchmark scoring without the official evaluation service (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** The failure audit covers only 136 failures from Grok 4.5 and begins with model-proposed labels subsequently checked by one human researcher; inter-annotator agreement is not reported (Appendix A.8.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can agents improve cross-modality integration without sacrificing single-modality performance?** Every tested backbone declines on multimodal tasks, but the analysis is non-causal. A next experiment could construct matched task pairs that preserve analytical logic while varying only whether evidence is split across modalities.

2. **Which harness mechanisms explain the 15.36-point spread?** Native planning, tool use, and context management vary together in the harness comparison. Component-level ablations could standardize one mechanism at a time, such as submission validation, memory, source routing, or recovery after tool errors.

3. **Can output-contract checks prevent materialization failures?** More than half of audited Grok failures involve output interpretation or projection. A plausible experiment would add a schema-and-shape verification stage before submission and measure accuracy gains and new failure modes.

4. **How well does performance transfer beyond the benchmark’s source domains and languages?** DataSpace is limited to Chinese/English and is predominantly derived from a financial Text-to-SQL corpus. A follow-up could apply the same construction and evaluation protocol to independently authored tasks in additional domains and languages.
