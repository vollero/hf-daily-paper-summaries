# PaDoc: Layout-Grounded Parallel Decoding for Document Parsing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06146) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06146)


## One-sentence takeaway

PaDoc branches region-content generation from shared full-page image and layout prefixes, substantially accelerating end-to-end document parsing while keeping quality close to a same-backbone sequential model.

## Problem and motivation

End-to-end parsers serialize every layout and content region into one autoregressive sequence, making decoding depth grow with total page content even when regions are independent. Crop-based two-stage systems expose regional parallelism but repeat visual encoding and can lose page-level context. The authors seek region-parallel decoding that retains a single full-page representation and requires neither separate detector/recognizer heads nor speculative branches unrelated to document layout (Introduction; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Prefix-conditioned factorization:** Under a region-specific content-sufficiency assumption, each region’s content is conditioned on the full page and its layout prefix, but not sibling contents or future regions. This lets the next layout region and current content branch decode concurrently (Method, Eqs. 5 and 8–14).
- **Single-model training:** Ancestor attention gives each branch access only to its page/layout ancestors and private history. A packed tree-variable-length implementation uses causal FlashAttention while retaining ordinary next-token training and avoiding a dense quadratic mask (Training Paradigm; Appendix C).
- **Parallel serving:** At inference, layout and content streams run as concurrent vLLM requests with branch isolation and cache-resident reuse of identical image/layout prefixes (Inference Speedup; Appendix E).
- **Efficiency evidence:** The authors provide controlled same-backbone quality, forward-step, latency, throughput, and training-backend comparisons in addition to cross-system results (Tables 3 and 8–13).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong end-to-end layout accuracy | On all 1,651 OmniDocBench pages, PaDoc obtained Overall IoU/F1 of **81.4/91.1%** and Overall precision of **93.3%** under countable-coverage, acceptable-category PageIoU. | Table 1; Experimental Setup |
| Competitive parsing quality | On OmniDocBench v1.6 Full, PaDoc scored **94.24 Overall**, **0.038 Text Edit**, **95.59 Formula CDM**, and **90.94/93.39 Table TEDS/TEDS-S**. Among listed end-to-end parsers, HunyuanOCR-1.5 had a higher Overall score (**94.74**), while PaDoc had the best Text Edit and Formula CDM. | Table 2 |
| Same-backbone quality was mixed | Against Sequential SFT, PaDoc improved Overall layout F1 from **89.666 to 91.126**, but end-to-end Overall fell from **94.585 to 94.240**; Text Edit and table metrics improved, while Read Order Edit and Formula CDM worsened. | Tables 9–10 |
| Large controlled serving gains | On a fixed 384-page subset and one A800, PaDoc delivered **1.640–1.699 valid pages/s/GPU** versus **0.751–1.015** for Sequential SFT across C16–C256: **67.4–118%** higher throughput. P95 latency was **39.2–54.9%** lower. | Table 3 |
| Structural reduction translated into wall-clock gains | At C64, median critical-path steps decreased from **725.0 to 297.5**, a **58.4%** median reduction. Across C16–C256, median paired end-to-end speedup ranged from **1.453× to 1.937×**. | Table 11 |
| Tree-varlen improved training execution | Relative to dense SDPA, tree-varlen was **1.163–1.185×** faster at 16K and **1.053–1.254×** faster at 8K across 1–16 nodes, with modest peak-memory reductions. | Table 8; Appendix C.3 |

The evidence establishes a substantial controlled serving advantage over a same-backbone sequential baseline and competitive benchmark quality. It does not establish universal speedups across hardware, runtimes, workload distributions, or documents that violate the regional-independence assumption. Cross-model quality and efficiency comparisons are contextual because architectures, parameter counts, pipelines, and possibly training data differ.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The critical-path reduction is an ideal structural quantity, not a wall-clock guarantee; actual cost includes request admission, residual prefill, scheduling overhead, and unequal branch lengths (Appendix E.2).
- **Author-stated comparison caveat:** Cross-architecture and cross-paradigm comparisons are contextual. Only Sequential SFT controls for backbone and training configuration (Inference Efficiency, “Results”).
- **Quality trade-off:** The controlled comparison does not show uniformly preserved quality. PaDoc’s Overall end-to-end score is 0.345 points lower, and its Formula CDM and reading-order metric are worse than Sequential SFT (Table 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Region-specific content sufficiency may fail for captions, cross-region references, section hierarchy, or content whose interpretation depends on neighboring regions. The paper motivates these dependencies but does not report results stratified by assumption violations.
- **Analyst inference:** Serving evidence is narrow: one A800 GPU, one 384-page subset, vLLM 0.19.1, and five concurrency settings. Generalization to other accelerators, runtimes, or low-concurrency interactive workloads remains untested.
- **Analyst inference:** Training reproducibility is limited because the approximately 11-million-example corpus includes in-house collections, and the supplied text does not give its exact composition or release status.
- **Validity asymmetry:** All PaDoc requests were valid, whereas 20–25 Sequential SFT outputs per setting reached the generation limit. Invalid work remains in the throughput denominator, but latency is reported only over valid requests, complicating interpretation of latency comparisons (Appendix E.3).

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is branching when content depends on other regions?** This remains open because the factorization assumes regional sufficiency. A next experiment could stratify pages by captions, cross-references, multi-region tables, and section hierarchy, then compare PaDoc with sequential decoding.

2. **Can the method retain the sequential baseline’s strongest quality metrics?** PaDoc loses Overall score, Formula CDM, and reading-order quality in the controlled comparison. An ablation could selectively permit cross-branch or completed-sibling attention and measure the quality–latency frontier.

3. **How portable are the serving gains?** Results cover one A800/vLLM configuration. Testing multiple GPU generations, runtimes, cache budgets, batch sizes, and branch-count distributions would show whether the gains depend on the evaluated scheduler.

4. **What components drive the end-to-end speedup?** Prefix caching, parallel scheduling, reduced critical-path depth, and output-validity differences coexist. Controlled serving ablations that disable cache reuse or cap active branches could isolate their contributions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported formulation, OmniDocBench results, and single-A800 measurements because the supplied text includes detailed methods, protocols, tables, and appendices.

Material missing information includes the exact composition and availability of the training corpus, statistical significance or repeated-run variance for serving results, quality stratification by document type/language or branch count, experiments on other hardware and runtimes, and direct tests of the region-sufficiency assumption.
