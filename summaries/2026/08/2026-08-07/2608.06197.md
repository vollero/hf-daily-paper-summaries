# EnvACE: Internalizing Environment Dynamics via World Rehearsal for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06197) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06197)


## One-sentence takeaway

EnvACE trains one LLM policy to alternate between tool actions and self-generated environment responses, improving tool-use benchmarks and enabling useful private rehearsal before real execution, though evidence is limited to models up to 8B and primarily tool-interactive tasks.

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon tool-use agents usually need executable environments or external simulators to supply responses during training. The authors argue that executable environments are costly to construct and verify, while simulators may be inaccurate and still require grounding in real environments. Both approaches leave environment modeling outside the acting policy.

EnvACE addresses this dependency by making the policy generate the environment response induced by each of its own actions. The intended benefit is to internalize action–response dynamics while producing self-unfolded training trajectories without external environment interaction (Introduction; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **World rehearsal:** A shared policy first generates an action and then, under a rehearsal role, generates the corresponding observation. Subsequent actions condition on these self-generated observations (Section 4.1, Equations 3–5; Figure 2).

- **Joint role-wise optimization:** Acting and rehearsal outputs inherit the trajectory-level task reward. Role-specific reward baselines produce separate advantages, but both roles update the same parameters through a clipped GRPO objective (Section 4.2, Equations 6–8).

- **Private test-time rehearsal:** Before one committed external execution, the trained policy imagines multiple trajectories in parallel or sequentially, evaluates them, summarizes them into rehearsal memory, and conditions execution on that memory (Section 4.3).

- **Empirical contribution:** The method is evaluated on four tool-use benchmarks, with controlled comparisons against standard GRPO and separate acting/rehearsal policies, plus tests across 1.7B and 8B backbones and rehearsal budgets (Sections 5.2–5.5).

The results support the authors’ claim that world rehearsal improves the tested Qwen3 policies and that moderate private rehearsal can help final execution without extra external interaction. The controlled GRPO and separate-policy comparisons offer more direct evidence for the method than the cross-paper baseline table.

They do not establish that rehearsed observations accurately match true environment transitions, that gains generalize beyond the four benchmark families or beyond 8B, or that EnvACE is uniformly superior: several baselines win individual benchmark columns, and ScaleEnv lacks the complete results needed for the overall comparison. No uncertainty estimates or significance tests are supplied.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation stops at 8B because of computational constraints, and it focuses primarily on tool-interactive tasks (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Test-time-scaling results come from a single run because of their computational cost; non-TTS results use Avg@4 over four independent runs (Section 5.1).

- **Author-stated interpretation:** The decline at \(N=3\) may result from longer rehearsal context approaching or exceeding the model’s effective context range, but this explanation is not directly tested (Section 5.5).

- **Analyst inference:** The paper evaluates downstream task success but provides no direct measurement of whether generated observations faithfully reproduce actual environment dynamics. Thus, “internalization” is supported indirectly through task performance and parameter-sharing ablation.

- **Analyst inference:** Using Qwen3-30B-A3B as the training judge introduces possible reward-model bias; judge reliability or sensitivity to alternative evaluators is not reported (Section 5.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Cross-method comparisons may be confounded by model size, training data, and environment construction. The supplied text does not document matched compute or matched training data across the external baselines.

- **Analyst inference:** Figure references around the ablations are inconsistent: prose attributes controlled comparisons to Figure 4, while the retrieved captions identify Figure 3 as the ablation and Figure 4 as scale performance. This weakens evidence traceability.

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is incomplete from the retrieved text because Table 2’s values are missing, detailed reward/checklist construction is not supplied, and availability of trained weights is not stated, although code is claimed public.

## Open research questions

1. **How faithful are rehearsed responses to real environment transitions?** Downstream success does not directly measure response accuracy. A next experiment could compare rehearsed and real observations for held-out actions using exact-match, structured-state, and task-relevant consistency measures.

2. **Does world rehearsal remain effective above 8B and across model families?** The paper tests only Qwen3 at 1.7B and 8B. A matched-data, matched-compute study across larger and architecturally different backbones would test scaling and model dependence.

3. **Can rehearsal help outside tool-interactive tasks?** The authors identify the current domain as narrow. A next experiment could apply the same shared-role objective to web navigation, GUI control, or another setting with independently verifiable transitions.

4. **Why does additional test-time rehearsal eventually hurt?** Context-length pressure is proposed but not isolated. Experiments varying \(N\), context truncation, memory compression, and total token budget independently could distinguish context saturation from poor self-evaluation or correlated simulation errors.
