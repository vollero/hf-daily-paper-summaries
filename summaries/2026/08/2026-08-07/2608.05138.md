# Teaching Nemotron Greek: Mining a Corpus, Adapting Retrieval, and Grounding Generation for Modern Greek across Specialist Domains

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05138) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05138)


## One-sentence takeaway

Adapting Nemotron models with Greek specialist-domain data substantially improves retrieval and grounded reading, but dense retrieval’s advantage over BM25 remains domain-bound and abstention remains weak.

## Problem and motivation

Modern Greek is absent from the supported languages of the studied Nemotron retrieval models and from major multilingual retrieval benchmarks, leaving performance on long, jargon-heavy Greek documents largely unmeasured. The authors target retrieval and grounded generation across energy, legal, finance, medical, and general-foundation material, while identifying three practical gaps: scarce native Greek instruction data, no suitable multi-passage Greek RAG benchmark, and severe truncation of long Greek passages under common 512-token settings (Sections 1–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Greek retrieval corpus:** The authors reduce 407,053 mined pairs to 65,773 per-query training records, synthesize style-matched grounded queries, mine same-domain hard negatives, remove positive/negative conflicts, and train at a 4,096-token maximum length (Section 3).
- **Adapted retrieval stack:** A 1B Nemotron embedder is fully fine-tuned with InfoNCE and hard negatives; a 1B cross-encoder is trained pointwise with binary cross-entropy. Dense and BM25 rankings are also combined through reciprocal rank fusion (Sections 6–7).
- **Grounded reader:** A 30B-A3B mixture-of-experts reader is LoRA-tuned on a 246,909-row blend that includes 40,000 synthetic citation-and-abstention examples (Sections 3.1 and 9).
- **HERA benchmark:** The released 4,946-item benchmark evaluates Greek long-context retrieval, citation, grounded answering, positional robustness, and unanswerable questions (Section 4; Table 1).
- **Empirical diagnosis:** The study measures lexical versus dense retrieval, in-domain versus general-domain transfer, reranking against a no-rerank floor, context-position effects, and several evaluation-pipeline failures (Sections 5–10).

These results establish large improvements over each model’s unadapted counterpart and strong in-domain retrieval performance under synthetic held-out queries. They do not establish an adapted dense-retrieval advantage on general Greek, a statistically clear advantage over BM25 in the medical set, or deployed end-to-end RAG quality: reader evaluation uses gold-provided rather than retrieved context (Sections 6, 8–9; Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** All retrieval queries are synthetic and likely overestimate production performance because they echo source-passage vocabulary. An unpublished internal observation suggests the margin over BM25 was roughly +0.04 on real queries versus +0.080 on the synthetic test, but no query count or labeling protocol is supplied (Section 3).
- **Author-stated:** Retrieval relevance uses the query’s source chunk as gold rather than human relevance judgments (Section 3).
- **Author-stated:** HERA is model-generated, has only an unsized human spot-check, and its correctness judge shares a model family with the generator. This particularly threatens judged correctness (Section 4).
- **Author-stated:** The benchmark-generation acceptance rate was not logged; it is bounded only at at least one-third (Section 4).
- **Author-stated:** The retrieval training corpus is not released, preventing full retraining from scratch (Availability).
- **Author-stated:** Reader results are single-run evaluations; abstention remains weak, the Greek data skews formal and official, and conversational Greek is underrepresented (Sections 9 and 11).
- **Author-stated:** The instruction pool is translation-heavy, so base-model limits cannot be cleanly separated from data-quality limits (Section 3.2).
- **Author-stated:** Reader supervision lacks multi-document citations, uses only two canned abstention targets, and contains 8.7% ultra-terse answers (Section 3.1).
- **Author-stated:** No comparison is made with Greek-adapted reader models, and multilingual E5 is omitted as a retrieval baseline (Section 2).
- **Analyst inference:** Because the reader is tested with gold-provided context while retrieval is evaluated separately, the reported component gains cannot determine actual end-to-end answer correctness under retrieval errors; the authors explicitly acknowledge not running this composition (Section 8; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Fusion evidence covers limited configurations—fixed \(k=60\), top-100 lists, and no study of candidate depth or score normalization—so broader serving robustness remains uncertain (Sections 6 and 11).

## Open research questions

1. **How well do the retrieval gains survive on independently authored user queries?** Synthetic-query vocabulary overlap likely inflates performance. A next experiment should evaluate BM25, dense retrieval, and fusion on a sized, human-labeled production-query set across all domains.

2. **What is the true deployed end-to-end RAG improvement?** Reader testing fixes retrieval success by supplying gold context. The natural next experiment is to run both readers on passages retrieved by each first-stage/reranker configuration and score correctness, faithfulness, citations, and abstention jointly.

3. **Can abstention improve without increasing false refusals?** Adaptation raises refusal of unanswerable items only to 30.5% while increasing false abstention. An experiment could vary abstention supervision, replace canned targets with diverse responses, and report full precision–recall tradeoffs.

4. **Which gains come from Greek exposure versus specialist-domain supervision?** The in-domain advantage reverses on Wikipedia, and the paper lacks a controlled general-Greek adaptation arm. A factorial experiment using matched amounts of general Greek, specialist Greek, and mixed training data could isolate language and domain effects.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported experimental results and caveats because the supplied text includes detailed settings, tables, appendices, and explicit validity boundaries.

Material missing information includes the unreleased retrieval training corpus, a sized systematic human audit of HERA, benchmark-generation acceptance logs, production-query evaluation details, repeated-run variability, a Greek-adapted reader baseline, multilingual E5 results, a zero-English-replay reader arm, and end-to-end reader evaluation on retrieved context.
