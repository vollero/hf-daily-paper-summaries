# Continual Learning in Transition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06216) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06216)


## One-sentence takeaway

This survey reframes continual learning as system-level capability evolution along three axes—when learning occurs, where capability is stored, and how updates are performed—extending the classical focus on gradient-based parameter updates during training.

## Problem and motivation

Classical continual learning chiefly addresses catastrophic forgetting during sequential parameter training, typically assuming that learning happens before deployment, capability resides in model weights, and updates use gradients over supplied data. The authors argue that this framing no longer covers large-model and agent systems, which may adapt at inference time, store capability in external memory, skills, or protocols, and learn through on-policy or gradient-free mechanisms. A unified vocabulary is needed to compare these developments and expose underexplored combinations (Sections 1–2; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Three-axis framework:** The survey characterizes methods by **When** capability evolves—pre-training, post-training, or inference time; **Where** it is carried—parameters or harness components; and **How** it changes—off-policy gradients, on-policy learning, or beyond-gradient mechanisms (Sections 2.2–3.4; Figures 2–6).
- **Cross-paradigm synthesis:** It jointly reviews areas usually treated separately, including continual pre-training, alignment, test-time training, model merging, external memory, skill libraries, and prompt evolution (Section 1).
- **Multi-label method profiles:** Methods can span several labels or move between carriers over time, rather than occupying immutable taxonomy cells (Section 3.5; Table C.1).
- **Research agenda:** The authors identify carrier coordination, active forgetting, long-horizon evaluation, error accumulation, and autonomous harness evolution as major open problems (Section 4).

The evidence establishes a broad conceptual taxonomy and a structured synthesis of prior literature. It does **not** experimentally validate that the taxonomy improves prediction or system design, quantify research density, or demonstrate superiority over earlier taxonomies. The numerical statements above summarize cited studies rather than original experiments, and the supplied text omits their exact metrics, datasets, and uncertainty estimates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Some methods cross axis boundaries, so alternative classifications can be equally reasonable (Conclusion).
- **Author-stated:** Coverage is limited by the submission cutoff and may lag rapidly appearing work (Conclusion).
- **Author-stated:** Harness and cross-axis evaluation remains immature; concrete evaluation protocols are left for future research (Conclusion; Section 4.4).
- **Author-stated:** Figures and tables are representative rather than exhaustive, so empty cells and method counts are not evidence of absence or research density (Section 3.5; Appendices B–C).
- **Analyst inference:** The framework’s three axes may not be fully independent—for example, particular harness carriers and inference-time mechanisms often co-occur—but the paper supplies no formal independence analysis.
- **Analyst inference:** Many conclusions are qualitative syntheses of cited work. The supplied text provides neither a systematic review protocol nor controlled comparisons across method families, limiting reproducibility and comparative validity.
- **Analyst inference:** The proposed long-horizon and multi-carrier evaluation agenda lacks operational metrics, making claims about “system-level continual learning” difficult to test consistently.

## Open research questions

1. **How should capability be allocated among context, memory, skills, protocols, and parameters?**  
   The paper identifies this scheduling problem but provides no formal policy. A next experiment could compare learned and rule-based allocation under fixed storage, inference, and update budgets on a long-horizon task stream.

2. **Can forgetting be measured consistently across heterogeneous carriers?**  
   Parameter interference, retrieval decay, context overflow, and skill mismatch have different failure modes (Section 4.3). A next experiment could introduce controlled corruption at each carrier and test whether a unified retention metric predicts downstream trajectory success.

3. **When does on-policy updating reduce forgetting relative to off-policy training?**  
   The survey reports structural and empirical arguments favoring smaller policy drift, but no matched cross-task comparison is presented. A next experiment could hold model, task sequence, reward, and compute constant while comparing on-policy RL, supervised fine-tuning, and replay.

4. **How robust are self-updating agents to compounding errors?**  
   Section 4.4 warns that incorrect state can be written back and amplified. A next experiment could inject retrieval or tool-selection errors at known steps and measure recovery, propagation, and contamination of persistent memory.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text is complete enough to characterize the survey’s thesis, taxonomy, scope, and stated limitations. Material missing information includes the contents of Appendix B’s benchmark rows, most quantitative results from the cited studies, exact literature-selection criteria, formal definitions for axis boundaries, controlled comparisons with prior taxonomies, and concrete evaluation protocols for harness-level or cross-carrier continual learning.
