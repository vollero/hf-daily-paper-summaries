# Weights or Skills? A Survey of Robot-Learning Techniques: from Action-Predicting Weights to Robots that Write their Own Skills

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.01851) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01851)


## One-sentence takeaway

This focused survey frames robot learning as frozen action-predicting weights versus executable skills and identifies feedback, persistent memory, and population search as the defining—and still rarely combined—mechanisms of code-based self-improvement.

## Problem and motivation

Robot-learning methods distribute competence in fundamentally different forms: end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) models ship frozen weights, whereas code-as-policy agents produce executable programs that may be repaired, stored, and searched. The authors argue that prior surveys cover VLA and foundation-model approaches but do not organize code-as-policy systems by degree of self-improvement or address emerging robot-skill marketplaces (Table 1; §1).

This matters because commercial skill distribution currently provides static playback rather than adaptation. The survey therefore connects research on self-improving programs to unresolved deployment issues including cross-embodiment portability, safety, provenance, composition, and standardization (§1; §4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Weights-versus-skills taxonomy:** Organizes 77 selected systems into six technique branches, centered on whether a system ships frozen weights or executable skills (Figure 2; §2).

- **Self-improvement ladder:** Classifies code-as-policy methods into zero-shot synthesis, closed-loop repair, persistent skill-library accumulation, evolutionary search, and a full loop combining feedback, memory, and search (§3.1; Tables 3–4).

- **Operational mechanism definitions:** Defines when execution feedback, cross-task memory, and multi-candidate search count, including explicit boundary cases (Table 3).

- **Skill-form analysis:** Separates five meanings of “skill”—latent policy, option/primitive, executable code, robot app, and market product—and compares their inspectability, adaptability, composability, and distributability (Table 5).

- **Broader landscape and research agenda:** Catalogues 225 additional works and proposes four future evaluation quantities: success versus interactions, skill-library reuse, cross-embodiment transfer drop, and provenance coverage (§2.1–§2.2; Tables 9 and 11).

The evidence establishes a structured literature taxonomy, corpus-level descriptive counts, and operational comparison criteria. It does not establish that code-based full-loop systems empirically outperform VLA or other families: protocols differ, top-rung systems are recent, and the paper deliberately avoids numerical rankings (§5). Figure 11 is explicitly schematic rather than a report of measured outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The taxonomy is deliberately code-centric, so weight-based policies and RL skill discovery receive contextual rather than equally deep treatment (§5).

- **Author-stated:** The full-loop systems are recent or concurrent, and their benchmark numbers are not directly comparable; capabilities are therefore summarized qualitatively (§5).

- **Author-stated:** Coverage is a fast-moving snapshot, while the skill-economy argument is motivated by one vendor marketplace rather than a mature empirical literature (§5).

- **Author-stated:** The corpus is representative, not exhaustive. Taxonomy selection used seeding and snowballing, and the count of candidates rejected for unverifiable metadata was not logged (§2.1; Figure 4).

- **Analyst inference:** Because the taxonomy admits only systems that fit its predefined axis and comparison columns, its selection process may reinforce the weights-versus-skills framing and underrepresent hybrid or ambiguous methods. This follows from the axis-placeability criterion but is not directly tested (§2.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Several family-level judgments—especially interpretability, safety, and failure modes—are qualitative syntheses without a shared empirical protocol, limiting their use as comparative performance evidence (Tables 7–8; §5).

## Open research questions

1. **Do full-loop systems improve reliably under a common interaction budget?**  
   The surveyed systems use incomparable splits and no standard benchmark plots held-out success against accumulated experience. A next experiment could evaluate full-loop and static baselines on identical LIBERO-style task streams, reporting complete success-versus-interactions curves and their area (§3.6; Table 9).

2. **Does persistent skill memory produce measurable cross-task compounding?**  
   The taxonomy records whether memory exists but does not quantify whether stored skills are reused effectively. A next experiment could log the fraction of new-task solutions invoking prior skills, alongside success and mean reuse per stored skill (Table 9).

3. **Can adaptation reduce cross-embodiment degradation without compromising safety?**  
   Portability remains unresolved across bodies with different action spaces and dynamics. A next experiment could measure matched-task success drops on held-out Open X-Embodiment-style platforms before and after bounded on-device adaptation (§4; Table 9).

4. **Can latent learned policies and inspectable code share a bidirectional skill interface?**  
   The survey identifies separate RL and code skill lineages but does not demonstrate their integration. A next experiment could compare code wrappers around learned primitives—and distillation of those primitives into editable programs—on reuse, success, and verification cost (§3.4; §6).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the survey’s stated taxonomy, corpus counts, scope, and proposed agenda because the supplied text includes the relevant methodology and tables.

Materially missing are independent verification of cited systems’ classifications, detailed quantitative results for most primary papers, inter-rater or reviewer-agreement procedures for taxonomy assignments, complete search strings and rejected-record counts, direct head-to-head evaluations, and measured results for the proposed protocol in Table 9.
