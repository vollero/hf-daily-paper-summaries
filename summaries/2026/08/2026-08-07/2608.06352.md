# CalibForge: Adversarial Solver Calibration for Scaling Learnable Terminal Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06352) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06352)


## One-sentence takeaway
CalibForge iteratively revises validated terminal tasks until heterogeneous solvers disagree or a designated strong solver passes while a weak solver fails, producing training data that outperforms matched synthesis baselines across terminal and repository-level evaluations.

## Problem and motivation
Executable validation can establish that a terminal task works, but not whether it is useful for learning: a valid task may be trivial for all target solvers or effectively unsolvable. The authors identify a gap in existing synthesis pipelines, which generally validate finished tasks without using verified solver behavior to control task difficulty during construction (Introduction; Related Work).

## Approach and contributions

- **Environment-level behavioral calibration:** Task learnability is defined relative to specified solvers and made actionable through verified pass/fail outcomes (Sections 1 and 2.1).
- **Adversarial author–solver loop:** An authoring agent jointly revises instructions, Docker-based environments, and verifier tests, with every revision undergoing structural validation, self-solving, and renewed probing (Sections 2.1–2.3; Algorithm 1).
- **Two retention strategies:** Multi-solver calibration requires at least one success and one failure among heterogeneous solvers; contrastive calibration requires a designated strong solver to pass and weak solver to fail (Section 2.3, Equations 1–2).
- **Training asset and empirical study:** The pipeline produces 5,431 calibrated tasks, from which successful trajectories are distilled for full-parameter SFT of two Qwen backbones (Sections 3.1 and 3.3).

The matched distillation and training protocol, plus the task-count-controlled ablation, supports the authors’ claim that solver-relative calibration improves the usefulness of training data. It does not isolate every component of the revision process, establish that solver disagreement is an intrinsic measure of difficulty, or demonstrate generalization beyond the two Qwen backbones and three reported benchmarks. SWE-bench Pro differences are especially uncertain because each model was evaluated only once.

## Limitations and validity risks

The supplied text contains no dedicated author-stated limitations section.

- **Analyst inference — solver dependence:** “Learnability” is defined by the selected solver pool. Tasks calibrated for DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, GLM-5, and Kimi K2.5 may occupy a different capability interval for other solvers (Sections 2.3 and 3.1).
- **Analyst inference — limited model coverage:** Downstream evidence uses only two related Qwen backbones, so architecture-level generality is not established (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — stochastic uncertainty:** SWE-bench Pro is evaluated once, preventing run-to-run uncertainty estimates there (Section 3.1; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete causal isolation:** Table 3 matches task counts and training recipes, but retained trajectory counts differ, and no ablation separates outcome signals, feedback summaries, and full trajectories.
- **Analyst inference — high resource demands:** Construction permits up to 50 rounds with multiple 100-step, 30-minute solver attempts; SFT uses 64 NVIDIA H20 GPUs and a 131,072-token context (Section 3.1; Appendix E). End-to-end compute and monetary costs are not reported.
- **Analyst inference — verifier validity:** Success is defined by all verifier tests passing. Appendix B.3 shows that overly prescriptive verifiers can initially reject valid alternatives, indicating dependence on verifier quality.
- **Analyst inference — benchmark scope:** All evaluations concern terminal or repository-oriented software tasks; transfer to non-software agent environments is untested (Section 3.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How solver-specific is the calibrated learnable zone?** The paper uses one fixed heterogeneous pool and one strong/weak pair. Recalibrate identical candidates with different solver families, then measure retention overlap and downstream utility.
2. **Which feedback component drives revision quality?** Outcomes, structured diagnoses, and full trajectories are provided together. Ablate each channel under equal task, trajectory, and compute budgets.
3. **What calibration budget is cost-effective?** Figure 9 shows a long tail but reports no accuracy-versus-construction-cost curve. Train on datasets retained under several round caps and compare benchmark gains per solver-hour.
4. **Does calibration transfer beyond terminal software work?** The evidence covers three software-oriented benchmarks. Apply the same retention criteria to another executable, verifier-backed agent domain and test both in-domain and cross-domain performance.
