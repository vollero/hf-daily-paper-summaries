# Activity Frames: Deterministic Screen-Activity Compilation for Agent Memory and Replay

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper presents a deterministic, model-free compiler that converts local screen-activity logs into auditable agent memory, substantially reducing prompt size and improving day-level question answering on a single-user corpus, while its routine-replay cost estimates remain largely modeled.

## Problem and motivation

Computer-use agents typically remember conversations and their own trajectories, not the user’s prior desktop activity. Raw screen-capture logs are too large and unstructured for routine prompting, while LLM-generated summaries add inference cost, nondeterminism, and potential factual distortion.

The authors identify a missing layer between capture and consumption: a compact, reproducible representation of observed activity that preserves provenance and explicitly separates measured facts from inferred interpretations (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Activity-frame schema:** Represents bounded episodes with application/site, timing, dwell-based activity, typed pages, input volume, interruptions, and pointers to source rows. Coverage gaps and systematic blind spots are mandatory (Section 3).
- **Measured/inferred separation:** Deterministic fields form Tier 1; optional interpretations must be namespaced, confidence-tagged, and evidence-linked in Tier 2 (Sections 3.1 and 3.4).
- **Deterministic compiler:** Uses fixed dwell, session-gap, flicker-merge, event-attribution, click-resolution, and URL-typing rules without an LLM in the compilation path (Section 4; Algorithm 1).
- **Open implementation:** Provides a dependency-free Python compiler, local capture integration, CLI, multiple output formats, six MCP tools, replay-plan compilation, and evaluation/certification harnesses (Section 5; Table 1).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Measures compression, latency, reproducibility, URL typing, activity fragmentation, and downstream day-level QA on one professional’s capture history (Section 6).
- **Cost and recurrence instrument:** Defines Routine Overhead Ratio \(R\), mines repeated UI-action sequences, estimates recurrence \(h\), and demonstrates one guard-matched two-step replay with zero model tokens during execution (Sections 7 and 8.4).

The evidence supports deterministic reconstruction from stored capture, strong compression, low compilation latency, and better performance than the selected raw-row and LLM-summary representations in this particular day-level QA benchmark. It does not establish population-level effectiveness, generality across capture engines or model families, attention measurement, or realized fleet-wide savings. The large \(R\) ratios and dollar savings use a modeled screenshot-driven-agent numerator; only the replay denominator received a limited live confirmation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** All empirical measurements come from one professional, one machine, and one platform. The work is a mechanism characterization, not a user study (Sections 6, 8.3, and 8.4).
- **Author-stated:** Dwell measures how long content remains frontmost on an awake display, not human attention. Heartbeat-terminated intervals account for 45% of credited time, with uninterrupted runs reaching 42 minutes (Section 8.3).
- **Author-stated:** Per-monitor accounting can double-credit time; 27% of captured minutes involved two active monitors. On the overflow day, the dominant application differed by 26% between compiler and oracle conventions (Sections 6.2 and 8.3).
- **Author-stated:** Off-screen work is absent, intent and task structure are not observed, and new capture engines require adapters (Section 8.3).
- **Author-stated:** The QA evaluation uses only two models from one family, one answering pass, unpinned sampling, one user, and tolerances of 30% and 45 minutes by default (Sections 6.2 and 8.3).
- **Author-stated:** The activity-frame representation receives precomputed per-application durations; thus quantitative QA partly tests deterministic preprocessing rather than equal-information reading alone (Section 6.2).
- **Author-stated:** The compact block omits some information: its only QA error was a domain removed by the token budget (Section 6.2).
- **Author-stated:** \(R\)’s numerator and the three-arm dollar comparison are modeled, not billed. A live accessibility-tree comparison found only about 14% savings while retaining a model in the loop (Sections 7.1, 7.3, and 8.4).
- **Author-stated:** The replay demonstration covers only one two-step routine, uses seeded accessibility names, and does not exercise guard-miss deoptimization (Section 8.4).
- **Author-stated:** Determinism begins with stored OCR output, not pixels; capture-engine compromise can fabricate the underlying evidence (Sections 8.2 and 8.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The paper does not report ablations over the 90-second dwell cap, 300-second session gap, or 20-second flicker threshold on downstream QA, so sensitivity to these fixed conventions remains unclear.
- **Analyst inference:** The QA question set excludes longest-session and detailed per-profile recall, narrowing evidence about whether the compact representation supports richer episodic queries.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the method generalize across users, occupations, platforms, and capture cadences?** The evaluation has one subject and machine. A next experiment could preregister a multi-user study and report compression, QA, typing coverage, \(R\), and held-out \(h\) by participant and role.

2. **How sensitive are results to segmentation and monitor-accounting rules?** The paper shows a material multi-monitor discrepancy but no systematic parameter ablation. A factorial experiment could vary dwell caps, gap thresholds, flicker windows, and per-monitor versus unified accounting while measuring oracle agreement and QA.

3. **What are the realized costs and failure rates of replay?** The main economic comparisons are modeled, and live evidence is one two-step run. A next experiment could bill memoryless, plan-in-context, and deterministic-replay arms across diverse authenticated routines while measuring tokens, success, guard misses, harmful actions, and recovery behavior.

4. **How should bounded memory trade compression against recall?** One visited domain was omitted, and detailed profile recall was excluded. A next experiment could compare fixed-budget selection policies using precision–recall curves across domain, entity, duration, and session questions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for summarizing the supplied text and its internal claims; **medium** for the paper’s broader empirical implications because evaluation is single-user and several cost claims are modeled.

Material missing information includes the contents of Tables 1–4 beyond their prose descriptions, per-representation default-tolerance accuracy values for every baseline/model combination, question-level outputs, confidence intervals beyond the compiled block’s reported Wilson interval, detailed hardware specifications, capture-engine resource utilization beyond storage/OCR coverage, cross-user results, independently billed replay costs, and systematic parameter ablations.
