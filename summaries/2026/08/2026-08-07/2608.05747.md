# GST-Bench: Can VLMs Develop Global Spatial Awareness from Video?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05747) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05747)


## One-sentence takeaway

GST-Bench finds that current VLMs struggle to integrate long-horizon egocentric video into globally consistent spatial representations, although targeted training substantially narrows the measured gap.

## Problem and motivation

Embodied agents must remember observations across time, localize themselves and objects from new viewpoints, and relate egocentric views to a global map. The authors argue that existing spatial benchmarks mainly test single images, a few views, coarse directional categories, or questions potentially solvable from one informative frame; they therefore do not isolate global cross-frame spatial reasoning or explicitly test correspondence with top-down scene representations (Introduction; Section 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **GST-Bench:** A video VQA benchmark containing 2,762 human-verified questions and 6,790 minutes of cumulative QA-level video input, generated from 50 indoor simulation scenes (Introduction; Section 3.3).
- **Task taxonomy:** Twelve subtasks covering self localization, object localization, and scene-structure understanding, with numerical distance, angle, and point predictions plus multiple-choice tasks (Sections 3.2 and 4.1).
- **Shortcut controls:** Query views are sampled off the exploration trajectory, and object-localization targets must be visible during exploration but absent from the query view (Sections 3.1 and 3.3).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** The authors evaluate 22 VLMs and introduce Global, Local-Video, and Local-Image variants to separate cross-frame integration failures from local perception failures (Sections 4.1–4.3).
- **Training resource:** GST-Train provides targeted global-spatial-reasoning supervision using scenes disjoint from GST-Bench evaluation scenes (Sections 3.3 and 4.4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| The strongest zero-shot model remains far below humans. | Gemini-3-Pro scores 42.68 versus the human baseline of 79.08 on the arithmetic mean of all 12 subtasks, a 36.40-point gap. | Table 1; Section 4.2 |
| Open-source performance is generally weak. | The strongest reported open-source average is InternVL3.5-38B at 30.71, compared with random guessing at 20.01; four listed models score below random overall. | Table 1; Section 4.2 |
| Embodied-oriented models do not outperform comparable general VLMs. | RoboBrain2.5-8B scores 24.61 and Cosmos-Reason2-8B 21.64, both below Qwen3-VL-8B at 25.89; Robix-32B scores 29.26 versus Qwen3-VL-32B at 30.43. | Table 1; Section 4.2 |
| Strong proprietary models improve sharply when direction reasoning becomes local. | On semantic egocentric direction, Gemini-3-Pro rises from 22.11 Global to 61.20 Local-Image; GPT-5 rises from 31.09 to 65.61. | Table 2a; Section 4.3 |
| Local variants expose mixed bottlenecks among open models. | On semantic direction, Local-Image changes range from −8.03 for InternVL3.5-8B to +7.29 for InternVL3.5-2B; all four tested open models improve on semantic distance, but remain below the strongest proprietary local scores. | Tables 2a–2b; Section 4.3 |
| Targeted training produces the largest reported model improvement. | Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on GST-Train raises its 12-task average from 25.89 to 53.52, a 27.63-point gain, with train and evaluation scenes stated to be disjoint. | Table 1; Section 4.4 |
| A substantial human gap remains after targeted training. | The fine-tuned score of 53.52 remains 25.56 points below the reported human score of 79.08. | Table 1; analyst calculation from reported scores |

These results support the authors’ claim that the evaluated models have difficulty with this benchmark and that targeted supervision improves in-distribution benchmark performance. The local controls provide evidence that cross-frame integration is a bottleneck for several proprietary models, but they cover only two semantic object-localization tasks. The evidence does not establish general real-world embodied competence, identify which component of GST-Train causes the gain, or demonstrate transfer to unrelated benchmarks or environments.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** GST-Bench targets synthetic indoor environments and uses generated trajectories, geometry-derived answers, and fixed video observations rather than active exploration (Sections 3.1 and 3.3).
- **Author-stated residual gap:** Fine-tuning improves performance but does not close the gap to the human baseline (Section 4.4).
- **Analyst inference — limited human estimate:** Human performance uses only 20 samples per task, and the supplied text reports no uncertainty or annotator-count details (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — narrow diagnostic controls:** GST-Bench-Local examines only semantic egocentric direction and distance, so its proprietary/open-source bottleneck conclusions may not extend to the other ten subtasks (Section 4.3).
- **Analyst inference — training attribution is unresolved:** No ablation separates GST-Train’s size, constituent simulation sources, task mixture, or general instruction-data mixture; the causal ingredient behind the 27.63-point gain is therefore unclear (Section 4.4).
- **Analyst inference — reproducibility details are incomplete:** The supplied text does not give GST-Train’s size, optimization settings, video sampling policy, prompts, compute requirements, or asset-release terms.
- **Analyst inference — possible pipeline alignment:** GST-Train and GST-Bench use the same generation pipeline. Scene disjointness limits direct scene leakage, but the reported gain does not by itself establish transfer beyond that pipeline (Sections 3.3 and 4.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does GST-Train improve spatial reasoning outside GST-Bench?** This remains open because only GST-Bench results are reported. A next experiment could evaluate the fine-tuned model on independently constructed real-video and spatial-reasoning benchmarks.

2. **Which supervision components drive the improvement?** The paper reports no GST-Train ablation. A factorial experiment could vary task families, simulation sources, training-set size, and the proportion of general instruction data.

3. **Can models maintain global maps over longer or less complete trajectories?** The benchmark supplies broad scene-coverage trajectories, but sensitivity to duration, coverage, and observation order is not reported. A next experiment could systematically truncate, reorder, or sparsify exploration videos.

4. **Do the local/global bottleneck conclusions hold across all competencies?** The controlled study covers only semantic direction and distance. Equivalent local controls for self localization, visual target specification, top-down alignment, and trajectory selection would test the generality of the diagnosis.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the benchmark design and reported comparisons because the supplied text includes task definitions, metric formulas, and detailed result tables; **medium** for broader claims about general spatial intelligence because evaluation is synthetic and narrowly scoped.

Material missing information includes GST-Train’s sample count and composition, training hyperparameters and compute, prompt templates, video frame-sampling details, statistical uncertainty, full human-evaluation protocol, real-world evaluation, cross-benchmark transfer, component ablations, and release/licensing details.
