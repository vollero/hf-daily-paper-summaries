# ContextMaster: Interactive Multi-Shot Video Creation via Fixed-Budget Sparse Context Routing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04956) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04956)


## One-sentence takeaway

ContextMaster unifies stateful multi-shot generation, reference conditioning, and editing while bounding repeated context-read cost through cached sparse routing, with reported gains in cross-shot consistency and 16.74 FPS throughput on one H200 GPU.

## Problem and motivation

The paper formalizes interactive multi-shot video creation (IMVC): at each turn, one model generates, follows a visual reference, or edits source footage, then adds accepted output to an evolving history. Existing unified video systems generally expose these capabilities as separate operations over fixed inputs rather than composable stateful actions (§1).

This setting creates two gaps: heterogeneous inputs—references, historical shots, aligned source video, and noisy targets—have different temporal roles; and accumulated history makes dense context reads increasingly expensive during denoising. Prior bounded-memory strategies can discard history, while retrieval approaches do not necessarily preserve explicit reference and frame-aligned editing constraints (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Unified IMVC formulation and representation:** Role-aware rotary coordinates combine native frame positions with role- and shot-specific phase offsets, distinguishing reference, history, source, and target tokens while retaining source–target temporal alignment (§3.1, Equations 1–3).

- **Cacheable fixed-budget context:** An asymmetric attention topology makes clean-context keys and values independent of target noise, denoising timestep, and classifier-free-guidance branch, allowing one prefill per interaction round (§3.2, Equation 4).

- **Constraint-preserving sparse routing:** Each target block reads at most \(B\) context blocks. ConstraintSink reserves reference and frame-aligned source blocks, while content-based routing allocates remaining capacity across source and history (§3.2, Equations 5–7).

- **Privileged context distillation:** A dense full-context teacher first trains the sparse four-step student through consistency distillation; distribution matching then refines deployment-matched rollouts and student-generated histories (§3.3, Equations 8–12).

- **Optional workflow automation:** An external Interactive Director can plan shots, retrieve references, and localize edit segments, but is not part of the latency-critical video model (Appendix A).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that the method can combine multiple video-creation operations, improve reported cross-shot behavior, and make repeated target-to-context reads independent of history length. It does not establish constant end-to-end latency: full-history prefill still grows and empirically lowers throughput. Moreover, most exact entries from Tables 1–4 are missing, preventing independent verification of broad “best overall” claims, effect sizes, or statistical reliability.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** Context prefill remains bidirectional over the entire accumulated history. Throughput therefore falls by about 0.4 FPS per added shot despite the fixed repeated-read budget (Appendix E).

- **Analyst inference—restricted reproducibility:** Training depends on an internal collection of approximately one million multi-shot videos and synthetic editing targets generated with Kling 3.0; the exact internal data and construction details are unavailable (Appendix B.2).

- **Analyst inference—missing quantitative detail:** The retrieved text omits the numerical contents of Tables 1–4. Many comparative and ablation conclusions therefore cannot be checked beyond the few values stated in prose.

- **Analyst inference—limited human evidence:** The user study has 10 video professionals and 20 X2MV trajectories, with no uncertainty estimates, inter-rater agreement, sampling procedure, or exact mean ratings supplied (§§4.1, 4.3).

- **Analyst inference—evaluator validity:** Task fulfillment relies on Gemini 2.5 Pro ratings, but agreement with human judgment or sensitivity to evaluator prompts is not reported (§4.1; Appendix D.2).

- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation uses 832×480, 81-frame shots, four denoising steps, a 6-FE budget, and a single H200 for efficiency measurements; generalization to other resolutions, shot lengths, budgets, or hardware is not established (§4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can full-history prefill also be made independent of session length?** It remains the principal stated scalability bottleneck. A next experiment could compare persistent per-shot KV caches and compact shot summaries against current prefill across increasingly long sessions.

2. **How robust is sparse routing over much longer histories?** Tests described here use five-shot sequences for key comparisons and budget ablation. Evaluate identity, task fulfillment, retrieval selections, latency, and memory over substantially longer controlled trajectories.

3. **Which context blocks does routing select, and when does it fail?** The paper ablates ConstraintSink but provides no reported retrieval-quality analysis. Compare selected blocks with annotated relevant history and stratify failures by reference, aligned-source, and distant-history dependence.

4. **Do automated task-fulfillment scores track professional judgment?** The paper reports both Gemini and human evaluation but no agreement analysis. A blinded study could measure rank correlation and disagreement patterns across tasks and edit types.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methodology, evaluation design, training configuration, and a stated limitation, but the actual numerical contents of Tables 1–4 are unavailable. Material missing information includes most baseline scores, exact ablation values, user-study means and uncertainty, statistical tests, per-task efficiency, data licensing and release status, model/checkpoint availability, detailed profiling conditions, and performance beyond the reported shot lengths and hardware.
