# HarnessOpt-Bench: Evaluating LLMs at Harness Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06301) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06301)


## One-sentence takeaway

HarnessOpt-Bench evaluates whether LLM-based optimizers can improve agent harnesses under noisy, budgeted feedback, finding that optimizer-model choice matters more on average than coding-harness choice but performance remains task-dependent and often unresolved.

## Problem and motivation

Agent performance depends on prompts, tools, memory, control flow, and orchestration code—not only model weights. Automating improvements to this surrounding harness requires diagnosing stochastic failures, modifying code, allocating a limited evaluation budget, and selecting a final candidate without seeing test results.

The authors argue that prior studies use incompatible seeds, target agents, budgets, and scoring protocols, confounding the effects of the optimizer model, its coding harness, and the evaluation setup. They address the absence of a common, held-out protocol for measuring end-to-end harness optimization under expensive and noisy evaluation (Section 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark task:** An optimizer receives a seed harness, development traces, aggregate validation feedback, and a fixed evaluation budget. It edits the harness and nominates one candidate, scored by normalized held-out gain over the seed (Sections 3.1–3.2; Algorithm 1; Equation 3).
- **Trusted protocol:** Isolated execution prevents test access or modification of the target model, environment, and verifier; it meters resources and versions candidates for audit (Figure 1).
- **Controlled suite:** Four downstream tasks use fixed, disjoint development, validation, and test splits, pinned seeds and target models, repeated scoring, and task-specific noise-resolution bands (Sections 3.2 and 4).
- **Factorized comparison:** Five frontier optimizer models are tested through a common coding harness and their native harnesses; two additional harnesses are evaluated on GAIA (Section 4).
- **Released evaluation assets:** The benchmark includes seed agents, exact split manifests, build configurations, and evaluation infrastructure (Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Model choice had the larger average effect. | Across controlled contrasts, changing the optimizer model moved normalized gain by **0.142** on average, versus **0.079** for changing its coding harness—about **1.8×** larger. | Section 5.1; Figure 2 |
| Fine-grained rankings were often unsupported. | The strongest configuration captured roughly **two-thirds** of OfficeQA headroom and **one-half** of BrowseComp-Plus headroom, while intermediate differences were often smaller than run-to-run variation. | Section 5.1 |
| The benchmark detected release-level progress, but not uniformly. | On OfficeQA, **5 GPT releases** rose monotonically from **+0.03 to +0.49** normalized gain, with **3 of 4** transitions exceeding the **±0.045** resolution band. Across **5 Claude Opus releases**, gain ranged from **+0.37 to +0.59** non-monotonically, though the first-to-last spread exceeded the band. | Section 5.2; Figure 3 |
| Broader exploration correlated with better outcomes. | Across the four tasks, the fraction of eight predefined harness levers touched had Spearman \(\rho\) values from **+0.34 to +0.88** with held-out gain. | Section 5.3; Figure 4 |
| Detailed trace reading was rare and not positively associated with gain. | The share of actions reading evaluation output correlated with gain from **−0.31 to −0.64**; detailed trace spans were requested only **16 times by 7 of 111 cells**. | Section 5.3 |
| Case-pass budgets constrained search more than call counts. | The median optimizer used **8 calls (4%)** but **82%** of its case allowance; **55 of 100 cells** exhausted at least one partition’s case budget. | Section 5.3; Figure 15 |
| Native coding harnesses had no consistent overall advantage. | Among **20 model–task pairs**, the shared harness won **11** and the native harness won **9**; **11** pairwise differences exceeded the relevant resolution band, but their directions varied. | Section 5.4; Figure 12 |
| Harness effects depended on the optimizer model. | On GAIA, the two GPT models improved under Codex by **+0.179** and **+0.131** relative to their best shared-harness result, while Claude and Kimi differences remained within one or two resolution bands. | Section 5.4; Figure 5 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that this protocol can distinguish some optimizer models and expose model–harness interactions under the evaluated tasks and budgets. It does not establish a complete model ranking, causal benefits from broad exploration or reduced trace reading, or generalization beyond the tested seeds, target models, languages, and tasks. Correlations from search trajectories are observational, and the resolution bands are descriptive thresholds rather than formal significance tests.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The benchmark is hack-resistant rather than hackproof; repeated feedback may reward exploitation of stable evaluator artifacts.
- **Author-stated:** Seed complexity is not varied systematically. GAIA begins from a nonfunctional stub, whereas the other three seeds are competent but naive, limiting cross-task interpretation.
- **Author-stated:** Candidates are restricted to Python, and each task has only one pinned target model; other languages, runtimes, architectures, and target models remain untested.
- **Author-stated:** Broader task coverage, matched compute, and more replication are needed before treating the benchmark as comprehensive.
- **Author-stated:** Optimizer inference is metered but uncapped, so results do not represent a setting where optimizer reasoning cost is constrained.
- **Analyst inference:** Two optimizer runs per configuration provide weak information about variability; Appendix A explicitly says the reported ranges are not confidence intervals.
- **Analyst inference:** The positive breadth–gain association is confounded by modification volume and overall search effort, as the authors acknowledge, so it cannot identify breadth as the cause.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text is internally inconsistent about evaluation-call caps: Section 3.1 states **100 calls per partition**, while Section 5.3 states **200 invocations** per development and validation partition.
- **Analyst inference:** The retrieved text omits the numerical cells of Tables 1–4, preventing independent verification of many summarized comparisons.

## Open research questions

1. **How does optimization ability vary with seed maturity?** The suite mixes a nonfunctional stub with competent seeds without a controlled complexity ladder. A next experiment could evaluate identical tasks using seeds ranging from stub to mature production harness.

2. **Do broader searches cause higher held-out gains?** Current correlations cannot separate lever coverage from search effort or modification volume. A randomized experiment could assign optimizers different lever-access sets while matching evaluation and inference budgets.

3. **How robust are optimized harnesses to evaluator and environment changes?** Fixed artifacts may permit evaluation-specific strategies. A next version could introduce per-run case, tool-behavior, and verifier jitter, as proposed in the Limitations section.

4. **Do conclusions persist under matched total compute?** Optimizer inference is uncapped, and the authors call for matched-compute comparisons. A follow-up could cap both optimizer tokens and target-agent evaluation resources across every model–harness configuration.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed protocol and qualitative results, but the actual numerical contents of Tables 1–4 are absent. Material missing information includes exact per-configuration gains and ranges, task-specific resolution bands other than OfficeQA’s, pinned target-model identities, split sizes, seed baselines, off-the-shelf harness scores, token-use results, and the numerical LSS-\(\lambda\) estimates.
