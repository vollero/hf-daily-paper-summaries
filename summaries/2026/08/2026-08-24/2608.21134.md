# Llama-Mobile: Efficient 2.7-Bit Quantization of VLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21134) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21134)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors compress Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct to a 3.73 GB deployable model using a self-generated-data QAT pipeline and an Arm-oriented 2.68-bpp weight format, retaining a 0.661 average across four sampled VQA evaluations while enabling 3.8 decoding tokens/s on a Pixel 8a.

## Problem and motivation

Vision-language models require too much memory and computation for many mobile devices, while aggressive sub-3-bit quantization can severely damage multimodal question-answering performance. The authors target two practical gaps: quantizing without the original training data or recipe, and designing a representation that both preserves accuracy and can be decoded efficiently on Arm CPUs (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Synthetic-data QAT:** ImageNet images are paired with randomly sampled, generic prompts and responses generated by the original model. A quantized student then minimizes token-level KL divergence from the full-precision teacher on those responses, without downstream benchmark data (Sections 3.2–3.3 and A.1–A.2).
- **S3D8 format:** Three signed weights share a 5-bit vector-centroid index and use three sign bits, storing them in one byte—approximately 2.7 bits per parameter after scaling overhead. The format reconstructs INT8 weights using small lookup tables and Arm SIMD operations (Section 3.4; Figures 2 and 7).
- **Hardware-aware implementation:** The authors provide custom decoding, matrix-multiplication kernels, and full C++ VLM inference for Linux and Android on Arm CPUs (Sections 4.3–4.4 and C–D).
- **Controlled comparisons:** S3D8 is compared with scalar INT, Student-\(t\), and Lloyd–Max formats under the same QAT procedure, and direct casting, GPTQ, and QAT are compared across compression rates (Section 4; Figures 1 and 3a).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that their format and QAT recipe offer a favorable compression–quality trade-off for this model and that S3D8 can support practical Arm decoding. It does not establish similar accuracy on other VLMs, other task families, GPUs or non-Arm hardware. The end-to-end mobile timing excludes loading and prefill, so it is not a complete interaction-latency measurement.

## Limitations and validity risks

Author-stated limitations:

- Experiments cover only Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct and CPU execution (Section 5).
- S3D8’s decoder depends on an Arm-optimized 64-entry lookup; other architectures need specialized kernels (Section 5).
- Evaluation is limited to four VQA-style benchmarks from the original Llama evaluation suite (Section 5).
- A single quantization format is used across all weights; tested mixed-precision variants did not meaningfully improve the trade-off (Section 5; Table 4).
- Prompt and image selection may not be optimal for downstream generalization (Section 5).

Analyst inferences:

- **Subset uncertainty:** Each benchmark uses only 1,024 fixed examples, but task-score uncertainty intervals are not reported; the robustness of small score differences is therefore unclear (Sections 4 and A.5).
- **Baseline asymmetry:** GPTQ is weight-only with bfloat16 activations, whereas direct-cast and QAT models use INT8 activations. This limits procedure-level runtime comparison, although format comparisons within GPTQ remain informative (Section 4 and A.4).
- **Data-independence boundary:** ImageNet is separate from the downstream samples, but the paper does not report checks for semantic or image overlap beyond stating that no benchmark images or benchmark-specific prompts were used (Section A.1).
- **Incomplete mobile latency picture:** The 3.8-token/s result excludes model loading, vision encoding, and prompt prefill, and only one phone is tested end to end (Section 4.4).
- **Asset verifiability:** A repository is named, but the supplied paper text does not document its exact contents, license, or instructions sufficient to confirm independent reproduction.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the method generalize across VLM architectures and sizes?** Only one 11B model is tested. A next experiment could apply identical data generation and rate-matched QAT to several VLM families and report the same four task metrics plus broader multimodal evaluations.

2. **Which synthetic-data properties drive preservation most strongly?** The paper compares sampled prompts with one fixed prompt, but does not isolate image diversity, response length, template probability, or individual instruction probes. A factorial ablation could vary each component while holding QAT compute constant.

3. **Can mixed precision outperform the tested fixed-format allocation?** Only the output projection and vision encoder are reported as INT8 exceptions. A next experiment could allocate precision by measured layer sensitivity under a fixed total-size budget.

4. **What is the full user-perceived mobile latency and energy cost?** Reported decoding excludes loading and prefill. A next experiment could measure cold start, image encoding, prompt prefill, decoding, peak memory, and energy across several mobile Arm devices.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, experiments, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material missing information includes task-level confidence intervals or repeated-run variance, QAT hardware and total training cost, the exact number of distinct QAT samples actually consumed or repeated over 2,048 steps, end-to-end prefill/loading latency and energy, evaluations on additional models or hardware families, and independently verifiable repository contents or licensing.
