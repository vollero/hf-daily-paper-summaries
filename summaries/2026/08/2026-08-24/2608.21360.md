# OmniAssistBench: Assistant-style Interaction Benchmark for Omni-LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21360) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21360)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmniAssistBench evaluates audio-visual assistants through fixed-path, open-ended, multi-turn interactions and finds that even its strongest tested model scores only 66.4/100, with major failures in visual prompting, long-context tracking, and response timing.

## Problem and motivation

Static video-QA benchmarks cannot faithfully evaluate assistants whose replies would influence users’ subsequent actions, because each reply can create a different interaction path. The authors identify a gap in comprehensive evaluation of continuous assistant-style interactions involving audio, video, user goals, temporal continuity, and decisions about when to respond (Introduction, pp. 1–3; Related Work, pp. 12–13).

## Approach and contributions

- **Fixed-path construction:** The authors extract procedural priors from source videos and require models to follow those paths, avoiding ambiguity from multiple valid routes (Sec. 2.2; Figs. 1 and 4).
- **Reverse-engineered interactions:** Internet videos are filtered, assigned plausible user goals, segmented into temporally continuous turns, and edited to embed spoken or visual prompts (Sec. 2).
- **Two-tier benchmark:** The Basic tier measures foundational social, temporal, referential, and non-audio-prompt perception; the Advanced tier covers context-aware responses, proactive timing, and process tracking (Sec. 3; Fig. 2; Tables 5–6).
- **Real-world cases:** Three scripted, filmed cases combine abilities in meeting simulation, blind assistance, and handicraft tracking (Sec. 3.3; Fig. 6).
- **Open-ended scoring:** Answers are assessed by an LLM judge against reference sentences and 1–3 required key points, with penalties for redundancy, hallucination, and failure to remain silent (Secs. 2.2 and 4.1; Table 4).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that current systems remain unreliable across the benchmark’s fixed-path, offline interaction simulation. It does not establish effectiveness in live user interactions, causal reasons for model failures, or generalization beyond the selected scenarios. Cross-model comparisons are also partly conditioned on different preprocessing: non-Gemini models use 1 FPS, while the Gemini family does not follow that stated sampling rule (Sec. 4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated constraints:** Static data cannot adapt to arbitrary model-directed paths, so the benchmark supplies a single predefined route (Introduction; Sec. 2.2). Long inputs require FIFO deletion of old history, which can directly affect multi-turn performance (Sec. 4.1).
- **Author-stated evaluation caveat:** Gemini-3-Pro’s video-present output format can incur rubric penalties despite relevant content, showing sensitivity to response style (Sec. 4.3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Treating one valid route as ground truth improves comparability but may penalize assistants that propose another safe and effective route.
- **Analyst inference:** LLM-judge correlations above 0.75 demonstrate ranking agreement, not correctness against human judgments; no human–judge validation is supplied.
- **Analyst inference:** The three real-world scenarios are detailed but too few to support broad claims about deployment reliability.
- **Analyst inference:** Comparability is weakened by model-dependent video preprocessing and official model-specific inference configurations (Sec. 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Data provenance, licensing, public download availability, annotator agreement, and contamination checks are not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can evaluation allow multiple valid assistance paths?** The benchmark fixes one route to make offline scoring possible. A next experiment could annotate several safe routes per goal and compare path-aware scoring with the current single-route protocol.
2. **Would explicit memory mechanisms improve long-horizon interaction?** FIFO truncation discards older turns, and resolution reduction yields little overall gain. A controlled experiment could compare retrieval memory, state summaries, and unchanged-context baselines on process tracking and real-world cases.
3. **Can models distinguish user speech from background dialogue?** Visual-only input improves proactive-response results, suggesting audio-source confusion. A next experiment could add speaker enrollment or diarization and measure response timing and false activations.
4. **How well does automated scoring match human judgments?** Agreement among three LLM judges does not validate score accuracy. A blinded human study could measure judge–human correlation and disagreement by task and failure type.
