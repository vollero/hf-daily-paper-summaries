# Towards Faithful Simulation of Human Shopping Behavior

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20707) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20707)


## One-sentence takeaway

RecVerse combines learned hierarchical memory with trajectory-level reinforcement learning to simulate long-horizon shopping behavior from GUI screenshots, improving behavioral and intent alignment over the strongest reported GUI baseline while still remaining distinguishable from real users.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses realistic simulation of multi-turn e-commerce browsing for offline recommender evaluation and reinforcement-learning environments. The authors identify two central gaps: long sessions either lose earlier context or overload the model with raw history, while step-wise action imitation cannot penalize unrealistic session-wide patterns such as excessive exploration or passivity. Existing rule-, text-, and GUI-based simulators also fail to jointly model the visual interface, long-range user state, aggregate behavioral distributions, and fine-grained shopping intent (§1–2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **RecVerse:** A GUI-grounded agent that consumes page screenshots and jointly generates a user mindset, an environment action, and memory updates (§3.1; Figure 2).
- **Learned hierarchical memory:** Working Memory retains recent visual context; Episodic Memory records selected in-session events; Preference Memory distills evolving intent. Episodic and preference writes are treated as policy actions rather than rule-based operations (§3.2).
- **Trajectory-level optimization:** After imitation-learning warm-up, GRPO optimizes complete rollouts using a macro reward for action-count alignment, a micro reward for intent-weighted hierarchical-category alignment, and an output-validity reward (§3.3).
- **USB benchmark:** The authors introduce an interactive dataset of real GUI shopping sessions with screenshots, profiles, product metadata, eight action types, and executable alternative actions (§4.1.1; Tables 1–2; Appendix B.2).

The results support the authors’ claim that RecVerse improves intent overlap and perceived realism relative to the reported GUI baselines. They do not establish human-equivalent simulation: human raters still identify real trajectories as preferable in most RecVerse comparisons, and some behavioral dimensions—notably conversion rate—remain far from the real-user reference. The evidence is also confined to the paper’s USB marketplace setting.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** RecVerse remains distinguishable from real users; the authors attribute the remaining gap to insufficient personalization and modeling of individual preferences (§4.4).
- **Author-observed calibration issue:** Scaling improves several metrics but does not uniformly align behavior; IPVR falls below the real reference and ACR remains mildly high (§4.5.2; Figure 7).
- **Analyst inference:** USB’s concentration in Apparel (51.0% at L1) may limit evidence for performance across less frequent product domains (Appendix B.1; Figure 3).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation covers one major East Asian marketplace and its mobile homepage feed, so cross-platform, regional, and interface generalization are untested (§4.1.1; Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The ablation plot reports normalized values without exact scores, limiting quantitative assessment of each memory component and reward term (Figure 5).
- **Analyst inference:** The human-evaluation sample size, annotator count, sampling procedure, and demographic composition are absent from the supplied text, despite reported agreement statistics (§4.4; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** IL targets include teacher-generated latent mindsets and memory updates rather than observed human cognitive states, so the semantic faithfulness of those internal representations is not directly validated (Appendix C.2).
- **Reproducibility risk:** A condensed prompt is supplied, but the full prompt, data-access conditions, code availability, and benchmark license are not established (Table 5; Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the realism gap to humans be reduced through stronger personalization?** Real trajectories are still preferred in 74% of RecVerse comparisons. A next experiment could condition on longer user histories or learned user embeddings and repeat both blinded human evaluation and behavioral calibration.

2. **Does RecVerse generalize across markets and interfaces?** Evidence comes from one mobile marketplace feed. A cross-domain study could train on USB and evaluate on unseen regions, retailers, layouts, and product distributions without interface-specific fine-tuning.

3. **Which learned memory operations actually matter?** Figure 5 supports the complete hierarchy but does not isolate learned writing from comparable fixed or heuristic memory policies. A controlled ablation could hold memory capacity constant while comparing learned, periodic, salience-based, and no-write strategies.

4. **Can intent alignment improve without distorting behavior?** Table 4 shows that increasing the micro-reward weight improves intent scores while increasing trajectory-length error. A next experiment could test constrained or multi-objective optimization against a Pareto frontier over all five behavioral metrics and intent consistency.
