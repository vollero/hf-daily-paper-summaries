# InfinityEdit: Infinite Video Editing with a Lightweight Edit-Ignition Adapter

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20910) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20910)


## One-sentence takeaway

InfinityEdit adds a trainable three-attention adapter to a frozen streaming video generator, applying each instruction only to the next chunk and then using bounded history and anchor resetting to propagate edits through an open-ended stream.

## Problem and motivation

The paper introduces **infinite video editing**: given previous video frames and an edit instruction, generate the temporally subsequent segment while applying the edit, then repeat as new instructions arrive (Section 3.1, Eqs. 1–2).

The authors argue that existing in-place editors rewrite a fixed, temporally aligned clip, while prompt-switching generators lack explicit relational edit control. Neither directly addresses three requirements of ongoing streams: instruction alignment, faithful continuation of unaffected content, and stability as generated errors feed into later rounds (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task formulation:** Defines editing as sequential continuation rather than frame-aligned rewriting, including an unbounded sequence of instructions (Section 3.1).
- **Synthetic training pipeline:** Constructs `(preceding video, edit instruction, target continuation)` triplets from UltraVideo clips. Gemini expands edit types into scene-specific instructions; connection frames are either edited or preserved depending on edit category; Wan2.2-I2V-A14B generates targets; 20 annotators score candidates on four criteria (Section 4, Figure 1).
- **Edit-Ignition Adapter:** Freezes the 14B Helios-Distilled backbone and inserts adapter blocks containing history cross-attention, temporal causal self-attention, and edit cross-attention (Section 5.1, Figure 2, Eqs. 3–8).
- **Robustness-oriented training:** Uses history corruption, noise-level mixture sampling, and a two-phase curriculum progressing from broad edit acquisition to detail refinement (Sections 5.2.2–5.2.4).
- **Ignite-then-continue inference:** Enables the adapter only for the first chunk after each instruction; subsequent chunks use the frozen backbone. A sliding history bounds memory, while resetting the anchor frame and scene prompt helps retain the current edit (Section 5.3, Figure 3).

These results establish strong performance on the authors’ newly constructed benchmark and provide qualitative evidence beyond 1,000 frames. They do **not** establish literal unbounded stability, independent human preference, or generalization to other backbones and resolutions. Moreover, the VLM assessment uses a single named model as judge, and some baselines lack source-video conditioning, limiting the symmetry of those comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Control is limited to natural-language instructions; image or video references are unsupported (Section 8).
- Some edit boundaries remain abrupt despite coherent continuation (Section 8).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text**

- The main quantitative benchmark contains only three edit rounds, so it is limited evidence for the claimed unbounded regime (Section 6.1).
- The over-1,000-frame test is qualitative and appears to show a single highlighted sequence; no long-horizon metric or baseline comparison is reported (Section 6.4, Figure 7).
- No component ablation is supplied in the retrieved text, so the separate effects of the three attention modules, history corruption, curriculum, low-noise step, and anchor reset cannot be isolated.
- “Lightweight” is not quantified with adapter parameter count, training cost relative to full fine-tuning, latency, or memory overhead. The paper only states that training and evaluation used 32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs (Sections 5.1.2 and 6.1).
- Benchmark instructions and judging both rely on Gemini-family models, which may introduce correlated model-specific biases; no human evaluation of final outputs is reported (Sections 6.1 and 6.2.2).
- Prompt-switching baselines do not receive source videos, while InfinityEdit does. The paper flags this distinction, but it complicates direct preservation comparisons (Sections 6.1–6.2).
- The number of retained training triplets and train/test entity distributions are not reported, limiting reproducibility and coverage assessment (Sections 4 and 6.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which components are responsible for sequential stability?** The paper provides no component ablations. A next experiment could independently remove history corruption, anchor resetting, temporal causal attention, and the two-phase curriculum while tracking round-wise quality and faithfulness.

2. **How far does stability extend quantitatively?** The benchmark stops at three edits and the longer demonstration is qualitative. A next experiment could evaluate dozens of edits and thousands of frames using fixed checkpoints for identity, instruction retention, temporal quality, and failure rate.

3. **Can edit transitions be made gradual and controllable?** The authors acknowledge abrupt boundaries. A next experiment could compare temporal instruction interpolation or transition-specific training against first-chunk ignition using boundary smoothness and edit-latency measurements.

4. **Does the adapter transfer beyond Helios-Distilled?** Only one frozen 14B backbone is evaluated. A next experiment could attach the same adapter design to multiple streaming generators and compare quality, trainable parameters, inference overhead, and required retraining.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the task definition, architecture, reported benchmark setup, and tabulated results; **medium** for broad claims about unlimited stability.

Material missing information includes the training-set size, retained-triplet count, adapter parameter count, latency and memory overhead, detailed hyperparameters referenced only as being in an unavailable appendix, statistical uncertainty or significance tests, human evaluation of generated outputs, component ablations, and quantitative long-horizon comparisons.
