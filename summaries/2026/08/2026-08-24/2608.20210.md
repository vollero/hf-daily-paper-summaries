# Daedalus-150M: A Convolution-Attention Hybrid Designed for CPU Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20210) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20210)


## One-sentence takeaway

Daedalus-150M replaces two-thirds of its attention layers with fixed-state short convolutions, matching a parameter-matched all-attention model on downstream quality while decoding 1.76× faster at 2,048-token context on an 8-thread CPU.

## Problem and motivation

The paper targets batch-size-one autoregressive decoding on ordinary CPUs with 4-bit weights. In this regime, weight and key–value-cache traffic can dominate computation, while an all-attention decoder rereads a cache that grows with context in every layer.

The authors argue that small language models are commonly adapted from GPU-oriented architectures rather than designed for CPU memory behavior. Their identified gap is a controlled test of whether the ratio of cache-free to attention layers can improve long-context CPU decoding without materially reducing language-model quality (Sections I and II).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hybrid architecture:** Daedalus-150M has 18 blocks—12 depthwise short-convolution blocks and six interleaved full-attention blocks. Each convolution maintains only two timesteps of state, while the attention blocks use grouped-query attention (Section III; Fig. 1).
- **CPU-first design:** The layer ratio, tied embeddings, narrower feed-forward dimension, and Q4_0 deployment format are selected around batch-size-one CPU memory traffic and compatibility with stock inference binaries (Sections III, IV, and XII).
- **Controlled architectural experiment:** A 160.49M-parameter hybrid and 161.25M-parameter, 24-layer all-attention twin were trained for 5B tokens with identical data and schedules; the validation criterion and 0.5% winning margin were fixed before scoring (Section VII).
- **Context-dependent performance evidence:** Decode speed was measured at multiple context depths against both the internal twin and an external 135M-parameter peer, with alternating runs to reduce background-load bias (Sections VII-C, VII-D, and XI-B).
- **Negative and reproducibility findings:** The paper reports a failed quantization-aware training attempt, unusable structural pruning of inert convolution channels, training-run deviations, and published half-precision and 4-bit weights (Sections IX, X, and XIV).

The evidence supports the authors’ central claim within the tested setting: the hybrid preserves comparable quality while its decode advantage increases with context length. The matched twin, preregistered criterion, and alternating measurements strengthen the architectural attribution.

It does not establish seed-level statistical robustness, multilingual quality, behavior beyond 2,048 tokens, prompt-processing gains, or superiority on retrieval-sensitive tasks. Detailed absolute throughput values and per-task scores are not present in the supplied text, so they cannot be assessed here.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- All results come from one seed, so the 0.81% validation advantage is not a confidence interval (Section IX-E).
- Evaluation is English-only and stops at the trained 2,048-token context (Sections IX-E and XIII).
- The realized training mixture exceeded its preregistered drift limit: L1 distance was 10.42 percentage points against a limit of 10.0 (Section IX-A).
- A restart reset optimizer momentum and the data cursor; the final 4.8B tokens also used a corpus snapshot smaller by 0.42B tokens (Section IX-B).
- Quantization-aware training failed immediately and was not diagnosed, leaving an approximately 6% Q4_0 perplexity penalty (Section IX-C).
- The 49,152-token vocabulary consumes 37.7M parameters, or 23% of the model, and is acknowledged as oversized (Section IX-D).
- Nearly half the convolution channels are inert, but stock-runtime tensor constraints prevent structural pruning without sacrificing compatibility (Section X).
- Generation timing excludes prompt processing, and absolute throughput is affected by other machine load (Section XI-B).

**Analyst inferences**

- The central comparison changes both block type and depth—18 hybrid blocks versus 24 dense blocks—so the result does not isolate convolution versus attention independently of layer count. The authors identify the depth ablation as designed but unrun (Sections IV and XIII).
- The reported external-peer result is useful but narrow: the supplied text does not identify the peer or provide its detailed configuration.
- Retrieval capability is assumed to motivate retaining six attention layers but is not directly evaluated, limiting conclusions about whether six is the optimal number (Sections III-B and XIII).
- Reproducibility artifacts are claimed to be public, but their contents, licenses, hardware specification, and exact absolute timing values are unavailable in the supplied text (Section XIV).

## Open research questions

1. **How much attention can be removed before long-range retrieval degrades?**  
   This remains open because the paper does not run a retrieval-sensitive evaluation or vary the six-layer allocation. A next experiment could train matched hybrids with several attention fractions and evaluate associative-recall and document-retrieval tasks alongside CPU speed.

2. **How much of the gain comes from block type versus reduced depth?**  
   The hybrid has 18 blocks while the dense twin has 24. A controlled 18×768 all-attention comparison, or the authors’ proposed 18×768 versus 24×640 depth ablation, could separate these effects.

3. **Does the 0.81% validation advantage replicate across seeds?**  
   Only one seed was run. Repeating both arms across multiple seeds with the same preregistered rule would estimate uncertainty and test whether the margin is stable.

4. **Can deployment quality improve without sacrificing CPU throughput?**  
   Quantization-aware training failed, leaving a 6% perplexity cost. Diagnosing that failure and comparing QAT or alternative 4-bit formats under matched speed measurements would test the quality–throughput trade-off.
