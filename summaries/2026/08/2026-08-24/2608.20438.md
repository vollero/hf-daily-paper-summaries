# Peer-Voted LLM-Agent Stress Tests Find Feed-Induced Lexical Convergence but No Reliable Matched-Exposure Advantage for Distributed Sources

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20438) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20438)


## One-sentence takeaway

In synthetic LLM-agent populations, a peer-ranked feed modestly but consistently increased final-post lexical similarity, while four adversarial sources showed no reliable stance-shifting advantage over one source when exposure was matched.

## Problem and motivation

Single-agent benchmarks cannot measure recursive platform dynamics in which agents create posts, vote, receive ranked feedback, and then adapt their later outputs. The authors target two unresolved platform-risk questions: whether peer-feedback surfaces change population behavior, and whether multiple coordinated sources are inherently more persuasive than one source after controlling for exposure. They argue that prior simulations demonstrate feasibility but leave mechanism isolation and run-level auditability open (Introduction).

## Approach and contributions

- **PV-SST testbed:** Twelve persona-conditioned agents interact for six rounds, producing short structured posts, updating stance and confidence, and voting on peers. Posts, votes, exposures, updates, parse status, and deterministic request seeds are logged (§3.1; Figure 1).
- **Paired feed experiment:** A topic-only condition is compared with exposure to five previous-round peer posts ranked by endogenous likes. This estimates the combined feed surface, not ranking alone (§3.3).
- **Matched-exposure source experiment:** One adversarial source is compared with four distributed sources while fixing exposure at 60 adversarial impressions per trial. The contrast changes both source multiplicity and cross-viewer message realization (§3.3).
- **Preregistered multi-model evaluation:** The confirmatory matrix contains 448 trials across seven open-weight model variants, four platform-policy topics, four seeds, and four conditions, analyzed as 112 paired blocks (§3.2–§3.5).
- **Audit and measurement evidence:** The authors report full run-level traces, exposure checks, parse-failure rates, and exploratory failures of keyword, embedding, and LLM-judge misinformation measures (§3.5; §5).

The evidence supports a modest lexical-convergence effect for the complete peer-ranked feed under the tested synthetic setting. It does not isolate ranking from peer-post exposure, show that agents converged in stance, establish general minority suppression, demonstrate an inherent advantage from account multiplicity, or estimate effects on humans or production platforms. Feed p-values are nominal secondary-outcome tests rather than family-wise adjusted primary tests (§3.5; §6.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The agents do not model human emotion, account deletion, long-term relationships, off-platform information, or platform-specific recommenders; no effect magnitude should be transferred to people or named platforms (§6.3).
- **Author-stated:** The feed contrast bundles peer-post exposure with like-based ranking, preventing a ranking-only interpretation (§3.3; §6.3).
- **Author-stated:** The distributed treatment bundles account multiplicity with independently generated message realizations, so it does not isolate account count (§3.3; §6.3).
- **Author-stated:** Coverage is limited to seven open-weight variants and four platform-policy topics; proprietary frontier models and other domains are untested (§6.3).
- **Author-stated:** Blocks are simulation replications, not independent real platforms, and feed outcomes use nominal, unadjusted p-values (§3.5; §6.3).
- **Author-stated:** Simulation-to-human transfer is uncalibrated; the human replay used only synthetic placeholders (§6.3).
- **Author-stated:** Exploratory semantic measures suffered paraphrase leakage, stance confounding, and poor inter-judge agreement (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Although deterministic seeds and extensive audit counts are reported, the supplied text does not provide artifact links, licenses, prompt templates, sampling parameters, or hardware-runtime details beyond an RTX 5090, limiting reproducibility assessment.
- **Analyst inference:** Four seeds and six interaction rounds leave uncertainty about sensitivity to stochastic variation and longer-term dynamics; no seed-count or duration ablation is reported.

## Open research questions

1. **How much convergence comes from peer exposure versus popularity ranking?** The current feed treatment changes both. A next experiment could compare topic-only, unranked peer-feed, randomly ranked feed, and like-ranked feed arms.

2. **Does account multiplicity matter when message realization is held constant?** The distributed treatment also changes cross-viewer message realization. A factorial experiment could independently vary account count, message diversity, and exposure.

3. **Does lexical convergence predict semantic or stance convergence?** TF-IDF overlap does not establish agreement, while exploratory semantic measures were unreliable. A follow-up could use blinded human annotation or a validated stance-aware metric alongside lexical similarity.

4. **Do the effects persist across broader populations and longer interactions?** The confirmation is limited to open-weight models, policy topics, 12 agents, and six rounds. A preregistered extension could vary model provenance, domain, population size, network structure, and duration.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported design, numerical results, and stated scope because the supplied text includes detailed methods, pooled estimates, uncertainty intervals, and validity qualifications.

Materially unavailable information includes the code/data link and license; full preregistration text; complete prompts and persona definitions; decoding and sampling settings beyond disabled reasoning-token emission; per-model and per-topic numerical tables; sign-test results; runtime and compute cost; raw artifact contents; and any human-validation data.
