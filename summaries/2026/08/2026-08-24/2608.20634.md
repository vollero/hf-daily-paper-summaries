# AgentMercury: Your Agent Can Synthesize Verifiable Environments for Business Scenarios at scale

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20634) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20634)


## One-sentence takeaway

AgentMercury generates persistent, executable business worlds with deterministic verification, and the authors report that these worlds improve agent policies across enterprise and out-of-domain tasks while their construction traces teach a model to author new environments.

## Problem and motivation

Agent environments are commonly built around predefined tasks or benchmarks, coupling world design to specific evaluation objectives. The authors argue that this limits environmental diversity and does not reflect evolving workflows involving persistent state, multiple services, tools, and cross-system constraints (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

AgentMercury instead addresses scenario-grounded world construction: converting a high-level business scenario into a reusable executable environment from which multiple tasks and trajectories can subsequently emerge. This matters because scalable, verifiable worlds could supply broader training signals without being tailored to downstream benchmarks.

## Approach and contributions

- **Scenario-to-world synthesis:** A proposed `PLANET` role constructs a world containing entities, services, tools, schemas, seeded state, executable transitions, and world-level invariants (Section 3.2, Eqs. 6–9).
- **Separation of world, task, and policy:** Tasks are instantiated afterward by adding task-specific state, an instruction, and a rubric, allowing one world to support multiple objectives (Sections 3.1 and 3.3; Figure 2).
- **Deterministic interaction and grading:** Seeded states, executable transitions, and preferably database-level verification permit trajectory replay and post-episode grading against task and hidden world constraints (Sections 3.4–3.5).
- **Large synthesized collection:** The authors report 4,783 environments across 14 industries and 50 countries; the released RL corpus contains 43,300 tasks from 4,326 of those environments (Figure 1; Appendix A, Tables 4–5).
- **Policy and authoring training:** The environments support GRPO/SAO policy optimization, while 29,823 construction-trace samples supervise brief-to-world generation, partial completion, repair, and intent-to-diff prediction (Sections 4.1–4.4; Appendix E).
- **Released assets:** The paper claims release of environments, scenarios, task instructions, construction differences/code, and trained policy models (Introduction, p. 3).

These results support the authors’ claim that training in the synthesized worlds correlates with improvements across heterogeneous evaluations and that construction traces can teach structural authoring. They do not isolate which environment properties cause transfer, establish superiority over alternative synthetic-environment training corpora, or demonstrate deployment validity in real businesses. The three-run evaluations quantify run variation but do not establish statistical significance for most policy comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** The system does not close the environment–agent loop: synthesis is offline and does not use a learned world model or policy failures to choose new scenarios (Section 5).
- **Author-stated optimization boundary:** SAO was unstable or insufficiently informative for the 4B model and was omitted from the main comparison (Section 5; Appendix B).
- **Corpus redundancy and restricted structure:** Task language is template-like; median nearest-neighbor TF-IDF similarity is 0.83, 8.3% of tasks exceed 0.99, and tool counts occupy only five discrete values (Appendix A.1; Figure 6).
- **Synthetic scope:** Both training environments and held-out authoring briefs are synthetic and drawn from the environment library’s country–industry distribution, leaving real-world fidelity untested (Section 4.4; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference — causal attribution:** No comparison against equal-compute RL on alternative environments, static tasks, or a matched task-centric corpus is supplied, so the gains cannot be uniquely attributed to scenario-grounded construction.
- **Analyst inference — reward purity:** Although grading is described as preferably programmatic, RL reward configuration includes DeepSeek-V4-Flash as a reward judge; the supplied text does not quantify its contribution relative to database assertions (Tables 8–9).
- **Internal reporting inconsistency:** Table 3 lists four API models with means of **78.3%/77.5%**, whereas Sections 4.4 and Appendix E describe five API models with means of **80.7%/78.0%**; the supplied text does not resolve this discrepancy.
- **Analyst inference — authoring sample size:** Authoring conclusions rest on 30 held-out briefs, limiting precision across industries, countries, and structural patterns despite the reported significance test.

## Open research questions

1. **Which aspects of the synthesized worlds produce transfer?** The paper combines persistent state, service diversity, tools, invariants, and task sampling without component ablations. A next experiment could hold task count and compute fixed while independently removing cross-service invariants, persistent state, or service diversity.

2. **Does scenario-grounded synthesis outperform matched task-centric generation?** No equal-scale environment-generation baseline is reported. A controlled study could train identical policies on matched numbers of scenario-grounded and task-conditioned worlds using the same optimizer and verifier.

3. **Do synthetic-world gains transfer to real enterprise systems?** Evaluation remains benchmark- and simulator-based. A next study could test the trained policy on sandboxed replicas of real workflows with human-reviewed outcomes and operational safety measures.

4. **Can environment generation become an adaptive curriculum?** The authors explicitly leave the policy-to-`PLANET` feedback loop open. An experiment could generate scenarios from measured policy failures, then compare adaptive selection against random scenario sampling under a fixed environment budget.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text is extensive and includes methods, configurations, benchmark protocols, and numerical tables. Material gaps include unresolved authoring-table inconsistencies, detailed construction-model prompts and fine-tuning hyperparameters, per-validator results, environment-generation costs, contamination checks for synthetic tasks, matched environment-generation baselines, most policy-result significance tests, and evidence of real-business fidelity or safety.
