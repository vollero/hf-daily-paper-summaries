# Partition the Support, Reconstruct the Residual: Training-Free Sparse Attention for Video Generation and World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18484) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18484)


## One-sentence takeaway

SparsePR combines response-based token grouping with per-call residual calibration to preserve near-dense video quality at 21.92–25.96% executed-pair density while delivering 1.48×–2.61× end-to-end speedups across four evaluated models.

## Problem and motivation

Training-free sparse attention could reduce the quadratic inference cost of long video-transformer sequences, but two gaps make row-wise sparsity insufficient for executable block sparsity: queries sharing a route may require poorly overlapping key supports, and retained attention mass does not uniquely determine post-softmax output error. The authors argue that partition geometry must therefore account for both shared-route support and whether skipped-interaction residuals can be reconstructed (Sections 1 and 3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Response-Coupled Partitioning:** Sampled-query responses define low-dimensional coordinates for clustering paired K/V tokens; the resulting key-group centroids then define normalized query-response coordinates for query clustering. This is an online, asymmetric single pass rather than alternating partition refinement (Sections 4.1, B.1–B.2).
- **Probe-Fitted Residual Reconstruction:** For each attention call and query head, 64 stratified query rows receive exact attention. Their dense-minus-sparse residuals fit a weighted ridge affine map from sparse outputs, projected onto a rank-16 subspace observed in the probe residuals (Sections 4.2, A.2–A.3).
- **Exact accounting and evaluation:** Probe costs are included in executed-pair density, while latency includes partitioning, routing, permutation, probes, fitting, correction, and output restoration (Section 4.3). The method is tested on four heterogeneous video-generation and world models (Section 5).

These results support the authors’ claim that both partitioning and residual reconstruction improve sparse-attention fidelity, with reconstruction contributing the larger measured gain. They also establish end-to-end acceleration under the reported hardware and model configurations. They do not establish generalization beyond the four evaluated video models, other hardware, training workloads, or attention regimes outside full-sequence inference.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The work considers full-sequence attention, not autoregressive K/V-cache compression or retrieval (Section 2).
- **Author-stated modeling boundary:** The affine residual predictor is an empirical local model for one attention call; the omitted statistics are not assumed to be uniquely determined by the sparse output (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference — narrow systems evaluation:** All experiments use BF16 on one NVIDIA H100, so portability of speedups to other accelerators or precisions is untested in the supplied text (Section 5; Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference — limited model/task coverage:** Evidence covers four video models at 720p; no language, audio, multimodal-understanding, or non-video transformer evaluation is reported (Section 5; Table 1).
- **Analyst inference — incomplete statistical evidence:** The supplied text does not report sample counts, confidence intervals, variance across runs, or significance tests for benchmark-quality differences.
- **Analyst inference — comparison ambiguity:** Some HunyuanVideo and Wan2.2 baselines are values reported by prior work, while others are reproduced; Table 1 marks provenance, but complete comparability details and baseline tuning are not supplied.
- **Analyst inference — ablation coverage:** Probe count is fixed at 64 and principal ranks at 48/64/16 in the reported experiments. Probe-selection and projection choices are ablated, but sensitivity to probe count, ridge coefficient, and response-coordinate ranks is not shown (Tables 4 and C.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How does accuracy and runtime scale with the exact-probe budget?** Only 64 probes per query head are reported. A next experiment could sweep probe counts while measuring error, density, fitting stability, and end-to-end latency across all four models.

2. **Do the learned per-call corrections transfer across nearby layers or denoising steps?** The estimator is recalibrated for every active call, and temporal reuse is explicitly outside the method. Comparing per-call fitting against safely reused or incrementally updated fits would test whether calibration overhead can be reduced.

3. **How robust is SparsePR across hardware and sparse kernels?** Reported speedups come from one H100 implementation using a variable-block sparse primitive. Repeating matched-density tests on other accelerators and kernel implementations would separate algorithmic gains from hardware-specific efficiency.

4. **Does response-coupled partitioning help outside full-sequence video attention?** The paper does not evaluate autoregressive cache attention or non-video transformers. A controlled study on those regimes could test whether shared-support and residual-representability findings generalize.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported table values because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.
