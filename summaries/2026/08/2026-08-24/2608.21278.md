# CLEAR: Continuous Latent Adapter Routing for Utility-Preserving LLM Safety Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21278) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21278)


## One-sentence takeaway

CLEAR conditionally scales a safety LoRA using a prompt-level hidden-state gate, sharply reducing harmful completions while preserving more benign-task utility than always-on SFT or LoRA in the tested open-weight LLMs.

## Problem and motivation

Global safety tuning applies the same aligned parameters to harmful and benign inputs, potentially causing over-refusal and degrading reasoning—the “alignment tax.” The authors identify a gap between always-on model updates and external moderation: neither provides an integrated, continuously varying intervention that preserves the frozen model’s behavior when risk is low (Introduction; Background, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Continuous latent routing:** A prompt-only MLP gate predicts \(g(x)\in[0,1]\), scaling a safety LoRA as \(h'=(W+g(x)\Delta W)h\). The backbone remains frozen, and one sequence-level score controls all adapted `q_proj` and `v_proj` modules throughout decoding (Method, pp. 3–4).
- **Joint risk and behavior learning:** The gate and adapter are jointly optimized, while the adapter learns only from unsafe prompts paired with safe responses (Method; Algorithm 1, pp. 4–5).
- **Gate-separation objectives:** Subtype-weighted binary cross-entropy emphasizes adversarial benign/harmful prompts, while a hard pairwise margin loss pushes unsafe scores above safe scores (Method, p. 4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The paper compares safety, over-refusal, utility, routing cost, scaling behavior, and an exploratory latent-attack defense across several open-weight model families (Tables 1–4, 7–9; Figure 3).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that conditional routing can improve the measured safety–utility balance relative to globally applied tuning in these settings. It does not establish universal superiority: some baselines win individual metrics, CLEAR can increase over-refusal, and all principal results concern specific benchmarks, judges, training data, and open-weight text models. Reported main results are medians over three runs unless otherwise stated, but uncertainty intervals and significance tests are not supplied (Experimental Setting, p. 5).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Safety depends critically on gate reliability: false positives cause unnecessary intervention, while false negatives can bypass the adapter (Conclusion, p. 7; Limitations, p. 10).
- Evaluation primarily uses relatively small open-weight models; generalization to frontier-scale or multimodal systems is unknown (Limitations, p. 10).
- Unseen jailbreak styles and distribution shifts may evade a gate trained on the available safety distribution (Limitations, p. 10).
- The method covers single-turn text safety, not multi-turn attacks, long-horizon deception, or broader deployment risks (Limitations, p. 10).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- HarmBench results inherit dependence on one classifier judge, while XSTest refusal uses phrase-based string matching; neither protocol fully measures semantic harmfulness or helpfulness (Evaluation Metrics, pp. 13–14).
- The external-guard comparison is not fully task-matched: the authors explicitly note that PromptGuard targets prompt injection rather than harmful-versus-benign routing (Gate Quality Analysis, p. 6).
- The adaptive-attack result combines CLEAR with an additional gate-adaptive PCA projection, so it does not isolate continuous adapter routing alone; it is also limited to 100 Gemma HarmBench prompts and one reported perturbation budget (pp. 11–12).
- Although three-run medians are reported generally, dispersion and statistical significance are absent from the supplied text (p. 5).
- No code or checkpoint release is stated, although checkpoints, hyperparameters, hardware, and evaluation tooling are documented (pp. 10–13).

## Open research questions

1. **How well does the gate calibrate under unseen jailbreak distributions?** Distribution shift is an acknowledged bypass risk. A next experiment could train on WildJailbreak and evaluate gate calibration, false negatives, and generated safety on held-out attack families without retraining.

2. **Can token- or turn-level rerouting improve multi-turn safety?** CLEAR computes one prompt-level score and holds it fixed during generation, while multi-turn interactions are outside scope. A next experiment could compare fixed sequence routing with gate recomputation per turn or at selected decoding steps.

3. **Does CLEAR scale to frontier-sized or multimodal models?** The evaluated models are open-weight text systems up to 8B parameters. A next experiment could reproduce matched SFT/LoRA/CLEAR comparisons on larger backbones and image–text safety tasks.

4. **Which component drives latent-attack robustness?** The reported defense adds PCA projection to gated routing. A factorial experiment could independently ablate the gate, routed LoRA, static PCA, and gate-adaptive PCA across multiple perturbation budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, reported benchmark values, and stated scope because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.
