# Let&#x27;s Scale Step by Step: Compute-Efficient Hyperparameter Transfer for Large-Scale Mixture-of-Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-08-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20061) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20061)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors combine MoE-adapted maximal-update parameterization with a token-budget scaling law to predict a learning rate for 155B-parameter MoE pretraining from smaller proxy runs, although full-scale optimality is not directly verified.

## Problem and motivation

Learning-rate tuning for large Mixture-of-Experts language models is prohibitively expensive because the optimum can change with both model scale and training-token budget. Existing µP transfer methods primarily address width scaling in dense models, while practical MoE scaling also changes sparsity by increasing total experts with a fixed active-expert count. The authors identify two gaps: whether learning rates transfer along this coupled width–sparsity path, and whether short proxy runs can predict suitable rates for trillion-token horizons (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **MoE-specific µP adaptation:** Parameters are classified as vector-like or matrix-like; µP initialization is applied accordingly, while learning-rate scaling is restricted to matrix-like hidden weights. The formulation covers MLA-based MoEs trained with Muon (Section 2.1; Tables 1–2).

- **Coupled width–sparsity transfer:** Model width, attention-head count, and total experts grow together, while depth, active experts, and expert intermediate dimension remain fixed. Experiments test zero-shot transfer from a 0.6B-total-parameter proxy through models up to 30.7B parameters (Sections 2.1 and 3.2; Figure 3; Table 3).

- **Token-horizon extrapolation:** At each proxy token budget, validation loss is fit as a quadratic function of log learning rate. The estimated optima are then regressed against token budget in log-log space and extrapolated to the target horizon (Sections 2.2.1–2.2.2, Equations 2–3).

- **Efficient checkpoint reuse:** Constant-rate proxy runs use EMA checkpoints to estimate optima at multiple token budgets without conducting a separate decay run for every budget (Section 2.2.1).

- **Large-scale application:** The resulting rate is used to pretrain a 155B-total/17B-active MoE for 10T tokens, with training stability and downstream evaluations offered as practical—rather than definitive optimality—evidence (Section 3.3.3; Figures 6–8).

The evidence supports transfer across the tested coupled width–sparsity configurations and accurate **shorter-range** prediction near 500B tokens. Stable 10T-token training shows that the extrapolated rate is workable. It does not establish that \(3.85\times10^{-4}\) is the true 10T optimum, because no competing full-scale learning rates were trained. Likewise, \(R^2=0.95\) measures fit on proxy-budget observations rather than direct accuracy at 10T tokens.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** A full learning-rate sweep at the 155B/10T target is computationally infeasible, so full-scale optimality is unverified (Conclusion; Appendix E).

- **Author-stated:** Results are restricted to MLA-based MoEs and Muon; transfer to other MoE architectures or optimizers remains unknown (Conclusion).

- **Author-stated:** Width and sparsity are expanded jointly, preventing the independent effect of expert-count sparsity from being disentangled (Conclusion).

- **Author-stated:** Depth is fixed because depth transfer under µP is considered unstable; the method therefore does not validate depth scaling (Section 2.1).

- **Author-stated:** Batch size is not transferred. Proxy fitting also excludes points immediately following a batch-size increase (Sections 2.2 and 2.2.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The 10T prediction extrapolates far beyond the 255B–502B regression range. Table 4 validates only extrapolation from roughly 350B to 500B, not from 502B to 10T.

- **Analyst inference:** EMA checkpoints substitute for completed decay schedules. The paper motivates this using the large 32M-token batch regime, but does not present a direct EMA-versus-WSD-decay ablation for this model family.

- **Analyst inference:** Data and training infrastructure are internal, and the supplied text does not state that code, proxy losses, model weights, or corpora are released, limiting independent reproduction.

- **Analyst inference:** The selected-baseline Pareto comparison depends on estimated \(6ND\) compute and reported token counts; exact benchmark scores and uncertainty are unavailable in the retrieved text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does learning-rate transfer hold when sparsity changes independently of width?**  
   The experiment couples hidden dimension and expert count. A controlled study could fix width and active experts while varying only total experts, then repeat identical rate sweeps.

2. **How accurate is the token law at genuinely trillion-token held-out horizons?**  
   Validation stops near 500B while the target is 10T. A plausible experiment is to fit below 500B and run several proxy models to 1T–2T at predicted and neighboring rates.

3. **Does the procedure generalize beyond MLA and Muon?**  
   The paper explicitly restricts its main evidence to this combination. The same proxy/target protocol could be repeated with standard multi-head attention and AdamW or another optimizer.

4. **Can EMA-based proxy optima reliably predict decayed-schedule optima across batch sizes?**  
   EMA replaces decay during proxy estimation, and the justification is tied to a large-batch setting. A factorial experiment could compare EMA and completed WSD decay at several token budgets and global batch sizes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains the complete retrieved paper and clearly describes the method, scaling configurations, and principal reported results. However, exact values underlying several plots—especially transfer losses, final benchmark scores, and Figure 8 comparisons—are unavailable in the text. Also missing or not established are confidence intervals or multi-seed variability, detailed corpus composition for proxy experiments, regression coefficients, complete checkpoint-level observations, hardware counts and wall-clock costs, and any public release of code, data, or model weights.
