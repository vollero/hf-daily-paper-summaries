# CoVeR: Coverage-Based Token Pruning for Multi-View 3D Reasoning in VLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08345) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08345)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoVeR uses geometry-only token selection to enforce exact per-scene budgets, reporting 93.5% of full-token aggregate performance at roughly 8–9% retention across three indoor 3D QA benchmarks with LLaVA-OneVision-7B (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation

Multi-view images let pretrained 2D VLMs answer questions about 3D scenes, but overlapping views generate redundant visual tokens and increase inference cost. The authors identify two shortcomings in existing pruning: learned importance can concentrate selections on repeated observations of prominent regions, while voxelization produces scene-dependent token counts and saturates at practical spatial resolutions (§§1, 3.2).

The target is therefore spatial coverage with an exact token budget for every scene, supporting predictable resource use.

## Approach and contributions

- **Geometry-only selection:** Back-project visual tokens from posed RGB-D views into world coordinates. Formulate selection as discrete Euclidean k-center: minimize the greatest distance from an original token to its nearest retained token (§3.1).
- **Two-stage construction:** Adapt voxel size per scene, retain an original token near each occupied voxel’s center, then repeatedly add the farthest unselected token until the budget is filled. A safeguard handles initialization exceeding the budget (§§3.3–3.5).
- **Budget and coverage guarantees:** Exact cardinality is guaranteed for feasible budgets. When the safeguard is inactive, coverage distance is bounded by the voxel diagonal and cannot increase during expansion. The approximation guarantee compares against the optimum at the **expansion budget**, not the full budget (Appendix B).
- **Empirical contribution:** Coverage analyses, component ablations, and evaluations across four VLMs support the proposed combination. Voxelization and farthest-point sampling are existing ingredients; the contribution is their budget-controlled integration and evaluation for multi-view VLM pruning (§§4.1–4.4).

All tokens still pass through the visual encoder and projector; pruning reduces the tokens entering the language model (§3.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong aggregate performance under aggressive pruning | Twelve-view LLaVA-OV-7B: reported relative aggregate performance is 93.5%, versus SeGPruner’s 89.6% and VisPruner’s 85.9%, at 9% ScanQA and 8% OpenEQA/SQA3D retention. | Table 2 |
| Gains differ across tasks | At those budgets, CoVeR versus SeGPruner achieves ScanQA EM@1 27.1 vs. 24.5; OpenEQA GPT-4o LLM-Match 53.0 vs. 52.5; SQA3D EM@1 48.6 vs. 48.4. | Tables 2, A5 |

| Selection itself is slower than learned baselines | Same 9% ScanQA setting: CoVeR pruning takes 0.034 s, versus 0.008 s for SeGPruner and 0.010 s for VisPruner, while peak memory is 17.2 vs. 22.1 GB. | Table 6 |
| Better measured spatial coverage | ScanQA at 9% retention: CoVeR versus SeGPruner yields NNI 0.924 vs. 0.458 and normalized worst-case coverage NND₁₀₀ 0.977 vs. 0.917. Authors report significant paired-test differences. | §4.2, Table 3 |
| Additional-backbone comparison is mixed | Video-3D LLM, 16 views, 10% retention: CoVeR versus Geo3DPruner achieves ScanQA EM@1 26.5 vs. 26.0, but SQA3D 55.1 vs. 55.7; their full-token baselines differ. | Table 7 |

These results support a favorable accuracy–resource tradeoff in the evaluated settings. They do not establish universal superiority across individual tasks or categories. Coverage and accuracy move together, but geometric proximity alone does not demonstrate preservation of equivalent visual or answer-critical information.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Requires depth and camera information, targets indoor scenes, and may depend on estimated geometry quality (§5). Aggressive pruning may remove important cues (Appendix F).
- **Comparison limits:** VTC/DTC and Geo3DPruner lack publicly available official code according to §4; comparisons follow reported protocols or published scores. VisPruner and SeGPruner are reproduced in the same environment (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** The benchmarks share underlying indoor datasets, limiting evidence for broader environmental generalization (Appendix A.1). No geometry-corruption experiment is supplied.
- **Theory limits:** Voxel-size search is heuristic because occupancy is not monotonic. The voxel-diagonal bound can fail when the safeguard drops occupied voxels (§§3.4, 3.6).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** The tightest-budget recovery radius differs between §4.2/Figure 4 and Appendix C. Table 8’s reported ScanQA averages also do not reconcile directly with Table 2’s component scores at matching budgets; the text does not explain this.
- **Analyst inference—statistical certainty:** Coverage significance is asserted without supplied test statistics or p-values; downstream accuracy uncertainty is not reported.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is selection to inaccurate geometry?** Geometry dependence is acknowledged but not tested explicitly. Perturb depth and camera poses while measuring coverage, QA accuracy, and retained-token stability.
2. **When does spatial coverage miss critical visual details?** Nearby coordinates do not establish equivalent appearance or visibility. Evaluate questions requiring small attributes or viewpoint-specific evidence, comparing geometry-only selection with a controlled appearance-aware variant.
3. **Can the method scale to outdoor or streaming scenes?** These are proposed future directions (§5). Test hierarchical or incremental selection with fixed memory, reporting accuracy, coverage, and latency as observations accumulate.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The algorithm, evaluation splits, package versions, and principal comparisons are documented, but reporting inconsistencies and comparison differences constrain stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes a confirmed code release, downstream confidence intervals, coverage-test statistics, geometry-noise evaluations, detailed numerical Qwen results beyond figure captions and prose, and implementation conventions for singleton voxels and deterministic tie-breaking.
