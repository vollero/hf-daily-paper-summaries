# Recognition-Refusal Misalignment in LLMs: Why Models Answer Structurally Unanswerable Questions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29109) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29109)


## One-sentence takeaway
A simple probe detects structural impossibility before generation, and steering its direction changes invalidity-aware behavior, supporting—but not fully proving—the authors’ explanation that models fail to route recognition into abstention.

## Problem and motivation
Models can produce ordinary answers to mathematically undefined operations or Python expressions that raise exceptions. The authors ask whether these failures reflect absent recognition or failure to act on an internally available signal. Their identified gap is mechanistic: output-level abstention evaluations do not establish how impossibility recognition relates to trained safety refusal (§§1–2).

The headline scope is formally checkable structural impossibility; missing-evidence and false-premise questions are separate boundary tests.

## Approach and contributions
- **Recognition diagnostic:** CosNSRT removes the top 100 principal components fitted on answerable training states, then uses a normalized unanswerable-minus-answerable mean direction and cosine scoring. Evaluation uses class-stratified held-out splits and five seeds (§3).
- **Geometric comparison:** The study compares recognition with a harmful-versus-harmless safety-refusal direction and an in-domain direction contrasting invalidity-aware responses with ordinary answers. Same-space and multidimensional controls test whether separation is a projection artifact (§4.2; Appendices K–L).
- **Behavioral intervention and training-stage comparison:** Generation-time activation steering tests whether recognition affects behavior; paired base/instruct checkpoints test whether geometric separation precedes instruction tuning (§§4.3–4.4).
- **Evaluation assets:** The authors report releasing matched math800/code800 datasets, code, aggregate artifacts, and analysis scripts. The contribution is their combined diagnostic investigation; MeanDiff refusal extraction and activation steering are adopted methods (§§3, 6; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Structural impossibility is accessible to a simple probe | Across 11 models × math800/code800, five-seed held-out mean AUC is **0.939**, range **0.841–0.993**; identical MeanDiff/cosine scoring gives **0.907** in full space and **0.890** in top answerable PCs. | §4.1; Table 2 |
| Recognition and safety refusal have low directional overlap | Across the same 22 cells, mean cosine is **0.087**; projecting both directions into answerable-null space gives **0.097**. Safety-refusal behavior verification passes in **20/22** cells; two use proxies. | §4.2; Fig. 2 |
| Recognition is closer to observed invalidity-aware behavior | On the six primary structural behavior cells, recognition–behavior mean cosine is **0.401**, versus **0.127** for recognition–safety refusal in that same subset; behavior–safety cosine is **0.076**. | Appendix K; Table 20 |
| Steering changes behavior in both directions | Mistral-7B’s best-dose signal-minus-random gated effects are **+32.7/+37.9 pp** for math and **+35.4/+44.4 pp** for code, ordered answerable→invalidity-aware / unanswerable→answer-like. Doses are selected from the tested sweep. | §4.3; Tables 16–17 |
| Causal effects vary substantially | Code exceeds the authors’ **+30 pp** criterion in both directions for **3/4** anchors; only Mistral does so in both structural domains. Qwen3-14B math remains below criterion in both directions. | §4.3; Fig. 3 |
| Separation exists before instruction tuning | Six math800 base/instruct pairs show mean instruct-minus-base cosine change **+0.037**, range **−0.008 to +0.110**; base recognition mean AUC is **0.977**. Five pairs are fully verified; one base refusal direction is a proxy. | §4.4; Fig. 2; Table 22 |
| Epistemic behavior does not cleanly replicate structural results | fact800 answerable→invalidity-aware effects are **+4 to +24 pp** across four anchors; reverse-direction baseline gates contain only **0, 1, 4, and 2** examples. | §4.3; Table 19 |

These results establish a readable signal and a causal intervention handle in the tested structural settings. They do not establish that normal generation routes through the measured directions, exhaust all abstention mechanisms, or demonstrate a useful selective-abstention intervention: inducing invalidity claims on answerable prompts is itself an error. The authors’ routing account is supported mechanistic interpretation, rather than a complete traced circuit.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Behavioral evidence is concentrated in four anchors and controlled structural prompts. External transfer is detection-only; the broader deterministic sweep uses a different, non-comparable metric (§4.3; Limitations).
- **Author-stated measurement risk:** Labels are LLM-assisted, with provisional second-pass review in some cells and candidate-only labels for Qwen3-8B. Mixed outputs and degeneration materially alter effects (Appendix J).
- **Author-stated representation limits:** Single directions and limited linear-subspace checks do not exclude nonlinear or distributed abstention routes. PCA dimensionality and layer selection are heuristic; data generation and some PCA results are nondeterministic (Limitations; Appendices A, D, K).
- **Analyst inference:** Best-dose reporting and high-dose degeneration limit conclusions about reliable operating points. A held-out dose-selection evaluation is needed before treating steering as a practical remedy (Tables 16–17).
- **Analyst inference:** Random class-stratified splits do not establish unseen-template or unseen-category generalization; the supplied protocol does not clearly specify whether matched pairs stay together across splits (§3; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **Can recognition improve selective abstention?** Steering demonstrates behavioral control, including erroneous abstention. Next experiment: train a conditional recognition-based intervention and evaluate both impossible-question handling and answerable-question accuracy on held-out data (§7; Appendix F).
- **What actually transmits recognition into behavior?** Directional geometry does not exhaust generation circuits. Next experiment: activation patching across layers and candidate downstream components, testing necessity as well as sufficiency (§4.2; Appendix K).
- **Does recognition generalize beyond construction patterns?** Current structural datasets use matched, generated templates. Next experiment: hold out entire templates and operation categories, then evaluate on independently authored prompts (Appendix A).
- **Why do models differ in steering tolerance?** The proposed tolerance explanation is post hoc and confounded by layer depth and training. Next experiment: compare matched depths and perturbation norms across controlled checkpoints (Appendix J.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial quantitative evidence, controls, and explicit caveats; confidence is stronger in recognition and measured geometry than in a complete routing explanation.

Material missing information includes intervention-effect confidence intervals, complete independent human label validation, clear pair-grouping rules for splits, detailed safety-direction verification criteria, and held-out operating-point performance. Reported repository contents were not independently inspected.
