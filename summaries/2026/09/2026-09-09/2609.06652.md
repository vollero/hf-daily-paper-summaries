# VidaForge: Open Research Infrastructure for Video Pretraining Data Recipes

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06652) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06652)


## One-sentence takeaway
VidaForge provides traceable video-data pipelines and reports that broader mixed-quality data outperforms stricter selection on downstream benchmarks in matched-budget early pretraining of Wan and V-JEPA, although the comparison also changes data reuse (§§ 2–3, G.4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify an infrastructure barrier: video pretraining pipelines rarely expose enough processing details or sample histories to reproduce and compare data recipes. Researchers must build ingestion, curation, annotation, and training interfaces before testing how data choices affect models (§ 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Executable, traceable infrastructure:** Five stages—ingestion, segmentation, selection, annotation, and packaging—retain intermediate assets, parameters, sample decisions, and run dependencies. Changed selection thresholds can reuse existing scores and duplicate relations (§ 2; Appendix B).
- **Controlled recipe study:** Mixed, Selected, and Rejected datasets share upstream processing and annotation. Within each model family, training settings and processed-clip budgets are fixed; Mixed encounters more distinct clips, while the other recipes repeat smaller pools (§§ 3.1, G.1).
- **Reusable dataset:** The authors report releasing VidaForge-3M with scene clips, automatic camera annotations, multilevel captions, semantic tags, quality measurements, and duplicate-group records. It retains every annotation-complete clip, including duplicates, to support alternative recipes (§§ E.1, E.4–E.5).

The infrastructure contribution is the integrated processing-history and training workflow; individual filtering, annotation, and deduplication components use existing tools and models (Appendices A–B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Final generation evaluation favors Mixed | Wan, 5,950 updates, batch size 96, three training seeds, five generations per official prompt: VBench Total **60.64±0.83** Mixed, **59.74±0.55** Selected, **59.42±1.06** Rejected; mean±std. Mixed leads each matched seed. | § G.2, Tables 9–10 |
| Final representation evaluation favors Mixed | V-JEPA, 2,280 updates, batch size 256: frozen attentive-probe SSv2 top-1 **16.256±0.153%**, **15.712±0.382%**, and **15.099±0.338%** for Mixed/Selected/Rejected across three pretraining seeds. Probe protocol selects the best of three predefined learning-rate heads. | § G.3, Table 12 |
| Loss rankings disagree with downstream rankings | Wan training loss favors Rejected, validation loss favors Selected; V-JEPA training and validation losses favor Rejected. Final downstream benchmarks favor Mixed. | §§ 3.2–3.3; Figures 5–7, 16 |
| Intermediate rankings depend on objective and training stage | Approximately matched one-pass comparison: Wan at step 3,149, one seed, VBench Total **60.210/59.363/58.885**; V-JEPA at update 1,140, three seeds, SSv2 top-1 **10.059±0.173% / 10.604±0.146% / 9.884±0.424%**, for Mixed/Selected/Rejected. | § G.4, Table 13 |
| Dataset construction reaches million-clip scale | Reported release: **3,141,246 clips**, **6,475.1 hours**, from **800,000 source videos**, with all released clips passing Camera, Caption, and Tag schema checks. | § E.1, Table 5; § E.5, Table 8 |

These results support the authors’ conclusion that loss alone can misrank recipes in these experiments. They establish a practical recipe comparison, rather than an isolated causal effect of semantic coverage: Mixed changes distinct-clip count, composition, quality, duplication, and repetition together. The intermediate results further qualify any universal preference for broader data (§ G.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-scoped:** Evaluation concerns early pretraining; generated motion remains unstable. Segmentation and annotation experiments are left for future work (§§ 3.2, 4).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Filtering and deduplication are varied jointly, so their individual contributions remain unidentified. The intermediate comparison uses existing schedules and approximate checkpoint alignment; its Wan result covers only one seed (§§ 3, G.4).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** One source collection, two model families, and their respective downstream benchmarks provide limited evidence for other sources, objectives, or longer training (§ 3).
- **Analyst inference—annotation validity:** Schema completeness does not establish semantic correctness; annotations are automatic, and no quantitative human accuracy audit is reported (§ E.1).
- **Comparison scope:** Infrastructure comparisons are static capability audits under the authors’ definitions, without rerun training or comparative speed measurements (Appendix A).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 6 lists more Tag inputs than the preceding clip population without explaining the discrepancy.

## Open research questions
- **Does Mixed remain preferable later in training?** Evidence stops at early checkpoints. Extend matched-seed runs across larger training budgets and track downstream rankings.
- **Which selection decisions help independently?** The study combines quality gates and duplicate quotas. Ablate individual rules while matching distinct-clip counts and processed-clip budgets.
- **How do annotation and segmentation choices affect utility?** These stages remain fixed in the study. Compare caption levels and segmentation policies through the same training interfaces, alongside human annotation audits.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported workflow and within-study rankings; **medium** in broader conclusions about coverage versus quality.

Material gaps include long-run outcomes, isolated selection-rule effects, quantitative annotation accuracy, fully specified optimizer and sampler settings, and direct VidaForge code/dataset release links or licensing details in the supplied text.
