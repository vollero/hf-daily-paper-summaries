# Environments as Scaffold: Enriching Feedback to Bootstrap Self-Evolving Agents in Long-Horizon Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08404) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08404)


## One-sentence takeaway
Feedback-enriched training improves average held-out performance across the tested Qwen3/RL configurations, but some task categories regress and the approach fails to bootstrap learning on AppWorld (Table 1; Limitations).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agent RL can stall when sparse rewards leave most trajectories unsuccessful. The authors argue that supervised fine-tuning warm-ups require costly expert trajectories and may constrain exploration; they instead investigate how changing environmental feedback can make learning easier (§1).

The identified gap is systematic design of **what feedback to provide and when**, both within an episode and across training (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Environment-side scaffolding:** FEEs augment observations with action guidance (AG) or observation enrichment (OE). Training still uses trajectory-level rewards, with environment-generated tokens excluded from policy-gradient updates (§2; Appendix A).
- **Feedback schedule:** The proposed strategy uses early AG followed by late OE. The SciWorld pilot defines early interaction as steps 1–3 and late interaction as steps 6–10 within a 15-step episode. Main training switches from AG to OE at training step 100 of 200, using enrichment probability 0.5 (§§3–4).
- **Benchmark implementations:** SciWorld guidance uses existing expert trajectories, while enrichment exposes internal progress checks. BFCL adds query hints and richer tool outputs. These are adaptations of existing benchmarks, not new underlying tasks (Appendix B).
- **Empirical analysis:** The paper compares standard and enriched training across Qwen3-4B/8B and GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO, then examines entropy, difficulty-specific success, feedback recall, and within-group consistency (§§4–5).

The evidence supports improved aggregate benchmark performance after training with enriched feedback and evaluation without it. It does **not** support uniform gains: Figure 4 contradicts the prose claim of improvement across every difficulty tier, and Table 1 contains category regressions. Entropy and success-rate analyses provide indirect evidence about optimization and exploration; they do not directly measure gradient variance or state coverage.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Feedback rules and timing are environment-specific and manually selected; sensitivity is underexplored. Broader generalization remains untested, and Qwen3-4B/8B training on AppWorld still produces no positive rewards (Limitations).
- **Author hypothesis:** Guidance may encourage execution over clarification, contributing to missing-parameter/function regressions. The causal explanation is not isolated experimentally (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** SciWorld still requires expert trajectories for AG, so FEEs do not establish independence from the expert-data resource motivating the work (Appendix B.1). No direct SFT-warm-up comparison is reported.
- **Analyst inference:** The probes establish learned expectations about feedback format more directly than useful state reasoning. Table 7 asks about `_new_directory_contents`, whereas §5.3 describes added absolute-path information.
- **Analyst inference:** AG and OE are not completely separated: the BFCL OE example includes procedural `_hint` text, complicating attribution to observation information alone (Table 6).
- **Reporting risk:** Table 1 reports selected peak scores without seed counts or uncertainty estimates. Figure 2 mentions variance but does not explain its source.

## Open research questions
- **Can feedback timing adapt to competence?** Manual thresholds lack sensitivity analysis. Compare fixed schedules with schedules triggered by recent success rates under matched training budgets.
- **Can enrichment preserve clarification behavior?** BFCL insufficiency categories sometimes regress. Test clarification-focused guidance against the current schedule, separately measuring execution and clarification success.
- **Does feedback recall cause better planning?** Current probes emphasize output expectations. Test novel state-tracking tasks without enriched feedback and randomize output formatting to separate semantic learning from format recall.
- **What enables bootstrapping on AppWorld?** The reported negative result leaves the necessary scaffolding unresolved. Compare AG, OE, and their combination across task-difficulty strata while measuring first-positive-reward frequency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported benchmark differences, but mechanistic conclusions are less secure.

Material missing information includes random-seed counts, main-result uncertainty, full task-split sizes and identifiers, checkpoint-selection safeguards, compute and feedback-construction costs, schedule sensitivity, detailed AppWorld failure settings, and verified repository/checkpoint contents.
