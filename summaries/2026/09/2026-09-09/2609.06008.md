# Cadence: Error-Bounded Lossy Compression of Demand Time Series with a Time-Series Foundation Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06008) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06008)


## One-sentence takeaway
Cadence reports a 21.4% median body-size reduction over matched classical predictors on two demand corpora, but bootstrap costs, slow inference, and hardware-dependent decoding substantially limit its practical advantage (§§5.2, 5.5–5.8).

## Problem and motivation
The paper asks whether better forecasting from a pretrained time-series model translates into better numeric compression. The author argues that lossless savings grow only logarithmically with residual-accuracy improvements, whereas error-bounded lossy coding can benefit when predictions fall within the permitted tolerance (§1).

The practical target is historical demand-data retention: preserving every sample within a specified absolute error instead of downsampling away extrema. The identified research gap is the compression value of time-series foundation models under fair entropy-coder comparisons and complete storage accounting (§§2–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Closed-loop neural codec:** TimesFM-3 predicts from reconstructed history; residuals are rounded with step \(2\tau\), giving an absolute per-sample error bound provided encoder and decoder reproduce identical predictions. Only the first forecast step and median prediction are used (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Matched coding and bootstrap accounting:** Neural and classical residual indices use the same adaptive binary range coder. Initial context is compressed with a classical predictor, making its storage cost explicit (§§3.5–3.6).
- **Empirical domain boundary:** Evaluation contrasts electricity demand and subway ridership with operational telemetry, synthetic signals, and scientific simulation data (§§5.2–5.3).
- **Deployment constraints and ablations:** Experiments examine batch-dependent numerical differences, context length, quantile conditioning, throughput, and matched-size downsampling (§§5.5–5.10).

The contribution is primarily the codec integration and controlled evaluation; residual quantization and pretrained forecasting are existing ingredients (§§2–3).

## Evidence and main results

Positive gains below denote reported storage reductions relative to the stated comparator.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Negligible median lossless benefit | Reported +0.03% over the best classical predictor using identical entropy coding across 12 series; the supplied table displays only 11 rows. | Table 1; §5.1 |
| Demand-series body gains | Grid: +13.3% median, 147/147 wins; transit: +28.3%, 150/150; combined: +21.4%, 297/297. Best-of-six classical comparator, real bytes, \(\tau=\rho\sigma(x)\), \(\rho\in\{0.01,0.05,0.2\}\). | Tables 2–4; §§3.1, 5.2 |
| Smaller or negative gains elsewhere | Same comparator: NAB +6.4%, 21/24 wins; synthetic +2.9%, 7/12; SDRBench −0.8%, 0/27. | Table 2 |
| Scientific smoothness is unfavorable | Hurricane scanlines lose 3.5%, 25.4%, and 41.0% at the three increasing tolerances; evaluation uses one-dimensional scanlines. | Table 5; §5.3 |
| Bootstrap reduces deliverable savings | Grid end-to-end gains at \(\rho=0.05\): +6.8% for 4,300 samples and +14.2% for 43,800, using context 512. Construction of the longer evaluation sequences is unclear. | Table 7; §5.5 |
| Better worst-case fidelity than downsampling | At matched file size, median downsampling maximum error is 28.0× Cadence’s bound on grid, 36.7× on NAB, and 56.1× on transit; reconstruction is linear. | §5.7 |
| Verified decoding requires numerical agreement | Bit-exact reconstruction reported for group sizes 8 and 16; batch-size changes and CPU/GPU execution produce different predictions. | §§5.5–5.6; Table 8 |
| Low throughput | 224 values/s with fp32, batch 256, context 512 on one RTX 5060; decoding is described as equally slow and sequential. | Tables 9; §§4.1, 6.3 |

These results support a demand-specific predictive advantage in the tested settings. They do not establish universal compression superiority, portable archives, or downstream benefits for anomaly detection. The matched entropy coder strengthens predictor comparisons, but body-only savings must remain separate from complete-file savings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated deployment limits:** Slow sequential execution, shared model storage, non-commercial weights, group-coupled decoding, and measured CPU/GPU disagreement restrict deployment (§6.3). Recording the device does not itself demonstrate portability across machines.
- **Narrow generalization:** Only two demand corpora support the headline result. The author reports no verified retail, call-centre, or utility-metering evidence (§6.3). Their asserted post-training status is not accompanied by an exact verified training cutoff (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope of theory:** The approximate logarithmic law assumes a well-matched residual model (§1); it does not by itself establish the paper’s categorical claim that neural lossless compression cannot help.
- **Internal reporting inconsistencies:** §1 gives synthetic/NAB gains differing from Table 2. Figure 4 conflicts with Table 10 and §5.9 on context penalties and the best short-archive context. Quantile conditioning’s +0.3% idealized result contrasts with −13.8% using actual arithmetic-coded contexts (§5.9).
- **Incomplete accounting and comparison detail:** Table 6’s component rates and shares are not transparently reconciled. Longer-archive construction, downsampling configuration, uncertainty estimates, and numerical ZFP results are unavailable in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference—failure probabilities:** Desynchronization probabilities are estimated from numerical drift, rather than demonstrated through reported long-archive failure trials (§5.6).

## Open research questions
- **Can inference become portable?** Fixed group sizes do not resolve CPU/GPU disagreement. Test integer or fixed-point inference across devices and software versions, checking complete reconstruction equality and error bounds (§§5.6, 6.4).
- **Can a smaller model retain the storage advantage?** Only TimesFM-3 is evaluated, and inference dominates runtime. Compare distilled predictors on end-to-end bytes, throughput, and identical tolerances (§§5.8, 6.4).
- **How broadly does the demand effect generalize?** Two corpora and unsuccessful proxy statistics leave domain selection unresolved. Test additional demand domains at matched relative errors and with bootstrap costs included (§§6.1, 6.3).
- **Does the fidelity advantage improve real retention workloads?** Matched-size maximum error does not establish application utility. Evaluate a database integration on retrieval latency, anomaly preservation, and archival storage (§§5.7, 6.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the core method and reported domain pattern, but inconsistent summaries and incomplete experimental details weaken confidence in exact deliverable gains.

Material missing information includes a verified training cutoff, longer-sequence construction, full container accounting, detailed downsampling settings, cross-machine decoding tests, uncertainty estimates, and numerical ZFP comparisons. Code and data availability are reported by the author and were not independently checked.
