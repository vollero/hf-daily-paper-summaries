# StudyBench: Can Self-Evolution Squeeze Textbooks for Olympiad Capability?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00787) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00787)


## One-sentence takeaway
StudyBench finds that tested self-evolution methods improve textbook problem solving much more than olympiad transfer, although its solution-informed guidance and limited compute experiments do not establish that algorithms alone explain the remaining gap.

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether models can turn a fixed body of learning material into transferable problem-solving ability. They argue that existing evaluations conflate the contributions of training data, base-model capability, and adaptation methods, or measure local adaptation without testing harder transfer (§1).

StudyBench addresses this with fixed physics textbooks, held-out textbook exercises, and olympiad problems selected to be difficult for Qwen3-8B but solvable with textbook-grounded guidance (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** Eleven textbooks provide approximately 6 million tokens, 1,420 answered instructions, and 646 unanswered instructions. Evaluation separates Application problems—88 parents, 109 sub-problems—from Transfer problems—90 parents, 280 sub-problems (§2.4, Table 1).
- **Capability and reachability filters:** Qwen3-8B sampling identifies difficult problems. A teacher decomposes olympiad solutions, retrieves and verifies textbook passages, and writes guidance; admission requires Qwen3-8B to succeed with that guidance. Application statements and solutions are redacted from training material (§§2.1, 2.3).
- **Evaluation and diagnostics:** Parent and sub-problem accuracy distinguish complete solutions from partial success. Existing SFT, RL, self-play, and context-evolution methods are compared, with guidance ablations and compute trajectories exposing remaining headroom (§§2.2–4). The contribution is the benchmark and analysis, rather than a new self-evolution algorithm.

## Evidence and main results
Par@k requires one attempt to solve every sub-problem of a parent; Sub@k counts each sub-problem solved in any attempt. Open-weight results average three independent eight-sample evaluations; Opus results use one single-sample evaluation (§2.2). Accuracy values below are percentages.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong Application gains, weak Transfer gains | Qwen3-8B → GEPA: Application Par@8 **17.05 ± 1.14 → 34.85 ± 1.31**; Transfer Par@8 **0.00 → 7.04 ± 2.80**. Transfer Sub@8 **56.43 ± 1.89 → 58.57 ± 1.43**. | Table 2 |
| Guidance exposes substantial conditional headroom | Qwen3-8B Transfer Par@8 is **100.00** with construction guidance, by selection. GEPA rises from **7.04 solo to 90.00 guided**, an **82.96 percentage-point** gap. | Table 4 |
| Reachability depends on the teacher | On the same retained Transfer items, independent GLM-5.1 guidance gives Qwen3-8B **62.22 Par@8**, versus the construction-conditioned **100.00** for DeepSeek V4 Pro. | Appendix D, Table 8 |
| Other models show limited or inconsistent gains | Llama GRPO improves Transfer Sub@8 **14.88 ± 1.76 → 22.74 ± 1.44**, but Application Sub@8 decreases **14.37 ± 0.53 → 14.07 ± 1.40**. Opus EvoSkill improves Transfer Par@1 **40.00 → 43.33**, while Application Par@1 falls **59.09 → 52.27**. | Tables 2–3 |
| Observed compute saturation | Qwen3-8B GEPA reaches approximately **44.3% Application Sub@8 at 30.5 GPU-hours**, remaining there through **112.5 GPU-hours** on A800 GPUs. These curves measure Application performance. | Appendix I, Figure 4 |
| Scoring substantially depends on LLM fallback | On **6,720** Qwen3-8B Transfer attempt-level judgments, the LLM accepts **1,753 of 5,981** rule-rejected answers. Reported agreement is **73.91%**, assuming all rule accepts would also be LLM accepts. | Appendix G, Table 11 |

The evidence supports weak autonomous transfer under the tested implementations and substantial benefits from targeted guidance. It does **not** establish that raw textbooks alone provide an equally accessible learning signal: guidance uses gold solutions to choose concepts and order steps. Nor do finite Application learning curves prove that more compute cannot improve Transfer performance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Physics only; Qwen-based selection; limited method–model coverage for guidance and compute analyses; verifier analysis restricted to Qwen Transfer rollouts. Application selection also admits 15 parents solved once in eight attempts (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—selection and contamination:** Failure under sampling does not demonstrate absence of pretraining exposure. The filter also does not screen other base models independently (§2.3).
- **Analyst inference—guidance comparison:** Gold-solution-informed guidance supplies problem-specific methodological information despite answer-leakage gates. Its effectiveness is evidence of conditional solvability, not a clean estimate of what autonomous textbook learning should achieve (§2.1).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Methods consume different supervision layers, use different training scorers, and receive unequal compute. Fixed benchmark inputs therefore do not isolate algorithm design completely (§3; Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Verifier precision is conditional on an unobserved acceptance assumption, without an independent human accuracy audit. Open-weight error bars describe evaluation sampling, not necessarily training variability (Appendix G; §2.2).
- **Reproducibility:** Raw textbooks and the parsed corpus are not distributed; reproducing corpus-based methods requires legal copies and reconstruction (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **How much headroom comes from solution-informed scaffolding?** The teacher sees gold solutions. Compare passage-only retrieval, guidance generated without solutions, and current guidance on the same items (§2.1).
- **Can transfer improve after Application performance plateaus?** Reported compute curves track Application Sub@8. Evaluate Transfer metrics at the same checkpoints and extend selected runs with controlled budgets (§4.2).
- **Do verifier errors constrain learning?** Rule-only rewards reject many answers later accepted by the LLM. Human-audit a stratified sample, then compare RL with original versus corrected rewards (Appendix G).
- **Does the finding generalize beyond Qwen-selected physics?** Repeat construction with another filtering model and another discipline, retaining shared and separately selected evaluation subsets (Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark design and reported performance pattern, but the causal claim that the gap is exclusively methodological is stronger than the experiments establish.

Material gaps include independent human verifier validation, guidance without gold-solution access, Transfer learning curves, comprehensive training-seed uncertainty, and complete configuration detail for every method–model pair. The instruction layers are also described inconsistently as nested sets versus separate answered/unanswered pools (§2.1; Table 1).
