# RelightFormer: Feed-forward Generative Transformer for Multiview Object Relighting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.07414) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07414)


## One-sentence takeaway
RelightFormer adapts a video diffusion Transformer for camera-conditioned object relighting, improving the reported multiview benchmarks while showing mixed perceptual gains and requiring additional training for novel viewpoints (Tables 1, 2, 4, 9).

## Problem and motivation
Object relighting changes illumination while preserving geometry, materials, and identity. The authors identify two obstacles: inverse-rendering pipelines require ambiguous intrinsic-property estimation and costly scene optimization, while many generative approaches process views independently or combine them through channel concatenation, limiting multiview reasoning (§1).

The intended applications include digital content creation, film, gaming, and augmented reality; these motivate the work but are not separately evaluated.

## Approach and contributions
- **Direct multiview generation:** RelightFormer adapts Wan2.1’s latent Transformer and rectified flow matching to jointly process reference images, noisy target images, and target illumination. Camera-derived Plücker ray embeddings supply spatial information without explicitly reconstructing geometry or materials (§3.1).
- **Illumination conditioning:** A dedicated cross-attention branch lets image tokens query environment-map tokens, alongside multiview self-attention. The authors motivate this through an analogy with the rendering integral; physical correctness is not demonstrated (§3.2).
- **Unordered-view adaptation:** The model adopts existing PRope and Geometry-Aware Attention mechanisms instead of frame-indexed encoding, and applies the frozen VAE’s first-frame pathway uniformly. These are adaptations of prior components, not newly invented encoding methods (§3.3; Appendix A).
- **Training asset and task extension:** LOD contains 90,545 objects and 39,008 illumination conditions, with lighting diversity expanded through rotations. A separately post-trained variant, RelightFormer-Post, handles novel-view relighting (§4; §5.4).

Despite the “feed-forward” terminology, generation uses a learned velocity field and ODE integration; the supplied text does not establish single-step inference (§3.1).

## Evidence and main results
PSNR and sPSNR below are reported in the paper’s metric scale; higher is better. Lower LPIPS is better.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Leads listed baselines on multiview LOD metrics | For 32-to-32 relighting, RelightFormer versus fine-tuned Neural Gaffer: PSNR **22.83 vs 20.26**, sPSNR **25.07 vs 22.34**, SSIM **0.906 vs 0.879**, LPIPS **0.080 vs 0.099**. | Synthetic-to-real environment-map transfer | On 42 OLATverse objects and 5,082 environment-map mappings, RelightFormer versus fine-tuned Neural Gaffer: PSNR **17.23 vs 16.64**, sPSNR **20.48 vs 19.61**, SSIM **0.964 vs 0.961**, LPIPS **0.047 vs 0.049**. | Table 2; Appendix C |
| Point-light results trade radiometric accuracy against perceptual similarity | On rotating point-light relighting of 42 OLATverse objects, RelightFormer versus original Neural Gaffer: PSNR **29.39 vs 19.28**, sPSNR **31.16 vs 21.05**, SSIM **0.826 vs 0.900**, LPIPS **0.209 vs 0.122**. | Table 2; Appendix C |
| TensoIR gains are metric-dependent | For 16-image relighting, RelightFormer has PSNR **17.64** and sPSNR **19.74**; original Neural Gaffer has higher SSIM (**0.906 vs 0.904**), and fine-tuned Neural Gaffer lower LPIPS (**0.090 vs 0.100**). | Table 9 |
| Novel-view variant improves over Reli3D | Zero-shot Stanford-ORB: RelightFormer-Post versus Reli3D has PSNR-H **20.99 vs 20.26**, PSNR-L **26.84 vs 25.13**, SSIM **0.952 vs 0.943**, LPIPS **0.061 vs 0.077**, with both reported at approximately **2 minutes**. Listed optimization methods achieve better quality. | Table 4; §5.4 |
| Ablations support architectural choices | Models trained on a 2,989-object subset and tested with 32 views: full-model PSNR **20.95**, versus **20.10** with channel concatenation, **15.98** without permutation invariance, and **14.87** with single-view-only training. | Table 5; §6; Appendix D |

These results support improved foreground relighting in the tested settings, particularly with multiple views. They do not establish universal metric superiority, explicit recovery of correct geometry/materials, or physically consistent light transport. The reported image metrics also do not directly measure cross-view consistency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Novel-view relighting requires post-training or a reconstruction pipeline. Difficult materials and concentrated specular illumination remain challenging; complex scenes and dynamic video are future work (§§5.3–5.4, 7).
- **Conflicting result descriptions:** Section 5.3 claims the best point-light SSIM and weaker sPSNR than Neural Gaffer; Table 2 shows the opposite. Tables 3 and 8 also show lower translucent-object PSNR than original Neural Gaffer despite the prose claiming superiority.
- **Metric ambiguity:** Table 2 lists DiLightNet’s environment-map sPSNR below its PSNR, which appears inconsistent with Appendix C’s least-squares scale-alignment definition. The discrepancy is unresolved.
- **Evaluation-count ambiguity:** Appendix B specifies an 8:1:1 object split, but Appendix C evaluates 453 LOD test objects without explaining their selection. TensoIR illumination and pair counts also differ across §5.2, Table 7, and Appendix C.
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** Ablations use a reduced dataset; replacing PRope changes geometric encoding as well as view-order treatment. No separate order-shuffling test or backbone/data-scale ablation isolates these effects.
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Only Neural Gaffer is explicitly fine-tuned on LOD. Training at 256×256 and foreground-only evaluation leave higher-resolution behavior and background interactions unestablished (§5; Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **Does the encoding improve order robustness independently of geometric information?** The existing ablation changes both. Test shuffled input orders and compare encodings with matched camera information (§6).
- **How consistent are generated views geometrically and photometrically?** Current metrics assess images individually. Add correspondence-based cross-view error and controlled material/light-transport tests (Appendix C).
- **Can perceptual quality improve under concentrated lighting?** Point-light SSIM and LPIPS trail original Neural Gaffer in Table 2. Evaluate targeted lighting/material training with a fixed held-out point-light protocol.
- **Can the model extend to scenes and motion?** These are proposed rather than evaluated. Test cluttered scenes with spatially varying lighting and video sequences with temporal-consistency measurements (§7).
