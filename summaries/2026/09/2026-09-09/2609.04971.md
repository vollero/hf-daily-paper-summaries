# BeaconKV: Key-Value Cache Compression Guided by Beacon Queries for Efficient Large Reasoning Model Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04971) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04971)


## One-sentence takeaway
BeaconKV uses representative historical queries to guide training-free KV eviction, achieving 5.8× lower peak GPU memory and over 4.3× higher throughput than full-cache inference in one Qwen3-4B setting, with LiveCodeBench accuracy falling from 54.4% to 51.1% (Table 4).

## Problem and motivation
Extended reasoning traces make KV caches grow with generated sequence length, constraining GPU memory and batch sizes. The authors identify a weakness in eviction based only on recent queries: later reasoning steps can revisit distant problem constraints or plans that currently receive little attention. They call these steps **Thought Revisiting Tokens (TRT)** and argue that recent-query scoring can prematurely discard their context (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Attention-pattern observation:** The authors identify distant-context revisiting and show examples of corresponding queries clustering in pre-RoPE embedding space (§3, Figures 1–4).
- **Continual query selection:** Continual Farthest Point Sampling periodically compresses a bounded historical-query buffer using cosine diversity, producing representative “beacon queries” without retaining the entire decoding query history (§4.2, Appendix D).
- **Beacon-guided eviction:** Beacon queries receive positional encoding for the current decoding step; recent queries retain their original positions. Maximum attention scores across observation queries and grouped heads rank KV entries, with protected prefix/recent entries retained alongside top-scoring entries. The method requires no training (§4.3, Algorithm 2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad accuracy advantage, according to authors | Four models and four benchmarks; average pass@1 over eight runs for AIME24 and four for other tasks. | Large efficiency gain with measurable accuracy loss | Qwen3-4B, 32K generation, batch 14, single A100 80GB: Full KV → BeaconKV at 2K budget reduces peak memory **77.0 → 13.3 GB**, raises throughput **82.3 → 356.4 tokens/s**, and changes LiveCodeBench accuracy **54.4% → 51.1%**. | §5.4, Table 4 |

These results support a useful accuracy–memory trade-off within the tested workloads. They do not establish lossless compression or universal speed superiority: BeaconKV sacrifices some full-cache accuracy and runs slightly slower than RPC and SnapKV at matched compressed budgets. Attention visualizations and selection ablations support the proposed mechanism, but do not independently prove that TRT preservation causes the gains.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Transfer to retrieval, summarization, and general long-context generation remains insufficiently explored; optimal query counts and budgets may vary across models and tasks (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Mechanistic illustrations center on one AIME24 sample from R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. Their generality across models and tasks is less directly established than the benchmark performance (§3, Figures 1–4).
- **Analyst inference:** Repeated-run averages lack reported uncertainty intervals, limiting assessment of small differences (§5.1).
- **Implementation ambiguity:** §5.1 protects 32 recent KV tokens for BeaconKV, whereas Appendix D uses the recent-query count—16 in the main configuration—for protected recent KV entries. Prefix size and output-only versus total-cache budget accounting also require clarification (§5.1, Algorithm 2).
- **Evaluation scope:** Efficiency measurements use one model and one GPU platform (§5.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does beacon selection transfer beyond reasoning?** Appendix C leaves this unresolved; evaluate retrieval and summarization with matched memory budgets.
2. **Can query allocation adapt during decoding?** Table 1 shows competing accuracy and latency outcomes; compare fixed allocations with policies driven by query diversity under matched resource limits.
3. **Is distant-context preservation causally responsible for the gains?** Existing evidence is observational and comparative; selectively remove beacon-preserved distant entries and measure subsequent attention and answer accuracy.
4. **How important is beacon positional alignment?** No dedicated alignment ablation is supplied; compare current-step rotation against original-position rotation across models and budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Numerical tables support the main trade-off, while mechanistic generality and implementation details remain less certain.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates, an explicit code-release location, the LiveCodeBench version/date range, complete numerical Figure 8 results, the named comparator behind the maximum accuracy gain, and resolution of the cache-budget and recent-token inconsistencies.
