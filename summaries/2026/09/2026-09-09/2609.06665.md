# TransNormal-2: Geometry-Grounded Rectified Flow with Edge-Aware Decoding for Precise Normal Estimation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06665) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06665)


## One-sentence takeaway
TransNormal-2 combines decoded-normal supervision with constrained post-decoder refinement, reporting strong monocular normal-estimation results, especially on transparent objects, although its rendering-loss ablation also changes the training schedule.

## Problem and motivation
Monocular surface-normal estimation must recover surface orientation despite ambiguous appearance, particularly around boundaries and refractive objects. The authors identify VAE reconstruction as an underexamined error source: encoding and decoding ground-truth normals already introduces angular error concentrated near edges (Section 3.1, Fig. 2). They therefore target both supervision of decoded predictions and correction using full-resolution RGB evidence.

## Approach and contributions
- **Reconstruction diagnostic:** Measures ground-truth normal degradation through the frozen FLUX.2 VAE, independently of predictor error, and motivates the spatial pattern with a simplified boundary-blurring model (Section 3.1).
- **Geometry-aware training:** Adapts FLUX.2 through LoRA for deterministic single-step prediction. Latent MSE is supplemented by decoded-normal vMF directional loss, wavelet supervision, and shading consistency using estimated lighting and normalized cross-correlation. Wavelet supervision is inherited from TransNormal; the expanded objectives and backbone reformulation are contributions of this extension (Sections 4.1–4.3, Table 3).
- **Geometric Refinement Module:** Trains a gated, scaled residual correction after freezing the core predictor. An opaque/transparent domain flag controls whether its anchor uses RGB-guided filtering; transparent images retain the coarse prediction as anchor (Sections 4.2–4.3).

TN-Syn is attributed to the prior TransNormal paper in Section 5.1, so it should not be counted as a newly introduced dataset here.

These results support the reported benchmark performance and an edge-focused refinement effect on indoor scenes. They do not establish that every corrected error originated in the VAE. **Analyst inference:** ground-truth round-trip degradation is a reconstruction diagnostic, not a demonstrated lower bound for every latent a predictor could produce. The annotation comparison also does not measure total pretraining data or compute.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Lambertian consistency weakly models refraction and strong specularity; GRM cannot repair large regions already mispredicted by the core model (Section 6).
- **Confounded loss attribution:** Table 4 adds rendering loss while extending the training schedule. Wavelet and vMF losses are also added together, preventing separate attribution.
- **Scope of generalization:** ClearPose uses a selected mesh-available subset of 120 images and transparent-object masks. Unlabeled in-the-wild examples provide qualitative evidence only (Sections 5.1–5.2).
- **Domain information:** Evaluation supplies the transparent/opaque flag by benchmark. **Analyst inference:** robustness to unknown or mixed material domains is not established (Section 5.1).
- **Mechanism and uncertainty:** Edge diagnostics cover NYUv2 and ScanNet using RGB edges, explicitly excluding transparent-scene mechanism validation. No repeated-run uncertainty is reported for the small general-scene margins (Tables 1, 8).

## Open research questions
- **How much does rendering consistency contribute independently?** Its ablation changes the schedule too. Run matched-schedule experiments with and without rendering, plus separate wavelet and vMF controls.
- **Can refinement handle unknown material composition?** The current flag is supplied per image or benchmark. Compare fixed flags, inferred flags, and spatial material gating on mixed opaque/transparent scenes.
- **Does GRM improve true transparent-object boundaries?** RGB-based boundary diagnostics are restricted to general scenes. Repeat edge-band analysis using mesh-derived geometric boundaries on ClearPose.
- **How much remains attributable to compression?** The reconstruction test does not isolate all prediction errors. Compare matched predictors across latent resolutions or geometry-specific decoders, with and without GRM, as suggested by Section 6.
