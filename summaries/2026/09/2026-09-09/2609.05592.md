# What Did I Just Say? Self-Listening for Full-Duplex Speech Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05592) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05592)


## One-sentence takeaway
Feeding already-played speech back into a full-duplex model raises reported AnchorSpeech anchoring accuracy from 7.8% to 73.0% in a matched comparison, but reduces conventional turn-management rates (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Text generation, speech synthesis, and playback advance asynchronously, so generated text can misrepresent what a user has actually heard. After interruptions such as “repeat the last item,” correct recovery requires tracking the played response, beyond deciding whether to stop speaking. The authors call this capability *anchoring* and motivate it through structured voice workflows (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task formulation:** Define anchoring against realized playback, including the last completed item in an ordered response (§§1–2).
- **Self-Listening architecture:** Interleave user speech, already-played model speech, and model text. The implementation combines Qwen2.5-Omni-7B’s Thinker with frozen MOSS-TTS-Realtime; native control tokens manage interruption and backchannel decisions. Two-stage LoRA training supervises text and control outputs (§3).
- **AnchorSpeech collection and evaluation:** Construct ordered-sequence interruption examples with homogeneous training/test splits and human screening of test candidates. Evaluation interrupts each system’s own generated response and judges recovery against its actual played prefix. Open-ended semantic interruption examples supplement training only (§4).

The matched ablation supports the value of played-speech feedback for this benchmark. It does not establish broad superiority in conversational interaction or open-ended semantic recovery. Tables 1 and 2 appear only as captions in the supplied text, so these results rely on the accompanying prose; complete scores and exact latencies cannot be inspected.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged trade-off:** Better anchoring accompanies lower interruption Respond and backchannel Resume rates than the matched variant (§5.2).
- **Explicit scope:** AnchorSpeech tests deterministic ordered content. Semantic anchoring has training examples but no separate evaluation; construction excludes partial-item and word-internal boundaries (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Shared sequence families and construction procedures across training/test splits limit evidence for transfer to unfamiliar structures or natural workflows (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** GPT-5.6-Terra judges correctness, but human agreement and judge validation are unreported. Duration calibration uses a separate generation, leaving actual interruption placement sensitive to variation between runs (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** The channel-removal ablation supports feedback’s utility, but does not establish whether audio feedback outperforms a playback-aligned text or item-position signal (§5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does anchoring transfer to open-ended content?** Semantic recovery is trained but not separately tested (§4.2). Evaluate held-out explanations and procedures with human-rated repetition and continuation accuracy.
- **Can anchoring and turn management improve together?** The reported trade-off remains unresolved (§5.2). Sweep control-token loss weights and reaction windows while measuring both benchmarks.
- **Is waveform feedback necessary?** The comparator lacks any played-output channel (§5.1). Compare audio feedback with playback-aligned transcripts and explicit completed-item markers under matched training.
- **How robust is recovery at difficult boundaries?** Construction excludes partial items (§4.1). Test interruptions inside words/items and under controlled feedback delays.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline comparisons are clearly described, but material gaps include complete result tables, test-set size, uncertainty estimates or repeated runs, exact latency definitions and values, judge prompts and human validation, detailed inference settings, playback-feedback overhead, and confirmed code/data/model release contents.
