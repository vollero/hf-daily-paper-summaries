# Marigold V2: Revisiting Diffusion Transformers for Monocular Depth Estimation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08084) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08084)


## One-sentence takeaway
Marigold V2 combines efficient image-editing transformer adaptation, depth-derived feature alignment, and local optimal-transport supervision to improve relative depth accuracy and boundary detail, leading the paper’s data-restricted benchmark ranking (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Single-image depth estimation is ambiguous, and existing models struggle with unfamiliar scenes, thin structures, and blurred depth boundaries. The authors identify a particular supervision problem: even synthetic depth annotations can assign inconsistent foreground/background values near transparent or thin objects, making exact pixel matching undesirable (§§1, 3.4).

Their goal is to preserve geometric detail in a single-step, VAE-based predictor while keeping adaptation affordable for small laboratories.

## Approach and contributions
- **Efficient two-stage adaptation:** Repurpose Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 using 4-bit weights and rank-128 QLoRA adapters. The model directly maps RGB latents to normalized log-depth latents through fixed-timestep regression; inference requires no iterative diffusion sampling (§§3.1–3.2).
- **iREPA-depth:** During Stage 1, align internal transformer representations with frozen DINOv3 features extracted from ground-truth depth rather than RGB, alongside latent, pixel, and gradient losses (§3.3).
- **SinkLoss:** During Stage 2, unfreeze the VAE decoder and introduce entropy-regularized matching between predicted and target depth values within local blocks. This relaxes exact spatial correspondence to accommodate ambiguous annotations (§3.4; Fig. 2).
- **Task extensions:** Demonstrate test-time LoRA depth completion and separately adapted see-through depth, surface-normal, and albedo predictors. These extend the recipe; their results do not establish uniform superiority across every metric (§5).

The evidence supports improved aligned depth geometry and boundary-sensitive performance across the reported settings. It does not establish directly metric monocular depth, universal superiority over larger-data models, or downstream reconstruction gains. Fine-detail claims also rely on qualitative figures whose images are unavailable in this text extraction; notably, Fig. 6’s caption acknowledges that PPD produces fewer flying pixels overall while losing more detail.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The large backbone prevents real-time use; reflections, motion blur, and defocus remain ambiguous (§6). Transparency behavior depends on training annotations (§5.2).
- **Author-stated cost:** Tiled completion costs 4–14 times the single-pass runtime and requires enough sparse anchors for stable local fitting (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** The main ranking excludes larger-data methods, mixes reproduced results with numbers imported from PPD, and does not control foundation-model pretraining exposure (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference—boundary validity:** SEE and SinkLoss both tolerate local spatial mismatch. Better SEE therefore does not alone demonstrate exact edge localization; edge evaluation is confined to synthetic HyperSim (§§3.4, 4.4).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** iREPA’s aggregate advantage becomes mixed after longer training, and SinkLoss slightly worsens some global metrics. Quantitative isolation of VAE unfreezing from SinkLoss is limited; the explicit decoder comparison is qualitative (Tables 3, 5, 10; Fig. 5).

## Open research questions
1. **Does SinkLoss improve precisely localized real-world boundaries?** Current edge tests use synthetic data and spatial tolerance. Next experiment: evaluate thin-object boundary displacement and reconstructed geometry against carefully annotated real scenes.
2. **Which Stage-2 component drives the improvement?** Decoder unfreezing is compared qualitatively, and matching hyperparameter sensitivity is unreported. Next experiment: a factorial study of decoder freezing, SinkLoss, and block size using both global and edge metrics.
3. **Can one model represent competing transparency interpretations?** The base and see-through checkpoints learn different target surfaces (§5.2). Next experiment: train a layered or uncertainty-aware predictor and evaluate both glass-surface and behind-glass geometry.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and table comparisons; **medium** for broad generalization and visual-quality claims.

Material omissions include actual figure images, repeated-run uncertainty, the exact GPU model and timing procedure, full main-training optimizer/schedule details, detailed iREPA implementation, and an explicit complete Stage-2 objective specifying which earlier losses remain active.
