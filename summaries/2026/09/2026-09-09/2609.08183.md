# NeoHorse-1: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement via Agentic Post-Training with Routing Harness

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08183) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08183)


## One-sentence takeaway
NeoHorse-1 improves Qwen3.5-based agent performance using routing-harness experience and staged post-training, but demonstrates only one improvement cycle rather than sustained recursive self-improvement (Tables 1–2; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Agent deployment produces trajectories, routing decisions, and observable outcomes, yet turning these records into subsequent model improvements requires a structured feedback mechanism. The authors identify two gaps: static trajectory supervision does not follow the student’s own generated prefixes, and curriculum construction often requires explicit difficulty labels or dataset-specific heuristics (§§1–2).

Their proposal uses the routing harness both to collect experience and to estimate capability demand, with evaluation feedback directing subsequent training mixtures.

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is an integrated training and feedback design; the text does not establish that its individual learning objectives are new.

- **Context-preserving supervision:** Convert trajectories into user-turn examples, retaining current-turn reasoning, tool interactions, and harness context while masking historical and non-assistant tokens from prediction loss. Structural checks, six semantic quality dimensions, and subscene labels govern data processing (§§3.1–3.3, 4.1).
- **Routing-guided curriculum:** Re-estimate capability demand from pre-response context rather than equating difficulty with the model actually served. Introduce higher-scored examples across three stages while retaining lower-scored coverage (§4.2).
- **Routing-guided on-policy distillation:** Schedule recorded starting contexts using the same progression, generate student responses, and apply fixed-teacher reverse-KL supervision on student-generated prefixes using shared top-K candidates plus a remaining-probability bin (§4.3).
- **Capability-guided allocation:** Use evaluated deficiencies to rebalance subsequent training data, with successful trajectories supplying targets and failures identifying coverage gaps. The report presents 4B and 9B models as an initial implementation (§3.5; §6).

The results support improved performance from the overall post-training recipe and a data-source advantage over the tested Toucan configuration. They do not isolate the contributions of routing, distillation, filtering, or feedback allocation, nor establish accumulating gains across generations. The five-benchmark development averages differ in composition from the headline ten-benchmark averages.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only one evaluation–selection–update cycle is validated; sustained improvement and broader capability coverage remain untested (§6).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No reported ablations compare routing order against shuffled order, SFT against SFT plus distillation, or adaptive allocation against a fixed mixture. Their individual effects remain unresolved (§§4–5).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and comparability:** PinchBench and VitaBench have single runs; three other interactive benchmarks report three-run means without dispersion. VitaBench substitutes DeepSeek-V4-Flash for its original simulator and judge, and one Table 1 reference result comes from an external report (§5).
- **Analyst inference—scaling confound:** Fixed passes over larger subsets also increase total training exposure, so Fig. 7 does not isolate data diversity from additional computation (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** The data-source comparison uses one public alternative with different sequence composition. Selected traces illustrate behaviors but do not establish their population frequency (§§5.1–5.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Do gains accumulate across iterations?** Only one cycle is reported (§6). Next experiment: compare several feedback-driven generations against a fixed-mixture control under matched compute.
2. **Does routing order itself help?** No ordering ablation is supplied (§4.2). Next experiment: compare routing, shuffled, and reversed ordering using identical examples and budgets.
3. **What does on-policy distillation add?** Its separate contribution is not quantified (§4.3; §5). Next experiment: compare SFT alone, unscheduled distillation, and routing-guided distillation under matched training budgets.
4. **Does allocation transfer beyond evaluated task families?** Broader harness capabilities remain unevaluated (§6). Next experiment: reserve new domains and harness environments for held-out evaluation across successive updates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Benchmark comparisons and the proposed mechanism are explicit; causal attribution and recursive improvement remain weakly established.

Material omissions include exact corpus/token counts and mixture proportions, training hyperparameters and compute, teacher identity, distillation K and rollout-refresh settings, curriculum allocation fractions, precise deficiency-to-mixture rules, judge validation, uncertainty estimates, and confirmed release contents.
