# SynthGait-19K: A Physically Grounded Synthetic Video Dataset for Gait Parameter Estimation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08108) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08108)


## One-sentence takeaway
MoCap-conditioned synthetic walking videos support gait estimation on real video across RGB and pose-based models, but strong correlations do not establish superior absolute accuracy or clinical validity.

## Problem and motivation
Monocular gait estimation could make mobility assessment more accessible, but synchronized video and biomechanical supervision are expensive to collect. Existing datasets offer limited subjects, viewpoints, and appearances, making generalization difficult to evaluate. The authors address this gap with synthetic videos that vary camera and visual conditions while preserving fitted motion and gait labels (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset:** SynthGait-19K pairs synthetic RGB walking videos with SMPL motion and six labels: cadence, walking speed, step length, step width, stooped posture, and arm swing. It combines five MoCap cohorts and uses a subject-disjoint training/validation split (§3).
- **Construction pipeline:** Gait2Vid converts heterogeneous MoCap recordings to SMPL, renders depth with controllable cameras and a ground plane, and conditions Wan2.1-VACE video synthesis on depth and appearance prompts. Labels come from fitted motion, with UnderPressure detecting heel strikes (§4; Appendix A).
- **Benchmark and reference model:** GaitXFormer fine-tunes a V-JEPA2 video encoder with parameter-specific cross-attention queries. Comparisons cover RGB, pose, biomechanical, and mesh-recovery pipelines, including synthetic-data transfer, viewpoint, scale, occlusion, and clinical-severity experiments (§§5–6; Appendix F).

The evidence supports useful transfer of synthetic gait supervision to two real-video representations. It does **not** establish an overall advantage over equivalently supervised STT, uniformly better absolute measurements, or diagnostic validity. Motion fidelity is measured against fitted SMPL through another pose estimator; PD4T provides severity labels rather than independent gait measurements.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Severe occlusion, assistive devices, clothing, and clinical diversity remain incompletely represented. Five-second windows exclude longer phenomena such as freezing of gait. Real evaluation centers on GPJATK, supplemented by PD4T (§7).
- **Analyst inference—reference dependence:** Shared SMPL-derived label definitions improve consistency but can carry fitting or extraction errors into both training and evaluation. Force-platform validation covers contact timing only and pools foot laterality (Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Most HMR and biomechanical baselines lack matched task-specific training. STT provides a stronger supervision-matched comparison, and its overall difference from GaitXFormer is statistically uncertain (Appendix D; Table 18).
- **Clinical coverage:** PD4T evaluation uses straight-walking segments and has substantially fewer UPDRS-2 samples; this limits conclusions about more severe or complex walking behavior (Appendix E; Figure 8).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Video totals alternate between 19,272 and 19,273; Table 1’s component counts do not all reconcile. Appendix D places adapted WHAM reconstruction evaluation on GPJATK-VACE, whereas Table 6 specifies real GPJATK. STT preprocessing is described as OpenPose in §6.3 but RTMPose for STT† in Appendix D.

## Open research questions
1. **Can absolute gait accuracy improve alongside correlation?** Table 11 shows substantial MAE disadvantages for GaitXFormer. Test calibration or alternative regression losses using a separate calibration cohort and held-out real subjects.
2. **Which synthesis components drive transfer?** Scale and architecture ablations are provided, but quantitative isolation of appearance diversity and diffusion synthesis is absent. Compare identical motions rendered conventionally versus diffusion-generated, with training size and model fixed.
3. **Does transfer survive broader acquisition and clinical variation?** Current evaluations leave assistive devices and longer abnormal episodes undercovered. Test prospectively on independent cohorts with synchronized gait references, reporting results by condition and camera setting.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed protocols, MAE results, ablations, and bootstrap analyses support the main transfer finding, but material reporting ambiguities remain.

Unavailable or unresolved: reconciled dataset counts; confirmed asset access and licensing; complete curation criteria and SMPL-fitting configuration; consistent WHAM reconstruction domain and STT runtime preprocessing; independent validation of all six gait quantities; uncertainty estimates for the reported PD4T classification improvement.
