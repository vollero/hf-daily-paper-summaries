# NOAH: Learning the Full Patient Journey. A Longitudinal Multimodal Time-Aware Model for Representation and Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09140) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09140)


## One-sentence takeaway
NOAH combines temporal attention and shared variational event generation to learn useful multimodal patient representations and forecast clinical events, but long-horizon drift and outcome-selection effects limit its simulation evidence.

## Problem and motivation
Clinical records combine heterogeneous modalities, irregular timing, and uncertain disease progression. The authors argue that existing models often restrict modalities or event vocabularies, inadequately condition on prediction horizons, or learn representations without supporting stochastic future trajectories (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

The intended gap is a common patient-state encoder and generative forecasting model that operates across longitudinal clinical records.

## Approach and contributions
- **Directional temporal attention:** Queries receive the next-event time gap; keys receive the preceding gap, alongside age and position. This supports irregular timing and optional control over future event times (Methods, pp. 17–18).
- **Shared stochastic event generation:** A history-conditioned Gaussian prior and target-informed training posterior learn a shared latent variable. Hierarchical decoding produces compatible event categories, modalities, specifics, values, and timing; randomness is shared across components (Methods, pp. 18–21).
- **Multimodal trajectory modeling:** NOAH extends the TAMME/APOLLO representation scheme using frozen text, image, ECG, and echocardiography encoders, numeric features, and categorical events. It generates embeddings rather than decoded raw images or text. The corpus contains 559 million events, with patient-disjoint training, validation, and test splits (Methods, pp. 15–17; Supplementary Table 15).
- **Multiple uses of one pretrained model:** Patient-state probing, event forecasting, Monte Carlo outcome classification, survival modeling, and intervention-conditioned rollouts are demonstrated. The authors’ “first holistic model” claim is not independently established by the supplied comparisons (Discussion, p. 14).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Free event forecasting exceeds persistence | On 13,551 test patients, with prompts ending 24 h after admission and 50 rollouts, **24 h event-type macro AUROC was 0.829** versus **0.610** for persistence; macro Brier was **0.092** versus **0.274**. | Extended Data Table 1 |
| Known future timing improves short-horizon discrimination | In the same cohort, **1 h modality macro AUROC was 0.952** with ground-truth next-event gaps versus **0.764** in free mode. Free-mode modality Brier, however, was **0.097**, worse than persistence’s **0.091**. | Extended Data Table 1 |
| Zero-shot mortality discrimination is strong at a late prompt | From admission +48 h, predicting mortality within 72 h: **AUROC 0.972**, 95% CI **0.943–0.996**, with **0.822** rollout coverage; 2,000 sampled test patients and 50 rollouts each. | Methods, p. 22; Extended Data Table 2 |
| Other zero-shot outcomes are weaker | Prolonged stay: **AUROC 0.741**, coverage **0.804**, using the same late prompt. Readmission: **AUROC 0.609**, coverage **0.645**, using a pre-discharge cutoff, 1,000 patients, and 25 rollouts. | Methods, p. 22; Extended Data Table 2 |
| Embeddings retain clinical information | Discharge-state logistic probes achieved AUROC **0.747–0.937** across 15 ICD chapters and **0.717–0.945** across 29 comorbidities; five-fold cross-validation over three seeds. These are retrieval results. | Supplementary Tables 8–9 |
| NEWS2 is recoverable from observed-vital states | On 39,952 windows from 4,414 held-out patients, the MLP achieved **MAE 1.336**, versus **1.509** for linear regression on the same embeddings; within-patient **R² was 0.252**. | Supplementary Table 11 |
| Later embeddings improve survival ranking | DeepSurv on 26,476 patients: time-dependent C-index increased from **0.760 ± 0.005** at admission to **0.849 ± 0.013** at the end-of-stay setting, across five folds. IBS did not improve: **0.023 → 0.024**. | Methods, p. 25; Supplementary Table 13 |
| Intervention contrasts depend materially on scoring | Across 1,085 sepsis patients, simulated saline-minus-Ringer’s mortality was **+8.86 percentage points** over explicit-outcome runs, versus **+4.30 points** over all runs. Factual-arm mortality was **73.7%** under explicit-outcome scoring versus **21.0% observed**. | Extended Data Table 3 |

These results support useful representations and forecasting beyond a simple persistence comparator within the supplied MIMIC evaluation. They do not establish superiority over strong clinical forecasting models, raw multimodal generation fidelity, prospective clinical utility, or causal treatment effects. Time-controlled results use future timing information, while discharge and NEWS2 probes retrieve information already observed.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ limitations:** Teacher-forced next-event training creates exposure bias during rollouts; errors accumulate over long horizons. Representations prioritize predictive information rather than complete historical summaries. Intervention simulations are statistical forecasts, explicitly not causal models (Discussion, p. 14).
- **Analyst inference—selective scoring:** Excluding unscorable rollouts changes the probability denominator. Arm-dependent coverage and the explicit-outcome versus all-run mortality contrasts undermine the authors’ assertion that mortality overprediction leaves within-patient contrasts unaffected (p. 9; Extended Data Table 3; Supplementary Table 5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and ablation gaps:** Forecasting comparisons use persistence and prevalence baselines. Attention-term removal measures changes in attention, but the supplied text lacks matched retraining comparisons isolating temporal encoding, variational sampling, or individual modalities on downstream performance (Methods, p. 21; Fig. 6).
- **Analyst inference—prospective validity:** Survival evaluation selects embeddings using fractions of eventual stay duration while retaining admission-based outcome times. This retrospective setup does not establish performance at prospectively defined prediction landmarks (Methods, p. 25).
- **Analyst inference—scope and information handling:** Evaluation stays within the MIMIC family. Long records are processed in chunks, but cross-chunk memory is insufficiently specified. Some demographic prefix values come from the window end, creating a potential future-information risk for earlier positions (Methods, p. 15).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** The Results prose gives a forecasting AUROC range that omits lower free-mode values in Extended Data Table 1. Figure 5e labels NEWS2 recovery as linear regression, whereas the surrounding narrative describes an MLP; Supplementary Table 11 separates both.

## Open research questions
1. **Can long-horizon drift be reduced without sacrificing short-term accuracy?** Readmission and sepsis simulations remain weak or miscalibrated. Compare next-event training with rollout-aware training under identical generation budgets, reporting calibration and coverage.
2. **Which architectural components produce the gains?** Existing attention diagnostics do not isolate downstream benefits. Retrain matched models with conventional temporal encoding, deterministic latents, and modality removal.
3. **Do intervention contrasts survive changes in outcome scoring?** Estimated effects vary with rollout inclusion rules. Test larger budgets, explicit handling of unresolved trajectories, and validation against intervention data with controlled treatment assignment.
4. **Do representations generalize prospectively beyond this corpus?** External evaluation and fixed-landmark survival prediction are absent. Evaluate a frozen model on another health-record cohort using only information available at each prediction time.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods and supplementary results, but internal inconsistencies and evaluation choices constrain interpretation.

Material missing information includes released code or checkpoints, total training duration, inference latency, exact cross-chunk state handling, a complete timestamp-leakage audit, strong learned forecasting comparators, component-level retraining ablations, external validation, and prospective clinical evaluation.
