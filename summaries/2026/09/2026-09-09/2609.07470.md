# Measuring Language Transfer in Robot Policies: Adding Greek to a Cosmos3 Vision-Language-Action Policy

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.07470) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07470)


## One-sentence takeaway
Machine-produced bilingual demonstrations enable partial Greek instruction-following in Cosmos3, but the strongest replicated result is only a 6.9-percentage-point margin over wrong instructions on ninety tasks, illustrating why language transfer requires explicit controls (§5).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address adding Greek to a robot policy without collecting new demonstrations. They identify an evaluation gap: task success can reflect scene-conditioned behavior rather than comprehension, especially when each scene has only one trained goal. Their contribution centers on measuring language-dependent behavior reliably, rather than claiming a new architecture or benchmark-leading policy (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Data-based localization:** Machine-rephrase existing English instructions into Greek using a glossary and review pipeline; sample English and Greek equally during imitation training from the base Cosmos3 policy, with the multilingual text tower frozen (§3).
- **Attribution controls:** Compare correct English, correct Greek, and wrong instructions; add an English-only policy as an empirical Greek null. Evaluate shared-scene goals, per-task control margins, and multiple training seeds (§5).
- **Robustness experiments:** Test independent machine renderings, English rewording controls, and seven Greek phrasings per task. Distinguish improvements in absolute success from reductions in within-policy rewording penalties (§5).
- **Negative results and assets:** Report unsuccessful world-model warm starts and aggressive tower adaptation, document five retracted conclusions, and describe a released policy plus accompanying evaluation code (§§4–5, Table 3, §10).

The evidence supports partial Greek-conditioned goal selection on trained simulated tasks. Raw success substantially overstates that contribution because scene-based behavior already succeeds often. Recipe comparisons show consistent orderings in some experiments, but their three-run tests do not establish conventional statistical significance. The results do not establish natural human Greek comprehension, novel-task generalization, or physical robot success.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One language addition, one model family, and no novel goals; every Greek instruction is machine-authored. The independent set contains a documented mistranslation (§8; Figure 7).
- **Statistical limits:** Few training seeds, numerous comparisons without multiplicity correction, and slightly anti-conservative ten-goal bootstrap intervals constrain claims (§3; Appendix A).
- **Confounded interventions:** Warm-start experiments also change robot/domain exposure. Tower comparisons differ in size and pretraining; unfreezing tests only one aggressive configuration (§§5, 8).
- **Weak generation instrument:** A single VLM judge was calibrated only for coherence, using calibration assumptions about Greek outputs; content-match judgments lack equivalent validation (§3).
- **Reproducibility:** Exact instruction sets are available only on request; translated corpora and the adapted world model are withheld. The released policy is the best Greek seed, not a representative run (§10).
- **Analyst assessment:** Reduced seed spread on ninety tasks cannot be attributed solely to suite size: training data also changes and Greek performance lies nearer its floor, caveats acknowledged in §7. Likewise, the authors’ claim that machine-to-machine phrasing loss lower-bounds human-language loss remains untested.

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer survive native Greek phrasing?** No human-authored Greek is evaluated. Collect independently written, semantically checked commands and compare their control margins with both machine sets.
- **Does phrasing diversity help on ninety tasks?** Neither larger-suite arm uses diverse phrasings. Repeat the diversity experiment there with additional seeds and paired rewording penalties.
- **What causes the residual language gap?** Tokenization and representational quality remain entangled. Compare controlled transliteration or vocabulary adaptation against frozen-tower encoding probes.
- **Does Greek conditioning emerge in real-robot training?** DROID evaluation stops early and uses an offline proxy. Evaluate later checkpoints with uncertainty estimates, then test correct and wrong instructions on physical hardware.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports a detailed assessment of the measured outcomes, but broader and causal conclusions remain limited.

Material missing evidence includes native-speaker evaluation, novel-task tests, physical rollouts, completed DROID training, larger-seed comparisons, an in-domain warm-start control, and validated content-match judging. Exact instruction assets and accompanying code are described but not supplied here.
