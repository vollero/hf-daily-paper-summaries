# TANGO: Humanoid Navigation in Cluttered Environments with a Whole-Body Vision-Language-Action Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09158) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09158)


## One-sentence takeaway
TANGO learns whole-body humanoid navigation from simulated demonstrations, reporting lower collision rates than evaluated modular baselines and zero-shot real-world transfer, although missing table entries limit verification (§§4.2–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Humanoid navigation requires coordinating arms, torso, and legs around three-dimensional obstacles; a valid planar route may still cause body collisions. The authors identify a gap between language-guided navigation, which typically predicts planar commands, and whole-body controllers that lack integrated navigation intent (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Whole-body navigation policy:** A Qwen2.5VL-7B backbone initialized from InternVLA-N1 processes language and front/downward RGB history. A flow-matching action expert uses this context and proprioception to predict 29 joint angles and base orientation over action chunks, executed by a separate SONIC tracker (§§3.1–3.3). The authors’ novelty claim concerns this whole-body navigation formulation and integration; the backbone, tracker, and real-time chunking are borrowed components.
- **Plan–Edit–Track data pipeline:** A* planning and gait synthesis produce reference walks; obstacle-aware inverse kinematics and footstep editing add sidling, crouching, and stepping. Simulation tracking filters failed or colliding trajectories; retained reference motions supply action labels (§§3.2, 8.2).
- **Training and deployment integration:** Joint navigation/VideoQA training, behavior rebalancing, and training-time real-time chunking support streamed execution on a server-connected Unitree G1 (§§3.3–3.4, 6–7). Dataset and system releases are promised, not confirmed (§1).

The supplied prose supports comparative improvements and component sensitivity, but does not establish statistical significance or general collision-free operation. SR measures stopping within 3 m of the goal and does **not** require collision freedom; simulation CR and real-world mean collision counts are distinct metrics (§9). Table bodies are missing, and §§4.2 and 4.4 contain table references inconsistent with the captions.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Tracker capability limits harder traversal such as stairs; RGB sensing may struggle with ambiguous geometry or low light (§5).
- **Author-acknowledged:** Behavior-explicit prompts assist training and appear in cluttered hardware trials, but no controlled language ablation establishes their contribution (§8.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Different executors and sensing modalities complicate attributing modular-baseline gains solely to action representation. The planar ablation helps, but its numerical entries are unavailable (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Hardware evidence covers one robot platform and a small set of scenes with explicit traversal cues; broader autonomous behavior selection remains unestablished (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Path-conditioned obstacle placement and feasibility filtering may favor behaviors already supported by the planner/editor/tracker; coverage beyond that distribution is unclear (§§8.1–8.2).

## Open research questions

- **Can TANGO choose traversal behavior from goal-only instructions?** Existing cluttered hardware trials specify the behavior. Compare matched scenes using behavior-explicit versus goal-only instructions (§§4.3, 8.1).
- **How much does geometric sensing improve robustness?** RGB ambiguity is an acknowledged limitation. Compare RGB and RGB-plus-depth policies under controlled lighting and obstacle visibility (§5).
- **Does tracker portability extend to harder terrain?** The tracker swap tests the same augmented benchmark, while stairs remain a limitation. Evaluate matched policies and trackers on progressively harder terrain (§§4.4–5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several numerical comparisons are explicit, but the supplied text lacks table bodies, exact hardware outcomes, uncertainty estimates, repeated-seed results, augmented evaluation episode counts, detailed collision-recording criteria, and confirmation that promised assets are released.
