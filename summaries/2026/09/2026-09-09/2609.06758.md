# Agentic Visual Generation: From Generative Models to Agentic Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06758) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06758)


## One-sentence takeaway

This survey classifies visual-generation systems by how far controller decisions extend—from preparing inputs to reusing experience across tasks—and proposes matched evaluations to separate controller benefits from generator strength and additional computation (§§III, X).

## Problem and motivation

The authors identify inconsistent definitions of agenticity: planning, tool use, multiple roles, and reinforcement learning can appear without meaningful control over generation. This makes systems difficult to compare and improvements difficult to attribute. Their proposed organizing question is which later generation decisions a controller can change (§§I–II).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controller hierarchy:** L0 denotes fixed supporting components; L1 constructs conditions for a predetermined executor; L2 selects and invokes operations; L3 revises actions using current-task outcomes; L4 carries experience into later independent tasks. Classification uses maximum demonstrated causal reach, with conservative assignment when evidence is ambiguous (§III; Tables I–II).
- **Structured literature corpus:** The authors report releasing deduplicated records with capability, task, mechanism, feedback, persistence, resource, and provenance annotations, plus CSV/JSON exports and a statistics-generation script (§III-E).
- **Mechanism taxonomy:** Systems are further organized by specifications, executable objects, feedback sources, and persistent experience carriers (§§III-B, IV–VIII).
- **Evaluation framework:** Proposed counterfactuals remove one controller capability while matching generators, tools, budgets, and evaluators. This is a methodological contribution; the supplied text does not report experimental validation of its performance benefits (§X; Table VIII).

The evidence establishes the composition of the authors’ annotated corpus and specifies a comparative methodology. It does not establish that higher levels produce better outputs, that the corpus represents all visual-generation research, or that the proposed interventions have demonstrated causal gains.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Corpus construction favors controller-bearing systems, limiting interpretation of level frequencies as field-wide prevalence (§III-E1).
- **Author-identified evaluation risks:** Extra sampling, shared critics and evaluators, and unequal budgets can mimic controller improvements; persistent experience can introduce leakage, negative transfer, and stale decisions (§§X-E–G).
- **Analyst inference:** Classification reproducibility remains unverified here: the supplied text gives decision rules but no annotator agreement or independent classification audit (§III).
- **Analyst inference:** Half-year counts require caution because the latest interval is partial; raw counts alone do not establish comparable growth rates (Figure 5).
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated, and some figures have rendering artifacts; this analysis does not imply complete coverage.

## Open research questions

- **Does adaptive repair outperform equal-budget sampling?** The paper identifies selection bias but supplies no matched results. Next experiment: compare closed-loop repair with open-loop candidate generation using identical tools, budgets, and an independent evaluator (§X-E).
- **When does persistent experience transfer safely?** Staleness and negative transfer remain unresolved. Next experiment: evaluate chronological held-out tasks with intact, removed, shuffled, and stale memory, measuring transfer and rollback costs (§§VIII-F, X-F).
- **Can generator-side control reduce interaction costs without weakening verification?** This remains a proposed future regime. Next experiment: compare unified, hybrid, and external-controller designs with matched generator capacity and budgets, measuring repair, preservation, latency, and independent task success (§XI-D).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The framework and corpus trends are explicit, but the supplied source is truncated. Missing material includes the actual corpus and script, a usable project-repository address, detailed search and annotation procedures, agreement measurements, and experimental results validating the proposed matched evaluations or generator-as-controller benefits.
