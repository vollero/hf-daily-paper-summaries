# What LLM Trading Agents Actually Do in Production: A Six-Month, Population-Scale Record from Two Fleets

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05663) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across two related trading-agent deployments, the authors find no demonstrated directional edge, while configuration, interface rendering, and order mechanics strongly shape behavior and mechanical exits improve measured returns without establishing profitability.

## Problem and motivation
Trading-agent leaderboards report profits over short windows but reveal little about how user instructions, configuration controls, and execution infrastructure determine behavior. The authors address this gap with population-scale production telemetry, emphasizing behavioral mechanisms and failure modes rather than profitable trading strategies (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Production measurement record:** Connects a real-capital Base memecoin tournament with a mostly paper Hyperliquid perpetuals fleet through shared configuration and prompt lineage. Analyses combine comparable metrics, not raw rows, and resolve historical configuration at each turn (§§2–3).
- **Behavioral diagnosis:** Adds evidence on rendering-driven selection, volatility-insensitive sizing, concentrated liquidations, lost favorable price excursions, and contrasting text–slider conflict resolution across systems (§§4–6; §10).
- **Intervention and model evaluations:** Measures mechanical exit policies, a provisional neural order-risk probe, and paired model replay. Proposed volatility scaling, further training, and infrastructure changes remain a development program (§§5.4, 6.2, 8).
- **Methodology:** Provides a 17-rule analysis canon covering temporal clustering, permutation controls, fee normalization, censoring, and provenance, informed by the lab’s retracted claims (§9).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large but separated deployment windows | Terminal Pro: 3,505 funded vaults, 7.5M invocations over 21 days. DXAP: 231,638 finalized turns and 14,596 fills over 69 days; mostly paper execution. | Table 1 |
| DXAP underperforms the retail comparison | Trailing four days ending Aug 15: roundtrip win rate 41% of 353 agent roundtrips versus 50% of 16,009 retail roundtrips; median holds 2.05 versus 2.18 hours. | §3.2; Table 2 |
| Losses persist without fees | Jun 8–Jul 26 cumulative realized DXAP P&L: −$217K at a common 5.5 bps/side fee versus −$148K at zero fee. | §2.3 |
| Rendering routes selection | At the gainers leaderboard’s displayed rank-3/hidden rank-4 boundary, regression-discontinuity selection ratio is 1.75× [1.49, 2.06]; 2,339 entry observations across ranks 1–15, Jun 16–Jul 24. | §4.2; Fig. 3 |
| Leverage ignores measured volatility | Across 6,400 closed DXAP positions, median leverage remains 5.0× in every volatility sextile. Median net return falls from −10.6 to −98.2 bps between calmest and wildest sextiles. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| Liquidations concentrate in one configuration cell | Momentum posture with frequency slider 5 contains 128/205 liquidations, approximately 11% of positions; day-stratified odds ratio 22.37 [12.59, 37.45]. | §5.2; Fig. 5 |
| Favorable excursions are poorly retained | Of 6,400 closed positions, 2,765 reached at least +300 bps maximum favorable excursion within 24 hours; 49.3% of those closed with negative trade return. | §6.1; Fig. 7 |
| Mechanical brackets improve measured returns | A fixed 2% stop/4% target improves paired return by +39.0 bps/position [21.3, 56.5], or +16.6 [2.1, 30.8] excluding liquidations. No tested exit policy establishes outright profitability. | §6.2 |
| Neural risk increment is provisional and post-order | Against concrete-feature PR-AUC 0.1145, encoder increment is +0.0150, with 90% day-clustered CI [0.0013, 0.0312]; pre-order anchors show no significant increment. | §5.4; Fig. 6 |
| Replay finds no significant model-quality separation | Three models on 416 scenarios across seven days: regret 263.27–264.37 bps; minimum Holm-adjusted comparison p=0.46. Forced-action choice-flip rates differ: 35% versus approximately 90–95%. | §8.4; Fig. 8 |

These results support substantial operating-layer influence and identify losses that mechanical protection could reduce. The authors identify the rendering discontinuity as their clean causal result; configuration–outcome associations otherwise do not isolate causality. Replay non-significance does not prove model equivalence or predict live profitability, and the bracket result does not establish profitable deployment.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated execution limits:** DXAP paper fills omit slippage and funding and use a maintenance-margin placeholder that delays liquidation relative to the real venue. Fee restatement cannot correct these mechanics (§11).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Each era uses one venue, the systems share a lineage, and production models are concentrated in the Qwen family. The risk probe reads a separate encoder, not production-model activations (§11).
- **Author-stated statistical concerns:** Liquidation findings depend on a limited event set; closed-position analyses can censor unfinished lifecycles. Earlier apparent profitable cohorts disappeared after calendar checks (§§9, 11).
- **Analyst inference—comparison ambiguity:** Table 2 describes 1,961 sampled retail traders but reports 4,828 week-active retail accounts without explaining their relationship. The relationship between the 14,596 reported fills and 5,035 real-money fills also needs clearer accounting.
- **Analyst inference—causal and replication limits:** The supplied text gives limited detail on discontinuity diagnostics and bracket evaluation construction. Restricted raw telemetry prevents full independent reconstruction. The “six-month” record contains a two-month deployment gap (§3).

## Open research questions
- **Do protection mechanics improve live outcomes?** Current evidence is predominantly paper-based, and trigger attachment can fail. Next experiment: prospectively randomize atomic protected entry versus existing execution under realistic fees, funding, and margining.
- **Would volatility-scaled sizing reduce losses?** Flat leverage is documented, but enforced volatility scaling is proposed rather than tested. Next experiment: randomize order-path volatility scaling while holding entry and exit policies fixed.
- **Does the neural risk check generalize?** Its small increment appears only after order text and uses a proxy encoder. Next experiment: evaluate on a fresh time window against stronger order-feature baselines at a fixed flagging budget.
- **Can training produce durable decision improvements?** The reported fine-tuning improvement is internal and early. Next experiment: compare trained and base models on held-out replay followed by a preregistered prospective evaluation (§8.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the descriptive summary, but broader causal and deployment conclusions remain limited.

Material missing information includes complete per-analysis cohort definitions, retail denominator reconciliation, detailed bracket evaluation procedures, discontinuity diagnostics, full replay scoring specifications, and independently reproducible raw telemetry. Public asset availability is asserted by the authors and was not verified.
