# Omni Interaction Agent Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08977) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08977)


## One-sentence takeaway
Gander combines streaming multimodal conversation with an asynchronous task agent, achieving strong turn timing but lower end-to-end tool-task accuracy than every listed baseline on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 (§5.2, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
The authors target assistants that must continuously listen, observe, speak, and execute tasks while users interrupt or revise requests. They identify a tension between immediate conversational responses and sustained reasoning, arguing that external turn-taking modules and loosely connected voice interfaces inadequately coordinate conversation with task execution (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated interaction and execution:** A realtime “Cerebellum” delegates complex work to a training-free “Brain.” A persistent runtime supports task creation, steering, side queries, cancellation, permissions, and intermediate feedback. Evaluation uses a Codex worker driven by GPT-5.6 (§§3.2, 5.1).
- **Streaming interaction control:** The Cerebellum builds on MiniCPM-o 4.5 and a Thinker–Talker design. Each one-second chunk combines perceptual inputs, a listen/speak/interrupt decision, and generated text; a sliding window retains 128 chunks. Separate speech decoders generate audio incrementally (§3.3; Table 5).
- **Interaction-focused data:** The reported 2.7M-example corpus combines speech, audiovisual, agentic, and robustness supervision, including synthesized interruptions and asynchronous task trajectories (§4, Table 2).

The contribution is primarily their integration and training recipe: the paper explicitly builds on earlier dual-brain, Thinker–Talker, and flattened-stream approaches. Models, code, and data are announced, but release availability is not established by the supplied text (§1).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong turn timing, frequent fillers | On 100 Full-Duplex-Bench v3 scenarios, Gander reports 100% Take-turn and 8.0% Interrupt versus GPT-Realtime’s 96.0% and 13.5%. Filler is 51.6%, evaluated on 91 eligible scenarios. | §§5.1–5.2, Table 3 |
| Lower end-to-end task accuracy | On that benchmark, Gander’s strict Pass@1 is 0.400 versus 0.410–0.600 across listed baselines; ToolSel F1 is 0.759 and ArgAcc is 0.503, also below all listed baselines. Scoring includes synthesized speech and ASR. | §§5.1–5.2, Table 3 |
| Removing the frontend improves task scores | Transcript-driven back brain alone obtains Pass@1 0.520 and ToolSel 0.934 versus full-stack 0.400 and 0.759. This condition has no measurable audio interaction metrics. | Table 3 |
| Mixed spoken-conversation results | Across a 2,052-utterance evaluation, Gander versus Audio-Interaction: SpokenQA Llama/Web accuracy 75.60%/59.30% versus 67.31%/54.34%; VoiceBench AlpacaEval 3.96 versus 4.28 on a 1–5 scale, and SD-QA accuracy 46.84% versus 52.14%. Outputs are scored as text; Gander never invokes its Brain. | §§5.1, 5.3, Table 4 |
| Perception regresses from initialization | Audiovisual multiple-choice accuracy: WorldSense, 3,172 questions, 49.62% versus MiniCPM-o 4.5’s 55.70%; Daily-Omni, 1,197 questions, 78.53% versus 80.20%. Evaluation is turn-based without speech generation or Brain participation. | §§5.1, 5.4, Table 5 |
| Both modalities improve accuracy | On the same question sets, audiovisual input exceeds the better single modality by 5.01 percentage points on WorldSense and 19.13 on Daily-Omni. | Table 6 |

These results support strong benchmark-specific turn control and useful combined audiovisual input. They do not establish reliable sustained multimodal task execution. The back-brain comparison removes several components simultaneously, so it cannot isolate routing versus speech-channel failures. Likewise, modality gains do not establish the authors’ stronger claim about a particular fusion mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Data-sensitive routing, limited optimization for long-horizon interaction, restricted inter-component communication, memory management, and missing unified evaluation remain open (§6).
- **Internal inconsistency:** §4.2 and Table 2 describe substantial streaming video-QA training, whereas §5.4 says the corpus contains no video-QA supervision and largely irrelevant video distractors. This undermines the proposed explanation for perception regressions.
- **Analyst inference:** No controlled architectural or routing ablations establish that the control-token design causes better timing, or that task failures can be fixed through training alone (§5.2).
- **Evidence gaps:** No measured wall-clock latency, compute cost, repeated-run uncertainty, or detailed human-evaluation results accompany the low-latency and expressive-dialogue claims. Dedicated quantitative noise, multiparty, and long-workflow evaluations are also absent.
- **Comparison limits:** Baseline scores are imported from prior reports, and model sizes and interaction regimes differ (§5.1; Tables 3–5).

## Open research questions
- **Where does delegation lose accuracy?** The back-brain-only comparison confounds routing and audio processing. Next experiment: separately substitute oracle routing, verified transcripts, and text-only scoring.
- **Can perception improve without harming interaction?** Regression is observed, but the training-data account conflicts. Next experiment: audit the corpus and vary fine-grained visual supervision while measuring both perception and duplex behavior.
- **Does richer communication improve long workflows?** Current delegation primarily forwards transcripts and selected frames (§3.2.1). Next experiment: compare this interface with temporally structured multimodal context on tasks requiring mid-execution corrections.
- **What is the actual responsiveness–accuracy trade-off?** Timing fractions do not measure response delay. Next experiment: measure response and interruption latency, task success, and compute across chunk sizes and runtime modes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The reported benchmark outcomes and architecture are explicit, but broader causal and deployment claims are weakly supported.

Missing information includes a complete optimization recipe, hardware and latency measurements, statistical uncertainty, detailed human-study protocols, verified release artifacts, sustained-workflow evaluation, and a consistent account of audiovisual training data.
