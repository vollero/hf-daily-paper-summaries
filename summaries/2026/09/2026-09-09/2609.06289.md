# Steering Geometry: Validating Human Value Geometry in LLM Steering Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-09.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06289) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06289)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across the tested models, distribution-driven steering better preserves theory-specified value relationships than behavior-centric steering, and this geometric fidelity correlates with more theory-consistent cross-value transfer.

## Problem and motivation
Steering evaluations usually measure changes in one target behavior, overlooking effects on related or conflicting values. The authors ask whether steering directions preserve meaningful relationships among values or merely achieve desired outputs through behavior-specific shortcuts. They use Schwartz’s value circumplex as a reference structure and Moral Foundations Theory (MFT) as a coarser cross-framework check (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Contrastive benchmarks:** Construct 26,428 question–value–aligned-answer–neutral-answer quadruples from ValueBench and Touché across 20 Schwartz values, plus 1,200 samples across six MFT foundations (§3.1; Appendix E, G; Table 9).
- **Geometry evaluation:** Represent each intervention by its average residual-stream activation shift, then compare centered, normalized value directions against circumplex similarities and hierarchical relationships. Evaluate four distribution-driven and three behavior-centric methods (§§3.2–3.3, 4.1).
- **Cross-value transfer evaluation:** Steer each value and measure changes on other values’ held-out questions. Remove source and target average effects, then score whether residual transfer follows theoretical compatibility and opposition (§3.3.2).
- **Empirical findings:** Compare steering paradigms, model scales, and tuning regimes, with resampling, paraphrasing, and annotation-sensitivity checks (§5; Appendices C–K).

The contributions are benchmarks, evaluation protocols, and comparative findings; the steering algorithms themselves are existing methods.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution-driven methods recover stronger geometry | On Qwen3.5-9B Base, SAS achieves theory Spearman ρT = 0.5069, reported p = 8.5×10⁻¹⁴; CAA achieves 0.4606, versus raw activations at 0.2228 and OPT at 0.1138. | Table 1 |
| Target accuracy alone misses the distinction | On Qwen3.5-9B Base, aligned-answer selection gains over the unsteered baseline are +9.57 percentage points for OPT, +10.03 for SAS, and +11.74 for CAA. | Table 4; §4.2 |
| Llama reproduces the geometry split, with larger accuracy differences | On Llama3.1-8B Base, CAA has ρT = 0.4996 versus OPT’s −0.0486; their target-selection gains are +17.64 and +6.03 percentage points, respectively. | Tables 6–7 |
| Scale improves theory correlation in tested families | Qwen3.5 CAA ρT rises from 0.2537 at 0.8B to 0.4606 at 9B; SphericalSteer rises from 0.2186 to 0.3962 over the same sizes. | Table 8 |
| Instruction tuning reduces circumplex correlation | Qwen3.5-9B CAA ρT falls from 0.4606 Base to 0.2351 Instruct; SAS falls from 0.5069 to 0.3256. | Table 1 |
| MFT supports a coarser separation | Within-family minus cross-family cosine similarity is 0.0969 for CAA versus 0.0107 for OPT on Qwen, and 0.1038 versus 0.0272 on Llama; no significance tests are reported for this metric. | Table 2; Appendix G |
| Geometry correlates with transfer fidelity | Across seven methods and two backbones, n = 14: geometry’s Spearman correlation with continuous/hierarchical transfer is 0.84/0.77, versus 0.78/0.59 for target accuracy gain. | Table 12 |
| Dataset perturbations retain the main separation | Disjoint resampling, full paraphrasing, and single-label-prioritized sampling preserve stronger theory correlation for distribution-driven methods on both backbones. | Table 10 |

These results support a relationship between extracted geometry and theory-consistent transfer in the tested setting. They do not establish that geometry causes better transfer, that poorly aligned vectors necessarily exploit shortcuts, or that matching the theoretical reference guarantees desirable real-world behavior. “Comparable” target performance also applies unevenly across backbones.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Fine-grained evaluation centers on Schwartz; MFT validates only family separation. Cross-cultural, cross-language, and broader framework generalization remain open (§7).
- **Author-stated representation limits:** Directions aggregate potentially multiple values. The single-label-prioritized subset remains approximately 50% single-label, so it is not a clean monosemanticity test (Appendix F.2).
- **Data quality:** A manual audit judged 89% of 100 generated examples valid; failures included value leakage and insufficient contrast. Generation and filtering also share a model in the Touché pipeline (Appendices E.1, F.1).
- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** Correlation tests treat 190 pairwise similarities as observations despite shared value vectors. Value-label permutation tests would better assess dependence-sensitive significance. Table 12 does not test whether the two predictors’ correlations differ significantly (Appendices H, K).
- **Analyst inference—scope of behavior:** Two-option answer selection does not establish effects on open-ended generation. Base/Instruct comparisons do not isolate which post-training stage explains the observed association (§4.2; Table 1).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Llama CAA ρT is 0.3701 in Table 3 but 0.4996 in Table 6, without an explicit reconciliation. Some Qwen original-setting entries also differ between Tables 1 and 10. The claim of degradation across all metrics is too broad: COLD-Steer’s polarity separation increases after instruction tuning (Table 1).

## Open research questions
- **Does preserving geometry causally improve transfer?** Current evidence compares methods observationally. Add a geometry regularizer to an output-optimized method and compare transfer at matched target accuracy.
- **Does the result survive alternative human reference structures?** Fine-grained tests use an idealized circumplex. Repeat evaluation with population-specific human similarity matrices and multilingual contrastive data.
- **Does geometric fidelity predict open-ended behavioral effects?** Evaluation uses answer selection. Test generated responses to held-out scenarios with independent human ratings of target and collateral value changes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The paradigm separation has substantial supplied evidence, but stronger causal and generalization claims remain unsupported.

Material missing or unclear information includes exact validation partitioning, complete per-method hyperparameter configurations, seed-level variability, numerical per-method transfer values underlying Figures 4–5, argument-level split isolation, and explanations for inconsistent table entries. Code and data availability is author-reported and was not independently verified.
