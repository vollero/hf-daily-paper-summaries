# Beyond Selection: Token Parameterization for Extreme Visual Token Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35232) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35232)


## One-sentence takeaway
Braco combines a truncated DCT backbone with coordinate embeddings and learned spatial pooling, retaining 94.0% of the uncompressed model’s normalized aggregate score at 16 visual tokens while reducing prefill FLOPs from 8.67T to 1.25T on LLaVA-1.5-7B (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Very small visual-token budgets reduce VLM inference costs but can discard evidence needed for grounding and compositional understanding. The authors identify two weaknesses in existing approaches: pruning may miss rare, important regions, while learned resamplers add computation and alignment complexity (§1).

Their proposed gap is conceptual: compression depends both on **which subspace preserves useful information** and on **how its coordinates support downstream learning**, rather than token selection alone (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Parameterization framework:** Separate basis transformation and structured truncation from orthogonal coordinate changes within the retained subspace (§3.1).
- **Diagnostic objectives:** Combine retained energy and task-direction readability into a compressibility score; combine token-correlation/scale penalties and geometric compatibility into a learnability surrogate (§3.2). These are diagnostics, not guarantees of VLM performance.
- **Braco coder:** Keep a low-frequency DCT block, add input-independent polar Fourier coordinate embeddings, optionally apply inverse DCT, and append sparsemax-pooled tokens from the original spatial grid (§§3.3–3.4, Appendices G–I). The “residual” branch pools original tokens rather than explicitly pooling the discarded residual.
- **Budget-dependent interface:** Use coefficient coordinates for smaller backbones and inverse-DCT coordinates for larger ones; calibrate this choice using unlabeled encoder outputs (Appendices H, J.6).

## Evidence and main results
“Acc.” is the mean of eight benchmark scores individually normalized to the corresponding uncompressed model; it is not raw prediction accuracy (Appendix J.5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong performance at small budgets | LLaVA-1.5-7B, 576 input tokens: Braco retains 95.2/94.0/93.2/91.2 Acc. at 25/16/9/4 tokens; prefill FLOPs are 1.37/1.25/1.15/1.09T versus Vanilla’s 8.67T. | Table 1 |
| Lower latency than QueCC, but not every baseline | At 16 tokens, Braco: 94.0 Acc., 40.59ms; QueCC: 93.9, 63.22ms; TokenPacker: 93.7, 37.87ms. | Table 1 |
| Cheap compressor relative to QueCC | At the 16-token pre-projector boundary: Braco 1.073ms/1.389G FLOPs versus QueCC 17.864ms/109.504G—reported reductions of 16.6×/78.8×. | Table 2 |
| Hybrid allocation improves aggregate score | At nine tokens: hybrid c2s5 scores 93.2 Acc., versus backbone-only c3s0 at 89.8 and pooling-only c0s9 at 92.0. | Figure 5a; Table 6 |
| Coordinates affect optimization despite preserving the subspace | With backbone sizes 16/64, inverse-DCT coordinates reach held-out pretraining cross-entropy <2.50 in 707/237 steps versus coefficient coordinates’ 860/590; at size four, only coefficients reach the threshold. | Appendix J.1; Figure 3c |
| Larger-input performance persists | Qwen2.5-3B, 3,645 input→20 retained tokens: Braco scores 90.8 Acc. versus QueCC’s 68.9; prefill costs are 3.50T/88.64ms versus uncompressed 25.91T/240.66ms. | Table 8 |
| Key aggregate comparisons survive repeated training | Three matched seeds at 16 tokens: Braco 94.12±0.10 Acc., QueCC 93.88±0.12, TokenPacker 93.70±0.15; values are mean±sample SD. | Table 12 |

The evidence supports a favorable aggregate accuracy–prefill-cost trade-off under the tested retraining harness. It does not establish superiority on every benchmark or latency metric, statistical significance of small differences, or full generation-time speedups: the main pipeline measurements stop at prefill.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation primarily covers LLaVA-family pipelines and standard single-image benchmarks. Video, dense localization, irregular features, and new domains may require calibration (Appendix K).
- **Author-stated:** Only selected Braco–QueCC and Braco–TokenPacker comparisons have three training seeds; other accuracy settings use one checkpoint (Appendix K).
- **Author-stated:** The coordinate cutoff is a heuristic, not universal or a theorem over all rotations. Calibration thresholds vary across encoders and distributions (Appendix H; Table 11).
- **Analyst inference:** CelebA readability probes and linear task-direction modeling provide limited evidence for complex grounding behavior (§3.2; Appendix J.1).
- **Analyst inference:** A100/A800 prefill results do not directly validate the mobile-deployment motivation or decode-time benefits (§1; Appendix J.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does compression preserve localized evidence reliably?** Aggregate scores do not isolate rare-detail failures. Next experiment: evaluate controlled small-object, text, and dense-localization cases across backbone–pooling allocations.
- **Does calibration predict downstream winners on new encoders?** Table 11 reports cross-encoder calibration, but not corresponding end-to-end winner checks. Next experiment: retrain coefficient and inverse-DCT variants around each predicted crossover.
- **How much benefit survives complete generation and device constraints?** Reported costs emphasize GPU prefill. Next experiment: measure full-response latency, memory, and energy across output lengths and resource-constrained hardware.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported method and experimental comparisons; broader deployment conclusions remain uncertain.

Material omissions include numeric diagnostic weights λ/β/γ, exact fixed prompt length, detailed residual-scorer architecture and annealing settings, per-benchmark seed uncertainty, full-generation measurements, and independently verified code/checkpoint availability.
