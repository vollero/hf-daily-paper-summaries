# CaptchaArena: A Large-Scale, Fine-Grained Dataset for Training Computer-Use Agents on Interactive CAPTCHAs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31957) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31957)


## One-sentence takeaway
CaptchaArena supplies execution-verified interactive CAPTCHA trajectories that support training a unified 9B agent reaching 71.7 Pass@1 on its balanced test set, while precise grounding and transfer to unfamiliar CAPTCHA types remain weak.

## Problem and motivation
A failed CAPTCHA can block a computer-use workflow, yet training resources often trade broad task coverage against realistic interaction and detailed supervision. The authors identify gaps in reasoning-annotated screenshot–action trajectories, executable browser evaluation, and grading of irregular targets: bounding boxes can accept clicks outside the actual target (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset and environment:** 50,000 puzzles covering 20 types and five interaction modes, split into 42,000 training, 4,000 validation, and 4,000 test puzzles. Every reference solution is executed and accepted by the browser verifier; each puzzle includes a screenshot–action trajectory (§3; Table 2).
- **Fine-grained supervision:** 46,000 training/validation trajectories receive teacher-generated reasoning with independent judging and additional review. Three irregular-target types carry pixel masks; point targets retain distance tolerances (§§3.2, 4.1; App. D).
- **Unified trained policy:** CaptchaAgent uses Qwen3.5-9B, language-only LoRA supervised fine-tuning, then GRPO with verifier-derived correctness, task-specific partial credit, submission incentives, and turn penalties. Vision components remain frozen (§4; Apps. K–L).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Per-type comparisons, submission rates, repeated-rollout results, external evaluations, and fixed-click regrading distinguish procedural failures, grounding weaknesses, and grading artifacts (§5; Apps. E–J).

The authors claim novelty in combining broad coverage, fine-grained supervision, and executable interaction; the supplied text does not independently establish their “first” claim.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Training substantially improves internal performance | On 4,000 test puzzles, 200 per type: untrained 9B backbone 11.4, SFT 70.5, RL 71.7 Pass@1; human reference 94.1. Trained policies use five rollouts per puzzle to estimate Pass@1; other systems use one. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| RL adds a modest aggregate gain with regressions | SFT→RL: Pass@1 70.5→71.7; Pass@5 86.0→86.8. Click Order Pass@1 falls 93.2→90.1 on the same test split. | Table 5 |
| Training improves episode completion | Internal-test submit rates: backbone 20.5%, SFT 96.8%, RL 97.9%. | Tables 7–8 |
| Some capabilities remain weak | RL Pass@1: Place Dot 20.1 versus humans 100.0; Patch Select 10.4 versus 73.5; Dice Count 7.3 versus 83.0; each type has 200 test puzzles. | Tables 3, 9 |
| External transfer improves but remains limited | One rollout per puzzle, locally served, same screenshot interface: SFT→RL Pass@1 47.2→51.0 on Open CaptchaWorld and 13.6→20.0 on Halligan. Scores are unweighted type averages. | App. I; Tables 10–11 |
| Rectangle grading inflates measured accuracy | Holding RL clicks fixed, mask→rectangle accuracy rises 68.2→73.4 on Geometry Click and 60.6→82.3 on Pick Area, with 1,000 rollouts per type. | App. J.1; Table 13 |

These results support the value of the complete training pipeline under the evaluated interface. They do not isolate the contribution of reasoning annotations, masks, dataset scale, or individual reward terms. External results demonstrate transfer to independently built local suites, not success against deployed anti-bot systems. The authors’ interpretation that large Pass@1–Pass@5 gaps indicate execution variance is plausible, but these metrics alone do not identify the underlying cause.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Annotation depends on commercial models; frozen vision components may constrain grounding; discrete actions cannot reproduce mouse-trajectory signals used by behavioral detectors (§6).
- **Reported generalization boundary:** Six types reuse source images across training and test. Image Recognition overlap was not measured by the audit. Reuse does not establish duplicate puzzles, but weakens claims of entirely unseen visual inputs (App. H).
- **Analyst inference:** Fixed layouts and viewport may permit interface-specific learning; external evaluation partly addresses this, though Halligan performance remains low (Apps. C, I).
- **Analyst inference:** No controlled ablations isolate reasoning supervision, mask supervision, or reward components. Observed reward shortcuts are described, but corresponding comparative results are absent (App. L).
- **Comparison limits:** General GUI agents are unadapted while CaptchaAgent receives task-specific training; UI-TARS also lacks timed hold support. Converted Open CaptchaWorld scores are explicitly incomparable with its published protocol (Apps. I, J.2).
- **Uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated training-seed results are reported. The human reference uses two annotators, with each puzzle attempted once (App. G).

## Open research questions
- **Does reasoning annotation improve performance beyond verified actions?** Its effect is not isolated. Train matched policies with and without reasoning, holding puzzles and compute constant.
- **Would trainable vision components improve precise grounding?** Vision stays frozen and Place Dot remains weak. Compare language-only training against vision adaptation on source-disjoint grounding tests.
- **How robust is the policy to interface changes?** Layouts are fixed and external transfer remains limited. Evaluate controlled changes to control positions, render scales, and visual assets.
- **Can RL improve consistently without task regressions?** Aggregate gains coexist with Click Order regression. Repeat training across seeds and ablate mining and reward terms using validation-based selection.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported dataset structure and evaluation findings; causal explanations are less certain.

Material missing information includes component ablations, training-seed variability, statistical uncertainty, Image Recognition source-overlap measurements, total RL runtime, and controlled layout-robustness tests. Artifact availability is claimed by the authors but was not independently checked.
