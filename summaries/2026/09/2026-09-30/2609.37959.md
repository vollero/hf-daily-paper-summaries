# TabFM: A Zero-Shot Foundation Model for Tabular Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37959) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37959)


## One-sentence takeaway
TabFM transfers synthetic-only pretraining to supervised tabular prediction without task-specific weight updates, leading default foundation models on TabArena, while validation-fitted ensembling and LLM-guided pipelines further improve reported performance.

## Problem and motivation
Tabular learning usually requires fitting models and searching configurations separately for each dataset. The authors seek to amortize this work through in-context prediction, but identify mixed numerical/categorical features and expensive attention across table cells as obstacles to scaling (§1).

Here, “zero-shot” means no task-specific model tuning: prediction still requires labeled training rows as context (§1, §3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **TabFM architecture:** A roughly 400M-parameter model combines learned Fourier cell embeddings, grouped features, alternating induced column attention and row attention, CLS pooling, and a deep masked in-context predictor. The contribution is this integrated design; several components explicitly build on prior methods (§3.1, Table 1).
- **Synthetic-only pretraining:** Structural causal models generate tables with varied dependencies, categorical features, missingness, noise, and class balance. A curriculum increases context length while keeping rows processed per optimization step constant (§3.2, Figure 3).
- **TabFM+:** Frozen-weight inference combines feature crosses, SVD features, transformed table views, validation-fitted ensemble weights, and classification calibration (§4).
- **TabFM-Auto:** Gemini-3.8-Flash synthesizes and evaluates dataset-specific Python pipelines around frozen TabFM. Auxiliary models may also be fitted and blended; full details are deferred to a separate paper (§5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Base TabFM leads default models on classification | Across 38 datasets and 594 tasks: 1768.6 Elo versus EXAONE-Tabular’s 1768.0 and AutoGluon 1.5 extreme’s 1669.7; geometric-mean error 0.0957 versus 0.1009 and 0.1010, respectively. | Table 2(a), §3.3 |
| Base TabFM has a larger regression lead | Across 13 datasets and 222 tasks: 2055.2 Elo versus EXAONE-Tabular’s 1973.1 and AutoGluon 1.5 extreme’s 1851.2; geometric-mean error 16.40 versus 16.58 and 16.78. | Table 2(b), §3.3 |
| Frozen-weight extensions improve aggregate rankings | TabFM+ reaches 1838.0 classification / 2189.2 regression Elo; TabFM-Auto reaches 1940.7 / 2392.4, taking the top positions on both tracks. Same TabArena protocol. | Table 2 |
| Auto improves most datasets, but can regress | Relative to base TabFM, Auto improves 41 of 51 datasets, including all 13 regression datasets; on the ten classification datasets without improvement, reported test-error increases are at most 1.8%. | §5, p. 10 |
| Auto uses substantial task-specific search | Candidate pipelines receive internal three-fold validation; search ends at 96 evaluations or six hours. Test splits are excluded from candidate execution. | §5 |
| Scaling remains partly quadratic | Pretraining reaches 16,384 rows and 100 columns; induced column attention is linear in rows, but pooled predictor attention remains quadratic in rows. | §§3.1–3.2, Figure 3 |

TabArena uses repeated ten-fold cross-validation, equal dataset weighting, and median Elo over 100 bootstrap rounds (§3.3). These results support strong aggregate performance on this benchmark. They do not establish statistical separation for the near-tied classification leaders, universal superiority, or lower deployment cost. The supplied text does not identify each dataset’s underlying error metric, so geometric-mean errors should not be interpreted as a single shared physical unit.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Pretraining covers limited table sizes; larger tables require generalization or subsampling. Free text lacks semantic tokenization, and relational databases require joining or flattening (§6).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** No component ablations isolate Fourier embeddings, grouping, predictor depth, curriculum, or individual TabFM+ transformations. Explanations attributing regression gains to spectral embeddings or variance reduction remain hypotheses (§§3.3–4).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Confidence intervals and significance tests are absent. The small classification Elo lead over EXAONE-Tabular therefore has unclear statistical robustness (Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency and calibration:** No measured latency, memory, training cost, or calibration-specific results substantiate the efficiency and calibrated-prediction claims.
- **Analyst inference—Auto attribution:** Auxiliary-model blending is permitted, and pipeline selection uses the first fold’s training split. The text does not isolate blending effects or test sensitivity to that selection split (§5).

## Open research questions
- **Which components drive transfer?** Component contributions are not isolated. Next experiment: matched-budget ablations of embeddings, grouping, attention stages, and curriculum.
- **How far does table-size generalization extend?** Larger shapes fall beyond pretraining support (§6). Next experiment: controlled row/column scaling with accuracy, memory, and latency measurements.
- **How robust are Auto’s gains?** Search-seed and validation-split sensitivity are unreported. Next experiment: repeat searches across splits and seeds, separately disabling auxiliary-model blending.
- **Are base predictions reliably calibrated?** Calibration is claimed without dedicated evidence. Next experiment: compare base and calibrated variants using reliability diagrams and calibration metrics across held-out datasets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture, benchmark protocol, and aggregate results are explicit, but causal explanations and broader validity remain uncertain.
