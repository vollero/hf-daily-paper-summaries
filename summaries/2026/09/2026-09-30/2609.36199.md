# PreviewDiff: Multimodal Critic-Guided Search over Diffusion Latents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36199) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36199)


## One-sentence takeaway
PreviewDiff uses multimodal critiques of intermediate diffusion previews to guide latent continuation search, with reported image and video prompt-following gains over compute-matched final-sample selection, although metric labeling and budget details limit interpretation.

## Problem and motivation
Diffusion generators still miss object counts, attribute bindings, spatial relations, and temporal actions. Best-of-N selection spends additional compute ranking completed outputs but cannot repair a promising partial generation. The authors identify a gap in using multimodal judges as sources of corrective semantic actions during denoising, rather than solely as scalar reward models (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Intermediate semantic control:** Decode a clean-latent estimate at selected checkpoints; ask a multimodal critic to score prompt satisfaction and propose compact correction notes (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Corrected latent search:** Append recent fix notes to the original conditioning, optionally add negative-prompt fragments, locally re-noise the latent, and continue denoising. Beam pruning retains promising branches; the highest-scoring completed survivor is returned (§§3.2–3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **Training-free evaluation across modalities:** Compare this combination against Best-of-N, scalar trajectory search, particle sampling, and a video search baseline across multiple backbones and benchmarks (§§4–5). The distinctive contribution is semantic action generation inside diffusion search; tree search and multimodal scoring themselves are established components in the paper’s related work.

The evidence supports improved prompt adherence in the tested settings, with auxiliary evaluators and human judgments providing corroboration beyond the main critic. It does not establish universal superiority, latency savings, or complete compositional correctness. Tables labeled “P@4” contain values above one despite §4.1 distinguishing mean scores from exact-score-4 pass rates; those entries cannot confidently be interpreted as pass rates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Early previews can be unreliable, and aggressive re-noising can destabilize generation. A restart-depth ablation is identified as useful but is not reported (§3.2).
- **Author-stated:** Exact counting, spatial grounding, attribute binding, and precise temporal sequences remain failure modes (Appendix D, Fig. 11).
- **Analyst inference:** Using the same Gemini model for search and primary evaluation leaves evaluator alignment as a risk. Different scoring prompts, auxiliary evaluators, and human judgments mitigate it without fully resolving it (§4.1, Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Compute fairness is difficult to audit: exact fixed-budget configurations and baseline implementation settings are not supplied. Wall-clock latency is explicitly unmeasured, despite sequential search dependencies (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Component attribution remains unclear: “without language feedback” and “without prompt editing” are not operationally distinguished in enough detail (§5.7). Human evaluation forces choices without ties, and numerical uncertainty is unavailable in the extracted figure (Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Which restart depths balance correction and stability?** The paper flags this missing ablation (§3.2). A next experiment could sweep restart depth while holding checkpoint schedules and total compute fixed.
- **How much improvement comes specifically from semantic edits?** Table 3’s component definitions are unclear. Compare score-only search, critic edits, and control edits under identical branching and restart budgets.
- **Does the advantage persist under matched latency or monetary cost?** Only denoiser steps and verifier calls are reported (§4.3). Measure end-to-end performance while allowing parallel Best-of-N execution.
- **Can search preserve precise temporal sequences?** Appendix D shows skipped actions and incorrect color ordering. Test temporally explicit critique against the current critic on ordered-action prompts, using independent evaluation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and direction of reported gains are clear. Material missing or ambiguous information includes metric definitions for “P@4,” the “MCTS-CGD” row naming, exact fixed-budget/search configurations, most automatic-evaluation sample counts, seed variability and uncertainty, numerical human results, detailed component-ablation behavior, runtime/cost measurements, and confirmed code availability.
