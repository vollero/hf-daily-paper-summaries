# Periodic Weak Spots: Phase Sensitivity from Chunked KV-Cache Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36322) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36322)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors find that chunked KV-cache compression creates stride-periodic retrieval weak spots hidden by average accuracy, with causal interventions implicating phase-dependent attention contributions and learned gate preferences in tested models (§§2–5).

## Problem and motivation
Chunked KV-cache compression reduces long-context memory and attention costs by summarizing consecutive tokens before future queries are known. It also introduces a token’s **phase**: its position modulo the compression stride. Identical information can therefore be retained differently after small input shifts.

The authors identify a gap in aggregate evaluation: average retrieval accuracy can conceal recurring failures at particular source phases, distinct from broad absolute-position effects (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Phase-aware evaluation:** Measure retrieval across source-position residues while fixing prompt length and query position and matching the mean and variance of target positions; supplement this with code-completion filler sweeps (§2; Appendix C.1).
- **Controlled architectural study:** Pretrain Qwen3-0.6B-derived compressed models and full-attention baselines, varying stride, window overlap, gates, positional encoding, head count, and memory policy (§3; Table 8).
- **Mechanistic evidence:** Use mean-replacement attention-readout knockouts to identify phase-dependent contributions, then cycle offset-specific gate parameters to test whether retrieval weak spots move with gate preferences (§4; Appendices E, H).
- **Restricted theoretical account:** Prove concentrated adjacent key–value selection is optimal under stated conditions, and that gate-only population gradient flow can produce persistent phase preferences; the general dynamics theorem does not guarantee distinct selections across heads (§5; Theorems 8, 12).

The contribution is primarily a failure-mode diagnosis and explanatory analysis; the supplied text does not establish a successful repair.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large models exhibit periodic retrieval gaps | At 128,000 tokens with 16,000 key–value pairs, best-to-worst answer-prefix accuracy gaps are **40.2/19.1 pp** for Flash base/post-trained, **34.8/14.8 pp** for Pro base/post-trained, and **6.1 pp** for post-trained V4.1-Flash; periods match compression strides. | §2.2; Fig. 2; Appendix C.1 |
| Similar averages can conceal much worse weak phases | Eight-KV-head W8/S8 compression achieves **59.4% mean, 9.9% minimum** full-vocabulary retrieval accuracy versus **61.1% mean, 58.4% minimum** for full attention, over 24 source residues under Prefix Padding. | Table 8, p. 50 |
| Learned gates are unnecessary for the effect | W8/S8 uniform averaging retains a **19.2 pp** full-vocabulary accuracy gap versus **75.3 pp** for the reference under Prefix Padding; under In-sequence Padding, its gap is **37.0 pp**. | Table 8; Fig. 11 |
| Boundary splitting is insufficient | In the W8/S8 reference, accuracy differs by **more than 50 pp** among phases whose adjacent key and value remain in one window. | §3.2, p. 6 |
| Attention contributions depend on phase | Mean replacement produces heterogeneous phase-dependent losses in compressed models, compared with relatively uniform effects across residues in the full-attention baseline. | Fig. 4; §§4.1, E.5–E.6 |
| Gate edits redirect rather than repair weak spots | Across five non-overlapping-window models and cycles of ±1–3 offsets, non-boundary predicted-versus-observed change correlations have squared Pearson **R² = 0.66–1.00**; cycling lowers overall full-vocabulary accuracy by **3.3–18.4 pp** on Prefix Padding retrieval. | Appendix H.3; Fig. 20 |
| Code predictions can flip periodically | Flash-Base’s FP8 reference completion switches in an exact four-token cycle across 16 raw-prefix docstring lengths; V3.1-Base ranks the competing token higher on only **4/64** matched raw-prefix filler variants. | Fig. 1; Appendices B.1–B.2; Table 4 |

The controlled pretraining comparisons support chunked compression as a source of periodic sensitivity in the tested architectures. Interventions establish causal reliance on particular readouts and support a gate contribution to within-window asymmetry. They do not establish exclusive information storage, a universal mechanism, or broad application-level failure rates.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The mechanism is best supported in controlled settings. Gate cycling does not restore boundary retrieval, and uniform averaging remains phase-sensitive; learned gate specialization is therefore incomplete as an explanation (§7; Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Knockouts affect combined compressed/local readouts and may produce atypical activations. Identity-intervention and equal-size alternative-head controls are not reported; heatmap cells are exploratory point estimates, without significance established for every cell (Appendices E.3–E.5).
- **Author-stated:** DeepSeek comparisons include architecture, training, precision, backend, and intervention-granularity differences. Code accuracies describe selected shifted examples rather than a code benchmark (Appendix B.2; E.6).
- **Theory scope:** Dynamics assume exact embedding geometry requiring dimension at least vocabulary size, fixed embeddings, gate-only population training, and small initialization; head coverage need not be complementary (Assumption 11; Theorem 12).
- **Analyst inference:** Synthetic adjacent single-token retrieval and a few code examples provide limited evidence about multi-token semantic retrieval or refusal/compliance robustness; the latter is explicitly left for future work (§7).
- **Coverage:** The supplied source is truncated, including material evaluation and training details; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions
- **Can phase coverage improve without losing efficiency or average accuracy?** Gate cycling moves failures and reduces overall accuracy. Next experiment: train coordinated head/layer coverage objectives and compare mean accuracy, worst-phase accuracy, and inference cost (§7; Appendix H).
- **What explains boundary failures and sensitivity without learned gates?** Neither gate cycling nor the gate-preference account resolves these cases. Next experiment: vary key–value separation and window overlap, with separate compressed/local readout interventions (§§3.2, 7; Appendix E.5).
- **Does sensitivity generalize to semantic and robustness tasks?** Current evidence centers on synthetic retrieval and selected code completions. Next experiment: phase sweeps over multi-token factual retrieval and meaning-preserving request shifts, measuring task success and refusal/compliance (§7).
- **Does static specialization survive realistic training conditions?** The dynamics theorem uses restrictive geometry and gate-only population training. Next experiment: vary embedding geometry, jointly train payload maps, and use finite-sample optimization while tracking gate concentration and phase accuracy (§5; Assumption 11).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied results consistently support the central empirical finding, while mechanistic generalization remains restricted.

Material missing information includes portions of the exact retrieval construction, per-residue DeepSeek results, backbone/local-memory specifications, and pretraining settings removed by truncation. Code, checkpoint, and dataset release availability is not established in the supplied text.
