# On the Off-Policy Teacher in On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38360) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38360)


## One-sentence takeaway
SCOUT adapts a trainable teacher to student-generated prefixes using outcome-reward reinforcement learning, improving student macro-average accuracy over frozen-teacher on-policy distillation by 1.2–2.6 percentage points across three math configurations and 3.1 points in one code configuration (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) trains students on their own generated trajectories, but asks teachers to supervise prefixes outside their usual generation distribution. The authors identify this teacher-side mismatch as a neglected optimization target: existing approaches generally keep the teacher fixed and regulate where or how its supervision is used (§§1, 6).

Their diagnostic finds declining teacher continuation accuracy and higher entropy on longer student prefixes. This motivates adapting the teacher itself, although continuation performance is an indirect measure of token-level supervision quality (§3.1, Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Teacher adaptation:** SCOUT periodically samples teacher continuations from student-generated prefixes and updates the teacher with GRPO using verifiable outcome rewards. Only teacher continuation tokens receive gradients; the updated teacher supplies subsequent student supervision (§3.2).
- **Coupled training:** Student OPD continues over full student trajectories while the teacher adapts to their evolving prefix distribution. Prefix ratios increase linearly during training; main experiments update the teacher every ten student steps (§3.2; §5).
- **Mechanism and comparison evidence:** Fixed-prefix diagnostics, a teacher-training-without-prefix-conditioning control, a fixed-data compute comparison, and an OPTR combination test examine why adaptation helps (§5). The implemented student objective uses a sampled K1 reverse-KL approximation with clipped policy gradients (Appendix B.2).

Main aggregates use three training seeds and benchmark-specific repeated sampling; reported ± values are cross-run SDs. Average-improvement one-sided p-values versus OPD are 0.0598, 0.0497, 0.0140, and <0.001 for 4B math, 4B code, 8B math, and Skywork math respectively (Appendix B.3; Tables 10–13). The paper marks significance at p<0.1.

The evidence supports better aggregate performance than frozen OPD in these settings and a benefit from prefix conditioning. It does not establish universal benchmark gains: Skywork-setting HMMT accuracy decreases, and AIME 2025 ties OPD (Table 3). Lower entropy and improved continuation accuracy support the proposed mechanism but do not directly prove more accurate token-level supervision.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Evaluation covers contained math and function-level code tasks, not long-horizon interactive agents or repository-level engineering (Appendix A).
- **Authors’ stated requirements and cost:** SCOUT requires trainable teacher parameters and verifiable rewards, and adds wall-clock and memory costs. The compute comparison does not rule out OPD gains from fresh data (Appendix A; §5).
- **Analyst inference—frequency confounding:** The update-frequency experiment also changes teacher learning rates for frequent updates; its every-step setting uses one run. It therefore does not isolate update frequency alone (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference—statistical limits:** Three training runs and one-sided tests at p<0.1 provide limited precision; the supplied significance procedure does not describe multiple-comparison correction (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Relay-OPD code results use one training run after syntax failures. SCOUT has the strongest code aggregate among tested OPD methods, but GRPO scores higher (Appendix C.1; Table 11).

## Open research questions
- **Does adaptation help long-horizon agents?** These tasks remain unevaluated (Appendix A). Next experiment: compare SCOUT and frozen OPD on repository-level or interactive tasks under matched compute.
- **Can weaker feedback replace verifiable rewards?** Current adaptation depends on verifiable outcomes (Appendix A). Next experiment: compare exact verification with learned rewards and measure both student accuracy and teacher continuation reliability.
- **Which prefix curriculum and update schedule work best?** The paper uses linear prefix growth, and frequency comparisons change learning rates (§3.2; Appendix C.2). Next experiment: jointly vary curriculum, frequency, and learning rate with replicated, compute-matched runs.
