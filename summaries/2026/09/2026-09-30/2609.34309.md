# MaLiang-Harness: A Programmable Path to Image and Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34309) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34309)


## One-sentence takeaway
MaLiang-Harness organizes executable image and video generation around persistent programs, traceable edits, and revision-specific visual checks, with strong reported results for GPT-6-Astra but no controlled evidence isolating the harness components’ benefits.

## Problem and motivation
A runnable visual program can still produce incorrect composition, appearance, or motion—the authors’ **Program-to-Visual (P2V) gap**. Addressing it requires inspecting rendered outputs and revising code while preserving earlier artwork and checking whether edits invalidate previously satisfied requirements. The authors identify a need for a shared, stateful process across image and video rendering backends (§1, pp. 3–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **P2V formulation:** Separates executable correctness from satisfaction of visual requirements (§1).
- **Unified generation interface:** An MLLM plans, selects a backend such as Canvas, SVG, Scene2d, or Three.js, generates code, renders it, and revises it using visual feedback. Optional supplied, generated, or searched assets supplement code (§3.1).
- **Three coordinated mechanisms:** Persistent Executable Generation (PEG) stores programs, assets, context, and revisions; Traceable Generation Process (TGP) links operations and rendered evidence to revisions; Revision-aware Editing and Verification (REV) supports restoration and requires current-revision checks before completion (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Empirical characterization:** Evaluates model differences in completion, visual quality, and cost, plus an exploratory association with public general-capability scores (§4).

The contribution is a generation framework and evaluation study; the text does not describe training a new visual generation model.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Astra leads reported image qualification | On 50 MaLiang-IBench prompts, GPT-6-Astra completes 50/50 and qualifies 48/50 (96%); GPT-5.6-Sol completes 48/50 and qualifies 43/50 (86%). Qualification requires ≥4/5 in alignment, aesthetics, and composition. | Tables 2–3; §4.1 |
| Completion can exceed visual qualification substantially | GPT-5.6-Luna completes 46/50 image tasks, but only 22/50 meet all three quality thresholds. | Table 3 |
| Astra leads reported video qualification | On 13 MaLiang-VBench prompts, Astra completes 13/13 and qualifies 10/13 (76.9%); GPT-5.6-Sol completes 7/13 and qualifies 5/13 (38.5%). Qualification adds motion coherence to the image criteria; review uses 12 ordered frames per successful video. | Tables 4–5; §§4.1, 6.2 |
| Temporal quality remains restrictive | Astra’s 13 successful videos all meet the aesthetics threshold, but only 10 meet motion coherence ≥4/5. | Table 5 |
| Reported efficiency depends on the denominator | Image cumulative time per qualifying output is 3.70 minutes for Astra versus 3.28 for GPT-5.6-Sol; video time per successful output is 10.00 versus 9.27 minutes. Times include failed attempts and retries; run conditions differ. | Tables 2, 4; §6.2 |
| General scores only partly track visual performance | Across 11 configurations, AA Index and full-set image quality pass rate have Spearman ρ=0.65. Both Luna models have displayed AA score 37, but pass rates are 44% and 88%. | Fig. 9; §§4.3, 6.3 |
| Refinement can introduce defects or lose detail | Path-tracing edits introduce biscuit overlap artifacts; finer Canvas brushwork leaves the image stylized and reduces some textures. These are qualitative cases. | Figs. 7–8; §4.3 |

The evidence establishes differences among the **evaluated configurations**, and shows why successful execution alone is inadequate. It does not establish a causal benefit from PEG, TGP, or REV individually, superiority over direct image/video generators, or reliable continuous video motion.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ caveats:** Image results combine batches, retries, and historical runs. Cost columns use inconsistent aggregation rules; budgets, timing conventions, and feedback differ, including no visual feedback for DeepSeek-V4-Pro (§6.2).
- **Assessment limits:** Completion verdicts are MLLM self-assessments. Quality evaluation primarily uses GPT-6-Sol; video review uses a single reviewer and was not formally blinded. Sampled frames cannot rule out brief faults or establish frame-by-frame fluidity (§§3.3, 6.2).
- **Subset bias:** Mean image quality excludes failures, so models with few successful outputs have poorly comparable means (§4.2).
- **Authors’ failure modes:** Photorealism remains difficult; refinement can stagnate, cycle, or exhaust budgets. State preservation and traceability do not guarantee effective corrections (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied study lacks component ablations, controlled direct-synthesis comparisons, and reported uncertainty estimates. Its small task sets limit how confidently rankings generalize.
- **Association caveat:** AA settings may differ from harness settings, and the evaluated DeepSeek-V4-Pro checkpoint is unconfirmed (§6.3).

## Open research questions
- **Which components improve outcomes?** No component ablation isolates PEG, TGP, or REV. Next experiment: remove each mechanism under matched models, prompts, budgets, and feedback.
- **Does verification catch continuous motion defects?** Ordered-frame review leaves intermediate behavior unresolved. Next experiment: compare sampled-frame judgments with blinded full-video assessment.
- **Can refinement recognize diminishing returns?** The paper documents stalled runs without a demonstrated progress-aware stopping strategy. Next experiment: compare fixed budgets with progress-based stopping and recovery.
- **Do richer renderers improve realism consistently?** Path tracing is demonstrated qualitatively without a controlled comparison. Next experiment: evaluate matched prompts across backends with equal budgets and blinded realism ratings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the framework and reported outcomes, but comparative validity is constrained.

Material missing information includes complete benchmark prompts and selection procedures, exact per-model settings and budgets, full judge instructions and calibration, repeated-run variability, component ablations, and verification of repository contents and released assets.
