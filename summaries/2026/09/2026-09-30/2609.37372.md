# Think Before You Score: Thinking Reward Model for Visual Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37372) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37372)


## One-sentence takeaway
TRM generates case-specific evaluation rubrics before assigning visual rewards, and structured training followed by PD-GRPO improves reported preference accuracy and downstream image generation/editing performance, although rubric-specific causality and absolute score calibration remain unproven.

## Problem and motivation
Visual reward models provide preference signals for reinforcement learning of image generators and editors. The authors argue that direct scoring—and even reasoning before scoring—often leaves implicit which requirements matter for a particular candidate. Their proposed gap is explicit, case-adaptive evaluation criteria that capture instruction fulfillment, visual defects, and preservation requirements (§§1, 3).

A second problem concerns training: the authors argue that Bradley–Terry-style preference optimization keeps rewarding larger score gaps after candidates are correctly ordered, pushing bounded pointwise scores toward opposite extremes (§4.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive rubric evaluation:** TRM generates atomic criteria from the task condition and candidate, makes binary criterion-level judgments, summarizes three evaluation dimensions, and predicts a final score. Generation evaluates alignment, aesthetics, and technical quality; editing evaluates instruction following, source consistency, and visual quality (§3.2; Appendix A.5).
- **Structured supervision:** Approximately 20K generation and 28K editing cases receive teacher-generated rubrics, expert review, judgments, and calibrated scores. Three complementary training formats jointly teach rubric generation, rubric-based scoring, and integrated evaluation (§4.1).
- **PD-GRPO:** Starting from Qwen3.5-9B with structured SFT, the model learns from approximately 4K preference pairs. Independently sampled evaluations of each candidate receive a threshold reward based on separation from the other candidate’s mean score. Further separation earns no additional preference reward, while inference remains pointwise (§4.2).
- **Downstream reward use:** TRM supplies the reward for FlowGRPO optimization across multiple image generation and editing models (§5.3). These experiments test practical utility beyond reward-model benchmark accuracy.

These results support improved preference discrimination and useful downstream optimization signals in the evaluated settings. They do not establish that adaptive rubrics independently cause the gains, that scores are absolutely calibrated, or that TRM outperforms every proprietary evaluator. Main generation comparisons exclude predicted ties for pointwise models; the appendix addresses this for TRM and its same-protocol baseline, without providing equivalent tie-aware results for all competitors.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. It explicitly acknowledges additional multimodal reward-inference costs and finite scoring granularity that produces ties (Appendices A.3.2, A.4.3).

**Analyst inferences supported by the experimental coverage:**

- **Component attribution remains unresolved:** SFT/RL comparisons do not isolate adaptive rubrics from extra data, teacher calibration, reasoning traces, or fixed-rubric alternatives (§5.4).
- **Training–benchmark independence is unclear:** Several benchmark families supply training cases. Prompt-level train/validation separation is described, but decontamination against final evaluation benchmarks is not established (§4.1; Appendices A.1–A.2).
- **Calibration evidence is limited:** Reduced polarization and better rankings do not directly validate absolute score calibration (§4.2; Figure 7).
- **Uncertainty is unquantified:** Tables report no confidence intervals or repeated-run variability, making small differences difficult to assess.
- **Reproducibility is incomplete:** Extensive hyperparameters and prompts are supplied, but annotation agreement, parsing-failure rates, full artifact-release contents, and SenseNova-U1.5 downstream training configuration are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Do adaptive rubrics outperform fixed rubrics under matched supervision?** No component-isolating comparison is reported. Train matched models with adaptive, fixed, and absent rubrics using identical cases and optimization.
- **Does candidate-conditioned rubric generation omit inconvenient requirements?** Rubrics depend on the candidate, but coverage reliability is not measured. Compare prompt-only and candidate-conditioned rubrics against expert checklists on controlled failure cases.
- **Does PD-GRPO preserve absolute calibration?** The paper measures rankings and score distributions rather than calibration against held-out human scores. Compare PD-GRPO and BT using calibration error, preference accuracy, and repeated-score stability.
- **How robust are gains on independent distributions?** Training draws from multiple benchmark families, while final benchmark decontamination is unclear. Evaluate on newly collected prompts, unseen generator outputs, and expert-labeled editing cases.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and reported benchmark gains, but stronger causal and generalization conclusions are limited by missing rubric ablations, benchmark-overlap audits, statistical uncertainty, absolute calibration tests, annotation agreement, parsing-failure rates, measured inference costs, and verified release contents.
