# SAKI: Maximal-Coupling-Routed Teacher Supervision for On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36601) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36601)


## One-sentence takeaway
SAKI uses maximal-coupling correction events to route teacher-argmax supervision during teacher-guided distillation, improving seven-benchmark average math accuracy over matched TRB baselines at two student sizes while accelerating exact guided rollout.

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation trains on student-generated prefixes, reducing training–inference mismatch, but weak students can reach states that poorly represent the teacher’s reasoning behavior. Teacher-guided rollout improves these states, yet TRB retains the same reverse-KL update at every position. The authors identify an objective-side gap: reverse KL gives little weight to teacher-preferred tokens with low student probability, especially where guided rollout must override a student proposal (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Correction-triggered supervision:** SAKI retains sampled-token reverse-KL supervision on accepted proposals and replaces it with negative log-likelihood of the teacher’s highest-probability token at correction positions. The residual correction token controls the next prefix; the teacher argmax controls the update (§3.4; Appendix L).
- **Minimal-intervention realization:** The geometric, student-centered KL bridge is inherited from TRB. SAKI realizes that distribution through maximal coupling, whose correction probability equals student–bridge total variation and is bounded by \(\sqrt{\epsilon/2}\). This provides the supervision-routing event without a separate selection threshold (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Efficient execution:** An engine-resident speculative verifier batches teacher evaluation, commits through the first rejection, samples an exact residual correction, and discards the invalid suffix. The authors prove equivalence to sequential guided sampling under the stated execution assumptions and report correctness checks (§3.5; Appendices I, K).

The results support improved aggregate performance under the reported protocol, with controls supporting a benefit from correction-based placement beyond supervision count or scalar TV weighting. They do not establish universal benchmark gains, training-seed robustness, or generalization beyond the tested model family and domain. The fixed-prefix results measure teacher alignment rather than reasoning correctness, and the speedup measures rollout rather than end-to-end training.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated systems limitation:** Online teacher verification remains intrinsically costly; throughput depends on response length, correction rate, and committed tokens per verification wave (Appendix K.5).
- **Analyst inference—narrow scope:** Experiments use two Qwen3 Base student sizes, one constructed teacher, and mathematical reasoning. Compatibility across different tokenizers or termination conventions is not demonstrated (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain repeatability:** Main accuracy tables provide no training-seed confidence intervals or repeated-run summaries. Probe bootstrap intervals quantify variation across sampled prompts, not training runs (Tables 1–2; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic sampling boundary:** The probe deliberately balances initial correction and accepted positions, so its alignment effects need not represent their natural rollout frequencies (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—limited tuning evidence:** The paper adopts one guidance schedule and unit-weight correction loss; sensitivity to those choices is not evaluated (§4.1; Appendix L). Sharing a rollout schedule controls that configuration, but does not establish identical prefixes as student policies evolve.

## Open research questions
- **How stable are the accuracy gains across training seeds?** Main-table uncertainty is unavailable. Repeat matched SAKI, TRB, and placement-control runs with multiple seeds and report paired benchmark differences.
- **Does routing transfer beyond this model family and mathematics?** Current evaluation is narrow. Test another token-compatible teacher–student family and non-math tasks under the same controls.
- **Which guidance duration and correction strength work best?** Only the adopted annealing schedule and unit weighting are evaluated. Sweep trust-region radius, annealing duration, and correction-loss weight at matched training budgets.
- **Does persistent teacher support predict correctness gains?** The probe establishes alignment, not a causal link to successful reasoning. Measure alignment on naturally sampled held-out prefixes and relate changes to answer correctness across controlled routing variants.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the supplied method description and reported results; **medium** in broad effectiveness claims.
