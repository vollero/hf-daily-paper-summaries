# Anisotropic Representations Improve Planning in JEPA World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37441) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37441)


## One-sentence takeaway
Learning a constrained anisotropic Gaussian regularization target improves LeWorldModel’s visual planning success in four environments while preserving its predictor and Euclidean planner, although task alignment depends on the training distribution (Secs. 4–5).

## Problem and motivation
JEPA world models use latent distance to rank predicted outcomes against a visual goal, so representation geometry determines which terminal errors matter. The authors show that preventing collapse and accurately predicting dynamics do not ensure that these rankings agree with task costs.

In their linear Gaussian analysis, isotropic regularization induces inverse-state-covariance weighting: errors in low-variance training directions receive greater weight. When feasible actions require trading off errors across directions, this can produce positive planning regret even as prediction error vanishes (Secs. 3.1–3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Prediction–planning separation:** The authors characterize the geometry selected by joint prediction–SIGReg training and construct finite-horizon systems where exact conditional-mean prediction still yields suboptimal task decisions. A nonlinear toy experiment illustrates the same separation (Sec. 3).
- **AnisoWM with ΛReg:** Replace the fixed isotropic target with a learned diagonal Gaussian covariance. Its trace fixes total variance, and a condition-number bound limits anisotropy. Features are standardized only inside the regularizer; prediction and planning retain their original coordinates (Secs. 4.1–4.2).
- **Conditional alignment analysis and evaluation:** Predictive training selects variance allocation through the state-whitened noise covariance. The authors establish conditions for reduced regret and evaluate planning success and latent–outcome ordering across four visual control environments (Sec. 4.3; Apps. C–E; Sec. 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate prediction can coexist with planning regret | Under the constructed linear Gaussian family, every isotropic-training optimum has exact encoded conditional-mean rollouts, yet exact latent planning retains strictly positive limiting task regret as process noise vanishes. | Theorem 1; App. E.5 |
| Learned anisotropy can remove the constructed mismatch | For the specified two-level state covariance and matching anisotropy bound, the learned metric becomes proportional to the Euclidean task metric and limiting regret is zero. | Sec. 4.3; Theorem 4 |
| Nonlinear toy model preserves the separation | Across ten seeds, reducing process-noise scale by 30× lowers conditional-mean prediction error while normalized physical planning regret stays near 0.16, close to the 0.1607 inverse-covariance reference. | Sec. 3.4; Fig. 1 |
| Planning success improves over LeWM | Rounded mean success over three training seeds, with shared κ=2: TwoRoom **93% vs 87%**, Reacher **89% vs 86%**, PushT **97% vs 96%**, Cube **79% vs 74%**. Each environment uses 50 initial–goal pairs and a 50-step budget per pair. | Fig. 2; Sec. 5.2; App. F.1 |
| Predicted-cost ordering improves everywhere | Pairwise outcome-ordering accuracy, LeWM → AnisoWM: TwoRoom **0.575 → 0.646**, Reacher **0.676 → 0.723**, PushT **0.594 → 0.621**, Cube **0.537 → 0.553**, using identical recorded candidate sequences within each comparison. | Table 1; App. F.2 |
| Representation-only ranking has an exception | PushT’s realized-observation cost ordering is **0.647 → 0.641**; the other three environments improve. | Table 1 |

The strongest empirical evidence is the matched LeWM comparison: architecture, training settings, seeds, evaluation pairs, and planner are shared. The results support benefits from changing regularization in these settings. They do not establish universal task alignment, statistical significance of each success gain, or superiority over all reference methods; some references perform better in TwoRoom and Cube (Secs. 5.1–5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated distribution dependence:** Learned allocation can worsen regret. In the two-dimensional analysis, reversing the relevant noise ordering makes every anisotropic bound worse than the isotropic baseline; excessive anisotropy can also overshoot alignment (Apps. C.1, C.4).
- **Author-stated theoretical scope:** Results concern global optima under Gaussian linear assumptions and small-noise, fixed-horizon limits. Nonlinear robustness establishes stability near those models, not their selection by unrestricted nonlinear training (App. E.7).
- **Author-stated diagnostic limits:** The anisotropy sweep uses single runs. Latent MSE across models is measured in different learned coordinates and is not a common physical prediction-error metric (Apps. F.5, F.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Four environments and three primary training seeds provide limited evidence for broader transfer. LeWM per-seed results and uncertainty for the primary success differences are absent; Table 4 gives only AnisoWM per-seed values.
- **Analyst inference:** No matched fixed-anisotropic-target ablation isolates the value of learning the allocation from simply permitting anisotropy. Ranking diagnostics lack reported uncertainty across seeds.
- **Comparison boundary:** Other baseline success rates are imported from prior work rather than rerun in the matched pipeline (Sec. 5.1).

## Open research questions
- **When does predictive allocation align with task priorities?** The theory gives both favorable and harmful distributions. A next experiment could vary noise covariance and task metric independently while measuring selected geometry and physical regret (App. C.4).
- **How much does learning the spectrum contribute?** Environment-specific spectra evolve during training, but fixed-target controls are absent. Compare learned targets with fixed admissible spectra under identical seeds and budgets (Fig. 4; App. F.4).
- **How robust are gains to anisotropy and evaluation sampling?** The sweep uses single runs and primary evaluation uses a fixed pair set. Repeat the sweep across seeds and independently sampled initial–goal pairs (Apps. F.1, F.5).
- **Does compensation persist under broader nonlinear dynamics and longer horizons?** Nonlinear theory covers bounded perturbations of selected linear models. Test stronger nonlinearities and increasing rollout horizons with physical prediction-error and regret measurements (App. E.7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, proofs, protocols, and consistent matched-baseline improvements, but broader effectiveness remains uncertain.
