# When Does Dense Retrieval Need Asymmetric Geometry? A Bias-Variance Theory of Shared and Dual Projections

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32488) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32488)


## One-sentence takeaway
Dual projections beat shared projections when directional signal outweighs their extra estimation variance in the authors’ local Gaussian model, and a training-only selector successfully predicts the lower held-out operator-loss geometry in the reported experiments.

## Problem and motivation
Frozen query and document embeddings can be adapted with one shared projection or separate dual projections. Shared projections constrain inner-product scoring to a positive-semidefinite (PSD) operator; dual projections permit any low-rank operator, reducing approximation bias while adding opportunities to fit noise. The authors identify a missing theoretical criterion for deciding when that flexibility is justified by signal and available training data (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Exact approximation analysis:** Characterizes the two operator families and derives shared geometry’s approximation error from skew-symmetric energy, negative eigenvalues, and discarded positive eigenvalues, compared with dual’s singular-value truncation error (§2, Theorem 1).
- **Local bias–variance boundary:** Derives the additional tangent-space dimension \(k=r(2p-r-1)/2\) and proves that dual has lower local asymptotic Frobenius risk exactly when directional signal \(\delta^2>\sigma^2k\) (§3, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1).
- **Geometry selection:** Gives a Stein-unbiased selector with exact Gaussian selection-power and model-choice-regret formulas, then introduces CARS, which rewards agreement between dual–shared residuals across training halves and penalizes disagreement (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Empirical tests:** Separately evaluates controlled retrieval, trained full-corpus adapters, and spectral operator estimation on real embeddings (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Shared geometry has an exact approximation penalty | Nearest rank-constrained PSD error comprises skew energy, negative-eigenvalue energy, and omitted positive-eigenvalue energy; dual error is the singular-value tail. | §2, Theorem 1 |
| Dual’s risk advantage has an exact local boundary | Under isotropic Gaussian operator noise and local rank-\(r\) alternatives, dual wins iff \(\delta^2>\sigma^2r(2p-r-1)/2\). | §3, Corollary 1 |
| Stronger synthetic mismatch moves the crossover to fewer samples | First positive mean Dual-minus-Shared NDCG@10 occurs at \(n=256\) for 0.8-radian rotation and \(n=64\) for 1.2 radians; rank 8, five paired seeds, one positive against 299 negatives. | §5.1, Figure 3; Appendix F |
| Query rotation increases the mean retrieval advantage | Across five datasets and BGE-base/GTE-base, mean NDCG@10 gap rises from approximately 0.0032 at 0° to 0.0146 at 90°, a reported 4.6-fold increase; rank-16 adapters, 1,024 training queries, three paired seeds, exact full-corpus search. | §5.3, Figure 5 |
| More training data favor dual in displayed retrieval grids | Shared wins 13/16 cells at \(n=32\); dual wins all 32 cells across \(n=1,024\) and \(2,048\). Four dataset–encoder grids use ranks 4/8/16/32 under specified query rotations. | Figure 6; Appendix F.1 |
| Held-out operator-loss trends move toward dual | All 168 comparable sample-size slopes are positive across FEVER, NQ, ArguAna, SciFact, four encoders, and three seeds. This is a trend toward dual, not universal dual dominance. | §5.4; Figures 8–9 |
| CARS improves operator-based geometry selection | Reported mean accuracy is 90.1%, with 85/100 strict encoder–dataset fold wins and 49–96% mean-regret reduction relative to the better fixed geometry; five datasets, four encoders, five held-out folds. | §5.4, Table 1; Appendix F.1 |

The evidence supports the predicted signal–variance tradeoff and CARS’s usefulness for **held-out operator loss**. It does not establish an exact boundary for NDCG or demonstrate that CARS improves retrieval rankings. The Gaussian regret theorem concerns choosing between unconditional fixed-model risks, explicitly excluding the selected estimator’s post-selection risk (§3.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The exact boundary assumes local, isotropic Gaussian noise. Anisotropic noise, ranking-metric risk, and partial sharing remain future directions (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Rank-changing points require tangent cones; targets far from the PSD manifold introduce curvature terms outside the local theorem (Appendix B.2).
- **Author-stated:** Operator-level conclusions require bilinear scoring or additional norm control; an identical dual operator can produce different projected-cosine rankings (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Artificial query rotation provides controlled mismatch evidence, but its applicability to naturally occurring mismatch remains uncertain. The rank–sample grids also use rotated settings (Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The reported MS MARCO full-corpus retrieval test contains only 43 queries, limiting that comparison’s precision (Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** CARS’s expectation identity assumes independent, centered half-sample residual errors; the paper provides no exact finite-sample selection guarantee for nonlinear spectral residuals on real embeddings (Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does CARS improve NDCG-based geometry choice?** Selection is evaluated against operator loss. Next experiment: compare CARS with fixed geometries using held-out full-corpus NDCG and ranking regret.
- **How robust is the boundary to anisotropic noise?** The exact theorem assumes isotropy. Next experiment: vary noise covariance in controlled simulations and test covariance-aware risk penalties.
- **Can partial sharing outperform binary selection?** The authors propose selecting separately parameterized directions alongside rank. Next experiment: compare partial-sharing adapters with Shared, Dual, and CARS at matched sample sizes.
- **Does the crossover persist without imposed rotation?** Controlled rotations underpin the mismatch study. Next experiment: repeat rank–sample grids on unrotated embeddings across the same datasets.
