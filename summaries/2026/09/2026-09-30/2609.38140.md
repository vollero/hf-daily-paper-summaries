# Breaking the Uniformity Trap: Scaling Video Diffusion Model via SplitMoE

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38140) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38140)


## One-sentence takeaway
SplitMoE combines separate semantic and generic experts with VAE-guided routing to improve all reported benchmark dimensions over a controlled standard-MoE baseline at matched activated parameters, while retaining sparse-routing latency overhead (Table 1; Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that conventional token-wise MoE routing and uniform expert usage conflict with video’s correlated, spatially continuous, and semantically imbalanced tokens. Coherent objects can be scattered across experts, producing fragmented assignments and temporal inconsistency. Their identified gap is routing that preserves video structure while allowing sparse capacity scaling (§1; Fig. 2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Split-role architecture:** Separate semantic and generic expert pools receive independently selected tokens. Semantic experts target structure; generic experts retain residual modeling capacity. Only generic experts use explicit loss-free balancing (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Prototype-guided specialization:** Clean VAE features supply soft prototype assignments for router alignment. Pull-push regularization and a historical token bank keep prototypes near visual features while discouraging collapse (§3.3).
- **Controlled evaluation and routing analysis:** Same-source ablations assess partitioning and prototype guidance, while routing visualizations and denoising analyses examine coherence and claimed coarse-to-fine specialization (§§4.2, 4.5).

The evaluated model upcycles Wan 2.2’s low-noise checkpoint, using 20 semantic and 80 generic routed experts per MoE layer, with two semantic and six generic experts selected per token plus a continuously active shared expert (§4.1).

The controlled comparisons support the complete method over the tested same-source alternatives. They do not establish universal superiority across video models or isolate uniform balancing as the sole cause of fragmentation. External-model comparisons have different training recipes and capacity, as the authors acknowledge (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reliance on frozen visual features and sparse-routing overhead; adaptive partitioning, distributed routing, and broader tasks remain future work (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is untested beyond the reported checkpoint, fixed partition, and benchmark setting (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No isolated semantic-balancing toggle is reported. The full method changes partitioning, guidance, and balancing together, limiting causal attribution specifically to the “uniformity trap” (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The temporal-weight interpretation needs clarification: Eq. 5 normalizes weights separately within each branch, and Eq. 6 sums both branches. The reported changing aggregate semantic weight therefore requires a definition absent from the supplied text.
- **Comparison caveat:** Official Wan2.2 uses a high-/low-noise ensemble, whereas controlled fine-tuning starts from its low-noise checkpoint (§4.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does removing semantic balancing independently improve coherence?** Existing ablations do not isolate it. Compare otherwise identical prototype-guided models with semantic balancing enabled and disabled.
- **How sensitive is performance to expert allocation?** Only one evaluated semantic/generic split is described. Sweep partitions and active-expert allocations at matched budgets.
- **Does the method transfer beyond this upcycling setting?** Broader tasks remain future work. Repeat controlled comparisons on another video backbone and longer outputs.
- **What precisely changes across denoising stages?** The reported temporal weights are unclear under branchwise normalization. Measure explicitly defined branch contributions and test selective branch suppression across stages.
