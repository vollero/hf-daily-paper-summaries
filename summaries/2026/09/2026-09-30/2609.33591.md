# Pretraining Transformers with Quantized Softmax in Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33591) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33591)


## One-sentence takeaway
In matched language-model pretraining experiments, differentiating through attention-grid calibration and choosing an appropriate straight-through surrogate sharply reduce quantized-softmax loss gaps, with selected configurations approaching softmax validation loss but without demonstrated hardware speedups (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Low-precision Transformer systems often retain higher-precision attention exponentials and normalization. The authors investigate whether coarse exponential reconstruction can support pretraining, where an approximation changes both predictions and the gradients used to learn model weights (§1).

Their identified gap is the interaction between data-dependent calibration and backward rules: detaching a row maximum is harmless for exact softmax, but detaching extrema that define an approximation grid can remove necessary gradient terms. Inference-only evaluations cannot establish whether such operators train successfully (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **A controlled operator family:** K-interval attention reconstructs exponentials using K+1 grid values, then normalizes. It varies row-wise MinMax versus fixed-window calibration, interpolation (LERP) versus nearest-grid rounding, and weight-level versus probability-level straight-through estimators (STE). The fixed window uses τ=6 and zeroes its tail (§3, Table 1).
- **Explicit backward rules:** The authors derive calibration derivatives and distinguish normalization Jacobians evaluated at hard weights (Weight-STE) versus interpolated weights (Prob-STE). They verify formulas against automatic differentiation (Appendix B).
- **Training interventions and comparisons:** Unchanged-forward calibration ablations expose delayed failure; factorial experiments identify calibration–surrogate interactions; longer training and downstream evaluation distinguish near-baseline language-model loss from task equivalence (§5).

These are studies of training behavior with fp32 attention arithmetic, not implemented low-bit kernels (§§1, 4).

The strongest evidence concerns the damage from omitted calibration gradients and the large differences within the tested operator family. Single-seed bootstrap intervals measure evaluation sampling, not training variability. Near-baseline loss does not establish downstream equivalence, and the proposed range–resolution feedback mechanism remains an author hypothesis rather than an identified causal mechanism (§§4–6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** Long-horizon and larger-model suites use one seed. The 1B-model experiment is an early-training probe on another corpus; no learning-rate sweep was run (§6).
- **Forward and environment confounds:** Historical STE implementations share a mathematical forward but differ in floating-point arithmetic. Training stacks also correlate with operator families. A strict-forward replication supports contrast directions in one setting, without resolving all long-horizon comparisons (Appendix C.2).
- **Window selection:** τ=6 was selected on frozen weights using WikiText-103 validation later reused in two suites; no alternative window width was trained (§6, Appendix C.3).
- **Incomplete mechanism isolation:** Fixed-window calibration changes resolution, calibration derivatives, and tail handling together. The tail control is single-seed and not cost-matched; geometry associations do not identify feedback causality (§5.4, Appendix D.4).
- **Evaluation limits:** The reference model barely solves induction and retrieval probes, limiting conclusions about those abilities. Downstream operator differences have no cross-seed replication (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference:** Because experiments simulate reconstruction in fp32 and measure no kernels, performance findings alone cannot establish practical compute or memory savings (§1).

## Open research questions
- **What causes the proposed resolution feedback?** Geometry correlates with loss, but interventions do not isolate the mechanism. Freeze grid width or swap the surrogate at a saved checkpoint, then compare subsequent loss and geometry (§5.4, Appendix D.6).
- **Which window width works best during training?** Only τ=6 was trained after inference-based selection. Run paired, multi-seed training sweeps over window width using a separate selection split (§6).
- **Do near-baseline outcomes survive larger, longer training?** Larger-model evidence covers only early training. Extend matched strict-forward comparisons across model sizes and training budgets with multiple seeds (§§4, 6).
- **Can the method deliver hardware savings without losing its training behavior?** No kernels or speed measurements are supplied. Implement reconstruction and backward kernels, then measure throughput, memory, and matched loss (§1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported large-effect comparisons and method definitions; **medium** in broader generalization and mechanistic interpretation.

Material information unavailable includes demonstrated kernel efficiency, larger or longer training results, multi-seed long-horizon downstream results, training sweeps over window width and learning rate, and confirmation of the promised public artifact release. Some training software versions were not recorded (Table S4).
