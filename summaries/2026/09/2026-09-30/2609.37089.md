# Real2Gym: Building Gyms from Videos, Bringing Skills to Robots

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37089) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37089)


## One-sentence takeaway
Real2Gym combines physics-validated video-to-simulation reconstruction with reusable code-based robot skills, improving manipulation success on selected simulated tasks and demonstrating transfer for one previously unsuccessful physical task.

## Problem and motivation
Physical robot trial-and-error requires costly resets, supervision, and hardware use. Simulation could support cheaper exploration, but constructing task-faithful environments remains labor-intensive, and visually plausible reconstructions can have incorrect geometry or invalid contacts. The authors target both executable scene construction and experience reuse for subsequent robot control (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Interactive gym construction:** Reconstruct paired Blender–MuJoCo scenes from demonstration videos and robot descriptions; correct contact-event keyframes; validate native-physics execution; and accept task-conditioned variations only after feasibility checks (§3.1).
- **Experience-driven control:** Generate Python programs for manipulation stages, inspect execution feedback, and extract applicability conditions, object-relative motions, checks, and recovery guidance into a skill library. Model weights remain fixed (§3.2).
- **Integrated transfer demonstration:** Re-ground simulation-derived skills through a shared perception-and-control interface for physical execution, including recovery of a failed shelf-placement task (§4.3). The contribution is the integrated pipeline; the underlying perception tools and simulators are existing components.

These results support effectiveness on the selected tasks and a concrete transfer example. Reconstruction “simulation success score” is a model-judged composite of action fidelity and completion, **not** binary policy success (Appendix E). Skill-conditioned results measure repeat execution of the same tasks, so they do not establish generalization to unseen tasks. Reported token savings do not establish lower total pipeline cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Cross-view misalignment can produce ghosting and residual geometry or texture errors; stage-level programs lack fine local reactivity; physical evaluation covers only Franka arms with parallel-jaw grippers (§5).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation breadth:** Selected scenes and four physical tasks provide limited evidence of broader deployment reliability. The transfer recovery is demonstrated on one task (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Comparisons change control granularity and skill availability together. Component ablations isolating event correction, augmentation, perception tools, and stage-level control are not supplied; Appendix D instead studies model choice and reasoning effort on one assembly task.
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Reconstruction uses GPT-6 Astra as judge while the pipeline also uses that model family; independent judge agreement is unreported (§4.1). No uncertainty intervals are supplied.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The stated standard budget is 50 model decisions, yet Table 4 contains baseline response counts above 50. The relationship between responses and budgeted decisions is unexplained (§4.1; Table 4).

## Open research questions
- **Do skills generalize beyond repeated tasks?** Evaluation primarily reuses each task’s first-run experience. Test extracted skills on held-out object geometries and placements without further extraction (Table 2).
- **Which construction stages drive downstream gains?** Component effects are not isolated. Remove event correction, physics validation, and augmentation separately, then measure reconstruction quality and manipulation success.
- **Can adaptive control improve precision?** The authors identify insufficient local reactivity. Compare stage-only control with observation-triggered fine-action control on narrow-clearance tasks (§5).
- **When does the full pipeline become cost-effective?** Reported efficiency excludes construction and extraction. Measure all costs across repeated deployments to determine amortization (§4.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported comparisons are well documented, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes evolved-skill trial counts, uncertainty estimates, independent reconstruction-score validation, component ablations, end-to-end construction and extraction costs, explicit asset-release status, and clarification of response counts versus decision budgets.
