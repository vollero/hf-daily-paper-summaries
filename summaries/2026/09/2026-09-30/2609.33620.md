# Learning Dynamics of Continual Learning: A Unified View of Data Attribution, Forgetting, and Plasticity Loss

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33620) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33620)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors derive a forward-computable, two-channel approximation of token-level update effects that supports data selection, distinguishes two forgetting mechanisms, and links declining readout transmission to reduced future learnability in tested language models.

## Problem and motivation
Continually updated language models must select useful experience, preserve existing behavior, and remain able to learn new tasks. The authors argue that data attribution, forgetting, and plasticity loss are usually studied separately despite sharing an evolving update–behavior interaction. Existing example-level analyses obscure token and layer mechanisms, while exact full-parameter Jacobians are costly to evaluate repeatedly (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Structured interaction approximation:** Starting from a first-order gradient interaction, the authors separate token forces \(g=e_y-\pi\), the shared readout, and the residual backbone. CH1 captures direct vocabulary-force alignment; CH2 captures readout-mediated coupling modulated by layer-wise representation similarity. Identity-path and linear-block approximations make scoring possible from forward-pass quantities (§2, Eq. 5).
- **Data selection:** Aggregate interactions against a target probe set to rank candidate examples, testing attribution, multilingual memory retrieval, and actual subset fine-tuning (§3).
- **Mechanism-specific forgetting controls:** Distinguish concentrated **collision**, associated with strong conflicting token forces, from accumulated **erosion** through many weak interactions. Test energy masking for collision and response-format separation for erosion (§4).
- **Plasticity diagnostic:** Define task-conditioned readout transmission \(R_D=\mathbb{E}[\|w^\top g\|^2/\|g\|^2]\), then test whether its degradation predicts slower adaptation and benefit from restoring the base readout (§5, Eq. 7).

The claimed novelty is the finer structural unification and resulting diagnostics, building on an existing learning-dynamics framework rather than introducing gradient interaction itself (§1; Appendix A.2).

These results support practical scoring utility and the proposed mechanisms within the tested settings. They do not establish universal superiority: CH2 gains vary, energy control does not eliminate erosion, and readout restoration provides partial recovery. Correlation plus reset intervention supports a readout contribution to plasticity loss, rather than identifying its sole cause (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated approximation limits:** The theory is local and first-order; the forward score omits residual-branch propagation and approximates block geometry. Multi-update forgetting and plasticity predictions are empirically validated extrapolations, not an exact trajectory theory (Appendix A.3).
- **Optimizer mismatch:** Derivation uses SGD, while experiments use AdamW. Adaptive weighting can alter rankings and aggregate signs; omitted history-dependent drift means the score does not predict the exact full AdamW update sign (Appendix B.4).
- **Operating-regime dependence:** Signed scoring can fail at cold start. Subset selection uses a warm start, whose cost is included in reported scoring GPU-hours (Appendix C; Table 5).
- **Scope and intervention limits:** The decomposition targets residual architectures with a shared readout. Reset experiments do not address preserving capabilities acquired during long training. The contraction/budget assumption underlying the degeneration bound is not derived for all observed settings (Appendix A.3, B.3; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Memory retrieval here tests translated counterparts of the query’s own question-answer pair, limiting evidence for retrieval from naturally collected, heterogeneous memories (Appendix D.2). Some appendix prose also conflicts with tabulated metrics or overstates instruction-tuned format robustness; the tables warrant greater confidence than those broad summaries (Tables 9 and 13).

## Open research questions
- **Can signed fidelity be predicted before warm-up?** Its rapid recovery and depth-dependent failure remain unexplained (Appendix C.4). Compare warm-up distributions and selectively restore residual Jacobian paths while measuring sign agreement.
- **Can plasticity recover without losing acquired capabilities?** Readout reset is explicitly diagnostic (§5). Test partial or direction-specific restoration while measuring both new-task loss AUC and retained PubMed performance.
- **How robust is format protection outside controlled templates?** Results depend on model and inherited format preferences (Tables 10–13). Test diverse deployment prompts and several sequential task transitions.
- **What causes degeneration without weight decay?** Transmission declines similarly with and without it, while the theoretical budget constraint remains assumed (Appendix B.3; Fig. 21). Manipulate readout norm constraints and task-force overlap independently.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains extensive derivations, settings, and quantitative tables, but some narrative claims exceed or conflict with those tables.
