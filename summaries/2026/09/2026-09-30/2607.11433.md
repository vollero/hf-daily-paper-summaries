# Omni-Decision: Evidence-Ledger Planning for Omni-Modal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11433) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11433)


## One-sentence takeaway
Omni-Decision replaces accumulated tool history with a critic-filtered evidence ledger, achieving 81.39% accuracy on OmniGAIA while controlled comparisons support the importance of planning and structured state within its tested configuration.

## Problem and motivation
Evidence-seeking multimodal QA requires agents to combine media observations, external facts, and computation. The authors argue that noisy observations overwhelm persistent dialogue history, while weak planners lose track of missing evidence, contradictions, and answer readiness (§1).

Their identified gap is explicit control state: existing approaches use free-form traces or textual memory, whereas Omni-Decision makes a typed, task-scoped ledger the shared basis for planning, verification, and stopping (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evidence-ledger control:** The ledger records open needs, fact/computation slots, sourced evidence, and unresolved conflicts. A transient critic evaluates observations; a deterministic reducer commits updates. The planner reads bounded ledger text and the latest observation (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Backend diagnosis:** Controlled replacements vary planning or perception backends while retaining the remaining configuration, supporting the authors’ claim that planning capability strongly affects performance (§4.3, Table 2).
- **Trajectory-based training:** Correct teacher runs supply state–action pairs for supervised fine-tuning. Offline decision-level RL scores candidate actions using schema validity, readiness rules, and a reward judge, without executing system rollouts (§3.4, Appendix F). No manual step annotation is required, but the RL judge receives ground truth.

These are the authors’ claimed contributions; the supplied comparisons establish advantages over the tested controls rather than universal novelty.

The evidence supports the complete system’s effectiveness on these benchmarks and the value of structured state relative to the tested summary control. It does not establish statistical significance or isolate every component. Lower billing also does not imply fewer processed tokens: Appendix E reports greater total token usage for Omni-Decision.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundary:** The critic sees text rather than media, so evidence quality depends on perception-tool descriptions (§5).
- **Training overlap:** Easy evaluation draws from the same task source as training; only Medium and Hard are explicitly disjoint by task ID. Although SFT targets actions, the RL reward judge receives ground truth (Appendix F).
- **Comparison asymmetry:** Omni-Decision blocks Hugging Face retrieval; the direct Gemini comparator cannot receive the same restriction, and spot checks suggest benchmark retrieval (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—ablation scope:** No-ledger ReAct also removes readiness checks and answer verification, so its gap cannot be attributed solely to context representation. Memory is the closer control (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—readiness qualifications:** Forced answers after exhausted revisions and permissive answer-checker prompts qualify the strict readiness guarantee. The checker may accept approximate answers or override insufficient-evidence metadata (§3.3, Appendix C.3, Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—context ambiguity:** The evidence-critic template references `accumulated_observations`; how this reconciles with claims that raw observations are transient is unclear (Appendix G versus §3.1).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic scope:** Backend results support sensitivity within this harness. Because one LLM instantiates planner, critic, and finalizer, the precise role coverage of a “planner” replacement deserves clarification (§4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Would a directly multimodal critic improve verification?** Text-only access is an acknowledged boundary. Compare text and multimodal critics with identical planners, tools, and budgets, measuring accuracy and conflict-resolution errors (§5).
- **Which ledger components drive the gains?** Existing controls do not separately isolate typed state, critic filtering, conflict tracking, and finalization gates. Run component ablations while preserving equivalent verification and stopping behavior (Table 3, Appendix B.2).
- **Do training gains generalize to entirely unseen tasks?** Easy overlap and ground-truth-assisted RL limit that conclusion. Train on task-disjoint data and evaluate both benchmarks with held-out task IDs (Appendix F).
- **How should the system trade abstention against forced answers?** Documented runs produce incorrect forced outputs despite open evidence gaps. Compare strict abstention and current revision exhaustion using accuracy, coverage, and unsupported-answer rates (Appendix C.3).
