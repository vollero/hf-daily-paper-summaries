# Learning Beyond What You Sample: Off-Policy-Aware Cross-Model Trajectory Exchange for RLVR

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37868) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37868)


## One-sentence takeaway
GRAFT replaces a model’s entirely unsuccessful RLVR rollout groups with compatible peer trajectories, improving five-benchmark math pass@1 averages in all six tested model–peer settings at the same per-model rollout count (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
GRPO computes advantages from reward differences within sampled response groups; entirely incorrect groups therefore provide no reward-based policy-gradient signal. More rollouts can uncover successes but cost additional compute. The authors identify complementary successes across heterogeneous models as an alternative source of supervision, and argue that existing exchange methods do not adequately combine receiver-failure selection with control of cross-model mismatch (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Selective group replacement:** Transfer only when the receiver fails every rollout and the peer produces both correct and incorrect responses. Replace the failed group with the full peer group, retaining source-computed advantages. Balance exchange directions by candidate counts and rank candidates by peer success count, retaining boundary ties (§4.1).
- **Compatibility-aware optimization:** Re-tokenize peer responses for the receiver; filter and weight them using relative average token log-likelihoods, with weights capped at one. Apply token-level clipping relative to the receiver’s old policy and process peer-containing minibatches last. The compatibility score is explicitly a proxy across tokenizers, not an exact importance ratio (§4.2).
- **Stored-trajectory reuse:** Apply the same selection and update machinery to recorded peer GRPO trajectories without loading or simultaneously training the peer; directional balancing is inactive (§6.2).

The claimed contribution is this combination of selection, full-group supervision, and mismatch control; cross-model trajectory sharing itself predates GRAFT (§2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Peers supply otherwise missing successes | In independent GRPO runs with eight rollouts per prompt, SmolLM3 solves 47.9% of Qwen3’s all-fail prompts; the reverse fraction is 18.7%. | Figure 1; §§1–2 |
| Online exchange improves every model–peer aggregate | With eight rollouts per model, gains over independent GRPO are 0.66–4.46 percentage points in the unweighted five-benchmark pass@1 average; the reported mean gain is 2.11 points across six settings. | Table 1; §6.2 |
| Gains persist across training runs | Across three independent training runs, aggregate mean gains over GRPO range from 0.91 to 3.97 points across the six settings. | Appendix C, Table 4 |
| Pair 1 improves the reported compute trade-off | Pair-mean score is 35.25 at 40.9 GPU-hours to selected checkpoints: 1.18 points above GRPO with 32 rollouts at 0.45× its checkpoint cost. Costs exclude validation and checkpoint writing. | §6.1; Appendix D, Table 5 |
| Stored trajectories retain substantial benefit | With eight self-rollouts, stored-peer training gains 0.80–2.68 points over GRPO across six settings, averaging 1.78 points—84% of the online average gain. Prior peer-log collection costs are excluded. | Appendix E, Table 6; Table 5 |
| Compatibility and full-group transfer matter in Pair 1 | Removing the compatibility gate lowers SmolLM3/Qwen3 aggregate scores by 8.36/2.51 points; success-only transfer lowers them by 7.84/3.93 points relative to full GRAFT. | Table 2; Appendices F–G |

These results support aggregate improvements within the tested math settings, including repeated training runs. They do not establish improvement on every individual benchmark, generalization to larger models or other domains, or an exact off-policy correction. Main-table uncertainties describe five evaluation runs of the first training run; Table 4 separately measures training variability (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Gains depend on peer complementarity; evaluation covers only two-model pairs, math, and base models no larger than 3B. Cross-tokenizer compatibility is a heuristic (§7, p. 10).
- **Analyst inference—selection bias:** Checkpoints are selected by validation on benchmarks used for reported evaluation, and the compatibility threshold is selected on Pair 1. An untouched evaluation set would better establish generalization (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism attribution:** Component ablations cover only Pair 1. The pooled-group variant changes advantages, included responses, and loss normalization together, so it does not isolate advantage pooling alone (Appendix G).
- **Comparison boundaries:** HACPO uses different minibatch and regularization settings. Stored-trajectory savings assume peer logs already exist (Appendix B; Table 5).
- **Accounting ambiguity:** Appendix D’s conservative per-model GRAFT costs are 40.9/39.4 GPU-hours, whereas Table 5 lists 25.3/15.0 under per-model checkpoint columns without clearly explaining their allocation. The joint checkpoint total is consistently 40.9 GPU-hours.

## Open research questions
- **Does the gate generalize across tokenizer differences?** Its score is a proxy, tested on limited pairs. Compare alternative compatibility measures across more tokenizer combinations with thresholds fixed before evaluation.
- **How should peers be selected?** Gains vary with complementarity. Test whether pre-training measurements of all-fail rescue rates predict gains across a larger set of model pairs.
- **Can exchange scale beyond math and two peers?** Both are explicit scope limits. Evaluate multi-peer selection on another task with verifiable rewards.
- **How reusable are older logs?** Stored trajectories correspond to the same prompt batches and training steps. Vary log age and prompt alignment, including collection cost in the comparison.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported math results; broader effectiveness remains unestablished.

The supplied text does not establish public code, checkpoint, or trajectory releases; independent held-out evaluation after checkpoint selection; multi-seed robustness of baselines and ablations; or a clear reconciliation of per-model compute accounting.
