# Fractional State Space Transition for Long Sequence Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36314) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36314)


## One-sentence takeaway
FRAC uses a fractional-memory-inspired bank of recurrent modes to improve long-context extrapolation and LongBench scores over tested SSM baselines while preserving competitive short-context performance (§4, Tables 2–4).

## Problem and motivation
SSMs compress sequence history into a bounded state, making their forgetting behavior important for long-context modeling. The authors argue that ODE-derived exponential forgetting inadequately retains information across broad temporal ranges. Fractional dynamics offer slower, polynomial-tail memory, but directly computing them requires access to the full history. The paper addresses how to make this memory prior compatible with efficient finite-state recurrence (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Finite-state fractional construction:** The authors approximate Mittag–Leffler memory kernels with positive, geometrically spaced exponential modes and establish a bounded-horizon approximation guarantee. Exponential-sum approximation itself is acknowledged as an existing numerical technique; its integration into a selective sequence architecture is the contribution (§2.5, Proposition 3).
- **Selective FRAC layer:** Token-dependent fractional controls and learned read/write routing operate over the mode bank. Softmax routing incorporates a log-timescale prior. The experimental implementation deliberately omits the exact discretization’s write-amplitude division by λ to improve training stability (§3, Appendix D).
- **Implementation and evaluation:** Custom Triton kernels support parallel scan training/prefill and bounded-state decoding. Experiments cover synthetic memory tasks, language modeling, DNA modeling, and component ablations (§4, Appendices F–G).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Stronger NIAH extrapolation, with an exception | Authors report slower accuracy degradation beyond the 4K training length through 64K; GDN is the principal exception on S-NIAH-1. Exact plotted accuracies are unavailable in the retrieved text. | §4.2, Figure 3 |
| Competitive short-context performance | LM Harness average accuracy **55.1%**, versus **55.3%** for Transformer and Mamba3-MIMO; average excludes the two perplexity columns. | §4.2, Table 3 |

| Favorable long-context prefill throughput | At 16K, batch size 1, FRAC achieved **77,490 tokens/s**, versus **74,314** for Mamba3-SISO and **56,297** for Transformer, on the same unspecified accelerator. Training throughput was below Mamba2’s. | Appendix F, Table 8 |

| DNA results favor longer contexts | Authors report comparable or lower perplexity than GDN and Mamba3-SISO for approximately 7M-parameter HG38 models trained and tested at matching lengths. Exact curve values are unavailable. | §4.3, Figure 4; Appendix E.4 |

The evidence supports improved long-context performance in the tested settings, with small short-context tradeoffs. It does not establish universal retrieval superiority, unlimited-horizon fractional memory, or DNA length extrapolation. Ablations support the design collectively, but the pure multiscale comparison changes several components simultaneously.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Benefits depend on mode count and timescale coverage; dependencies outside the bank’s range or tasks requiring rapid forgetting may reduce the advantage. Computation grows with mode count, and broader domains remain untested (Appendix H.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The fixed-system approximation guarantee does not directly guarantee the behavior of the full token-selective architecture. Appendix D explicitly describes selectivity as an architectural generalization and documents the modified injection factor.
- **Analyst inference:** Appendix G’s approximation check fits coefficients for homogeneous relaxation over a restricted normalized-time range; it does not directly validate learned routing or the forced-response kernel.
- **Reporting limits:** Main language-model results lack reported run variability. Accelerator identity is unspecified, limiting throughput reproducibility. LongBench combines different metrics, and the real-world retrieval evaluation truncates inputs to short contexts (Appendices E.3–F).

## Open research questions
- **How robust is FRAC outside its timescale range?** Appendix H.1 identifies this boundary. Test fixed and expanded banks on dependencies beyond the evaluated horizons.
- **Which fractional components independently cause the gains?** The pure multiscale ablation changes several features together. Run matched, single-factor ablations of controls, priors, and grid initialization.
- **Can fractional retention improve associative retrieval when combined with delta updates?** The conclusion proposes this combination but does not test it. Compare a hybrid against FRAC and GDN on long-context multi-query retrieval.
- **Does DNA improvement reflect extrapolation or matched-length training?** Current DNA tests use the training length. Train at a fixed short length and evaluate progressively longer sequences.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons; **medium** in causal attribution and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes exact plotted NIAH/DNA values, main language-model seed variability, accelerator identity, verified code/checkpoint availability, and approximation guarantees for the full learned selective layer.
