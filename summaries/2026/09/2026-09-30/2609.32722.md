# Scaling Properties of Same-Family On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32722) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32722)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across Qwen2.5 math-reasoning experiments, same-family on-policy distillation shows approximately linear early accuracy gains in square-root token-level KL, while joint scale-and-teacher-score laws predict held-out-scale peak accuracy with sub-point RMSE (§§4–5, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers an RL-trained teacher’s expertise through token-level supervision on the student’s own generated responses. The authors ask whether teacher and student size can predict how much capability transfers and how quickly, instead of requiring a full training run to discover the outcome (§1).

Weak-to-strong transfer could amortize task-specific RL across a model family: train one small expert, then distill into larger students. The identified gap is systematic characterization of transfer dynamics and outcomes across model scales (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled scale study:** Evaluate Vanilla-OPD across all teacher–student scale combinations, covering weak-to-strong, same-base, and strong-to-weak transfer; compare selected cells with Delta-OPD, direct RL, and off-policy distillation (§§2–3, 6).
- **Trajectory characterization:** Fit early held-out accuracy against \(d=\sqrt{k_3}\), where \(k_3\) estimates token-level reverse KL from the student initialization. A local geometric derivation explains the leading linear term, but not the full empirical window or later dynamics (§4, Appendix D).
- **Predictive empirical laws:** Fit peak remaining error and initial transfer slope using student size, teacher size capped at student size, and effective teacher remaining error; validate through scale holdouts and an intermediate teacher checkpoint (§5, Appendix H).
- **Design-choice findings:** Test objective choice, off-policy cold starts, and multistage bootstrapping (§6). Delta-OPD represents existing teacher-checkpoint-contrast methods; the contribution is its scaling comparison, rather than a newly invented objective (§2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Early transfer is approximately linear | Across 25 Vanilla-OPD runs, first-30-checkpoint fits achieve \(R^2=0.932–0.988\), with RMSE 0.0026–0.0129 accuracy units against \(d\). | §4, p. 5 |
| Weak students can supervise stronger students | Every observed weak-to-strong student peak exceeds its teacher’s accuracy. With the 0.5B RL teacher scoring 39.8%, the 14B Vanilla student peaks at 77.7%. Direct student RL remains above the weak-teacher variants. | Figure 4, §§6.3, pp. 9–10 |
| Larger teachers can hurt small students | The 0.5B Vanilla student peaks at 40.8% with a 3B teacher, versus 39.0% and 37.7% with 7B and 14B teachers. | Figure 4, §4 |
| Joint laws improve peak prediction | Leave-one-scale-out accuracy-point RMSE falls from scale-only 3.43 to joint 1.66 for Vanilla, and 2.47 to 0.82 for Delta. Largest-student/teacher holdout RMSE is 0.55/0.68 and 0.20/0.32, respectively. | Table 2 |
| Teacher accuracy alone does not order teaching value | For a 7B Vanilla student, an intermediate 3B teacher scoring 66.0% yields a 73.8% peak, below 77.5% from a 1.5B endpoint teacher scoring 63.6%. The joint law predicts 74.1% for the intermediate teacher. | Table 3, Figure 5 |
| Delta often improves transfer | Delta has higher matched-KL local slope in 15/17 shared cells and higher baseline-normalized peak gain in 12/17. Absolute peaks improve in nine of ten weak-to-strong cells. | §6.1, Table 9 |
| Off-policy cold starts harm weak-to-strong transfer | With the 0.5B teacher, cold-start-plus-OPD loses 6.6, 15.7, and 19.4 accuracy points against pure OPD for 3B, 7B, and 14B students, respectively. | §6.2, Figures 8, 20 |
| Bootstrapping underperforms direct transfer | Full-chain Delta peaks are 64.5%, 74.0%, and 79.4% at 3B, 7B, and 14B, versus direct-from-0.5B peaks of 65.3%, 75.0%, and 79.9%. All tested chains underperform their direct counterparts. | §6.3, Figure 9 |

These results support predictive regularities within the tested family and recipe. Sub-point extrapolation errors are **RMSE across held-out cells**, not guarantees for every prediction. Transfer slope measures gain per unit KL, not speed per unit compute. The intermediate-teacher test supports the predicted ordering, but its teacher scores are approximately matched rather than identical.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One model family, one math mixture, one canonical run per configuration, capped responses, and intentionally stopped trajectories; other families, domains, and recipes remain untested (Appendix B).
- **Uncertainty:** Aggregate evaluation records prevent prompt-bootstrap intervals; cell-bootstrap intervals measure cross-pair fit variation rather than training-seed uncertainty (Appendices B, E.5).
- **Identification:** Teacher size and accuracy remain strongly correlated after adding a small number of OPD-product teachers. Controlled checkpoint comparisons beyond the single reported test remain future work (Appendices B, H).
- **Prediction limits:** Rate laws are noisier, the scale-only rate power law loses to a raw-affine baseline, and transfer endpoints can be right-censored. The teacher-size cap leaves small-student reversals unmodeled (§5, Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting observed peaks through repeated held-out evaluation leaves performance on an independent final test unestablished (§3, Appendix H).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix K describes an initial sequence-level-KL attempt, while Appendices B/I state full-sequence \(k_3\) is unavailable in the archives; that diagnostic cannot be fully assessed from the supplied text.
- **Safety:** Math accuracy does not establish broader reliability or safety; distilled defects may propagate or amplify (Ethics statement).

## Open research questions
- **Do these laws transfer across families and tasks?** Scope is limited to Qwen2.5 math reasoning. Repeat scale grids on another family and domain, testing both transferred coefficients and refitted laws.
- **Does smaller-teacher advantage survive tighter controls?** Only one approximately score-matched checkpoint comparison is reported. Match multiple teacher sizes on accuracy at several training stages and repeat student runs across seeds.
- **What determines late regression and transfer endpoints?** The local theory explains only the leading exponent. Run longer, densely evaluated trajectories while measuring proxy reward, response length, and alternative KL coordinates.
- **Does weak-to-strong OPD save total compute?** Reported slopes use KL rather than compute, and direct RL performs better. Compare accuracy at matched total training cost, including teacher creation and repeated student reuse.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the descriptive findings and fitting protocols, but generalization and uncertainty remain limited.

Material missing information includes replicated seed results, prompt-level evaluation outcomes, independent final-test performance, matched-compute comparisons, full-sequence KL records, broad safety evaluations, and an explicit public release location for code, checkpoints, or analysis artifacts.
