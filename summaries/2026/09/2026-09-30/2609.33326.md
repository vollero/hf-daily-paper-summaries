# ANTMAN: Adaptive Need Tracking for Multi-Agent Navigation in Large Information Spaces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33326) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33326)


## One-sentence takeaway
ANTMAN uses a revisable graph of unresolved information needs to guide worker activation and search, achieving nearly constant active coordination as distractor context grows while maintaining answer quality in the reported evaluations.

## Problem and motivation
Partition-based multi-agent systems can activate more workers simply because searchable information grows, even when a query requires the same evidence. The authors identify a gap between adaptive retrieval and adaptive coordination: evolving information needs should govern both what to search and how much computation to activate (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent Need Graph:** Tracks requirements, dependencies, evidence, attempt history, resolution status, and progress; workers’ reports can resolve, refine, or introduce needs (§3.3).
- **Need-conditioned execution:** A coordinator selects unresolved needs and routes them to territory workers using static WorkerCards. Workers search locally; stalled searches trigger reframing, rerouting, or fallback (§§3.2–3.4). Candidate routing combines lexical, exact-term, and dense retrieval before an orchestrator decision (Appendix A.1).
- **Conditional coordination bound:** Distinct activated workers satisfy \(|A(q)| \leq \min\{M,\rho H(q)\}\), where \(M\) is available territories, \(H(q)\) realized needs, and \(\rho\) the per-need routing limit. Constant coordination requires bounded realized demand and a routing limit independent of space size (Appendix A.3).
- **Empirical validation:** Evaluations cover document QA, controlled space/demand scaling, repository navigation, text-only tool use, and smaller execution models (§4). The claimed novelty is the explicit need state controlling coordination—not adaptive multi-agent behavior alone (§2.2).

These results support selective coordination and transfer across the tested interfaces. They do not establish constant total computation, universal superiority, or a compute-matched causal advantage for the Need Graph: routing retrieval can scale with space, and ablations consume different realized resources.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope qualification:** Space decoupling concerns active workers, not all retrieval computation; its constant-growth corollary assumes bounded realized needs and routing attempts (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited sampling:** Document QA, controlled scaling, and ablations use small question sets. The supplied text reports no confidence intervals or repeated-run variability (§4; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—resource confounding:** Full ANTMAN uses more calls and cost than graph-free adaptation in controlled scaling and SWE-QA-Pro, despite equal maximum budgets. The ablation therefore does not isolate state representation at equal realized compute (Tables 11–12).
- **Analyst inference—narrow transfer evidence:** GAIA excludes attachments and uses one territory worker, so it tests need tracking and tool navigation rather than multi-worker routing there (Appendices D.2, F.1).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility gaps:** Appendix E gives behavior summaries rather than verbatim implementation prompts; source code, frozen question IDs, pricing assumptions, and several routing/recovery parameters are not supplied.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table 3 reports 8.0 distinct workers at highest demand, while Table 15 reports 8.2 “worker activity”; their relationship is not fully defined. SWE-QA-Pro “direct score” wording in Table 12 also leaves its scale insufficiently explicit.

## Open research questions
- **Does explicit need state help at equal realized compute?** Current ablations match maximum budgets only. Compare full and graph-free systems under matched call, token, and cost limits (Appendix D).
- **How costly is the passive territory pool?** Active-worker scaling excludes candidate-discovery overhead. Measure indexing, WorkerCard construction, routing latency, and total cost as territory count grows (Appendix A.1).
- **Does coordination remain effective under more complex demand?** Demand scaling uses planted records and a fixed territory pool. Test branching dependencies, conflicting evidence, and recovery-heavy queries while measuring realized needs and dispatches (Appendix F.2).
- **Does transfer extend to richer modalities?** GAIA is text-only without attachments. Evaluate attachment-bearing or multimodal tasks using the same coordinator and adapted access interfaces (Appendix F.1; §5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported comparisons clearly, but statistical uncertainty and implementation completeness are limited. Missing material includes repeated-run results, confidence intervals, executable code, exact prompts and subset identifiers, detailed routing/recovery settings, end-to-end preprocessing costs, and broader modality evaluations.
