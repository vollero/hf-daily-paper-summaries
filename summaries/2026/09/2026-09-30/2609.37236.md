# Asking for What Was Never Requested: Horizontal and Vertical Proactivity in Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37236) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37236)


## One-sentence takeaway
Q&D trains agents to retrieve more required evidence per question, including evidence deeper in benchmark decompositions, but final-answer improvements remain undetected and most annotated prerequisites do not actually gate retrieval (Table 10; Appendix I.4).

## Problem and motivation
Agents often need information users never explicitly request. The authors distinguish **horizontal proactivity**, pursuing other unresolved needs already nameable from context, from **vertical proactivity**, pursuing needs revealed by newly acquired evidence. They identify a gap in evaluating and training *what* agents seek, beyond whether and when they act; question volume can also obscure question quality (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Need-graph evaluation:** Mechanically recover required evidence and prerequisite edges from benchmark decompositions. Score transcripts for coverage, breadth, depth, ordering, and stopping through evidence identifiers, without a model judge. The graph substrate and its transcript readings are the contribution; several underlying metric families are borrowed (§2; Appendix A).
- **Q&D training:** A questioner asks or stops, while a frozen drafter updates the draft. At a recorded state, sample eight candidate questions and continue each trajectory. Rank candidates by answer success, time to complete evidence, and evidence gained; train through imitation, question-pair DPO, then DPO including ask-versus-stop contrasts (§3).
- **Controlled evaluation and transfer:** Compare retrieval at matched question counts, keeping other components fixed, and transfer the trained questioner to simulated customer service without further training. Need graphs label and evaluate behavior but are unavailable to the deployed questioner (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| More evidence at equal retrieval spend | Against prompted Qwen3-8B, coverage gains were **11.2, 7.0, and 5.0 percentage points** on MuSiQue, StrategyQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA, respectively; 200 held-out tasks per suite, both arms read at their lower question count. All intervals exclude zero. | Table 10, p. 28 |
| More decomposition-deep evidence | In the same comparison, depth-weighted recall rose **12.5, 5.4, and 5.5 points**, respectively. | Table 10 |
| Advantage over the larger questioner is conditional | Against prompted GPT-OSS-120B at equal spend, coverage differences were **+7.0, +4.3, and −3.5 points**, respectively. | Table 10 |
| Final-answer gains remain undetected | At each policy’s own stop, capped at eight calls, answer token F1 differences versus prompted Qwen3-8B were **+2.4, −0.3, and +3.1 points**; every interval spans zero. | Table 10 |
| Evidence-dependent questioning matters on MuSiQue | Removing evidence from the trained questioner’s state reduced equal-spend coverage by **8.4 and 8.7 points** across its two training seeds; StrategyQA showed no detectable separation. | Appendix F, p. 30 |
| Retail transfer improves success and reduces user effort versus GPT-OSS-120B | Over 25 retail tasks with simulated customers and separately enforced question/tool-call budgets, success rose **17.0 and 12.7 points**, while customer follow-up turns fell **1.60 and 1.75**, under base/stop prompts. All four intervals exclude zero. | Table 20, p. 37 |
| Most prerequisites are not retrieval barriers | The automated probe validated only **20 of 1,040 edges** under its declared criterion; therefore depth mostly measures benchmark decomposition structure. | Appendix I.4, Table 23 |

These results support improved evidence retrieval per question and retail transfer under the tested harness. They do not establish better final answers, reliable stopping, or general superiority over larger models. Against prompted Qwen3-8B, customer-service follow-up reductions were not established; against GPT-OSS-120B, neither airline success nor follow-up differences were decided (Tables 18, 20).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:**

- Training labels and the primary metric share need graphs; answer success determines only a small fraction of training preferences (§7).
- Small retrieval pools permit incidental deep retrieval. StrategyQA depth is a lower bound, and ordinary suites poorly separate horizontal from vertical behavior (Appendices A, I.4).
- Graph matching omits some genuinely required 2WikiMultiHopQA nodes, overstating coverage; systematic edge errors remain untested (Appendix I.3).
- Reported training seeds share a supervised checkpoint and preference export; task bootstrap intervals do not capture those sources of variation (§7).
- No checkpoint passed the original selection rule. Selection used an alternative precommitted ground on a run later excluded for a trainer defect; reported runs miss stopping criteria (Appendices D, G, L).
- QA treatment contrasts are exploratory, without multiplicity control. Length controls were development-only; answer-length and compute controls could not rule out their explanations (Appendices A, C, F).
- Customers are simulated; no on-policy RL comparison or iterative rollout training was performed (§7).

**Analyst inference:** Matching retrieval calls establishes a narrow efficiency comparison, not equal total compute or monetary cost: training requires multiple continuations, and the main 8B questioner relies on GPT-OSS-120B drafting and answering (§§3–4; Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **Can stopping training turn evidence gains into answer gains?** Current answer differences are undetected and stopping criteria fail. Next experiment: compare revised stop supervision on sealed held-out tasks, measuring answer F1 and both stopping rates.
- **Does vertical proactivity survive genuine retrieval dependencies?** Most tested edges are retrievable without prerequisites. Next experiment: use larger pools with dependent entities inaccessible until prerequisite evidence reveals them.
- **Does Q&D independently improve horizontal search?** Standard suites do not cleanly separate the axes; composed tasks are author-built. Next experiment: evaluate naturally branching tasks with both deep chains and competing inquiry lines.
- **Does reduced user effort generalize to people?** All user-facing results use simulated customers. Next experiment: run a human customer-service study measuring success, follow-up turns, and unnecessary questions.

## Confidence and missing information
