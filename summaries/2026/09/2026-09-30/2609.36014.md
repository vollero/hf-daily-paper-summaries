# Persistence Forcing: Exploiting Feature Specialization in Pixel-Space Diffusion

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36014) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36014)


## One-sentence takeaway
Persistence Forcing combines unequal feature-refinement budgets, persistent-to-active conditioning, and internal sampling guidance to improve JiT image generation on ImageNet, with PerF-H reducing FID from 1.86 to 1.63 at 256×256 (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Pixel-space diffusion Transformers must learn global structure and fine detail directly from pixels, yet standard architectures update every hidden feature at every block. The authors question whether these different representational needs warrant equal computation. Their identified gap is both architectural and functional: unequal refinement can produce complementary feature roles, but those roles must interact explicitly to improve generation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Heterogeneous refinement and specialization:** A contraction–expansion width schedule updates only a prefix of feature channels at each block; remaining channels bypass unchanged. Backbone attention/MLP parameters are approximately matched to JiT. RGB decompositions and frequency analysis associate sparse refinement with coarse structure and frequent refinement with finer detail (§2; Appendix B).
- **Persistent-to-active conditioning:** Bypassed features supply spatially aligned, token-wise modulation to active features through independently learned low-rank projectors. Coarse and fine roles emerge within one backbone rather than being prescribed as separate modules (§3.1; Appendix D).
- **Persistence Guidance (PG):** Training randomly drops persistent conditioning. Sampling contrasts predictions with and without that pathway and amplifies their difference, optionally alongside classifier-free guidance (CFG) (§3.2).
- **Scaling evidence:** The full recipe improves corresponding JiT models across tested scales and resolutions. Main experiments also use early REPA alignment restricted to persistent features; ablations distinguish its contribution from PerF’s (§4; Appendix F).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Refinement budget tracks frequency content | Equal-sized 64-channel RGB contributions generally have higher normalized spectral centroids with greater refinement across evaluated timesteps; ordering is not strictly monotonic. | Appendix B.2; Figure 8; pp. 15–16 |
| Heterogeneous refinement alone does not improve generation | Parameter-matched 131M models: at 100 epochs, uniform/heterogeneous FID is 42.1/42.7; at 200 epochs, 33.1/33.8. | §2.3; Table 1 |
| PerF improves JiT across scales | ImageNet 256×256, 600 epochs: B FID 3.66→2.81; L 2.36→1.91; H 1.86→1.63. PerF uses early REPA and CFG+PG, with 50-step Heun sampling. | Table 2; Appendix C; Table 8 |
| Higher-resolution improvement | ImageNet 512×512, 600 epochs: JiT-H/32→PerF-H/32 FID 1.94→1.76, IS 309.1→335.3, recall 0.61→0.64. Same PerF sampling framework. | Table 3; Appendix C |
| Conditioning and PG contribute separately | PerF-B, ImageNet 256×256, 100 epochs without REPA: heterogeneous-only→conditioning→PG FID is 42.71→40.35→24.92 without CFG, and 8.02→7.19→5.81 with CFG. | §4.3; Table 4 |
| Benefits remain without CFG | Class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, 600 epochs: JiT→PerF FID is 25.42→12.53 for B, 13.85→5.60 for L, and 7.15→3.48 for H. PerF retains internal guidance. | Appendix E; Table 9 |
| Early alignment improves the full recipe | PerF-B/16 FID: no REPA 3.10; full-training REPA 3.38; first-100-epoch REPA 2.81, under the main ImageNet 256×256 setting. | Appendix F; Table 10(a) |
| Architectural overhead is modest per forward | ImageNet 256×256: parameter overhead is 4.6%/2.6%/3.6% and GFLOP overhead 5.1%/3.3%/4.7% for B/L/H. | Appendix G; Table 11 |

The evidence supports consistent improvements over the tested JiT baselines and a useful refinement-frequency association. It does not establish universal transfer or causal proof that global structure explains PG’s gains. Main results evaluate a combined recipe; the alignment ablation supports improvement without REPA for the Base model. Per-forward overhead does not measure the total cost of guidance sampling.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported sensitivities:** Excessive PG strength degrades FID, prolonged REPA worsens final quality, and frequency ordering is not strictly monotonic (§4.3; Appendices B and F).
- **Analyst inference—limited scope:** Experiments cover class-conditional ImageNet generation on JiT. Other datasets, conditioning modalities, and backbones remain untested in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic evidence:** Frequency measurements support coarse-to-fine specialization, but do not independently establish semantic structure or its causal role. Stability during bypass also follows partly from leaving features unchanged (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and uncertainty:** Main JiT comparisons combine architecture, conditioning, guidance, and early alignment changes. No repeated-run uncertainty is reported, and retraining ablations mostly use shorter Base-model training (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency:** Combined CFG+PG uses three prediction terms, while reported compute covers one forward pass. End-to-end latency and memory are unavailable (Equation 4; Appendix G).

## Open research questions
- **Does specialization cause better structural coherence?** Readouts and spectra show association, not causal attribution. A next experiment could spatially shuffle persistent conditioning while preserving projector capacity and measure structural errors alongside FID.
- **Does PerF transfer beyond JiT and ImageNet?** The evaluation is confined to that backbone and dataset. Test matched architectures on another image dataset and a different pixel-space Transformer.
- **What is PG’s quality–compute trade-off?** Sampling latency is unreported. Compare CFG and CFG+PG under equal forward-pass or wall-clock budgets.
- **How does REPA interact with PerF at larger scales?** The explicit alignment-schedule ablation covers Base. Repeat no/early/full alignment experiments for Large and Huge with repeated runs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and tabulated outcomes; **medium** in the proposed structural interpretation.

Material missing information includes verified code/checkpoint availability, REPA teacher and detailed alignment configuration, repeated-run uncertainty, complete metric-evaluation protocols and sample counts for every table, guidance scales for some ablations, and end-to-end training/sampling resource measurements.
