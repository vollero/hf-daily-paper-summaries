# Chinese-Jev: Bringing System One Model to Chinese-Language Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36965) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36965)


## One-sentence takeaway
Chinese-Jev adapts a compact encoder to Chinese candidate-based decisions, slightly surpassing hosted Jev on general accuracy and outperforming it in medicine, while remaining weaker in law, finance, and specialist calibration (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Applications often need a label, judgment, or rating rather than generated text. The authors identify limited Chinese decision accuracy in existing Jev models and incompatible supervision formats across Chinese datasets. Their goal is to combine broad task coverage with inexpensive inference and domain specialization (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified data construction:** Convert categorical labels, proposition judgments, ordered ratings, and multiple-answer questions into candidate-probability targets. Preserve rating order and available inter-rater variation; deduplicate decisions and partition shared source material together (§3.1).
- **General and specialist models:** Initialize from Laya Multilingual, jointly encode context and candidates, and score candidate markers through a type-conditioned head. Train on a general Chinese corpus, then independently specialize for medicine, law, and finance. Cross-entropy is combined with the existing RLCD objective; the backbone and RLCD are adopted components (§3.2; §4.1).
- **Benchmark and deployment:** Introduce CJ-Bench for accuracy, calibration, and latency comparisons, plus an INT8 browser prototype for smartphone inference. Models, data, benchmark, and construction code are **promised for release**, rather than demonstrated as available in the supplied text (§1; §4.3; §5).

ACC and ECE cover single-label examples, including hard rating targets, and exclude soft targets (§4.1). The results support improved performance on the constructed held-out benchmark; they do not establish performance on unseen source datasets or real clinical, legal, or financial workflows. Hosted Jev latency comparisons measure end-to-end response time, not isolated model speed.

Table 1’s Chinese-Jev ECE annotation, “−6.76,” conflicts with its displayed values; the analysis above uses the raw ECE values and accompanying prose.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified:** Accuracy gaps remain in law and finance, and calibration gains are uneven after specialization (§5).
- **Author-disclosed:** Near-duplicate screening covers held-out text available at checking time; paraphrases across the training corpus are not exhaustively merged (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** No reported ablations isolate RLCD, soft supervision, type balancing, or general pre-training before specialization. Their individual contributions therefore remain uncertain (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** Medical training is much larger than legal or financial training, so domain outcomes cannot cleanly distinguish task difficulty from supervision quantity (§4.1; Table 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate hard-target metrics leave soft-rating distribution quality and ordinal error insufficiently evaluated (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The mobile demonstration reports one device configuration without quantized accuracy, memory use, energy consumption, or a latency distribution (§4.3). Source licenses also include noncommercial restrictions (Appendix A.4).

## Open research questions
1. **What does RLCD add beyond cross-entropy?** No objective ablation is reported. Compare matched training runs using cross-entropy alone and cross-entropy plus RLCD, measuring accuracy, calibration, and rating-distribution quality.
2. **Would more legal and financial supervision close the accuracy gaps?** Training volumes differ substantially across domains. Run domain-specific learning curves with controlled data budgets and unchanged held-out sets.
3. **Do gains transfer to unseen sources?** CJ-Bench uses held-out partitions from the constructed corpora. Evaluate leave-one-source-out training and independently collected Chinese decision tasks.
4. **Can confidence and mobile efficiency improve without sacrificing accuracy?** Specialist ECE remains weaker than Jev’s, and quantization accuracy is unreported. Test calibration fitted only on the reserved calibration partitions, then compare full-precision and INT8 accuracy, latency, and resource use.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported aggregate comparisons; causal explanations and broader generalization remain less certain.

Material missing information includes seed variability or confidence intervals, component ablations, per-source and per-decision-type results, soft-target evaluation, optimizer and learning-rate details, the RLCD noise scale, complete medical source enumeration, quantization accuracy and resource measurements, and confirmation that the promised assets have been released.
