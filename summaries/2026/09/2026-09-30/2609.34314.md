# PlaylistEval: Can Video-Language Judges Be Trusted at Day Scale and Beyond?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34314) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34314)


## One-sentence takeaway
PlaylistEval’s automatically constructed benchmark exposes unreliable judging over roughly 100-hour video collections: the best tested system reaches 75.4% pairwise accuracy, with evidence retrieval a major failure source (Table 1; §4.5).

## Problem and motivation
Video-language judges evaluate model responses and supply training rewards, but the authors argue that existing benchmarks mostly test short clips, permit text-only shortcuts, and require costly human annotation. Their gap is whether judges can locate and verify scattered visual evidence across day-scale collections, where incorrect preferences could distort evaluation or training (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Automatic benchmark construction:** After manual playlist selection, the pipeline indexes video chunks using frames and ASR transcripts, generates two-segment questions with evidence-cited answers, and applies structural, shortcut, and grounding checks with regeneration feedback (§§3–3.3).
- **Controlled visual distractors:** Four progressively degraded answers introduce visual errors while preserving fluent long-form structure. Automated gates check ranking detectability with and without video; answer-rating gaps control pair difficulty (§3.2).
- **Benchmark and diagnostic evaluation:** PLAYLISTBENCH contains 630 preference pairs over 327 questions from seven domains. Experiments vary collection size, retrieval, modalities, answer position, and resource budgets (§3.3; Table 12; §4; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Human validation supports most intended preferences | On 152 pairs with three annotators each, 93.0% of individual ratings agree; majority agreement is 96.7%; Krippendorff’s α is 0.781. | Appendix F; Table 22 |
| Best tested judge remains imperfect | Gemini-3.7-Flash scores 75.4% with retrieved evidence versus 71.0% with uniform sampling over 630 pairs; judges receive 128 frames plus audio or transcripts. | Table 1; §4 |
| Judge-tuned models transfer poorly in this setting | Retrieved-evidence accuracy: InternLM-2.5-Reward 52.7%, VideoJudge-3B 48.3%, VideoJudge-7B 47.8%, against a 50% chance floor. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Larger search corpora reduce accuracy | With retrieval, oracle-to-full accuracy falls from 73.6% to 67.5% for Qwen-3.7-Flash, 63.9% to 59.5% for Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite, and 62.9% to 59.7% for Gemma-4-26B-A4B; 630 pairs per scope. | §4.2; Fig. 4 |
| Both evidence segments are difficult to retrieve | Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B with video-text input achieves 37.9% both-segment Hit@10 and 45.0% nDCG@10 over 343 questions in domain-level corpora. | Tables 2, 15; Appendix B.2 |
| Combining modalities helps | Removing frames or transcripts costs approximately 2–7 percentage points across the three ablated judges under the default video-text retriever. | §4.3; Fig. 5 |
| Evidence delivery dominates inspected failures | Authors classify approximately 61% as retrieval errors and 15% as frame-sampling errors in 100 manually inspected failed pairs involving the four strongest judges. | §4.5; Appendix D |

These results establish difficulty under the paper’s synthetic preference task and evidence budgets. They do not establish reliability for arbitrary real model outputs or direct processing of continuous day-long video. The human–model comparison also uses different evidence conditions: humans receive targeted clips and optional answer-difference highlighting, while the main judges receive retrieved samples (Appendix F; G.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** A single Gemini generator may introduce family-specific artifacts; evaluation cost limits expansion; primarily English content restricts language coverage (Appendix A). Public video URLs may disappear (ethics statement).

**Analyst inferences supported by the protocol:**

- **Shortcut resistance is incompletely established.** The distractor gate rejects only when both text-only judges recover the entire intended ranking, and supplies neither transcripts nor video. Passing therefore does not prove every retained pair is indistinguishable to transcript-only judges (§3.2; G.2.7).
- **Verification may favor intended labels.** The visual-ranking verifier receives generator-authored degradation analyses, while evaluation judges receive a general rubric. An independent verification ablation without these analyses is not reported (G.1; G.2.8).
- **Difficulty selection narrows interpretation.** Pairs solved by both filtering judges are removed, so reported accuracy measures a deliberately challenging subset (§3.3).
- **The bottleneck conclusion has limits.** Low oracle accuracy and modest oracle tool-use gains show that retrieval is not the sole unresolved problem (§4.2; Appendix C.2).
- **Release status is ambiguous.** The abstract states a release, whereas the reproducibility statement promises release upon publication.

## Open research questions
- **Would diversified generators change judge rankings?** Single-generator bias remains acknowledged. Generate matched benchmarks with several families and compare rankings and blinded human agreement.
- **Can adaptive retrieval and sampling close the delivery gap?** Retrieval and missed frames dominate inspected errors. Compare query refinement and targeted resampling against fixed retrieval under equal budgets.
- **Do results transfer to naturally generated answers?** Current preferences come from controlled visual corruption. Evaluate independently produced model answers with blinded human preferences.
- **How robust are the labels without generator guidance?** Visual verification uses intended degradation analyses. Repeat verification without them and independently audit disagreements.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods, prompts, configurations, and human evaluation, but important validity questions remain.
