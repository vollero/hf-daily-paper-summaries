# Jev thinks &quot;I don&#x27;t know&#x27;&#x27;, but doesn&#x27;t say it: Introducing Sys1Cal-v1 Dataset for Probability Calibration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35342) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35342)


## One-sentence takeaway
Sys1Cal-v1 reveals systematic distortion in Jev’s binary Choice probabilities that a fitted uncertainty model partly corrects, without proving that Jev internally represents a third truth value.

## Problem and motivation
Structured decision models expose probabilities for downstream decisions, so selecting the correct label is insufficient: each probability must have a reliable numerical meaning. The author identifies a gap between top-label confidence calibration and evaluating complete returned distributions, consistency across equivalent inputs, and agreement across Jev’s Noul, Choice, and Score interfaces (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** Sys1Cal-v1 contains 365 rendered examples from 92 underlying problems, with exact proposition probabilities generated by construction. Six families span explicit probabilities, counts, compound and conditional probabilities, Bayes’ rule, and sequential Bayesian updates (§2.1).
- **Evaluation:** Compare predicted probabilities with exact targets using binary total variation, \(TV=|\hat p-p^*|\), and distributional overlap, \(OVL=1-TV\). Evaluate Jev’s three primitives and the Choice-style SemIf baseline; measure variation across equivalent renderings (§§2–3, Appendix A).
- **Calibration model:** Fit a latent True/Uncertain/False interpretation of Score–Choice discrepancies. Score expectation supplies True mass; a parametric uncertainty component explains Choice through renormalization. Inverting this map produces corrected binary probabilities; retaining uncertainty produces probability intervals (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Noul and Score outperform Choice | On Sys1Cal-v1, using probabilities averaged over ten queries per item: mean OVL is 0.918 for Jev-Noul, 0.886 for Jev-Score, 0.764 for Jev-Choice, and 0.629 for SemIf-Choice. | §§3–3.1, §5; Table 1 referenced |
| Uncertainty model captures Score–Choice distortion | Fitted parameters \(\alpha=0.530\), \(\beta=1.011\); Choice-probability prediction \(R^2=0.834\). Mean absolute-error reduction is 0.110, with a reported 95% confidence interval excluding zero; endpoints are absent. | §4, Figure 4; Table 2 referenced |
| Inverse correction improves binary recovery | Raw versus corrected Jev-Choice: mean OVL 0.764 → 0.880; median OVL 0.771 → 0.903. | §5; Table 3 referenced |
| Interval compatibility scores higher under a different metric | T/U/F interval OVL: mean 0.931, median 0.978, with mean interval width 0.292. The conclusion instead reports median 0.971. | §5 versus §7 |
| Equivalent renderings change outputs | Jev-Noul mean pairwise TV is 0.044; Jev-Choice variation is more than twice that. SemIf-Choice is reported least stable. | Appendix A, Figure 5; Table 6 referenced |

The evidence supports differences in probability recovery across interfaces and an effective fitted binary correction on this benchmark. It does not establish a hidden internal truth value, held-out generalization, or real-world decision improvements. Interval OVL is more permissive than binary OVL because it accepts a range of probabilities; its gains cannot be treated as equivalent calibration improvements.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Synthetic families and templated renderings offer limited ecological coverage; richer language and domain-specific problems remain future work (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Compressing the Score distribution into its expectation may discard useful information (§6).
- **Author-stated:** The uncertainty interpretation is observational, not causal evidence about Jev’s implementation (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** The text describes fitting on Sys1Cal-v1 but supplies no held-out evaluation or comparison with alternative calibration functions, leaving generalization and explanatory uniqueness unresolved (§§4–5).
- **Reporting issue:** The abstract and contribution summary describe interval-score gains as Choice soft-accuracy improvements. Section 5 makes the necessary metric distinction, while §7 gives a conflicting interval median.

## Open research questions
- **Does correction generalize?** No held-out result is supplied. Fit on separate latent problems and evaluate binary OVL on unseen problems and probability families.
- **Is uncertainty the best explanation?** Alternative distortion models are not compared. Test generic calibration maps against the proposed model on held-out data.
- **Does inferred uncertainty improve deferral?** Section 5 provides an illustrative decision example. Evaluate realized decision costs and deferral rates over a benchmark with specified utilities.
- **Does the full Score distribution help?** The expectation discards distributional shape (§6). Compare expectation-based correction with methods using all Score probabilities.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central setup and results are explicit, but the supplied text lacks table bodies, exact confidence-interval endpoints and hypothesis-test results, model versions and execution settings, held-out fitting details, and alternative-calibrator comparisons. The conflicting interval medians remain unresolved.
