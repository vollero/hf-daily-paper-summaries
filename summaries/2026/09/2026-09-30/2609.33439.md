# Raven: The Harness of Harnesses for Composable Agentic Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33439) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33439)


## One-sentence takeaway
Raven combines heterogeneous model–harness agents through explicit dependency graphs, reporting improved planning and specialist scores while providing conditional—not empirically verified—guarantees of expanded task coverage under a shared budget.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify two barriers to cross-domain agent workflows: manually configuring increasingly complex harnesses does not scale, and specialized agents have incompatible tools, contexts, and output conventions. Their proposed gap is a system that both improves specialized harnesses and coordinates reliable handoffs between them (§1, pp. 4–5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Harness composition:** A Host Agent selects native or third-party agents from a capability registry and submits a DAG. Runtime admission checks, dependency-ready scheduling, recorded artifacts, completion adjudication, and bounded recovery support execution (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Conditional composition theory:** Typed contracts and explicit cost accounting yield sufficient conditions for reliable execution and task coverage beyond individual agents. These require compatible handoffs, valid planning, and reliability bounds applicable to actual invocation contexts (§2).
- **Orchestration benchmark:** MAOB evaluates specialist selection and dependency prediction against reference graphs before workers execute (§1, p. 5).
- **Adaptation and reuse:** Frozen-model harness evolution diagnoses failures and screens patches; memory and Skill Forge support reuse. The paper explicitly builds these components on prior HarnessBank, EverOS, and SkillCorpus work, so their reproduced results are not independent new Raven experiments (§§4–6; Appendices B.4, C.7).

These results support improved graph agreement and several reported execution or reuse outcomes. They do not establish general cross-domain superiority under matched total costs, satisfy the theory’s conditional reliability premises, or isolate every Raven component’s contribution. The supplied source is truncated, including most main evaluation sections.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** MAOB graph agreement does not establish semantic correctness or end-to-end success (§2.6; §3.1).
- **Author-stated:** A completion judge can fail or time out, triggering acceptance of a normally returned call; its verdict does not certify correctness (§3.2).
- **Author-stated:** Research evaluation reused development questions, allowed public benchmark access, and retained unequal execution policies. Contamination and observational comparisons limit interpretation (Appendix C.3).
- **Author-stated:** Design evaluation excludes failed generations from some means, uses incomplete task sets for some configurations, and departs from official grading protocols (Appendix C.5).
- **Author-stated:** Shared group memory is disabled by default, unevaluated, and based on potentially incorrect host judgments (§3.4).
- **Author-stated:** HarnessBank lacks complete per-run manifests; adaptive screening has no family-wise significance guarantee (§4.4; Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied evidence does not demonstrate end-to-end new-task coverage beyond strong individual agents with matched interfaces, initial information, and total costs—the comparison the theory itself requires (§2.5).

## Open research questions
1. **Does composition expand practical task coverage at equal total cost?** Planning agreement and specialist scores leave this unresolved. Compare complete workflows against strong standalone agents with matched tools, memory, and budgets.
2. **Do local reliability estimates transfer to downstream invocation contexts?** The theory requires context consistency that isolated averages cannot establish. Measure contract failures on actual successful-prefix workflow inputs (§2.4; Appendix A.4).
3. **Does shared group memory improve outcomes or propagate mistaken assessments?** It is unevaluated. Run a controlled memory-on/off study with independently checked feedback (§3.4).
4. **How reproducible and transferable is harness evolution?** Missing manifests impede reconstruction. Release complete search records and repeat selection on fresh held-out task families (Appendix B.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture and theoretical scope are explicit, but source truncation prevents complete coverage. Missing material includes most main evaluation discussions, detailed MAOB construction and comparator identities, complete evolution manifests, and full specialist result tables. The supplied text also does not establish integrated-system ablations or whether deployed workflows satisfy the theoretical reliability assumptions.
