# ROSS: Relearning from Self-Generated Rollouts through Selective Supervision

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35954) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35954)


## One-sentence takeaway
ROSS improves evaluated post-training checkpoints by applying masked supervised fine-tuning to verified historical self-rollouts, preserving their full context while supervising selected continuations without generating new policy rollouts (Sections 3–4).

## Problem and motivation
RL and on-policy distillation accumulate trajectories that are often discarded as the policy changes. The authors argue that these histories can preserve useful behaviors that a later checkpoint expresses unreliably. However, successful trajectories can also contain incorrect reasoning and unnecessary detours, making outcome-level filtering insufficient for reliable imitation (Sections 1–2).

The identified gap is selective consolidation of experience from intermediate policies: deciding both which historical trajectories to retain and which tokens within them to supervise.

## Approach and contributions
- **Historical-experience reuse:** Start from the upstream checkpoint and reuse saved trajectories from the original training run. Outcome verification defines the initial positive pool (Sections 3.1–3.2).
- **Selective supervision:** For math and code, an LLM reviewer proposes correct source spans, followed by independent audit and possible repair. For agentic histories, reviewers identify and localize defects for exclusion. Deterministic checks compile masks against the original stored tokens; no replacement targets are generated. Instruction-following positives retain full-response supervision (Appendix E).
- **Context-preserving SFT:** Train only on selected assistant tokens while retaining mistakes, observations, and other unselected text as conditioning context (Section 3.3).
- **Empirical contribution:** Test this consolidation stage after domain-specific RL, multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD), and agentic RL, including a controlled masking comparison on identical examples (Section 4).

The novelty is the historical self-rollout setting and its combined selection pipeline. Context-preserving loss masking itself has close precedents acknowledged by the authors, particularly SWE-Prime (Section 5).

These results support useful historical replay and an additional benefit from masking in the tested settings. They do not establish universal superiority or identify behavior recovery as the sole causal mechanism. Cross-domain retention is mixed: Code ROSS loses accuracy on AIME 2025 and slightly on IFBench, while Math Positive-Rollout SFT exceeds ROSS on all three reported cross-domain code/instruction benchmarks (Appendix G).

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section, but explicitly records several boundaries:

- **Comparison confounds:** Filtering changes dataset composition and size; masking changes supervised-token counts per epoch. Matching epochs does not equalize supervision volume (Appendix C.1).
- **Incomplete comparisons:** Code Positive-Rollout SFT cross-domain evaluation is unavailable. The agentic results omit continued RL and a matched ROSS-without-mask comparison (Appendix G; Table 2).
- **Incomplete annotation specification:** Core prompts are provided, but service wrappers and task-specific demonstrations are omitted (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—reviewer dependence:** GLM-5.2 supplies selection judgments and masked-content classifications. Human agreement, reviewer substitution, and annotation-error rates are not reported, leaving sensitivity to reviewer quality unresolved.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization and uncertainty:** Main evidence centers on one 35B model, with a separate 4B math case. Confidence intervals and repeated training-seed results are not reported.
- **Analyst inference—cost claims remain limited:** Avoiding new policy rollouts does not establish lower total cost; annotation cost and compute-matched comparisons are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **How much do gains depend on reviewer quality?** The paper uses one reviewer model without agreement measurements. Next experiment: compare alternative reviewers against a human-audited sample and measure downstream performance.
- **Does masking outperform replay under equal resource budgets?** Supervised-token volumes differ, and annotation cost is unreported. Next experiment: match total training compute and supervised tokens across masking, filtering, and full replay.
- **Which historical checkpoints provide the most useful experience?** Initialization transfer is tested, but checkpoint-window selection is not isolated. Next experiment: compare early, late, and mixed histories with matched retained-data budgets.
- **Does agentic masking itself drive the software-engineering gain?** The agentic comparison combines selection and masking. Next experiment: add an identical-example, full-supervision control and a continued-RL baseline.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, evaluation protocols, and useful controlled comparisons, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, training-seed variation, annotation accuracy and cost, exact agentic retained-dataset size and execution budgets, public code/model/data release status, and several matched or cross-domain controls.
