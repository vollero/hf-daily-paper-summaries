# Follow the Entities: A Corpus Map for Agentic Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37226) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37226)


## One-sentence takeaway
CorpusMap exposes reusable entity–document links to search agents, improving dataset-balanced answer quality across three benchmarks while reducing aggregate input-token ratios, although relevant candidate-document selection is central to its efficiency gains (Tables 1 and 8).

## Problem and motivation
Questions over document collections often require evidence scattered across sources. Flat-corpus search gives agents access to documents without showing their relationships, forcing repeated searches and reconstruction of connections for each query. The authors identify a gap between improving an agent’s search policy and providing persistent, query-independent structure that helps it find complementary evidence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Entity-based navigation:** CorpusMap builds a bipartite graph connecting documents to resolved entities mentioned in multiple documents. Entity Pages summarize explicitly documented facts with source attribution and list linked documents; original documents remain searchable (§3.2).
- **Offline construction:** A corpus-derived entity-type catalog guides local extraction, cross-document resolution, and page rendering. Ambiguous observations remain unresolved, and entities occurring in only one document are excluded from the navigation layer (§3.3; Algorithm 1).
- **Agent interface and candidate selection:** Pages are ordinary files accessible through shell tools. At query time, BM25 ranks Entity Pages, pools their linked documents, and reranks those documents to supply candidate file paths (§3.4; Appendix B.3).
- **Reuse and maintenance:** Experiments examine maps reused across answering models, incremental updates, and GLinker-based extraction and resolution with deterministic rendering (§5.2). The described catalog induction still uses an LLM (§3.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher aggregate quality with lower aggregate token ratios | Across three benchmarks and three runs, CorpusMap versus Raw Corpus: GPT-5.5 quality **72.55 vs 66.11**, Luna **65.58 vs 53.85**, Terra **72.51 vs 65.75**, Sol **74.68 vs 67.69**. Corresponding geometric-mean input-token ratios: **0.43×, 0.65×, 0.66×, 0.43×**. Quality is dataset-balanced, combining benchmark answer metrics. | Table 1; Appendix A |
| Gains over navigation baselines are statistically supported | Across the four main models, gains over Raw Corpus, Document Page, Group Page, LLM Wiki, and Corpus2Skill have positive paired-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and Holm-corrected **p < 10⁻⁴**. | Table 6; Appendix B.1 |
| Benefits extend to other answering models, with mixed token outcomes | EnterpriseRAG-Bench: DeepSeek quality **71.11 vs 68.02**, tokens **1,043.3k vs 1,349.4k**; MAI quality **45.07 vs 34.06**, tokens **158.8k vs 129.6k**, comparing CorpusMap with Raw Corpus. | Table 2 |
| Relevant candidates drive much of the efficiency gain | EnterpriseRAG-Bench, GPT-5.5: CorpusMap without candidates achieves quality **69.04** using **496.4k** tokens; relevant entity-linked document candidates yield **76.60** using **88.1k**. Raw Corpus yields **65.60 / 206.5k**; raw-corpus candidates yield **66.19 / 107.4k**. | Table 8 |
| Cheap-builder maps remain useful across models | A Luna-built map improves EnterpriseRAG-Bench quality for every answering model in Table 4; for GPT-5.5, quality rises from **65.60 to 73.59**. Reported one-time Luna construction cost is **≤$74.65**. | Table 4 |
| Construction cost requires substantial reuse | Estimated amortized-cost break-even versus Raw Corpus occurs at **9.0k, 26.3k, 17.3k, and 12.4k queries** for GPT-5.5, Luna, Terra, and Sol, respectively. | Figure 5 |
| Incremental updates reduce construction work | EnterpriseRAG-Bench, GPT-5.5: successive updates save **34%, 69%, and 71%** of construction tokens relative to full rebuilding; the final map has qualitatively comparable evaluation scores to a full rebuild. | Figure 3; §5.2 |

The evidence supports the combined system—entity organization, relevant candidate selection, and agent exploration—under the tested settings. It does not establish universal token savings: some individual benchmark/model combinations consume more tokens, including MAI in Table 2. Alternative judging preserves CorpusMap’s leading position on the tested GPT-5.5 answers, but absolute scores change (Table 7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated deployment risks:** Entity Pages can aggregate sensitive information or reveal inaccessible documents; maps and answers may also contain harmful or biased content. Permission-aware construction and serving are proposed, rather than evaluated (Ethics Statement).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Main evaluations emphasize selected multi-document questions. EnterpriseRAG-Bench uses a gold-containing subset, while HERB’s corpus is assembled from cited evidence plus metadata. Distractor scaling reaches 20k documents, leaving larger and differently composed deployments unestablished (Appendix A; Figure 4).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism attribution:** Candidate ablations show that efficiency depends strongly on relevant starting paths. Separate effects of entity links, page summaries, and resolution accuracy are not fully isolated (Table 8).
- **Analyst inference—construction robustness:** No direct entity-resolution accuracy evaluation is reported. Chronological additions are tested, but deletion, correction, and changing entity identities are not established (§5.2).
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Prompts and benchmark choices are supplied, but the exact execution-budget value, several construction settings, default candidate counts, and a code/artifact release are not established. Fully LLM-free construction is also ambiguous because catalog induction uses an LLM (§3.3; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
- **How sensitive is navigation to entity-resolution errors?** Resolution accuracy is not directly measured. A next experiment could annotate entity links and inject controlled false merges and missed links, measuring evidence recall and answer quality.
- **Which components produce the gains?** Candidate ablations do not fully separate summaries from graph connectivity. Compare matched candidate lists with links-only pages, summaries-only pages, and complete Entity Pages.
- **Can maps remain reliable under corrections and deletions?** Updates cover incoming documents. Test revised, removed, and conflicting documents, measuring stale facts, link validity, quality, and maintenance cost.
- **Do benefits survive permission constraints and larger distractor corpora?** Access-control safeguards are proposed, and scaling is limited to the reported corpus sizes. Evaluate permission-filtered maps over progressively larger corpora, including leakage checks and amortized cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the principal benchmark findings and includes meaningful ablations and statistical checks. Material missing information includes direct map-quality measurements, exact execution budgets and implementation settings, numeric scaling results beyond the plotted presentation, deletion/correction experiments, evaluated access controls, and confirmed code or map releases.
