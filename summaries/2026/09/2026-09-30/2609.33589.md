# TGRL: Temperature-Grouped Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Exploration in LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33589) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33589)


## One-sentence takeaway
TGRL combines within-prompt temperature contrasts with JS-weighted token updates to improve fixed-budget RLVR, reporting higher math, coding, and agent performance and a 36% reduction in time to a specified math validation target (Tables 1–3, 7).

## Problem and motivation
RLVR needs diverse successful trajectories, but exploration can collapse under limited rollout budgets. The authors identify a gap between generating diversity through temperature changes and measuring whether that diversity improves reward for each prompt; existing sampling and global exploration interventions do not directly supply that training signal (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Temperature-grouped reward signal:** Sample conservative and exploratory responses for each prompt, then normalize rewards across both groups. The authors show that the high-temperature advantage contains an additive reward-contrast term; the raw contrast is unbiased under independent sampling (§3.1).
- **JS-based token allocation:** Compute low-/high-temperature next-token distributions from the same logits, then use log-compressed, unit-mean JS weights to redistribute rollout advantages. Only exploratory responses receive actor updates (§3.2).
- **Theory and empirical diagnostics:** Bounds characterize temperature sensitivity, while ablations, a local-cooling probe, synthetic diagnostics, and wall-clock comparisons test the design (§3.1; Appendices A, D, J).

The default math/code configuration uses one reference response at temperature 0.3 and three exploratory responses at 1.2, following a 20-step warmup (Table 13).

These results support improved performance in the reported settings and complementary benefits from grouping and weighting. They do not establish universal benchmark dominance, robustness across independent training runs, or that JS identifies tokens causally responsible for reward. The efficiency result concerns crossing a validation threshold, rather than attaining exactly the same final accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The method requires verifiable scalar rewards; direct applicability to subjective preferences, open-ended dialogue, safety-critical decisions, or noisy and difficult-to-verify feedback is not claimed (Appendix L).
- **Analyst inference—credit interpretation:** The theory bounds possible continuation-value changes. A larger bound does not guarantee a larger realized reward effect or correct causal attribution (§3.1.2).
- **Analyst inference—unisolated update choice:** The uniform-credit ablation retains high-temperature-only updates. No empirical update-both ablation is supplied, so grouping and reference-branch exclusion are not separately isolated (Table 5; Appendix A.9).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Math multi-seed results vary evaluation sampling, not explicitly independent training runs. Coding and wall-clock tables lack uncertainty estimates; agent averages lack dispersion (Tables 2, 3, 7, 10).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** §4.1 says all methods use four rollouts, but agent configurations specify eight (Table 14). Appendix H describes the four-rollout TGRL–GRPO gap as 1.4 points, whereas Table 1 gives 69.4 versus 67.0.
- **Validation ambiguity:** Table 15 uses AIME-Combined for math checkpoint selection while AIME24/25 also appear in reported evaluations; disjoint validation and test subsets are not established.

## Open research questions
- **Would updating both temperature branches help?** Appendix A.9 provides a decomposition but no empirical comparison. Run matched high-only and update-both variants with identical grouping and token weights.
- **Does JS outperform alternative training-time credit weights?** The cooling probe compares selectors, but training ablations compare JS mainly with uniform credit. Train entropy-, margin-, and JS-weighted variants under matched budgets.
- **How robust is the reward contrast to verifier noise?** Appendix L excludes broader noisy-feedback claims. Inject controlled reward noise and measure gain-estimation accuracy and downstream performance.
- **Do the efficiency gains persist across training seeds and backbones?** Wall-clock evidence is confined to the 14B setup. Repeat time-to-target measurements across independent training runs and additional model families.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text contains substantial method, ablation, and configuration evidence, but reporting ambiguities limit stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes GPU model and memory, exact benchmark versions and splits, disjointness of math validation/test data, independent training-seed uncertainty, agent temperature-group allocation, and complete baseline tuning ranges. Code/configuration availability is an author claim and was not independently checked.
