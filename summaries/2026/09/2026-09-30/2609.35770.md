# FurE: Efficient Instance-Specific 3D Fur Reconstruction without Animal-Fur Datasets

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35770) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35770)


## One-sentence takeaway
FurE combines shell-guided body estimation with a human-hair PCA strand prior to reconstruct editable animal fur from calibrated multiview images, reporting substantially faster optimization than NeuralFur with similar rendering quality.

## Problem and motivation
Animal fur obscures the skin where strands should attach, while fine detail, self-occlusion, and variation across body regions make explicit strand reconstruction difficult. The authors identify two gaps: scarce animal-fur training data and expensive dense per-strand optimization. Explicit strands matter because they support editing, rendering, and simulation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shell-guided defurring:** Reconstruct the outer coat with NeuS2, then combine Gaussian Frosting thickness cues, manually verified part segmentation, and part priors to estimate a bounded, smooth inward displacement. Shell thickness is treated as indirect evidence, rather than measured skin depth (§3.1; Appendix A.1).
- **Compact strand optimization:** Predict root-conditioned latent coefficients and decode them through a fine-tuned human-hair PCA basis. Local frames and part-specific lengths place the decoded strands on the estimated body, without a separate animal-fur training dataset (§3.2).
- **Rendering and practical demonstration:** Optimize strand-aligned cylindrical Gaussians with multiview appearance and geometry losses; export explicit strands for downstream use. The authors also demonstrate reconstruction on a noisy real-world bison sequence and state that they release its data (§3.3; Figures 7, 9; Appendix A.5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Faster reconstruction stages | With 36 views and an A5000 GPU, strand training takes **52 min** for FurE versus **10.5 h** for NeuralFur; preprocessing takes **1 h** versus **10 h**. Authors summarize strand speedup as **10×**. | Table 1; §4 |
| Higher ground-truth strand F-scores | On the synthetic tiger, at distance/angular thresholds **2 cm/20°, 3 cm/30°, 4 cm/40°**, FurE reports F-scores **24.30, 39.80, 50.86**, versus NeuralFur **23.06, 36.51, 46.84** and GaussianHairCut **19.21, 29.87, 37.93**. The table does not explicitly label score units. | Table 2 |
| Rendering quality is close to NeuralFur | Across four Artemis scenes, using 36 training frames and remaining frames for novel-view evaluation, FurE has lower LPIPS and higher SSIM in every scene. PSNR ties on Panda, is slightly lower on whiteTiger (**43.10 versus 43.11**), and slightly higher on Fox and Cat. | §4; Table 4 |
| Geometry consistency has mixed outcomes | Four scenes are evaluated without ground-truth strands. FurE improves several curvature and direction statistics over hair baselines, but does not dominate every metric or NeuralFur consistently. | Table 3 |
| Real-world feasibility is demonstrated qualitatively | A noisy bison sequence produces explicit strands; authors report that NeuralFur’s underlying surface reconstruction fails. No quantitative real-world comparison is supplied. | Figure 7; Appendix A.5 |
| Exported strands support simulation | Blender wind examples demonstrate usable strand geometry; physical accuracy is assessed qualitatively. | Figures 9 and 12 |

The evidence supports a substantial runtime advantage in the reported setting, modest rendering differences, and improved strand F-scores on one ground-truth asset. It does not establish broad real-world robustness or accurate hidden skin geometry. Consistency statistics without ground truth cannot by themselves establish reconstruction accuracy.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** FurE requires calibrated multiview images of a static animal and relies on dense viewpoints. Severe occlusion can degrade shell and length estimates. Evaluation lacks ground-truth examples with strong within-part variation, such as shaved patches or irregular grooming. The model uses one strand layer on a root surface fixed before strand optimization (§5; Appendix A.4).

**Author-stated implementation dependency:** Metric initialization requires assumed eye separation; no validated automatic fallback is available. Part labels receive manual verification, and lengths use fixed part multipliers (Appendix A.1).

**Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is narrow: only one real-world sequence is presented, qualitatively. The supplied ablations do not numerically isolate the PCA codec’s contribution to speed or quality, and the reported results do not identify whether V1 or V2 length initialization was used. Hidden-body accuracy also remains unverified by quantitative ground truth.

## Open research questions
- **Can local shell cues recover irregular within-part fur?** Such ground-truth cases are explicitly missing. Test controlled shaved patches and variable-length grooms, comparing local cues against part-only priors.
- **How much does the PCA codec independently improve efficiency?** Its contribution is not isolated quantitatively. Compare dense strand optimization and PCA optimization with identical roots, losses, strand counts, and hardware.
- **Can reconstruction tolerate sparse views or animal motion?** Dense static capture is a stated requirement. Evaluate controlled view subsampling and motion sequences against known geometry.
- **Would multilayer coats and jointly optimized roots improve accuracy?** Both are identified as future work. Compare fixed single-surface roots with joint root-and-strand optimization on multilayer ground-truth assets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the pipeline and main comparisons, but broader accuracy and robustness remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes the PCA latent dimension, complete part multipliers and root densities, selected V1/V2 evaluation variant, repeated-run variability, quantitative bison and hidden-skin accuracy, and a controlled codec-specific ablation. Data release is stated, but access and contents are not verified here.
