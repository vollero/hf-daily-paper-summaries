# SoL-Refiner: Speed-of-Light One-Step Refinement for High-Resolution Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37969) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37969)


## One-sentence takeaway
SoL-Refiner combines staged training and inference acceleration to refine generated videos in one denoising step, beating evaluated external refiners on Refiner-Bench’s average quality metrics while achieving an 8.91× refinement speedup in a specific 2K setting.

## Problem and motivation
High-resolution video generation repeatedly processes large spatiotemporal token sequences. Generating at lower resolution and then refining reduces this cost, but multi-step refiners introduce another sampling bottleneck. The authors also identify limited evaluation of refiners across different upstream generators (§1).

The target task is repairing local synthesis artifacts and improving detail, optionally alongside upsampling; it is broader than resolution enlargement alone (§2).

## Approach and contributions
- **One-step refinement:** Starting from LTX-2.3, the model refines outputs from multiple generators without retraining those generators (§§1, 4).
- **Three-stage training:** Continual training learns a truncated flow-matching path using aligned low/high-fidelity pairs, primarily augmented internal real videos. Reward Feedback Learning then optimizes sampled frames using HPSv3++ and DeQA. Finally, distribution-matching distillation proceeds through a three-step student to a one-step student with projected DiT adversarial supervision (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Refiner-Bench:** A benchmark of generated videos uses shared inputs to compare refiners independently of upstream generator choice (Appendix B).
- **Integrated acceleration:** Existing TAE and Sol-Engine components reduce encoding, decoding, and denoising cost; these are incorporated tools, rather than newly introduced methods (§4.4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported averages among external refiners | On 150 shared 1024×576 inputs, one-step SoL outputs 2048×1152 and scores **0.81048 VBench / 60.4150 UniPercept**. Three-step LTX-2.3 scores **0.80388 / 56.1243**; one-step SEEDVR2 scores **0.80351 / 56.8192**. LingBot outputs a different resolution, 1920×1088. | Tables 1–2; Appendix B |
| Gains extend to 4K against the direct baseline | At 3840×2176, one-step SoL versus three-step LTX-2.3 scores **80.68 versus 77.69** VBench on a 0–100 scale and **60.34 versus 49.14** UniPercept; reported relative gains are **3.86% / 22.79%**. Inputs are half the target dimensions. | Figure 3; Appendix B |
| Distillation retains much, but not all, quality | Refiner-Bench VBench/UniPercept averages: continual training **0.80888 / 56.6620**; reward post-training **0.81691 / 61.3170**; one-step distillation **0.81048 / 60.4150**. | Table 3 |
| Full stack substantially reduces refinement latency | One H100, 2048×1152, 241 frames at 24 fps: three-step LTX-2.3 takes **57.461 s**, versus **6.447 s** for one-step SoL with TAE and Sol-Engine, yielding **8.91×**. Distillation alone gives an adjacent **1.82×** speedup. | Figure 6(b); §5.2 |
| Faster cascades improve aggregate scores across tested generators | One H100: direct generation versus low-resolution generation plus SoL takes **87.70→39.75 s** for WAN-5B, **272.69→78.71 s** for WAN-1.3B, and **264.78→94.38 s** for Cosmos-Nano; both quality averages improve. WAN uses 81 frames/50 steps; Cosmos uses 189 frames/35 steps. WAN-1.3B’s direct/refined outputs differ: 1280×720 versus 1280×704. | Figure 4; Table 9 |
| Large H3 pipeline speedup changes both generation and refinement | For a 5-second, 1344×768, 24-fps video, published 50-step full-resolution H3 takes **152.3 s** on one GB200; four-step low-resolution H3 plus SoL takes **5.64 s**, using one GPU per stage: reported **27.03×**. | Figure 1(b) |

These results support improved aggregate perceptual metrics and lower latency in the tested settings. They do not establish universal superiority: some individual temporal/consistency metrics decline, the multi-step SoL model has higher averages, and the largest pipeline speedups combine upstream acceleration with refinement. The 8.91× result depends on the complete acceleration stack.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** One-step refinement trails the 23-step model on both averages. Large semantic, geometric, and motion errors are outside the explicit training objective. Rewards evaluate sampled frames rather than long-range temporal consistency. Camera-captured degradations and broader editing tasks are outside the study (Appendix E).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**
- Aggregate gains can conceal tradeoffs: one-step SoL has lower background consistency and motion smoothness than the resized input (Table 1).
- Transfer evidence spans three benchmark source generators, but pooled results do not reveal performance separately for in-distribution and out-of-distribution sources (Appendix B; Tables 1–2).
- Comparisons are not uniformly resolution-matched, particularly LingBot and the WAN-1.3B cascade baseline (Tables 1–2, 9).
- Human preference is optimized through reward models, but no direct human evaluation or uncertainty estimates are reported.
- Internal training data, incomplete training settings, and unspecified components of the reward “regularization recipe” limit reproducibility. The text also provides no isolated final one-step quality ablations for TAE, sparse attention, or GAN supervision.

## Open research questions
- **Would temporal rewards improve consistency without sacrificing detail?** Frame rewards do not directly measure long-range coherence (Appendix E). Compare otherwise matched training runs with frame-only and video-level rewards on longer clips.
- **How robust is refinement on unseen generators?** Pooled benchmark scores leave generator-specific transfer unclear (Appendix B). Run held-out-generator evaluations with per-source results.
- **Which acceleration components cause quality loss?** The final stack’s latency is decomposed, but its one-step quality effects are not isolated (Figure 6). Measure quality and latency while independently toggling TAE and Sol-Engine.
- **Can refinement repair large structural errors?** Such correction is outside the current objective (Appendix E). Evaluate targeted semantic, geometric, and motion failures before testing additional training objectives.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported methods and numerical comparisons; **medium** in broader generalization and practical quality conclusions.

Missing material information includes training-data size and access, training compute and full hyperparameters, exact reward regularization details, confirmed model/code/benchmark availability, per-generator benchmark breakdowns, statistical uncertainty, direct human evaluation, and final-stack component quality ablations.
