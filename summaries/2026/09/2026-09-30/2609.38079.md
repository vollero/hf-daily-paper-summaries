# OmniTaskonomy: When Does Visual Generation Improve Visual Understanding?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38079) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38079)


## One-sentence takeaway
On BAGEL, image-to-image training before image-to-text fine-tuning improves selected visual understanding capabilities, with transfer depending on curriculum and task choice and positively correlating with gradient alignment (§§3, 5–6).

## Problem and motivation
Visual generation supplies dense supervision about appearance, geometry, and spatial structure, but its benefit for visual understanding remains uncertain. The authors identify two gaps: unclear training recipes for enabling transfer, and no shared capability taxonomy for systematically comparing generation objectives with understanding tasks (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled curriculum study:** Pair Jigsaw and Zoom-In image generation with textual permutation prediction using the same inputs and underlying problem. Compare six training recipes on BAGEL; an initial generation stage that updates parameters also used by understanding enables consistent scaling benefits (§3; Appendix A).
- **OmniTaskonomy:** Organize 19 generation objectives and 25 understanding capabilities under Recognition, Reconstruction, and Reorganization. Construct capability labels for existing benchmark examples through VLM annotation, manual refinement, and human validation (§4; Appendices B–C).
- **Transfer map and optimization analysis:** Measure generation-to-understanding transfer against an I2T-only baseline, then examine whether pretrained-checkpoint gradient alignment tracks downstream gains (§§5–6; Appendices D–E).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Curriculum determines scaling behavior | With 1k I2T examples, I2I→I2T and I2I→Mixed improve consistently as generation data increases; mixing from the outset or freezing shared parameters gives weaker or less stable gains. Exact curve values are not tabulated. | §3; Figure 2(b) |
| Generation can reduce direct understanding supervision | On Zoom-In, 100k I2I + 1k I2T examples achieve comparable exact-match accuracy to 10k I2T alone; on Jigsaw, 100k I2I + 3k I2T are comparable to 10k I2T alone. Curves average three seeds. | §3; Figure 2(c) |
| Transfer is selective | Five of the 19 displayed capabilities have significant gains from at least one source. Metric 3D relation and counting each benefit from 12 sources; 2D ordering from 11. Comparisons use approximately 50k I2I followed by 50k I2T examples versus I2T-only, averaged over three seeds, with paired tests at p < 0.05. | §§5.1–5.2; Figure 4 |
| Matched operations produce useful gains | Relative to I2T-only capability accuracy: Z-depth → metric 3D relation **+3.6 pp**, Euclidean depth **+3.8 pp**, surface normals **+3.4 pp**; localization → counting **+2.5 pp**, object pointing **+2.0 pp**; Jigsaw → 2D ordering **+6.8 pp**. Same transfer setting as above. | §5.2; Figures 4, 10 |
| Benefits can cross task boundaries | Inpainting → counting **+1.5 pp** and 2D ordering **+7.2 pp**; 2.5D segmentation → category recognition **+1.2 pp**. Same transfer setting; authors report these as significant. | §5.2; Figure 4 |
| Negative transfer also occurs | Surface normals → lighting understanding **−7.9 pp**; 2.5D segmentation → visual correspondence **−5.5 pp**, relative to I2T-only under the same recipe. | Figures 4, 10 |
| Alignment tracks transfer | Pearson **r = 0.795** across seven capability means and **r = 0.529** across 133 source–target pairs. Alignment uses pretrained-checkpoint pre-attention RMSNorm gradients; transfer averages three seeds. | §6.3; Figure 5(c–d); Appendix E.4 |
| Human review supports capability assignments | Reviewers accept **378/400 judgments (94.50%)** across 250 examples; acceptance is **97.4%** among 388 definite judgments, excluding unsure responses. | Appendix B.5 |

The evidence supports capability-specific transfer within the studied BAGEL setup. It does not establish universal improvement, lower total training cost, or a causal role for gradient alignment. The authors’ explanations for unexpected transfer—such as inpainting encouraging global spatial structure—remain hypotheses (§5.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences grounded in its protocols**:

- **Architecture and curriculum scope:** Experiments use one model family. Curriculum comparisons cover two paired puzzles; the broader taxonomy uses the selected sequential recipe (Appendices A.1, D.1).
- **Additional-training confound:** Transfer models receive an extra generation stage. No compute-matched additional-training control is reported, so supervision effects are not fully separated from extra optimization (§5.1; Table 11).
- **Multiple comparisons:** The authors test each source–target pair at p < 0.05 without describing a multiple-testing correction (Appendix D.3).
- **Uneven coverage:** Some capabilities have few examples; the main map excludes targets with at most 100 examples, and alignment analysis covers only seven targets (Table 7; Appendices D.2, E.4).
- **Annotation and judging dependence:** Capability labels force one primary category, and consensus can retain marginal or poor fits. Generated puzzle outputs receive one VLM judgment per instance (Appendices F.2–F.3).
- **Correlation is not intervention:** Alignment is measured before fine-tuning; no experiment manipulates alignment or prospectively selects sources using it (Appendix E.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does the recipe generalize across architectures?** Only BAGEL is tested. Repeat the controlled curriculum comparison and selected transfer pairs on another unified model.
- **Does generation outperform equal-cost alternatives?** Extra training is not compute-matched. Compare generation pretraining with additional I2T training at matched compute.
- **Can alignment guide task selection prospectively?** Current results are retrospective correlations. Rank previously untested sources by alignment and evaluate their transfer against random selection.
- **Can complementary sources be combined without harmful transfer?** Taxonomy sources are trained separately. Compare multi-source curricula with the best individual source while tracking both gains and regressions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported setup and numerical findings; broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes compute-matched controls, cross-architecture replication, correlation uncertainty estimates, and prospective validation of alignment-based selection. Accompanying exports and code are claimed but cannot be verified from the text. Exact Figure 2 curve values are not tabulated, and the main-text human-agreement statistics are not fully reconciled with the different statistics presented in Appendix B.5.
