# Org-Agent: Beyond Personal Assistants Towards Organizational Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34392) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34392)


## One-sentence takeaway
ORG-AGENT combines task dependency graphs with constraint-aware retrieval and execution to improve aggregate multi-user benchmark performance, while some backbones suffer substantial privacy or access-utility regressions (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
A shared organizational agent must coordinate users with different authority and permissions while interpreting information whose meaning depends on its author and temporal validity. The authors identify two complementary needs: cross-user coordination and decision-making, and question answering over accumulated multi-user conversations. They argue that concatenating conversations leaves these constraints implicit, while handling users independently misses dependencies between their requests (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Organizational constraint framing:** The authors organize requirements around users, information, and joint decisions, applying this framing to both coordination and memory use (§§1, 3).
- **Dynamic task dependency graph:** An LLM decomposes pending requests into inquiry, decision, and response nodes, identifies prerequisite edges, checks for cycles, and revises the remaining graph when inputs change. Topological sorting orders execution (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Tool-supported execution:** Nodes receive relevant constraints and accumulated execution memory. Retrieval combines lexical and semantic scoring, metadata filtering, and traversal of explicit record links; memory read/write operations retain intermediate evidence and conclusions (§4.4).
- **Evaluation across complementary tasks:** Existing benchmarks test both capabilities, component ablations, group-size trends, retrieval-weight sensitivity, and token consumption (§5; Appendix E). The paper does not introduce a new benchmark.

The evidence supports aggregate improvements in the evaluated settings and performance contributions from dependencies and the combined tool package. It does **not** establish reliable permission enforcement, superiority on every task, real-organizational effectiveness, or statistical significance. The authors’ model-generality claim is supported by several tested backbones, but remains bounded by those evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied paper has no dedicated limitations discussion. The following are **analyst inferences grounded in its reported setup**:

- **Constraint compliance is incomplete:** Table 2’s privacy and utility regressions show that higher aggregate scores can conceal worse access-control behavior.
- **Evaluation scope is bounded:** MUSES-Bench uses simulated users driven by the same backbone as the agent, caps interaction length, and evaluates only partial-disclosure meetings (§5.1; Appendix A).
- **Component attribution is coarse:** The tool ablation bundles three removals, preventing attribution to filtering, traversal, or memory separately (§5.4).
- **Uncertainty is unreported:** No confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, or significance tests are supplied.
- **Efficiency evidence is partial:** Token totals cover GroupMemBench build and QA stages; they do not establish latency, monetary cost, or coordination-task efficiency (Appendix E.3).
- **Reproducibility remains difficult to assess:** The statement claims an anonymous repository referenced in the abstract, but no such URL appears in the supplied text (p. 10).

## Open research questions
- **What causes the access-control regressions?** Table 2 leaves their mechanism unresolved. A next experiment could audit failed requests and compare current execution with an explicit permission gate.
- **Which tools produce the gains?** The bundled ablation cannot separate their effects. Independently remove filtering, traversal, and memory under matched retrieval and token budgets.
- **Does coordination transfer to human participants?** Evaluation uses simulated users. Test the same workflows with humans, measuring completion, interaction burden, and permission violations.
- **How robust is planning to erroneous dependencies?** Graph construction relies on LLM judgments without a reported graph-quality evaluation. Annotate prerequisite graphs and test execution under missing or incorrect edges.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark comparisons; lower in deployment-level generalization.

Material missing information includes an accessible repository URL, exact executable prompts and tool schemas, retrieval limits and context budgets, decoding and run settings, statistical uncertainty, isolated tool ablations, graph-construction error rates, latency, and human organizational evaluation.
