# EngiWorld: What Can Frontier Agents Deliver in Professional Engineering Environments?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37686) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37686)


## One-sentence takeaway
EngiWorld evaluates agents through engineering artifacts across professional software, with the strongest tested model scoring 44.3/100 on a 300-task subset and only 6 of 168 multi-software attempts succeeding, although some failures reflect restrictive delivery checks rather than invalid designs (Table 2; Appendix C.3).

## Problem and motivation
Engineering automation requires agents to preserve geometry, physical constraints, and dependencies through construction, simulation, export, and delivery. A plausible screenshot or completed software operation does not establish that an artifact works.

The authors identify gaps in existing benchmarks: limited domain and software coverage, insufficient interaction depth, and incomplete evaluation of feedback and cross-application workflows. Their claim that EngiWorld is the first benchmark covering the complete design loop is supported by their benchmark comparison, rather than independently established here (§§1–2; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 1,301 expert-curated tasks across six engineering domains and 26 platforms/workbenches, partitioned into six task types covering visual modeling, tool selection, single-tool execution, quantitative design, multi-tool workflows, and open-ended work (§3.3).
- **Artifact evaluation:** Domain-specific verifiers reopen final and required intermediate files to check geometry, physical settings, connectivity, and cross-stage consistency. Binary tasks require every criterion to pass; quantitative tasks receive continuous quality scores only after feasibility checks (§3.4; Appendix B.2).
- **Comparative evaluation and diagnostics:** Seven models share a zero-shot, stratified 300-task evaluation. Observation and history ablations, verifier audits, and artifact-level case studies investigate where execution fails (§4; Appendices B–D).

The contribution is an evaluation framework and benchmark, rather than a newly trained engineering agent.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best overall performance remains below half the score range | On the shared 300-task subset, Claude Opus 5 scores 44.3 and GPT-5.6 Sol 38.0. EngiScore equally averages binary success and feasibility-gated quantitative quality, scaled to 100. | Table 2; §4.1, Eq. 3 |
| Multi-software success is rare | Seven models attempt 24 multi-software tasks each: 6/168 attempts succeed, reported as 3.6%. Claude scores 8.3 versus 50.3 on 175 single-software tasks. | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Many tasks defeat every evaluated model | All seven models receive zero on 128/300 tasks, including 65 single-software and 22 multi-software tasks. | Appendix C.1; Figure C.1 |
| Quantitative scoring distinguishes validity from quality | Across 33 quantitative tasks × seven models, 169 runs score zero, 53 receive partial credit, and nine receive full credit. | Table C.1 |
| Easier reference access can help without uniformly improving outcomes | On 60 GUI reference-image tasks, placing drawings in the message raises GPT EngiScore from 15.0 to 31.7; 15 failures become successes while five successes become failures. | Table 3; Appendix C.4 |
| Completion signals frequently disagree with verification | Among 1,487 analyzed main-evaluation failures, 695 end with DONE and 704 exhaust decision rounds; together they account for 94.1%. Required-field and missing-file failures are excluded. | §4.4; Table D.2 |
| Prescribed delivery obligations can determine failure | All seven models fail multi-software task 09, while four pass its open-ended version. Gemini’s multi-software run fails a log-size check before geometry evaluation; the open-ended version requires fewer deliverables and permits direct construction. | Appendix C.3; Table C.3 |
| Completed verifier audits show acceptance within limited populations | After review and repair, 96/96 audited reference artifacts pass; alternative-valid tests accept 80 solver outputs and five lightweight designs. Tolerance-boundary tests are outside completed audit populations. | Appendix B.3; Table B.3 |

These results establish substantial difficulty under the specified harness and acceptance criteria. They do not establish equivalent failure rates across the full benchmark or isolate engineering reasoning from interface, budget, schema, and delivery effects. EngiScore also combines distinct task objectives; it is not a universal measure of design quality.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-disclosed coverage limits:** Main results use a subset, and open-ended evaluation includes only four tasks. The paired workflow comparison covers two cases (§4.1; Appendix C.3).
- **Author-disclosed verifier limits:** Completed alternative-solution audits cover selected families; tolerance-boundary testing is incomplete (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference—acceptance sensitivity:** A pressure load corresponding approximately to the requested resultant force is rejected because the checker requires native force/displacement parameters. A CSV alias mismatch prevents downstream geometry checks. These examples weaken any interpretation that every zero score means physically invalid engineering (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference—causal ambiguity:** Open-ended versus multi-software comparisons change tool freedom and deliverable obligations together. Initial-image conditions in the runtime-image ablation use different task sets, which the authors explicitly acknowledge (Appendices C.3–C.4).
- **Reporting gaps:** The supplied text provides no repeated-run variability or confidence intervals. Several ablation details appear only graphically, and public availability of complete executable assets is not established.

## Open research questions
- **Would in-loop artifact verification reduce false completion?** DONE-with-zero failures are common. A next experiment could expose selected verifier feedback during execution and compare success and cost under matched budgets (§4.4).
- **How much multi-software difficulty comes from handoffs versus delivery rules?** Task 09 fails before geometry checks. A factorial experiment could independently vary prescribed software, intermediate artifacts, and documentation requirements (Appendix C.3).
- **How robust are verifiers to equivalent engineering representations?** Audit coverage is limited, and load/schema examples expose restrictive acceptance. A next audit could test independently labeled equivalent representations and tolerance-boundary perturbations across domains (Appendices B.3, D.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark design and reported outcomes clearly, but broader capability conclusions depend on subset selection and verifier behavior.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, full executable task/checker packages and release status, complete tolerance-boundary audits, and exact numerical results and task composition for several graphical ablations.
