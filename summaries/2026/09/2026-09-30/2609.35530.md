# AutoRef: Harness Optimization for Agentic Multi-Reference Image Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35530) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35530)


## One-sentence takeaway
AutoRef automatically rewrites the code coordinating frozen reasoning and image models, producing a three-image harness that raises FLUX.2 [klein] 4B’s held-out four-reference MultiBanana score from 5.72 to 7.37/10 and transfers to several additional settings (§§4–6, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-reference image generation must preserve subjects and attributes from separate images while integrating them into a coherent scene. Generators can omit, duplicate, misassign, or unnaturally paste reference content. The authors identify a gap in automatic harness optimization: existing approaches largely target verifiable tasks, while visual generation supplies noisy scores and ambiguous failure signals (§§1–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Search procedure:** A coding agent rewrites executable harnesses around frozen models. Proposal feedback comes from training tasks; separate validation tasks select candidates, with their scores and artifacts hidden from the proposer. A beam retains two of four new candidates per iteration; parents do not compete again (§4, Algorithm 1).
- **Discovered harness:** AutoRef-Harness grounds prompts in reference roles, generates two structurally different drafts, creates a third from reference-specific complaints, and selects using hard-failure counts followed by comparisons in both presentation orders (§5).
- **Empirical contribution:** The paper evaluates the resulting harness against generators, human-written workflows, and alternative search procedures, with component ablations, transfer tests, and human preferences (§6, Appendices G–I). Authors report releasing code, the harness, splits, and pinned model revisions (reproducibility statement).

## Evidence and main results

For MultiBanana, scores below average five criteria scored 1–10 and then average across task types, using Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Main held-out improvement | On 133 four-reference tasks, FLUX.2 4B improves **5.72 → 7.37**, using three images instead of one. Single-image Nano Banana Pro scores 7.20, GPT-Image-1.5 7.07, and Seedream 4.5 7.03. | §6.1; Tables 1, 6 |
| Better than tested workflows and search outputs | Same four-reference setting: Best-of-3 6.01; IPR 7.02; Idea2Img 7.12; Meta-Harness 6.23; Greedy Search 6.67; AutoRef-Harness 7.37. Image counts are respectively 3, 3, 9, 4.2, 5, and 3. | §6.4; Table 6 |
| Transfer across generators and reasoning models | Four-reference setting: FLUX.2 9B **5.78 → 7.10** and Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 **4.44 → 5.69**, each with one versus three images. Replacing GPT-5.5 with Qwen3-VL-32B yields 6.90 for FLUX.2 4B. | Table 1; Figure 4; Table 6 |
| Transfer across reference counts, with an exception | On 96 tasks per setting, FLUX.2 4B improves **6.94 → 7.76** with three references and **5.19 → 6.36** with five, using one versus three images. Five-reference Qwen instead changes **1.89 → 1.87**. | Table 2; Tables 7–8 |
| Transfer to another benchmark and evaluator | On 120 OmniContext tasks, GPT-4.1 evaluates prompt following and subject consistency. Their per-task geometric mean, averaged across eight types, improves **8.30 → 8.85/10** for FLUX.2 4B, using one versus three images. | §6.1; Table 3 |
| Components improve the full workflow | Four-reference held-out score falls from 7.37 to 7.01 without diverse drafts, 6.99 without complaint revision, and 6.93 without failure-aware selection; these variants retain three images. Grounding alone scores 6.91 with one image. | Table 4; Table 9 |
| Human preferences support improvement | Four raters compare outputs on 50 held-out tasks per baseline. AutoRef-Harness wins/ties/loses **70/13/17%** against base FLUX.2 4B and **46/14/40%** against Nano Banana Pro. | §6.6; Figure 5 |
| Gains also occur after fine-tuning | Four-reference Qwen setting: DyRef alone scores 4.91 with one image; DyRef plus the unchanged harness scores 5.90 with three. | Appendix H.3; Table 6 |

The evidence supports improvements for the tested complete systems and several unchanged-harness transfers. Equal-image-budget workflow comparisons indicate that additional sampling alone does not explain the gains. It does not establish universal proprietary-model superiority, statistical significance, or superiority at equal total compute cost. Human evaluation supports competitiveness with Nano Banana Pro, with wins only modestly exceeding losses.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The harness remains bounded by the frozen generator’s capabilities. For five-reference Qwen tasks, its failure checker flags all three drafts on 87 of 96 tasks; selection cannot rescue consistently unacceptable candidates (§7, Appendix I).
- **Author-stated:** Search maximizes one VLM evaluator’s score and inherits model and evaluator limitations and biases. Cross-evaluator transfer and human evaluation provide supporting checks (§7, ethics statement).
- **Analyst inference:** Repeated selection on the same validation set can still adapt the search indirectly through survivor identities; hiding scores reduces direct feedback but does not eliminate selection dependence (§4, Algorithm 1).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied results report no uncertainty intervals or repeated independent search runs. Search reliability and small score differences remain difficult to assess. Meta-Harness is tested for five iterations, versus its original 20–40, limiting conclusions about longer searches (Appendix G.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Grounding lacks a clean leave-one-component-out ablation; grounding-only and selection-only variants change several components together (§6.5).
- **Author-disclosed:** Appendix J examples were selected for large improvement gaps, so they illustrate successes rather than representative output quality.

## Open research questions
- **How stable is automatic search?** Independent search repetitions and uncertainty are unreported. Repeat all search methods across proposer sessions, generation seeds, and task splits, then compare score distributions.
- **Does validation separation retain its benefit under longer searches?** Survivor identities provide indirect feedback. Vary search length and compare fixed versus refreshed selection sets on a separately untouched test set.
- **Can staged composition overcome generator failures?** Five-reference Qwen remains unsuccessful. Compare simultaneous composition with sequential or grouped-reference generation on the same tasks (Appendix I).
- **What is the quality–cost trade-off?** Image counts are reported, but complete reasoning and search costs are unavailable. Measure latency, model calls, and monetary cost while varying draft budgets and adaptive stopping.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported workflow and benchmark results; lower in claims of general superiority or search robustness.

Material missing information includes uncertainty estimates, repeated-search variability, complete runtime and API costs, human-rater agreement and aggregation details, and exact pinned revision hashes within the supplied text. The claimed release was not independently checked.
