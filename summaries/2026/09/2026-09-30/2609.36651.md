# FocusVTC: Efficient and High-Performance Visual Text Compression with Adaptive Resolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36651) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36651)


## One-sentence takeaway
FocusVTC learns to selectively read high-resolution crops of compressed document images, achieving 87.38% on the evaluated RULER v1 subset at 2.9× input compression versus Glyph’s 57.53% at 3.0×, including FocusVTC’s tool observations (§4.4; Tables 10, 15).

## Problem and motivation
Rendering long text as images reduces input tokens, but fixed resolution ties local readability to whole-document cost: low resolution obscures characters, while high resolution spends tokens on irrelevant passages. The authors identify selective evidence access as the gap: global coverage and precise local reading need not share one resolution (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive reading:** A Qwen3.5-9B policy reads low-resolution pages and requests page-indexed bounding boxes from aligned high-resolution sources, incorporating returned crops into ongoing reasoning. Default evaluation uses 72-DPI pages and 144-DPI enhancement (§§3.1, 4.1).
- **Grounded supervision:** REL-CoT contains 29,411 examples linking reasoning and answers to page indices and evidence boxes. Separate models filter context-independent questions, annotate evidence, and verify crop support; seven rendering resolutions expand the supervised candidate pool (§3.2; Appendix B.1).
- **Two-stage training:** REL-SFT teaches localization and answering; GRPO learns enhancement and stopping, rewarding correctness, valid outputs, and correct-answer-gated efficient localization. No separate continual-pretraining stage is used (§§3.3–3.4).

The contribution is this combination of grounded supervision and learned iterative enhancement; adaptive resolution itself has related precedents acknowledged in §2.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher retrieval/QA performance at similar compression | At 72 DPI, RULER v1’s ten-task subset: FocusVTC 87.38% versus Glyph 57.53%; respective input compression 2.9× and 3.0×. FocusVTC counts initial prompt plus observations. | §4.4; Tables 10, 15 |
| Slightly exceeds text backbone on LongBench | Unweighted mean over eleven displayed non-code tasks: 56.40% versus Qwen3.5-9B Text 55.86%, fixed-resolution Vision 35.86%, and Glyph 52.34%; FocusVTC input compression is 3.0×. | Tables 1, 10 |
| Improves MRCR aggregate | Macro-average across six length bins and two/four/eight needles: 45.56% versus text backbone 31.65%, a 13.91-point gain. Longest-bin prompt-plus-observation compression is 3.3×/3.1×/3.0× for the respective needle counts. | §1; Table 14; Appendix C.1 |
| VTCBench gains are uneven | At 72 DPI, Retrieval/Reasoning/Memory averages are 91.00%/36.25%/26.33%, versus fixed-resolution backbone 81.26%/41.57%/20.94%; authors report a 51.19% macro-average. Each category averages four length bins. | Table 2; §5 |
| Learned tool use matters | LongBench: full model 56.40%, without tools 36.82%, direct GRPO without REL-SFT 49.30%, and REL-SFT with tools but without GRPO 25.41%, all at 72 DPI. | Table 3 |
| Lower online latency in one controlled setting | On 300 paired MRCR four-needle examples with 64K–128K contexts, two A100 80GB GPUs, sequential requests and greedy decoding: mean latency falls from 187.09 to 67.06 seconds, a 2.79× speedup; page rendering is excluded. | Appendix C.2; Table 11 |
| General benchmark scores largely preserved | MMMU rises from 65.12 to 66.73 and MME from 2424.02 to 2457.62 versus the backbone, on their benchmark scales. All six general benchmarks improve, but MMLongBench summarization and DocQA decline from 29.19/68.96 to 28.40/67.27. | Table 17 |

These results support effective learned evidence acquisition under the tested rendering and benchmark settings. They do not establish universal improvement: VTCBench Reasoning trails the fixed-resolution backbone, and LongBench’s advantage over text is small. The latency gain accompanies substantially fewer generated tokens, so it cannot be attributed solely to input compression (Appendix C.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Only one backbone scale and a limited localization corpus are studied; broader languages, layouts, reasoning demands, larger models, and larger context scales remain unestablished (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** The compression metric excludes generated tokens, and latency excludes rendering. Neither alone measures complete deployment cost (Appendices C.1–C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Main benchmark comparisons lack reported uncertainty or repeated-run variability, limiting interpretation of small score differences. Latency examples are each timed once after warmup (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Stage and tool ablations support the combined training recipe, but individual reward components and multi-resolution supervision are not separately ablated in the supplied results (§3.4; Table 3).
- **Scope caution:** RULER v1 excludes three original tasks; v2 permits relaxed matching on some tasks. The reported averages therefore concern these specified evaluation protocols (Appendix C.4).
- **Illustration caution:** Figure 10 adapts tool boxes and observations; it illustrates behavior rather than preserving an untouched execution trace (Figure 10 caption).

## Open research questions
- **Does the method scale across backbones and supervision sizes?** Appendix D leaves this unresolved. Train several backbone sizes with controlled REL-CoT subsets and compare quality against total training and inference cost.
- **Why does aggregate VTCBench Reasoning decline?** Table 2 shows longer-bin gains alongside a lower overall average. Analyze errors by length and evidence dispersion, then test adaptive resolution on matched localized and distributed-evidence questions.
- **How robust is localization across languages and layouts?** The authors acknowledge limited corpus diversity. Evaluate unseen scripts and document layouts with independently checked evidence boxes.
- **Which training ingredients are necessary?** Existing ablations do not isolate reward terms or resolution diversity. Compare matched-budget training runs removing each component separately.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported settings and descriptive comparisons; causal explanations remain less certain.

Material missing information includes benchmark uncertainty and repeated-run results, an independent human audit of REL-CoT annotations, isolated reward/resolution ablations, training GPU models, measured rendering-inclusive latency, and verified downloadable artifact locations.
