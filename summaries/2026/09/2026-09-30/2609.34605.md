# PMOPD: Task Ordering, Cycling, and Parameter-Update Subspace Protection in Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34605) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34605)


## One-sentence takeaway
PMOPD combines task scheduling with projection of gradients and optimizer updates away from remembered task-update subspaces, improving all three evaluated capabilities over MOPD in two language-model families (Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation integrates specialist teachers into one student using teacher supervision on student-generated trajectories. Shared parameters create a “capability seesaw”: learning one domain can degrade another. The authors identify a gap in single-task distillation research and ordinary task mixing, which do not explicitly protect useful updates from earlier teachers (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Geometric characterization:** The authors study cumulative parameter displacements rather than individual gradients, finding early-emerging task subspaces with low cross-task overlap (§3.2).
- **Subspace protection:** For each two-dimensional trainable matrix, PMOPD extracts the top-16 right singular vectors of completed task-block displacements and combines them into an orthogonal memory. It projects both gradients and preconditioned Adafactor updates away from this memory; memory resets each cycle (§§4.3–4.5).
- **Task organization:** A probe ranks tasks by symmetrized removed-gradient ratios. The selected Code→Reason→Math order and four-cycle schedule balance revisitation with subspace-estimation consistency (§7).

The distinctive contribution is protection based on realized OPD displacements, including optimizer effects, combined with dual projection and scheduling diagnostics. Projection and low-dimensional adaptation themselves are acknowledged prior method families (§2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Llama improves across tasks | PMOPD versus MOPD: Math 21.33 vs 18.22; Reason 69.45 vs 68.63; Code 32.33 vs 30.00. Average: 41.04 vs 38.95, a 2.09-point gain. | Table 3; §6.2 |

| Probe recovers the best tested order | All ten 60-example probe folds rank Code<Reason<Math. Code→Reason→Math achieves the highest one-cycle average, 65.81, among six orders. | Table 5; Figure 3; Table 6 |
| Four cycles best among tested schedules | Fixed task order and total prompts: average percentage score peaks at 66.97 across schedules with 1, 2, 4, 5, 8 and 10 cycles; mean revisit-subspace similarity also peaks at four cycles, at 0.525. | Figure 4; Table 8 |
| Gain persists against stronger mixing variants | Best listed Qwen mixing variant averages 64.83 versus PMOPD’s 66.97, a 2.14-point difference. | Appendix A.3; Table 9 |

These results support improved integration in the tested setting and an incremental benefit from optimizer-update projection. They do not establish universal scheduling rules or functional preservation from geometric orthogonality alone. Gains are not uniformly monotonic across projection variants: Reason decreases when optimizer-update projection is added (Table 4). Llama PMOPD also remains below its Code teacher’s Code score (Table 3).

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper provides no explicit limitations section.

**Analyst inferences grounded in the supplied text:**

- Evaluation covers three domains and two base-model families; broader transfer remains untested (§5).
- PMOPD scores average three seeds, but score dispersion and baseline seed treatment are unavailable, limiting assessment of statistical reliability (§5.2).
- Projection removes all components aligned with memory, without a test that each component is harmful. The linear preservation statement does not establish preservation of whole-model behavior (§4.5).
- Rank is fixed at 16; rank sensitivity and comparisons against instantaneous-gradient projection methods are not reported (§§2.2, 3.2).
- Scheduling evidence comes from the same task trio. Performance–consistency correlation supports the proposed mechanism but does not establish causality (§7).
- Table 3’s Qwen MOPD row differs from both within-batch variants in Table 9; their exact relationship is unclear.

## Open research questions
- **Does protection scale to more tasks?** Only three are tested. Add domains and measure capability retention, memory growth and projection cost.
- **How sensitive is protection to rank?** Only rank 16 is reported. Sweep ranks under the same schedule and compare performance, removed-gradient ratios and overhead.
- **Does the probe select good orders elsewhere?** Its ranking matches the best order for one task trio. Test new task combinations against exhaustive ordering where feasible.
- **Does scheduling guidance work prospectively?** Four cycles maximize both measured consistency and evaluation performance. Select cycles using diagnostics alone, then evaluate on held-out settings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The tables support the reported gains, but uncertainty and reproducibility details are limited.

Material missing information includes exact task-score definitions and decoding settings, seed-level results, full optimizer and RL-teacher recipes, numerical QR threshold, runtime and memory overhead, precise Llama data configuration, and code/checkpoint availability.
