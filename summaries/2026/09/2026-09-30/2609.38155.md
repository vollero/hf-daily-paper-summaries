# Beyond the Timeline: Augmenting Long-Video Memory with Grounded Entity Biographies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38155) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38155)


## One-sentence takeaway
Grounded Entity Biographies links visually matched object and person observations across events, improving long-video question answering over matched memory baselines, although identity accuracy remains unmeasured and the gameplay advantage over the strongest competitor is uncertain.

## Problem and motivation
Long-video questions often require following one physical object or person across disconnected encounters. Chronological captions can retrieve relevant events while conflating different objects with similar descriptions or splitting one object across changing names. The authors identify physical-instance correspondence as a gap in language-derived entity memory: relevant events alone do not establish that they concern the same entity (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Grounded biography memory:** Detect and track subjects within clips, describe encounters using crops, scene frames, and available textual context, then organize observations into temporally ordered biographies linked to episodes and source clips (§3.1–3.3).
- **Conservative instance association:** Multimodal similarity must satisfy both consistency across retained references and a strong individual match. Evidence that two observations occupy separate boxes in shared frames vetoes association. Named-person consolidation adds appearance-profile checks (§3.3; Appendix A).
- **Identity-guided retrieval and reading:** Personalized PageRank propagates relevance through instance and context links. Biography excerpts show selected encounters and times of uninspected appearances, giving the controller targets for further searches; notes distinguish proven separation from unresolved identity (§3.4).
- **Controlled empirical analysis:** Matched controller and answer models, retrieval diagnostics, description controls, and graph/text ablations test whether identity organization contributes beyond extra descriptions (§4).

The contribution is this integrated memory construction and reading framework; the detector, tracker, embedding models, and PageRank are adopted components.

These results support the authors’ claim that biography organization improves retrieval and answering in the tested settings. They do not directly establish reliable physical identity: evidence coverage counts temporal overlap, including description-context windows extending beyond visibility. Most headline confidence intervals treat the baseline score as a fixed anchor rather than incorporating paired uncertainty from both systems (Appendix D, p. 20).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Tracking and re-identification can assign observations to incorrect biographies; descriptions can attribute nearby actions to the wrong entity; the larger graph increases retrieval latency (Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Several benchmarks reuse participant A1’s same week, while the additional long recording is one gameplay stream. Benchmark diversity therefore exceeds recording diversity (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—calibration dependence:** Association thresholds are selected per recording by inspecting sampled merges. Transfer without recording-specific inspection is untested (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—identity validation:** No annotated instance-association accuracy or quantitative action-attribution evaluation is reported. QA ablations support usefulness without measuring the correctness of the underlying biographies.
- **Analyst inference—cost and comparisons:** Query-time measurements exclude controller and answer-model calls; offline construction cost is unreported. WorldMM has different per-store retrieval allowances, partially addressed through retrieved-duration reporting (Appendices B–C).
- **Evidence qualification:** The strongest gameplay comparison is statistically unresolved, and association removal leaves the MultiHop judge score unchanged (Tables 11, 14).

## Open research questions
- **How accurate are the biographies themselves?** No direct identity evaluation is reported. Annotate cross-clip instance correspondences and measure false merges/splits alongside downstream QA.
- **Can association transfer without manual calibration?** Thresholds vary by recording. Freeze thresholds on one recording, evaluate unseen participants and domains, and compare with automatic calibration.
- **When does better coverage improve answers?** WorldMM has comparable complete coverage but weaker answers, while association removal preserves MultiHop judge score. Hold retrieved intervals and context budgets fixed, then vary identity links and biography rendering.
- **Can the framework operate efficiently online?** Memory is constructed offline and retrieval timing excludes model calls. Test incremental construction with end-to-end latency, memory usage, and QA accuracy.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and results; lower in broad generalization and identity reliability.

Material unavailable information includes direct association/action-attribution accuracy, offline construction time and cost, complete end-to-end latency, threshold sensitivity, independent participant-week replication, and confirmed code or memory-asset availability.
