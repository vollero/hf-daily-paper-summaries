# Memory Is a Derivation: The Distributed-Evidence Paradox in Long-Term Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36130) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36130)


## One-sentence takeaway
DERIVAUDIT shows that expanding historical evidence rescues many valid agent memories with incomplete citations, but neither broader evidence nor composition-aware verification reliably prevents unsupported memories from entering storage (§4.1, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Persistent memories become premises for later agent tasks, so errors introduced during compression can outlive their original context. The authors distinguish two failures: incomplete citations can make a valid memory appear unsupported, while separately supported facts can be combined into an unsupported relation or event status. They identify a gap between checking provenance or recalling stored information and verifying whether the complete memory follows from the history available when written (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Derivation criterion:** “Semantic derivation integrity” requires historical support for every factual claim, relation, and qualification stored in a memory (§3.2).
- **DERIVAUDIT framework:** Compare citations with expanded pre-write history, decompose memories into semantic obligations, and measure supported-memory retention alongside unsupported-memory admission. Expansion adds up to 12 BM25 passages within a 16-passage total budget; retrieval-limited insufficient cases remain unresolved (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Empirical characterization:** Natural audits and controlled interventions examine incomplete provenance, unsupported composition under distributed evidence, rejection caused by additional correct checks, and downstream consequences. This is an audit framework and failure-mode study; the authors do not establish a universally effective admission method (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broader history repairs incomplete provenance | Among citation-insufficient writes, expansion recovers support for 60.0% on LoCoMo and 59.8% on HaluMem-Medium; supported fractions rise from 53.5%→79.5% and 47.0%→77.0%, respectively. Natural audit: 200 writes per corpus. | §4.2, Fig. 3(a); weighting described in Appendix D |
| Repaired-memory retention improves, but unsupported admission remains high | On 391 resolved writes—313 supported, 78 negative—citation-only→expanded→obligation-aware repaired retention is 82.1%→93.8%→92.9% for Qwen, 58.0%→95.5%→92.0% for Gemma, and 85.7%→98.2%→97.3% for Llama. Corresponding unsupported admission is 84.6%→80.8%→76.9%, 66.7%→76.9%→59.0%, and 83.3%→89.7%→88.5%. | §4.1, Table 2 |
| Composition-aware representations show trade-offs | On the 512-record suite, Qwen compact relations achieve 59.5% unsupported-relation detection and 94.9% valid multi-passage retention, versus holistic verification’s 60.8% and 84.6%. Gemma compact relations reject every valid multi-passage memory. | Table 3 |
| Plausible premises increase acceptance of unsupported compositions | In the 69-family Qwen mechanism control, replacing plausible distributed premises with length- and position-matched irrelevant content reduces invalid acceptance from 88.4%→10.1% for holistic, 88.4%→0% for obligation-aware, and 65.2%→0% for predicate–argument QA. | Appendix G, Table 7 |
| Additional true checks can reject valid memories | Adding four independently verified true obligations leaves the candidate unchanged but reduces acceptance from 99.4%→98.1% for Qwen, 93.3%→2.6% for Gemma, and 97.4%→35.5% for Llama. | §4.2, Table 6 |

Retrieval position and surrounding context are fixed. | §4.3, Appendix H |

The evidence supports a two-sided verification problem in the evaluated settings: recovering evidence helps preserve valid memories, while admitting unsupported compositions remains common. Controlled probes establish effects of memory-slot interventions on selected queries, not performance gains in a complete autonomous memory system. Operating points differ by backbone, so the paper explicitly restricts admission comparisons to within-model contrasts (Appendix F).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Reference labels primarily come from model instances; stratified human review reports 96.0% agreement, but is not the primary labeling process. Retrieval is bounded, natural contradictions and explicit relations are relatively scarce, controlled mutation coverage is partial, and results depend on models, prompts, representations, and aggregation. Consolidation drift is observational; downstream probes are not closed-loop evaluations (§5).

**Analyst inference:** Reporting ambiguities limit reconstruction from the text alone. Appendix C accounts for 160 annotated records without clearly connecting them to the 400-write audit. Composition-graph results differ between Tables 3 and 4 without a clearly stated explanation. The artifact is described, but no repository or download link is supplied. Appendix C also records a failed preregistered rare-label occupancy gate as a protocol deviation.

## Open research questions
- **Can better retrieval improve support recovery without increasing false admission?** Expansion remains bounded and worsens unsupported admission on two backbones. Next experiment: vary retrieval budgets and compare against exhaustive pre-write evidence on tractable histories.
- **Can relation-preserving verification generalize across backbones?** No representation consistently balances detection and retention. Next experiment: evaluate frozen interfaces on held-out relation types with equal prompt-search budgets.
- **Can aggregation avoid accumulated verification noise?** Tested aggregation rules shift rather than eliminate the trade-off. Next experiment: compare calibrated aggregation methods while systematically varying the number of independently true obligations.
- **Do admission improvements survive full memory lifecycles?** Downstream interventions hold retrieval context fixed. Next experiment: run closed-loop writing, consolidation, retrieval, and answering under competing gates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main findings have explicit settings and numerical evidence, but generalization and reproducibility remain constrained.

Missing or unclear information includes an accessible artifact location, the connection between annotation accounting and the full natural audit, the cause of differing graph results, detailed downstream sample allocation, and complete uncertainty estimates for several headline results.
