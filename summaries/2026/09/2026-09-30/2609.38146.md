# LIFT: Layout-In-Future Video Generation under Large Viewpoint Change via On-Policy Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38146) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38146)


## One-sentence takeaway

LIFT transfers dense-layout supervision to a camera-conditioned video generator using dual-mode on-policy self-distillation, achieving stronger final-frame layout control than the tested baselines with competitive—but not uniformly superior—video quality (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation

Large camera movements expose content absent from the initial image. Camera trajectories specify viewpoint movement, while text prompts cannot precisely determine the objects and spatial composition of newly revealed views. Existing video layout controls typically require dense frame-wise guidance and focus on initially visible objects, increasing user effort (§1).

The authors target image-to-video generation controlled by a camera trajectory and an optional final-frame layout, so users can specify future composition without annotating every frame.

## Approach and contributions

- **Future-layout control:** LIFT combines first-frame appearance, text, camera trajectories, and colored bounding-box layouts. A shared VAE encodes layout maps; color-linked local prompts specify object semantics; Plücker-ray camera tokens enter the diffusion Transformer through token-wise addition (§3.2; Figure 3).
- **Dual-mode OPSD:** After camera-control and dense-layout training, a frozen dense-layout teacher supervises a shared student on states generated by the student itself. Training alternates between final-frame-layout and camera-only conditioning, emphasizing early denoising states and retaining a flow-matching anchor to preserve quality. The contribution is this task-specific training scheme, rather than OPSD itself (§§3.1–3.3).
- **LIFT-Vista:** The authors curate camera- and layout-annotated clips from RealEstate10K, Sekai, and SpatialVID, filtering for viewpoint expansion and newly revealed content. Final-frame objects are detected and tracked backward through clips (§2; Appendix A.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest reported layout control | On the curated 600-clip test set, LIFT achieves mIoU **0.51**, entity success **0.59**, and local CLIP **0.24**, versus MagicMotion’s **0.41/0.53/0.21**. MagicMotion receives dense layouts; LIFT receives only final-frame layouts. | Table 1; §4.2; Appendix A.1 |
| Best camera errors, mixed quality ranking | LIFT RotErr/TransErr: **2.97/0.59**, versus Uni3C **3.23/0.70**. LIFT FVD **99.35** trails GEN3C **89.59**; FID **12.84** trails Uni3C **12.75**; LPIPS **0.42** ties Uni3C. | Table 1 |
| OPSD improves sparse-layout adaptation | Final-frame mIoU: OPSD **0.51**, direct SFT **0.44**, curriculum SFT **0.47**. Full adaptation from the same camera checkpoint uses **136K** sample updates for OPSD, including dense-layout training, versus **256K** for either SFT alternative. | Tables 2, 7 |
| Anchor improves generation quality | Removing the anchor raises FVD from **99.35** to **129.14** and lowers mIoU from **0.51** to **0.49** in the OPSD ablation. | Table 2 |
| Dual-mode gains involve tradeoffs | Camera-only FVD improves to **95.66**, versus **105.22** for camera-only single-mode training, but RotErr worsens to **3.30** versus **3.03**. Final-frame mIoU is **0.51**, versus **0.49** for layout-only single-mode training. | Table 3 |
| Additional layouts improve spatial alignment | Without retraining, dense layouts yield mIoU **0.6217**, versus **0.5094** with final-frame-only layouts; FVD is **98.84** versus **99.35**. | Table 6 |
| Human preference favors LIFT | Seven participants provide **210** responses across **30** comparisons each. Preference is **65.96%** for LIFT, **22.34%** for Uni3C, and **11.70%** for MagicMotion, excluding “None preferred” responses. | Appendix A.2.4; Table 10 |

These results support improved future-layout control and useful sharing between conditioning modes in the reported setting. They do not establish superiority on every metric, generalization beyond the curated distribution, or proportional compute savings: sample-update counts omit differing per-update rollout and teacher-query costs.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Bounding boxes and local prompts provide coarse constraints without explicit depth, orientation, or occlusion relationships (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Evaluation uses 81-frame clips at 16 FPS and 352 × 640 resolution; longer or higher-resolution generation remains untested in the supplied text (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Camera annotation and evaluation both use Depth Anything 3, while layout evaluation uses an automatic object detector. Independent validation of these measurements is not reported. The evaluator is named “Qwen3.6-27B” but linked to a Qwen3-VL reference, leaving its identity ambiguous (Appendix A.2.2).
- **Analyst inference—split validity:** Test clips are excluded from training, but source-video-level separation is unspecified; extraction permits multiple windows per source video (Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and uncertainty:** Baselines differ in supported controls and supplied conditioning. No confidence intervals or repeated-run variability are reported; the small user study also omits the number of “None preferred” responses (§4.2; Appendix A.2.4).

## Open research questions

- **Would richer geometry improve future composition?** The current representation omits depth and occlusion. Compare box-only control against depth-, orientation-, or instance-aware layouts under matched training budgets (Appendix A.3).
- **Does OPSD retain its advantage under equal compute?** Sample updates favor OPSD, but its updates include additional generation and teacher work. Compare methods at matched GPU-hours, including dense-teacher training (Tables 7–8).
- **How well does LIFT generalize beyond its curation?** Crowded scenes and some landscapes are filtered, and evaluation uses curated clips. Test independent source videos and these excluded scene categories (Appendix A.1).
- **Can early-state supervision be improved adaptively?** Prefix-state selection is tested in layout-only single-mode OPSD. Compare fixed and adaptive state selection in dual-mode training at matched compute (Appendix A.2.3; Table 8).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported comparisons clearly, but broader validity is uncertain.

Material missing information includes public artifact availability, source-video split separation, annotation accuracy audits, camera-error units and normalization, repeated-run uncertainty, complete compute costs, user-study exclusions and participant details, and an unambiguous layout-evaluator specification.
