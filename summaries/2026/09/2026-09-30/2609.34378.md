# Marathoner: Ultra-Long-Horizon Autonomous Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-30.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34378) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34378)


## One-sentence takeaway
Marathoner’s post-training pipeline improves a Qwen3.5-9B coding agent across five benchmarks, but its strongest evidence for execution beyond ten hours is one selected training-style task rather than a measured benchmark-wide success rate.

## Problem and motivation
The authors target autonomous agents that must sustain useful work over many hours, including environment setup, implementation, debugging, and testing. They identify limited disclosure of proprietary training methods and weak long-horizon performance in open-source models as the main gaps (§1). The demonstrated scope is software engineering and terminal use; broader autonomous intelligence remains an author ambition.

## Approach and contributions
- **PR-derived task synthesis:** Construct Harbor-format tasks from GitHub repositories checked out before substantive pull requests, with instructions and binary unit-test verifiers. Difficulty is defined by added code volume. **Multi-Task Chaining** combines five independent tasks, their repositories, instructions, and verifiers into a larger task (§2.1).
- **Teacher distillation across harnesses:** Generate Kimi K3 trajectories using Claude Code, Codex, and OpenClaw; retain successful trajectories and fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B with a 256k-token sequence length (§2.2; Appendix B.2).
- **Environment-interacting reinforcement learning:** Apply GRPO through Harbor sandboxes with randomly assigned harnesses. The proposed **Later Stage Bonus Reward** adds 0.5 when an LLM judge identifies an exceptionally valuable operation in the latter half of summarized execution phases (§2.3; Appendix D).

The distinctive proposals are the task-construction pipeline, chaining, and late-phase reward; teacher distillation, ReAct, and GRPO are adopted components.

The tables support consistent reported gains over the same base model and harness, plus additional gains after RL. They do not establish superiority over proprietary models generally: several proprietary baselines score substantially higher, and the Gemini comparison changes harness.

The case study demonstrates one extended execution. It does not establish how frequently such runs succeed, and its final success assertions come from the agent transcript rather than a reported task-verifier result.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. The authors acknowledge that excessive chaining can hurt benchmark performance and that an overly large bonus may reduce emphasis on final correctness (Appendix C).

**Analyst inferences and reporting gaps:**

- Evaluation supports coding and terminal capability; transfer to unrelated domains is untested.
- “Later” means the latter half of LLM-defined phases, which need not correspond to the latter half of elapsed time or tool calls (§2.3; Appendix D).
- Unit-test passage establishes verifier satisfaction, not complete specification coverage. The excerpt provides no systematic verifier-quality audit.
- No uncertainty estimates, repeated-run results, or matched evaluation budgets are supplied. Fractional scores on small benchmarks cannot be interpreted fully without aggregation details (§3.1; Table 1).
- RFT/RL stage comparisons and component ablations are present, but judge reliability, reward gaming, and compute-matched alternatives are not evaluated.
- Training–benchmark contamination checks and public artifact availability are not documented. Appendix B.3 explicitly states non-overlap for directly sampled RL tasks; overlap handling for chained components is less clear.

## Open research questions
- **Does the bonus improve useful persistence rather than duration?** Current results measure scores and average execution, without directly measuring late-stage progress. Compare matched-budget policies with and without the bonus using independent progress annotations.
- **Does chaining teach coordination or simply greater workload tolerance?** The tasks concatenate independent repositories. Compare independent bundles with equally costly tasks containing genuine cross-task dependencies.
- **How reliable is the LLM reward judge?** Prompts are provided, but agreement and error rates are absent. Audit phase boundaries and valuable-action labels against blinded human judgments.
- **How often do very long runs succeed?** One selected case supports the strongest duration claim. Report verifier success and productive-progress distributions across held-out tasks and execution budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported score improvements are clearly described; stronger claims about generality and consistently successful ultra-long execution have limited direct evidence.

Material missing information includes precise evaluation metrics and aggregation, inference budgets, run variance, contamination controls, judge-validation results, full long-run success distributions, case-study verifier outcome, total training cost, and confirmed model/data releases.
