# Fingers as Legs: Learning Self-Supported Locomotion and Manipulation with an Anthropomorphic Hand

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17172) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17172)


## One-sentence takeaway

A self-contained commercial anthropomorphic hand learned via simulation-trained reinforcement learning to crawl, steer, recover from falls, press keyboard keys, and push objects while using the same unequal fingers for support and interaction.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses mobile manipulation with an anthropomorphic hand that has no separate locomotion mechanism. The same fingers must propel the body, maintain balance, and manipulate controls or objects, while their unequal reach and opposed-thumb geometry prevent straightforward use of symmetry-based locomotion methods.

This matters for confined environments where a larger robot could place the hand near a restricted opening and retrieve it after it operates controls or moves objects. The identified gap is an untethered system that preserves a commercial hand’s original finger geometry and position controller while combining locomotion with self-supported manipulation (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Self-contained platform:** An 818 g mobile manipulator comprising a 738 g commercial WUJI hand and an 80 g dorsal module with battery, IMU, and Raspberry Pi. It retains the hand’s 20 actuated joints, finger kinematics, controller gains, and position-control interface (Section III-A, p. 2).

- **Morphology-adapted locomotion reward:** Each unequal fingertip is pulled toward its own nominal stance location in a stance-calibrated frame. Lateral and vertical departures receive four times the penalty of fore–aft departures. Auxiliary objectives encourage speed-dependent lifting and command-aligned swing motion without prescribing which finger steps when (Section IV-B; Fig. 3, pp. 3–4).

- **Hardware-calibrated sim-to-real training:** Separate feedforward policies are trained with PPO in Isaac Lab. Simulation parameters are derived from hardware measurements of stiffness, fingertip friction, delay, filtered joint speed, and command-filter cutoff, with further contact, actuator, payload, and sensor randomization (Sections III-C and IV-A; Tables II–III, p. 3).

- **Task-specific mobile manipulation:** Separate policies perform crawling and recovery, keyboard pressing based on proprioception and a requested-key indicator, and cube pushing using overhead visual observations (Table I, p. 3; Section V, pp. 4–5).

The evidence supports feasibility on one hand platform and shows a controlled simulation advantage for the footprint-based reward under the tested setup. It also documents several hardware capabilities.

It does not establish generalization to other hands, autonomous task planning, operation beyond the external camera workspace, or statistically characterized hardware superiority over alternative controllers. The 14-surface locomotion and 15 pushing trials are presented as capability demonstrations rather than controlled comparative evaluations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Keyboard operation depends on manual alignment and does not track hand translation relative to the keyboard; misalignment requires manual realignment (Section V-A, p. 5).

- **Author-stated:** Pushing depends on an overhead camera. The authors identify onboard perception and tracking as needed for operation beyond that camera’s workspace (Conclusion, p. 8).

- **Author-stated:** Left-turn rate plateaus, and 30° left heading commands fell short because drift compensation pushed commands beyond the trained limit (Section VI-B, pp. 6–7).

- **Author-stated:** Hardware recovery failures occurred when fingers caught on one another and the hand stalled (Section VI-C, p. 7).

- **Author-stated:** The ablations do not demonstrate independent speed benefits from the lift or direction objectives. The direction term was retained because deployed hardware policies had already been trained with it (Section VI-A(d), p. 6).

- **Analyst inference:** Baseline selection may favor tuned stock because six of its 12 reported seeds were also involved in selecting the configuration. The paper discloses this overlap, but no held-out 12-seed baseline evaluation is reported (Section VI-A(a), p. 5).

- **Analyst inference:** Hardware evidence is narrow: only the proposed formulation was deployed, surface tests were qualitative, and pushing used 15 demonstrations with one cube geometry and mass. This limits causal claims about reward design and robustness outside the tested conditions (Sections VI-A, VI-B, and VI-E, pp. 5–8).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is incomplete because the supplied text does not establish release of source code, trained models, simulator assets, or evaluation data.

## Open research questions

1. **Can one policy integrate locomotion, recovery, and manipulation?**  
   The paper trains separate task-specific policies. A next experiment could train a shared policy with task conditioning and compare transition reliability and success against the separate-policy system.

2. **How well does the method transfer across anthropomorphic hand designs?**  
   Evaluation uses one commercial right hand with fixed geometry and controller settings. A next experiment could train and deploy the stance-calibrated reward on hands with different finger proportions, masses, or actuator dynamics.

3. **Can onboard perception remove manual and external registration?**  
   Keyboard operation requires manual alignment, while pushing uses an overhead camera. A next experiment could add onboard hand, key, and object tracking and measure accuracy over workspace shifts and repeated autonomous realignment.

4. **Do the auxiliary lift and direction objectives improve hardware robustness?**  
   Simulation ablations did not establish their independent benefit, and only the full formulation was tested on hardware. A next experiment could deploy matched policies with these terms removed and compare speed, contact posture, falls, and terrain success.

- Performance over longer deployments, battery duration, wear, energy use, and repeated impacts.
