# VC-Attention: Value Smoothing and Softmax Casting for Low-bit Attention

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15810) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15810)


## One-sentence takeaway
VC-Attention combines value-token clustering with direct FP8 probability encoding to improve training-free video-attention fidelity, reporting 1.13–1.70× faster Wan2.2 clip generation than BF16 FlashAttention-4 across four GPUs (Table 2; Figure 6).

## Problem and motivation
Low-bit matrix multiplication does not automatically yield accurate, fast attention. The authors identify two remaining bottlenecks: value outliers inflate quantization scales, while high-precision softmax exponentiation and probability conversion limit throughput once matrix products become faster. Existing query/key smoothing leaves substantial value-side error in their video-diffusion measurements (§§1, 3.1).

The goal is to address both bottlenecks without model-specific retraining or changing the full attention pattern.

## Approach and contributions
- **V-Smooth:** Online k-means groups similar value tokens; keys and values are permuted together, preserving exact non-causal attention before quantization. Each hardware block’s mean is subtracted, only its residual is quantized, and the mean contribution is restored inside the online attention recurrence (§3.2).
- **ExpCast-FP8:** An affine map, rounding, and clipping produce E4M3 probability codes directly from log-domain scores, replacing elementwise exponentiation and FP8 conversion. The authors derive a normalized-row error bound, with an additional underflow-tail term (§3.3; Appendix D).
- **Integrated implementation:** CuTe/CUDA kernels fuse preprocessing, while clustering and demeaning operate during the first quarter of denoising steps, with permutations refreshed every four steps within that window. Datacenter configurations combine both methods at 8 bits; workstation configurations use V-Smooth at 4 bits (§3.4; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results
All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Value-guided grouping reduces quantization error | On 100 Wan2.2 heads, with block demeaning and per-channel FP8 values, reported rMSE falls from 1.81×10⁻⁴ in sequence order to 1.28×10⁻⁴ with k-means. Warm-started grouping costs 9.5% of one attention call on grouping steps. | Table 1 |
| V-Smooth improves 8-bit fidelity | Against same-seed BF16 outputs over 100 prompts per model, Wan2.2 PSNR is 22.6 dB versus SageAttention2’s 20.3; HunyuanVideo-1.5 is 18.4 versus 15.6. | Table 2; §4.1 |
| ExpCast introduces a measurable fidelity tradeoff | Wan2.2 PSNR drops from 22.6 to 20.5 dB when ExpCast is added. On MiniMax-H3, combined PSNR is 20.2 versus 19.9 for SageAttention2, but below 20.5 for FP8 FlashAttention-4 with QK Hadamard. | Table 2 |
| V-Smooth improves 4-bit fidelity | On RTX PRO 6000, LongCat-Video PSNR rises from SageAttention3’s 15.1 to 18.7 dB; LPIPS falls from 0.382 to 0.226, against the same BF16 reference. | Table 2 |
| Acceleration reaches clip generation | Wan2.2, 1280×720, 81 frames, 40 steps: attention/clip speedups over BF16 FlashAttention-4 are B200 **1.59×/1.19×**, H200 **1.46×/1.13×**, RTX PRO 6000 **2.27×/1.36×**, and RTX 5090 **3.58×/1.70×**. Attention timing excludes preprocessing; clip timing includes it. | Figure 6; Appendix A |
| Early grouping is a useful operating point | LongCat-Video, H200, 32-prompt subset: first-quarter grouping gives 26.4 dB PSNR and 351.0 s/clip, versus 23.8 dB and 353.2 s for uniformly distributed quarter-step grouping, and 26.9 dB and 367.0 s for all-step grouping. | Table 3 |
| Probability error is bounded conditionally | Proposition 3.1 bounds row total variation below 3.64% without underflow, or 0.0364+τ with tail mass τ. Across 204.8K Wan2.2 rows, measured mean TV is 1.6%, versus 1.1% for exponential-then-FP8 casting; maximum TV is 17.8%. | §3.3; Appendix D |

The evidence supports improved fidelity to BF16 and faster generation in the tested configurations. It does not establish better human-perceived video quality, universal superiority over low-bit alternatives, or equivalent gains outside these models and GPUs. The theoretical bound isolates probability approximation; residual-value quantization contributes separate error (Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** ExpCast does not support the deployed NVFP4 probability format; no 4-bit datacenter configuration is evaluated (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Comparison boundaries:** Attn-QAT is evaluated without its model-specific training. SageAttention2 lacks a Blackwell-specific kernel, so its B200 comparison uses a recompiled earlier kernel. These results do not compare against trained Attn-QAT or a native Blackwell SageAttention2 implementation (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited quality assessment:** Reference fidelity and two near-saturated VBench dimensions cannot establish broader perceptual or audiovisual quality. No human evaluation or audio-specific metric is reported (§4.1; Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—limited schedule validation:** The grouping-placement ablation covers one model and a prompt subset; broader optimality of the shared schedule remains untested (Table 3).
- **Internal reporting inconsistencies:** The claim that 8-bit V-Smooth “leads every column” is not supported by all VBench columns in Table 2. Appendix A restricts ExpCast to B200/H200, whereas §4.5 reports it on B300. Table entries and explicit settings should take precedence over broad prose claims.

## Open research questions
- **How transferable is the grouping schedule?** Only LongCat-Video receives the placement ablation. Sweep grouping windows and refresh intervals across all evaluated models, measuring fidelity and total clip latency.
- **Can underflow-related approximation error be reduced efficiently?** The empirical maximum row error exceeds the no-underflow bound. Test a tail-aware fallback and measure row-error distributions, video fidelity, and kernel latency.
- **Do fidelity gains improve perceived generation quality?** Current evaluation emphasizes matching BF16, and VBench dimensions are near-saturated. Run blinded paired video assessments and add audio-quality and synchronization evaluation for MiniMax-H3.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed comparative results and timing protocols, but broad claims require qualification.

Material missing information includes a public code/release location, exact clustering hyperparameters and initialization details, statistical uncertainty for aggregate results, human-quality evaluation, and audio-specific evaluation. The text also does not fully clarify how ExpCast probabilities interact with the row-sum mean-restoration path described using exact exponentials in Equation (6).
