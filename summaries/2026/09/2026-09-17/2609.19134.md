# ScienceIDE: Turning World&#x27;s Scientific Codebase into Agent Learnable Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19134) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19134)


## One-sentence takeaway

ScienceIDE packages expert-defined scientific software contracts into reusable, verifier-backed environments for agent evaluation, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning, with reported gains on held-out repairs and selected general benchmarks but limited evidence for transfer beyond existing codebases.

## Problem and motivation

Scientific repositories contain executable domain knowledge, but heterogeneous dependencies, implicit numerical conventions, and domain-specific correctness criteria make them difficult to convert into reliable agent training experience. The authors call this the **scientific experience bottleneck**: runnable code alone does not provide meaningful tasks, calibrated rewards, reproducible execution, or recorded interactions suitable for learning (§1, pp. 3–4).

Existing scientific coding and reproducibility benchmarks mainly evaluate agents. The identified gap is infrastructure that repeatedly produces validated tasks and learning signals from expert-approved scientific behavior across repositories (§1; §4.2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Reusable scientific environments.** Each environment packages a pinned repository module, runtime, official tests, expert-calibrated scientific checks, editable workspace, and private verifier. Checks use pointwise tolerances or scientific invariants, with nominal and perturbed cases supporting calibration (§2.1–2.2; Figures 2–3).

2. **Task manufacturing with executable validation.** Environment-specific factories turn proposed mutations, missing implementations, or other objectives into candidates. Candidates are admitted only when executable evidence demonstrates an attainable objective, observable behavioral contrast, and valid grading (§2.3–2.4; Figures 4–5).

3. **A shared learning interface.** Agent episodes record tool interactions, artifacts, verifier rewards, execution status, and resource use. The same environments can support fixed-budget evaluation, trajectory-based SFT, and online RL (§2.5; Figure 6).

4. **ScienceIDE assets and evaluation.** The reported registry contains 64 environments derived from 27 codebases, 2,812 tasks, and 1,076 executable checks. ScienceIDE-Hard provides 85 repair and implementation tasks from 18 environments (§3.1; Table S1).

5. **Learning studies.** The authors train three SFT models from verifier-selected trajectories and conduct online verifier-guided RL with Qwen3.5-4B. Their RL method masks budget-truncated trajectories from the policy loss while retaining their rewards in the group baseline (§3.3–3.4; Eq. S3).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that executable scientific checks can produce useful evaluation and learning signals. It also shows gains from selected verified trajectories and online rewards in the evaluated settings.

It does not establish consistent improvement across benchmarks, transfer to unseen repositories, superiority of a specific model among the overlapping leading results, or correctness beyond the observables encoded by each verifier.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Evaluation concerns reference-verifiable software tasks, mostly repair and implementation in computational physics and geoscience; it does not test open-ended discovery (§5).
- Factory variants may share code paths and assumptions, so the 2,812-task count is not a count of independent scientific problems (§5).
- ScienceIDE-Hard covers only one-hour horizons and at most two coupled edit sites (§5).
- Passing selected observables within calibrated tolerances does not prove correctness outside the checks; legitimate implementations may differ from the reference (§5).
- External audits and adversarial reward-hacking studies remain outstanding (§5).
- Results compare complete model–harness systems, with unequal repeat coverage and serving-speed differences. Fable lacks repeated measurements (§3.2.1; §5).
- Retrieval controls cannot exclude pretrained exposure to public code. Historical audits found successful fetches and provider-side retrieval channels, which were subsequently corrected for affected trials (§5; Appendices S3.1–S3.2).
- SFT split separation uses task identifiers and does not independently audit related variants. RL evaluates hinted tasks held out within the same environments and lacks between-seed variance estimates (§5; Appendix S5).
- Repository, dependency, or hardware changes require revalidation; source versioning alone does not preserve scientific validity (§5).

**Analyst inference:** The SFT evidence is partly exploratory because 33 configurations were screened, reported intervals lack multiple-comparison correction, and at least one screened gain failed confirmation. The supplied results therefore support benchmark-specific effects more strongly than broad positive transfer.

**Analyst inference:** The RL truncation mask is supported by one unmasked collapse comparison and two masked runs, but the absence of multiple random seeds prevents separating the method’s effect from run variability.

## Open research questions

1. **Does training transfer to entirely unseen scientific repositories?**  
   This remains open because SFT uses held-out tasks within existing codebases and RL uses held-out tasks within training environments. A next experiment could hold out complete repositories and domain families during both trajectory collection and training.

2. **How robust are scientific verifiers to reward hacking and valid alternative solutions?**  
   The authors explicitly leave adversarial audits outstanding, and reference agreement may reject legitimate alternatives. A next experiment could commission independent domain experts to evaluate adversarial patches and scientifically valid non-reference implementations.

3. **Does masking truncated trajectories reliably improve long-horizon RL?**  
   The reported comparison lacks between-seed variance. A next experiment could run matched masked and unmasked training across multiple seeds, environments, and budget limits while tracking reward, completion, and trajectory length.

4. **Can the framework support open-ended discovery without fixed reference outputs?**  
   Current evidence is dominated by repair and implementation under predetermined checks. A next experiment could evaluate hypothesis-generation tasks using preregistered invariant, predictive, or experimental validation criteria.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported design, experimental settings, numerical results, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and relevant supplementary sections.

Material unavailable from the supplied text includes the released registry and task files, executable containers, private verifier implementations, full per-task results, machine-readable trajectories, independent audit results, and the source artifacts referenced for failure analysis. Consequently, reproducibility and verifier correctness cannot be independently assessed from the text alone.
