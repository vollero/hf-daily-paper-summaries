# EvolveTrade: Experience-Driven Policy Refinement for Self-Evolving LLM Trading Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17632) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17632)


## One-sentence takeaway

EvolveTrade periodically rewrites a frozen LLM trading agent’s tool-use policy from decision traces and realized returns, improving Sharpe ratio and cumulative return over fixed-policy LLM baselines in three of six tested model-regime settings while underperforming them in others.

## Problem and motivation

Tool-enabled LLM trading agents generally follow prompts and workflows fixed before deployment, even though markets are non-stationary and effective evidence gathering, validation, and risk control may vary by regime. The authors identify the static operational policy as the gap: richer context and tool access do not let an agent learn how to use those resources from realized trading outcomes (Sections 1–4).

Under a fixed GPT-5-mini policy, the same core metrics appeared on 90–100% of trading days across sideways, drawdown, and uptrend months, while EMA, RSI, VaR, and signal normalization were unused. News queries and price lookbacks were similarly repetitive (Figure 2; Section 4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Text-parameterized online policy refinement.** EvolveTrade treats the system prompt governing evidence collection, tool calls, risk control, and output construction as an editable policy while keeping the backbone LLM and tools fixed (Sections 3 and 5).

2. **Experience-based update records.** Each record combines the submitted allocation and per-asset rationale with subsequent portfolio and asset returns. After every \(N\)-day batch, a separate Policy Agent rewrites the complete policy using the accumulated records (Equations 2–5; Sections 5.1–5.2).

3. **Controlled comparison of adaptation targets.** Experiments compare static observation and tool-calling agents with tool-free policy evolution, strategy-only evolution, and full operational-policy evolution, helping distinguish access to tools from adaptation of their use (Section 6.1).

4. **Behavioral and case-level analysis.** The paper measures changes in tool-call frequency and analytic vocabulary, then traces selected allocation changes to realized return differences (Figures 3–5 and 7).

The evidence supports the claim that online prompt refinement can alter tool use and sometimes improve simulated risk-adjusted performance. It does not establish consistent superiority: EvolveTrade loses materially in both April windows, and even its best Gemini November result remains negative. The case studies demonstrate plausible mechanisms for selected days, but they do not establish that the identified policy edits causally explain aggregate performance across all days.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The update interval remains fixed within each run; dynamically scheduling updates as markets change is left open (Limitations, page 9).
- **Author-stated:** The transaction-cost analysis uses proportional costs and omits slippage, market impact, and liquidity constraints (Limitations; Appendix D).
- **Author-stated:** Elevated cash allocations may explain underperformance in April 2025 and April 2026, where EvolveTrade held 10.1 and 35.9 percentage points more cash than the selected baseline (Appendix F; Table 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Six short market windows and one 50-day extension provide limited evidence about long-horizon robustness, diverse asset universes, and unseen regimes.
- **Analyst inference:** Each LLM condition has only three runs. Some differences have substantial dispersion—for example, GPT-5-mini September SR is \(8.43\pm1.68\)—and no statistical significance tests are reported (Tables 4–5).
- **Analyst inference:** Behavioral evidence is strongest for GPT-5-mini in three 2025 regimes; comparable tool-use analysis for Gemini is not supplied.
- **Analyst inference:** The evolved prompts become lengthy and highly specific (Figures 12–16). Figure 6 tracks length, but the paper does not isolate whether gains come from particular rules, added computation, or policy complexity.
- **Analyst inference:** The update prompt says execution and evaluation occur at the same close, while the main setup says allocation is evaluated over the next holding interval. The supplied text does not fully resolve this timing description (Section 3; Figure 9).

## Open research questions

1. **Can update timing adapt safely to regime changes?** The paper fixes the interval within each run. A next experiment could compare a change-point-triggered scheduler with fixed 1-, 3-, 5-, and 7-day updates on identical market windows.

2. **Which policy edits cause the performance gains?** Tool-use frequency and selected cases are suggestive, but individual additions such as VaR, normalization, sizing rules, and longer lookbacks are not separately ablated. A factorial intervention could enable each edit independently while holding the policy length and tool budget constant.

3. **Does the method remain effective under realistic execution?** The reported friction model omits slippage, liquidity, and market impact. A next experiment could replay allocations with volume-dependent costs, delayed fills, and position-size constraints.

4. **How robust is policy evolution over longer and broader evaluations?** Only one 50-day extension and a 15-stock universe are reported. A pre-registered multi-year study across larger universes and more backbones could measure performance decay, prompt growth, and regime-specific failure rates.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported results because the supplied text includes the full main tables, appendices, prompts, and metric definitions.

Material missing or unresolved information includes statistical significance tests, complete uncertainty for metrics beyond SR and CR, repeated-run details for the 50-day study, exact regime-labeling criteria, realistic execution modeling, public code or artifact availability, comparable behavioral analysis for Gemini, and a clear reconciliation of the stated execution/evaluation timing.
