# Agora: Git as Shared Memory for Collective AutoResearch

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18094) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18094)


## One-sentence takeaway

Agora records autonomous agents’ research as a Git backed contribution graph with provenance, verification, and diversity aware frontier views; in one uncontrolled 13-worker run, agents improved a frozen language model initialization from 3.3923 to 1.899044 bits per byte, though the study does not establish that Agora caused the improvement.

## Problem and motivation

Autonomous research agents usually retain discoveries, failures, and experiment context only within individual sessions. With multiple independently scheduled workers, this can cause duplicated search, premature convergence, and repeated reconstruction of prior work. Existing multi-agent systems primarily coordinate agents within a single workflow or conversation, whereas the authors seek durable community state that persists across workers and exposes lineage, negative results, open branches, and independent verification (Sections 1–3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Contribution DAG:** Every result, hypothesis, insight, verification, or report becomes an append-only Git commit. Parent edges represent “builds on” relationships, while commits carry artifacts, authorship, tags, metadata, optional metrics, and timestamps (Section 3.2, Equation 1).
- **Evidence model:** A node’s evidence score counts weighted direct follow-on contributions from other accounts, excluding self-citations and non-evidentiary endorsements or work in progress. Verification verdicts remain historically visible but their newest version determines the score (Section 3.2, Equation 2; Table 2).
- **Diversity aware attention:** Derived views expose leaders, leaves, unverified claims, hypotheses, clusters, and neglected branches. A heuristic upper-confidence score populates separate exploit, known-exploration, and novel-exploration slots (Section 3.3, Equation 3).
- **Working prototype:** The implementation combines canonical Git repositories with a rebuildable SQLite contribution index, a Go service, CLI, HTTP API, and Next.js interface (Section 3.4; Figure 1).
- **Sustained case study:** Thirteen language-model workers used the system for nearly 12 days on a frozen-model weight-transfer task, generating 1,703 contributions and a donor-behavior initialization method (Sections 4.1–4.5).
- **Evaluation proposal:** Because the case study lacks a control condition, the authors specify a future matched comparison among isolated workers, a flat log, a central planner, and Agora (Appendix C, Table 5).

The evidence establishes that this community produced a large, auditable research trace and found an effective initialization under the paper’s specific evaluator and constraints. The authors also checked the trace, code lineage, evaluator outputs, and absence of gradient updates (Section 4.8).

It does not establish that Agora outperforms isolated agents, a flat contribution log, or centralized planning. All method choices were selected on the same 200-text development evaluator, so the final decimals and apparent contributions of individual edits are not reliable held-out estimates or controlled ablations.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated causal limitation:** There was no matched run without Agora or with only a plain leaderboard. The coordination system’s causal effect on discovery or compute efficiency is therefore unknown (Section 5).
- **Author-stated human influence:** Researchers defined the task, evaluator, donor zoo, and target, then introduced clustering and diversity views during the run. The community escaped its initial basin only after that intervention (Sections 4.7 and 5).
- **Author-stated selection bias:** Every component of the final recipe was retained based on the same development evaluator. Table 4 is a search trajectory rather than a controlled ablation, and cross-hardware variation exceeds the last improvement (Sections 4.5; Table 4).
- **Author-stated verification boundary:** The authors inspected artifacts and corroborating agent reproductions but did not themselves rerun the winning method (Section 4.8).
- **Analyst inference — narrow external validity:** Evidence comes from one synthetic weight-transfer task, one target architecture, one evaluator, and frontier coding agents. The text does not show that the system generalizes to other scientific tasks, longer projects, or human-agent communities.
- **Analyst inference — incomplete efficiency evidence:** The paper reports duration, hardware, evaluation times, and contribution counts, but no aggregate compute consumption or discovery-per-compute comparison. It cannot support a systems-efficiency advantage.
- **Analyst inference — verification independence:** Verifiers used different accounts and sometimes different GPU types, but all operated inside the same project and evaluator. The supplied text does not establish external replication or held-out evaluation.
- **Analyst inference — implementation reproducibility:** Appendix A specifies what a retained run should include, but the supplied text does not establish public availability of the code, full trace, containers, donor artifacts, or frozen manifests.

## Open research questions

1. **Does Agora improve discovery per unit of compute?**  
   This remains open because the study has no control arm. Run the preregistered four-arm experiment from Appendix C with matched agents, models, compute, evaluator, and wall-clock budgets, measuring held-out best score, unique discoveries, duplication, and total compute.

2. **Which component produces any coordination benefit?**  
   Git provenance, verification scores, clustering, and exploit/explore recommendations were deployed together or observed within one run. Factorially ablate lineage visibility, evidence scoring, clustering, and UCB recommendations across repeated community runs.

3. **Does diversity guidance reliably prevent monoculture?**  
   The sole reported escape followed one human-triggered deployment, so it may be task-specific or coincidental. Randomize when communities receive diversity views and compare cluster concentration, branch survival, novel-method yield, and held-out performance.

4. **Does the transferred initialization generalize beyond the development evaluator?**  
   All edits were selected on 200 FineWeb-Edu texts. Freeze several candidate commits, then evaluate them on unseen text samples, alternate token distributions, and multiple mismatched target architectures without further tuning.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for summarizing the reported design, trace, and development-evaluator results; **low** for causal claims about Agora’s effectiveness.

Material missing information includes public artifact locations and licenses; full compute and cost accounting; repeated runs or statistical uncertainty across communities; held-out evaluation of the winning initialization; controlled component ablations; the complete Appendix C protocol beyond the arm matrix; agent sampling and tool-policy details; and an independent rerun by the paper’s authors or an external group.
