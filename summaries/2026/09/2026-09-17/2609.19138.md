# In-Context Robot Learning with VLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19138) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19138)


## One-sentence takeaway
GPT-Policy shows that test-time demonstrations can improve a fixed VLM’s success on selected real-robot tasks, but small trial counts, incomplete ablations, and observed collisions limit claims about generalization and reliable autonomy (Table 1; §5).

## Problem and motivation
Robots must adapt to unfamiliar tasks and scenes beyond their training coverage. The authors ask whether general-purpose VLMs can use demonstrations, goal images, and interaction history for this adaptation without robot-specific training or parameter updates. The key gap is understanding which capabilities context supplies—and which failures persist when task interpretation becomes physical action (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared context-to-action framework:** GPT-Policy combines instructions, current camera observations and robot state, task references, and interaction history. A fixed VLM issues structured robot-tool requests; embodiment-specific adapters execute or reject them and return feedback for replanning (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Heterogeneous context representation:** Chronological demonstration keyframes can include aligned measured states and recorded actions. Goal images specify outcomes, while interaction history retains observations, actions, results, and human feedback. Recorded actions inform reasoning rather than direct trajectory replay (§3.2; Appendix C).
- **Empirical study of context benefits and execution failures:** Real-robot experiments compare demonstration conditions and illustrate goal-image following, exploration, and human interaction. The contribution is the framework and evidence about general-purpose agents; the supplied results do not establish broad superiority over dedicated robot policies (§§4–5).

The execution harness interpolates Cartesian targets, solves inverse kinematics, and applies backend-specific trajectory checks. These checks do not establish collision-free motion or physical task success (§3.3; Appendices A–B).

## Evidence and main results
All Table 1 results use GPT-6 Astra on real robots. Decisions and time are averages over **all trials, including failures**.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Human video improves towel pickup | None → Human Video: success **0/3 → 2/3**; decisions **96.3 → 76.7**; time **24.6 → 18.9 min**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Human video improves notebook pickup | None → Human Video: success **0/3 → 2/3**; decisions **94.0 → 66.7**; time **24.6 → 16.1 min**. Human videos contain no robot action labels. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Action-augmented context gives highest observed bottle-opening success | None / Robot Video / Video + Action: success **0/3 / 2/3 / 3/3**; decisions **71.0 / 74.3 / 54.7**; time **16.1 / 15.2 / 17.9 min**. Success requires the opened bottle to remain standing securely. | Table 1; §4.3 |
| Plug success appears only with action-augmented context | Same condition order: success **0/3 / 0/3 / 2/3**; decisions **24.0 / 33.7 / 48.3**; time **5.3 / 7.9 / 10.8 min**. The reinserted plug must remain fully seated after release. | Table 1; §4.3 |
| Other contexts support successful demonstrations | **3/3 success per task** for T-shape and fruit arrangement with target images; lemon placement and mobile exploration with self-history; tic-tac-toe and pointed-fruit pickup with human interaction. Tic-tac-toe wins and draws both count as success. Quantitative context-free controls are absent for these tasks. | Table 1; §§4.4–4.6 |
| Model comparisons remain illustrative | Individual red-towel runs report task progress of **55% without video and 100% with video** for GPT-6 Astra, versus **30% for Fable 5.1** and **20% for Kimi K3**. Progress is not success rate; authors explicitly reject a reliable ranking from these examples. | §5; Table 2 caption and surrounding prose |
| Physical safeguards are insufficient | Authors repeatedly observed inter-arm collisions; the Cartesian planner does not check collisions. | §5; Appendix A.3 |

The evidence supports context-sensitive changes in behavior and improved observed success for the matched demonstration comparisons. It does not establish robust success probabilities, unseen-skill acquisition, or safe autonomous operation. Action references do not consistently reduce execution costs, and failure-inclusive averages complicate efficiency comparisons (§§4.3, 5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Small task series, selected platforms and models, incomplete ablations, and unknown pretraining limit causal and novel-skill claims. Context quality, alignment, latency, human intervention, contact execution, and outcome verification remain constraints (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Without quantitative no-context controls for goal images, self-history, and human interaction, their incremental benefit cannot be isolated from the reported successes (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** Video + Action adds measured states as well as action records, so the comparison does not isolate action commands alone. The proposed benefit of denser temporal information remains a hypothesis (§4.3; Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Provider-dependent history handling, geometric inputs, rejection rules, and completion review can complicate model comparisons unless matched explicitly (Appendix B).
- **Reporting gap:** Several tables and prompt listings appear only as captions or descriptions in the supplied retrieval, limiting reproducibility assessment (Tables 2–7; Appendix D).

## Open research questions
1. **Which reference information causes the contact-task gains?** States, commands, and temporal density change together. A next experiment could vary these separately while fixing images and non-action annotations (§4.3; Appendix C).
2. **Do goal images and history improve success across scene variations?** Current results lack quantitative context-removal controls. Test matched conditions across randomized layouts with more repeated trials (§§4.4–4.6).
3. **Can improved context use coexist with reliable physical safety?** Collisions persist despite execution checks. Compare the current harness with joint collision checking and contact-aware recovery, measuring success, collisions, near misses, and interventions (§5).
4. **Can the agent compose demonstrations into unfamiliar task sequences?** The authors identify composition as future work. Supply separate subskill demonstrations and evaluate new orderings that require retaining completed subgoals (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the reported task outcomes and framework description, but broader conclusions remain weakly established.

Material missing information includes full Tables 2–7 and prompt listings, per-trial variability, detailed scene randomization and execution budgets, intervention and collision counts, exact per-run configurations, quantitative controls for several context families, and confirmed code/data availability.
