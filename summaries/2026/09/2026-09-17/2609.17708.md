# Confidence Comes from Experience: Experiential Confidence Estimation from Reasoning to Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17708) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17708)


## One-sentence takeaway
XConf combines retrieved outcomes from a model’s past attempts with renewed self-assessment, reporting competitive confidence ranking against ten-sample self-consistency while requiring only one answer generation plus a recalibration call.

## Problem and motivation
Confidence estimates determine which model outputs to accept, retry, or escalate. The authors argue that inspecting or resampling the current answer misses useful evidence: how often the same model actually succeeded on similar tasks despite feeling similarly confident. Their target is correctness estimation after an answer or agent episode is completed, rather than improving the answer itself (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Experience-based estimation:** Store tasks, pre-grading reflections, stated confidence, verified outcomes, and post-grading lessons. Current outcomes are withheld during estimation (§3.2).
- **Recall:** Retrieve similar graded episodes and aggregate their outcomes. The implemented version uses embeddings, stated confidence, reflection and length features, PCA, a correctness-supervised logistic reweighting, and confidence-weighted outcomes from 50 neighbors. The simpler task-plus-confidence formulation is separately evaluated (§3.3; §§E, K).
- **Reflect and combine:** Show historical episode cards to the actor, ask it to identify a recurring failure pattern, and elicit revised confidence; average this with Recall. The implementation shows eight cards (§3.4; §§M–N).
- **Empirical contribution:** Evaluate across reasoning, coding, multimodal QA, and agents, with analyses of bank size, transfer, label quality, temporal evaluation, and model size. “Training-free” means no LLM weight updates; the retrieval geometry still uses a fitted supervised probe (§§4–6, E).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad ranking advantage over sampling | Authors report 21 wins, 2 ties, 1 loss in AUROC against SC@10 across six non-agent datasets and four models. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| Strong coding result | LiveCodeBench, Gemini 2.5 Flash: XConf AUROC **0.887**, ECE **0.062**, versus code-similarity SC@10 **0.777**, **0.374**; XConf AUROC 95% CI **[0.867, 0.907]**. | Tables 3, 5 |
| Agent gains can be substantial | AppWorld, Qwen3.5-397B: XConf AUROC **0.837**, ECE **0.116**, versus verbalized **0.583**, **0.397**, and same-actor judge **0.665**, **0.368**. | Table 4 |
| Abstention improves retained-set accuracy | Removing the lowest-confidence **10%** raises accuracy by a reported **4.8 percentage points** averaged across 36 cells; AppWorld/Gemini 3.5 Flash rises **0.805 → 0.892**. | §5.5; Fig. 6 |
| Stated confidence is a major retrieval input | Removing confidence from the key and kernel reduces Recall’s mean AUROC **0.822 → 0.741** on six non-agent datasets and **0.852 → 0.728** on three agent domains, each across four models. | Table 22 |
| Reflect is not uniformly beneficial | ScienceWorld/Gemini 2.5 Flash: Recall alone achieves AUROC/ECE **0.960/0.010**, versus the blend’s **0.939/0.071**. | Table 21 |
| Temporal evidence supports Recall | LiveCodeBench walk-forward evaluation after 150 warm-up episodes loses **0.006–0.020 AUROC** versus matched random-fold evaluation across four models. | §H; Table 24 |

These results support useful correctness ranking and selective filtering under the tested protocols. They do not demonstrate improved underlying task-solving accuracy or actual retry/escalation outcomes. XConf consumes historical labels that label-free SC@10 does not; labeled baselines are also included. The generation-count advantage excludes a full accounting of historical grading and lesson creation, and Reflect remains an additional LLM call (§§4, L).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundaries:** Voting remains stronger on some factual-recall tasks; self-generated outcome labels can reinforce confident errors; bank transfer degrades when models or domains have different failure patterns (§§6–7, C, I).
- **Author-stated design limitation:** Equal weighting can underperform Recall alone, and Reflect weakens for smaller actors. Combining calibration memory with an evolving actor is untested (§7; §§D, J).
- **Analyst inference—temporal scope:** Random out-of-sample folds prevent direct item leakage but do not establish chronological causality. Strict temporal evidence covers Recall on LiveCodeBench, narrower than the general history-only language (§§H, K).
- **Analyst observation—reporting inconsistencies:** The claim of universally lower ECE than SC@10 has an exception: Qwen OlympiadBench is **0.038 versus 0.036** (Table 3). Appendix A claims best Brier scores in **32/36** cells, but Table 7 appears to show **30/36** against its displayed comparators.
- **Analyst observation—implementation ambiguity:** The main method describes a minimal key and plain mean, whereas the frozen implementation adds features and weighting. Reflect descriptions also differ on card count; the actual prompt and worked example specify eight (§3; §§E, G, M–N).

## Open research questions
- **How should stale experience be forgotten as an actor improves?** Actor evolution is explicitly untested (§7). Evaluate successive actor versions with age-weighted, version-specific, and pooled banks on future tasks.
- **When should Reflect be skipped or down-weighted?** Recall sometimes performs better, particularly on ScienceWorld and with smaller actors (§§D, J). Compare fixed averaging with held-out selection of blend weights, including latency and calibration.
- **How robust is the complete pipeline to deployment drift and imperfect feedback?** Temporal testing covers Recall on one dataset, while independent weak-grader evidence is limited (§§C, H). Run chronological full-pipeline evaluations with controlled grading errors and delayed feedback.
