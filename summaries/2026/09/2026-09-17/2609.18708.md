# Rethinking Critic Learning in PPO: Understanding and Mitigating Value Flattening

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18708) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18708)


## One-sentence takeaway
Sparse critic supervision improves average reasoning accuracy over PPO and GRPO on two Qwen3 model sizes while reducing the mismatch between critic predictions and policy-conditioned value changes within responses.

## Problem and motivation
PPO relies on critic values to construct token-level advantages, but the authors observe **Value Flattening**: critic predictions remain nearly constant even when intermediate states have sharply different estimated probabilities of success. They diagnose this using repeated Monte Carlo (MC) continuations from fixed states (§§3.2–4.1).

The identified gap is that response-level outcome discrimination does not establish whether a critic captures useful value changes *within* a response. Such errors can affect finite-batch actor updates, although they do not invalidate expected policy-gradient invariance to action-independent baselines (Appendix A.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnosis:** Compare critic predictions with policy-conditioned MC values across training checkpoints, and examine flattening as the state space grows in stochastic FrozenLake (§4.1).
- **Mechanistic analysis:** Decompose the shared-terminal-target squared loss into response-mean error and within-response prediction variance. Measure strong alignment among neighboring critic representations and gradients, supporting redundant updates as another contributing factor (§4.2; Appendix A.2).
- **SP3O:** Apply critic loss only at well-separated response positions while retaining predictions at every state and preserving the actor objective, rollout procedure, and return targets. The main configuration uses positions 0.3, 0.6, and 0.9, plus 0.95 for responses of at least 6,144 tokens—so supervision is not always limited to three states (§§4.3, 5.1).

The results support improved **suite averages**, not universal gains on every benchmark: for example, 8B SP3O trails both baselines on Minerva (Table 1). Critic diagnostics and policy gains are consistent with the proposed explanation, but do not separately establish the causal contribution of variance suppression and gradient redundancy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified theory:** The actor-gradient decomposition concerns finite batches at the rollout policy; its norm bounds do not prove directional alignment or identical behavior across subsequent clipped PPO updates (Appendix A.2).
- **Scope:** Experiments cover two sizes within one model family, one mathematical training dataset, terminal binary rewards, zero KL, and γ=λ=1. Broader applicability remains untested (§§3.1, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference—causal isolation:** Sparse supervision changes both selected states and response weighting: dense loss is normalized over tokens, whereas sparse loss is normalized over selected anchors. No ablation isolates this weighting change from the proposed mechanisms (Equations 3 and 6).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic limits:** Illustrative trajectories are selected for large MC changes, and the fixed diagnostic panel contains 64 responses. MC estimates use finite sampling, while PPO and SP3O profiles use their respective policies rather than a common fixed actor (Figures 1, 4; Appendix A.1).
- **Reporting limits:** The authors acknowledge non-negligible variation in the density ablation, but main-result uncertainty, training-seed counts, and the meaning of its variation bands are not supplied. SP3O also generates longer responses; matched-compute comparisons are absent (§§5.2–5.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does the benefit persist with different reward structures?** The shared-target derivation assumes terminal rewards and γ=λ=1. Next experiment: vary λ, KL shaping, and intermediate rewards while measuring centered value error and policy accuracy.
- **Which mechanism drives the improvement?** Existing interventions jointly change spacing, density, and weighting. Next experiment: compare dense, random, and spaced supervision under matched response weights and controlled gradient scaling.
- **Do gains survive compute matching and broader replication?** SP3O produces longer responses, and main-table uncertainty is unavailable. Next experiment: replicate across seeds and another model family with matched generated-token budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported average improvements; causal attribution and generalization are less established.

Material missing information includes main-result uncertainty and seed counts, compute costs, the repetition metric definition, complete FrozenLake implementation and reference-value computation details, and verified repository contents.
