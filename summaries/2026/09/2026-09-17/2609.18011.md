# Gaze as Evidence for Common Grounding: A Cross-Corpus Analysis of MapTask and MUNDEX

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-17.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18011) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18011)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across two asymmetric collaborative tasks, grounded states correlate with more task-directed and less partner-directed gaze, but effects and predictive gains are small and sensitive to participant clustering and evaluation splits.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether video-coded gaze provides comparable evidence of conversational grounding across tasks with asymmetric information. Existing corpora use different gaze ontologies and grounding labels, which hinders cross-corpus analysis. The authors therefore seek a shared representation that can reveal recurring gaze patterns while preserving differences between referential alignment in MapTask and judged understanding in MUNDEX (Sections 1 and 6).

## Approach and contributions

- **Shared representation:** Maps each corpus’s discrete gaze labels into `partner`, `task`, and `away`, then computes proportions, entropy, transitions, temporal dynamics, transition bigrams, coordination measures, and derived ratios (Sections 3–4; Appendix B).
- **Cross-corpus association analysis:** Tests aligned versus non-aligned MapTask references and UND versus non-UND MUNDEX judgments using Mann–Whitney tests, rank-biserial correlations, BH correction, and cluster-robust logistic GEE (Section 4).
- **Process analysis:** Compares gaze at the last non-aligned mention with the first later aligned mention of the same landmark, restricted primarily to same-speaker pairs (Section 5; Figure 2).
- **Grouped prediction study:** Evaluates balanced logistic regression under held-out-dialogue or held-out-explainer cross-validation and ablates gaze feature groups (Section 4; Table 2).
- **Reusable artifacts:** Releases processed gaze-feature tables and analysis code; source annotations are publicly available, although MUNDEX audio and video are not released (Data and Code Availability).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Positive grounding states show more task gaze and less partner gaze. | MapTask speaker task gaze: \(r=.058\); partner gaze: \(r=-.057\). MUNDEX explainer task gaze: \(r=.181\); partner gaze: \(r=-.172\). All four window-level Mann–Whitney tests have \(p<.001\). | Table 1; Tables 5–6 |
| Cluster-aware results are weaker, especially in MapTask. | Dialogue-clustered MapTask GEE retains speaker entropy and transitions; participant-group clustering leaves no MapTask feature significant after correction. Corrected explainer-cluster MUNDEX analysis retains explainer task/partner gaze and explainee entropy/transitions. | Section 5; Section 8; Appendix E |
| Associations concentrate in task-leading roles. | Giver-produced MapTask references have six significant features and maximum \(|r|=.086\), while follower-produced references have no corrected significant feature and maximum \(|r|=.043\). MUNDEX effects survive correction only for explainer judgments in the role-stratified analysis. | Section 5; Tables 8–9 |
| Speaker entropy falls at a resolving mention. | Across 189 same-speaker pairs from 45 dialogues, entropy changes from .355 to .263, \(d_z=-.20\), \(q=.044\), with dialogue-cluster bootstrap 95% CI \([-.34,-.07]\). Dialogue-level aggregation gives \(q=.20\). | Figure 2; Table 14; Appendix F |
| The best gaze groups yield modest prediction gains. | MapTask structured+temporal LR reaches macro-F1 .532 versus .472 for controls; MUNDEX raw proportions reach .564 versus .544. Evaluation uses 10-fold dialogue-grouped and 5-fold held-out-explainer CV, respectively. | Table 2; Tables 16–17 |
| Prediction gains depend on the partition. | Across 30 reshuffled grouped partitions, MapTask’s best-group gain ranges .015–.070, mean .038; MUNDEX’s mean gain is .017, is non-positive once, and never exceeds .027. | Section 5; Appendix G |

The evidence supports directionally similar associations between gaze and two related grounding measures. It also shows that some gaze signal is recoverable by simple classifiers under grouped evaluation.

It does not establish that gaze causes grounding, that the shared categories have identical conversational functions across tasks, or that the patterns generalize beyond these corpora. Several findings weaken when recurring participants or dialogues become the inference unit.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- The three shared categories cannot identify the particular landmark or object being viewed, and matching category names do not guarantee matching interactional functions (Section 8).
- MapTask’s `up → partner` interpretation is literal only in the eye-contact condition; the formal condition interaction is not significant (Sections 3 and 8).
- MUNDEX combines retrospective self-reports with partner judgments. Of 135 fully retained linked pairs, 44 disagree on the binary label (Section 8; Appendix A.3).
- The samples are limited to 46 MapTask dialogues and 26 MUNDEX interactions with nine explainers; participants recur across observations (Section 8).
- Coverage filtering excludes 17 MapTask and 149 MUNDEX windows, with two explainers contributing 139 of the MUNDEX exclusions (Sections 3 and 8).
- Effects are small, chain results depend on the inference unit, and prediction gains vary across cross-validation partitions (Sections 5 and 8).
- Only two asymmetric face-to-face tasks in English and German are studied; other task structures and multimodal predictors remain untested (Section 8).
- MUNDEX audio and video are unavailable, although annotations, processed tables, and analysis code are released (Data and Code Availability).

**Analyst inferences**

- The observational design cannot distinguish gaze as evidence of grounding from gaze responding to task difficulty, dialogue actions, or mention position. The same-position analysis partly addresses the latter, but only second-mention task gaze survives correction (Table 15).
- The prediction comparison does not establish added value over strong linguistic or task-state models because the reported controls contain only roles and, for MapTask, eye-contact condition (Section 4; Table 2).
- Role differences are descriptive rather than confirmed moderation effects: feature-by-role interactions do not survive correction (Section 6; Appendix D).

## Open research questions

1. **Does gaze add signal beyond language, dialogue acts, and task state?**  
   The classifiers compare gaze against limited role and condition controls. A next experiment could evaluate gaze, linguistic, dialogue-act, and task-state models individually and jointly under the same participant-grouped splits.

2. **Which objects or dialogue functions explain the shared directional pattern?**  
   The three-category mapping cannot distinguish landmarks or determine why a participant looks at the task or partner. Object-level gaze annotation combined with dialogue-act labels could test whether the same functions recur across corpora.

3. **Do the effects generalize to new participants and task structures?**  
   MapTask results disappear under participant-group inference, and only nine MUNDEX explainers are available. A preregistered study could collect more independent participants across symmetric and asymmetric tasks and evaluate fully held-out participants.

4. **Is reduced gaze entropy a within-person marker of resolution?**  
   The chain result is significant across pairs but not after dialogue-level aggregation and is concentrated in two participant groups. A larger repeated-reference study could estimate participant-specific changes around independently annotated resolution points.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported methods, sample sizes, and numerical results because the supplied text includes the main paper and detailed appendices.

Material unavailable or unresolved in the supplied text:

- The exact public repository URL is printed, but repository contents and execution reproducibility were not inspected.
- No confidence intervals are reported for the prediction scores or their gains.
- The text does not provide full demographic information for participants or gaze annotators.
- Annotation reliability for the gaze labels is not reported.
- The rationale for the 30% coverage threshold is not empirically justified beyond sensitivity analyses of window length.
