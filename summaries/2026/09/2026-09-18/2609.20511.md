# When EOS Tokens Disagree: Understanding Length Inflation in On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20511) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20511)


## One-sentence takeaway
In the studied single-turn math settings, aligning semantically equivalent EOS tokens in the distillation signal substantially reduces stopping failures, but does not eliminate late-stage length inflation (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Sampled-token on-policy distillation (OPD) can produce increasingly long, repetitive responses that exhaust the generation budget even after reaching a correct answer. The authors investigate a specific mechanism: students and teachers may express “stop” through different tokens, causing token-level supervision to penalize the student’s native termination action (§§1, 3.1).

The gap is diagnostic rather than the first recognition of EOS mismatch: the paper acknowledges prior special-token fixes and EOS canonicalization, then systematically separates decoding configuration from learned termination preferences and probability-level supervision (§5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Mechanism diagnosis:** Measure individual EOS probabilities and total stopping mass at student rollout endpoints. A teacher-preferred EOS with negligible student probability receives little direct sampled supervision while the student’s native EOS is suppressed (§3.1).
- **Controlled intervention comparison:** Compare shared-set decoding, teacher-side EOS mapping, semantic EOS aggregation, and a canonical single-EOS action space. Semantic aggregation supervises the summed EOS probability as one stopping action, without requiring a shared preferred token (§3.2).
- **Cross-family and training-stage evidence:** Test within-family distillation for Qwen3, Llama, and Gemma, plus K2-Horizon students initialized at different training stages. These comparisons expose mismatch within identical declared EOS sets and residual collapse after alignment (§§3.3–4).
- **Analytical contribution and implementation:** Derive a stopping-logit contribution omitted by local OPD relative to trajectory-level reverse KL; present it as a possible contributor to transient length growth. The authors also report an implementation with termination corrections and evaluation protocols, although release wording is inconsistent (Appendices A, C).

The intervention comparisons support termination mismatch as a causal contributor in the tested settings, rather than a complete explanation of OPD length dynamics. Endpoint stopping mass includes both naturally terminated and truncated rollouts; it is not a natural-termination rate (§2). Most effect sizes are available only as qualitative descriptions in the supplied extraction, so exact length reductions or accuracy gains cannot be recovered.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments cover single-turn mathematics. Message completion, turn completion, and tool handoff may require distinct semantic classes in interactive settings (§6).
- **Author-stated unresolved mechanisms:** Early recovery differences and late K2-Horizon collapse remain unexplained. The theoretical analysis supplies a plausible relative continuation bias, not an empirical demonstration of its cause (§4.2, Appendix C).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Full-vocabulary OPD is not evaluated; template analysis is confined to Qwen3. TTRL–DAPO comparisons change both prompting and grading (§3.1, §6, Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** The full correction ablation is limited to Qwen3; other families primarily validate semantic aggregation. This does not establish equivalence among all corrections across families (§§2–3).
- **Analyst inference—theory-to-training gap:** Appendix C assumes expected, unclipped, trajectory-summed updates before optimizer preconditioning, whereas experiments use token-mean loss aggregation and AdamW. Its proposition does not directly characterize the complete training update.
- **Reproducibility uncertainty:** Training-seed replication and uncertainty estimates are not reported in the supplied text. The introduction says implementation is released, while Appendix A says code and configurations will be released.

## Open research questions
1. **What causes late collapse after EOS alignment?** Corrected K2-Horizon still saturates its budget (§4.2). A next experiment could compare local OPD with a future-mismatch correction while tracking stopping mass at matched prefixes.
2. **How much initial EOS support is sufficient for transfer?** K2-Horizon transfers the teacher’s EOS, unlike the severely mismatched Qwen3 case (§4.1). Controlled variation of initial teacher-preferred EOS probability and rollout sampling volume could test the proposed coverage mechanism.
3. **Does full-vocabulary supervision change recovery?** The authors argue probability weighting remains problematic but do not test it (§3.1). Compare sampled-token and full-vocabulary OPD, each with and without semantic aggregation, under matched budgets.
4. **Can semantic termination alignment preserve interaction control flow?** Multi-turn and tool-use termination events are not interchangeable (§6). Test context-dependent termination classes between capable post-trained models, measuring task success and control-flow errors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the qualitative mismatch diagnosis and its limited scope; **medium** in quantitative benefit and generality.

Missing information includes exact numerical series for most figures, repeated-run variability, training seeds, verified release status, and experiments establishing the cause of residual collapse or effectiveness beyond single-turn mathematics.
