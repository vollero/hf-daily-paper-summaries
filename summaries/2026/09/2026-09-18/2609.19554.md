# VABench: Measuring Embodied Spatial Intelligence through Visual Demonstrations, Active Perception, and Metric Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19554) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19554)


## One-sentence takeaway
VABench finds that strong behavioral localization scores coexist with unreliable simulated manipulation, while model-directed camera control improves success over fixed five-view input in four matched conditions (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
The authors address whether general-purpose multimodal models can acquire missing visual evidence, infer robot–object geometry, execute metric actions, and recover from mistakes. Their identified gap is that supplied views, structured object geometry, or predefined execution skills can leave parts of this loop untested; correctly describing a spatial relation does not establish contact-level control (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Joint observation and control protocol:** Models summarize RGB demonstrations, select viewpoints, generate numerical translations and rotations plus gripper commands, and revise actions using RGB and execution feedback. A shared controller handles trajectory execution; object poses, depth, oracle waypoints, and learned action heads are excluded (§§3.2–3.5).
- **Evaluation suite:** Fourteen base families—eleven single-arm and three with two available arms—plus seven controlled transfer variants and a separate five-object composition task. Base instances are verified through successful expert execution (§§3.1, 3.6; Appendix C.3–C.4).
- **Complementary measurements:** Physics-based terminal success, nine human-annotated behavioral criteria, and checkpoint progress. Twelve primary conditions receive three runs on the same twenty seeds per family; ablations examine camera access and demonstration-summary source (§§3.7–5).

The contribution is an evaluation protocol and benchmark, rather than a newly trained manipulation policy.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Best base success remains limited | Across 14 families, three runs and 20 fixed seeds per family: Qwen3.8-max **53.93 ± 3.17%**, Opus-5 **52.86 ± 2.79%**, GPT-5.6-sol **51.55 ± 4.31%**; ± denotes sample SD across runs. | Table 3; Appendix A.1 |
| Localization does not ensure completion | Qwen3.8-max receives **100.0%** target-localization and **78.9%** spatial-relation scores over its 280-episode annotated base run. These are behavioral labels, separate from three-run terminal success. | §4.2; Table 11 |
| Active camera access improves matched outcomes | Qwen3.8-max: active **161/280 (57.50%)**, passive **78/280 (27.86%)**, a **29.64 percentage-point** advantage. All four tested conditions improve with active access; each comparison uses one matched run. | §5.1; Table 4 |
| Transfer degradation differs substantially | Across seven matched families, 20 seeds each, GPT-5.6-sol falls **80.00% → 47.86%**; Qwen3.8-max falls **77.86% → 67.86%**. Base summaries are reused without new demonstrations. | Table 5 |
| Partial composition does not reach strict completion | All four evaluated composition conditions achieve **0%** strict success over 20 episodes each. Qwen3.8-max completes **61/100** object placements; GPT-5.6-sol **53/100**. | Table 5 |
| Summary transfer depends on source and reader | On three tasks, 60 episodes per reader/source, Opus-5 summaries improve the other four readers by **7.50 points** on average; Qwen3.8-max summaries reduce them by **31.25 points**. | §5.2; Table 6 |
| Diagnostic agreement is high overall | Across **29,232** applicable base episode–criterion items, unanimity is **95.69%**, Fleiss κ **0.942**; fine-grained pre-contact analysis has lower unanimity, **87.38%**. | Appendix C.9.1; Table 28 |

These results establish differences within the reported model–harness–simulator conditions. They do not reliably rank the top three base models, identify the cognitive causes of failure, or establish real-world robot performance. The camera comparison changes viewpoint selection, available refinement, and visual payload structure together; it does not isolate which mechanism produces the advantage (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated causal limits:** Behavioral diagnostics are observational. Camera effects may reflect view relevance, cross-view integration, or visual-context length; summary effects confound constraint explicitness with writing style (Appendix B.1).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Transfer uses finite controlled variants; composition covers one family. Physical camera reachability, collision, calibration error, and continuous motion cost are excluded (Appendices B.2, C.6).
- **Author-stated conditioning limits:** Primary scores combine demonstration summarization with online control. Full-suite expert-summary and no-summary controls across models, and matched modular VLM–VLA comparisons, remain absent (Appendix B.3–B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Disabled background, lighting, camera, table-height, and clutter randomization restrict evidence for visual robustness (Appendix C.4).
- **Interpretation caution:** “Dual-arm” includes shoe placement requiring selection of the reachable arm; aggregate dual-arm success is therefore not a pure measure of simultaneous coordination (Appendix C.3).
- **Annotation limits:** Retained vote tallies omit annotator identities, preventing per-rater bias analysis; individual diagnostic rankings can change with voting rules (Appendix C.9.1).

## Open research questions
1. **What causes the active-view advantage?** Existing protocols confound selection, refinement, and image load. Compare active selection restricted to canonical views against passive bundles with matched image budgets (Appendix B.1).
2. **Which summary properties transfer?** Source comparisons vary style and explicitness together. Test expert-written summaries with controlled constraint content and length across the complete suite (Appendix B.3).
3. **What blocks complete composition?** Object-level progress coexists with no strict completions. Add explicit inventory tracking and checkpoint-based recovery separately, then compare placement progress and episode success on the same seeds (Table 5).
4. **Does viewpoint selection survive physical sensing constraints?** The virtual camera bypasses motion feasibility and calibration. Repeat matched camera comparisons with reachable trajectories and controlled calibration perturbations (Appendix C.6).
