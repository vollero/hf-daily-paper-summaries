# DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19969) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors report that combining cross-layer cache sharing, FP4 caching, and approximate sliding-window replay reduces global KV storage to roughly one-quarter and persistent KV storage to one-eighth of DeepSeek-V4-Flash while improving several agent benchmarks, although component-level accuracy and deployment-cost evidence remains limited (§6).

## Problem and motivation
Long-horizon agents repeatedly ingest large tool outputs and reuse growing contexts. Sparse attention reduces computation, but prefill, cache storage, and cache movement remain deployment bottlenecks. The authors distinguish global KV held in GPU memory from persistent caches retained for prefix reuse, arguing that both require further compression (§1).

The identified gap is joint optimization: reusing sparse indices alone does not reduce main KV storage, while exact reconstruction of discarded sliding-window attention (SWA) states requires expensive replay (§§2.3, 3.2.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Causal Encoder–Decoder (CED):** Decoder global KV is projected from final encoder states, allowing most prompt tokens to bypass decoder computation. Layer-local SWA remains, with bounded replay supplying approximate decoder states (§2.2).
- **Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2):** Full, Reindex, and Reuse modes separate cache sharing from sparse-index refresh. A decoder-only hierarchical indexer restricts later searches to candidates selected by an earlier layer; the first indexer still scans the full visible context (§2.3).
- **Cache precision and reconstruction:** Post-training introduces FP4 main-KV quantization; sensitive SWA caches remain FP8. SWA Bounded Replay reconstructs missing local states from the latest window, enabling removal of SWA from persistent storage while retaining short-lived encoder SWA in host memory (§§2.4.4, 3.2).
- **Supporting architecture and training:** Single-Pass mHC enables fused residual processing. The model integrates previously introduced Engram memory and DSpark speculative decoding, plus multimodal training and automated agent-task construction. The authors explicitly claim no new post-training algorithm: improvements center on data and environments (§§2.4, 5.1).

The model contains 552B backbone parameters plus 196B Engram parameters and is pretrained on 45T multimodal tokens; its small activated footprint should therefore not be confused with small total storage requirements (§§2.1, 4.2).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Smaller global and persistent caches | Global KV: **890 bytes/token**, approximately **¼** of V4-Flash at equal sequence length. Persistent KV: approximately **⅛** under identical workloads, combining global compression with removal of persistent SWA. | §6; §3.2.1 |
| Reduced prefill computation | **8B** activated parameters/token in prefill versus **16B** in decode. CED approximately halves computation when sequence length greatly exceeds the SWA window; this is not a measured latency ratio. | §§2.1–2.2 |
| Slow growth in decode FLOPs | Extending context from **4K to 1M** increases single-token decode FLOPs by approximately **25%**, using BF16/FP8/FP4 weights of **1/0.5/0.25**. | §1, Fig. 2 |
| Strong coding-agent scores | At Max effort, DeepSWE v1.1 **Resolved: 74.2%**, versus V4-Flash **54.4%** and Opus-5 **74.0%**; V4.1 uses mini-SWE. Terminal-Bench 2.1 **Pass@1: 90.6%**, versus **82.7%** and **89.1%**, respectively; V4.1 uses DeepSeek Harness Minimal with a **1M-token** context. | §§5.3.1–5.3.2, Table 3 |
| General-agent improvement | AutomationBench public v1.0.6, official scaffold, Max effort: **54.8% Pass@1**, versus V4-Flash **37.7%** and Opus-5 **50.3%**. | §§5.3.1–5.3.2, Table 3 |
| Difficult-task and visual gaps remain | Max effort: Terminal-Bench 4.0 **Pass@1 31.2%**, versus Opus-5 **51.8%**; Chartography with tools **Pass@1 78.9%**, versus **84.0%**. V4.1 visual evaluations use Claude Code and **512K-token** context. | §§5.3.1–5.3.2, Table 3 |
| Base-model long-context evidence is mixed | LongBench-V2, **1-shot EM**: V4.1-Flash-Base **45.2**, V4-Flash-Base **44.7**, V4-Pro-Base **51.5**, under the same internal evaluation settings. | Table 1 |
| Effort trades tokens for accuracy | Effort **25→100** raises average Pass@1 across eight reasoning benchmarks from **67.1%→76.3%**, DeepSWE from **66.0%→74.2%**, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 from **82.4%→90.6%**, with roughly **2.5×** more output tokens. | §5.3.2, Fig. 9 |

The evidence supports substantial reported cache savings alongside strong benchmark performance for the complete system. It does not isolate how much capability each architectural change preserves or contributes. Global KV bytes exclude other memory requirements, and persistent-cache savings depend on workload and retention policy. FLOP accounting also does not establish equivalent wall-clock or monetary savings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Sparse selection errors and approximate SWA reconstruction could degrade performance on untested boundary cases. Reconstructed states are explicitly not mathematically equivalent to full computation (§§3.2.2, 6).
- **Author-stated:** Difficult reasoning, science-oriented agent tasks, and visual tasks retain gaps against leading closed models. Evaluation environments remain susceptible to gaming despite mitigation (§§5.3, 6).
- **Analyst inference:** No controlled numerical ablation separates CED, CSA2 sharing, hierarchical indexing, FP4, and bounded replay. Claims of negligible quality loss therefore lack a clearly quantified component-level bound.
- **Analyst inference:** Hardware-specific serving latency, throughput, and cost comparisons are absent. The reported cache reductions alone cannot determine deployment savings.
- **Analyst inference:** Small leaderboard differences lack reported confidence intervals, and Table 3 does not fully establish matched competitor scaffolds and budgets. The broad claim of completing over 95% of real-world tasks lacks a defined supporting evaluation (§1).
- **Internal qualification:** Appendix B.2 reports non-monotonic accuracy across scaffold–effort settings, narrowing the main text’s claim of steadily improving accuracy.
- **Multi-agent scope:** Experiments are preliminary, compare strongest observed configurations, and use filtered ProgramBench and no-GPU FrontierSWE subsets; wall-clock matching does not establish equal total compute (§5.3.5).

## Open research questions
1. **When does bounded replay fail?** Cache-hit position changes reconstructed states, and extreme cases remain uncharacterized. Compare exact and bounded replay across resumption positions, repeated evictions, and long dependency tasks (§§3.2.2, 6).
2. **Which compression choices determine the quality–memory trade-off?** Joint changes obscure individual effects. Run matched ablations varying cache sharing, quantization, and candidate-pool size, measuring retrieval accuracy and cache footprint (§§2.3–2.4).
3. **How much serving benefit survives realistic workload variation?** Persistent savings depend on session behavior, and direct serving measurements are missing. Benchmark latency, throughput, and total memory under controlled cache-hit rates and turn lengths (§3.2).
4. **Does multi-agent collaboration help at equal total compute?** Current comparisons emphasize deadlines and selected configurations. Compare single and multiple agents under both matched token budgets and wall-clock limits (§5.3.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture and reported benchmark values are well specified, but causal attribution and practical efficiency are less firmly established.

Material missing information includes numerical compression ablations, replay failure distributions, hardware-level serving measurements, statistical uncertainty, complete competitor evaluation configurations, detailed training-data access, and exact numerical values from several extracted figures. Checkpoint availability is stated in the paper but was not independently verified.
