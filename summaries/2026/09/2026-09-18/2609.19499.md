# Sample Count Is Not Enough: Candidate-Generation Strategy Shapes the Energy and Performance of LLM Test-Time Scaling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19499) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19499)


## One-sentence takeaway
At a fixed eight-candidate budget on GSM8K/A100, the authors report that serial generation consumes 4.64–4.86× the gross GPU-device energy of one batched call, showing that candidate count alone does not specify inference cost (§V-C).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-candidate test-time scaling is commonly described by candidate count, but identical counts can require different numbers of sequential generation calls. These execution choices affect latency and energy, particularly in finite HPC batch jobs. The authors identify incomplete reporting of generation schedules and energy in representative prior studies; their audit is explicitly non-exhaustive (§§I, II-D).

## Approach and contributions
- **Schedule specification:** Represent execution as a sequence of batch sizes whose sum equals the candidate budget. Compare `1×8`, `2×4`, `4×2`, and `8×1`, where calls execute sequentially on one GPU (§III-A).
- **Controlled systems characterization:** Hold candidate count, prompts, decoding settings, extraction, and plurality voting fixed while measuring schedule costs. Candidates are sampled independently across schedules; accuracy versus candidate count is evaluated separately using prefixes of shared candidate sets (§§III–IV).
- **Robustness and reporting guidance:** Check additional A100 nodes and a short-output SciQ/V100 workload, then recommend reporting generation-call structure and measurement boundaries. The largest-feasible-batch heuristic is proposed as practical guidance, not a universal optimizer (§§V-D–VI-B).

The contribution is an empirical characterization and reporting framework, rather than a new reasoning or answer-selection algorithm.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| More candidates improve accuracy | On 500 GSM8K prompts, paired candidate prefixes from N=1 to N=8 increase Phi-3 accuracy from 81.4% to 89.8%: +8.4 percentage points, 95% CI [5.8, 11.2]. Qwen rises from 51.4% to 69.8%: +18.4 points, CI [14.8, 22.0]. | §V-A, Fig. 2 |
| Larger batches improve token efficiency while increasing query energy | Batched N=1→8 on 100 GSM8K prompts over three repetitions: Phi-3/V100 increases from 631→1286 J/query while decreasing from 2.634→0.655 J/generated token; Qwen/A100 changes from 596→975 J/query and 2.222→0.459 J/token. | §§IV-A, V-B; Table V caption and accompanying prose |
| Serial schedules substantially increase energy | Fixed N=8, GSM8K/A100, 100 prompts over three repetitions: `8×1`/`1×8` gross-energy ratios are 4.64 for Phi-3, 95% CI [4.48, 4.79], and 4.86 for Qwen, CI [4.71, 5.02]. | §V-C |
| Serial schedules worsen tail latency and throughput | Same setting: P95 latency ratios are 5.77 for Phi-3, 95% CI [5.38, 5.99], and 6.12 for Qwen, CI [5.70, 6.75]. Throughput falls to 16.7% and 17.9% of the batched baseline, respectively. | §V-C |
| Candidate token volumes remain closely matched | Across fixed-N GSM8K schedules, mean logical generated-token volume varies by 0.8% for Phi-3 and 1.0% for Qwen. | §V-C |
| Direction persists across nodes and short outputs | Three independently scheduled A100 jobs per model show consistent endpoint effects. On 500 SciQ prompts over three V100 repetitions, serial/batched energy ratios are 2.57 for Phi-3 and 3.34 for Qwen; mean-latency ratios are 2.88 and 4.42, respectively. | §§IV-A, V-D, V-F |

The evidence supports a substantial schedule-dependent **end-to-end systems cost** in these configurations. It does not establish identical accuracy across schedules: accuracy and systems measurements use separate generations. Nor does it isolate which low-level mechanisms cause the penalty. Confidence intervals characterize variability within primary jobs, not uncertainty across hardware environments (§IV-E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Two models, two GPU architectures, and batch-scheduled Hugging Face generation; larger models, continuous batching, and multi-GPU deployments remain untested (§VII).
- **Author-stated measurement boundary:** Energy includes idle GPU power during the query interval but excludes whole-node consumption, model loading, and warm-up (§III-D).
- **Author-stated causal limits:** Independent candidate sampling and combined execution changes prevent decomposition of prompt processing, synchronization, call overhead, and utilization effects (§§VI-A, VII).
- **Author-stated workload confounding:** SciQ changes both workload and hardware, so smaller penalties cannot be attributed solely to output length (§V-F).
- **Analyst inference:** The largest-feasible-batch rule remains a heuristic beyond the tested schedules; the supplied evaluation does not validate it under concurrent traffic or larger candidate budgets (§VI-B).

## Open research questions
1. **Which execution mechanisms dominate the penalty?** The study measures their combined effect. Next experiment: profile prefill, decoding, synchronization, and call overhead separately while matching candidate lengths (§VI-A).
2. **Does the batching heuristic hold under serving traffic?** Continuous batching and concurrency are outside the evaluation. Next experiment: sweep candidate batch sizes under controlled arrival rates and memory limits (§§VI-B, VII).
3. **How much does output length change schedule efficiency?** SciQ and GSM8K use different GPU architectures. Next experiment: vary output length on the same model, GPU, and inference stack (§V-F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported directional result within the tested settings; limited for broader generalization.

The supplied retrieval contains table captions but omits table bodies, preventing inspection of full schedule results, cross-node ranges, and reporting-audit entries. It also does not establish a code/raw-data release, exact prompt templates, complete seed specifications, software versions, or schedule-specific accuracy results.
