# JEPA-Anything: Learning Predictive Models across Different Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20800) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20800)


## One-sentence takeaway
JEPA-Anything organizes latent predictions into orthogonality-regularized factors, reporting improvements across diverse prediction tasks while leaving cross-domain transfer, causal interpretation, and consistent planning gains unestablished.

## Problem and motivation
Standard joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) predict a single latent target through one pathway. The authors argue that this can concentrate learning on easy or high-variance structure and underrepresent weaker predictive components. They seek a common way to allocate predictive capacity across heterogeneous systems while preserving domain-specific observations and encoders (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared predictive interface:** Domain adapters define tokens, structural descriptors, and context–target pairs; an online encoder and an exponential-moving-average target encoder provide latent states. This is a shared algorithm instantiated separately across domains (§2.1, Table 1).
- **Orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF):** Learned projectors divide the target into complementary subspaces with dedicated predictors. A pseudoinverse combines predicted factors into a complete latent state for forecasting and planning (§2.2).
- **Training and synthesis guarantees:** Factor prediction, within- and cross-factor orthogonality, factor activity, and encoder variance losses augment each domain’s existing objective. The paper proves synthesis stability under exact orthogonality and a bounded approximate-orthogonality condition (§§2.2–2.3; Appendix A).
- **Multiple uses of learned states:** Experiments test encoder readouts, recursive dynamics, planning, and factor-level scientific analysis across seven domains (§§2.4–3).

The evidence supports OPF as a useful training and state-synthesis design across several separately configured applications. It does not establish a single transferable cross-domain model, universal planning improvements, or causal meaning for the factors. The geometric audit measures exact-coordinate transpose reconstruction; it does not independently demonstrate accurate learned transitions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundaries:** Orthogonal factors do not establish causal mechanisms, and sharing an architecture does not guarantee aligned representations across domains. Uncertainty calibration under distribution shift remains future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** OPF changes factorization and several regularizers together. The supplied experiments do not isolate each regularizer’s contribution to downstream gains; the orthogonality audit primarily isolates geometry (§3.3.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited guarantees:** Synthesis bounds depend on orthogonality conditions and constrain coordinate-error amplification, not accumulated rollout error or planning success (§2.2, Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—scientific validation gaps:** The biological nomination procedure, quantitative effect sizes, and detailed wet-lab protocols are not supplied. Orbital validation uses one analyzed run without a reported comparative baseline (§3.4, Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—reporting gaps:** Several results appear only as qualitative statements or figure captions in the supplied extraction. Many tables provide means without uncertainty, limiting assessment of consistency and significance.

## Open research questions
- **Which OPF components drive predictive gains?** The complete objective is compared against monolithic JEPA. Next experiment: capacity-matched ablations of factorization, orthogonality, and each activity term across representative domains.
- **Why does planning improve unevenly?** Hopper favors the baseline despite improvements elsewhere. Next experiment: compare action-sequence ranking accuracy and rollout errors across planning horizons in each control environment.
- **Can factorization enable cross-domain transfer?** Separate instantiations demonstrate algorithmic breadth, while alignment remains unestablished. Next experiment: transfer learned predictors or factors between related physical systems against training-from-scratch controls.
- **Do factors improve prospective intervention selection?** One nominated biological combination receives support, but comparative selection quality is unreported. Next experiment: prospectively test ranked candidates from OPF and matched monolithic models under an identical experimental budget.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and tabulated comparisons are clear, but broader scientific and clinical claims have thinner supporting detail.

Material missing information includes clinical cohort size and forecasting horizons; numerical values from several figures; complete task-specific training settings and regularization parameters; uncertainty for many reported means; detailed biological nomination and experimental procedures; and verifiable code/model release contents.
