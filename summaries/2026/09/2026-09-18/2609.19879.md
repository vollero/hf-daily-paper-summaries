# VākQA: A Benchmark and Evaluation Study for Telugu Spoken Factoid Question Answering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19879) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19879)


## One-sentence takeaway

VākQA introduces a human-verified Telugu spoken factoid QA benchmark and finds that input language, speech-processing errors, and judge selection materially affect measured performance, with Gemini leading the tested models but remaining an imperfect evaluator (§§II–V).

## Problem and motivation

The authors identify two gaps: limited Telugu spoken QA resources and unquantified reliability of automatic evaluation in this setting. Speech recognition errors can change question meaning, while lexical metrics can reject correct answers expressed differently from references. Translated benchmarks can also lose culturally specific scope and wording (§I).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark:** The authors release VākQA with 2,001 QA pairs, 2.53 hours of Telugu question audio, and manually translated English counterparts across six domains. Audio comes from YouTube quiz content; five annotators verify transcript–audio correspondence (§II; Table I).
- **Construction pipeline:** Voice activity detection, Telugu ASR, Gemini-based QA extraction, word timestamps, and fuzzy matching produce candidate audio–text pairs before human verification (§II-B).
- **Evaluation reliability study:** Human ratings calibrate comparisons among Gemini and Gemma judges, lexical metrics, and BLASER-2.0 (§§III-B–IV).
- **Controlled input comparisons:** Existing QA models are tested with oracle Telugu/English text, direct Telugu speech, ASR transcripts, and ASR→MT pipelines, followed by domain analysis (§§III-A, V). The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation study, not a new QA architecture.

## Evidence and main results

QA scores below use Gemini-as-a-judge on a 1–5 scale; parentheses denote standard deviations.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Human agreement is strong after filtering | Five raters evaluated 400 answers to 100 Telugu text questions; Krippendorff’s α reached 0.836 after excluding 20 items whose ratings spanned the full scale. | §III-B |
| Gemini best matches human rankings among tested judges | Spearman’s ρ / Kendall’s τ: Gemini 0.86 / 0.77; Gemma-12B 0.81 / 0.71; Gemma-27B 0.80 / 0.70; Gemma-4B 0.57 / 0.50. | §IV; Table III discussion |
| Best judge still has systematic disagreement | Gemini mean judge-minus-human error was −0.28; agreement limits were −1.28 to 0.72. Authors report greater leniency for low-quality answers and strictness for high-quality answers. | §IV; Fig. 2 discussion |
| Gemini leads reported oracle Telugu comparisons | Gemini 3.63 (1.71), Gemma-27B 2.55 (1.79), Gemma-12B 2.01 (1.60), Sarvam-m 1.98 (1.60), Gemma-4B 1.43 (1.11). | §V-A; Table V discussion |
| Human-translated English slightly lowers Gemini’s mean | Oracle English: 3.52 (1.74), versus Telugu: 3.63 (1.71); English scores were worse on 18.8%, better on 16.5%, and unchanged on 64.7% of questions. | §V-B; Tables V–VI discussion |
| Direct speech lowers Gemini’s mean | Telugu speech: 3.28 (1.84), versus oracle Telugu text: 3.63 (1.71); speech scores worsened on 21.2% and improved on 13.1% of questions. | §V-C; Tables V–VI discussion |
| Translation pipelines underperform oracle Telugu | Gemini scored 2.74 after translating oracle Telugu with Seamless MT; full ASR→MT configurations scored 2.44–2.80, versus 3.63 for oracle Telugu. | §V-D; Table V discussion |
| Culture is particularly translation-sensitive for Gemini | Mean correctness fell from 3.76 with oracle Telugu to 2.42 with Seamless-translated English. | §V-E; Figs. 3–4 discussion |

These results support performance differences within the tested benchmark and scoring setup; they are not accuracy percentages. Qualitative examples illustrate plausible acoustic and translation failures but do not quantify their prevalence. The authors claim progressively compounding pipeline errors, but the reported aggregate range does not establish that every cascade is worse than the MT-only score: its upper end exceeds 2.74.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Faithful translations can leave cultural scope ambiguous; Gemini’s uneven strictness limits fine-grained comparisons; consistency between English transliterations and native Telugu answer equivalents was not assessed (§VI).
- **Analyst inference—judge validation:** Prompts were refined to maximize human correlation, but a separate held-out validation set is not described. Excluding disagreement-heavy items may make reliability look stronger; agreement before exclusion is unavailable (§III-B).
- **Analyst inference—comparison validity:** Gemini both answers and judges. Independent human validation of the full model ranking is not reported, leaving potential evaluator preference unresolved (§§III–V).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Quiz-style YouTube speech and direct-speech evaluation of only Gemini do not establish performance on conversational speech or across speech-capable model families (§§II-A, III-A).

## Open research questions

1. **Does judge reliability transfer beyond the calibration sample?** Validation uses Telugu text answers from a limited model set. Next experiment: freeze prompts and evaluate unseen questions, English answers, and speech-pipeline outputs against independent human ratings.
2. **How much translation loss comes from missing cultural scope?** The paper provides examples rather than a controlled attribution. Next experiment: compare faithful translations with explicitly disambiguated translations of the same questions.
3. **Which ASR errors most damage QA?** Examples show semantic failures, but aggregate recognition error does not isolate their causes. Next experiment: correct question words, named entities, and other error categories separately and measure QA recovery.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main findings, but Tables II–VII contain captions without their full contents, and figure values are available only where described in prose.

Material missing information includes the complete scoring rubric and prompts, detailed ASR/MT results, decoding settings, unfiltered human agreement, held-out judge validation, statistical significance tests, speaker/dialect coverage, and contamination checks. The text states that the dataset is released; its contents and accompanying code were not independently inspected.
