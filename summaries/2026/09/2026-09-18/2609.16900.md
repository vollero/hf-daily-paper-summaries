# RiskChainBench: A Benchmark for Obfuscated Platform Message Restoration and Evidence-Grounded Web Investigation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16900) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16900)


## One-sentence takeaway
RiskChainBench links synthetic message restoration to controlled website investigation, showing that destination-recovery errors and web execution failures substantially constrain performance under its offline entry-gating protocol.

## Problem and motivation
Abuse campaigns disguise messages and destination addresses using phonetic substitutions, character decomposition, emojis, and redundant tokens. Recovering the wrong destination can undermine downstream investigation even when a model broadly understands the message. The authors identify a gap in benchmarks that evaluate obfuscated text and website risk separately, without measuring this cross-stage loss (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Linked benchmark:** 3,600 synthetic text inputs—six variants for each source session—paired with 600 human-labeled local website environments. Obfuscations have reversible transformation traces; websites reproduce relevant content and interactions within an isolated sandbox (§§3–3.1).
- **Separated tasks with offline composition:** Models first commit to message, intent, and destination predictions. The same underlying model independently investigates the correctly associated website without message semantics or original-domain cues. A predetermined primary variant’s destination prediction then gates the frozen website result; incorrect destinations are not actually browsed (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Distinct correctness and evidence evaluation:** Human labels determine website decision and type correctness. A fixed multimodal judge separately assesses evidence faithfulness, sufficiency, investigation completeness, and reasoning consistency. Ten-model baselines localize failures across restoration and investigation stages (§§3.4–4).

The contribution is a benchmark and evaluation protocol, rather than a new trained model.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Destination recovery and complete restoration favor different systems | Across 3,600 inputs, GPT-5.4 leads Entry Top-1 at **95.22%**; GPT-5.6 SOL leads full reconstruction at **73.31%**, with the lowest reported CER, **1.11%**. | Table 1(a), p. 9 |
| Correct-routing website investigation remains difficult | Across 600 websites per model, GPT-5.6 SOL leads three-way decision accuracy at **62.8%**, decision macro-F1 at **59.3%**, and hierarchical exact match at **61.0%**; missing decisions count as failures. | Table 1(b), p. 9 |
| Fine-grained typing has a different leader | Kimi K2.5 achieves the highest type macro-F1, **48.1%**, evaluated on **394** gold-violation websites. | Table 1(b), p. 9 |
| Entry gating can substantially reduce accuracy | On the same 600 websites, GPT-5.6 SOL falls from **62.8%** web-only accuracy to **60.8%** gated accuracy; Qwen3.6 Plus falls from **51.7%** to **19.3%**, with a reported **32.3 percentage-point** loss. | Table 2, p. 10 |
| Execution dominates first-failure attribution | Pooled Task 2 runs: **31.9%** execution failures, **8.2%** missing-report failures, **12.3%** decision failures, and **0.9%** type failures after a correct decision. | Figure 5, p. 8 |
| Website labels underwent disagreement resolution | Two initial judgments per website achieved **82.50%** decision agreement and **80.17%** joint decision–type agreement, followed by review/adjudication where required. | §3.1, p. 5 |

These results establish performance differences within the frozen benchmark and isolate the scoring loss associated with incorrect primary-entry predictions. They do **not** evaluate actual navigation to wrong destinations, recovery from routing mistakes, or the effect of passing restored message context into investigation. Small discrepancies between displayed accuracy differences and reported losses may reflect rounding; unrounded values are unavailable.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One trajectory per model–website pair prevents estimating rerun variance. Website-level bootstrap intervals measure website-composition uncertainty only (§4.1; §5).
- **Author-stated:** Several violation types are sparsely represented; only six of nine codebook types have nonzero support. Broader type and language coverage remains future work (§3.4; §5).
- **Author-stated:** Evidence scores depend on successful judge coverage. No four-dimensional human evidence-audit result is reported, and the authors call for human validation (§3.3, Figure 4; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Synthetic messages and deterministic obfuscation recipes leave transfer to naturally occurring evasive messages unestablished. The deliberately balanced website selection supports capability testing, not prevalence estimates (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Offline gating measures failure to recover the intended entry but omits consequences of investigating an incorrect target or attempting alternative candidates (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** The small post-decision typing failure share is conditional on reaching that stage with a correct decision; it does not establish that typing is generally solved, particularly given limited type support (Figure 5; Table 1).

## Open research questions
- **How stable are model rankings across repeated runs?** Single trajectories leave stochastic execution variability unknown. Repeat each model–website investigation under identical budgets and compare ranking uncertainty.
- **Does synthetic restoration performance transfer to real evasive messages?** No platform-collected messages are evaluated. Test on a separately annotated, safely sanitized natural-message set with destination-recovery labels.
- **How well does the automated evidence judge agree with humans?** Human validation of the four evidence dimensions is absent. Conduct a blinded trajectory audit stratified by model, outcome, and judge coverage.
- **How does actual routing change end-to-end performance?** Offline gating excludes wrong-target exploration and recovery. Compare it with isolated browser runs that follow predicted destinations and permit explicitly bounded alternative-entry attempts.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the protocol and headline results as reported; **medium** in broader generalization and evidence-score validity.

The supplied text lacks the referenced supplement: detailed construction strata and language/type distributions, full prompts and judge configuration, numerical evidence scores and coverage, bootstrap intervals, stratified restoration results, and complete failure-handling rules. It also provides no concrete release location or repeated-run measurements.
