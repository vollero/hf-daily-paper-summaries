# RetireOPD: Self-Retiring On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20784)


## One-sentence takeaway
RetireOPD combines a separately trained skill-conditioned teacher with adaptive removal of distillation, reporting higher success than GRPO, continuous distillation, and its own teacher across two agent benchmarks and three Qwen2.5 model sizes.

## Problem and motivation
Multi-turn agent RL supplies sparse trajectory rewards; on-policy distillation adds token-level guidance on student-generated trajectories. The authors identify two weaknesses: privileged skills do not automatically produce a reliable teacher, and continued teacher matching can restrict later reward-driven improvement. They propose learning both a useful teacher and an online stopping point for its supervision (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Train the teacher explicitly:** Initialize teacher and student from the same base model, optimize the skill-conditioned teacher with GRPO and environment rewards, then freeze it (§3.1).
- **Internalize skills through joint training:** Train the skill-free student with GRPO plus reverse-KL distillation on its own trajectories. Teacher scoring uses retrieved skills and the same trajectory prefixes; sampled-token log-probability differences approximate the KL (§3.2).
- **Retire supervision adaptively:** Remove distillation permanently when discrepancy reduction stalls and student success reaches a target fraction of teacher success; continue with GRPO alone. Default thresholds are relative competence γ = 0.9 and discrepancy change δ = 0 (§§3.3, 4.1).
- **Explain the stopping signal:** A local first-order analysis links non-decreasing discrepancy to conflicting gradients or diminishing distillation guidance, under simplifying assumptions (Appendix A).

The distinctive contribution is the combination of explicit teacher preparation and signal-driven global retirement; joint GRPO–OPD itself follows prior work (§3.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no dedicated limitations section. The following are **analyst assessments** grounded in the text:

- **Narrow scope:** Evaluation covers two interactive benchmarks and one model family; broader transfer is untested (§4.1).
- **Unreported uncertainty:** No seed counts, confidence intervals, or significance tests accompany the main results.
- **Inconsistent reporting:** The 3B ALFWorld main result is 93.8%, whereas retirement ablations report 92.2%, without explanation. Table 3 also reports a later retirement at δ = 0.03 that appears inconsistent with §4.5’s claimed timing stability over a range including that value.
- **Limited schedule comparison:** The annealing baseline retains nonzero distillation and uses different loss-weight magnitudes; its comparison does not isolate adaptive timing alone. A tuned fixed-switch sweep is not reported (§4.4, Appendix B).
- **Theory is local:** Appendix A ignores finite-sample noise and assumes inactive clipping. It allows discrepancy stagnation to mean diminishing guidance, rather than necessarily gradient conflict.
- **Efficiency remains unmeasured:** Retirement eliminates subsequent teacher forward passes, but teacher pretraining and validation also cost resources; no end-to-end timing or compute comparison is supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Does adaptive retirement beat tuned fixed switching under equal compute?** Only a particular annealing comparison is reported. Next experiment: sweep fixed switch steps and annealing schedules with matched teacher-training and student-training budgets.
- **How reliable is the discrepancy signal under noise?** The theory omits sampling noise, and empirical uncertainty is unreported. Next experiment: repeat across seeds and monitoring-window sizes while measuring gradient alignment around retirement.
- **Does the method transfer beyond these skills and environments?** Evaluation uses SkillBank retrieval on two benchmarks. Next experiment: vary skill quality and retrieval errors, then test additional environments and model families.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and main numerical comparisons are explicit, but reporting inconsistencies and missing uncertainty limit confidence in robustness.

Material missing information includes seed counts, uncertainty estimates, training-set sizes and split construction, main-result checkpoint selection, detailed teacher-training budget, hardware and total compute costs, and explanations for the conflicting ablation and timing values. Code availability is author-reported; its contents were not examined.
