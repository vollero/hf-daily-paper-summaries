# What Does Privileged Information Add to On-Policy Self-Distillation?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20612) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20612)


## One-sentence takeaway
In two mathematical reasoning models, privileged solutions add benefits that depend on the model and checkpoint beyond reference-free self-distillation, while switching to long thinking-enabled training rollouts can reverse gains into losses (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a model on its own responses using feedback from a frozen copy that can see a reference answer or solution. Improvement over the original model does not establish that the reference helped: distillation without a reference can also improve performance.

The authors isolate the reference’s additional contribution and examine whether it depends on solution representation, student-generated prefixes, training stage, and evaluation mode. Crucially, their reference-free control still has a thinking-enabled teacher supervising direct-response student rollouts (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Answer-matched resource:** AMPLE-MATH contains 5,319 mathematical problems with six views sharing a canonical answer: Answer Only, Gist, Key Points, Clean Solution, Summary, and Full Trace. It includes construction audits, difficulty annotations, and frozen experimental splits (§2.3; Appendix B).
- **Controlled empirical comparisons:** Qwen3-1.7B and SmolLM3-3B undergo LoRA OPSD with a frozen teacher, using clipped forward KL on student-generated prefixes. Reference-free controls and matched checkpoints separate distillation gains from reference contributions; additional comparisons vary training and evaluation modes (§§2–3).
- **Diagnostics tested through interventions:** Teacher profiles measure correctness alignment, pressure on reconsideration markers, and where token-level disagreement occurs. Reference substitutions, marker-loss edits, and alternative loss windows test whether changing those signals improves transfer (§§4–5). A theoretical counterexample shows that equal aggregate alignment and KL can accompany opposite effects on accuracy (Appendix D.4).

The contribution is principally an evaluation resource and controlled analysis of OPSD, rather than a new distillation algorithm.

## Evidence and main results
All intervals below are reported 95% problem-cluster bootstrap intervals; accuracy differences are percentage points. Avg@n averages sample correctness within each problem, then across problems.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Reference-free training explains substantial Qwen improvement | After direct-response training, three-seed reference-free Qwen gains **+1.80 [0.20, 3.47] Avg@4** over base in domain at step 100, and **+4.07 [1.73, 6.54] Avg@12** pooled across 90 external problems at step 50; evaluation is thinking-enabled. | Table 1 |
| Qwen’s additional reference benefit is modest | Clean Solution exceeds reference-free training by **+1.30 [0.20, 2.41] Avg@4** at step 100, using three seeds each and thinking-enabled evaluation. The authors report that the benefit does not survive correction across six views. No external view-versus-control interval lies entirely above zero. | §3.1; Appendix C.1; Tables 5, 10 |
| Full Trace helps SmolLM3, but training stage matters | With direct-response training and thinking-enabled evaluation, three-seed Full Trace exceeds reference-free by **+2.00 [0.82, 3.17] Avg@4** at step 50. At step 100, all three tested reference configurations score below base. | Table 6 |
| Long thinking-enabled training reverses transfer | At step 50, Qwen Full Trace scores **82.42% Avg@4** after direct-response training versus **75.13%** after thinking-enabled training; base is **80.21%**. The paired gap is **−7.29 [−9.44, −5.14]**. SmolLM3’s two-seed Full Trace gap is **−6.84 [−8.50, −5.18]** under thinking-enabled evaluation. | Tables 11–12 |
| Reference relevance still matters | Single-seed, step-100 Qwen substitutions with length-matched other-problem references reduce thinking-enabled Avg@4 by **−1.95 [−3.78, −0.20]** for Clean Solution and **−2.15 [−3.97, −0.39]** for Key Points versus genuine references; both pass within-family Holm correction. | Table 2; Appendix E.1 |
| Diagnostic changes do not guarantee behavioral gains | Excluding or downweighting sampled reconsideration-marker loss produces no accuracy gain at step 100 and barely changes marker use. For long Full Trace rollouts, broader/distributed loss coverage has no significant advantage at any matched tested checkpoint. | Table 2; Figure 5 |
| Reference rankings depend on inference mode | SmolLM3’s three-seed, step-50 Full Trace-minus-reference-free contrast changes from **+2.00 [0.82, 3.17] Avg@4** with thinking enabled to **−10.63 [−13.19, −8.07]** under direct-response evaluation. | Table 6 |

These results establish that gains over base and gains attributable to references are different quantities. They support the authors’ interpretation that training can improve access to existing reasoning through parameters shared across inference modes, but do not establish that mechanism causally. Intervals containing zero do not establish equivalence (Appendix A.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evidence concerns short-run LoRA training on mathematics in two model families; it does not establish broader reasoning or safety benefits (§7; Ethics Statement).
- **Confounded training-mode comparison:** Thinking-enabled training simultaneously changes reasoning mode, rollout length, and supervised fraction. The experiment cannot isolate which change causes degradation (§3.2).
- **Limited replication:** Several views and interventions use one training seed. Main bootstrap intervals condition on observed seeds; seed variability is checked separately for selected contrasts (Appendix A.4; Tables 1–2, 10).
- **Dataset and diagnostic selection:** Experimental splits balance model-relative success bands and require privileged-teacher recoverability for zero-success problems. Profiles exclude short completions and require mixed correctness outcomes for alignment, so their populations differ across modes (Appendices B.3, D.1).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** Generation and fidelity review use the same model and may share blind spots; fidelity review does not certify proofs. Reconsideration detection is lexical, and saved-prefix grading is not evaluation of newly generated budget-constrained answers (Appendices B.2, A.2–A.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Answer matching does not isolate representation independently of wording, detail, and length, which vary together. Likewise, other-problem substitutions change both reasoning and answer content (§2.3; Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions
1. **What causes long-rollout degradation?** Mode, horizon, and supervision coverage change together. A factorial experiment varying these independently, with matched checkpoints and multiple seeds, could separate their effects (§3.2).
2. **Can references adapt productively to the student?** Reference benefits and alignment change during training, but references remain fixed. Compare attempt-conditioned reference selection against fixed views and reference-free training under matched budgets (§7).
3. **Do reference benefits generalize beyond this setting?** The evidence is restricted to short-run mathematical LoRA training. Repeat matched reference-free comparisons on other tasks, model scales, and longer schedules (§7).
4. **Which diagnostics predict useful parameter updates?** Aggregate alignment and KL can imply opposite transfer in the paper’s counterexample. Test whether diagnostics incorporating parameter sensitivity better predict held-out gains across interventions (Appendix D.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons; **medium** in the proposed shared-parameter explanation.

The supplied text does not expose the actual code or dataset release URLs, provide row-level outputs directly, or fully specify the answer-grading implementation. It also lacks independent human proof audits and experiments isolating rollout mode from horizon and loss coverage.
