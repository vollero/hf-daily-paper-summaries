# Don&#x27;t Mask the Environment: Observation Supervision Changes How Agents Explore Under RL

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20715) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20715)


## One-sentence takeaway
Supervising environment observations alongside actions during SFT improves subsequent GRPO performance in the tested terminal agents, with stronger repeated-sampling coverage but reduced single-attempt reliability at 8B (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Standard agent SFT treats environment feedback as context while masking it from the prediction loss. The authors ask whether this discards useful preparation for later RL: similar immediate task performance may conceal different capacities to predict action consequences and explore alternatives (§1).

The practical question is whether existing trajectories can provide a better RL initialization without collecting additional data or changing the model.

## Approach and contributions
- **Loss-mask change:** ActObs jointly predicts action and observation tokens, with prompts masked. A weight λ controls observation supervision; the default λ=1 weights both token types equally, with loss normalized by their weighted token count. It adds no data, parameters, sequence tokens, or forward passes (§2, Eq. 1).
- **Controlled downstream comparison:** The authors train Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B on the same terminal trajectories, then apply a common GRPO recipe. Controls include action-only SFT, sequential observation-then-action training, observation shuffling, and observation-aware RL through ECHO (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Mechanistic evidence:** Gradient, prediction-loss, entropy, and policy-displacement probes support the authors’ explanation that joint supervision preserves consequence prediction and changes subsequent exploration. These are supporting diagnostics, not a definitive causal decomposition (§§4–5).

## Evidence and main results
All scores below are percentages; ± denotes the paper’s one bootstrap standard error.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger 4B post-GRPO results | Terminal-Bench: ActObs versus ActionSFT achieves pass@1 **7.2±0.4 vs 5.6±0.4**, and pass@16 **19.1±1.4 vs 18.0±1.4**; higher point estimates at every reported k. Evaluation: 89 tasks, 16 attempts each. | Tables 1, 3 |
| 8B trades reliability for coverage | Same benchmark/budget: pass@1 **11.0±0.5 vs 12.3±0.5**, but pass@16 **27.0±1.3 vs 23.6±1.2**; **24 vs 21** tasks solved. | Tables 1, 2 |
| Cross-domain gains after GRPO | aider-polyglot, 225 tasks with four attempts each: 4B pass@1 **13.9±0.7 vs 9.7±0.6**, pass@4 **25.3±1.0 vs 20.4±0.9**. At 8B: **14.2±0.8 vs 12.7±0.8**, and **28.9±1.2 vs 28.4±1.3**, respectively. | Table 1; §3.3 |
| Sequential supervision does not reproduce the high-budget benefit | Terminal-Bench post-GRPO pass@16, ActObs versus Obs→Act: **19.1 vs 16.9** at 4B and **27.0 vs 23.6** at 8B; 16 attempts per task. | Table 1 |
| Observation-aware RL has mixed outcomes | On Terminal-Bench, ActObs initialization leads or ties ActionSFT under ECHO at seven of eight reported scale/budget combinations, but loses at 8B pass@16. Code-editing ECHO results are also mixed. | Table 1; §3.2 |
| Prediction and gradient probes support the proposed mechanism | On 256 held-out trajectories, gradient cosine falls from **0.83** to the empirical noise floor within **10–20 SFT steps**; observation/action gradient norm ratio reaches approximately **41** under ActionSFT versus **0.5** under ActObs. Observation prediction improves beyond the fixed wrapper. | Fig. 5; Fig. 6 |
| Entropy alone does not explain success | At 8B, post-GRPO self-entropy is **0.779 vs 0.716**, and fixed-state entropy **0.755 vs 0.719**. Temperature-matching ActionSFT adds no solved tasks and leaves the pass@16 gap intact. | Table 7; §4.2 |

The evidence supports a beneficial initialization effect under these training and evaluation settings. It does not establish universal superiority, statistical significance for every difference, or that preserved consequence prediction alone causes the gains. Behavioral analyses locate the advantage mainly in successes spread across more tasks, rather than more distinct strategies within already-solved tasks (Appendix F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Training covers terminal agents, two sizes of one model family, and one demonstration teacher. Code-editing transfer does not establish generality to web, GUI, embodied, or broader coding environments (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated scaffold mismatch:** SFT, RL, and evaluation differ in formatting, context construction, shell persistence, and execution/scoring behavior. Within-stage comparisons are controlled, but absolute scores and stage-to-stage changes may depend on these differences (Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Bootstrap errors resample attempts within fixed tasks; they do not measure training-seed variability or uncertainty over new task populations. Independent training-seed replication is not reported (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Shuffled-observation and unrelated-log controls are reported before RL, leaving their downstream effects unresolved. Entropy and gradient associations do not isolate causal mediation (Table 2; §§4–5).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** Table 4’s direct differences do not always match displayed endpoint subtraction: for example, it reports a 4B post-GRPO pass@1 gain of 1.5 points, while Table 1 gives 7.2−5.6=1.6. Unreported precision could explain this, but the text does not clarify it.
- **Reproducibility:** Code, data, per-task outcomes, and analysis artifacts are promised upon acceptance rather than supplied as released assets (Appendix I).

## Open research questions
- **Does the effect generalize across model families and observation modalities?** Current evidence is restricted to Qwen3 and terminal-text training. Repeat matched-mask experiments with another model family and web or GUI observations.
- **Does consequence prediction causally drive downstream coverage?** Existing diagnostics and SFT controls leave alternative explanations open. Run GRPO from paired, shuffled, unrelated-log, and wrapper-only supervision checkpoints with matched budgets and multiple seeds.
- **How should observation weight depend on deployment sampling budget?** The 8B sweep shows a reliability–coverage tradeoff, with intermediate settings evaluated using fewer attempts. Repeat the sweep with equal attempt counts and select λ on separate validation tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main comparative results and method clearly; causal interpretation and broader generalization remain less certain.

Material missing information includes independent training-seed variability, released implementation and raw outcomes, downstream RL results for several diagnostic controls, and clarification of small numerical inconsistencies between endpoint and difference tables.
