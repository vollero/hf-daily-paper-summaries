# Reflect, Revise, Reuse: Training-Free Skill Evolution for GUI Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-18.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17653) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17653)


## One-sentence takeaway
EvoSkill-GUI improves reported mobile and desktop task success by revising reusable skill files without model training, although inconsistencies in supervision and evaluation reporting limit confidence in the precise gains.

## Problem and motivation
GUI workflows encounter changing layouts, pop-ups, delayed loading, and stale accessibility information. The authors argue that static procedural skills cannot adequately adapt, while monolithic documents make targeted corrections difficult and leave useful failure knowledge trapped in individual trajectories (§1).

The proposed gap is **deployment-time revision of GUI skill artifacts**, rather than updating model weights or merely retrieving stored experience (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Structured skill packages:** Separate metadata, plans, backup localization, recovery rules, accessibility utilities, and failure examples. Restricted file tools support localized, inspectable edits (§3.2).
- **Reflect–revise loop:** The executor edits skills during interaction; following failure, the same backbone in a separate critic session diagnoses observable execution evidence, and the executor translates feedback into file changes. The stated protocol excludes skills, executor reasoning, and ground truth from critic inputs (§3.3).
- **Persistent retrieval and reuse:** Metadata scoring combines token overlap, application/keyword bonuses, and a divergence penalty. Matching tasks reuse packages; unmatched tasks trigger generation (§3.4).
- **Empirical contribution:** Evaluations across three GUI benchmarks, component ablations, a repeated-sampling comparison, and held-out reuse experiments support the combined framework (§4; Appendices A.2–A.5). Its distinctive contribution is this GUI-specific integration; the text does not establish that reflection or skill storage individually are new.

Reported differences below are **percentage points**, not relative percentage improvements.

| Related-task reuse helps | AndroidWorld, sequential sets of 116 tasks each: seed 30 success **68.1%→70.7%**, reuse **37.9%**; seed 42 success **55.2%→61.2%**, reuse **100%**. Backbone is not identified in this result table. | Tables 2, 7; §4.4 |
| Evolution exceeds repeated sampling in the reported budget comparison | Qwen3.6-Plus on MobileWorld: three-round EvoSkill-GUI **69.5%**, **94.75M tokens**; baseline pass@3 **62.8%**, **103.00M tokens**. | §4.4; Table 13 |
| Package structure and revision matter | MobileWorld: structured versus single-file success **69.52% vs 66.67%**. Qwen3.6-Plus: full system **69.52%**, without instant revision **62.86%**, without information isolation **60.95%**. | Tables 3–4 |
| Held-out results support limited transfer | Library built from 87 MobileWorld tasks; held-out success **22/30 (73.3%)** versus pass@3 **12/30 (40.0%)**. Among 16 retrieval cases, successes were **12/16 vs 7/16**; 15 retrieved skills were judged appropriate. | A.2, Table 10 |
| Critic judgments remain fallible | Manual audit across 56 failed tasks: **115/143 judgments correct (80.4%)**; at least four false success judgments prematurely stopped evolution. | A.3, Table 11 |

These results support improved aggregate performance within the reported settings, including evidence beyond simple retries. They do not establish universal domain improvements or broad transfer to unseen applications. The held-out comparison also changes the entire framework, so it does not isolate the causal contribution of retrieval alone.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated limitations:** Perception and diagnosis errors can produce harmful edits; no formal verifier vetoes regressions. Benchmark environments underrepresent real interface changes, personalization, latency, and privacy-sensitive states. AndroidWorld reuse concerns parameterized variants of existing task families. Failed rollouts add critic and revision calls, and skill guidance can overcomplicate simple tasks (Limitations; A.4).

**Analyst observations from the supplied text:**

- **Supervision is ambiguous:** The formal protocol excludes ground truth, but the critic template conditionally accepts an `oracle_outcome`, and a case-study failure file explicitly contains the correct answer. Whether these inputs were used during evaluation is unspecified (§3.3; A.8.3; A.6.4, p.20).
- **Round reporting conflicts:** The five-round analysis places Qwen3.6-Plus at 68.6% after three rounds and 69.5% after five; Table 13 labels 69.5% as three-round performance (§4.4; Table 13).
- **Reuse accounting conflicts:** Table 2 reports complete second-phase reuse, while the task-stream narrative reports further library growth and a 56.1% reuse ratio without reconciling definitions (§4.4).
- **Verification status is unclear:** Algorithm 1 saves revised packages after exhausting unsuccessful iterations, complicating claims that library entries represent verified knowledge (§3.4; A.7).
- **Uncertainty and reproducibility remain limited:** No aggregate confidence intervals are reported. The twelve-case retrieval ablation selects successful reuse cases, although the held-out analysis partly addresses this selection issue. The relationship between MobileWorld task counts and excluded tasks is not fully explained (Tables 5, 10, 13; A.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer survive genuinely new applications?** Current reuse evidence emphasizes related tasks. Next experiment: construct libraries on disjoint application categories and evaluate frozen versus editable reuse on unseen categories.
- **Do gains persist under demonstrably oracle-free feedback?** Templates leave supervision ambiguous. Next experiment: audit all critic and revision inputs, remove environment verdicts and answer labels, and rerun the budget-matched comparison.
- **Can harmful edits be detected before reuse?** There is no formal edit veto, and critic false positives occur. Next experiment: compare immediate registration with replay-based regression checks and rollback.
- **What is the reproducible cost–accuracy curve?** Round counts conflict and aggregate latency is unavailable. Next experiment: report success, tokens, and elapsed time after every iteration under identical task sets and stopping rules.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported results are detailed, but internal inconsistencies weaken precise causal and reproducibility claims.

Material missing information includes actual oracle-input usage, reconciled round and reuse accounting, exact MobileWorld task selection, AndroidWorld and held-out evaluation backbones, aggregate run variability, annotation procedures, and release contents beyond the code-availability claim.
