# Schrödinger&#x27;s Code Repository: Have LLMs Learned SWE-bench or Memorized It?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.27891) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27891)


## One-sentence takeaway

SchrodingerRepo’s repository transformations reduce coding-agent success and increase exploration costs, demonstrating sensitivity to familiar representations while leaving memorization’s causal contribution unresolved (§V).

## Problem and motivation

Static, public code repositories make benchmark success difficult to separate from familiarity with repository names, structure, or previously encountered fixes. The authors argue that newer-instance benchmarks sacrifice coverage, while fixed perturbations can themselves become training artifacts; they propose varying repository presentation at evaluation time while preserving the underlying task (§I, §VII).

## Approach and contributions

- **Seeded repository views:** SchrodingerRepo combines problem-statement reconstruction, reversible namespace mapping, dependency-aware definition reordering, and functionality-preserving local rewrites. Namespace changes translate observations and actions; reordering and rewriting modify working repository state (§III-B–E).
- **Validation and submission recovery:** Human review checks task preservation; executable tests require previously passing behavior to remain passing and the target bug to remain unfixed. Final submissions are reconstructed against the original repository (§III-F–G).
- **Representation-sensitivity study:** Joint and individual transformations measure success, interaction cost, and action distributions across issue resolution and repository QA. The authors announce code and data availability in the abstract; availability was not independently checked.

Levels 3–4 target gold-patch-related files or regions, rather than entire repositories. Evaluation uses mini-swe-agent, temperature 0, a 250-action limit, and averages transformed evaluations across three seeded views (§IV-A, §IV-C).

## Evidence and main results

Numbers below come from the supplied narrative; Tables I–III appear only as captions and references.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Human judgments suggest task-specific recall | On SWE-bench Verified, each evaluated model reportedly shows leakage evidence on **>65%** of instances and patch/test-level recall on **>18%**, without repository access during elicitation. | §II; Fig. 1 |
| Full transformations reduce issue-resolution success | SWE-bench Verified Pass@1: GPT-5.4-mini **46.8%→35.6%**; DeepSeek-v4-Flash **72.8%→66.8%**; GPT 5.1 **44.6%→36.2%**. Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite: **56.7%→42.3%**, evaluated on **300 instances selected for strongest leakage evidence**, versus the benchmark’s 500-instance setting for the other models. Authors report **p<0.05**. | §IV-A; §V-A; Table I referenced |
| Namespace mapping is the strongest individual transformation | On SWE-bench Verified, Pass@1 drops **7.4, 6.4, and 6.0 percentage points** for GPT 5.1, GPT-5.4-mini, and DeepSeek-v4-Flash, respectively; average actions rise **63.3%, 32.6%, and 112.4%**. Reported success-rate significance: **p<0.01**. | §V-A; Table I referenced |
| Additional actions mostly support exploration | Under full transformations on SWE-bench Verified, navigate/search/read/probe comprise **83.6%** of additional actions for DeepSeek-v4-Flash and **81.6%** for GPT-5.4-mini. | §V-B; Fig. 3 |
| QA quality falls while interactions increase | SWE-QA: **144 instances**, 48 each from Conan, Reflex, and Streamlink. GPT-5.4-mini Average Score **70.35→65.71**, average actions **6.28→7.42**; DeepSeek-v4-Flash score **72.97→72.42**, actions **24.49→35.02**. | §V-C; Table II referenced |
| Temporal control preserves aggregate success | On **110 March 2026 SWE-rebench instances**, described as post-release for GPT-5.4-mini, its Pass@1 remains **17.27%** under full transformations; average actions rise **15.82→17.11**, input tokens **22.01%**, and output tokens **15.58%**. | §V-D; Table III referenced |

The evidence establishes representation sensitivity and increased exploration in the tested settings. The authors interpret this as partial reliance on memorized cues. **Analyst inference:** these comparisons do not uniquely distinguish memorization from useful naming semantics or general familiarity with software conventions; unchanged aggregate success in one temporal control does not establish unchanged difficulty universally.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Primarily Python, open-source benchmarks and command-line tools; other languages and IDE/language-server interfaces remain untested (§VI).
- **Analyst inference—selection and coverage:** Gemini’s leakage-enriched subset limits cross-model comparison. Gold-patch-guided transformations limit conclusions about repository-wide perturbations (§IV-A, §IV-C).
- **Analyst inference—semantic preservation:** Tests and human inspection support preservation but do not prove equivalence for every behavior. Joint-transformation validation is not separately detailed (§III-F).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** The temporal control reports one model and one split; no controlled training-exposure experiment or matched naming-readability control is supplied (§V-D).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** §V-C’s DeepSeek QA scores imply a **0.55-point** decrease, whereas §VIII gives a **0.75–4.64-point** range. The supplied text does not resolve this discrepancy.

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gap is memorization rather than naming utility?** The transformations change both familiarity and lexical cues. Next experiment: compare agents with controlled repository exposure using transformations matched for identifier informativeness.
2. **Can additional exploration recover lost success?** Trajectories show association, not a causal benefit from exploration (§V-B). Next experiment: vary exploration policy and action budget within the same model and transformed instances.
3. **Does robustness generalize beyond this implementation?** Language and tool coverage are explicitly limited (§VI). Next experiment: replicate paired evaluations in another programming ecosystem with consistent IDE or language-server translation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The narrative supports the main robustness findings, but stronger causal conclusions are underdetermined.

Material missing information includes full numerical tables and figure contents, statistical test definitions and confidence intervals, seed-level variability, recall-reviewer agreement, transformation prompts and model identities, transformation rejection rates, and detailed adaptation of Levels 3–4 to SWE-QA.
