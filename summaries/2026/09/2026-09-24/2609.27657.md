# FLEET: From Logits Entropy to Enhanced Trajectories in Text Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.27657) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27657)


## One-sentence takeaway
FLEET uses memory of evaluated generation paths to guide decoding, improving reported coding accuracy at a fixed candidate budget, although reward-model selection limits practical gains and the claimed speedup lacks supporting timing details.

## Problem and motivation
Repeated temperature sampling cannot use feedback from earlier completions, so it can revisit unsuccessful paths and waste additional inference effort. The authors argue that adaptive sampling addresses where to introduce variation but still lacks action-specific memory of success and failure (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **VectorDSU:** An online clustering structure maps intermediate hidden states to compact representatives, retaining action, transition, reward, and visit information without storing every state vector (§3.1).
- **Memory-guided decoding:** Joint entropy and varentropy thresholds identify branching points using intermediate-layer Logit Lens distributions. A modified pUCT objective scores actions from historical rewards and model priors; logit penalties discourage evaluated alternatives that do not maximize this objective. Experiments use greedy decoding (§§3–3.2).
- **Calibration and exploration schedule:** Layer scoring, distribution clustering, and similarity–divergence measurements initialize principal hyperparameters. Thresholds relax during search to increase the fraction of states explored (§§3.2–3.3).

The contribution combines these components into a decoding framework; Logit Lens and pUCT are explicitly adopted techniques.

## Evidence and main results

All results below use Llama 3.2-3B; the baseline is temperature sampling tuned through Bayesian search (§4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Coding improves with ground-truth feedback | LiveCodeBench Pass@32: baseline **59.9%**, FLEET **66.2%**; authors report **14 additional solved tasks** among **222 easy tasks**. | §§4, 4.1.1, 5; Table 4.2 discussion |
| Math improvement is modest near saturation | GSM8K ground-truth Pass@32: baseline **97.27%**, FLEET **97.80%**; **7 additional solved tasks**. | §§4.1.1–4.1.2 |
| ORM selection helps coding but hurts math relative to baseline | At 32 candidates, LiveCodeBench selected accuracy rises from **19.36% (43 tasks)** to **25.22% (56 tasks)**; GSM8K falls from **0.8544** to **0.8362**. | §4.1.2; Table 4.3 discussion |
| Correct candidates substantially exceed ORM-selected successes | ORM-guided FLEET candidate-pool ground-truth Pass@32 is **0.6937** on LiveCodeBench and **0.9780 (1,290 tasks)** on GSM8K. These are oracle pool results, not ORM-selected accuracy. | §4.1.2 |
| Speedup is asserted but insufficiently documented | Abstract claims equal baseline accuracy with **3× speedup**; the supplied text does not identify the associated benchmark, accuracy target, or timing protocol. | Abstract |

The evidence supports better candidate discovery in the reported ground-truth settings, with mixed results when an ORM selects the final answer. It contradicts the introduction’s blanket claim of always higher accuracy under equal budget. Equal candidate counts do not establish equal compute or wall-clock cost.

For intermediate budgets, FLEET uses deterministic prefix success, whereas sampling uses a combinatorial estimator over its candidate pool (§4); these different curve constructions should be retained when comparing scaling behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The implementation requires model patching and internal activations. Reflective reasoning states can be problematic, and outputs sometimes contain gibberish; suggested repair is not evaluated (§3.2).
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers one dense model, two benchmarks, and only easy LiveCodeBench tasks. Cross-session transfer, parallel speedup, and training with trajectory metadata remain untested (§§3.1–4).
- **Analyst inference:** No component ablations establish how much improvement comes from memory, clustering, uncertainty triggers, or penalty choices. No reported repeated-run uncertainty establishes robustness to sampling and few-shot variation.
- **Analyst inference:** Similarity-based cluster assignment is an empirical proxy for equivalent search states. Historical members need not remain similar to their representative, leaving reward-transfer errors within clusters insufficiently assessed (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The candidate-pool results do not establish absence of reward hacking; the authors’ interpretation exceeds what aggregate correctness comparisons alone demonstrate (§4.1.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which components drive the gains?** The supplied evaluation lacks ablations. Compare full FLEET against variants without state merging, varentropy gating, or reward-dependent scoring under matched budgets.
- **Does candidate efficiency translate into runtime efficiency?** Timing evidence for the speedup claim is missing. Measure end-to-end time, generated tokens, evaluator cost, and peak memory at matched accuracy.
- **Can better reward ranking recover discovered solutions?** ORM-selected accuracy trails candidate-pool correctness (§4.1.2). Hold generated pools fixed and compare alternative evaluators against ground truth.
- **Does FLEET generalize to reflection-heavy models and harder tasks?** Current evaluation is narrow and the authors flag reflective-state failures. Repeat calibrated evaluations on additional architectures and coding difficulty levels.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the method description and headline accuracy comparisons, but not broad efficiency or generalization claims.

Material gaps include the contents of Tables 4.1–4.3, readable plot data, timing and hardware measurements, component ablations, and repeated-run uncertainty. Exact checkpoints, software versions, calibration details, and the coding-task filter/list are deferred partly to the linked repository and are unavailable in the supplied text.
