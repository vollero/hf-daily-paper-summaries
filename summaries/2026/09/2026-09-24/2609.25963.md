# GeoPair: Geometry-Preserving Cross-Layer Factorization for Training-Free Transformer Compression

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25963) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25963)


## One-sentence takeaway
GeoPair combines calibration-aware shared dictionaries, graph-based projection pairing, and sparse coefficients to improve reported compression–accuracy trade-offs without fine-tuning, although deployment gains and some theoretical guarantees remain insufficiently established.

## Problem and motivation
Independent layer compression misses redundancy across transformer projections. Existing sharing methods, the authors argue, restrict pairing to adjacent layers and average activation covariances, potentially losing nonlocal similarities and distorting each layer’s calibration geometry (§§1–2). GeoPair seeks to exploit shared structure while preserving separate activation-weighted reconstruction objectives.

## Approach and contributions
- **Geometry-aware dictionary sharing:** Factor two compatible projections as a shared dictionary with separate coefficients, retaining individual whitening transforms. Alternating updates use weighted least squares and a generalized Sylvester solver (§3.2).
- **Optimized pairing:** Match projections with the same input dimension using Blossom matching over a whitened Frobenius submatrix distance. Pairing can cross depths and projection types; optimality concerns the chosen surrogate, not downstream accuracy (§3.4).
- **Sparse coefficients:** Integrate column-wise Hard Thresholding Pursuit with masked conjugate-gradient refinement. The authors claim convergence to a block-stationary point under stated conditions (§3.3; Appendix A.2).

“Training-free” means no backpropagation-based fine-tuning; calibration and iterative factor optimization are still required (§3.1).

These results support improved aggregate accuracy against the listed compression baselines. They do not establish universal superiority: Table 6, for example, gives CoSpaDi better Lambada perplexity than GeoPair at CR=0.3. Video alignment scores do not establish temporal or perceptual quality, and compression runtime does not establish inference acceleration.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The closed-form approach is restricted to pairs; larger groups require alternative numerical methods. Optimization reaches local stationarity, and premature HTP truncation can stall progress (§6; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—guarantees need clarification:** Regularized Gram updates differ from the displayed unregularized subproblem, and Appendix A.2 asserts sufficient descent for truncated HTP without establishing it for every implemented update. The matching formulation also does not justify why merely positive edge weights guarantee maximum cardinality (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Analyst inference—reporting inconsistencies:** Table 4 labels its grouping results KS=2.0, while matching values appear under KS=2.5 in Table 5. Table 13’s baseline task scores duplicate Table 1, but its reported average differs. Distance-computation complexity also differs between §3.4 and Appendix B.
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Calibration and seed studies concentrate on the small Llama model. Audio and video each use one model and automatic metrics; the authors explicitly call audio a proof of concept. No inference latency, throughput, or measured compressed storage accounting is supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Can larger sharing groups improve the trade-off?** Pairwise restriction remains unresolved (§6). Compare iterative multi-layer dictionary updates against pairs at matched storage and preprocessing budgets.
- **Do reported parameter reductions improve deployment efficiency?** Only compression-time measurements are supplied (Appendix D.6). Measure serialized size, peak memory, latency, and throughput using the actual sparse factors.
- **How robust is calibration across models and domains?** Existing calibration ablations focus on Llama3.2 1B (Appendices D.3–D.4). Repeat matched-budget calibration-source experiments on larger language models and generative models.
- **Does the implemented optimizer satisfy the claimed descent conditions?** Appendix A.2 leaves the practical truncated-update argument incomplete. Track the precise regularized objective after every block update while varying HTP/CG budgets and normalization.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method summary and reported comparisons, but internal inconsistencies weaken stronger conclusions.
