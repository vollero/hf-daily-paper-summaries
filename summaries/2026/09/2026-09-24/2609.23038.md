# Spatial-Interactor: Learning Spatial Reasoning through Interaction with the Observable Physical World

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23038) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23038)


## One-sentence takeaway
Spatial-Interactor improves spatial reasoning across four Qwen VLM backbones using interaction-derived supervision and training-only transition traces, with matched ablations showing a smaller additional benefit from privileged on-policy distillation beyond supervised training (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Dynamic spatial reasoning requires models to recognize local changes and accumulate them across viewpoints and time. The authors argue that conventional spatial QA provides insufficient supervision for these transitions. Their diagnostics show weak sensitivity to shuffled video frames and improved camera-displacement scores when explicit transition descriptions are supplied (§1, Fig. 2).

These observations motivate transition-based training, although the comparison between local SAT-Real questions and long-horizon VSTI questions also changes the benchmark and task, so it does not isolate trajectory length.

## Approach and contributions
- **Interaction curriculum:** L1 covers external-world changes under approximately stable viewpoints; L2 covers ego-motion in approximately stable environments; L3 integrates camera transitions over complete trajectories (§2.2).
- **LSI-108K dataset:** Contains 107,518 QA records from simulated interactions, real camera trajectories, and robot recordings. Targets come from actions or geometry, deterministic templates generate questions, and a frozen VLM filters visual answerability without changing targets (Appendix B, Table 6).
- **Two-stage training:** SFT combines local-transition examples with public spatial QA. Long-horizon training adds privileged on-policy distillation to GRPO: a pre-update policy snapshot receives question-independent segment descriptions and supervises the student on its own reasoning prefixes. Answer tokens are excluded from distillation; traces are absent at inference (§§2.3–2.4, Appendix C).

The contribution is this spatial curriculum, dataset, and application of privileged transition supervision; the authors explicitly adopt an existing general on-policy distillation framework (Appendix A.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad benchmark gains | Across four backbones, Overall improves **16.4–25.0 points** over corresponding Base models; the 8B variant reaches **65.9**. Overall averages four benchmark scores using categorical accuracy and numerical MRA; videos use 32 frames. | Table 1; §3.1 |
| Transfer to excluded benchmarks | Mean accuracy across MMSI, ViewSpatial, SAT-Real, and SAT-Syn improves **4.3–10.6 points** over Base; OPD adds **1.0–1.4 points** beyond SFT. | Table 2 |
| Local data adds gains beyond external QA | Qwen2.5-VL-7B Overall: **35.1 Base → 56.0 external-data SFT → 58.4 full SFT**. | Table 3 |
| Matched OPD advantage | From identical full-SFT initialization, GRPO reaches **59.2** Overall versus **60.1** for OPD; OPD exceeds GRPO by **5.1 points** on Route Planning accuracy and **3.1 points** on Camera Displacement numerical score. | Table 3; §§3.1, 3.4 |
| Ordered traces help a frozen model | On **839 camera-displacement questions from 310 videos**, Qwen2.5-VL-7B SFT scores **32.6** without traces, **35.4** with four ordered segments, **33.8** with shuffled segments, and **35.3** with eight ordered segments. | Fig. 9; Appendix D.1 |
| Closed-loop gains remain modest | WalkerBench Standard-100 success: **7.0% → 14.0%**, with actions/task **17.71 → 6.52**. Eight ESI categories, 30 matched questions each: success **32.9% → 41.2%**, rounds among solved episodes **12.34 → 10.55**. | Tables 4–5 |
| Broader interaction results are mixed | Across 27 ESI categories, macro success rises **26.3% → 31.1%**, but Long-Term Navigation falls **20.0% → 6.7%** and Traversable Passage **40.0% → 20.0%**. | Table 7 |

The results support improved benchmark performance and an incremental benefit from OPD under the reported matched settings. They do not establish a faithful internal spatial-state representation or reliable general embodied competence. Most of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B Overall improvement already appears after external-data SFT; the full Base-to-final gain cannot be attributed solely to the proposed interaction supervision.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified future directions:** Adaptive segmentation and more diverse real interactions remain unexplored (§4).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Adding L1/L2 increases training data; no equal-size alternative-data control is reported. OPD’s matched comparison tests privileged conditioning and distillation together, rather than separating their effects (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference—interaction scope:** ESI subsets are selected from completed paired runs, and two categories lack valid paired runs. This limits representativeness; broader results include regressions (Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency interpretation:** ESI step counts condition on success, while WalkerBench counts actions per task. Neither alone establishes improved action efficiency on equivalent successful episodes (Tables 4–5).
- **Reporting gaps:** No repeated-run uncertainty is reported. Section 3.1 uses 82,596 local SFT examples, whereas Appendix B.4 lists 84,596 local records in the full dataset; the selection accounting is unexplained.

## Open research questions
- **Does transition supervision outperform equally sized additional spatial QA?** The data-volume confound remains; compare matched example counts and training compute.
- **How robust is OPD to inaccurate traces?** Reported perturbations concern inference-time trace order and granularity; train with controlled trace errors and different annotators.
- **Does the method handle simultaneous object and camera motion?** L1/L2 approximately isolate these changes; evaluate controlled scenes varying both independently.
- **Do interaction gains generalize across complete task distributions?** Diagnostic ESI subsets show regressions; run complete paired evaluations with repeated trials and explicit failure accounting.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and reported comparisons, but causal and generalization claims require caution.

Missing information includes annotator/verifier identities, annotation-error audits, repeated-run variability, complete dataset split accounting, wall-clock and annotation costs, and explicit dataset/checkpoint release details.
