# Knowledge Pull Requests for Continual Document Authoring

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26634) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26634)


## One-sentence takeaway
Knowledge Pull Requests improve source-information coverage in Wikipedia revisions and report updates by filtering and routing explicit claims before rewriting, but their proposed human-review and computational-efficiency benefits remain untested.

## Problem and motivation
Maintained documents must incorporate new information while preserving useful content and exposing disagreements. The authors argue that direct rewriting obscures which facts changed, while regeneration discards the existing document; ordinary text diffs reveal wording changes without explicitly identifying knowledge changes (§1).

The paper distinguishes **revision**—learning more about an unchanged world—from **update**, where sources reflect a changing world, and evaluates both (§§1, 5–6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Knowledge-level change artifact:** A KPR produces a ChangeLog containing a claim proposal, including flagged conflicts, alongside a document diff. This separates proposed factual changes from their textual realization (§3).
- **Three-stage implementation:** An LLM extracts atomic claims, filters covered or irrelevant claims, flags contradictions, routes eligible claims to sections, and rewrites affected or newly created sections. Non-English sources are decomposed directly into English claims (§4).
- **Comparative evaluation:** The authors test multilingual Wikipedia revision and three constructed RAGTIME update settings against raw-text rewriting, unfiltered-claim rewriting where feasible, and regeneration (§§5–6).

The pipeline uses Qwen3.5-27B. Experiments have no human reviewers: flagged conflicts are withheld, not adjudicated (§4.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| KPR improves Wikipedia information coverage and precision | On 599 articles with sources spanning 49 non-English languages, KPR achieves InfoP **0.878**, retained recall **0.950**, and source recall **0.891**, versus ConClaim **0.853/0.943/0.786** and ConText **0.837/0.946/0.716**. | Table 2; Appendix A |
| Grounded multilingual QA improves, with some English-QA degradation | Across seven models, average multilingual accuracy is **69.2%** with KPR, **58.5%** with ConClaim, **48.4%** with ConText, and **29.5%** with the original English article. English-QA averages are **87.7%, 88.0%, 87.6%, and 92.0%**, respectively. | Table 1 |
| KPR grounding beats the tested search conditions on a selected hard subset | On 100 multilingual questions selected for closed-book failures by dense models, Sol-5.6 answers **62** correctly with KPR, **54** with ConClaim, **46** with ConText, **38** with web search, and **41** with translated-question web search. | Tables 4, 9; Appendix A |
| KPR leads RAGTIME recall, but Scratch has higher precision | KPR InfoP/retained recall/added recall: temporal **0.729/0.709/0.682**; conflict **0.811/0.782/0.571**; balanced **0.841/0.856/0.632**. Both recalls exceed ConText and Scratch in every setting. Scratch precision is higher: **0.882/0.875/0.862**, respectively. | Table 6 |
| Review proxies favor KPR on Wikipedia, but not uniformly on reports | Wikipedia: KPR averages **11.2** contiguous insertion/replacement blocks versus **25.3** for ConClaim and **14.7** for ConText; verbatim preservation is **97.3%/94.6%/96.9%**. RAGTIME: KPR has **20.5** blocks and **96.3%** preservation versus ConText’s **12.5** and **98.2%**. | Tables 5, 7; Appendix D.2 |

These results support improved information integration under the tested settings, rather than uniform superiority on every metric. Table 6 contradicts the broad wording in §6.1 that KPR leads precision among rewrites: Scratch scores higher in all three settings.

Metric scope matters: Wikipedia InfoP evaluates added sentences against non-English sources, whereas RAGTIME InfoP evaluates the updated report against both rounds’ sources. RAGTIME retained recall measures coverage of round-1 nuggets, including facts missing from the original report; it is not pure preservation of existing text. Wikipedia source recall is defined over non-English source claims without an explicit novelty restriction in Appendix D.1.

The evidence does not establish factual truth independently of sources, actual reviewer savings, total computational savings, or general superiority over search.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Every pipeline stage requires LLM computation; output-size and edit-shape metrics do not measure total compute. Human review and conflict adjudication are unevaluated (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Qwen3.5-27B powers both the authoring pipeline and the MiRAGE support judge, creating possible correlated errors. QA gold answers also derive from extracted claims (§4.2; Appendices A, D).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** ConClaim provides a useful combined-filtering ablation on Wikipedia, but separate coverage/conflict/relevance ablations are absent. ConClaim is omitted on RAGTIME because its inputs exceed the context window (§6).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** RAGTIME uses relevance judgments instead of retrieval, constructed two-round splits, and imposed section outlines. Its conflict split includes complementary AND-nugget evidence as well as conflicting OR answers (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—implementation ambiguity:** The method describes classification against cached main-document claims, but Figure 12 supplies the full article text. Figure 9 also includes an example invoking outside knowledge despite its source-based judging instructions; the impact is unmeasured.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or significance tests are reported, and the selected hard-QA subset cannot establish broad search performance.

## Open research questions
1. **Does a ChangeLog improve human review?** No reviewers participated. A randomized editor study could compare review time and factual-error detection for ChangeLogs versus text diffs.
2. **Can KPR safely replace outdated facts?** Withholding conflicts leaves adjudication unresolved. Test human-assisted or source-trust-based resolution against withholding on independently labeled updates.
3. **Do benefits persist over many updates with realistic retrieval?** RAGTIME supplies relevant documents in two rounds. Run longer update sequences with noisy retrieval and measure error accumulation, coverage, and document growth.
4. **Which stages justify their computational cost?** Total compute and individual filtering effects are unreported. Ablate each filter and compare LLM versus lightweight classification/routing while measuring quality, latency, and total token use.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied tables substantiate improved coverage, but broader claims about reviewability, efficiency, and search require stronger evidence.

Material missing information includes RAGTIME topic/report counts, statistical uncertainty, per-language results, conflict-detection accuracy, human-review outcomes, full computational costs, detailed search configuration, and confirmation of repository contents and evaluation-asset release.
