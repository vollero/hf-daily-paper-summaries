# The Past Frames the Future: Memory for Autoregressive Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28466) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28466)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey organizes memory for autoregressive video generation around persistent historical influence, arguing that reliable long-horizon generation requires accurate, accessible, and revisable state rather than storage capacity alone (§§1–4).

## Problem and motivation
Autoregressive video generators extend sequences while operating with bounded context, storage, and computation. Earlier identities, layouts, motion states, narrative commitments, and intervention effects can leave the active window while remaining relevant to future frames (§§2.3–2.5).

The authors identify fragmented terminology and the absence of a memory-centered taxonomy specific to causal video rollouts as obstacles to comparing designs (§1). **The supplied source is truncated**, so this analysis covers the visible formulation and taxonomy, with limited access to the promised learning and evaluation discussion.

## Approach and contributions
This is a **survey and conceptual framework**, not a newly evaluated video generator. Its stated contributions are:

- **Operational definition and formulation:** Memory persists across outer autoregressive steps and influences generation after its source evidence leaves local context. A common formulation combines local context, persistent state, and external conditions (§§1, 2.4).
- **Representation and function taxonomies:** Four carrier families—visual, implicit state, explicit state, and adaptive parameters—are distinguished from identity, spatial, dynamic, semantic, and causal preservation responsibilities (§§3–4; Tables 2–5).
- **Common lifecycle:** Reading and integration precede generation; writing, updating, and budget management follow it (§2.4, Eqs. 10–15).
- **Learning and evaluation synthesis:** The authors announce a review of training distributions, memory-aware optimization, and tests that distinguish memory from general video consistency. The detailed sections are absent from the supplied excerpt (§1; contents, pp. 2–3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated attribution risk:** Forgetting-like failures can have non-memory causes, so visual consistency alone cannot establish memory effectiveness (§2.5).
- **Author-described design risks:** Stored generated errors can be reused; compression can erase later-relevant evidence; geometric updates can corrupt persistent maps; adaptive weights can retain stale information (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Author-stated scope boundary:** World-action models receive selective coverage, and short-video systems without persistent cross-step memory are excluded (§1, p. 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Without matched budgets and controlled memory interventions, the qualitative taxonomy cannot establish which design best balances quality, latency, and storage.
- **Source limitation:** The truncation removes the detailed operations, learning, evaluation, and future-work sections. Their protocols, supporting comparisons, and qualifications cannot be assessed here.

## Open research questions

- **How can storage failure be separated from retrieval or integration failure?** The survey distinguishes retained evidence from accessible evidence (§3.2.1). A next experiment could compare normal retrieval, oracle retrieval, and memory removal while holding local context and stored content fixed.
- **How should memory reconcile stale observations with valid world changes?** Ordered revision remains a central challenge (§4.5). A next experiment could apply successive reversible interventions, insert observation gaps, and test whether later generation reflects the latest state.
- **Which carrier combinations preserve rare facts under tight budgets?** The survey describes complementary carriers and information loss through consolidation (§3). A next experiment could compare single and hybrid carriers under matched storage and latency budgets, varying the delay before a rare fact becomes relevant.
