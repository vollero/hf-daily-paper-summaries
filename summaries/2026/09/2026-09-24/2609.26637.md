# Capable yet Parsimonious: Extracting and Characterizing Hidden Chain-of-Thought in Frontier Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26637) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26637)


## One-sentence takeaway
A forced-tool protocol elicits useful reasoning traces whose compactness and transferability vary across models, but the evidence does not establish that these traces reveal hidden internal computation.

## Problem and motivation
Closed models typically expose answers or reasoning summaries rather than native reasoning traces, limiting comparisons of how models solve problems. The authors seek an observable proxy for studying reasoning efficiency, structure, and reuse beyond benchmark accuracy, while acknowledging that elicited explanations could be post-hoc rationalizations (§§1, 5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trace elicitation and validation:** FORCED-REASONING requires an initial call to a custom text-only scratchpad tool, replays its content with a fixed acknowledgment, then permits automatic tool selection. Native reasoning is disabled where supported; Astra instead uses low effort. Open-model validation compares accuracy, lexical similarity, and episode structure against observable native traces (§3; Appendices A–B).
- **Comparative characterization:** The authors combine output length, lossless compression, reasoning-activity annotation, and adapted LCoT2Tree analysis. Astra externalizes fewer intermediate operations while retaining a broadly similar activity repertoire (§4; Appendices F, H).
- **Cross-model reuse:** Donor traces supplied as context help recipients unevenly: weaker recipients lose more accuracy with Astra’s compact traces. This tests in-context reuse, not training or distillation (§4.4).

The method builds on earlier tool-channel exposure work; its distinctive emphasis is comparative behavioral measurement (§2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Open-model performance approaches native reasoning | On MATH, mean pass@1 across ten rounds: DeepSeek-V4-Flash **30.5% None / 73.3% Forced / 70.0% Native**; GLM-5.2 **21.4% / 84.3% / 89.9%**. | Appendix A.1; Fig. 6 |
| Closed-model recovery is substantial but incomplete | On 80 MATH problems, Forced versus Native-high accuracy: Opus **85.0% / 86.3%**, Sonnet **81.3% / 77.5%**, Sol **91.3% / 97.5%**, Astra **93.8% / 97.5%**. | Table 1; Appendix C |
| Recovery extends beyond mathematics | On separate 100-question subsets, Astra Forced versus Native-high achieves **32.0% / 35.0%** on HLE and **89.0% / 92.0%** on LCB. | Table 1; Appendix C |
| Tool use matters in the Sol controls | MATH pass@1 is **36.3%** for maximal no-tool prompting, **81.3%** for default forced-tool prompting, and **91.3%** for maximal forced-tool prompting, with native reasoning disabled. | Table 5 |
| Astra has the shortest recorded Forced outputs | Mean MATH non-native completion tokens: Astra **1,752**, Sol **3,925**, Opus **16,283**, Sonnet **27,851**. Counts include tool framing and final answers. Astra also has the highest reported zlib ratio across all three benchmarks. | Table 8; §4.1; Appendix E |
| Astra’s visible trees are narrower | On MATH, median width/depth/nodes: Astra **5/11/27**, Sol **12/11/60**, Opus **22/12/96**, Sonnet **28.5/13/136**. Depth denotes the largest occupied sketch-step index. | Table 3; Appendix F |
| Correct answers inside traces do not guarantee successful reuse | Among recipient errors with Astra traces, the correct answer was explicitly exposed in **15/18** Haiku errors, **11/16** Nano errors, and **11/15** DeepSeek errors. These are conditional error shares, not overall error rates. | Appendix I; Table 10 |

These results support the usefulness of elicited traces and differences in their visible organization. They do **not** establish internal-computation fidelity, computational savings, or a causal effect of compression on transfer. Appendix H further qualifies “compression”: Astra’s units are often comparable in length to Sol’s and Opus’s, with the larger difference being fewer externalized units.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Closed-model native traces are unavailable; behavioral similarity cannot establish identity with internal reasoning (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Transfer comparisons confound compression with correctness, content, and style; distillation is untested (§4.4).
- **Author-stated:** Extraction requires particular API controls and fails on the tested newer Claude configurations; those probes do not establish universal failure rates (§5; Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Model-specific prompts and Astra’s enabled native reasoning complicate attribution of differences solely to model capability. Provider-reported zero tokens do not independently verify absence of internal computation (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Tree and episode findings depend on punctuation-based segmentation and model judges. The reported annotation pilot does not establish tree-assignment reliability or robustness across writing styles (Appendices F, L).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Astra’s main MATH Forced accuracy is 93.8%, while the think-here ablation reports 95.00% and nonzero mean native-token usage. Sol’s HLE no-tool accuracy also differs between Tables 1 and 5. The text does not clearly reconcile these settings or runs.

## Open research questions
1. **Does expanding omitted steps improve weak-recipient reuse?** Compression is not isolated here. Compare original and expanded versions of the same correct traces while preserving conclusions and substantive content.
2. **How closely does elicited text track causal reasoning?** Current validation measures similarity and utility. On open models, intervene on matched native and forced intermediate steps and compare downstream answer changes.
3. **Are structural rankings robust to annotation choices?** Trees depend on segmentation and step assignment. Repeat the analysis with alternative segmentation, independent judges, and manually checked shared reasoning sketches.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the behavioral findings more strongly than claims about hidden reasoning.

Material gaps include exact numerical values from several figure-only analyses, closed-model uncertainty estimates and repeat counts, independent tree-annotation validation, a clear reconciliation of conflicting tables, and an established public release of code and traces.
