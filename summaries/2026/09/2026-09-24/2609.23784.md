# PackLab: A Comprehensive Framework for Developing, Training, and Evaluating MLLMs in Robotic Bin Packing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23784) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23784)


## One-sentence takeaway
PackLab combines physics-based trajectory generation, a supervised multimodal packing policy, and a benchmark, achieving the best average reported packing score while trailing geometric heuristics on hard cases (Table II).

## Problem and motivation
Each placement changes the space available for subsequent objects, making robotic bin packing a long-horizon decision problem. The authors identify a gap between heuristic or configuration-specific RL policies and an MLLM that directly selects objects and placements using updated observations. They also argue that existing work lacks an integrated platform for physical simulation, training-data generation, policy training, and standardized evaluation (§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **PackLab-Suite and PackData-20K:** Partition container space into target layouts, reconstruct placement sequences by reversing object removal, and replay them in simulation to reject invalid trajectories. The resulting dataset contains 20,000 training trajectories (§III-C).
- **PackLab-VLM:** Fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B with supervised action-token prediction. Inputs combine current and historical heightmaps, object identities and dimensions, and executed actions; outputs specify an object, horizontal orientation, and planar placement. Gravity determines vertical placement (§III-A, III-D, IV-A).
- **PackLab-Bench:** Provide 60 independently sampled test cases, evenly divided across three difficulty levels, covering 905 packing steps. Difficulty varies buffer size, packing layers, object count, and container dimensions (§III-E; Table I).

The contribution is the integrated framework and packing-specific policy, dataset, and evaluation protocol; the backbone and supervised training objective are adopted components.

## Evidence and main results
Success Ratio measures **packed volume**, with success determined by an object's center lying inside container boundaries. Compactness divides successful volume by the container-base-area × maximum-height envelope; Overall Score is their per-case product (§III-E).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported benchmark average | Across fixed PackLab-Bench cases and three random-seed runs, PackLab-VLM achieves Success Ratio **0.882**, Compactness **0.718**, and Overall Score **0.660**; SDF-Pack achieves **0.855 / 0.694 / 0.602**, and official TAP-Net **0.845 / 0.556 / 0.474**. | Table II; §IV-B |
| Advantage does not hold on hard cases | Easy/medium Overall Scores are **0.922 / 0.665**, highest in Table II. Hard Overall Score is **0.394**, below DBLF and SDF-Pack, both **0.469**. | Table II |

The results support improved average performance within this benchmark and show that training, visual input, and history matter. They do not establish universal superiority, generalization to unseen physical regimes, or a specific internal long-horizon reasoning mechanism. The prose reports a **0.059** advantage over SDF-Pack, while the rounded Table II values imply **0.058**; the supplied text does not resolve this discrepancy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Broader object geometries, physical settings, and user-defined objectives remain future work. Compute constraints limited model scaling to 9B (§IV-C, V).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Training spans the benchmark's difficulty levels. Independently sampled, disjoint cases establish held-out performance, but do not by themselves establish extrapolation beyond the training distribution (§III-C, III-E).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Center-based success does not require full-object containment. The reported metrics therefore need not capture strict containment or manipulation reliability (§III-E).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and uncertainty:** RL checkpoint interface adaptations are unspecified. Retraining uses an “equivalent” trial-and-error budget without sufficient detail to assess resource matching. Main tables provide averages without uncertainty estimates (§IV-A–C).
- **Analyst inference—physical evidence:** The text provides a depicted physical comparison, without aggregate trial statistics or an identified physical baseline (§IV-D).
- **Analyst inference—ablation interpretation:** Black-heightmap replacement tests sensitivity to corrupted input; it does not isolate the value of vision under matched retraining. No separate open-loop comparison establishes the incremental benefit of feedback (§IV-C).

## Open research questions
- **What causes the hard-case deficit?** Geometric heuristics outperform the model there (Table II). Next experiment: vary buffer size, object count, and container geometry separately and categorize placement failures.
- **Does performance extrapolate beyond generated training configurations?** Disjoint sampling does not establish distributional extrapolation. Next experiment: hold out entire container-size ranges and object-shape families.
- **How much does feedback improve robustness?** History ablations do not separately test closed-loop correction. Next experiment: compare feedback-enabled and fixed-sequence execution under controlled placement disturbances.
- **How reliable is physical deployment?** Aggregate hardware results are unavailable. Next experiment: repeat matched robot trials across object sets, reporting strict containment, grasp failures, replanning frequency, and execution time.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark findings; **low** in broader deployment or extrapolation claims.

Material missing information includes simulator identity and contact parameters, stability thresholds, container-size distributions, full prompt and context limits, inference latency, baseline adaptation and retraining details, uncertainty estimates, and aggregate physical-trial results. The claimed asset release was not independently verified.
