# All modalities are equal, but video is more equal: Closing the Cross-Attention Gap in Joint Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.27901) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27901)


## One-sentence takeaway
RecCAR uses a stop-gradient KL loss to align reciprocal cross-modal correspondences, with reported improvements in human anatomy and audio–video synchronization beyond standard fine-tuning (§§3, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Joint generators allow video and companion modalities to exchange information bidirectionally, but the authors find that video influences motion or audio more effectively than those modalities constrain video. This asymmetry can leave anatomical errors or synchronization failures uncorrected despite useful information in the companion stream. The identified gap is that ordinary generation objectives do not explicitly enforce reciprocal correspondence (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Comparable correspondence distributions:** Normalize both directional attention logits over video tokens, enabling a KL-based “reciprocal correspondence gap.” For the modality-to-video direction, this is a diagnostic re-normalization, not its native forward-pass attention (§3.2).
- **Directional regularization:** Use the video-to-modality distribution as a stop-gradient target for the reciprocal distribution, adding this KL loss to the original generation objective. Adapt attention projections through LoRA while freezing backbone parameters; no additional inference component is required (§3.3).
- **Cross-setting demonstration:** Apply the method to EchoMotion for video–motion and LTX-2/JavisDiT++ for audio–video, including comparisons with ordinary fine-tuning and direct attention diagnostics for video–motion (§§4–6, 8.2).

The regularizer needs no external correspondence labels, but motion-training data curation uses external pose estimators and Gemini-based calibration (§§4.1, 8.1).

These results support the usefulness of the added objective in the tested settings. However, the supplied retrieval contains table captions without table cells, preventing inspection of most exact comparisons. Attention diagnostics support the proposed mechanism but do not independently establish that stronger causal use of the companion modality explains the output improvements.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated boundary:** The anatomy-filtering threshold is empirical and is explicitly not a universal anatomical error boundary (§8.1).
- **Analyst inference—reference reliability:** RecCAR assumes the video-to-companion correspondence is a trustworthy target. The text does not test cases where that direction is incorrect or weaker (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—curation uncertainty:** Motion filtering was calibrated on only 25 generated videos using Gemini ratings; pose-estimation and evaluator errors could affect selected training pairs (§§4.1, 8.1).
- **Analyst inference—scope and mechanism:** Experiments cover motion and audio companions, while direct correspondence-gap validation covers only motion. No reverse-direction, symmetric-loss, or stop-gradient ablations are reported (§§6, 8.2).
- **Evidence limitation:** Missing table cells, uncertainty estimates, and repeated-run statistics prevent assessment of effect variability and most baseline margins.

## Open research questions
1. **When does the reference direction become unreliable?** The method assumes its superiority; test examples stratified by reference-correspondence accuracy and compare fixed versus confidence-weighted targets.
2. **Is directional stop-gradient alignment essential?** Existing ablations isolate the full loss from ordinary fine-tuning; compare reverse, symmetric, and no-stop-gradient variants under matched training budgets.
3. **Do attention changes explain synchronization gains in audio?** Direct diagnostics are reported only for motion; measure audio correspondence gaps and intervene on cross-modal pathways during generation.
4. **How sensitive are motion gains to data filtering?** Calibration is small and empirical; repeat training with alternative thresholds and independently assessed anatomy labels.
