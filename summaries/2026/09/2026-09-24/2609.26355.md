# PACT: From Credit Assignment to Critic Alignment

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26355) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26355)


## One-sentence takeaway
PACT combines an axiomatic definition of statistical token credit with actor-then-critic training, reporting gains over GRPO and PPO in agentic mathematics and coding, although its practical policy correction remains approximate (§§2–5; Appendix C.7).

## Problem and motivation
LLM reinforcement learning often receives one terminal reward while updating thousands of individual tokens. The authors identify a gap between this outcome-level supervision and the algorithm-dependent signals called “credit.” They also argue that conventional PPO workflows leave the critic tracking the previous policy, potentially worsening value estimation during long trajectories (§§1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Conditional uniqueness theorem:** Completeness, Prefix Consistency, and Neutrality uniquely determine credit as \(C_i=E[R\mid F_i]-E[R\mid F_{i-1}]\), under the stated finite-horizon and integrability assumptions. Segment credits follow by summation (§2; Appendix C.2).
- **Connections to existing algorithms:** An ideal KL-regularized teacher induces an OPD gradient proportional to the credit-based gradient; independent-response RLOO matches it in expectation. A bounded-reward sparsity theorem and GAE error decomposition motivate using \(\lambda=1\) (§3).
- **PACT training procedure:** Update the actor first, recompute token probabilities on existing rollouts, then train a sigmoid critic with BCE and importance-corrected reward targets. Implementation uses detached current-token ratios and masks extreme ratios; exact synchronization would require full continuation ratios (§4; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Credit has bounded expected squared magnitude | For rewards in \([0,1]\), conditional expected total squared credit equals reward variance and is at most \(1/4\); expected count above magnitude \(\epsilon\) is at most \(1/(4\epsilon^2)\). | Theorem 4; Appendix C.5 |
| Best reported mathematics results | Qwen3.5-4B with OpenCode: PACT averages **72.87% Avg@16**, versus GRPO **64.07%**, PPO \(\lambda=1\) **59.71%**, and SAO **51.14%**, across the four mathematics benchmarks; PACT leads on each. | Table 1; §§5.1–5.2 |
| Best reported coding result | Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with Codex: SWE-bench Verified **67.4% pass@1**, versus GRPO **65.4%**, PPO \(\lambda=1\) **65.0%**, and SAO **63.6%**. | Table 2; Figure 1 |
| Critic correction contributes to mathematics gains | PACT without critic IS averages **67.74% Avg@16**, versus **72.87%** with correction: **5.13 percentage points**. Other PACT settings are retained. | Table 1; §5.3; Appendix E.4 |
| GAE setting strongly affects PPO here | Mathematics average: **26.31% Avg@16** at \(\lambda=0.95\), versus **59.71%** at \(\lambda=1\); authors report policy collapse for the former. | Table 1; §5.2 |
| BCE improves fixed-policy critic fitting | With identical initialization and rollout data, BCE critics show lower BCE and MSE losses and greater success/failure value separation in both model settings; precise endpoint values are not tabulated. | §5.3; Figure 3 |

These results support PACT’s effectiveness in the reported settings and isolate a benefit from its practical critic correction in mathematics. They do not establish statistical significance, general superiority across model families, or exact updated-policy value estimation. The OPD and RLOO results concern expected gradients under explicit assumptions, not equal variance or empirical performance (§3; Appendix C.7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Uniqueness depends on the selected axioms; credit is statistical rather than causal; exact practical credit estimation remains unsolved (Appendix F).
- **Author-characterized approximation:** Current-token weighting estimates a hybrid policy that takes one updated-policy action and then follows the old policy. Its approximation guarantee is local for a fixed horizon, before practical masking (Appendix C.7).
- **Analyst inference—comparison attribution:** PACT additionally uses actor-side TIS correction absent from the listed baselines. Consequently, full-method gains cannot be attributed solely to critic alignment. The no-critic-IS ablation controls this difference (Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism evidence:** Fixed-policy critic fitting does not isolate BCE’s contribution to final task accuracy; no coding critic-IS ablation or isolated update-order ablation is reported (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope and uncertainty:** Evaluation uses one model per application, and the text reports no training-seed variation or confidence intervals. Figure 4 measures estimated value differences, so it does not directly validate sparsity of true credit (§5; Appendix E.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How accurate is one-step policy tracking over long continuations?** Appendix C.7 leaves omitted continuation terms and masking effects unresolved. Compare current-token, full-continuation, and first-order corrections against measured updated-policy returns in tractable environments.
2. **Which PACT components drive final performance?** Existing ablations isolate critic IS in mathematics and BCE in fixed-policy fitting. Run a factorial study of critic objective, update order, critic correction, and actor-side TIS.
3. **Does improved alignment reduce gradient variance?** Expected-gradient equivalence does not establish statistical efficiency (§3.2). At fixed policies, compare gradient variance and critic calibration against Monte Carlo reference values.
4. **How robust are the benchmark gains?** Reported results lack uncertainty estimates and broader model coverage. Repeat training across seeds and additional model families using matched rollout and compute budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the supplied theory and reported results; **medium** in causal attribution and generalization.

Material missing information includes training-seed variability, confidence intervals, measured compute overhead, complete optimizer and decoding configurations, critic initialization details, direct updated-policy calibration measurements, and verified repository or checkpoint contents.
