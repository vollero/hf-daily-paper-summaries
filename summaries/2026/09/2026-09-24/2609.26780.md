# SpeakerMem-R1: Speaker-Centered Dual-Track Memory for Multi-Party Dialogue

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26780) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26780)


## One-sentence takeaway
SpeakerMem-R1 combines attributed verbatim messages with structured person/group memory to improve multi-party dialogue QA in selected benchmark configurations, while a controlled study shows that reinforcement learning improves its small memory Writer.

## Problem and motivation
Multi-party conversational memory must preserve who spoke, whom a statement concerns, personal versus shared beliefs, and changing states. The authors argue that relevance retrieval and compressed summaries can omit infrequent participants, confuse attribution, or merge incompatible events and state versions (§1).

The targeted gap is therefore both **message attribution** and **query-specific state reconstruction** across participants, channels, and time.

## Approach and contributions
- **Traceable dual-track memory:** System 1 retains verbatim messages; System 2 stores personal Core/Profile records and group Interaction/Insight records. Derived records preserve source, owner, scope, and provenance. Updates append linked states without deleting history (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Query-conditioned evidence assembly:** Retrieval expands requested person/group rows, resolves event and temporal constraints, and combines independent verbatim and structured evidence budgets. Missing structured rows fall back to the corresponding person’s messages (§3.3).
- **Writer-specific training:** Qwen2.5-3B learns Add/Update/Noop actions using speaker-conditioned GRPO. SpeakerLevenshtein scores coordinate-consistent record matches using token overlap and sequence similarity—not standard Levenshtein distance. Rewards combine structural validity, state/update matching, and incremental QA benefit; retrieval and answering remain frozen (§4; Appendix E).
- **Diagnostic framework:** Five dimensions organize coverage, attribution, scope, disambiguation, and temporal/multi-hop failures. The authors explicitly describe these as interpretive categories, not independently validated diagnostic labels (Appendix F.1).

These results support configuration-specific benefits from structured and verbatim evidence and a controlled RL improvement. They do not establish universal superiority across models or broad RL generalization. The headline gains use the DeepSeek block and select ASK settings separately by benchmark. LoCoMo’s all-question score must not be compared directly with baselines reported only on non-adversarial questions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reliable rosters, attribution, and temporal identification are assumed; aliases, membership changes, implicit audiences, and parallel events remain challenging. Cross-evidence reasoning, outside knowledge, cost, and cross-domain/language generalization remain open (§6).
- **Narrow Writer evidence:** Training uses short SocialMem networks plus manually authored boundary cases, with model-generated proxy trajectories. Held-out evaluation separates complete networks but remains within SocialMem (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—objective attribution:** The controlled comparison tests the full RL objective against SFT; no reported reward-component ablations isolate the benefit of speaker conditioning, worst-owner weighting, or terminal QA gain.
- **Analyst inference—comparison controls:** Baselines retain their official prompts/configurations, and evidence budgets are not matched across architectures. Results compare complete systems rather than isolating memory representation alone (§5.1; Appendix F.5).
- **Metric and reporting boundaries:** Accuracy depends on an LLM judge; token-F1 measures token-set overlap. Most main comparisons lack uncertainty estimates. Ablation deltas use the best ASK variant per benchmark, complicating interpretation against one fixed configuration (Appendix C; Table 22).
- **Reproducibility ambiguity:** Main model-role descriptions assign writing to DeepSeek/GPT configurations, while Tables 8 and 24 describe a Qwen Writer. Appendix I promises complete prompts, but the supplied text contains module descriptions without the actual prompt bodies.

## Open research questions
1. **Which RL components drive the gain?** The full-objective comparison does not isolate them. Run matched ablations removing speaker-coordinate gating, worst-owner weighting, update-chain rewards, and terminal QA rewards.
2. **Does Writer-R1 generalize beyond its training distribution?** Controlled evidence covers held-out SocialMem networks only. Freeze the trained Writer and evaluate longer histories, other benchmarks, and additional languages.
3. **How robust is memory to uncertain participant identity?** Reliable rosters and attribution are explicit assumptions. Introduce controlled alias changes, missing speaker labels, and membership changes, then measure attribution and QA degradation.
4. **Can query-adaptive retrieval improve reasoning efficiently?** ASK helps some benchmarks but hurts SocialMem, and Appendix H shows budget-dependent performance. Compare adaptive budgets with fixed budgets at matched token cost on unseen networks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported results in detail, but broader causal and generalization claims remain limited.

Material missing or ambiguous information includes actual prompt bodies, unambiguous Writer assignments for every main result, reward-component ablations, uncertainty for most benchmark differences, hardware/latency measurements, and confirmation of released code/checkpoint contents.
