# Hunyuan-A13B Technical Report

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.27284) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27284)


## One-sentence takeaway
Hunyuan-A13B combines sparse activation and staged post-training to deliver competitive reasoning and tool-use benchmark scores with 13B active parameters, although its claimed efficiency advantage lacks hardware-matched comparisons (§§2–4).

## Problem and motivation
The authors target the inference cost and latency that limit deployment of capable language models. Their goal is to retain strong reasoning, coding, and agent capabilities while reducing active computation and letting users choose between shorter and deeper reasoning (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Model and pretraining recipe:** An 80B-parameter MoE activates 13B parameters, using one shared expert and eight selected specialized experts per input. Training combines a 20T-token foundation stage, annealing, and context extension to 256K; data curation includes 250B STEM tokens (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Four-stage post-training:** Reasoning SFT and GRPO-based RL precede all-scenarios SFT and RL. Rewards combine answer verification, sandbox execution, generative judges, and domain-specific checks (§§3.1–3.2; Figure 1).
- **Selectable reasoning modes:** Joint training supports fast outputs with an empty reasoning block and slow outputs with detailed reasoning. Users select a mode through control tags; slow mode is the default. An automatic task-complexity router is not specified (§3.3).
- **Agent data and evaluation assets:** The report describes a multi-role synthetic interaction engine and introduces ArtifactsBench plus internal instruction-control and language-understanding benchmarks (§§3.2.1, 4.2.1).

The contribution is chiefly the integrated model, training recipe, and reported evaluations; the text explicitly builds on existing MoE, GQA, GRPO, and reward-modeling methods.

## Evidence and main results
Scores below follow the paper’s reporting; most benchmark metric definitions and aggregation procedures are not supplied.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive base-model performance | EvalPlus, zero-shot: Hunyuan 78.64 versus Qwen3-A22B 77.60; MMLU-Pro, five-shot: 67.23 versus 68.18 | §4.1.1; Table 2 |
| Strong but mixed mathematical reasoning | Slow mode: AIME2024 score 87.3 versus Qwen3-A22B 85.7; AIME2025 76.8 versus 81.5 | Table 3 |
| Coding remains uneven | Slow mode: LiveCodeBench 63.9 versus Qwen3-A22B 70.7; FullstackBench 67.8 versus 65.6 | Table 3 |
| Tool-use strengths are benchmark-dependent | Slow mode: BFCL v3 78.3 versus Qwen3-A22B 70.8 and o1-1217 67.8; τ-Bench 54.7 versus o1-1217 60.4 | Table 3 |
| Slow mode does not improve every task | Hunyuan slow versus fast: AIME2024 87.3 versus 30.6; ComplexFuncBench 61.2 versus 74.0 | Tables 3–4 |
| Long-context results are competitive | LongBench-v2 score 55.0 versus Gemini 2.5 Pro 60.9 and Qwen3-A22B 48.4; RULER QA at 64K–128K: 73.9 versus 80.2 and 66.6, respectively | Tables 5–6 |
| Absolute throughput is reported | A16W16C16 precision, 2,048 input tokens and 14,336 output tokens: 190.84 tokens/s at batch 1 and 1,981.99 at batch 32; hardware unspecified | §4.4; Table 7 |

These results support competitive performance on selected benchmarks with fewer active parameters. They do not establish universal superiority, a causal benefit from individual training components, or lower deployment cost under matched hardware and serving conditions. The authors’ broad agent-leadership claim also has exceptions, including slow-mode τ-Bench.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged:** Coding performance is somewhat weaker overall, and FRAMES results leave room to improve RAG-specific processing (§§4.2.2, 4.3.1).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No quantitative ablations isolate STEM curation, shared experts, training-stage order, reward choices, or agent synthesis.
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Post-training prompts, decoding budgets, repeated-run uncertainty, and detailed internal-benchmark protocols are missing. Contamination mitigation is asserted without a reported audit (§§1, 4.2).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency:** Throughput lacks accelerator specifications, device count, framework configuration, and baseline measurements. “Lossless” quantization is asserted without supporting quality comparisons (§4.4).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** The displayed RULER evaluation ends at 128K despite the advertised 256K context. Benchmark tool use does not by itself establish the claimed ability to handle open real-world problems (§§4.2.2–4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Which training components drive the gains?** Their effects are not isolated. Run matched-compute ablations of STEM curation and each post-training stage.
2. **When should fast or slow mode be selected?** Accuracy varies by task, and token-cost measurements are absent. Compare both modes and a learned selector under equal latency or token budgets.
3. **Does efficiency persist under controlled serving conditions?** Hardware and comparative throughput are missing. Benchmark models on identical accelerators, precision, batch sizes, and sequence lengths while measuring latency, throughput, and memory.
4. **How reliable is the full context window?** RULER results stop below the advertised maximum. Extend QA and multi-document evaluations through 256K with controlled evidence placement.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture and headline scores are explicit, but causal and efficiency conclusions are under-supported.

Material missing information includes detailed data mixtures and provenance, complete post-training hyperparameters, evaluation prompts and sampling settings, internal-benchmark construction, contamination audits, uncertainty estimates, inference hardware, and release links or licensing details. The supplied document also has an unexplained date discrepancy: a June 2025 report header and September 2026 arXiv stamp.
