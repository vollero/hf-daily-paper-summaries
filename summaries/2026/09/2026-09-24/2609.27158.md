# The Linear Representation Hypothesis Needs a Group Action

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-24.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.27158) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27158)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors formalize linear representation claims through explicit transformation symmetries, showing why probes, steering vectors, geometric metrics, and attention analyses can support different conclusions despite using similarly shaped arrays.

## Problem and motivation
Representation studies often seek conclusions that extend beyond one trained parameterization without specifying which changes of coordinates preserve the representation. The authors argue that this omission conflates distinct hypotheses: linear decodability, additive feature displacements, superposition, and feature subspaces involve different mathematical objects and transformation laws (§§1–2).

This matters when assumptions change within an analysis—for example, when probe coefficients become intervention vectors or an affine-compatible estimator is evaluated using angles that require additional geometry.

## Approach and contributions
- **Formal specification:** Define a representation claim by an equivalence group \(G\), an object space \(M\) with its transformation action, an extraction procedure \(F\), and a predicate \(P\). Require \(F\) to be equivariant and \(P\) invariant (§3).
- **Architectural and pipeline constraints:** Require equivalence to include function-preserving architectural transformations at the chosen reading point, and assess symmetry across the complete analysis pipeline (§4).
- **Audit and reporting principle:** Classify common quantities by their preserving groups, examine existing interpretability analyses, and recommend reporting the object, transformations, procedure, predicate, and reading point (§§5–8).
- **Constructive attention example:** Derive a query-weighted key distance that is invariant under the query–key reparameterization symmetry and measures expected squared attention-logit change (Appendix C.3).

These are theoretical and methodological contributions; the paper reports no new empirical benchmark.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Probe coefficients cannot generally be identified with steering displacements equivariantly. | For \(\dim V\geq2\), no nonzero \(GL(V)\)-equivariant map exists from \(V^*\) to \(V\). A fixed metric permits vector identification under isometries and projective identification under similarities. | Proposition 3.2; Appendix B |
| Architectural symmetry depends on the reading point. | In ordinary dot-product attention, inverse-transpose query–key transformations preserve the model function while changing head-space geometry; this transformation acts trivially on the residual stream. | Corollary 4.2 |
| RoPE generally retains geometry-changing transformations. | Under the stated frequency-separation conditions, independent scaling and rotation of rotary planes preserve logits. Unequal plane scales can alter key cosines and spectral quantities. | Appendix C.1–C.2 |
| Common representation metrics require different structures. | The audit places centered exact rank and CCA under affine equivalence, angular and normalized spectral quantities under similarities, and absolute norms and reconstruction loss under isometries. | Table 1 |
| Pipeline preprocessing can narrow invariance. | SVCCA’s singular-value truncation is not preserved by general invertible transformations; the subsequent CCA stage does not restore that invariance. | §6.4 |
| An invariant alternative to key cosine is available. | With \(\Omega=E[qq^\top]\), \(d^2=(k_1-k_2)^\top\Omega(k_1-k_2)\) is invariant under the paired query–key action and equals expected squared logit change. | Appendix C.3, Equation 20 |

The evidence establishes mathematical distinctions and conditional invariance results. It does not establish improved steering, cache-eviction quality, or predictive performance. The authors explicitly separate their critique of KeyDiff’s explanatory geometry from its reported empirical effectiveness (§6.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** Geometry specific to a trained artifact remains legitimate. Generalizing such geometry beyond that run requires evidence of stability across seeds and training runs (§8).
- **Authors’ stated cost:** Explicit assumptions can narrow conclusions, require additional maps between object spaces, or make claims depend on a chosen metric (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** The practical magnitude of the identified coordinate dependence remains unmeasured; the supplied paper contains derivations and literature audits rather than controlled reparameterization experiments.
- **Analyst inference:** The proposed key distance depends on the realized query distribution. Estimation cost, distribution shift, and effects on cache-eviction performance are not evaluated (Appendix C.3).
- **Scope boundary:** Table 1 reports largest preserving groups within the chosen hierarchy, with generic spectral qualifications; it is not an exhaustive classification of all possible symmetries (§5; Table 1).

## Open research questions
1. **How much do symmetry violations change practical decisions?** The paper establishes possible dependence without measuring its prevalence. Apply function-preserving query–key transformations to fixed models and compare geometric statistics and selected eviction tokens.
2. **Can invariant key distance improve cache eviction?** Appendix C.3 supplies a formula without performance or cost evaluation. Compare it with cosine eviction under matched cache budgets, measuring output quality, latency, and estimation overhead.
3. **Which artifact-specific geometries generalize across training runs?** Section 8 calls for stability evidence but provides none. Repeat matched probing and steering analyses across seeds, recording both geometric stability and intervention outcomes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the paper’s stated framework and analytical scope; practical benefits remain unestablished.
