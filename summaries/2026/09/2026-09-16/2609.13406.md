# Generalized Agent Iteration: One Formal Framework for Iterative Policy Improvement and Recursive Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13406) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13406)


## One-sentence takeaway
GAI provides a conceptual formalism for comparing policy improvement and recursive self-improvement through modifier editability and evaluation grounding, but supplies neither new empirical validation nor general improvement guarantees.

## Problem and motivation
“Self-improvement” describes systems ranging from fixed output-refinement loops to agents that rewrite their own improvement procedures. The authors identify a missing common criterion for distinguishing these systems and explaining when classical generalized policy iteration (GPI) guarantees cease to apply (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified representation:** Model a system through policy, action critic, modifier, modification critic, and evaluation base; define the agent as its modifiable components. Learning alternates evaluation and modification (§3.1; Figure 2).
- **Two classification axes:** Whether the modifier belongs to the agent distinguishes fixed improvement from RSI; evaluation grounding distinguishes anchored, goal-drifting, and fully self-referential RSI (§§3.2–3.3; Table 2).
- **Comparative taxonomy:** Map existing systems onto these coordinates, including fixed training loops, proof-gated self-modification, and co-evolving evaluators (§4; Table 3).
- **Failure catalogue:** Identify candidate-self search, self-evaluation, ungrounded evaluation, and goal drift as places where classical guarantees no longer apply (§5; Table 4).

These are framework and classification contributions, rather than a new executable learning algorithm.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

The evidence establishes the authors’ proposed vocabulary and structural arguments. It does not establish a new empirical advantage, convergence of general RSI, or measured rates of goal drift. The numerical observations come from cited work; sample sizes, score definitions, and experimental details are unavailable here. Section 6 explicitly acknowledges that the GPI reduction remains prose rather than a proposition with explicit hypotheses.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** No reachability or computational-complexity guarantees; iteration steps do not model compute or time (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Human acceptance, auditing, and rollback are outside the formalism; whether guards belong inside the agent remains a design choice (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Self-consistency solutions, their relationship to external goals, and internal faithfulness monitoring remain unresolved (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Classifications depend on how component boundaries are assigned. Table 3’s drifting-system row lists the editable critic but does not explicitly include the evaluation base in its agent set, making that assignment less transparent than Table 2’s criterion.
- **Analyst inference:** “Observed” goal drift is supported here by an example of evaluator co-evolution, without quantified divergence from a fixed external goal (§5.3). That supports a structural risk more directly than demonstrated goal degradation.

## Open research questions
1. **When does self-consistency imply external improvement?** Section 6 leaves the solution set uncharacterized. A next experiment could enumerate modifiers and critics in a finite toy system and compare self-consistent configurations against fixed external return.
2. **Which restrictions permit a rigorous reduction to GPI?** The reduction lacks explicit hypotheses (§6). A next experiment could freeze components individually in a finite MDP and check when resulting updates reproduce classical policy iteration, alongside a formal proof.
3. **Can an internal monitor detect evaluation drift?** The authors leave this open (§§5–6). A next experiment could permit evaluator rewrites while comparing an internal monitor’s alerts against an immutable external audit score.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The conceptual contribution and stated limitations are clear, but the retrieved text omits the displayed Definition 1 and key RSI/self-consistency equations, despite referring to them. The notation appendix contains no entries. New experiments, explicit reduction hypotheses, formal proofs of the proposed results, implementation artifacts, and detailed protocols for the cited numerical evidence are unavailable.
