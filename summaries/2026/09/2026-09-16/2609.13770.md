# Training Specialist Models without Reasoning Trajectories for Domain Expert Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13770) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13770)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors show that, under question–answer-only training, specialist optimization controls the reasoning trajectories used for distillation, allowing distributional drift constraints to steer a student’s trade-off between domain specialization and general capability retention.

## Problem and motivation

Specialist distillation commonly trains a domain expert on question–answer pairs and then uses its generated reasoning trajectories to supervise a student. Because the specialist never receives gold reasoning, answer supervision constrains final-answer behavior but leaves the trajectory distribution underdetermined. The paper asks what selects these trajectories and whether that selection predictably affects the distilled student (§§1, 3.1; Figure 1).

This matters because seemingly interchangeable specialist tuning methods may generate supervision with very different specialization, robustness, and structural quality. Prior work, according to the authors, does not isolate this trajectory-level ambiguity or treat specialist optimization as a direct control over distillation data (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Trajectory-distribution framing.** The authors model QA-only specialist training as implicitly selecting among multiple answer-compatible trajectory distributions. Training the student on sampled trajectories approximately minimizes forward KL from the specialist’s trajectory distribution to the student (§3.1, Equations 2–5).

2. **Student distillation as a probe.** Students start from the same initialization and use identical full-fine-tuning configurations and equal-sized filtered datasets. Their differences are therefore used as operational evidence about the supervision generated by FFT-, LoRA-, or LST-trained specialists (§4.1; Table 1).

3. **Distributional-drift control.** LST restricts trainable layers and acts as an implicit anchor, while ASFT explicitly penalizes KL divergence from the origin model. The authors vary ASFT anchoring strength to steer specialization versus retention (§§3.2, 5.3–5.4; Figure 5).

4. **Multi-domain and cross-family evaluation.** The pipeline is evaluated on chemistry, physics, and eight-language translation, with in-task, shifted in-domain, and out-of-domain tests; an Intern-S1-mini-to-Qwen3-8B experiment tests transfer across model families (§§4–5; Tables 2 and 6; Figure 3).

5. **Released artifacts.** The abstract states that code, models, and datasets are publicly available, although their contents cannot be verified from the supplied text.

These results support the authors’ claim that specialist optimization and drift control predict downstream capability profiles within the tested pipeline. The controlled student configurations strengthen the attribution to generated supervision, while the cross-family experiment shows that identical teacher and student parameterization is unnecessary.

The evidence does not directly identify or validate the full latent trajectory distribution. Structural completeness, length, behavioral KL, and student performance are proxies; answer-correct filtration also cannot establish that individual rationales are logically faithful. The experiments show strong association and controlled intervention effects, but they do not establish that distributional drift is the sole causal mechanism across other architectures, domains, or scales.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Primary experiments are at the 8B scale; behavior above 70B and in explicitly reasoning-optimized models remains unknown (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Answer-equivalence filtration cannot remove “right answer, wrong reasoning,” especially for open-ended translation (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Coverage is incomplete because the supplied source was truncated by 7,883 characters in Appendix E. Some data preparation and evaluation details may therefore be missing.
- **Analyst inference:** Most central comparisons use one origin/student family, Qwen3-8B. The cross-family evidence is limited to chemistry with Intern-S1-mini, and the 14B extension is also chemistry-only (§5.2; Appendices C.2–C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The main tables provide point estimates without seed-level uncertainty. The permutation test addresses rank-profile dependence, but uncertainty for individual performance differences is not reported in the supplied text (§4.2; checklist item 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Physics It evaluation relies on an LLM judge for roughly 83.4%–85.9% of sub-answers. Uniform judging supports comparison, but judge validity is not independently quantified in the supplied material (Appendix E.2.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Claims of trajectory “collapse” rely on indirect measures such as answer-token confidence, format completeness, and length. No human or formal evaluation of reasoning correctness is reported in the supplied text (§§5.5–5.6).
- **Analyst inference:** The text alternates between \(\lambda\) and \(\alpha\) when describing ASFT strength in §5.4, creating a notation ambiguity.
- **Analyst inference:** Although the paper states that code, models, and datasets are released, reproducibility, licensing, and artifact completeness were not independently verified.

## Open research questions

1. **Does drift control work at larger scales and with reasoning-trained models?** Appendix A leaves models above 70B and reasoning-optimized origins untested. A next experiment could repeat the matched FFT/LST/ASFT pipeline across several larger and reasoning-trained origins.

2. **Are the generated trajectories logically faithful rather than merely answer-correct?** Exact-answer filtration cannot exclude shortcuts. A next experiment could combine expert or formal step verification with blinded trajectory-quality assessment and relate verified faithfulness to student performance.

3. **Does the trade-off generalize beyond the three studied domains?** The evidence covers chemistry, physics, and low-resource translation. A next experiment could test domains with open-ended outputs, such as clinical or legal QA, using domain-appropriate verification.

4. **Which aspect of drift predicts transfer most reliably?** The paper associates lower behavioral KL and structural constraints with better retention, but does not isolate competing factors. A next experiment could independently vary parameter-update structure, KL strength, trajectory length, and filtration quality while holding specialist answer accuracy fixed.
