# Drift-Constrained Optimization: Only Direction Matters in Fine-Tuning Instruct Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13680) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13680)


## One-sentence takeaway
Layer-selective QA-only fine-tuning improves chemistry and multilingual translation while often retaining more general capability than tested alternatives, supporting—but not globally proving—the authors’ local, matched-drift direction-selection framework.

## Problem and motivation
Fine-tuning strong instruct models can improve a target task while damaging existing capabilities. The authors propose specifying acceptable behavioral drift before optimization and comparing how effectively different updates spend that budget. QA-only supervision provides a stress test: training omits reasoning trajectories, although inference still requires reasoning (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Drift-first analytical framework:** Reference-anchored KL divergence defines a local Fisher geometry. At a fixed behavioral radius, first-order task improvement depends on update direction; the optimal local direction is the natural gradient (§§3–4). The contribution is the organizing framework, not the previously established natural-gradient result.
- **Unified comparison of update families:** Full fine-tuning, LoRA, and parameter-subset tuning permit different directions. Directional efficiency measures local task improvement per unit behavioral radius (§4).
- **Layer-selective empirical probe:** LST freezes unselected layers and tests continuous or split configurations. For example, `b4t16` first tunes the bottom four layers, then the top sixteen from that checkpoint. It probes useful direction families without explicitly maximizing directional efficiency (§§4.3, 5).
- **Practical adaptation findings:** Selected configurations improve chemistry and translation, with an additional transfer experiment and translation reinforcement-learning initialization study (§§5–6).

These results establish useful layer-selection choices in the tested settings. They do not establish universal capability preservation, superiority on every language pair, or a globally valid “only direction matters” rule. General Avg. combines math, coding, and BBEH scores; translation results summarize pivot groups rather than exhaustive pairwise superiority (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation mainly covers Qwen3-scale models, scientific tasks, and multilingual transfer. Larger families, multimodal evaluation, and other domains remain untested; underlying mechanisms and better direction structures remain unresolved (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—local theory versus training:** The derivation assumes small updates and a non-degenerate Fisher metric. A budget inequality bounds radius rather than fixing it automatically. LST does not explicitly solve the constrained problem, and the text does not specify an operational procedure guaranteeing a preset drift budget (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference—measurement mismatch:** Main experiments define efficiency as task improvement divided by √KL; Figures 3b and 5 instead use absolute accuracy divided by KL. These are different quantities and do not directly validate the same efficiency objective (§5; §6).
- **Analyst inference—retention and comparison scope:** Several successful translation configurations still lose General Avg. points. Expert systems also have differing stated language coverage, complicating interpretation of the broad multilingual averages (Table 1; Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Despite pointing to Appendix E for training details, the supplied text omits learning rates, optimizer settings, batch sizes, stage durations, training-seed variability, and precise KL-estimation data. Sequential split training also leaves a potential schedule confound without a reported simultaneous-versus-sequential ablation (§5; Appendix E).

## Open research questions
1. **Can useful layers be selected automatically for a preset drift budget?** LST currently serves as a structured probe. Compare an automatic selector with the reported configurations under explicitly enforced common KL budgets (§4.3).
2. **How far does the local Fisher approximation remain predictive?** No direct approximation-error study is reported. Measure actual anchored KL against its quadratic approximation along training trajectories and across drift regimes (§3; Figure 4a).
3. **Does layer placement or sequential training explain the split advantage?** Split configurations use staged training. Compare simultaneous and sequential updates to identical layer subsets with matched training exposure and drift (§5).
4. **Does QA-only adaptation remain competitive with reasoning supervision?** The authors explicitly frame QA-only training as a stress test, not evidence that reasoning supervision is unnecessary. Compare QA-only and reasoning-trajectory training within the same update families and budgets (Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed tables support the main empirical pattern, but the stronger geometric and generalization interpretations remain less established.

Material gaps include complete training and RL configurations, KL sampling and aggregation details, budget-enforcement and layer-selection procedures, training-run uncertainty, and independent verification of linked release contents.
