# ModularRSI: Modular and Generalizable Recursive Harness Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14857) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14857)


## One-sentence takeaway

ModularRSI improves coding-agent harnesses by aggregating contrasts between successful and failed trajectories, evolving five behavioral modules separately, validating changes, and integrating them before benchmark-disjoint evaluation.

## Problem and motivation

Agent performance on long-horizon coding and terminal tasks depends on the harness controlling execution, tools, observations, context, and termination. The authors identify three barriers to generalizable harness self-improvement:

- Existing methods often evolve on evaluation-benchmark data, obscuring whether gains transfer beyond those benchmarks.
- Individual trajectories mix recurring harness faults with task-specific reasoning, producing ambiguous improvement signals.
- Whole-harness editing makes it difficult to assign failures to specific mechanisms and can introduce interference between unrelated components.

These issues motivate an evolution protocol with separate development data, evidence aggregated across tasks, and constrained component-level editing (Sections 1 and 3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Contrastive trajectory analysis:** Each evolution task receives multiple rollouts with binary rewards. Same-task successes and failures are compared; historical successes can supply contrasts for current all-failure groups, while all-success groups are inspected for inefficiency (Section 3.3).

2. **Modular evolution:** The Terminus-2 harness is reorganized into Agent Loop, Tool Use, Observation Management, Context Management, and Task Completion Detection. Each module evolves independently within a restricted scope (Sections 3.2 and 3.4).

3. **Evidence aggregation and change control:** Similar diagnoses are consolidated and ranked by support from distinct tasks. Per-function evolution histories help prevent repeated or conflicting changes (Section 3.4).

4. **Validation and integration:** Proposed edits undergo static program checks, an LLM diff review for effectiveness and task-specific overfitting, and execution checks on two sampled tasks. A later integration epoch repairs conflicts among independently evolved modules (Sections 3.5–3.6).

5. **Function-library management:** Similar functions are merged, while an LLM selects task-relevant functions for each execution (Section 3.7).

6. **Benchmark-disjoint resource:** The authors curate 2,000 executable tasks from external sources, with quality checks and semantic-overlap screening against downstream benchmarks (Section 4; Figure 2).

The evidence supports gains on two held-out benchmarks, transfer between their task domains, and transfer across three inference models. The strategy comparison supports the value of separate module evolution followed by integration within the tested setup.

It does not isolate the causal contribution of contrastive analysis, establish performance outside coding and terminal environments, or show how results scale when all 2,000 curated tasks are used. The case studies demonstrate plausible behavioral changes but contain other simultaneous updates and therefore are not controlled causal estimates.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** There is no dedicated ablation isolating contrastive trajectory analysis; trajectory statistics and case studies provide only supporting evidence (Section 7).
- **Author-stated:** Main evolution uses 120 TB-related and 120 SWE-related tasks rather than the full 2,000-task collection because of computation and time constraints (Sections 5.3 and 7).
- **Author-stated:** Broader comparisons and larger-scale studies remain future work (Section 7).
- **Analyst inference:** The LLM performs diagnosis, modification, overfitting review, function merging, task-aware composition, and integration. The supplied evaluations do not separately measure error or bias from these LLM-mediated gates (Sections 3.3–3.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Execution validation samples only two tasks from the current batch after each edit, so behavior regressions outside that small check may pass the gate (Section 3.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark-disjointness relies partly on LLM semantic-similarity screening. The text does not report thresholds, screening error estimates, or an independent overlap audit (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons with AHE and Meta-Harness use adaptations to a unified framework and an epoch-matching rule; the text does not establish that this equalizes compute, trajectories, or effective optimization opportunities exactly (Section 6.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Statistical uncertainty, repeated-run variance, and significance tests are not reported for the headline benchmark results in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **How much of the gain comes specifically from contrastive analysis?**  
   This remains open because the authors report no dedicated ablation. A next experiment could compare identical modular evolution runs using paired trajectories, failure-only diagnosis, and randomly matched trajectories under equal rollout and editing budgets.

2. **How does performance scale with evolution-set size and diversity?**  
   Only 240 of the 2,000 curated tasks are used in the main evolution experiments. A controlled scaling study could evolve on increasing stratified subsets and measure held-out performance, variance, and compute cost.

3. **Which validation gates prevent regressions most effectively?**  
   Program checks, diff review, and two-task execution validation are applied together. A factorial ablation could remove each gate separately and measure acceptance rates, runtime failures, overfitting, and downstream accuracy.

4. **Does modular harness evolution transfer beyond terminal and repository tasks?**  
   Evaluation covers two closely related agent settings. The frozen harness, or an appropriately adapted module interface, could be tested on other executable tool-use environments while tracking which modules transfer without modification.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported method, experimental setup, and tabulated results because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material information unavailable or insufficiently specified includes:

- Statistical uncertainty and variation across independent evolution runs.
- A controlled ablation of contrastive trajectory analysis.
- Exact semantic-overlap thresholds and false-positive or false-negative analysis.
- Full prompts and runtime inputs, which the appendices explicitly abridge.
- Complete compute and token consumption for each compared method.
- Results from evolution on the full 2,000-task dataset.
- Evidence outside coding and terminal-agent domains.
