# Decoy Direction Optimization: A Post-Hoc Defense Against LLM Abliteration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16204) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16204)


## One-sentence takeaway

Decoy Direction Optimization cheaply edits selected MLP neurons so contrastive refusal-ablation attacks target orthogonal decoy signals, reducing standard-RFA attack success below 10% across tested model families, though stronger adaptive and weight-level attacks remain effective.

## Problem and motivation

Open-weight LLM guardrails can be removed through Refusal Feature Ablation (RFA), which estimates a harmful-versus-safe activation direction and projects it out while often preserving general capability. Existing defenses typically require safety finetuning for every checkpoint, making broad deployment expensive.

The authors seek a fast, post-hoc defense for resource-constrained white-box attackers who can inspect weights, collect activations, modify inference, and edit weights, but lack the original checkpoint and do not perform gradient-based finetuning (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Post-hoc decoy defense.** DDO estimates per-layer refusal directions, selects low-norm MLP neurons, and converts them into nonlinear gated units that read a refusal-related coordinate and write a large signal orthogonal to refusal. The edited harmful–safe contrast then points toward the decoy, causing RFA to remove it while leaving causal refusal features comparatively intact (Section 3; Figure 2).

2. **Lightweight optimization and deployment.** Decoy directions are optimized using refusal preservation, benign-retention, estimator-confusion, and first-token losses on 128 harmful and 128 safe probes. Base weights stay frozen; the edits compile into existing weights with no added inference architecture or runtime operations (Sections 3 and F.2).

3. **Formal analysis.** Theorems 1–2 bound the overlap between the attacker’s selected subspace and the causal refusal subspace. Corollary 3 shows that, under fixed decoy energy, evenly distributing energy across \(k\) singular modes maximizes protection of the weakest selected mode, exposing a tradeoff between rank-1 strength and higher-rank coverage (Sections 3 and E.1).

4. **Broader attack evaluation.** The paper tests standard RFA, eight-phase adaptive RFA, Heretic weight editing, prompt jailbreaks, undo heuristics, probe-budget changes, and rank-\(k\) attacks, including comparisons with trained defenses (Sections 4 and F.10–F.18).

These results support the authors’ claim that DDO is an effective, inexpensive defense against the evaluated standard-RFA procedures and can preserve useful behavior better than some trained alternatives. They do not establish durable security against unrestricted checkpoint modification: adaptive rank and phase increases approach undefended ASR, Heretic retains partial success, and the threat model excludes adversarial finetuning and access to the original checkpoint.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** All evaluated defenses eventually reach high ASR under sustained adaptive re-estimation; DDO’s eight-phase worst case is 65% (Appendix A; Table 16).
- **Author-stated:** DDO remains weaker than RepBend under Heretic on Llama-3, at 18.0% versus 1.3% ASR (Appendix A; Table 2).
- **Author-stated:** DDO can increase benign over-refusal, and choosing between replace and additive compilation currently requires model-specific evaluation (Appendix A; Tables 10–12).
- **Author-stated:** Single-reader protection declines sharply with attack rank, reaching 79% ASR at rank 32 (Appendix F.17).
- **Analyst inference:** The theoretical guarantee applies to a fixed contrast matrix under orthogonal-decoy and spectral-margin assumptions. The authors explicitly describe effective decoy rank as a diagnostic rather than a guarantee for iterative adaptive attacks (Section 3).
- **Analyst inference:** Cross-model success depends on per-model Optuna tuning, causal-zone analysis, compile-mode selection, and coherence filtering, so the evidence does not establish plug-and-play robustness for an unseen architecture (Sections F.3–F.7).
- **Analyst inference:** The main adaptive and diagnostic results often use LlamaGuard-2 alone, whereas standard-RFA results average three judges. Direct comparisons across these settings therefore require care (Appendix F.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Heretic utility after attack is not directly measured by task accuracy; the reported KL diagnostic and pre-attack utility columns do not fully establish capability-preserving attack success (Appendix F.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Several evaluations rely on GPT-4 or GPT-4o judging, which may complicate exact reproduction even though decoding and local stochastic components use fixed seeds (Appendices F.7 and F.13).

## Open research questions

1. **Can post-hoc edits resist longer or broader adaptive attacks?** Eight phases and rank-\(k\) sweeps eventually recover high ASR. A next experiment could jointly vary phase count, SVD rank, hookpoint choice, and non-DIM estimators while tracking post-attack task utility.

2. **Can weight-level robustness approach RepBend without finetuning?** DDO leaves a large Heretic gap relative to RepBend. A next experiment could test distributed post-hoc edits across multiple parameter surfaces, under a fixed editing and utility budget.

3. **Can configuration selection be predicted from checkpoint internals?** Compile mode and layer placement are model-specific and currently evaluated through sweeps. A next experiment could train and validate a selection rule based on causal refusal-zone measurements, neuron sensitivity, and refusal redundancy across held-out architectures.

4. **Does DDO compose effectively with inference-time safeguards?** The paper identifies this as future work but does not evaluate it. A next experiment could compare DDO, an input/output classifier, and their combination against RFA, weight edits, and semantic jailbreaks such as PAIR.
