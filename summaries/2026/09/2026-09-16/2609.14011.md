# Convergent Emergence of In-Context Learning Across Modalities

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14011) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14011)


## One-sentence takeaway

A shared controlled task suite finds significant paired-mapping in-context learning in language, genome, integer-sequence, image, time-series, and protein models, with correlated task-level benefits across five modalities but a distinct image profile.

## Problem and motivation

The paper asks whether few-shot in-context learning (ICL) emerges organically from next-token prediction outside language and whether different modalities share a common pattern of task difficulty. Existing evidence was sparse, modality-specific, and based on incomparable task suites, making it unclear which properties of ICL are general and which depend on language data (§1, pp. 1–2).

This matters because non-language tasks may be difficult to specify through verbal instructions; demonstrations could therefore provide a practical way to condition models without parameter updates (§1, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controlled cross-modality evaluation:** The authors instantiate the same 100 deterministic 8-bit transformations in each modality through modality-specific encodings (§3.2; Fig. 3; Tables 1–2).
- **Pairing-sensitive control:** Clean demonstrations are compared with prompts whose output labels are shuffled while preserving the output multiset and target. The clean-minus-deranged exact-match gap measures benefit from correct input-output pairings (§3.1; Eqs. 1–4).
- **Cross-modality convergence test:** Per-task clean-minus-deranged profiles are compared using Spearman correlations across representative models (§4.1; Fig. 2).
- **Scale and negative-control studies:** The paper examines multiple model sizes where available and applies related elicitation methods to chess and music (§§4.2–4.3; Figs. 6–7).
- **Reusable evaluation materials:** The authors report a complete task suite, prompt encodings, statistical protocol, and released replication code (Reproducibility Statement, p. 14; Appendices D–I).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Significant paired-mapping ICL appears in all six primary modalities. | At each modality’s maximum shot count, clean-minus-deranged exact-match gaps range from 9.9 to 31.4 percentage points; all exact one-sided within-task condition-swap tests remain significant after Holm adjustment. Each condition uses 100 tasks and 128 trials per task. | §4.1; Fig. 4; Table 3 |
| Integer sequences show the largest reported maximum-shot gap. | NextTerm-440M at 64 shots: 48.1% clean versus 16.7% deranged, gap 31.4 points, 95% CI [25.6, 37.6], Holm-adjusted \(p=1.1\times10^{-22}\). | Table 3 |
| Protein shows the smallest primary-modality maximum-shot gap. | ProGen2-base at 48 shots: 26.4% clean versus 16.5% deranged, gap 9.9 points, 95% CI [8.2, 11.7], Holm-adjusted \(p=9.3\times10^{-22}\). | Table 3 |
| Five modalities share positively associated task-level effect profiles. | Among language, genome, integer sequences, time series, and proteins, pairwise Spearman correlations span 0.35–0.89 using maximum-shot per-task gaps; genome–protein is 0.89. | §4.1; Fig. 2 |
| Image ICL has a more distinct task profile. | Image correlations with genome, protein, integer sequences, and time series are 0.06–0.15; its correlation with language is 0.44. ImageGPT’s cluster effects peak at 0.66 for bit reversal and 0.61 for local shifts. | §4.1; Figs. 2 and 5 |
| Scaling trends are generally positive but non-monotonic. | Effects increase monotonically for ImageGPT and NextTerm, rise through Qwen3-8B then plateau, peak at Evo2-7B, and peak around ProGen2-base before declining. | §4.2; Fig. 6 |
| The chess elicitation shows no paired-mapping effect. | ChessGPT-50M at 8 shots: gap \(-0.0016\), one-sided condition-swap \(p=0.640\), with 128 trials per task. | §4.3; Fig. 7a |
| Music shows a small late-shot effect. | Musicroll-50M at 32 shots: 0.1732 clean versus 0.1625 deranged, gap 0.0107; \(p=0.00349\), Holm-adjusted \(p=0.0174\). Earlier shots have \(p\geq0.098\). | §4.3; Fig. 7b |
| Held-out outputs are usually identifiable at moderate shot counts. | For 99 non-random tasks, the derived lower bound on output identifiability is 0.991 at 8 shots and above 0.999 from 16 shots. | Appendix B, pp. 20–21 |

These results establish that the authors’ specific encodings and prompt protocol elicit pairing-sensitive few-shot behavior in six model families. The correlated task profiles support a qualified convergence claim for five representative models.

They do not establish a universal ICL mechanism, prove that a failed elicitation means a modality lacks ICL, or support ranking modalities by intrinsic ICL ability. The experiments test synthetic deterministic functions rather than real domain tasks (§5, pp. 12–13).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Failure to elicit ICL cannot prove its absence; chess or music may require different prompts or elicitation methods (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Cross-modality scores confound ICL capability with model priors, encodings, architectures, training corpora, and decoding rules, so absolute modality rankings are unsupported (§5).
- **Author-stated:** The evaluation covers deterministic bitstring manipulation, excluding noisy or stochastic regression and classification (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Task difficulty is heterogeneous but not controlled through a scalar difficulty mechanism (§5).
- **Author-stated:** The framework cannot predict which modalities or model scales will exhibit ICL (§5).
- **Author-stated:** The derangement procedure is selected non-uniformly, and its influence on differing deranged-accuracy trends has not been tested (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Constrained decoding for ImageGPT, ProGen2, and Musicroll and sign-symmetrized forecasting for TimesFM may affect comparability with unconstrained generation. These procedures are documented, but no common decoding ablation is reported (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Some scaling comparisons are observational rather than controlled. The two NextTerm checkpoints differ in scale, vocabulary, architecture, and corpus; the authors explicitly say their difference cannot be attributed to scale alone (§5; Appendix H).
- **Analyst inference:** Semantic clustering includes judgment: the elbow heuristic favored five clusters, while seven were selected after inspection (§5; Appendix I).

## Open research questions

1. **Does convergence persist on noisy, stochastic, or longer-input tasks?** The current suite contains deterministic 8-bit functions. A next experiment could evaluate matched noisy classification and regression families across the same modalities.

2. **Which training-data properties cause emergent paired-mapping ICL?** The paper compares pretrained models but does not isolate corpus structure. Controlled models trained on corpora varying one property at a time could test causal effects.

3. **Can modality priors be normalized enough to compare intrinsic ICL strength?** Current encodings interact differently with each model’s prior. A next experiment could compare multiple randomized encoding families per modality and estimate sensitivity to encoding choice.

4. **Why do images diverge and some scale curves reverse?** ImageGPT has a distinct task profile, while Evo2 and ProGen2 show non-monotonic scaling. Architecture-, data-, and parameter-matched training experiments could separate these factors.
