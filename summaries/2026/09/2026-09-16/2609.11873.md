# The Last AI Built by Humans: Toward Genuine Recursive Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11873) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11873)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey proposes an autonomy-based framework for recursive self-improvement, distinguishing persistent adaptation from demonstrated improvement of the mechanisms that produce future successors.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that AI development still depends on costly human coordination of experiments, training data, verification, and deployment fixes. Automating individual steps does not establish that a system improves its own improvement process (§§1.1–1.3).

They identify an evidence gap: better task scores, retained memory, and self-modifying code can each occur without effective recursion. Their framework asks who controls improvement decisions, what changes persist, and whether those changes help subsequent improvement rounds (§1.7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Autonomy taxonomy:** A non-RSI reference level, B0, covers task-local refinement; L1–L5 cover execution, strategy selection, experience acquisition, deployment adaptation, and revision of inherited improvement mechanisms (§1.4; Fig. 2).
- **Operational criteria:** The survey decomposes improvement into experience, targets, improvers, verifiers, retained state, and successors. It distinguishes *structural recursion*—reusing a revised improvement mechanism—from *effective recursion*—producing stronger successors under comparable budgets and independent evaluation (§§1.7, 2.2).
- **Capability-trend analysis:** The Headroom-Closed Index (HCI) normalizes reported benchmark progress relative to each benchmark’s entry-year frontier, with protocol filtering and source weighting (§2.1).
- **Cross-domain synthesis:** The authors organize existing methods and industrial practices by improvement responsibility and feedback conditions, including science, embodiment, software engineering, and healthcare (Tables 2–6; Appendix C).

The visible contribution is primarily a survey, analytical framework, and benchmark synthesis. The supplied excerpt does not establish a newly implemented general RSI system.

The evidence supports a framework for comparing improvement loops and descriptive differences in reported benchmark progress. It does **not** show that RSI caused those trajectories or validate the illustrated future gains. Results attributed to individual systems are reports of cited work, not established here as independently replicated experiments.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated, omitting much of the central discussion, industry case studies, references, and detailed L5 treatment. Complete-paper coverage is impossible.
- **Author-stated:** Repeated evaluator access can enable overfitting or exploitation; extra search compute can masquerade as algorithmic progress; persistent errors can contaminate later rounds (§§1.3, 3.3.4, 3.4.4).
- **Author-stated:** Industrial reports have different evidentiary strength from independent replication. Some industry entries explicitly remain candidates or unverified claims (§3.3.4; Table 13).
- **Author-stated:** Cybersecurity trajectory comparisons require caution because task subsets or aggregation changed (§2.1).
- **Analyst inference:** HCI comparisons depend on entry frontiers, included benchmark families, and weighting choices. The visible text supplies formulas but no uncertainty intervals or robustness results for those choices.
- **Analyst inference:** The capability analysis motivates investigation of RSI but cannot identify which improvement mechanism would close the observed gaps.

## Open research questions
- **Does mechanism revision improve successor quality?** Structural recursion does not establish effective recursion (§1.7). Compare a revisable improver with a frozen improver across multiple rounds using matched total budgets and protected tasks.
- **Does adaptive experience acquisition outperform fixed schedules?** Learner responsiveness need not imply useful learning (§3.4.4). Freeze learner-state inputs or use learner-independent schedules while matching generation, assessment, and training costs.
- **How robust are the HCI domain rankings?** Protocol changes and weighting choices affect the synthesis (§2.1). Recompute trajectories under alternative source weights, entry-frontier definitions, and strict stable-protocol subsets.
- **Can retained improvements transfer safely across settings?** The domain synthesis identifies unresolved transfer and attribution problems (Appendix C). Test versioned updates on unseen repositories, environments, or institutional datasets with regression tracking and rollback.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The visible framework and reported HCI results are explicit, but the truncated source prevents full assessment.

Material missing information includes the detailed L5 argument, most preliminary industrial evidence, complete references, underlying HCI records and analysis code, robustness analyses, and sufficient experimental details to assess the cited systems’ causal claims or reproducibility.
