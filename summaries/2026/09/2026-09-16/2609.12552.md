# RelateAnything: Real-Time Open-Vocabulary Relation Prediction From Any Inputs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.12552) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12552)


## One-sentence takeaway
RelateAnything reports stronger cross-dataset relation prediction through label-independent region inputs, a replaceable predicate embedding bank, and geometrically filtered supervision, but unseen-concept generalization and accuracy at its fastest deployment setting remain unestablished (§§ 3–6, 10; Table 35).

## Problem and motivation
Scene-graph models commonly couple relation prediction to object labels and a benchmark’s predicate vocabulary, limiting reuse with different detectors or unnamed segments. The author identifies three gaps: insufficient verified free-text supervision, architectures that cannot readily change vocabulary, and evaluation that rewards annotation conventions and category priors rather than image-grounded relations (§§ 1, 5).

This matters because deploying a relation model requires handling new region sources, sparse annotations, and predicates outside a benchmark’s preferred wording.

## Approach and contributions
- **Separable relation model:** A fine-tuned DINOv3 backbone pools region features; a relation transformer combines subject, object, union, contact, geometry, and scene context. Spatial and semantic branches score independently embedded predicate strings through a predicate-conditioned gate. Object labels are absent from the forward pass, although categories inform two training losses (§§ 3.1–3.3).
- **Vocabulary-scale training:** Synonym-group InfoNCE discounts likely positive but unannotated predicates. A distilled text encoder adds antonym repulsion while preserving neighborhoods, addressing confusion between inverse relations (§ 3.3).
- **RA-4M corpus:** VLM annotation over numbered boxes is followed by geometric rejection, directional repair, and balancing. “Verified” denotes these structural checks; geometrically unconstrained claims remain unchecked (§ 4).
- **OV-SGG-Bench:** Six complementary axes cover transfer, precision, open vocabulary, detector-based deployment, graph information, and spatial discrimination. Associated experiments examine category priors, matching conventions, and transfer-sensitive model selection (§§ 5–8).

## Evidence and main results

All values below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher cross-dataset mean recall | Ground-truth boxes, graph-constrained mR@50: RelateAnything vs OvSGTR is **28.2 vs 10.4** on VG150, **30.6 vs 8.8** on PSG, and **29.5 vs 12.8** on IndoorVG. OvSGTR receives object labels; RelateAnything does not. | Table 4 |
| HICO transfer differs from supervised adaptation | HICO-DET mR@50: **12.7** for the zero-shot tower versus **4.5** for OvSGTR; adding HICO training annotations at a **5% relation share** raises RelateAnything to **31.4**. | Table 4 |
| Better ranking against explicit negatives | Haystack, ground-truth boxes: mean fAP **72.6 vs 52.1**, rare fAP **70.7 vs 44.6**, and predicate ROC-AUC **93.7 vs 83.9**. RelateAnything scores **89%** of labelled cells versus **100%**; unscored cells receive zero. | Table 6 |
| Detector-based advantage persists | PSG, shared YOLO-World, confidence **0.05**, maximum **60 boxes**: mR@50 **20.5 vs 6.2**, R@50 **25.4 vs 17.7**; common pair-recall ceiling **69.6%**. | Table 6 |
| Open-vocabulary ranking depends on matching | PSG with benchmark vocabulary withheld: exact mR@50 favors ROBIN-3B, **20.0 vs 13.0**; synonym matching at **τ = 0.60** favors RelateAnything, **31.3 vs 25.1**. ROBIN retains higher R@50 under both. | Table 5 |
| Graph information improves, acceptance does not | On **199 PSG images**, matched top-ten-pair graphs yield **18.6 vs 13.4** judge-accepted surprisal bits/image, but accepted relations/image are **4.07 vs 4.33**, favoring OvSGTR. | Tables 3, 7 |
| Spatial improvement has limited interpretation | On **2,758 SpatialSense cells**, macro AUC is **0.690 vs 0.591**. Separately reported accuracy is **62.5% vs 59.0%**, below the original boxes-only baseline’s **68.8%**. | § 6.6 |
| In-domain scaling gains exaggerate transfer gains | Doubling the corpus at matched gradient steps improves in-domain R@50 / mR@50 by **15% / 56%**, versus **2.5–3.8% / 9.9–11.9%** on VG150 and PSG transfer. | § 8.1; Figure 8 |
| Real-time cost requires configuration qualifiers | A40 batch-one relation inference with the actual **19,103-string bank** takes **19.3 ms**. End-to-end inference with **243 predicates** takes **30.5 ms eager** or **20.3 ms compiled**; compiled inference changes evaluation metrics. | Tables 34–35 |

The evidence supports stronger transfer and predicate-balanced performance than the evaluated OvSGTR configuration. It does not establish universal superiority, reliable visual spatial reasoning, or equivalent accuracy under compiled deployment. The frequency-baseline experiment measures per-edge top-one accuracy, so its result should not be treated as a direct leaderboard R@K comparison (Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Most evaluations hold out images and annotation styles, not predicate concepts. Concept holdouts across all four transfer benchmarks were not run (§ 10).
- **Author-stated:** RA-4M lacks human-audited precision. Wrong-instance grounding, gaze, misleading overlaps, and source-box errors can survive filtering (§§ 4.1, 10).
- **Author-stated:** Graph-level judging favors OvSGTR at deployed lengths, and per-relation acceptance also favors it. RelateAnything’s information score therefore does not establish better precision (§§ 6.5, 10).
- **Author-stated:** Several conclusions use one seed. The decoupling probes concern this training recipe; they do not rule out relational representations under other objectives (§ 10).
- **Analyst inference:** Latency comparisons confound box budgets, precision, and resolution; the paper acknowledges these differences. Fastest compiled timings cannot be combined with eager accuracy tables as a validated operating point (Tables 11, 35).
- **Analyst inference:** Spatial reporting needs care: Table 3 places the boxes-only accuracy beside AUC, and § G.1’s claimed spatial winner conflicts with Table 29. Neither supports a clean capacity-based conclusion without clarification.
- **Coverage limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, omitting substantial architecture, training, corpus, and evaluation appendix material.

## Open research questions
1. **Does the model generalize to genuinely unseen relation concepts?** Broad concept holdouts remain untested (§ 10). Next experiment: remove predicate families and their synonyms across all transfer benchmarks, then evaluate with a fixed independently calibrated matcher.
2. **How accurate is the generated supervision beyond geometric consistency?** No human precision audit exists (§ 10). Next experiment: audit a stratified sample by predicate family, frequency, and gate coverage, then retrain on corrected subsets.
3. **Can deployment retain accuracy at compiled speed?** Compilation materially changes metrics (Table 35). Next experiment: compare eager and compiled outputs and all evaluation axes under identical regions, vocabulary, and thresholds.
4. **Can different supervision improve spatial reasoning and shared representations?** Current probes are recipe-specific and spatial performance remains weak (§§ 6.6, 10). Next experiment: add patch-level relational supervision and compare matched shared versus separate backbones using counterfactual spatial tests.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied tables support the main comparative findings, but source truncation prevents complete coverage. Material gaps include omitted appendix specifications, human-audited corpus precision, broad concept-holdout results, uncertainty estimates for several comparisons, and validated accuracy for the fastest deployment configuration. Public asset availability was claimed by the paper and was not independently checked.
