# Disentangling Representation Evolution in Transformers through Directional Decomposition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15975) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15975)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across evaluated language models, parallel-update suppression is substantially less disruptive than perpendicular editing when applied to self-preserving attention value aggregation, while training with full-aggregate parallel suppression improves reported average benchmark accuracy.

## Problem and motivation
Transformer updates mix changes along the incoming representation with changes perpendicular to it. The authors ask whether the parallel component provides essential computation or redundant magnitude modulation, and whether its importance depends on where the decomposition occurs. They connect this question to activation editing, compression diagnostics, and pretraining (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Directional intervention framework:** Project updates onto a reference vector and its orthogonal complement, then scale each component independently. Residual-space interventions reference the incoming hidden state; attention value-space interventions reference the current token’s value (§§3–4).
- **Self-preserving value editing:** Separate direct self-attention from cross-token aggregation, edit only the latter, and restore the unchanged self message. This distinguishes contextual parallel suppression from removing the entire aggregate’s parallel component (§4.2).
- **Compression diagnostic:** Decompose compressed-model update errors relative to the *baseline update*, a different reference from the editing experiments, and compare directional distortion across compression settings (§6.3).
- **Training intervention:** Suppress parallel attention components throughout training and evaluation. The larger-model value-space experiments use **full-aggregate removal**, unlike the primary self-preserving inference intervention (§7; Table 12).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Directional sensitivity depends on intervention site | On Qwen3-1.7B/WikiText-2, editing every decoder layer, exclude-self parallel scaling changes PPL by less than one point over scales 0–1 and at most 1.3 points through scale 3; perpendicular edits are substantially more disruptive. | §6.1; Figure 3 |
| Preserving self messages substantially improves removal robustness | Seven-task mean scores: Qwen3-1.7B baseline **61.61%**, exclude-self **60.10%**, full-aggregate removal **53.41%**; Qwen3-30B-A3B **73.42%**, **73.31%**, and **63.42%**, respectively. | Table 1 |
| Long-context robustness is partial | Llama-3.2-3B mean accuracy across 13 RULER tasks at 12k tokens: baseline **79.30%**, half-scale exclude-self **76.88%**, complete exclude-self removal **62.02%**, complete full-aggregate removal **39.17%**. | Table 2 |
| Smaller models show larger retained-scale losses | RULER-4k exclude-self scaling at 0.5 gives reported changes of **−5.3**, **−4.8**, and **−3.2 percentage points** for Gemma-3-1B-IT, Qwen3-0.6B, and Qwen3-1.7B; larger listed models remain within 0.5 points. | Table 8 |
| Perpendicular compression error gives a clearer ordering | On Qwen3-4B over 12 prompts, perpendicular error orders 4-bit AWQ, then 50% Wanda unstructured, 4:8, and 2:4 pruning; parallel-error curves overlap. Reported layerwise Pearson correlations between total and perpendicular error range **0.970–0.998**. | Figure 6; Table 11 |
| Training-time removal improves mean downstream scores | Six-task means after FineWeb100BT pretraining: full-aggregate value removal improves **58.5% → 59.2%** at 1.4B and **60.2% → 61.7%** at 2.7B; residual attention removal reaches **58.8%** and **60.9%**. | Appendix E; Tables 3, 12 |

These interventions support a conditional robustness finding, rather than universal parallel-component dispensability. Compression results demonstrate geometric associations; they do not establish the authors’ stronger claim that perpendicular error is a causal predictor superior to total error. Training results support improved averages under the reported configurations, without directly measuring the proposed redistribution of representational capacity.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Other architectures and training regimes remain untested; practical training or inference applications require further validation (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation ambiguity:** Main-text evaluations are called zero-shot, but Appendix A lists nonzero default shot counts for several included tasks. Actual per-experiment overrides are unspecified.
- **Analyst inference—overstated generality:** Table 8 contains meaningful small-model degradation. Appendix E’s claim of uniform task gains also conflicts with the 1.4B BoolQ scores in Table 12.
- **Analyst inference—compression evidence:** No accompanying compressed-model downstream score table or controlled comparison against isotropic-error prediction is supplied. Correlation with total error cannot establish incremental predictive value.
- **Analyst inference—geometric ambiguity:** The text allows head-dependent block-diagonal attention operators but calls the self message necessarily collinear with the full value vector. That requires clarification of whether projection is per-head or over concatenated heads (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Reproducibility:** Training-seed variability and confidence intervals for benchmark improvements are absent; smaller-model training configurations are incompletely specified.

## Open research questions
- **What makes self-preservation more important at some scales and context lengths?** Tables 2 and 8 show uneven robustness. Sweep removal strength, model size, and context length with matched self-preserving and full-aggregate edits.
- **Does perpendicular error predict compression damage beyond total error?** The supplied evidence lacks that comparison. Evaluate held-out compression configurations using directional and isotropic predictors against measured downstream degradation.
- **How reliable are the pretraining gains, and which removal variant works best?** Larger-model results lack seed variability and use full-aggregate removal. Repeat matched training runs across seeds, directly comparing full-aggregate and exclude-self suppression.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The tables support the central conditional robustness and average training-gain findings. Material gaps include exact evaluation shot settings, multi-head projection semantics, training-run variability, complete smaller-model training recipes, quantitative compression-quality comparisons, and a usable code-repository link.
