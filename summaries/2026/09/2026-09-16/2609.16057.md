# OmniHarness: Harnessing Generalizable Visual Generation via Symbolic Policy Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16057) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16057)


## One-sentence takeaway

OmniHarness learns reusable ComfyUI workflow policies through self-generated practice and verified execution, reporting substantial gains in creative task completion without updating model weights, at additional computational cost (§§3–4; §A.9).

## Problem and motivation

Visual generation agents must construct reliable workflows, preserve task constraints, and transfer experience to unfamiliar requests. The authors identify three gaps: experience remains tied to individual examples, reflection arrives after errors propagate, and learning begins only when downstream tasks expose missing capabilities (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Symbolic policy learning:** Successful executions become workflow templates with preconditions, expected effects, dependencies, and reliability statistics. Instance-specific inputs are removed; a separate library stores failures and remedies (§3.3; §A.1.4).
- **Feedback-guided execution:** Agents plan, compile a restricted Code-as-Policy representation into ComfyUI graphs, verify intermediate outputs, and repair failed components while preserving verified work (§3.2; §§A.1.3, A.10).
- **Self-directed inquiry:** Practice selection combines underexplored capabilities with a heuristic favoring intermediate estimated competence. The default uses 50 inquiry iterations; model weights remain fixed while policy libraries evolve (§3.1; §A.2).
- **Policy portability:** Frozen inquiry snapshots are mapped into existing agents’ knowledge interfaces. This differs from ordinary OmniHarness evaluation, where downstream policy updates remain active (§§A.1.4, A.6).

These are the authors’ proposed contributions; the supplied evidence does not independently establish priority over all prior systems.

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. Unless specified otherwise, OmniHarness uses Codex GPT-4o; compared agents share generation models within each evaluation setting and have a maximum of four retries (§A.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong creative workflow performance | ComfyBench Total Resolve: 92.5%; Creative: 95.0% versus SymbOmni’s 67.5%. Complex: 83.3%, below ComfyMind’s 85.0%. | §4.1; Table 1 |
| Gains persist with matched reasoning models | GPT-4o Total Resolve: OmniHarness 89.5% versus ComfyMind 83.0%; Gemini-2.5-Flash: OmniHarness 90.0% versus SymbOmni 86.0%. | §A.4.5; Table 13 |
| Strong compositional generation | GenEval overall: 0.997, exceeding SymbOmni by 0.017. GenEval2 Attribute, Count, Position, Verb: 94.0, 94.0, 76.9, 89.0—respectively 2.6, 19.2, 6.7, 2.3 points above the strongest competitors. | §§A.5.2–A.5.3; Tables 2–3 |
| Knowledge-guided results are mixed | WISE WiScore: 0.86, above SymbOmni and GPT-Image-1 by 0.06. KRIS-Bench overall: 77.33, above SymbOmni by 5.15 points but below GPT-Image-1 by 2.76. | §§A.5.5–A.5.6; Tables 4, 15 |
| Editing advantages depend on scenario and metric | Reason-Edit Understanding: best reported PSNR 23.894 dB, SSIM 0.856, LPIPS 0.053, CLIP Score 24.554. Reasoning: best SSIM 0.796 and CLIP Score 21.318, but LPIPS 0.052 versus best 0.049. | §A.5.4; Table 5 |
| Inquiry and online updates contribute | Removing inquiry reduces ComfyBench Total Resolve from 92.5% to 87.0% and Creative from 95.0% to 72.5%; disabling downstream updates reduces Total Resolve to 88.5%. | §4.4; Table 6 |
| Planning and intermediate checks matter | Removing planning, intermediate verification, or localized recovery reduces Complex Resolve from 83.3% to 55.0%, 68.3%, or 76.7%, respectively. | §4.4; Table 8 |
| Original source images are not essential in the tested setting | Replacing ComfyBench inquiry images with 33 independently generated images yields 91.5% Total Resolve versus 92.5%. | §A.4.6; Table 14 |
| Frozen policies improve external agents | ComfyBench Total Resolve increases from 32.5% to 57.0% for ComfyAgent, 83.0% to 88.0% for ComfyMind, and 86.0% to 89.0% for SymbOmni. | §A.6; Table 16 |
| Image policies support video workflows | GPT-4o with frozen image-only policies reaches 85.9% Resolve on ComfyBench video tasks, 5.1 percentage points above SymbOmni; video-specific components supply temporal operations. | §A.7; Table 17 |

| Reported cost excludes generation runtime | Across 200 ComfyBench tasks: average 12.03K tokens and 52.9 seconds of agent-side reasoning per task; visual generation execution is excluded. | §A.4.4; Figure 11 |

The evidence supports gains across several tasks, useful component contributions, and portability within the evaluated ComfyUI ecosystem. It does not establish universal superiority, equal total-compute efficiency, or transfer to unrelated tool environments. The headline SymbOmni comparison uses different reasoning configurations; matched-backbone comparisons provide additional, narrower support.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Inquiry and repeated planning, verification, and recovery add computation, particularly for complex tasks (§A.9).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation order:** The live library learns across successive benchmark tasks, so performance may depend on task order. A frozen-library ablation exists, but order-randomization results are not supplied (§§A.4, A.4.3).
- **Analyst inference—verification dependence:** Verification governs success records, reliability estimates, and policy admission. The supplied text does not quantify verifier error or its effect on accumulated policies (§§A.1.3–A.1.4, A.10).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Video transfer combines image policies with video-specific components; it does not demonstrate learning temporal operations solely from image practice (§A.7).
- **Evidence availability:** Most quantitative tables contain captions but no table bodies in the retrieved text; many results are recoverable only from narrative summaries. Repeated-run uncertainty and statistical significance are not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **How much gain comes specifically from abstraction?** The available ablations do not isolate abstract templates from stored concrete executions. Compare both representations using identical practice histories, retrieval budgets, and generation models.
2. **How robust is continual learning to task order and verifier mistakes?** Online updates make both consequential, but neither sensitivity is quantified. Randomize task streams and audit verification against independent judgments.
3. **Do gains survive equal total-compute budgets?** Reported reasoning cost excludes generation execution and does not provide an end-to-end matched-budget comparison. Compare inquiry plus downstream execution against baselines at equal total cost.
4. **How portable are policies beyond ComfyUI?** All reported experiments use that environment. Test frozen policies through adapters to another workflow engine, measuring adapter effort and retained task performance.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported outcomes are well described, including substantial appendix evidence. Material gaps include most table bodies, complete generation-model configurations, total inquiry and generation costs, repeated-run uncertainty, verifier calibration, task-order sensitivity, and user-study sampling details. The supplied code link does not establish the completeness of released artifacts.
