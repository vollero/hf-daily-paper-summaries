# Register Tokens for Bounded-State Reasoning in Diffusion Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-16.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16372) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16372)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors train diffusion language models to preserve reasoning across cleared generation windows in four continuous register tokens, improving every main comparison against four-token discrete carry and showing the largest gains on multi-chunk code generation, although full-context decoding remains more accurate at a 1,024-token horizon.

## Problem and motivation

Diffusion language models generate masked tokens in parallel with bidirectional attention, but maintaining a coherent reasoning process across multiple chunks normally requires retaining all prior generated text. This makes attention cost grow with the total generation length.

The paper asks whether a model can instead clear each completed chunk and continue from a fixed-size state. Existing reasoning methods retain generated context, while prior compression and latent-reasoning methods do not directly address persistent state across cleared diffusion-generation chunks (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Continuous register state:** The model reserves fixed positions whose last-layer hidden states are saved after each chunk, injected into the same positions for the next chunk, and repeatedly overwritten. The prompt remains available, while completed chunks are removed (Sections 3.2–3.3; Figure 2).

- **Training against state-ignoring shortcuts:** Chunked supervised fine-tuning sometimes blocks continuation tokens and registers from attending to the prompt and always includes one fully masked pass among four denoising passes. Under both conditions, the carried registers are the only source of task and prior-chunk information (Section 3.3; Proposition 1).

- **Matched carry comparisons:** Four registers are compared with the last four generated token IDs, four reconstruction-trained memory tokens, and full-sequence SFT without carry, using LLaDA-8B-Base and Dream-7B-Base (Section 4.1; Table 1).

- **Reinforcement learning extension:** Chunked diffu-GRPO assigns reward to accumulated chunk prefixes and lets gradients reach the immediately preceding register-writing transition (Section 4.6; Equations 5–6).

- **State analysis:** Carry/reset and state-corruption interventions test whether later chunks use register contents; linear probes examine whether arithmetic state variables are decodable (Section 4.7; Tables 4–5).

- **Released assets claimed by the authors:** Code, the training mixture, and checkpoints are stated as available or forthcoming through GitHub and a Hugging Face collection (end of Section 1).

The evidence supports the authors’ central claim that trained continuous registers can transmit useful information across cleared diffusion-generation chunks and generally outperform an equal-count discrete-token carry channel under their main recipe. It also supports a narrower practical advantage for outputs that must span several chunks, especially code.

It does not establish superiority to full-context decoding in accuracy, equal information capacity between continuous and discrete slots, robust scaling beyond trained horizons, or consistent gains on math over ordinary full-sequence SFT. Several comparisons also combine channel differences with different prompt-masking recipes.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Full-context decoding is more accurate at the tested 1,024-token horizon, and the timing study omits peak memory, visible-history blockwise decoding, and performance beyond the backbone context limit (Section 5).
- Full-sequence SFT leads all eight main \(C=128\) math rows. At \(C=64\), registers remain below it on every reported math benchmark (Section 5; Table 11).
- Continuous and discrete channels are matched by slot count, not information capacity, and use different prompt-masking probabilities. The comparison therefore measures each channel together with its training recipe (Section 5; Appendix B).
- Prompt masking and the number of denoising passes are not separately ablated (Section 5).
- Most SFT comparisons use one seed, and not every numerical lead is individually significant. Historical analyses also use earlier scorers that differ from the corrected first-answer metric (Section 5; Appendix B).
- The unmodified recipe degrades an instruction-tuned initialization in one unmatched pilot, so transfer to instruction-tuned models is unresolved (Section 5; Table 14).
- Interpretability evidence is strongest on LongArithmetic, where the relevant state is explicit (Section 5).
- The synthetic write/read study covers one task family, is unstable across seeds, and requires direct state supervision to reliably initiate register use (Section 5; Appendix H).

**Analyst inferences**

- The main result does not isolate whether continuous representations, prompt masking, or their interaction causes the advantage because the discrete baseline uses \(p_{\text{prompt}}=0\), while register training uses 0.3 or 0.7.
- The four-register design has limited evidence for capacity scaling: the \(N=16\) result changes substantially with training duration and learning-rate cooldown and uses one seed (Section 4.5; Tables 22).
- Probe results establish linear decodability, but they do not by themselves show that the decoded variables are the precise causal features used during generation.
- The reported speedups are full-budget wall-clock measurements with early stopping disabled; they do not establish end-to-end latency under normal stopping behavior.

## Open research questions

1. **Which training component creates effective register use?**  
   Prompt masking, forced fully masked passes, and continuous slots are not independently ablated. A factorial experiment varying these components under matched update budgets and masking probabilities would isolate their effects.

2. **How should continuous and discrete channels be compared at matched capacity?**  
   Four hidden vectors can encode more information than four token IDs. A next experiment could constrain register dimensionality or bit rate and compare it with discrete summaries under a measured information budget.

3. **Can registers retain useful state beyond the trained horizon?**  
   Strict LongArithmetic exact match collapses beyond eight operations. Training on varied horizon lengths, then evaluating held-out lengths and boundary counts, would test systematic state extrapolation.

4. **Can the method transfer reliably to instruction-tuned models and other domains?**  
   The single LLaDA-Instruct pilot degrades under the base-model recipe, while the main evaluations cover math and Python code. Matched register, discrete-carry, and vanilla-SFT sweeps on instruction-tuned models across additional long-output tasks would test generality.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the full main paper and extensive appendices with protocols, counts, uncertainty intervals, ablations, and limitations.

Material gaps remain:

- The exact numerical main-table benefit attributable separately to prompt masking, fully masked passes, and continuous state is unavailable.
- Multi-seed variance is missing for most SFT and scaling experiments.
- Peak memory usage and comparisons with a visible-history blockwise decoder are not reported.
- Performance beyond the backbone context limit is not evaluated.
- Complete release contents and executable reproducibility cannot be verified from the supplied text.
- Some historical results cannot be rescored because their original outputs are unavailable.
