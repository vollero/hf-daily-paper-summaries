# Playing to Par: Reinforcement Learning for Provably Optimal Quadrilateral Block Decompositions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32146) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32146)


## One-sentence takeaway
A half-edge reinforcement learning policy initialized from constructed optimal solutions reaches certified minimum connectivity irregularity on an average 90.2 of 96 held-out planar domains, with weaker optimality at larger sizes and substantial repair costs on curved boundaries (Table 1; §7.1).

## Problem and motivation
The task is to partition planar domains into well-shaped quadrilateral blocks while minimizing vertex valence irregularity. Irregular connectivity persists through refinement and matters for structured numerical discretizations; existing manual and heuristic approaches often do not target optimality (§1).

The authors use a discrete Gauss–Bonnet lower bound, called **par**, determined by topology and corner angles. Reaching it certifies minimum irregularity, but random exploration rarely finds such solutions, making reinforcement learning from scratch ineffective (§§2, 5). The certificate concerns connectivity irregularity, not globally optimal element quality or element count.

## Approach and contributions
- **Mesh-editing policy:** Start from the bare domain boundary and build a decomposition using three local chord insertions and edge splitting. A convolution follows the half-edge structure’s `next`, `previous`, and `twin` pointers, with shared parameters that accommodate varying mesh sizes (§§3–4).
- **Certified demonstrations and RL training:** Construct optimal meshes from polyomino, annular, polar, and pinwheel families; undo legal edits and reverse the trajectories into demonstrations. Behavior cloning precedes PPO, using a size-normalized reward targeting par, quadrilateral completion, and quality (§5; Appendix C).
- **Benchmark and scoring assets:** Introduce GEO2D, a seeded mechanical-outline generator, and evaluate completion, usability, excess over par, and certified optimality against seven Gmsh configurations (§§1, 6; Appendix G).

The bound itself comes from prior work; the contribution is using it as an RL objective and termination certificate (§7.2). Geometry depends on smoothing, and reported performance includes multiple attempts and repair (§6.1; Appendix K).

Usability means minimum shape quality \(q\geq0.3\); certified-optimum counts require par and \(q\geq0.1\). Every method receives the same scoring smoother. Main agent results use five attempts, doubled move budgets, and up to three repair rounds (§6.1).

These results support strong regularity on the supplied synthetic suites. They do not establish universal optimality, single-rollout performance, or superiority in every quality comparison: denser Gmsh meshes sometimes have better minimum quality (Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The policy depends on smoothing; it is slower than Gmsh; curved-boundary quality requires repair search lasting up to ten minutes per domain (§7.1).
- **Author-stated:** Larger-domain optimality varies substantially across training seeds, so those results should be interpreted as ranges rather than a stable single value (Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is concentrated on generated mechanical outlines. The larger straight-sided suite caps boundary-edge length ratios, and nonzero-par whole-domain tests come from the certified generator; arbitrary CAD geometry remains untested here (Appendices D, K).
- **Analyst inference:** The Transformer comparison does not isolate convolution versus attention because the Transformer lacks explicit adjacency. The authors themselves suggest adjacency-aware attention could perform comparably (Appendix J).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** The authors report no curved-boundary training, but cloning model selection used a development set containing curved shapes. Transfer therefore reflects unseen training geometry, with some curved development exposure (Appendix I).
- **Release uncertainty:** The text says core code and checkpoint are available, while GEO2D and per-domain records “will be released”; their actual availability cannot be verified from the supplied text (p. 10; Appendix H).

## Open research questions
- **Can a policy produce usable geometry with less smoothing and repair?** Current quality depends on both. Train with explicit geometry actions and compare usability, irregularity, and runtime under reduced smoothing (§7.1; Appendix K).
- **What causes the larger-domain optimality drop?** Completion remains strong while at-par counts fall and training-seed variance grows. Compare larger training domains and larger observation windows under fixed evaluation budgets (§6.3; Appendix J).
- **Does explicit adjacency explain the architectural advantage?** The tested Transformer lacks it. Compare DCEL convolution with adjacency-biased attention using matched data, parameters, and training budgets (Appendix J).
- **Does performance transfer to broader geometry?** Existing suites constrain shape construction and geometric extremity. Evaluate independently sourced planar CAD outlines with wider feature scales and nonzero par (Appendices D, K).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and benchmark results; **medium** in broader generalization.

Unavailable evidence includes independently verified artifact availability, evaluations on external CAD collections, downstream numerical-simulation accuracy, and a matched adjacency-aware attention comparison. The supplied text does not establish that par is attainable for every admissible domain.
