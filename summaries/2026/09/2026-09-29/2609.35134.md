# VideoPhysEdit: Physical Counterfactual Video Editing via Rigid-Body Physical Scene Reconstruction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35134) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35134)


## One-sentence takeaway
VideoPhysEdit reconstructs and intervenes on a simulated rigid-body scene to guide video generation, achieving better counterfactual motion accuracy than the evaluated editors on PCVE-RigidBench, with qualitative evidence on real videos.

## Problem and motivation
Physical counterfactual video editing changes what happens after an intervention—such as removing a collision partner or changing friction—while preserving earlier footage. The authors identify two gaps: existing editors provide limited support for diverse physical interventions, and conventional editing metrics do not establish whether downstream motion is correct (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified task:** PCVE takes a source video, natural-language physical edit, and execution frame; it covers object insertion/removal, motion-state changes, and physical-parameter changes (§3.1).
- **Training-free reconstruction and editing pipeline:** VideoPhysEdit tracks objects, reconstructs sphere/box geometry and support surfaces, builds a motion prior, and initializes rigid-body parameters from motion and contact constraints. Simulation search then fits observed masks. The edited simulation supplies projected trajectories and a reference image to pretrained video generation (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Benchmark and metric:** PCVE-RigidBench supplies paired factual/counterfactual videos and physical ground truth. Physical Edit Score (PES) measures trajectory-error improvement over leaving the source unchanged (§4). These are introduced assets; their public release status is not clearly established in the supplied text.

The results support improved measured counterfactual motion within this synthetic rigid-body setting. Real-video comparisons remain qualitative and lack paired counterfactual targets (Appendix C.6). PES excludes inserted objects themselves; TE measures aligned image-centroid displacement rather than full 3D physical correctness (Appendix B.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Static cameras, planar supports, and sphere/box geometry exclude moving cameras, complex geometry, nonplanar supports, articulated bodies, and actively controlled agents. Fixed thresholds can depend on scale and observation quality (Appendix D).
- **Documented failures:** Incorrect identities and weak correspondences break reconstruction; dense contacts propagate simulation errors; generation can deviate after contact or retain objects after they leave view (Appendix C.2.3).
- **Analyst inference—identifiability:** Matching factual motion does not uniquely recover physics. The authors explicitly identify ambiguous material factors and absolute mass scales, so factual fit alone cannot establish correct responses to every intervention (§3.3; Appendix A.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Methods are evaluated over different output durations: Ditto covers 73 frames, Seedance 89, and VideoPhysEdit 96. This complicates direct comparison across downstream events (Table 5).
- **Resource burden:** Counterfactual generation averages **665.5 seconds per edit** and peaks at **39.0 GiB GPU memory**, following reusable source reconstruction (Table 15).

## Open research questions
- **Can equivalent factual fits produce different counterfactuals?** Parameter ambiguity remains explicit. A next experiment could retain several similarly fitting scenes and measure their counterfactual trajectory spread against ground truth.
- **How can dense contact sequences become reliable?** Documented failures begin at closely spaced or inseparable contacts. A next experiment could vary contact spacing systematically and compare contact-aware fitting with the current search.
- **Can generation enforce object presence as reliably as motion?** Trajectory controls do not directly enforce disappearance after image exit. A next experiment could add visibility conditioning and evaluate exits and insertion collisions.
- **How well does accuracy transfer to real footage?** Current real-video evidence lacks paired targets. A next experiment could record controlled factual/intervention pairs and evaluate motion and presence quantitatively.
