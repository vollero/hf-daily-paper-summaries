# REALM: A Coarse-to-Fine Generative Framework for Embodied Reactive Listening

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33095) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33095)


## One-sentence takeaway
REALM combines delay-aware audio–motion fusion with stochastic expression refinement, reporting better listener-motion accuracy and inter-frame dynamics than evaluated baselines on ViCo and L2L, plus higher perceptual ratings on Ameca (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Reactive listeners must respond to preceding speaker cues while continuing their existing motion, and generate brief, variable expressions such as blinks. The authors identify two gaps: insufficiently explicit temporal alignment and deterministic reconstruction that can average away plausible local variation (§1, Fig. 1). This matters for responsive conversational avatars and humanoid robots.

## Approach and contributions

- **Delay-aware reactive fusion:** Shifted ALiBi attention favors audio near a nominal response lag; a learned gate balances aligned audio against listener history. The lag is a soft prior, not a prescribed response latency (§3.2).
- **Expression-specific stochastic refinement:** A coarse decoder predicts expression and pose. Audio-conditioned latent noise produces expression residuals while leaving coarse pose unchanged. Training freezes the coarse generator before refinement with reconstruction and adversarial objectives (§3.3; Appendix B.6).
- **Embodied evaluation:** The authors evaluate motion benchmarks and component ablations, then retrain all evaluated methods using semantic blendshapes for Ameca. Robot execution uses deterministic mapping, smoothing, relative calibration, and final control clamping (§3.4; Appendices B.5, C).

The proposed contribution is this combination of task-specific mechanisms; the paper explicitly builds on prior history-conditioned and generative approaches (Appendix F.1).

These results support improvements under the evaluated coefficient-space and robot-video protocols. They do not establish streaming performance, universal superiority, or causal interpretation of gate activations. Blink evidence tests one class of local dynamics through a rendering-and-landmark pipeline.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Generation uses offline windows; streaming requires explicit temporal-access and latency control. A shared delay prior may miss individual variation, and other robots require new morphology-aware mappings (§5).
- **Author-stated:** ARIG and Wang et al. are not quantitatively reproduced; compatible inputs and evaluation pipelines remain an outstanding comparison (Appendix F.2).
- **Author-qualified:** Nearest gate-peak alignment needs peak-density and chance-alignment controls; velocity association does not establish causality or prevention of long-horizon drift (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Robot-video ratings on one platform and sampled ViCo sequences provide limited evidence for sustained live interaction. Benchmark and embodiment experiments also use different motion representations and retraining (Appendices B.5, E).
- **Analyst inference:** Component ablations support the refinement module, but do not fully isolate noise conditioning, adversarial supervision, channel attention, and temporal attention.

## Open research questions

- **Can REALM operate causally with acceptable latency?** Offline operation leaves this unresolved (§5). Test strictly causal audio encoding, refinement, and smoothing while measuring total latency and motion quality.
- **Would adaptive delays improve generalization?** The shared prior may miss individual differences (§5). Compare fixed and context-conditioned delays on held-out listeners.
- **Which refinement mechanisms produce structured variation?** Existing ablations do not separate all components. Hold capacity and training budget fixed while removing modulation, adversarial loss, or attention individually.
- **Do perceptual gains survive live interaction and different robots?** Current evidence uses Ameca video reviews. Run live conversational evaluations and transfer the mapping to another embodiment.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the architecture and reported comparisons, but Tables 1–5 and 8 contain captions without their full result rows. Missing material includes complete benchmark and MOS scores, numerical blink-rate results, benchmark uncertainty across runs, participant demographic details and exclusions, a chance baseline for gate alignment, measured end-to-end latency, and fully specified numerical retargeting weights.
