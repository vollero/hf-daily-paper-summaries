# Self-Evolving Coding Agents: From Digital Programs to Physical-World Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35432) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35432)


## One-sentence takeaway
HexaAnything combines executable world state, robot workflows, and independent verification to improve manipulation and support simulated experiments, but its evidence establishes bounded tool and model updates rather than autonomous evolution of the entire system.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that action-prediction interfaces leave task progress, completion conditions, and recovery implicit, making long tasks difficult to inspect and repair. Existing programmatic agents expose some of this structure, but often keep verification and representations outside the learning loop. The proposed gap is an executable interface that turns physical observations and failures into persistent, testable artifacts and training data (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Physical Coding:** Couples *Code as World*—objects, observations, constraints, and progress predicates—with *Code as Policy*—tool calls, branching, verification, and recovery. Entries retain observation provenance (§§3.2–3.3).
- **HexaAnything:** Implements this interface through a runtime, called the Harness, with typed workflows, explicit execution contracts, independent verdicts, and recovery. A VLA can serve as an action tool; benchmark evaluators determine simulation trial success (§§5.1–5.5).
- **Persistent improvement:** Verified traces train HexaModel; failed executions guide tool revisions evaluated against fixed task criteria. The demonstrated updates are narrower than the proposed co-evolution of representations, environments, models, and hardware (§§4, 6).
- **PhyBench:** Introduces a simulated laboratory benchmark requiring agents to design and execute experiments, collect valid evidence, and estimate physical parameters (§7).

The claimed novelty is the integration of state, policy, verification, and reusable learning records; code-generated robot policies themselves are acknowledged prior work (§§2.2–2.4).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; several table bodies are absent from the supplied extraction, so numbers come from accompanying prose.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Harness improves composite-task success | With fixed XR-1 and matched maximum environment steps, GPT-5.6-Sol HexaAnything versus native XR-1: Composite-Unseen **38.3% vs 34.3%**, Composite-Seen **61.5% vs 54.8%**, overall **61.1% vs 56.6%**. Evaluation: 50 seeds per task across 18 atomic, 16 seen-composite, and 16 unseen-composite tasks. | §6.1; Table 1 caption |
| Advantage over a general coding agent is modest | Same planner and action tool: HexaAnything overall **61.1%**, Codex **59.5%**; Composite-Unseen **38.3% vs 34.1%**. Codex leads Atomic-Seen by **0.8 percentage points**. Same evaluation as above. | §6.1; Table 1 |
| Extra action budget alone does not explain one task’s gain | PortionHotDogs: native XR-1 succeeds in **22/100** standard-budget trials and **21/100** doubled-budget trials; coding-agent condition succeeds in **37/100**. | §6.1.1, “Additional checks” |
| Fine-tuned planner improves within the same Harness | HexaModel versus base Qwen3.8-27B: Atomic-Seen **81.0% vs 80.8%**, Composite-Seen **62.3% vs 61.0%**, Composite-Unseen **39.5% vs 37.3%**, overall **61.7% vs 60.5%**. Same task/seed counts as the Harness comparison. | §6.2; Table 4 caption |
| Revised folding tool transfers to a small held-out seed set | Final tool succeeds on **3/5 development** and **4/5 held-out** seeds without privileged keypoints, one fresh run per seed without retries. A human selects pixels; this evaluates the tool/interface, not autonomous perception. | §6.3; Fig. 8 |
| Simulated experiments produce valid quantitative estimates | Across three tasks, ten trials each: GPT-6-Astra and Opus 5.5 each produce **30/30 valid runs**, GPT-5.6-Sol **27/30**. Each model’s mean relative error is **below 5% on every task**, averaged only over valid runs. Limits: **7,200 s** wall-clock and **18,000 simulation frames** per run. | §7.3; Table 6 caption |
| Physical deployment demonstrates limited tabletop feasibility | On AgileX with GPT-6-Astra, **five of seven tasks succeed in all three trials**. Folding and block tossing do not always complete. | §8.1; Table 7 caption |

These comparisons support gains from the complete Harness and from replacing its base planner with the fine-tuned checkpoint. They do not isolate each architectural component or establish sustained autonomous co-evolution. PhyBench validity is distinct from success: a valid estimate can exceed its task’s error threshold, and low mean error does not establish that every trial succeeds (§7.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The main Harness comparison uses one action model and one benchmark. Longer custom tasks are designed but unevaluated; architecture and hardware evolution remain future work (§§8.2–8.3, 9).
- **Author-stated sample and intervention limits:** Tool studies use few seeds; folding includes operator-selected pixels and privileged information during development, followed by a restricted re-evaluation. Physical trials are sparse, and timing references use other hardware (§§6.3, 8.1–8.3).
- **Analyst inference—training attribution:** HexaModel mixes Harness data with other embodied and general-domain data. No supplied ablation isolates verified traces, VQA, or recovery examples, so the improvement cannot be attributed uniquely to code traces (§6.2).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism and uncertainty:** The supplied results do not separately ablate world programs, verification, and recovery or report statistical uncertainty for benchmark margins. Their individual effects and the reliability of small differences remain unclear (§§6.1–6.2).
- **Author-stated verification risks:** Evaluator–policy co-adaptation, narrow evaluator optimization, privacy, and physical deployment safety remain unresolved (§8.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Which Harness components cause the gains?** Current comparisons evaluate the combined system. Next experiment: matched-seed ablations of explicit state, verification, and recovery under equal action and compute budgets (§6.1).
2. **What does trace training internalize?** Mixed training data and evaluation inside the same Harness leave this uncertain. Next experiment: data-source ablations followed by testing with reduced Harness assistance and held-out task compositions (§§4.5, 6.2).
3. **Can tool improvement survive autonomous perception and broader physical variation?** Folding uses human-selected pixels, and robot trials are limited. Next experiment: freeze the revised tool and evaluate autonomous point selection across new layouts and objects (§§6.3, 8.1).
4. **Can repeated updates avoid evaluator overfitting?** Sustained co-evolution is not demonstrated. Next experiment: multiple update rounds with a concealed external evaluator and fixed regression tasks (§§4.2, 8.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the core method and reported comparisons, but the source is truncated; this analysis does not provide complete coverage.
