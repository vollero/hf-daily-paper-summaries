# Routing Drift Alone Does Not Diagnose Failure in Merged MoE LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32821) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32821)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across three merged MoE architectures, source-relative routing drift poorly predicts restoration benefits, and the tested source-informed interventions do not establish reliable task recovery despite successful recovery under deliberate router corruption.

## Problem and motivation
Merging specialized MoE language models changes token-to-expert assignments, which can motivate router realignment. The authors identify a diagnostic gap: disagreement with a source model reveals a structural change, but does not establish that routing caused task harm. Router inputs, router parameters, and expert outputs must be examined separately before repair is justified (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled routing analysis:** Cross source and merged hidden states with source and merged router parameters on aligned tokens; test whether routing distances predict next-token likelihood gains; compare mixture outputs against matched random routes (§3).
- **Task-grounded diagnosis:** Define routing failure as loss recoverable under a specified routing intervention, holding non-routing parameters fixed while allowing downstream states to respond. Validate the tests using deliberate router corruption (§4).
- **Selective Router Repair (SRR):** Construct likelihood-weighted source-derived expert-pair targets, fit ridge corrections on cached merged inputs, and update selected router rows in the final five sparse layers. Independent diagnostics and benchmark evaluation distinguish successful proxy fitting from demonstrated task recovery (§§5–6). The authors report releasing the toolkit and code (abstract; §7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Most changed routes are locally attributable to input shifts | Across three architectures under Average/TA, 77.7–96.9% of changed expert sets change with input-only replacement but not router-only replacement; router-only attribution is at most 1.8%. Analysis uses 256 domain-balanced prompts per setting, continuation tokens, and all sparse layers. | §3.1; Tables A2, A7 |
| Routing divergence weakly predicts restoration utility | Full-routing JS predicts positive source-route NLL gains with AUROC 0.474–0.519 across six settings, versus chance 0.5, on changed-route events in the final five sparse layers. None of the original 16 primary diagnostic tests survives Holm correction. | Figure 3; Tables A3, A6 |
| Different routes can preserve output direction | Source/native mixture mean cosine is 0.896–0.976 across six settings; observed source routes exceed 32 matched random alternatives per event by 0.112–0.147, with positive paired 95% intervals throughout. Inputs and expert parameters are fixed. | §3.3; Tables A10, A12 |
| Tests detect imposed routing harm | On 256 ARC items per OLMoE parent, clean-route replay recovers 12.50–14.84 pp accuracy after four-layer corruption and 26.56–32.81 pp after sixteen-layer corruption. All eight comparisons pass Holm correction across twelve tests; one-layer comparisons do not. | §4.2; Table C15 |
| Natural routing references do not establish recovery | Source-route science replay has no strictly positive accuracy or margin interval across six model/merge settings; OLMoE LC replay likewise fails to establish recovery. Source replay uses 256 items for DeepSeekMoE/OLMoE and 64 for Qwen3. | Table 1; Tables A5, A8 |
| Source advantage does not establish useful correction selection | Across 440 matched positive-support/control pairs, source-direction utility enrichment is −0.341 × 10⁻³ nats/token versus native, 95% CI [−3.323, 2.631], and −0.997 versus the opposite direction, CI [−5.653, 3.868]. | Table 3; Appendix E.2 |
| SRR does not establish average task improvement | Across eight active OLMoE/Qwen3 settings, SRR-minus-parent equal-weight benchmark averages range from −0.056 to +0.139 pp; every paired 95% interval includes zero. Evaluation uses eight benchmarks and 35,326 matched items per pair. | Table 2; §6; Appendix G |

These results support the authors’ distinction between routing disagreement and demonstrated recoverable harm. They do **not** establish optimal merged routing, equivalence between interventions, or absence of recovery under other policies. Mixture cosine measures direction rather than magnitude or downstream task preservation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified scope:** Attribution establishes local sufficiency of input replacement, not the origin of upstream representation changes. Routing recovery also does not identify router-parameter changes as the original cause (§4.1; Appendix A.1).
- **Author-qualified uncertainty:** Benchmark intervals condition on fixed checkpoints and exclude variation across repair seeds, calibration samples, and training runs. They do not support HARC-versus-parent or SRR-versus-HARC significance claims (Appendix G).
- **Narrow intervention coverage:** Qwen3 task replay covers only science, with a smaller sample; local candidate comparisons are bounded rather than exhaustive (Appendices A.8–A.9).
- **Incomplete ablation evidence:** The weighting ablation measures residual MSE, lacks paired uncertainty and complete support-normalization details, and cannot establish task-level superiority (Appendix D.3).
- **Numerical and provenance caveats:** Qwen3 analyses retain different CPU/GPU route-label conventions; DeepSeekMoE SRR provenance and numerical validation are expressly unavailable in the supplied evaluation description (Appendices A.11, G).
- **Analyst inference:** SRR’s negative results constrain this fixed-trace proxy and update scope; they do not settle whether task-loss optimization or refreshed trajectories would yield useful repairs.

## Open research questions
- **Can a task-grounded routing policy recover natural merge losses?** Source and LC replay remain inconclusive. Next experiment: optimize a routing policy on separate training items, then evaluate paired recovery on held-out tasks with experts fixed.
- **Does refreshing hidden-state traces improve SRR execution and utility?** Joint updates alter later inputs, so fixed-input corrections need not execute as fitted (§5.3). Next experiment: compare joint writeback with sequential refitting under matched data and compute budgets.
- **Do intervention benefits generalize across independently constructed checkpoints?** Reported intervals exclude construction variability. Next experiment: repeat specialization, merging, and repair across independent runs, measuring both item and checkpoint uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the central, intervention-specific conclusion; lower for generalization beyond the tested settings.

Material gaps include independently constructed repair replicates, complete specialist-training and construction-prompt provenance, DeepSeekMoE SRR numerical validation, uncertainty for HARC comparisons, and task-level evidence comparing alternative SRR weighting rules.
