# DISCO: Distributed Long Context Scaling with Grounding-Reasoning Disaggregation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33485) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33485)


## One-sentence takeaway
DISCO separates parallel evidence extraction by small Worker LLMs from iterative planning and reasoning by a Driver LLM, improving tested long-context QA accuracy and reducing frontier-model evaluation costs, without establishing universal elimination of context degradation.

## Problem and motivation
Long context windows do not guarantee reliable reasoning over long inputs. The authors argue that searching for relevant evidence competes with reasoning within a monolithic model, producing “context rot.” They identify a gap between similarity-based retrieval, which can miss implicitly relevant facts, and sequential LLM workflows, which incur latency and information loss (§1).

This interference explanation is the authors’ hypothesis; the supplied experiments measure system performance rather than directly measuring representational competition.

## Approach and contributions
- **Distributed grounding with separate reasoning:** DISCO partitions context into persistent shards. Workers extract local evidence; the Driver receives accumulated evidence rather than raw context (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Iterative DAG orchestration:** The Driver plans atomic extraction (“Map”) and evidence synthesis (“Reduce”) actions, then replans when evidence is insufficient. This extends single-pass parallel extraction with dependencies and feedback (§§2.3–2.4; Figure 1).
- **RL-trained planning:** GRPO optimizes the Driver using answer correctness, judge-assessed evidence support, and DAG-format validity. Terminal correctness and evidence rewards propagate to intermediate planning steps (§2.5).
- **Training-data construction:** Recursive Wikipedia-linked expansion of MuSiQue examples produces a filtered long-context training set (§3.2; Appendix A.3). Public release is not established.

These results support DISCO’s effectiveness in the reported configurations. They do not prove the proposed interference mechanism, statistical equivalence to frontier full-context inference, or robustness at arbitrary lengths. “Full Long Context” Qwen baselines are middle-truncated to 128K, and their RULER-QA results are absent (Appendix A.2; Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no dedicated limitations section, but report extraction-loss failures, finite-budget termination, and weak RLM performance with smaller backbones.

**Analyst inferences grounded in the text:**

- **Causal attribution remains incomplete:** Worker/retriever and reward ablations exist, but no controlled ablation separately isolates atomic decomposition, replanning, and grounding–reasoning separation.
- **Comparison scope matters:** Truncation disadvantages the full-context Qwen baseline; the poor RLM results are explicitly associated with backbone capability (Appendix A.2).
- **Evaluation uncertainty is unavailable:** No confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, or significance tests are reported. ∞Bench En.QA uses a model judge instead of its standard F1 metric, without judge validation details (Appendix A.2).
- **Implementation details are uneven:** Case studies use substring extraction keys and a five-stage cap, whereas the main setup specifies ten stages; their relationship to the general shard-scanning algorithm is unclear (Appendix A.5).
- **Reporting inconsistencies require caution:** Figure 2’s “Overall” label and displayed values do not align cleanly with Table 1’s averages. Exact Figure 5 latency values and detailed cost accounting are unavailable.
- **Generality is bounded:** Evaluation covers text QA benchmarks; the claims of eliminating context rot and achieving linear scalability extend beyond directly demonstrated evidence.

## Open research questions
1. **Which orchestration component produces the gains?** Existing ablations do not isolate decomposition from replanning. Compare fixed versus iterative DAGs and atomic versus whole-query Worker prompts under matched token and compute budgets.
2. **Does robustness extend to longer or less locally extractable evidence?** Stable synthetic QA results and dropout tests leave cross-shard dependencies unresolved. Test controlled evidence spanning shard boundaries and additional context lengths.
3. **Can RL improve accuracy without the observed synthetic-QA regression?** Table 1 shows mixed effects. Sweep reward weights and training mixtures, reporting repeated-run results across all three benchmarks.
4. **How broadly does the efficiency advantage hold?** Cost and latency evidence use specific deployments. Vary concurrency, shard size, and hardware while measuring accuracy, total tokens, memory, and end-to-end cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Core methods and tabulated results are clear, but causal and general scalability claims have weaker support.

Material missing information includes artifact-release status, evaluation sample counts and uncertainty, complete prompts and routing rules, decoding settings, detailed cost accounting, judge validation, and exact latency measurements from Figure 5.
