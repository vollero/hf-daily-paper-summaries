# Not All Objectives Are Born Equal: Priority-Constrained Descent for Hierarchical Multi-Objective Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.29521) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29521)


## One-sentence takeaway
PCD combines scale-normalized, primary-anchored gradient projection with a single secondary-pressure parameter to improve reported compression trade-offs, but its directional guarantees do not establish convergence or per-step progress for the deployed optimizer.

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses optimization with one primary task loss and secondary structural penalties. The authors argue that symmetric multi-objective methods can stop at gradient-cancellation equilibria even when individual objectives remain nonstationary; weighted sums also require scale-dependent tuning (§§1–3).

The practical motivation is preserving classification accuracy while inducing sparsity or low rank. The identified gap is an explicit gradient-level hierarchy with a tolerance that remains interpretable across differently scaled penalties.

## Approach and contributions
- **Scale-normalized hierarchical projection:** PCD uses per-objective EMA gradient normalization, then finds the direction closest to the primary gradient subject to secondary inner-product margins controlled by shared tolerance \(\tau\). The authors explicitly acknowledge that the anchored QP already exists in dynamic-barrier methods; the distinguishing contribution is its per-objective, scale-normalized margins (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Analysis and solvers:** Closed forms for two and three objectives, general feasibility and active-set analysis, asymptotic scale invariance, and a normalized-direction fixed-point characterization. For positive \(\tau\) and feasible constraints, a zero direction requires every oracle gradient to vanish; identifying all such points with composite multi-stationarity additionally depends on the subgradient oracle (§§4.5–4.8; Apps. B–C).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Structured pruning, simultaneous unstructured sparsity and low rank, tuned application-specific comparisons, normalization ablations, and synthetic mechanism tests (§5; Apps. E–G).

Deployment rescales the direction to the primary-gradient norm before passing it to Adam, creating material differences from the analyzed QP (§4.7).

The evidence supports useful compression trade-offs under the reported protocols, including advantages over tuned alternatives. It does not establish universal dominance, convergence to joint stationarity, or guaranteed secondary-loss decrease during deployed training. The large general-MOO gaps also require context: the original weighted-sum grid excludes the smaller effective penalties tested in the stronger tuned comparison (§5.1.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ theoretical limits:** No convergence theorem for Algorithm 1. Severe conflict can sacrifice primary descent; incompatible secondaries can make the QP infeasible, triggering a primary-only fallback (§§4.5–4.8).
- **Authors’ deployment limits:** Rescaling halts at an exactly stationary primary even if secondaries remain nonstationary. Momentum and preconditioning can change progress signs (§4.7).
- **Authors’ nonsmooth limits:** A selected subgradient does not guarantee true directional decrease at kinks. The paper supplies a counterexample; the smooth descent window also shrinks as groups approach zero (App. B.8).
- **Analyst inference—selection risk:** Operating points are selected using mean test accuracy without a held-out validation split, which can favor reported results (App. H).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Neural experiments cover CIFAR classification and structural regularizers. Synthetic escape problems are separable and jointly satisfiable; they do not establish behavior on general incompatible objectives (§§5.1–5.3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** App. E.3 describes conflict at every step, while Table 10 reports conflict frequencies below one-half. This weakens that prose interpretation of the compression mechanism.

## Open research questions
- **Can the deployed method converge to joint stationarity?** The normalized-direction theorem leaves rescaling, stochastic gradients, and optimizer dynamics unresolved. Compare normalized steps and alternative rescaling rules on nonseparable problems with known joint stationary points (§4.7; App. C.3).
- **Would worst-case subgradient constraints improve sparse training?** The proposed conservative formulation is not evaluated. Compare it against standard PCD while measuring actual penalty changes and the kink gap near zero groups or singular values (App. B.8).
- **Can adaptive tolerances improve trade-offs with less tuning?** All experiments use one shared scalar tolerance. Test alignment- or feasibility-driven schedules against fixed-\(\tau\) sweeps using validation-based selection and matched search budgets (§6; Remark B.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the core method and reported protocol-specific findings; **medium** in broader superiority claims.

Material information unavailable or unclear includes a released code location, the promised comprehensive reproducibility checklist, CPU latency measurement details, validation-based operating-point selection, and precise thresholding details for the sparsity differences between Tables 5 and 15. No evidence is supplied for general deployed convergence or applications beyond the evaluated compression settings.
