# Recursive Harness Distillation across Agents for Robot Manipulation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33378) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33378)


## One-sentence takeaway
Recursive Harness Distillation transfers a strong agent’s robot-intervention experience into a recursively revised textual playbook, raising light-agent success on held-out SimplerEnv Bridge instances from 43.8% to 66.7% without updating agent or policy parameters (Figure 3).

## Problem and motivation
A vision-language-action (VLA) policy can understand a task yet fail during execution; an external agent must recognize failures and choose effective corrections. Strong agents can discover these interventions, but using them throughout deployment is expensive. The authors address how to retain their experience as guidance that a cheaper agent can reliably apply, including situations arising from that recipient’s own decisions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Recipient-informed playbook refinement:** A strong teacher first interacts with a frozen VLA and writes intervention guidance. A light recipient executes with it, and the teacher revises applicability conditions, intervention choices, and outcome checks using recipient feedback. Knowledge resides in text rather than parameter updates (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Three-site intervention harness:** Agents can rewrite instructions, bias attention over selected visual regions, and modify generated motion or gripper commands. They can inspect candidate actions before executing bounded prefixes (§3.2).
- **Development-time selection:** Candidate playbooks are evaluated on shared development instances; the first meeting the teacher’s empirical success target is accepted. The policy-optimization formulation motivates evaluating recipient trajectories, but does not provide a convergence guarantee (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Empirical demonstration:** The authors test transfer across two agent capabilities and adapt the simulation-derived guidance to physical manipulation with a different VLA (§4).

The claimed contribution is the combined teacher–playbook–recipient process grounded in robot execution, rather than the invention of textual reflection or robot orchestration individually (§2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Refined guidance improves simulation success | On 48 held-out Bridge instances: GR00T alone 41.7%; Luna without playbook 43.8%; Luna with refined playbook 66.7%. | §4.2; Figure 3 |
| Guidance benefits both agents | Same simulation evaluation: Astra without playbook 54.2%; Astra with refined playbook 79.2%. Luna with guidance reaches 66.7%, exceeding unguided Astra. | Figure 3 |
| Initial guidance can hurt | Luna’s simulation success is 31.3% with the initial playbook, versus 43.8% without guidance and 66.7% after refinement. | §4.3; Figure 3 |
| Teacher capability matters in the tested pairing | On 48 held-out Bridge instances with Luna as recipient: Luna-derived refined guidance achieves 22.9%, versus 66.7% for Astra-derived guidance; development limits and interface are matched. | §4.3; Table 2 |
| Instruction editing has the largest observed ablation effect | Luna with fixed refined playbook: full interface 66.7%; without instruction editing 47.9%; without attention editing 64.6%; without action editing 62.5%, on the same simulation evaluation. | §4.4; Figure 3 |
| Adapted guidance improves physical execution | Across 75 trials, 25 per task: π0.5 alone 37.3%; Luna without playbook 0%; Luna with adapted refined playbook 64.0%. Guided successes: Cube-to-Tray 18/25, stacking 13/25, button pressing 17/25. | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Light-agent deployment costs less | With the refined playbook on the same 48-instance Bridge test set, estimated API costs are $30.03 total/$0.63 per episode for Luna and $164.61/$3.43 for Astra, using the paper’s stated token rates. | §5.3; Figure 4 |

These results support useful playbook transfer within the tested tasks and agents. Evaluation configurations are excluded from development, playbooks remain frozen during evaluation, and simulation comparisons share reset seeds and policy checkpoints (§4.1). They do not establish generalization to unseen task families, zero-adaptation transfer to physical robots, or lower total development-plus-deployment cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Explicit methodological limit:** The acceptance rule may never find a candidate meeting the teacher’s target; termination is not guaranteed (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference—refinement attribution:** Later playbooks incorporate both additional interaction experience and recipient feedback. No matched-experience, teacher-only refinement comparison isolates the contribution of recipient feedback (§§4.1, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Evaluation covers four simulation tasks and three physical tasks. Physical deployment uses teacher-adapted guidance and a policy fine-tuned on task demonstrations (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—ablation interpretation:** Removing tools while retaining the full-interface playbook measures sensitivity to that removal, not the best achievable performance of a playbook developed for the reduced interface (§4.4).
- **Reporting gaps:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated independent refinement runs are reported. Development cost, execution latency, and full reproducibility artifacts are unavailable in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does recipient feedback outperform simply collecting more teacher experience?** Refinement adds both (§4.1). Compare recipient-informed and teacher-only revisions under equal rollout and token budgets.
- **How portable is the playbook?** Physical transfer requires adaptation (§4.2). Test unchanged versus adapted guidance on held-out tasks and another policy.
- **When does distillation repay its development cost?** Reported costs cover deployment inference only (§5.3). Measure complete development expenditure and deployment costs over increasing episode counts.
- **How stable are the gains?** The text reports a single set of aggregate outcomes. Repeat playbook development and evaluation independently, reporting uncertainty and variability across resulting playbooks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central method and reported comparisons are clear, but statistical robustness and reproducibility remain uncertain.

Missing information includes the full playbooks and prompts, exact refinement-round and proposal counts, numerical development-budget and playbook-length limits, detailed attention implementation, physical adaptation protocol and success criteria, end-to-end latency, development costs, and an explicit code or artifact release.
