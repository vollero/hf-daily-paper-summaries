# BaRe-Mem: Bayesian Reliability Memory for Robust and Adaptive Agent Consultation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35551) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35551)


## One-sentence takeaway
BaRe-Mem learns contextual advisor reliability from verified feedback to steer attention and choose between consultation and autonomous reasoning, remaining above autonomous accuracy across all tested corruption levels in the capability-challenging suite for six central models (Appendix B.5, Tables 4–9).

## Problem and motivation
Advisor competence varies by task, and fluent incorrect advice can undermine a central model’s correct judgment. The authors identify a gap between retaining interaction history and using it to estimate contextual reliability, regulate advisor influence, and decide whether consultation is worthwhile (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Contextual reliability memory:** A frozen central model represents question context and answer content. Bayesian linear regression combines these representations with source identity, updating reliability from verified correctness through exact rank-one posterior updates (Section 3.1; Appendix A.3).
- **Reliability-guided attention:** Relative advisor reliability supplies additive attention biases that downweight less reliable responses without training the backbone (Section 3.2).
- **Adaptive consultation:** A second Bayesian regression estimates consultation benefit and degradation; the system compares predicted consultation accuracy with its estimated autonomous accuracy (Section 3.3).
- **Worker-routing extension:** Reliability ranks workers for initial assignment and retries on MuSiQue, extending the mechanism beyond response weighting (Section 4.4; Appendix C).

The contribution is this integrated reliability-and-selection mechanism; the mathematical appendix derives its Bayesian updates rather than establishing a new regression family.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Robustness under severe misleading advice | At nominal 100% misleading advice in the challenging suite, Qwen3-14B accuracy is **68.6%**, versus **66.5%** autonomous, **54.7%** always-consult memory, and **50.6%** unweighted peers; Phi-4 achieves **65.0%**, versus **63.4%**, **52.0%**, and **48.3%**, respectively. | Tables 1, 6, 9 |
| Generalization across evaluated central models | Across six models, challenging-suite accuracy remains above autonomous reasoning at every tested corruption level; at nominal 100%, the margin is **1.5–2.8 percentage points**. | Appendix B.5; Tables 4–9 |
| Consultation frequency responds to corruption | From 0% to 100% misleading advice, challenging-suite consultation falls **90%→21%** for Qwen3-14B and **85%→18%** for Phi-4; supported-suite consultation changes **87%→85%** and **85%→83%**. | Table 1 |
| Predicted gains help order consultation decisions | Grouped observed consultation gains increase with predicted gains, with zero crossings reported near the predicted decision boundary for Qwen3-14B and Phi-4 across tested corruption levels. | Section 4.3.2; Figure 4 |
| Sparse feedback is useful | The authors report gains with feedback on less than **1%** of questions, approximately **170 samples**; exact accuracy improvements are not tabulated. | Section 4.3.3; Figure 5 |
| Worker routing improves MuSiQue completion | With dataset-verifier checking on **2,417 tasks**, Qwen3-14B task completion is **54.3%** for BaRe-Mem versus **50.4%** success counts and **43.0%** default lead routing; Phi-4 achieves **54.2%**, **50.5%**, and **42.8%**, respectively. All settings use subsequent gold feedback for queried workers. | Figure 6; Appendix C.1; Figures 25, 27 |
| Adaptive selection is not uniformly best | In the supported suite without injected corruption, Qwen3-8B achieves **77.4%** with BaRe-Mem versus **78.1%** with always-consult memory. | Table 5 |

These results support robustness within the tested benchmark streams and better worker ordering under the supplied feedback protocol. They do not establish universal superiority, calibrated correctness probabilities, or end-to-end cost savings. Nominal 100% corruption can retain some correct advisor responses because of limited sampling (Section 4.2 footnote).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified calibration gap:** Under severe corruption, reliability estimates remain above empirical advisor accuracy. Appendix B.4 attributes this partly to the Gaussian predictive mapping and persistent observation noise.
- **Analyst inference—feedback dependence:** Every routing condition receives gold labels after commitment, including “No check.” Thus, the experiment varies retry checking, not access to gold learning feedback (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference—restricted deployment evidence:** Routing uses dataset-provided sub-tasks, isolating allocation from decomposition quality. The “Random” baseline is the lead agent’s default selection, explicitly described as not completely random (Section 4.4 footnote; Appendix C.1).
- **Evidence inconsistency:** Appendix C.3 claims single-worker superiority over success counts for every lead model, but Figure 24 reports Qwen3-8B no-check task completion of **39.3%** for BaRe-Mem versus **40.6%** for success counts; its sub-task completion is higher. The apparent discrepancy is unresolved.
- **Analyst inference—unproven temporal adaptation:** The cumulative posterior has no stated forgetting mechanism, and no explicit reliability-change experiment is reported. Early learning does not establish recovery after advisor competence changes.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Feature extraction details, routing features available before worker responses, hyperparameter settings, uncertainty intervals, and runtime measurements are absent from the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Can reliability probabilities be better calibrated?** Appendix B.4 documents overestimation under severe corruption. Compare the Gaussian mapping with a binary-outcome likelihood while measuring calibration, decision errors, and final accuracy.
- **Can memory recover after advisor reliability changes?** Existing experiments show accumulation of useful feedback, not explicit change points. Reverse advisor competence midway through a stream and compare cumulative updates with forgetting or sliding-window variants.
- **How robust is routing to imperfect feedback?** Routing always learns from gold labels. Replace them with noisy or delayed verifier outcomes and measure worker ranking and task completion.
- **Does selective consultation reduce total computation?** Autonomous answers and advisor candidates enter the decision process, but costs are unreported. Measure total generation tokens, latency, and verification cost against accuracy-matched baselines.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main numerical robustness results and describes the mechanism clearly, but implementation and evaluation details limit reproducibility.

Material missing information includes precise belief-feature construction, prior and attention-strength settings, corruption-generation procedures, feedback sampling details, prompts and decoding settings, random-seed variability, statistical uncertainty, memory/runtime costs, and the contents of the linked code and dataset.
