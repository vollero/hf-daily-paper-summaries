# Safe Error Correction for Language Models: Frozen-Base Adjustment with Capability Preservation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16145) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16145)


## One-sentence takeaway
CRN v2 raises accuracy on training-overlapping correction exams while matching the frozen base on three small capability evaluations, but the reported “error correction” percentages measure exam accuracy rather than the fraction of base errors repaired (§§3.3, 4.1–4.2; Reproducibility).

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses adapting a language model to fix domain errors without degrading unrelated capabilities. The author frames this as a correction–capability tradeoff and investigates whether a trained correction module attached to a frozen model can improve answers while preserving tested behavior (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Method:** CRN v2 reads Gemma 4 E2B’s final hidden state and adds a gated, low-rank correction to its logits. Only the approximately 34M-parameter module learns; the base remains frozen (§3.1).
- **Training:** Supervised fine-tuning with answer-token weighting and KL anchoring to the base distribution precedes reference-free DPO on correct versus rejected answers. Training uses 83,400 correction pairs (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Empirical contribution:** The study compares correction and capability retention against LoRA and explores KL settings, hidden-state injection, multiple input depths, and longer training (§4).
- **Assets:** The author reports releasing main-result weights, code, evaluation scripts, and recorded results; deep-injection checkpoints are unavailable (Reproducibility).

The paper explicitly disclaims architectural novelty: its contribution is the tested design principle and empirical tradeoff, rather than a new low-rank architecture (§§1, 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| CRN improves original-exam accuracy, but LoRA improves it more | Autoregressive generation with gold-answer substring matching: base 7/60; CRN 32/60 (53.3%); LoRA 83.3%. CRN’s net gain is 25 correct answers. | §4.1; Reproducibility |
| Reworded accuracy remains below LoRA | CRN 52/120 (43.3%) versus LoRA 77.5%, using template-based near-duplicates rather than an independent generalization set. | §§3.3, 4.1; Reproducibility |
| Tested aggregate capabilities match the base | Identical prompts and greedy decoding: base and CRN both score MMLU 125/200, BoolQ 144/200, and car-wash 6/8. LoRA scores MMLU 64/200; reported losses across tasks are 17–75 percentage points. | §4.2; Reproducibility |
| Alternative KL configuration performs worse | Original/reworded accuracy falls from 53.3%/43.3% to 35.0%/25.8%, but rank, KL weight, and DPO duration change together. | §4.3 |
| Hidden-state injection gives mixed results | Layer-7 SFT-only scores 30/60 (50.0%) and 67/120 (55.8%): below logit correction on the original exam, above it on rewordings. These correction results have retained logs. | §4.4; Reproducibility |
| Reported runtime is modest for training, slow for decoding | Apple M4, 16 GB, MPS: approximately 25 minutes for main training; uncached full-sequence inference approximately 1 second/token. | §§3.4, 4.5 |

**Metric qualification:** The paper calls 32/60 a “53.3% correction” rate, although the denominator includes questions the base already answered correctly. Per-question transitions are unavailable, so the actual fraction of base errors repaired cannot be established.

The evidence supports improved accuracy on overlapping exams and unchanged aggregate scores on the tested capability samples. It does not establish out-of-distribution correction, universal preservation, or a causal benefit from KL anchoring alone. Equal aggregate scores also do not establish identical per-question behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** All original exam prompts occur in training; rewordings are near-duplicates, so neither exam establishes out-of-distribution generalization (§3.3).
- **Author-stated:** Capability samples are small, HellaSwag scoring is broken, and evaluation uses one base model (§§4.2, 6).
- **Author-stated:** The KL comparison confounds three variables. Strong claims elsewhere that KL is “critical” exceed this ablation’s causal evidence (§4.3).
- **Comparison limitation:** LoRA matches the older CRN v1 parameter budget, not CRN v2; CRN v2 has roughly five times as many trainable parameters, while LoRA receives longer training (§3.5).
- **Reproducibility limitation:** Most exploratory alternatives lack retained logs/checkpoints. Statements about verification differ across sections; Reproducibility explicitly identifies retained layer-7 SFT correction logs. The capability harness was ephemeral, although result JSONs remain (§4.4; Reproducibility).
- **Analyst inference:** Frozen weights do not guarantee preserved output behavior; the reported capability collapse under deep-injection DPO illustrates this boundary (§4.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does correction transfer to unseen prompts?** Current exams overlap training. Next experiment: hold out distinct facts, arithmetic structures, and goal scenarios before training.
2. **What does KL anchoring independently contribute?** The ablation changes rank and DPO steps too. Next experiment: vary only KL weight with repeated runs and fixed evaluation.
3. **How broad is capability preservation?** Three small samples cannot establish general retention. Next experiment: larger capability evaluations with paired per-question outcomes and repaired HellaSwag scoring.
4. **How does the tradeoff change under matched budgets?** Current methods differ in capacity and training duration. Next experiment: compare CRN and LoRA across matched parameter and training budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main counts and methodological caveats are explicit, but the supplied text contains table captions without complete table bodies.

Missing information includes per-question repaired/regressed outcomes, full baseline results, repeated-run variability, isolated KL effects, genuinely held-out correction tests, a retained capability harness, and verifiable artifacts for most exploratory configurations.
