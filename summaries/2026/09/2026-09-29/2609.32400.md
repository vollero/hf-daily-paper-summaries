# SkillDRE: Dual-Stage Red-Team Evolution of Agent Skills via Pre-Execution and Runtime Feedback

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32400) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32400)


## One-sentence takeaway
SkillDRE alternates scanner-guided and runtime-guided revision of malicious agent skills, achieving a reported 45.28% average attack success rate across four victim models while receiving no findings from the scanner used during optimization (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Reusable agent skills combine instructions, code, and resources; attackers can modify these packages while agents perform legitimate tasks. Passing a pre-execution scanner does not ensure that an attack executes successfully under runtime defense, and changes that improve execution can introduce scanner findings. The authors identify coordinated optimization across these two defense stages as the gap in existing skill-attack methods (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Adaptive threat model:** The attacker modifies one skill while keeping the task, other skills, victim agent, and defenses fixed, with repeated access to scanner and runtime feedback (§3.1).
- **Autonomous objective and evaluator construction:** A generator proposes task-conditioned malicious intents, skill-specific targets, and judge rules. An ensemble validates them; rules also undergo schema checks and an empty-sandbox negative test. Accepted targets and rules remain fixed during evolution (§3.2).
- **Cross-stage package evolution:** Scanner feedback guides package revision until no findings remain. Runtime-defense decisions, trajectories, and target outcomes then guide unsuccessful candidates, with every revision returning for scanner optimization before execution. Refiners are instructed to preserve legitimate functionality, which is evaluated separately (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Main comparisons, stage ablations, human validation, and a harness comparison test attack effectiveness, scanner acceptance, and benign-task performance (§4; Appendices E and G). The claimed novelty is the integrated loop and autonomous fixed-objective construction, rather than either defense tool itself.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| High attack success with scanner acceptance | On 249 skills associated with 94 SkillsBench tasks, SkillDRE averages **45.28% ASR** across four victims under SkillSonar; per-model ASR is **43.78–46.99%**, with **0% SkillScan DR** throughout. | §4.1; Table 2 |
| Large reported baseline advantage | Average ASR exceeds the strongest baseline by **40.3 percentage points**. SkillJect and SkillHarm have per-model ASR no higher than **9.00%**, with DR of **97.34–100%**. All methods use DeepSeek-V4-Pro as attacker. | §§4.1–4.2; Table 2 |
| Benign-task accuracy largely preserved | Relative to No Attack, ACC rises by **2.14–3.58 percentage points** on three victims and falls by **0.15 points** on GLM-5.2, using SkillsBench evaluators. | §4.2; Figure 2; Table 4 |
| Both stages address different objectives | With DeepSeek-V4-Pro as victim, full SkillDRE yields **46.99% ASR / 0% DR**, versus scanner-only iterative **34.94% / 0%** and runtime-only iterative **46.59% / 79.92%**. | §4.4; Table 3 |
| Runtime refinement adds successes | Across four victims, cumulative ASR rises from **34.54–37.35%** on the first runtime trial to **43.78–46.99%** within ten trials, gaining **8.03–12.45 percentage points**. | §4.3; Figure 5 |
| Cold-start scanner acceptance is attainable | All **249** task–skill pairs obtain a zero-finding candidate within the **30-round** Phase 1 budget; mean **3.23 rounds**, versus **34.54%** acceptance initially. | §4.3; Figure 4 |
| Partial human corroboration of evaluators | Uniform random review of **125/249** target–rule pairs finds **100%** agreement on target maliciousness and **96.8%** on judge correctness, including decisions on actual trajectories. | Appendix E |
| Results vary with harness | With DeepSeek-V4-Pro as victim, ASR is **46.99%** in Codex and **42.57%** in Claude Code; final SkillScan DR is **0%** in both. | Appendix G; Table 6 |

These results support the authors’ claim that feedback from both stages can produce attacks that satisfy the evaluated scanner and runtime constraints. Zero DR means no findings from the particular SkillScan configuration used during evolution; it does not establish general undetectability. Likewise, four victim evaluations do not establish transfer of a single evolved package to unseen models or defenses.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text has no dedicated limitations section, but identifies several boundaries:

- **Author-described scope:** The attacker repeatedly queries a fixed defense pipeline and changes only one skill (§3.1). Targets are sandbox-confined (§3.2).
- **Author-observed concentration:** Exfiltration accounts for **72.29% of 249 accepted targets**; other threat categories receive less coverage (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—comparison ambiguity:** SkillHarm uses a filtered subset, explicitly acknowledged for ACC comparisons (Appendix C). The supplied text does not fully explain how that filtering affects Table 2’s ASR comparison or whether baseline refinement budgets are matched.
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** Stage ablations use only DeepSeek-V4-Pro (§4.4). They do not separately isolate runtime-defense diagnostics from target-outcome feedback or test the contribution of optimization memory.
- **Analyst inference—measurement uncertainty:** No repeated-run uncertainty or significance estimates are reported. Human review covers only a subset, with imperfect judge agreement (Appendix E). Aggregate ACC preservation also does not demonstrate that every successful attack preserves its corresponding benign task.
- **Author caveat:** Declining aggregate scanner findings partly reflect accepted candidates leaving later rounds, rather than purely within-candidate improvement (§4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Do evolved packages survive unseen defenses?** Optimization uses the evaluated scanner and fixed runtime defense. A next experiment could freeze final packages and test held-out scanners and runtime policies without additional feedback.
2. **Which runtime feedback produces the gains?** Runtime refinement combines trajectories, stopping diagnostics, and judge outcomes (§3.4). An equal-budget ablation could remove each signal separately.
3. **How reliable are success judgments and benign preservation jointly?** Partial human agreement and aggregate ACC leave per-attack reliability unresolved. Independently audit successful trajectories and report joint malicious-target and benign-task completion rates.
4. **How much does target selection drive success?** Accepted objectives are concentrated in exfiltration (Appendix D). Evaluate category-balanced targets and report category-specific ASR, DR, and refinement cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied methods, tables, and appendices support the reported findings, but broader generalization and comparison fairness remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes baseline budget matching and target alignment, filtering effects on ASR denominators, repeated-run uncertainty, exact prompts and generation settings, token/time/cost measurements, human-review procedures, held-out-defense tests, and confirmation that generated targets, rules, and skill packages are released.
