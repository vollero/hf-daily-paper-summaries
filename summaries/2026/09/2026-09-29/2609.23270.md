# Specification Before Generation: A Pre-Registered, Five-Model Paired Evaluation of a Specification Frame for LLM-Generated Code in Money, Time, Idempotency, and Access Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23270) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23270)


## One-sentence takeaway
A fixed 267-word specification preamble reduced measured defect findings across five models on 50 paired Python backend tasks, but functional correctness was not tested and unequal truncation complicates the results.

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses domain-specific defects in generated backend code: inaccurate money arithmetic, timezone-naive timestamps, unsafe retries, and access vulnerabilities. Such defects matter in finance, healthcare, and insurance systems where errors can have financial and regulatory consequences.

The author argues that process and style instructions leave a specification gap: prompts omit constraints engineers already know. The identified research gap is a controlled, pre-registered evaluation of a fixed, task-independent specification artifact across model vendors (§§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **Specification intervention:** The same filled preamble precedes every treatment prompt. It specifies Python context and constraints covering money, timestamps, SQL, credentials, logging, idempotency, password hashing, redirects, paths, and boundary validation (§III).
- **Paired evaluation:** Each of 50 author-written tasks receives bare and frame prompts across five vendor lineages, producing 500 outputs. Successful outputs cannot be regenerated; primary analyses are per model with Holm-adjusted sign tests (§IV).
- **Measurement and findings:** Nine task-registered checkers measure named defect classes, with Bandit providing a separate security-scanner measurement. The author also reports an exploratory association between weaker bare-arm performance and larger improvement (§§IV-F–I, V).
- **Re-scoring assets:** The paper reports publishing prompts, outputs, checkers, fixtures, analysis code, manifests, audit records, and pre-registration under DOI `10.5281/zenodo.22850887` (Data Availability). Their availability was not independently verified here.

The evidence supports fewer detected instances of the specified defect classes in this setting. Bandit corroborates direction, but the supplied results do not establish corrected statistical significance for H2. They also do not establish overall correctness, production safety, or superiority to other prompting strategies.

The author’s “never hurt” wording applies to aggregate model/domain findings, not every task: five pairs worsened. The exploratory baseline–effect gradient is an association, not an established mechanism (§V-F).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Checkers deliberately sacrifice recall, score only registered classes, and cap each class at one finding per output. Functional correctness was not measured (§VII).
- **Author-stated:** No length-matched neutral preamble or reason-first control was run, leaving additional-context or deliberation effects unresolved (§VII).
- **Author-stated:** Tasks come from one practitioner rather than a sampled corpus; results cover Python, four domains, single-turn generation, and the tested model versions (§VII).
- **Author-stated:** One generation per arm leaves within-model variance unmeasured. Two endpoints used provider-default decoding, and output caps changed during execution (§§VII–VIII).
- **Author-stated:** Truncated files could evade parsing-based detection, disproportionately benefiting the frame arm. Sensitivity analysis helps but does not recover missing code (§VIII).
- **Analyst inference:** Independent human validation remains limited because the same author designed the intervention, tasks, checkers, and adjudication; the author also wrote the book introducing the frame (Disclosure).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Table I lists **105** checker registrations, while §IV-C says **106**. The checkers are described as AST-based, but the truncation account mentions regex-based checkers. Neither discrepancy is resolved in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does defect reduction preserve task functionality?** No behavioral tests were scored. Next experiment: jointly measure functional-test success and defects, with sufficient output budgets and truncation detection.
- **Which part of the preamble causes improvement?** Specification content is confounded with added context. Next experiment: compare the frame with length-matched neutral, reason-first, and constraint-ablation arms.
- **Does the effect persist in agentic workflows?** The study used stateless generation. Next experiment: compare inline and repository-delivered frames under the same multi-turn tool-enabled protocol.
- **How stable are the gains across samples and task populations?** Only one output per arm and practitioner-authored tasks were used. Next experiment: repeat generations on independently sourced tasks and report variability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported paired improvements, but broader reliability claims remain untested.

Material missing information includes functional-test results, repeated-generation variance, content-control ablations, independent adjudication, detailed Bandit significance results, and resolution of the checker-registration and implementation discrepancies. The released archive was not inspected.
