# Selecting Diverse SFT Traces Improves Post-RL Generalization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33780) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33780)


## One-sentence takeaway
At matched SFT example budgets and RL checkpoints, selecting varied reasoning traces improves sampled problem coverage across tested reasoning tasks, although the proposed mixed-reward mechanism remains unproven.

## Problem and motivation
Verified solutions can demonstrate different reasoning routes even when their answers agree. Because RL samples from the policy produced by SFT, the retained demonstrations may affect which problems supply successful attempts and useful reward signals. The authors identify a gap in selection criteria centered on correctness, length, quality, or instruction coverage: these do not directly capture procedural variety within an existing candidate pool (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled selection study:** Compare diverse and similar subsets from a shared candidate pool at equal trajectory counts, matching student initialization, training recipes, evaluation, and checkpoint steps. Single-teacher experiments test whether gains require multiple generators (§§2–3).
- **CPU-only selector:** Convert visible traces into rule-based fingerprints of step types, transitions, tree structure, and trace statistics. Allocate domain quotas, cluster fingerprints, then select farthest points within clusters; the similar control selects centroid-nearest traces. Feature construction varies by domain (Appendix A.2, Table 2).
- **Practical evaluation and mechanism diagnostics:** Compare against alternative selectors on released corpora and measure mixed rewards before RL. The diagnostics support a possible explanation without establishing causality (§§3.4–4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage expands beyond SFT environments | OLMo3-7B diverse selection improves post-RL pass@8 by **16.9 percentage points** on environments excluded from SFT but included in shared RL; both select 50,000 rows. | §3.3; Fig. 1; Table 7 |
| Gains extend beyond training difficulty | Qwen3-1.7B with 50,000 SFT rows reaches **20.48% versus 10.78% pass@64** on RLVE difficulty 11–15; SFT uses 1–5 and RL 1–10. | Fig. 19; §3.3 |
| Solved-set gains mostly add successes | At eight attempts and a 32,768-token cap, diverse OLMo3-7B solves **1,133** RLVE questions missed by similar, versus **53** in the reverse direction. | Fig. 1c; Appendix A.3 |
| Multiple teachers are unnecessary | With every candidate generated by Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, diverse selection raises Qwen3-4B-Base’s unweighted mean post-RL pass@8 by **3.39–6.17 points** across ten mathematics benchmarks and three SFT budgets; checkpoints match within each budget. | §3.4; Fig. 7; Table 7 |
| Pre-RL reward availability differs | On 64 shared mathematics training prompts, eight samples at temperature 1.0 yield mixed rewards on **54.7% versus 46.9%** of prompts for diverse versus similar OLMo3-7B, despite slightly lower mean solve rate. | §3.4; Appendix C.1 |
| Released-corpus aggregate performance improves | At RL step 64, the selector beats every evaluated baseline in equally weighted mean sampled accuracy and pass@8, with **1.2–10.8% relative gains** across three corpora; benchmark sets contain six or seven tasks. | §4; Fig. 8; Appendices C.4–C.5 |
| Selection requires no GPU inference | Nemotron-Cascade 2 preprocessing, fingerprinting, and selection take approximately **three elapsed hours on one CPU node**; large-corpus gradient and embedding baselines require **64–92** and **175–232 H100 GPU-hours**, respectively. These are different cost units. | Appendix C.4 |

These results establish advantages at the tested post-RL endpoints, including difficulty extrapolation and a single-generator setting. They do not establish greater improvement *during* RL or a causal link from semantic route diversity through mixed rewards to final coverage. Aggregate baseline wins also coexist with individual benchmark losses (Appendices A.3, C.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Most conditions have one training run, so reported margins do not quantify training-seed uncertainty. Fingerprints are domain-specific; lexical variation can affect distances, and fingerprint spread does not count distinct semantic algorithms. The proposed route-repertoire-to-success-probability link is unmeasured. Mixed-reward evidence is narrow, and its causal role needs intervention (Appendices A.3–A.5, H).

**Observed scope limits:** Diverse selection loses on some instruction-following and puzzle outcomes, including INTELLECT-3 Enigmata. Teacher-count sweeps change teacher identities alongside count; program simulation compares generator corpora rather than shared-pool selections. Some sampling-budget comparisons also change generation caps (Appendices B, C.3, A.3).

**Analyst inference:** Equal trajectory counts do not establish equal token budgets or matched prompt coverage; the supplied controls therefore leave these possible contributors unresolved.

## Open research questions
- **Does mixed-reward availability cause the gains?** The evidence is diagnostic. Equalize informative-group exposure between conditions and compare post-RL coverage (Appendix H).
- **Do fingerprints capture distinct procedures?** Semantic route counts are absent. Validate fingerprints against annotated or executable routes and measure route diversity before and after RL (Appendix H).
- **How stable and capability-wide are benefits?** Most comparisons lack seed replication and some capabilities regress. Repeat matched selections across training seeds and report task-specific outcomes (Appendices C.3, H).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed paired protocols support the reported endpoint findings, but mechanism and robustness remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes broad training-seed replication, causal reward-signal tests, semantic-route validation, matched token-budget and prompt-coverage analyses, CPU-node specifications, and an explicit code or selected-data release location.
