# Allspark: Weak to Strong Transfer via Alternating Chain of Thought

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32913) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32913)


## One-sentence takeaway
Allspark trains a weak reasoning collaborator without strong-model training rollouts and improves frozen stronger models in several development settings, although gains vary and fewer retained tokens do not reliably mean lower inference cost.

## Problem and motivation
Large-model RL rollouts make experimentation expensive. The authors ask whether reasoning behavior learned by a smaller model can help stronger models without sampling from or updating those strong models during training. Their proposed gap is a reusable weak teacher that transfers through text to frozen students, including students with different tokenizers (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Collaborative teacher training:** A trainable weak teacher alternates reasoning chunks with a frozen copy of its initial model. The frozen partner produces the final answer; only teacher reasoning tokens receive RL loss (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Transfer through shared text:** At inference, a stronger frozen student replaces the training partner. Each model tokenizes the accumulated reasoning using its own interface, allowing cross-family transfer (§3.3; Appendix B.2).
- **Empirical transfer and resource analysis:** Controlled Qwen comparisons test teacher-training choices; Inkling experiments examine ARC-AGI-2 transfer, reasoning effort, cross-family reuse, retained tokens, and modeled inference costs (§4; Appendix B.4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen transfer is mixed | On 128 questions per domain, one sampled answer each, Qwen3-4B accuracy changes from **96.9% to 96.1%** in math and **81.2% to 82.8%** in reasoning with the Allspark 1.7B teacher. | Table 2; §4.1 |
| Teacher training matters in Qwen controls | With the same 4B student, untrained-teacher accuracy is **93.0% / 79.7%**, and ordinary-RL-teacher accuracy is **89.8% / 79.6%**, for math/reasoning respectively. | Table 2 |
| Within-family ARC accuracy improves | Across 96 development tasks with three attempts each, Allspark’s highest observed mean pass@1 is **81.6%**, versus **78.1%** for Inkling alone across the effort sweep. | §4.2; Figure 4 |
| A selected ARC operating point improves retained-token efficiency | Allspark reaches **79.2% at 10.8k** mean retained output tokens, versus Inkling alone’s **78.1% at 12.3k**; tokens include both writers and the answer. | §4.2; Figure 4 |
| Cross-family gains depend on settings | On the same 96 ARC tasks, three attempts each: Nemotron medium gains **17.7 percentage points** with **72.5% more** retained tokens; Nemotron full gains **5.2 points** with **4.4% more**; Kimi gains **11.5 points** with **7.7% fewer**. | §4.3; Figure 5 |
| Token savings do not imply cost savings | Under archived discounted rates and prefix-cache assumptions, Kimi’s modeled cost rises from **$0.1466 to $0.1774 per attempt**, a **21.1% increase**, despite fewer retained tokens. | Appendix B.4; Tables 7–8 |

These results support teacher reuse across the evaluated students, without their training rollouts or weight updates. They do not establish universal gains, public-test performance, or causal effects of individual teacher spans. Cross-family comparisons assess the complete inference procedure: Allspark explicitly requests a separate answer, whereas solo baselines must transition naturally (§4.3; Appendix B.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Serving two models and performing handoffs can increase cost and latency. Evaluation covers limited models, tasks, and training runs; interfaces without resumable reasoning remain untested (§6).
- **Analyst inference—selection bias:** Qwen development questions are used for checkpoint selection and subsequent evaluation, limiting interpretation as independent generalization evidence (Table 1; Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference—procedure confounding:** Cross-family gains cannot be attributed solely to learned teacher content because answer-transition procedures differ (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—resource measurement:** Retained-token totals exclude discarded chunk suffixes and combine native tokenizer units; they do not measure total billed generation or comparable compute (Appendix B.2).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The main Qwen setup describes two training domains, but Appendix A specifies six groups, “two per domain,” and diagnostics include coding. Table 4’s selected checkpoint reports 119 correct math answers out of 128, inconsistent with Table 2’s 89.8% for the same stated checkpoint and weak-student setting.
- Selected traces include teacher overstatement and a correct-to-incorrect coding outcome, demonstrating possible harmful steering (§4.1; Figure 14).

## Open research questions
- **Does transfer survive independent evaluation?** Qwen selection and reporting share development data. Next experiment: freeze checkpoints and evaluate untouched splits across multiple training seeds.
- **How much gain comes from teacher content versus answer handling?** Cross-family procedures differ. Next experiment: give solo, untrained-teacher, and trained-teacher conditions identical reasoning termination and answer requests.
- **When does collaboration reduce actual serving expenditure?** Retained tokens and modeled costs diverge. Next experiment: measure billed tokens, wall-clock latency, and accuracy under matched dollar budgets.
- **How robust is teacher reuse to interface and model changes?** Non-thinking interfaces are untested and family coverage is limited. Next experiment: transfer one fixed teacher across additional reasoning interfaces and vary handoff schedules.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed protocols and development results, but reporting inconsistencies and inference-procedure differences weaken attribution.

Material missing information includes independent test results, multi-seed robustness, numerical bootstrap interval bounds, measured latency and training-cost comparisons, the Inkling checkpoint-selection rationale, clarification of Qwen discrepancies, and confirmed public access to code, trained adapters, and run archives.
