# MassAlloc Attention: Let Attention Allocate Its Own Compute

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32712) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32712)


## One-sentence takeaway
MassAlloc Attention skips low-contribution computation after scoring every causal attention tile, reporting comparable language-model quality with 23.1% fewer total training FLOPs at 14B and 32K context, while retaining quadratic score computation (§3).

## Problem and motivation
Dense attention executes softmax and value-related computation even for interactions receiving negligible probability mass. The authors identify an opportunity to use attention’s own scores and normalization state to skip this work inside fused kernels, while keeping every causal interaction accessible to score discovery (§§1–2).

The gap is a paired training-and-inference allocation rule that needs neither a separate router nor stored selection masks, and preserves useful long-range interactions.

## Approach and contributions
- **Normalized-contribution allocation:** MALA computes every legal QK tile, then skips its post-score path only if all relevant contributions fall below a length-normalized threshold, `τ/Lq`. The tolerance controls contribution size rather than imposing a fixed compute budget (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Paired fused operators:** Forward tests against its evolving retained-mass normalizer; backward uses the saved final normalizer. With matching tile partitions and causal masks, backward support is nested within forward support without storing a selection mask. Backward is approximate, not necessarily the exact derivative of forward (§§2.2–2.4).
- **Guarantee and evaluation:** The authors prove a forward omitted-mass bound of `τ/(1+τ)` in exact arithmetic, and test allocation quality, operator fidelity, recall, efficiency, and model quality. Output guarantees additionally require bounded value norms; gradient fidelity is empirical (Appendix B; §3).

## Evidence and main results
All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Online allocation approaches the diagnostic oracle | At 8K with exactly matched total post-score work, averaging approximately 1,024 slots/query: mean omitted mass **0.0188%** for MALA versus **0.0182%** for the per-instance reference-mass oracle; mean output L2 error **0.0174% versus 0.0164%**. | Table 1; Appendix C.2 |
| Low measured operator errors | On 256 sequences with prefixes from 1K–32K, using the trained 14B MALA checkpoint: maximum across lengths of mean output error **0.021%**; mean gradient errors at most **0.38%/0.35%/0.17%** for dQ/dK/dV against screening-disabled attention. Backward uses a deterministic Rademacher probe. | Figure 2; §3; Appendix C.3 |
| Strong controlled recall | With 256 bindings, 8,192-token sequences and model dimension 512: accuracy **89.67% MALA**, **89.97% FullAttn**, **52.61% DSA**, **47.25% MoBA**, **22.61% NSA**. Sparse baselines have a 1,024-slot ceiling; MALA has adaptive work near that scale. | Figure 3; Appendix C.4 |
| Lower attention-operator latency | At 128K, batch size one, TP=8 on eight H100s: **2.2× forward**, **3.0× backward**, and **1.6× decoding** speedups over FullAttn, with reported peak operator-memory parity. Measurements include score discovery and selection. | Figure 4; Appendices C.5, Table 4 |
| Lower total training FLOPs with similar perplexity | At 14B, perplexity differs from FullAttn by **less than 0.001** after both stages; total FLOPs fall **2.5% at 4K pre-training** and **23.1% at 32K long-context training**. | Figure 5; §3; Appendix C.6 |
| Comparable model-level results | At 14B, MALA/FullAttn knowledge averages are **72.48/72.32**, reasoning **64.66/64.46**, and RULER **89.45/89.42 at native 32K**, **65.75/65.84 after YaRN to 128K**. Separately continued-trained 32B models score **92.71/92.70** and **82.56/82.03** on those RULER settings. | Table 2; Tables 6–8; Appendix C.7 |

The evidence supports useful constant-factor savings and comparable quality in the evaluated settings. It does not establish subquadratic attention, smaller persistent KV caches, end-to-end model speedups matching the operator speedups, or universal quality equivalence. The matched-work oracle is a diagnostic control with finalized reference probabilities, not a deployable baseline.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** QK computation remains quadratic; diffuse attention can retain more work; split-KV decoding can also retain additional work. KV-cache capacity is unchanged (§§2.1–2.4).
- **Author-stated approximation:** Backward can drop forward-retained contributions, and gradient error depends on values and upstream gradients. The formal guarantees assume exact arithmetic ( §2.2; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—weak worst-case assurance:** At the evaluated `τ=1`, the proved omitted-mass ceiling is **50%**, obtained by substituting into Proposition 1. Thus the much smaller measured errors are empirical findings, not guaranteed by that bound.
- **Analyst inference—limited robustness evidence:** Fidelity tests use one MALA-trained checkpoint and one deterministic gradient-probe construction. No tolerance, tile-size, or traversal-order sweep is reported; scaling-law training uses one fixed seed (Appendices C.2–C.3, C.6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison boundaries:** Recall has no injected distractors and matches raw interaction slots, whereas operator/scaling studies approximately match post-score FLOPs across differing architectures. These comparisons do not isolate allocation policy alone (Appendices C.1, C.4–C.6).
- **Analyst inference—systems scope:** Operator timing excludes projections, MLPs, optimizer updates, and pipeline communication. Evaluation-run variability does not measure training-run variability (Appendices C.5, C.8).

## Open research questions
1. **How robust is the shared tolerance?** The paper primarily evaluates one tolerance. Sweep tolerance and tile sizes on both MALA-trained and dense-trained checkpoints, measuring latency, omitted mass, and task quality.
2. **When does approximate backward harm learning?** Probe-based errors leave task-gradient behavior uncertain. Compare the current backward rule with exact differentiation over retained forward support across multiple training seeds.
3. **Do savings survive harder retrieval distributions?** Controlled recall excludes distractors. Add distractors, vary binding locations and attention diffuseness, and measure recall alongside realized work.
4. **What are end-to-end deployment gains?** Operator timing leaves whole-model performance open. Benchmark training throughput and serving latency across batch sizes, hardware, and split-KV configurations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method summary and reported results; **medium** in generalization beyond the evaluated settings.
