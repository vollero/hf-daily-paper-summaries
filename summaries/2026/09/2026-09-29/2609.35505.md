# An RL View of OPD: Least Square Policy Distillation for Sample-Efficient LLM Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35505) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35505)


## One-sentence takeaway
LSPD combines robust student–teacher log-probability matching, entropy regularization, and trajectory reuse to improve mathematical reasoning distillation and reduce rollout requirements, while its theoretical guarantee applies to an idealized optimistic algorithm (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation supervises a student on its own generated trajectories, but common policy-gradient implementations repeatedly require fresh rollouts and provide limited historical-data reuse or explicit exploration. The authors identify an opportunity to transfer optimistic estimation and off-policy learning principles from KL-regularized RL into distillation (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **RL formulation:** Reverse-KL OPD is exactly rewritten as KL-regularized optimization with a teacher-to-reference log-ratio reward. The authors acknowledge prior work recognizing this connection; their contribution develops its algorithmic implications (§4.1).
- **Practical distillation objective:** LSPD matches sampled-token student and teacher log-probabilities using a Huber-type penalty, with an explicit student entropy bonus replacing the idealized pointwise optimism term (§§4.2–4.3; Appendix A.1).
- **Trajectory reuse:** LSPD performs multiple updates on each fresh rollout batch; LSPD-RB also samples historical trajectories from a replay buffer (Algorithm 1).
- **Theory and evaluation:** An idealized confidence-set algorithm receives a reverse-KL regret guarantee, while experiments assess reasoning accuracy, multi-sample coverage, entropy, and rollout efficiency (§§5–6; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher aggregate reasoning scores | Across six benchmarks and three Qwen3 teacher–student pairs, LSPD averages **31.60 Avg@16 / 53.30 Pass@16**, exceeding EOPD by **0.91 / 0.85 points** and OPD by **1.99 / 2.27 points**. | §5.2; Table 1 |
| Replay improves coverage with fewer rollout iterations | LSPD-RB averages **31.51 Avg@16 / 54.86 Pass@16**, exceeding EOPD by **0.82 / 2.42 points**; results use **10 training steps**, versus at least **40** for other methods. | §5.2; Table 1 caption |
| Stronger coverage at larger sampling budgets | For Qwen3-8B → Qwen3-4B-Base on AMC23, AIME24, and AIME25, mean LSPD Pass@32/64 is **58.51/61.94**, versus EOPD’s **57.00/60.43**. | §5.2; Table 2 |
| Replay accelerates saturation in rollout units | For Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B-Base, LSPD-RB saturates in approximately **10 rollout batches** on three competition benchmarks, using **256 optimizer updates per batch**; each batch contains **64 prompts × 4 responses**. | §5.3; Figure 2 |
| Entropy mainly benefits multi-sample coverage | In the Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B-Base setting, removing entropy reduces six-benchmark mean Avg@16/Pass@16 by **0.17/2.26 points** for LSPD and **0.75/1.64 points** for LSPD-RB. | §5.3; Table 3 |
| Limited out-of-domain transfer evidence | Math-trained Qwen3-1.7B-Base achieves mean scores of **56.02** with LSPD and **56.84** with LSPD-RB, versus **55.01** with EOPD, across GPQA 0-shot, MMLU 5-shot, WinoGrande 0-shot, and HumanEval greedy completion. | Appendix B; Table 5 |
| Conditional theoretical guarantee | Algorithm 2 has high-probability regret **O(d_E log(N_R K/δ))** for fixed reward bound, noise scale, and regularization, under realizability and conditionally unbiased sub-Gaussian feedback. | §6; Appendix C.4, Eq. C.53 |

These results support improved accuracy and solution coverage in the tested configurations. Pass@k and policy entropy provide evidence consistent with preserved diversity, but do not directly measure distinct reasoning strategies. Reduced rollout requirements do not establish reduced total compute: replay uses many more optimizer updates per rollout batch. The theorem establishes a guarantee for Algorithm 2, not directly for the practical Huber-and-entropy implementation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated theoretical scope:** Fast rates require bounded student–target log-ratios and reward-class realizability. The teacher-noise assumption is an analytical idealization, not robustness to arbitrary corruption (§6; Appendix C.6).
- **Analyst inference—implementation gap:** Practical entropy regularization substitutes for explicit optimistic confidence-set optimization; no guarantee is supplied for that substitution or Huber robustification (§4.3; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Training uses one mathematical dataset and one model family with non-thinking teachers. Out-of-domain testing covers only one teacher–student pair (§5.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and cost:** The supplied text reports no repeated-seed uncertainty, significance tests, or wall-clock and total-compute comparisons. Fixed hardware and shared implementation settings improve comparability but do not resolve these gaps (Appendix A.2).
- **Internal reporting inconsistency:** Appendix B and Figure 3 describe Huber as consistently improving performance, but AIME25 Avg@16 is lower with Huber (**8.33 versus 8.54**) in the reported Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B-Base replay ablation. The reported average improvement can coexist with this exception.

## Open research questions
- **Does replay reduce total training cost?** The paper measures rollout efficiency while increasing optimizer updates. A next experiment could compare wall-clock time, teacher scoring cost, and GPU-hours at matched accuracy.
- **How much improvement comes from entropy versus explicit optimism?** Entropy is ablated, but practical confidence-set optimism is not tested. A tractable next experiment could compare explicit optimistic updates with entropy surrogates under matched rollout budgets.
- **How broadly does the method transfer?** Evidence is concentrated on Qwen3 and math-only training. A next experiment could test other model families, thinking teachers, and non-mathematical training domains.
- **How sensitive is replay to stale trajectories?** The paper specifies uniform replay and FIFO eviction without replay-age or capacity ablations. A next experiment could vary buffer capacity and sampling by trajectory age.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the method and reported results; **medium** in broader efficiency and generalization conclusions.

Material missing information includes repeated-seed variability, total compute and wall-clock measurements, checkpoint-selection rules, detailed answer-verification procedures, replay-design sensitivity, and empirical validation of the theoretical coverage and noise assumptions. The advertised code release was not inspected.
