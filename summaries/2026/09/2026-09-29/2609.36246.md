# Learning from Teacher Continuations at Student States

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36246) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36246)


## One-sentence takeaway
OLIVE trains language models on teacher continuations of freshly generated student prefixes, improving average reasoning accuracy and most tested agent success rates while requiring only teacher text (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Offline supervised fine-tuning uses fixed teacher trajectories that may poorly match states reached by an evolving student. Token-level on-policy distillation (OPD) supervises student rollouts, but subsequent targets remain conditioned on the student’s original decisions rather than demonstrating what follows a correction. Distribution-matching OPD also requires teacher token probabilities (§§1–2).

The authors target this gap: providing coherent corrective trajectories from current student states, including difficult reasoning prefixes and failed agent interactions.

## Approach and contributions
- **Online teacher intervention:** At each iteration, the student generates a prefix, the teacher continues autoregressively, and the student learns through cross-entropy on teacher tokens only. Prefixes are regenerated as training progresses; the default method uses unfiltered, partial continuations (§3.1, Algorithm 1).
- **Multi-turn extension:** The student first interacts with an environment; the teacher then takes over from the resulting history. Student turns and environment observations are masked from the loss (§3.2).
- **Asynchronous implementation:** Student prefix generation overlaps teacher continuation generation, with bounded policy staleness (§3.3).
- **Empirical contribution:** Comparisons examine task learning, forgetting, continued training, and sequential adaptation. The authors explicitly relate the method to earlier learner roll-in/expert rollout and intervention approaches; novelty lies in this particular online, text-only distillation procedure and its evaluation (§§2, 4–5).

These results support OLIVE’s effectiveness in the evaluated settings, particularly average reasoning performance and ScienceWorld recovery. They do not establish universal superiority: Tables 1–2 contain exceptions to the broader prose claims. The forgetting study also changes the main recipe by using complete, verified continuations.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope boundary:** Teacher continuations demonstrate correction when the student prefix remains recoverable (§3.1); performance on irreversible failures is not quantified.
- **Authors’ limitation:** Terminal-agent evidence is preliminary because the evaluation subset is small (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** OPD and OLIVE use different learning rates, and OPD uses a top-16 probability approximation. Comparisons therefore do not isolate supervision placement alone (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** No uncertainty intervals or repeated-training-seed results are reported. RLVE evaluates ten test problems per environment, limiting precision (§4.1).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** OEC is described as offline but labeled online in Figure 6; figure captions and labels disagree on some reasoning metrics. Figure 4’s GPU-hour accounting versus elapsed training time is insufficiently explained.
- **Analyst inference:** One sequential task order supports the plasticity claim, but does not isolate plasticity from data refresh or establish robustness across orders (§5.2).

## Open research questions
- **When is takeover too late?** Recoverability is acknowledged, while handoff ablation covers only ALFWorld. Next experiment: vary handoff timing across environments and stratify prefixes by recoverability (§3.1; Appendix C.1).
- **How much benefit comes from refreshed states alone?** Optimization settings differ, and the forgetting experiment changes continuation length and filtering. Next experiment: factorially vary refresh, filtering, and continuation length under matched optimization and teacher-token budgets.
- **How robust is asynchronous training to staleness?** The main reasoning configuration uses one depth. Next experiment: sweep depth and report task accuracy, elapsed time, and GPU-hours (§3.3).
- **Does sequential adaptation generalize across task orders?** Evidence uses a fixed sequence and one held-out environment. Next experiment: randomize orders, track each task after every stage, and evaluate multiple held-out tasks (§5.2; Appendix C.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The algorithm and main tabulated results are clear, but reporting inconsistencies weaken some broader claims.

Material information unavailable includes uncertainty estimates, seed variability, exact Figure 7 scores and gain units, per-benchmark forgetting results, detailed cost accounting, several baseline/agent optimization settings, and confirmed code, checkpoint, or dataset releases.
