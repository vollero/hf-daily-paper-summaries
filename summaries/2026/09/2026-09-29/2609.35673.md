# FlowTool: Controlling Tool Parameter in Image Retouching via Flow Matching

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35673) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35673)


## One-sentence takeaway
FlowTool generates continuous retouching parameters through conditional flow matching, improving reference-based editing scores across four benchmarks while reporting substantially faster plan generation than autoregressive editing agents (Table 1; Fig. 4).

## Problem and motivation
Image retouching requires translating an image and a user’s instruction into tool selections and precise numerical settings. The authors argue that autoregressive MLLMs poorly match continuous parameter spaces, risk propagating earlier prediction errors, and incur unnecessary reasoning and decoding costs. Their proposed gap is direct, joint generation of coherent editing parameters without language-based reasoning at inference (Secs. 1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Continuous editing-plan generation:** FlowTool models a conditional distribution over normalized tool parameters. A VLM encodes the source image and instruction; a two-stream Diffusion Transformer predicts a rectified-flow velocity field that transforms Gaussian noise into parameters. A separate head selects active tools, and a renderer executes the plan in a fixed order (Secs. 4.1–4.2).
- **Training pipeline:** Expert editing records supply target parameters and tool masks; a VLM synthesizes instructions. Supervised training first freezes the backbone, then adapts it using LoRA. DiffusionNFT post-training combines negative pixelwise L1 reward with a VLM instruction-alignment reward (Secs. 4.3–4.4).
- **Evaluation and internal asset:** The authors evaluate four benchmarks, including their new held-out FlowTool-Eval set, and study integration steps, tool masking, training curriculum, and backbone scale. Public release of data, checkpoints, and code is not established; access requires internal approval (Secs. 5, reproducibility statement).

The methodological contribution is applying conditional flow generation to structured retouching parameters; flow matching, DiT, and DiffusionNFT are adopted components.

These results support improved reference matching and efficient plan generation in the tested setup. They do not establish uniformly better semantic alignment, statistically significant differences, or full editing-system latency including rendering. Table 1 selects the best SFT/RL variant separately for its improvement summaries; those summaries do not describe one uniformly winning checkpoint.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** FlowTool currently targets standardized linear parameter spaces. Cyclic/polar and discrete/categorical parameters remain future work (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation validity:** Synthetic instructions derived from expert edits may favor reference matching. GPT-5 judgments provide automated rather than human validation of instruction satisfaction (Secs. 4.3, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Proprietary models receive no task-specific training, whereas FlowTool trains on internal expert records. API response time and local plan-generation time are different measurement conditions (Secs. 5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference—mechanism uncertainty:** Ablations run only on MMArt-Bench. Separate reward-component ablations, matched-data autoregressive comparisons, and direct measurements of the proposed numerical-error mechanism are absent (Sec. 5.3).
- **Reproducibility:** Asset access requires internal approval. The supplied implementation details omit several training and architecture settings, and results lack uncertainty estimates (p. 11; Appendix A).

## Open research questions
1. **Can FlowTool handle heterogeneous tool parameters?** Appendix B leaves this open. Test cyclic hue representations and mixed continuous/categorical outputs against the current linear formulation.
2. **Does the gain persist with matched training data and compute?** Existing comparisons do not isolate generation formulation from training differences. Train an autoregressive parameter predictor on the same records and compare quality and latency.
3. **How well does it satisfy authentic user requests?** Training instructions are synthesized and evaluation uses an automated judge. Run blinded human comparisons on independently collected instructions.
4. **Which reward drives post-training improvements?** Table 1 shows alignment gains alongside some reference-metric regressions. Compare reference-only, VLM-only, and combined rewards across all benchmarks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reported results, but independent reproducibility and generalization remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes the exact tool catalog and parameter count, renderer implementation, detailed baseline prompts, benchmark sizes beyond FlowTool-Eval, statistical uncertainty, judge prompts and reliability checks, reward weights, optimizer and LoRA settings, DiT dimensions, and confirmed public asset availability.
