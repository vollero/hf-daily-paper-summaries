# AdaGuard: An Adaptive Guard Model with User-defined Policies

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34241) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34241)


## One-sentence takeaway
AdaGuard achieves strong policy-conditioned violation detection and rule attribution on AdaptiveSafety, but weaker external-benchmark results and uncontrolled training-stage comparisons leave SafePO’s contribution uncertain.

## Problem and motivation
Fixed safety taxonomies cannot capture deployment-specific permissions: the same tool action may comply with one policy and violate another. The authors identify a gap in assessing recorded agent behavior—including tool calls and outcomes—under user-defined rules, beyond conversational content safety. The task requires identifying every supported violation, rather than merely detecting that some violation occurred (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **AdaptiveSafety:** 10,939 training and 1,000 test examples with policies containing 1–100 rules. Policy counterfactuals change rules while holding behavior fixed; behavioral counterfactuals change actions or outcomes under a fixed policy; structural augmentation changes rule order, identifiers, and policy length. Examples include an explanation and complete violation labels; related variants stay within one split (§3.1).
- **SafePO:** Structured rewards distinguish exact verdicts, ordering mistakes, partial matches, and invalid outputs. Group-relative response advantages drive clipped updates; an independent prefix-value model redistributes token weights within explanation and verdict regions while preserving their respective one-third and two-thirds objective-weight budgets (§§3.2–3.3).
- **AdaGuard models:** Qwen3Guard-Gen initializations at 0.6B, 4B, and 8B undergo label-weighted supervised training followed by SafePO. At inference, only the actor is needed; it receives the policy and interaction and generates an explanation plus policy-local violation identifiers (§3.1; Figure 2).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Strong in-dataset detection | On the 1,000-example AdaptiveSafety test set, AdaGuard-4B achieves 89.30% accuracy and 88.53% binary F1; 8B achieves 89.50% and 88.67%. The strongest non-AdaGuard local results are DynaGuard-4B’s 67.30% accuracy and DynaGuard-8B’s 67.03% F1. | §4; Table 1 |

These results support strong verdict prediction under the reported protocols, especially on AdaptiveSafety. They do not establish uniform superiority, zero-shot transfer, verified explanation quality, or a causal benefit from SafePO. Invalid outputs count as binary errors and receive no exact-match credit (§4; Appendices A.2–A.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** All evaluations are single runs, without significance analysis or training-seed variability estimates. Reward and evaluation do not independently verify explanation correctness (Appendix A.6).
- **Author-stated:** No component ablations isolate structured rewards, value modulation, or region budgets. Historical SFT comparisons use different preprocessing, preventing controlled attribution (Appendix A.4).
- **Author-stated:** Model interfaces and inference budgets differ. Laya truncation affects every AdaptiveSafety example, so its scores do not estimate full-context capability; hosted models’ internal context handling is unknown (Appendix A.2).
- **Author-stated:** Policy-length groups differ in content and labels, so their scores do not isolate sensitivity to policy length (§4; Figure 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Offline verdict accuracy does not establish reliable intervention during live agent execution; the authors themselves call for broader deployment studies (§6).

## Open research questions
- **Which SafePO components help?** Component effects remain unisolated. Rerun matched-protocol SFT and RL experiments across seeds, removing value modulation, region balancing, and individual reward terms separately.
- **Are explanations evidence-grounded?** Neither rewards nor reported metrics verify them. Have independent reviewers assess explanations for unsupported claims and correspondence with recorded evidence.
- **How robust is policy adaptation?** Counterfactual augmentation is described, but paired consistency results are absent. Evaluate held-out policy/behavior counterfactual pairs and identifier-remapping pairs.
- **Does the guard improve live safety?** Deployment effectiveness remains untested. Compare guarded and unguarded executions, measuring prevented violations, unnecessary blocks, and monitoring cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and descriptive results; **low** in causal attribution to SafePO and deployment generalization.

Material missing information includes detailed trajectory-source composition, annotation procedures and quality checks, training compute and full optimization schedules, independent explanation assessments, controlled ablations, seed variability, and live intervention results. The repository is linked, but the supplied text does not establish which artifacts are downloadable.
