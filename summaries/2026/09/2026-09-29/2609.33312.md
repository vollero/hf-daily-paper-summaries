# When Privacy Moves ML-Mediated Decisions On Device: Information and Incentive Misalignment in Auctions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33312) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33312)


## One-sentence takeaway
In a synthetic on-device auction audit, stale shared budgets cause excess debit that pacing cannot prevent, while charging the runner-up’s paced score creates a separate incentive defect that critical-base-bid payment repairs only per auction.

## Problem and motivation
Privacy-preserving client inference can move eligibility and auction decisions onto devices while campaign budgets remain shared. Between broadcasts, each device sees its own spending but misses other devices’ debits, allowing locally admissible purchases to exceed a global budget.

The author identifies two distinct gaps: conventional pacing cannot correct invisible accounting updates, and using transformed ranking scores as payment units can undermine truthful bidding. The claimed novelty is the formalisation and measurement of replicated-budget failure under the audited auction logic—not discovering that delayed spending information can cause overdelivery (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Accounting theory:** A finite-window expected excess-debit bound relates lag, arrival intensity, budget-crossing campaigns, and conditional charge caps. It explicitly accommodates inconsistent device histories (§4.1).
- **Controlled simulation audit:** A Rust auction port, tested against live TypeScript, compares ASAP, proportional Even, and PI pacing using frozen budgets and paired demand paths. Pressure, visible-balance guards, demand heterogeneity, and label perturbations isolate sources of failure (§§5–6).
- **Incentive analysis:** A threshold argument and executable counterexample expose profitable deviations under score-space payment. Critical-base-bid payment is a standard repair; the contribution is diagnosing and measuring the implementation defect while separating payment from accounting and ranking (§§2, 4.2, 6.6).
- **Reported reproducibility assets:** The author provides context vectors, calibration and evaluation rows, frozen budgets, source snapshots, manifests, and parity tests (Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results
Unless otherwise stated, primary results use **36 campaigns, 50 devices, 3,000 arrivals/tick, 100 ticks, 20-times budget pressure, fresh sessions, and 30 paired evaluation paths**. Overspend is summed positive campaign excess debit divided by total budget; it does not offset excess against underspend. Reported ± values below are two-sided 95% Student-t CI half-widths.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lag substantially increases excess debit | Deterministic Even overspend: **0.5276 ± 0.0347%** at zero lag, **17.7731 ± 0.8702%** at one tick, and **1,669.3075 ± 3.8743%** at 50 ticks. | Table 1, §6.1 |
| Long-lag failure persists with milder budgets | Deterministic Even at 50 ticks: **106.95%, 367.37%, 801.41%, 1,669.31%** mean overspend at pressure **2, 5, 10, 20**, respectively; budgets change only through the calibration divisor. | Figure 1, §6.2 |
| A local guard fixes crossing overshoot, not hidden spending | Deterministic guarded Even has **zero excess debit in all 30 zero-lag paths**, but **11.8822 ± 1.1969%** overspend at one tick. | §6.4 |
| Demand stress test preserves the lag direction | With the declared Markov-bursty process and heterogeneous device weights, mean overspend increases across every tested lag; all paired lag-minus-zero CIs are positive. | §§5, 6.3 |
| The expected bound exceeds estimated overspend | All deterministic bounded-value cells have positive paired bound-minus-empirical CI intervals; the untruncated log-normal arm is excluded from the theorem. | Table 2, §6.5 |
| Score-space payment permits profitable deviations | At deterministic one-tick lag, **98.23% of rival auctions** and **96.97% of all sales** admit a profitable deviation. Critical payment with a **100-unit reserve** produces no detected per-auction deviation across **420 replicate cells**, conditional on realised multipliers. | §6.6 |
| PI helps only at short deterministic lags | Paired PI-minus-Even overspend: approximately **−10.33 percentage points**, CI **[−11.46, −9.21]**, at one tick; **−3.23**, CI **[−4.12, −2.34]**, at two ticks. Calibrated-synthetic contrasts are inconclusive. | §6.7 |
| Pacing also changes payment magnitude | Deterministic zero-lag Even delivers **19,358.1 impressions** at mean charge **7.07 score units**, versus ASAP’s **1,679.0** at **81.35**. | §6.9 |

These results support the author’s separation of accounting visibility, payment incentives, and ranking. They establish failures in the specified simulator, not real advertiser charges or deployment-scale effects. Positive empirical bound slack is consistency with an expectation bound, not a guarantee for each realised path. Base-value ranking disagreement is explicitly not a welfare measure (§§3, 6.5–6.6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** All context labels come from one teacher; there are no human-gold labels or completed human ratings. The perturbation test cannot establish semantic or safety accuracy (§§5, 7; Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Campaigns, values, and demand are synthetic. The robustness sweep covers one burst process and device mixture, excluding trace-linked contexts, network failures, diurnal cycles, and strategic timing (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Every opportunity starts a fresh session, so the within-session frequency cap never binds (§§5, 7).
- **Author-stated:** Delivery assumes truthful reports despite the score-payment incentive defect. Conditional per-auction truthfulness does not establish truthfulness in the repeated bidding–pacing process (§7).
- **Author-stated:** The theorem requires bounded conditional charges and excludes the untruncated proxy arm. Integer accounting granularity affects crossing residuals and guard results (§§4.1, 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Auction parity strengthens confidence in reproducing the specified auction rule, but cannot validate the synthetic billing convention or deployment demand. The supplied text reports artifacts; their execution was not independently checked here.

## Open research questions
1. **How should communication and hard reservations be traded off?** The paper proposes escrow but does not evaluate it. Compare faster broadcasts against device reservations and budget transfers, measuring excess debit and stranded inventory (§9).
2. **What changes under repeated sessions and realistic device traces?** Fresh sessions disable frequency-cap interactions. Replay sessionised, device-linked logs with persistent cap state (§§7, 9).
3. **Does critical payment remain attractive under strategic repeated bidding?** Current truthfulness is conditional on fixed multipliers. Simulate adaptive reporting policies that influence later pacing, comparing delivery and budget compliance (§§4.2, 9).
4. **Can local self-knowledge tighten the bound?** The current theorem deliberately omits explicit device count. Derive and test an assignment-aware bound across device-load distributions (§4.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the paper’s stated theoretical scope and reported simulation findings; deployment applicability remains limited.

Material information unavailable includes audited label accuracy, sessionised or deployment demand traces, real currency-ledger validation, repeated-game equilibrium evidence, measured reservation trade-offs, and complete project compute timing. Artifact contents and reproducibility are reported by the author rather than independently verified.
