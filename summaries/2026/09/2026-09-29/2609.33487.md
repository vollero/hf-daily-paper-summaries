# What masking geometry works best for EEG foundation models?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33487) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33487)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under a controlled EEG pre-training pipeline, short blocks with moderate spatial radius work well for both MAE and JEPA, while masking every channel at a time point triggers a JEPA positional shortcut that standard collapse checks miss (§4, Fig. 3).

## Problem and motivation
EEG foundation models typically change masking, architecture, data, and objectives together, making masking’s contribution difficult to isolate. The authors seek a principled masking default and an explanation of how spatial and temporal context affect learned representations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified masking family:** spatial radius \(r\), temporal block length \(L\), and target masked fraction \(\rho\) recover random patches, temporal blocks, spatial blocks, and mixed blocks (§3.1).
- **Controlled evaluation:** 29 geometries under each of MAE and JEPA produce 58 core checkpoints, sharing REVE-Small, the open REVE corpus, and training settings. Evaluation uses frozen-feature ridge probes on OpenEEGBench (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Practical findings and assets:** the authors recommend \(r=9\) cm, \(L=2\) patches and report releasing checkpoints, code, and cached results (§1; reproducibility statement).
- **Collapse characterization:** diagnostics identify JEPA features increasingly dominated by channel-position means rather than input-dependent variation (Appendix E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Shared recommended geometry | Both frameworks’ bootstrap winners use \(L=2\) patches, \(r=9\) cm. Final evaluation covers 12 datasets and 5 random-projection seeds. | §4, Fig. 3 |
| Broad competitive region | 11 MAE and 9 JEPA configurations are not statistically distinguishable from their respective winner under the authors’ hierarchical-bootstrap criterion. | §4, Finding 2; §3.2 |
| JEPA is more sensitive to poor masks | Best-minus-worst mean normalized downstream score is 0.32 for MAE and 0.77 for JEPA across the core geometry grid. | §4, Finding 2 |
| Competitive aggregate performance | Recommended MAE/JEPA scores are 0.800/approximately 0.83 on the core normalized OpenEEGBench scale; authors report aggregate REVE-Base-level performance. This does not mean matching REVE on every dataset. | Tables 13–14; Figs. 2, 11 |
| Low per-run compute | Mean cost is 12.70 H100-hours for MAE and 14.91 for JEPA, versus a cited estimate of 260 A100-hours for REVE-Base; model size, corpus, schedule, and hardware differ. | Appendix A.3, Table 3 |
| Bias-inflation collapse | At JEPA \(r=\text{all}\), feature-mean magnitude grows 2–3× while input-dependent residual magnitude shrinks 5–10×; measured using representative checkpoints on PhysioNet-MI and BCIC-IV-2a probe data. Standard callback triggers never fire. | Appendices A.7, E.1–E.2 |
| Collapse retains some information | On BCIC-IV-2a, collapsed JEPA’s channel-averaged band-power probe R² is −0.08 for delta but 0.69 for gamma, versus 0.47/0.67 for recommended JEPA. | Appendix E.3, Table 11 |
| Limited backbone transfer | Under MAE on the same corpus at target \(\rho=0.5\), recommended versus random masking increases normalized score from 0.597 to 0.640 for modified CBraMod and 0.605 to 0.642 for LaBraM. | Appendix H.1, Table 13 |
| Ratio interacts with geometry | Random-mask MAE reaches 0.83 at target \(\rho=0.70\), versus 0.81 for recommended geometry at that ratio; the fixed-ratio ranking is therefore not universal. | Appendix H.2, Table 14 |

The evidence supports a useful default within the tested pipelines and an observed JEPA collapse regime. The proposed redundancy and lookup-table explanations are author interpretations supported by diagnostics, rather than fully isolated causal demonstrations. Statistical indistinguishability does not establish equivalence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** the core grid uses one backbone and corpus; transfer to consumer EEG, other corpora, and hyperparameters remains unestablished. A full geometry-by-ratio sweep is absent (§5).
- **Training variability:** core runs use seed 42; probe seeds vary only random projection. **Analyst inference:** reported uncertainty does not capture independent pre-training variability (Table 2; Appendix C.2).
- **Imperfect ratio control:** realized masked fractions differ by geometry; Table 5 reports means ranging from 50.8% to 70.0% despite the 55% target. **Analyst inference:** geometry comparisons retain some masked-fraction confounding.
- **Benchmark exposure:** PhysioNet-MI occurs in this study’s pre-training corpus; REVE-Base additionally saw TUAB and TUEV. Authors acknowledge possible collective benchmark overfitting (§5).
- **Evaluation boundaries:** frozen linear probes establish representation accessibility under that protocol, not fine-tuning performance. SEED-VIG has negative test R² and subject-distribution shift; normalized scores use extrema from this sweep (§3.2; Appendices C.4–C.5).
- **Internal inconsistency:** §5 says VICReg/C-JEPA do not rescue collapse, but Appendix E.5 explicitly says they were not tested. Their ineffectiveness is a prediction, not an experimental result.
- **Qualified transfer and compute:** CBraMod’s positional encoding was replaced; the REVE compute comparison is not scale-matched (Appendices H.1, A.3).
- **Temporal labeling:** the main text calls \(L=2\) “2 s,” while the sampler uses patch units with overlapping one-second patches. Exact duration should be interpreted from tokenization details (Appendices A.1, A.4).

## Open research questions
- **Can regularization prevent the positional shortcut?** No rescue experiments were run. Compare auxiliary regularizers and positional-encoding changes at \(r=\text{all}\), tracking downstream scores and bias/residual diagnostics (Appendix E.5).
- **Does the default survive exact ratio matching and training replication?** Realized ratios vary and core training uses one seed. Repeat selected geometries with calibrated mask fractions and independent pre-training seeds (Tables 2, 5).
- **Can collapse be detected reliably during pre-training?** Proposed within-window thresholds lack independent validation. Test detectors on held-out corpora and montages, including healthy runs with slow early improvement (Appendix E.6).
- **Does the ranking transfer beyond frozen probes and clinical/BCI data?** Those boundaries remain unresolved. Repeat representative masks on consumer EEG and evaluate fine-tuning alongside linear probing (§5; §3.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported core findings; **medium** in their generality and causal interpretation.

Material missing evidence includes independent pre-training replications, a full joint geometry/ratio sweep, empirical regularizer-rescue tests, validated online collapse detection, and broader corpus/fine-tuning evaluations. The supplied text reports releases, but their accessibility and implementation were not independently verified.
