# Adaptive Consistency Graph for Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32754) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32754)


## One-sentence takeaway
ACG preserves source-linked execution history and supplies budgeted, requirement-centered context, improving average success over ReAct for both evaluated models while showing uneven benchmark gains and inference costs (Table 1, p. 6; §4.5).

## Problem and motivation
Long-running agents can lose the connection between original requirements, earlier observations, and current execution state. The authors argue that retrieving isolated facts or compressing history may discard the surrounding events needed to interpret evidence. Their identified gap is execution-time context construction: selecting relevant history while retaining recoverable provenance under a finite context budget (§§1–2).

The paper treats this as a motivation, not an established cause of agent failure; its trajectory analyses are observational (§1; Appendix D).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent execution memory:** ACG stores evidence units with source references, structured attributes, and separate event occurrences. Repeated content can share a unit without merging its execution origins (§3.2).
- **Relational retrieval:** Original-task and latest-interaction queries supply interleaved retrieval seeds. Embedding-based graph links and source-aware clusters expand the candidates (§3.3).
- **Requirement-qualified decision views:** Exact instruction spans define requirements. Evidence associations require validated action origins; semantic similarity cannot fill missing attribution. Temporary views prioritize current evidence and deduplicate shared content under a token budget (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Comparative evaluation:** The authors compare success, execution behavior, and inference expenditure across two models, three benchmarks, and five baselines (§4).

The contribution is this execution-memory and context-construction design. The structural-entropy clustering criterion is adopted, and ACG leaves planning and tool execution to the base agent (Appendix B.2; §3.1).

These matched-task results support conditional benefits from the complete ACG system. They do not isolate graph retrieval, provenance qualification, or rendering as the cause. Bootstrap uncertainty measures task-sampling variation conditional on recorded executions, not repeated-run variability (Appendix B.3). Failure-group diagnostics compare different outcome subsets and do not establish causal mechanisms.

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** Provenance establishes origin, not correctness or requirement completion. Ambiguous origins reduce requirement coverage; bounded views can omit relevant evidence; graph construction and retrieval add computation. Generalization beyond the tested models, tools, and task sets is unestablished (Appendix D).

**Analyst inferences supported by the supplied text:**

- No component ablations are reported, so the necessity of each memory mechanism remains unresolved (§4.6).
- Equal action limits do not provide equal inference budgets, limiting claims of efficiency superiority (§4.5).
- Repeated-run stability and paired uncertainty for ACG–baseline differences are not reported (Appendix B.3).
- Implementation detail is incomplete: the text does not identify the embedding model or fully specify memory decomposition, origin-declaration generation, and baseline adaptation procedures (§3; Appendix B).

## Open research questions
- **Which components drive improvement?** The paper does not isolate mechanisms. Next experiment: remove graph expansion, source-aware clustering, and requirement qualification separately under matched inference budgets.
- **How sensitive is ACG to missing action origins?** The authors identify reduced coverage but do not quantify it. Next experiment: systematically mask origin declarations and measure retrieval coverage and task success.
- **When does context organization justify its cost?** Call overhead and success vary by setting. Next experiment: sweep view budgets and retrieval settings while measuring success, total tokens, latency, and graph-processing cost.
- **Why does DeepSeek retrieval performance decline?** The benchmark-level reversal is reported without a causal explanation. Next experiment: inspect paired ACG/ReAct outcomes and test whether relevant evidence was omitted or incorrectly prioritized.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The reported method and benchmark outcomes are clear, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain incomplete.

Material missing information includes component ablations, repeated executions, paired significance estimates, complete implementation and baseline configurations, latency and graph-processing measurements, and raw assessable denominators for diagnostic percentages. Code availability is author-reported; its contents were not examined.
