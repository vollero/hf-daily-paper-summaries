# How Reproducible Are Evaluation Conclusions? A Self-Audit of LLM-Inferred Prompt Structure

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30074) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30074)


## One-sentence takeaway

This self-audit finds that repeated prompt-structure extraction is unstable and its small, uneven evaluation supports the bottom of the model ranking more strongly than the top, while analysis choices, correctness gaps, and endpoint retirement further restrict its conclusions (§§4–7).

## Problem and motivation

Tools that analyze existing prompts first need to recover their structure, but inconsistent extraction can make their diagnoses inconsistent. The authors investigate both this measurement problem and a second gap: whether a sorted evaluation table survives resampling, defensible analysis choices, correctness checks, and later attempts to rerun it (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Task-specific measurement:** Repeat identical requests with caching disabled, extract typed-node intermediate representations (IRs), and measure node-set Jaccard, ID-stability, and disagreement types. The main annotated panel covers eight model variants; a separate campaign tests unannotated SKILL.md prompts (§§2–4).
- **Evaluation self-audit:** Apply joint prompt-cluster bootstrapping, execute campaign-merge sensitivity comparisons, inspect stabilization specifications, check explicit annotations against ground truth, and audit endpoint availability. The contribution is this application and combined audit, not a new rank-uncertainty estimator (§§5–7).
- **Reported released assets:** Prompt corpora, 293 raw IRs, analysis scripts, and per-cell provenance support offline reanalysis (§§3, 10).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Annotated extraction is inconsistent | Across eight variants and 127 prompt-model cells, model-mean Jaccard spans 0.39–0.96; only 35 cells (28%) have identical node-id sets across runs. Repeats vary from 2–5. | §4.1, Table 1 |
| The ranking is strongest at the bottom | Among 9,583 usable joint-cluster-bootstrap replicates, gpt-oss:120b retains last place in 99% and qwen3-coder-next seventh in 86%; middle-model retention is 27–48%, and each top model retains its position in 68%. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Campaign merging changes conclusions | Higher-budget precedence versus earliest-campaign precedence changes four of eight rows and three ranks; the reported study-wide headline shifts by 7.1 percentage points. Its exact definition is not explicit in the supplied passage. | §5.2; Appendix E referenced |
| Inverse scaling is supported for one family | On 18 shared prompts, gpt-oss:120b has mean Jaccard 0.386 versus 0.766 for gpt-oss:20b: paired difference −0.380, Wilcoxon p=0.0046 with zero splitting and p=0.0036 with zero dropping. Other family comparisons do not support a general scaling conclusion. | §4.2 |
| Unannotated recovery has low agreement | Eleven models on 19 SKILL.md files, three requested runs each: 196 eligible cells yield mean exact-id Jaccard 0.22 and normalized Jaccard 0.25; 30 cells have exact-id Jaccard zero. | §4.3, Appendix C |
| Count disagreements dominate recorded drift | Across 98 cells with persisted IRs, macro-mean disagreement shares are 49% count, 31% metadata, and 19% id-label; the ordering survives alternative aggregations. | §4.4 |
| Stabilization cannot justify a rerun prescription | Among 98 node-count trajectories, absolute versus relative tolerance yields 50 versus 80 cells stabilizing before the endpoint; the endpoint itself passes by construction. | §5.1, Appendix C |
| Repeatability does not establish correctness | Annotation checks report mean recall 0.86 and explicit-node precision 0.82. Correctness and stability order models differently, and their correlations are not significant after removing the low outlier. | §6, Appendix B |
| Hosted retirement prevents repeating the original panel | The 2026-08-17 audit finds four of eight ranked variants retired on 2026-07-15; original measurements occurred from 2026-05-30 to 2026-06-11. | §7, Appendix A, Table 3 |

These results establish instability and analysis sensitivity in this particular extraction study. They do not establish a universal best model, a general effect of model size, or how often comparable failures occur on other LLM tasks. Rank retention is a bootstrap diagnostic, not a posterior probability. The recovery comparison changes corpus, annotation status, model panel, and completion pattern together, so it does not isolate annotation removal (§§4.3, 5.2, 8).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated scope limits:** One provider, a bounded measurement window, and unverifiable served weights restrict conclusions to models as served. The specialized task includes no standard benchmark or downstream evaluation (§8).
- **Authors’ stated statistical limits:** Unequal prompt support, small rows, varying repeat counts, and nonrandom completion failures weaken comparisons. Only specified within-family contrasts use matched prompts (§§3, 5.2, 8).
- **Authors’ stated measurement limits:** Node-id agreement misses metadata changes; normalization does not resolve synonyms; correctness covers explicit annotations only (§§3–4, 6).
- **Authors’ stated instrument defects:** Stabilization had specification errors. Historical accessibility failures remain unresolved, and the authors withdraw the family-level accessibility claim because model, provider, and harness causes were not distinguishable (§§5, 7).
- **Analyst inference:** Completion-based inclusion may make successful-run consistency look more favorable than whole-workflow reliability, consistent with the paper’s documented nonrandom missingness (Appendix C).

## Open research questions

1. **How much does annotation removal itself reduce consistency?** The current regime comparison is confounded. Next experiment: test matched annotated and annotation-stripped versions with the same models, repeat counts, and completion handling (§4.3).
2. **How much recovery disagreement is semantic rather than naming variation?** Normalization leaves synonyms unresolved. Next experiment: independently annotate semantic node correspondences across runs and compare semantic agreement with id-based metrics (§4.3).
3. **Does extraction instability change downstream tool decisions?** Node count is only an unvalidated proxy. Next experiment: run a concrete linter or coverage tool over repeated IRs and measure decision consistency and correctness (Appendix C).
4. **Can additional matched prompts resolve the leading ranks?** The leading rows have unequal support and uncertain rank retention. Next experiment: collect a larger, fully crossed panel under fixed settings and report rank intervals (§5.2).
