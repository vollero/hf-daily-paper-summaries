# SolveEdit: Benchmarking Visual Problem Solving in Generative Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35504) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35504)


## One-sentence takeaway
SolveEdit evaluates whether generators can infer and execute valid scene changes while preserving unrelated content, and the authors report that explicit transition planning raises GPT-Image-2’s SolveScore from 57.0% to 71.6% with one final generation (§5.4).

## Problem and motivation
Goal-driven visual editing requires determining *what* change solves a problem, rendering that change, and preserving everything outside its scope. The authors argue that existing evaluations often mix inference with execution of explicitly specified edits, while single-reference scoring can penalize alternative valid solutions (§1).

SolveEdit separates cases by where transition-defining information comes from: the grounded instruction, observable scene state, or an in-image rule (§3.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Benchmark and formulation:** 2,728 cases across 10 domains and 54 subdomains, labeled Instruction-Specified (IS), State-Dependent (SD), or Rule-Dependent (RD). These labels describe information dependence, not difficulty (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Contract-based evaluation:** Atomic required and protected conditions accept multiple valid outputs. SolveScore combines completion \(R\) and collateral damage \(D\) through a quality-gated \(\max(0,R-0.5D)\), with separate semantic, relational, and visual-quality diagnostics (§3.3).
- **Planning intervention:** SolveEdit-Plan uses Inspect to identify unresolved variables and request crops, then Resolve to compare candidate transitions and compile an instruction for an unchanged generator. It requires no training or hidden evaluation contracts (§4; Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results

All performance results below are author-reported; the main protocol uses the same 2,728-case manifest and SolveScore with \(\lambda=0.5\).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest direct generator remains limited | Among nine image and two video generators, GPT-Image-2 scores 57.0%, Seedream 5.0 Pro 56.6%, and Gemini 3.1 Flash Image 55.5%; video is evaluated using final frames. | §§5.1–5.2; Table 1 referenced, but its rows are absent |
| Similar scores conceal different preservation behavior | Direct GPT-Image-2 has completion R=67.6% and damage D=23.9%, versus Seedream’s D=17.6%. | §5.2 |
| Scene and rule dependence correlate with lower scores | Across the three leading models, SD trails IS by 7.0–8.8 SolveScore points and RD by 17.2–23.7 points. | §5.3; Fig. 5(a) |
| Required correctness deteriorates more than rendering quality | Domain-adjusted, pooled RD-minus-IS diagnostic gaps are −25.0 points for required semantic accuracy, −13.0 for required relational accuracy, and −5.6 for required visual quality. | §5.3; Fig. 5(b) |
| Structured planning improves GPT-Image-2 | SolveEdit-Plan raises SolveScore from 57.0% to 71.6% and exceeds Generic Vision Rewrite by 6.3 points; both planning methods use the same backend, two planning calls, and one final generation. | §5.4; Appendix C |
| Planning improves completion and preservation over generic rewriting | On GPT-Image-2, completion R rises from 72.8% to 75.8%, while damage D falls from 16.3% to 8.6%. | §5.4 |
| Gains transfer to two additional generators | Direct→Plan SolveScore: Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, 20.8%→26.9%; HunyuanVideo-1.5, 7.4%→14.0%, with terminal-frame video scoring. | §5.5 |
| Planning adds computation | Two planning calls per case average 3,207 input tokens and 635 output tokens before the single final generation. | §5.5 |

The evidence supports improved benchmark completion and preservation through the tested planning interface. The authors interpret diagnostic gaps as suggesting substantial solution-recovery errors; those gaps do not directly isolate recovery from execution. SolveScore is a weighted contract score, not a percentage of fully solved cases.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Video evaluation covers terminal states, excluding temporal reasoning (§§2, 5.1).
- **Author-stated residual uncertainty:** Low transfer scores leave substantial errors, and the experiments do not establish whether remaining failures originate in planning or generation (§5.5).
- **Author-stated budget caveat:** Planning adds computation over Direct, and equal call counts do not imply equal token usage (§5.5).
- **Analyst inference—control confound:** SolveEdit-Plan receives up to six additional crops; Generic Vision Rewrite receives none. Their comparison therefore bundles structured planning with additional visual inspection (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—measurement risk:** Evaluation relies primarily on GPT-5.6 Sol, with SAM/YOLO evidence and VLM reconciliation. Human agreement and evaluator error rates are unavailable in the supplied text (Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference—coverage sensitivity:** Protected abstentions contribute no established damage, so low D does not necessarily demonstrate preservation. Coverage must accompany interpretation (Appendix B.1).
- **Evidence gap:** Component ablations are mentioned, but their numerical results are unavailable in the retrieved text (§5.4).

## Open research questions

1. **How much benefit comes from structured reasoning versus crops?** The generic control lacks additional crops. A next experiment could match crop access and token budgets while varying transition variables and candidate comparison.
2. **Where do residual errors occur?** The authors leave planning versus generation unresolved (§5.5). Annotate intermediate transitions and compare inferred plans with oracle-valid instructions supplied to the same editor.
3. **How reliable are contract judgments?** Human agreement is not reported in the supplied text. Independently adjudicate a stratified sample across regimes and diagnostic dimensions, including abstentions.
4. **Does planning improve temporal validity?** Only final video frames are scored (§5.1). Extend contracts to intermediate states and evaluate continuity, preservation, and transition execution across clips.
