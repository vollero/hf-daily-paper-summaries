# Program-Verified Self-Evolution for Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33855) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33855)


## One-sentence takeaway
VQS computes training answers from structured image parses checked claim by claim, achieving 94.4% retained-answer correctness and reported average benchmark gains of 3.18, 2.32, and 2.43 points for Qwen3-VL-2B, 4B, and 8B, respectively (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Self-evolving vision-language models generate questions from unlabeled images but lack gold answers for training rewards. The authors identify unreliable pseudo-labels as a central gap: majority voting and model judging can reinforce incorrect answers despite apparent agreement. Their human evaluation finds retained-label correctness of 76.4% and 82.2%, respectively (Table 2).

The proposed remedy separates answer computation from solver consensus while retaining model-based verification of the underlying visual facts.

## Approach and contributions
- **Program-computed QA:** A schema-constrained parser converts photographs, charts, diagrams, and infographics into structured records. Handwritten, fixed templates generate questions, answers, and operation counts from these records; the parser paraphrases the questions (§3).
- **Claim-level verification and parser improvement:** The checker verifies facts used by each template. For parser training, it scores candidate parses by verified-claim fraction; sufficiently informative winners become supervised fine-tuning targets without human training labels (§3, Eqs. 5–6).
- **Filtered reinforcement learning:** Questions must pass fact verification, a text-only answerability gate, and a solver difficulty band. GRPO rewards agreement with computed answers plus answer-format compliance (§3, Eqs. 1–4).
- **Iterative self-evolution:** Subsequent cycles regenerate QA on the same images, retain earlier QA, and resample toward intermediate solver difficulty (§5, Fig. 8).

Program-generated questions themselves are precedented in the paper’s related work; the claimed distinction is integrating visual claim verification, label-free parser training, and solver self-evolution (§2).

These results support improved retained-label quality, structured parsing, and benchmark performance in the evaluated settings. They do not establish guaranteed visual correctness: deterministic execution is correct only relative to its parsed inputs. VQS also trades retention for accuracy, and the strongest-baseline claim concerns the overall average, not winning every individual benchmark.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged shared errors:** Parser and checker share weights and may share blind spots. The cross-checker audit identifies diagram arrows and chart values as warning areas without resolving their true error rates (A.3).
- **Author-acknowledged paraphrase risk:** Verification does not explicitly test whether paraphrases preserve program semantics. The audit found occasional year-order reversals that made stored answers wrong (§5, p. 7).
- **Analyst inference—completeness:** Checking parsed fields does not necessarily verify that all relevant objects or values were captured. Schema size caps and the truncated-chart example illustrate a potential risk for counts, extrema, and aggregates (A.4, A.5.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal precision:** Component ablations support the pipeline choices, but the text provides no repeated-run uncertainty or complete accounting of matched computation across the main baselines.
- **Analyst inference—bounded coverage:** Multiple model families are tested, but repeated-cycle evidence is confined to Qwen3-VL-2B and three cycles. Templates and schemas constrain the questions the system can generate (§5; A.3–A.4).

## Open research questions
- **Would stronger independent checkers improve training?** The paper audits other checkers but does not use them for training. Compare shared-weight and larger independent checkers at matched curation budgets, with human-rated labels and downstream scores (A.3).
- **Can verification detect missing visual facts?** Reported parse precision does not establish exhaustive coverage. Add completeness checks and test deliberately omitted objects or chart values on count and aggregate questions (A.4–A.5).
- **Do gains persist over longer evolution?** Only three cycles at 2B are reported. Extend cycles across scales while tracking human-rated label quality, benchmark scores, and QA diversity (§5, Fig. 8).
- **Can semantic paraphrase checking prevent answer corruption?** Existing gates do not directly check meaning preservation. Add a question–program equivalence check and measure correctness versus retention (§5, p. 7).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description and reported results; **medium** in broader validity.
