# RoboFoundry: System-as-Policy Evolution for Self-Learning Embodied Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32862) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32862)


## One-sentence takeaway

RoboFoundry improves frozen-model embodied agents by turning execution failures into persistent context and skill edits, with reported gains across decision-making, memory, perturbation robustness, and selected physical-robot tasks (§§2–3).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that embodied performance depends on the entire supporting system—context, memory, skills, and execution interfaces—while existing approaches typically optimize individual components. Interaction exposes recurring failures but does not automatically convert experience into validated improvements that persist across tasks (§1).

The proposed gap is therefore systematic diagnosis and revision of the agent stack, including deciding whether a repair should remain task-specific or become a general rule.

## Approach and contributions

- **System evolution with a frozen backbone:** An execution loop collects observations, actions, states, and feedback; an outer loop edits the supporting filesystem rather than model parameters (§2.1).
- **Capability-guided context and skill revision:** Failures are attributed to perception, reasoning, planning, saving, retrieval, or utilization. Context edits change persistent memory and active inputs; skill edits change atomic execution, compositions, and recovery structures (§§2.2–2.3).
- **Task repair and general promotion:** Task edits undergo another execution. General edits are assessed on other tasks’ recorded pre-repair traces using the model–system pair as a verifier; promotion requires a nonnegative capability-conditioned score difference (§2.3).
- **Semantic/execution separation:** Shared task logic is grounded through replaceable robot-specific bindings, intended to support reuse across embodiments (§2.2).

The authors claim the framework is the first embodied formulation of self-evolving system-as-policy; the supplied text does not independently establish that priority claim (§1).

These comparisons support improvements under the reported adaptive evaluation protocols. They do not establish universal superiority in every setting, broad autonomous deployment, or equivalence between stronger and weaker backbones. General-edit validation uses model-scored proposals from recorded traces rather than fresh physical execution on every held-out task (§2.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-identified bottleneck:** Aggregate gains with privileged object poses indicate remaining perception limitations (§3.3).
- **Author-documented incomplete repair:** The Sequence case improves intermediate progress without resolving the full task (Appendix A.2.6).
- **Analyst inference—verification risk:** The system itself judges general-edit transfer; independent verifier calibration and broad regression testing are not reported (§2.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal isolation:** Lite tests general evolution, but separate ablations of context, skills, recovery, and semantic bindings are absent from the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference—physical scope:** Physical evaluations use selected tasks and 10 trials per task; navigation and chemistry lack numerical comparative results (§3.4).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Table 1 and Tables 7–8 disagree on some suite scores; Table 1 also reports some gains inconsistent with its displayed baselines. Table 2’s “Overall” aggregation differs from Table 3’s category “Avg.” without an explicit explanation. Section 3.2 incorrectly points to Table 4 for architecture-specific context results, which appear in Table 3.
- **Missing uncertainty and cost:** Confidence intervals, repeated-run variability, and evolution compute/latency costs are not reported.

## Open research questions

- **Does model-scored promotion predict actual transfer?** Recorded-trace verification leaves this uncertain (§2.3). Compare promotion scores with fresh executions on untouched tasks, including regression checks.
- **Which evolution surface produces which gains?** Lite isolates general scope but not individual mechanisms (§3.1). Run budget-matched context-only, skill-only, and combined variants.
- **How broad is cross-embodiment reuse?** Physical demonstrations provide limited quantitative transfer evidence (§3.4). Freeze an evolved semantic system and test multiple unseen robots using only replacement execution bindings.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and principal comparisons are explicit, but inconsistent tables and limited uncertainty reporting constrain interpretation.

Material missing information includes verifier prompts and calibration, task-order sensitivity, repeated-run statistics, evolution costs, exact towel success counts, quantitative navigation/chemistry outcomes, reconciled table values and aggregation rules, and confirmed availability of implementation or trace artifacts.
