# AnswerMap: Faithful Spatial Interpretability of VLMs from Answer Posteriors

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35247) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35247)


## One-sentence takeaway
AnswerMap constructs spatial explanations from isolated-band yes/no posteriors, outperforming evaluated attribution baselines on model-point agreement and deletion tests while providing useful—but uneven—localization, hallucination detection, and crop-based answer refinement.

## Problem and motivation
The authors seek visual explanations of where a VLM’s answer comes from. They identify three gaps: text rationales lack explicit spatial regions, internal attribution requires model access and may poorly reflect final answers, and cell-wise occlusion is costly and can miss redundant evidence (§§1–2).

Their proposed alternative measures whether isolated image bands support a query, then turns those output probabilities into a spatial map and fixed answer read-outs.

## Approach and contributions
- **Posterior-based spatial map:** Show each of \(K\) horizontal and \(K\) vertical bands separately to a frozen VLM. Normalize first-token yes/no logits into posteriors and take their outer product, producing a \(K\times K\) map from \(2K\) queries (§3.1). The paper explicitly builds on GridProbe’s existing factorization; its contribution is applying it to image explanations and answer interfaces (§2).
- **Multigrid composition:** Upsample, normalize, and multiply maps from different band widths to combine context and precision (§3.2).
- **Fixed read-outs:** Use the map’s expectation for location, maximum for grounding strength, and top-mass region for an additional crop (§§3.1, 5).
- **Faithfulness protocol:** Evaluate agreement with the model’s generated point and answer changes after deleting mapped regions, with matched random controls (§3.3).

The evidence supports spatial correspondence with model outputs, intervention sensitivity, and selected downstream uses. Agreement with the model does not establish correctness against ground truth; deletion sensitivity does not uniquely establish the mechanism behind the original answer. The authors’ claim of guaranteed image dependence concerns the fixed read-out’s construction, not guaranteed localization accuracy or complete causal attribution. Closed-model examples are qualitative only (§4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Non-square inputs are required. Row–column factorization creates spurious intersections when multiple matching objects occupy different rows and columns (§7).
- **Context sensitivity:** Thin bands lose useful context; refining a single grid eventually degrades agreement. Multigrid improves agreement but provides no deletion gain in the reported comparison (§6).
- **Analyst inference—faithfulness scope:** Isolated-band sufficiency can differ from evidence used during full-image answering. The two tests support the explanation operationally but cannot identify that original computation uniquely (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference—intervention ambiguity:** Region deletion also changes answers labeled image-unused by whole-image deletion. The maximum does not predict whole-image reliance, with reported AUC **0.49** (Appendix A.4).
- **Comparison boundaries:** TAM uses a separate answer-conditioned regime; attention receives overlapping calibration/evaluation examples. Neither comparison is a uniform access-and-conditioning match (§4.1).
- **Uncertainty reporting:** No confidence intervals or significance tests are supplied. Small replicated deletion margins and the modest crop gain therefore have unclear statistical precision.

## Open research questions
- **Can additional probes resolve false intersections?** The separable map cannot associate rows with columns for multiple objects (§7). Test selective cell probes on multi-object scenes, measuring localization errors and added query cost.
- **How closely does band sufficiency track full-image answer evidence?** Current tests do not isolate redundant or contextual evidence (§3.3). Compare maps under controlled distractor, redundancy, and context interventions.
- **When should a map replace generated pointing?** It helps Lingshu but substantially trails Qwen3-VL-4B on RefCOCOg (Table 5). Evaluate a held-out selection rule using parse success and map uncertainty.
- **How robust is crop refinement?** The reported net benefit comes from one model and one TextVQA sample (Table 7). Repeat paired evaluations across models and tasks, reporting uncertainty and newly introduced errors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods, prompts, configurations, and results, but supports narrower conclusions than the strongest faithfulness claims.

Material missing information includes confidence intervals, exact example identifiers for several evaluations, quantitative closed-model results, handling of flat-map normalization and zero-denominator expectations, and independent verification of the linked code and reported experiments.
