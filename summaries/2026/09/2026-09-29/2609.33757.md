# YuE2: Unifying Symbolic and Audio Music Generation at Frontier Quality

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33757) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33757)


## One-sentence takeaway
YuE2 generates editable scores before audio, with controlled expert comparisons supporting improved song quality from symbolic planning and unified generation, while proprietary-system comparisons show competitive rather than uniformly superior performance (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Symbolic generators expose composition but typically stop before producing recordings; audio generators produce finished songs while leaving composition implicit. The authors seek a single generator whose melody, harmony, rhythm, and form remain readable and editable. Training this hierarchy is difficult because recordings generally lack aligned scores and semantic annotations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Score-to-audio generation:** A unified autoregressive/non-autoregressive Mixture-of-Transformers generates ABC notation, then semantic tokens, then acoustic latents through flow matching; a separately trained decoder produces stereo audio. Joint training includes conditions that omit intermediate representations, enabling within-checkpoint planning comparisons (§2).
- **Supervision from recordings:** MERT2 learns from quantized targets combining frozen MuQ and Qwen2-Audio representations, with separate transcription and semantic-tokenizer branches. SheetSage2 jointly transcribes musical attributes into lead sheets, learning from labels produced by its multitask Prober (§3).
- **Reusable composition interface:** The same YuE2 checkpoint renders generated scores, edited scores, and transcriptions of existing works without cover-specific training. External language-model editing is demonstrated through a curated case study (§5).
- **Controlled evidence:** The paper separately compares planning versus no planning and unified generation versus a separate language model plus diffusion Transformer, holding major inputs and representations fixed (§§4.2–4.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Symbolic planning improves perceived quality | Same checkpoint, prompts, decoder, and two-candidate budget on WSB. Among 211 judged overall-quality responses, planning wins 49.3% versus 34.6% without planning; ties retained. Clustered two-sided test: p = 0.0070. | §4.2; Fig. 8; Table 21 |
| Unified generation beats the tested separate architecture | With planning and matched data volume/representations, MoT receives 53.4% overall preferences versus 35.6% for LM+DiT across 208 judged responses; p = 0.0084. | §4.3; Table 21 |
| Strong automatic song-quality results | Across 192 WSB prompts, two-candidate YuE2 achieves SongBench Global Avg 6.7316, highest among evaluated public baselines. Best-of-8 reaches 6.9632, highest observed mean across evaluated systems, versus Mureka 9’s 6.9377 under the standard budget. | §4.1; Tables 1–2 |
| Expert comparisons qualify the automatic ranking | Best-of-8 overall preferences: 57.3% versus Suno v4.5’s 30.5%; 40.4% versus Suno v5’s 39.9%; 31.6% versus Suno v6’s 59.3%. Shares retain ties and exclude unable-to-judge answers. | §4.1; Fig. 7; Table 21 |
| Planned composition survives rendering | Expert-corrected transcriptions yield melody/chord sequence similarities of 0.9464/0.9246 for corresponding audio, versus 0.2160/0.2473 for another score’s audio under the same prompt. Metric-specific valid cases cover 191 WSB prompts. | §5.1; Table 3; Appendix D.1 |
| Score edits provide selective musical control | On eligible WSB scores, melody edits attain 84.17% target-pitch accuracy, harmony edits 79.54% target-chord agreement, and rhythm edits 73.43% onset accuracy; unchanged melody/harmony agreement spans 90.16–94.34%. All generated outputs are retained. | §5.2; Table 4; Appendix D.2 |
| Covers preserve work identity | Across 948 unseen SHS100K works and 3,792 outputs per method without selection, full-score YuE2 achieves CLEWS/Discogs-VINet mAP 0.647/0.288, versus SongEcho’s 0.419/0.122; it leads both evaluated cover baselines on all eight retrieval metrics. | §5.3; Table 5 |
| Representation and transcription gains | Frozen-feature MERT2-30s probes lead listed external baselines on 14/15 MARBLE metrics. The semantic tokenizer leads two quantized alternatives on 5/6 metrics at 375 bits/s. SheetSage2-AR leads 12/15 transcription benchmark–metric pairs and its Prober teacher on 10/15. | §6; Tables 6–8, 11 |

The strongest evidence supports planning within this checkpoint and unified generation against the specific controlled baseline. It does not establish universal superiority: automatic and expert rankings differ, and best-of-8 uses additional samples and selection metrics that also appear in evaluation. Editing results establish preservation of measured composition, not preservation of the original waveform or performance.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-disclosed tradeoff:** Fixed source scores improve cover identity retrieval but constrain style adaptation; removing chords or the entire score increases alignment and quality metrics while weakening identity preservation (§5.3).
- **Author-disclosed measurement scope:** Score-agreement metrics were developed on the evaluated cohort and use expert-corrected transcriptions. Rhythm excludes expressive microtiming; chord matching omits extensions and inversions. Some generated scores are invalid or lack evaluable content (Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference—selection and evaluator dependence:** Best-of-8 selects partly using SongBench Musicality and Q3O, so those scores are not independent selection checks. In cover evaluation, Qwen3-Omni supplies both style annotations and Q3O judgments; Q3O covers only the common available subset (Appendices A.3, D.3).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** WSB centers on Chinese/English requests. The cover cohort is almost entirely English and requires usable lyrics, complete transcriptions, and alternative performances. Broader linguistic and musical coverage is not established (§4.1; Appendix D.3).
- **Analyst inference—causal and statistical limits:** Planning adds score tokens, so candidate-budget matching is not compute matching. Architecture sizes are comparable rather than identical. Tests report unadjusted p-values; melody-only versus no-plan and full versus melody-only overall preferences are not statistically significant (Table 21; Appendix D.4.4).
- **Demonstration scope:** Agentic editing is a curated researcher-guided case study, not a systematic evaluation of autonomous editing (§5.4).
- **Coverage limitation:** The supplied source is truncated, including omitted material around the transcription and model-detail appendices; this analysis does not provide complete-paper coverage.

## Open research questions
- **Does planning remain beneficial at equal inference cost?** Additional score generation is not compute-matched (§4.2). Next experiment: compare planned and unplanned generation under equal latency or compute budgets with blinded listening.
- **Can identity and style adaptation be improved together?** Cover results show a tradeoff, and chord removal also changes serialization (§5.3). Next experiment: compare controlled reharmonization with chord deletion while holding serialization and sampling fixed.
- **How robust is the score interface outside the evaluated cohorts?** Language coverage, transcription eligibility, and supported meters restrict demonstrated scope (§4.1; Appendices B.3, D.3). Next experiment: evaluate broader languages, meters, and imperfect source transcriptions with independent annotations.
- **Does agentic editing work reliably across users and requests?** Evidence is limited to a curated case (§5.4). Next experiment: run a multi-user study measuring edit success, preservation, and required iterations against direct score editing.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed protocols, controlled comparisons, and statistical tables support the main findings, but truncation limits completeness and external validity remains bounded by the evaluated settings.

Material information unavailable or not established in the supplied text includes the omitted appendix content, complete training-corpus provenance, end-to-end inference cost comparisons, independent replication, and systematic agentic-editing results.
