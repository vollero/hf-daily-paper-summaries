# Learning Native Reflection in Unified Models with Interleaved Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35767) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35767)


## One-sentence takeaway
On BAGEL, jointly reinforcing complete reflection-and-editing trajectories improves GenEval by 12.05 percentage points over reflection SFT and transfers to three additional benchmarks, although reliable stopping and counting remain unresolved (§4; Appendices P–Q).

## Problem and motivation
Unified multimodal models can inspect and revise their own images, but imitating successful repair demonstrations does not ensure that they choose effective corrections. The authors identify a credit-assignment gap: a textual diagnosis becomes useful only through the image revisions it triggers, potentially across several rounds, whereas prior approaches optimize single renders or imitate multi-round trajectories (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Supervised initialization:** Distill 29,529 trajectories produced using GPT-5.5 and Qwen image models into BAGEL, teaching structured inspection, editing, and stopping (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Whole-trajectory RL:** Sample 16 sibling trajectories from one shared, detached initial image. A single group-relative advantage updates both reflection-token actions and flow-based image revisions, avoiding per-round branching (§3.4).
- **Outcome-shaped reward:** Combine terminal alignment, incremental improvement, additional credit for improvement across multiple edits, regression penalties, and premature-stopping penalties. The frozen verifier operates only during training (§3.4).
- **Behavioral analysis:** Head ablations, instruction swaps, rollout statistics, and representation probes support the authors’ interpretation that RL makes existing repair behavior more reliably useful (§§5–6; Appendices F–J).

“Native” refers to shared model parameters for reflection and rendering; an external controller still orchestrates the rounds (§3.4, footnote).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher compositional alignment | On 553 GenEval prompts, six-family macro accuracy rises from approximately 0.72 for SFT to 0.84 for RL; authors report +12.05 percentage points. Matched local evaluation uses 512×512 images and up to three repairs. | Abstract; §4.2; Table 1 |
| Transfer beyond RL training families | SFT → RL: original-WISE weighted score 0.6287 → 0.7384 on 1,000 prompts; OneIG alignment 0.7937 → 0.8286 on 695 eligible prompts; CompBench eight-category mean 0.5043 → 0.5506 on 2,400 prompts. | Tables 11–13 |
| Better repair, with more damage | On GenEval, conditional repair rises from 20.59% to 64.94%; conditional damage to initially correct images rises from 6.53% to 8.77%. Initial prompt-micro accuracy is 69.26% versus 72.15%. | Table 8 |
| Joint optimization beats either channel alone | GenEval macro accuracy: flow-only RL 73, text-only RL 78, joint RL 84 on the 0–100 scale. | Table 3 |
| Advantage persists at equal image count | Four-image-budget GenEval evaluation: Best-of-4 from T2I-RL scores approximately 80 with either native selector; UMM-Reflection scores 84. Forced three-edit SFT remains at 72. | §5; Table 3 |
| Learned instructions improve execution | With the RL renderer, input states, and noise fixed, repair succeeds for 48.4% with RL instructions versus 21.4% with SFT instructions and 27.6% with rule-written instructions; 154 failing states, two seeds each. | Appendix J |
| Existing correctness readout changes little | Held-out understanding-stream probe AUC at depth 20: Base 0.804, SFT 0.807, RL 0.815; folds are grouped by prompt. | Appendix A; Table 5 |

These results support improved alignment and repair under the paper’s controlled evaluation. They do not establish equal-latency superiority, uniform category gains, or a general mechanism across architectures. Stable probes and successful SFT rollouts support—but do not prove—the authors’ stronger claim that RL only selects existing capabilities.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Documented failures:** Incorrect reflections can damage correct images or perpetuate factual errors (Figure 19). Counting accuracy stays at 67.5% from the RL model’s initial to final images, despite its advantage over SFT’s final score (Table 10; Appendix Q).
- **Stopping remains ineffective:** The primary RL policy uses all three edits. A tested stopping-bonus variant causes premature acceptance and lowers accuracy (Table 3; Appendix P).
- **Author-disclosed comparison boundaries:** Published leaderboard protocols differ from local evaluation. Direct T2I-RL also differs in initialization and training/inference compute; equal updates are not equal compute (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Experiments cover one backbone and one evaluated output per prompt. The text provides no repeated-training-seed uncertainty or human assessment of overall visual quality.
- **Analyst inference—incomplete causal isolation:** No independent-root ablation isolates shared-root sampling. Removing the multi-improvement reward also changes initial accuracy substantially, complicating attribution to repair quality alone (Table 3).
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic caution:** Attention analyses cover only ten trajectories, and probe targets are verifier judgments; these do not establish comprehensive visual understanding (Appendices F–H).

## Open research questions
1. **Can stopping reduce cost without sacrificing repair?** The primary policy exhausts its edit budget, while the tested bonus stops prematurely. Evaluate alternative stopping objectives against accuracy, damage, and measured latency.
2. **Does shared-root sampling independently improve learning?** Its rationale is clear, but its contribution is not isolated. Compare shared and independent roots under matched rollout compute and repeated training seeds.
3. **Can reflection reliably repair counts?** Counting remains unchanged across RL inference rounds. Test count-focused training examples and edit instructions on held-out counting prompts.
4. **Does the method generalize across backbones and native resolutions?** Evidence is restricted to BAGEL at reduced resolution. Repeat matched SFT/RL comparisons on another unified model and at BAGEL’s native resolution.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Detailed controlled results support the reported gains; broader and mechanistic conclusions are less secure.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, matched wall-clock inference comparisons, human quality evaluation, independent-root ablations, and validation on additional backbones. The supplied text links artifacts but does not establish their current completeness or availability.
