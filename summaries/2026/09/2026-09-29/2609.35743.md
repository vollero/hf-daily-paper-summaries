# InfiniHand: Streaming World-Space Hand Motion Estimation from Egocentric Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35743) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35743)


## One-sentence takeaway
InfiniHand combines streaming hand–camera prediction with sparse bundle adjustment, reporting lower world-space hand errors on three benchmarks, while metric-scale recovery and quantitative in-the-wild validation remain unresolved (§§3.3, 4.3, 5).

## Problem and motivation
Recovering world-space hand motion from egocentric RGB video requires separating articulated hand movement from camera motion. The authors identify error propagation, scale inconsistencies, and computational overhead in pipelines that combine independent hand estimators and SLAM systems. Their goal is scalable geometric annotation of human video for embodied learning; robot-learning benefits are proposed rather than evaluated (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified streaming estimation:** A LingBot-Map backbone supplies geometric features and persistent memory for hand localization and camera prediction. Hand crops combine geometric features with WiLoR appearance features to regress MANO geometry; differentiable least-squares projection recovers lateral translation (§§3.2–3.3).
- **World-space refinement:** Joint hand–camera training couples local articulation with global motion. Sparse DROID bundle adjustment revisits selected historical frames to reduce camera drift before transforming hands into world coordinates. Thus, the complete system includes optimization despite its feed-forward predictor (§3.3; Appendix C).
- **Data and training pipeline:** The authors aggregate approximately 5,000 hours from nine public datasets, standardize annotations, fit MANO where necessary, and filter inconsistent labels. Camera-space pretraining precedes joint streaming training on the camera-annotated subset (§3.1). Release of the curated corpus or model is not established.

The contribution is the integration and training pipeline; the backbone, appearance encoder, MANO representation, and BA backend are adopted components.

These are author-reported results available in the prose; the supplied text contains table captions but no table bodies. It supports benchmark improvements and component contributions, but not independent verification of every ranking. PA-p includes missed-hand penalties, whereas world-space errors average matched hand-frame pairs only (Appendix A). In-the-wild evidence is qualitative. Hardware, timing details, and whether the reported FPS includes all BA and scale-alignment costs are unclear.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Metric scale requires an auxiliary alignment model whose errors can propagate into hand placement and trajectories (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Limited coverage of large two-hand motions and challenging camera dynamics restricts generalization under rapid viewpoint changes, occlusion, and intermittent visibility (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Derived monocular annotations can contain time-varying scale drift, compromising temporal smoothness and long-sequence accuracy (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** Matched-only world metrics do not measure the complete cost of missed hands; detection and world-space performance must be read together (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Ablations are confined to ARCTIC and do not isolate corpus size or cleaning effects. They therefore cannot establish how much improvement comes from data scaling versus architecture (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Windowed prediction and asynchronous refinement make throughput insufficient to establish online latency (§4.4).

## Open research questions
- **Can metric scale be recovered more reliably?** External alignment remains a stated weakness (§5). Compare scale-recovery variants against metric ground truth, separating scale error from articulation and camera drift.
- **How robust is reconstruction during prolonged hand invisibility?** The paper identifies intermittent visibility as a limitation and provides qualitative examples (§5; Appendix D). Evaluate controlled occlusion durations with detection, re-entry, and world-trajectory metrics.
- **What does data curation contribute independently?** No reported ablation isolates cleaning or corpus size (§4.5). Train matched models on controlled corpus sizes with and without filtering.
- **What accuracy–latency tradeoff does sparse BA create?** BA improves ARCTIC accuracy, but its full runtime cost is unclear (§§4.4–4.5). Sweep keyframe thresholds and sparsity while measuring complete-system latency, memory, and long-sequence error.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and selected numerical comparisons are explicit, but material gaps include full Tables 1–3, uncertainty estimates, timing hardware and complete-system costs, the auxiliary scale-alignment method, cleaning thresholds and retained dataset proportions, train/test separation details, BA threshold/sparsity values, and code/model/corpus release status.
