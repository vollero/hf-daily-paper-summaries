# Groupwise Agentic Grading and Advantage Redistribution for Code Agent RL

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32577) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32577)


## One-sentence takeaway

Gagar improves reported code-agent success, efficiency, and judged implementation quality by redistributing reinforcement among test-passing solutions, although its bounded training implementation does not always preserve the exact credit-conservation properties of the core formulation (§4; Appendix A.2).

## Problem and motivation

Binary test rewards give all successful trajectories within a rollout group identical advantages, even when their patches differ in precision, unnecessary changes, or unintended side effects. The authors seek a learning signal that distinguishes implementation quality while retaining reinforcement for task success. They argue that this requires repository-aware comparison and careful credit allocation, rather than simply penalizing weaker passing solutions (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Groupwise agentic grading:** An SFT MiMo-V2.6-Pro grader jointly inspects trajectories, patches, repository files, and test results, optionally executing checks. It ranks passing solutions using five quality criteria, tiers, and evidence-supported comparisons; failed attempts supply context (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Advantage redistribution:** Quality factors discount passing advantages, followed by proportional rescaling to restore their original total. The exact formulation preserves failed advantages and quality-induced ratios; it reduces to ordinary outcome advantages when passing factors are equal (§3.3).
- **Training integration and evaluation:** Asynchronous grading, validation, and fallback handling support large-scale RL. Controlled evidence comes from code-only Flash training; mixed-task Flash and Pro runs demonstrate deployment at larger training scales (§§3.4–4.5).

The implementation caps rescaling and re-centers advantages. When that cap activates, exact positive-credit conservation and unchanged failed advantages no longer hold (Appendix A.2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated qualification:** Exact conservation applies only to the uncapped formulation. Flash uses a rescaling cap of **1.5**; its activation frequency is unreported (Appendix A.2).
- **Author-stated scope of ablation:** The downweighting-only comparator lacks subsequent centering. Its instability does not establish that reward shaping generally causes instability (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No reported ablations separate groupwise grading, tool-based inspection, rubric choices, or integrity corrections (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Analyst inference—quality validity:** A distinct model judges the audit, but uses the same quality criteria. Human agreement and actual developer review effort are not measured (§4.3).
- **Analyst inference—generalization and uncertainty:** Controlled results cover Flash and two coding benchmarks; independent training replications and uncertainty intervals are not reported (§4).
- **Author-reported operational constraint:** Online grading averages approximately **600 seconds per group**, despite asynchronous overlap; net training cost is not quantified (§3.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Which grading capabilities matter most?** Their contributions are not isolated. Compare groupwise versus individual grading and interactive versus static inspection under matched budgets.
2. **Does exact conservation explain the stability gain?** The bounded implementation can violate it, and the ablation omits re-centering. Compare uncapped redistribution, capped redistribution, and re-centered downweighting while recording cap activation.
3. **Do judged quality gains translate into developer benefits?** The audit provides model preferences, not human productivity evidence. Conduct blinded maintainer reviews measuring acceptance, revision effort, and regressions.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported comparisons are clear, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain limited.

Material missing information includes training-task data and mixture composition, grader training and accuracy validation, full optimization and execution settings, actual use of length penalties, cap and fallback frequencies, independent runs and uncertainty estimates, total grading cost, complete table/figure contents, and release availability.
