# Knowing When Thinking Is Not Enough: Teaching Small Reasoning Models to Reason Beyond Their Parametric Knowledge

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34327) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34327)


## One-sentence takeaway
FlyBy trains small reasoning models to request targeted information from stronger models during reasoning, achieving 45.96% average pass@8 versus Qwen3-14B’s 41.64% at lower estimated serving cost on a selected hard subset (§5, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
Additional thinking may help execute an accessible solution but cannot reliably recover missing knowledge. The authors distinguish **execution bottlenecks** from **knowledge bottlenecks** and argue that existing tool-use systems insufficiently examine which intermediate failures require external information (§§1, 3).

Their distinction is operational: a state with no successful sampled continuation is “knowledge-like,” rather than proven incapable of reaching the answer. This matters for small models because the interventions suggest they struggle both to recover relevant knowledge internally and to use supplied information effectively (§§3.1–3.3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reasoning-state diagnosis:** Paired interventions compare reflection, relevant oracle hints, and unrelated hints from the same reasoning prefixes across Qwen and Gemma models. The authors find reflection mainly strengthens already accessible solutions, whereas relevant information can improve low-reachability states (§3).
- **Selective, multi-depth querying:** FlyBy interleaves local reasoning with short questions to external models, selecting among backends with different strengths, response budgets, and costs. Backends receive the query rather than the original problem; overlap filtering discourages problem delegation (§4.1, Table 1).
- **Learning to seek and integrate help:** Supervised fine-tuning teaches query generation and observation integration; cost-aware reinforcement learning rewards correct answers while penalizing relatively expensive successful trajectories. The 4B SFT set contains 475 examples derived partly from 95 single-query rescues (§4.2, §§D.3–D.4).

The bottleneck distinction motivates training; the text does not describe a separately supervised bottleneck classifier.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| FlyBy-4B improves coverage and estimated cost | Across 1,158 selected hard problems in six benchmarks, average pass@8 is **45.96%**, versus **21.17%** for Qwen3-4B and **41.64%** for Qwen3-14B; estimated cost per eight rollouts is **7.42**, **10.63**, and **20.35 m$**, respectively. | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Gains extend to individual rollouts | On the same hard subset, average pass@1 is **16.85%** for FlyBy-4B versus **15.31%** for Qwen3-8B; cost per rollout is **0.93 versus 1.92 m$**. | Table 6 |
| RL improves the SFT initialization | Average hard-subset pass@8 rises from **36.39% to 45.96%**, with cost per eight rollouts rising from **7.15 to 7.42 m$**. | Table 2 |
| A stronger local core improves coverage | FlyBy-8B obtains **51.81%** average pass@8 versus Qwen3-8B’s **34.23%**, at **14.75 versus 15.38 m$** per eight rollouts. | Table 2 |
| Selected query states benefit more from querying | Counterfactual analysis on 50 SuperGPQA problems, with eight FlyBy rollouts per problem and Qwen3-4B probes: querying yields **+5.30 percentage points** in state value; budget-forced additional thinking yields **−0.41 points**. | §5.2, Figure 6a |
| Zero observed success is sampling-dependent | Of **76** states with zero successes after eight null continuations, **28** become positive by 64; those reclassified states still show average value gains of **0.242** from oracle information versus **0.026** from reflection cues. | §B, Figure 7 |
| Backend substitution retains substantial performance | On the same hard evaluation, replacing DeepSeek without retraining gives average pass@8 of **43.21%** with GPT-5.6 Luna and **43.96%** with HY3, versus **45.96%** originally. | §G.1, Table 7 |

These results support selective querying as an effective accuracy–cost strategy in the tested setting. They establish expanded **observed** coverage, not absolute limits of parametric knowledge. The reported averages are across benchmarks; costs are modeled from measured throughput and price snapshots rather than end-to-end deployment measurements (§D.2). Oracle-hint interventions and learned query-time assistance also use different information-generation conditions (§C, §D.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** External APIs introduce availability, privacy, latency, and deployment constraints; incorrect backend information can propagate into reasoning (§I).
- **Author-acknowledged:** Finite continuation samples cannot establish literal unreachability. Higher sampling budgets reclassify some knowledge-like states (§B).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation selected for Qwen3-4B difficulty supports conclusions about that hard subset, not overall benchmark performance. Fresh base-model rollouts mitigate selection bias but do not broaden the population (§E.1, Table 5).
- **Analyst inference:** EV-token suppression measures a lexical intervention, so its effect need not isolate every form of self-refinement (§A).
- **Analyst inference:** The upfront-query comparison changes training, timing, and query count simultaneously; it does not isolate the contribution of each component (§E.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The leakage check provides reassurance rather than proof: it tests thinking-disabled accuracy on sampled observations and cannot exclude all indirect answer disclosure (§G.2).
- Retrieval costs are excluded for Search-R1, and the supplied results lack confidence intervals or repeated-seed estimates (Tables 2, 6; Table 4).

## Open research questions
- **How robustly does FlyBy recognize knowledge gaps across reasoning budgets?** Classification changes with sampling (§B). Next experiment: repeat query-state interventions using larger continuation budgets and multiple probe policies.
- **Which learned component drives the gain?** Current comparisons do not separately isolate query timing, query quality, observation integration, and repeated calls (§§D.3, E.2). Next experiment: matched-training ablations varying one component at a time.
- **Can the policy resist misleading assistance?** Backend hallucinations are acknowledged but not systematically tested (§I). Next experiment: inject controlled factual errors and measure verification, recovery, and final-answer accuracy.
- **Does selective querying remain useful outside the selected hard subset?** Main evaluation filters on base-model failure (Table 5). Next experiment: evaluate full benchmark pools, measuring unnecessary calls alongside accuracy and cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and experimental comparisons; **medium** in the broader knowledge-bottleneck interpretation.

Material information unavailable includes statistical uncertainty, repeated-seed results, end-to-end latency, exact RL corpus size, systematic erroneous-backend evaluations, and verified availability of code, checkpoints, and curated training data.
