# Draft-KV: Learning Useful Latent Communication Between Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34754) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34754)


## One-sentence takeaway
Draft-KV makes frozen language models benefit from question-specific draft KV states, achieving 78.04% MMLU-Redux accuracy versus 37.45% receiver-only and 36.40% with reassigned messages for Qwen3-8B→Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Higher receiver accuracy does not establish that latent communication conveyed useful information: improvement might come from the trained interface itself. The authors distinguish **system gain**, \(G=A_M-A_R\), from **pairing gain**, \(P=A_M-A_D\), where Matched uses the current question’s message, Deranged uses another question’s message, and Receiver-only disables communication (§1).

Their audit finds substantial system gains with little sensitivity to message reassignment in selected C2C and LatentMAS settings. They identify this “information-use gap” as an obstacle to transferring stronger models’ capabilities (§3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Diagnostic criterion:** Compare Matched, Deranged, and Receiver-only under a fixed interface to separate overall improvement from dependence on correctly paired messages (§§1, 3; Appendix B).
- **Draft-derived communication:** A frozen sharer generates an answer attempt. Draft-position KV states are linearly projected into separate side memory, which the frozen receiver reads through gated attention using its existing query and output projections (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Progressive training:** Train the interface through message reconstruction, reference-answer alignment, and ARC answer-text supervision. Reconstruction replay preserves readability; a one-sided loss guard limits harm from cross-question mismatches (§4.3).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Test message dependence, sharer scaling at fixed interface capacity, held-out benchmarks, and randomly split evidence (§5). The interface contains approximately 1.05M parameters for the smaller-receiver configurations, but 8.39M for Qwen3-8B→Qwen3-4B (Table 6).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Audited interfaces can improve without pairing dependence | Across five C2C/LatentMAS method–benchmark pairs, pairing gain stays within 0.60 percentage points while system gain reaches 15.44 pp; aggregate point estimates | §3; Fig. 1 |
| Strong Public result | Qwen3-8B→Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, MMLU-Redux option accuracy: Draft-KV 78.04% Matched / 36.40% Deranged; receiver 37.45%; sharer 75.04%; T2T 72.05% | Table 1 |
| Broad Public improvement | Across seven model pairs × five benchmarks, Draft-KV improves over receiver-only in all 35 settings, ranks first in 30, and beats T2T in 33; mean T2T advantage is 5.85 pp | §5.2; Table 1 |
| Pairing dependence generally accompanies improvement | Across 35 Public settings, mean system gain is 24.24 pp and mean pairing gain 25.65 pp; mean mismatch harm is 1.41 pp, with a maximum of 11.65 pp | Table 3; §5.3 |
| Larger sharers yield larger gains at fixed interface size | With Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct fixed, MMLU-Redux accuracy rises from 46.11% to 78.04% as Qwen3 sharer size increases from 0.6B to 8B; each separately trained interface has 1,048,584 parameters | Tables 1, 6; Appendix E |
| Split-evidence benefits are uneven | Across 14 HotpotQA/2WikiMultihopQA settings, Draft-KV beats T2T by 6.41 pp on average, but mean gain over receiver-only is 2.51 pp; mean mismatch harm is 10.98 pp | §5.2; Table 3 |
| Draft states matter in the source ablation | Qwen3-1.7B→Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct: replacing draft KV with retrained prompt KV reduces ARC-Challenge accuracy from 83.53% to 49.83% and pairing gain from 45.90 to 0.17 pp | Tables 2, 9 |
| The guard reduces mismatch damage | Same pair, ARC-Challenge: removing the guard raises mismatch harm from 2.47 to 4.18 pp and lowers Matched accuracy from 83.53% to 82.94%; single-run ablation | Table 10; Appendix D |
| Small interface does not imply small total computation | Qwen3-8B→Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on MMLU-Redux, batch-one timing: Draft-KV takes 12,019.5 ms versus 11,907.2 ms for sharer-only, but requires 13.745 versus 6.801 TFLOPs | Table 8 |

These results support dependence on question-specific packets and transfer across the evaluated benchmarks. They do not identify exactly which semantic information the receiver extracts. Pairing gain also includes mismatch damage, so it must be interpreted alongside system gain and receiver-only accuracy (Appendix D.2). The authors interpret the T2T advantage as evidence of information beyond decoded text; **analyst inference:** differing adaptation and input paths prevent that comparison alone from isolating the cause.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Cross-question reassignment does not establish robustness to incorrect drafts for the same question (Appendix D.2).
- **Author-stated uncertainty:** Core ablations are single training runs; adapter-only comparisons are point estimates. Reported paired bootstrap intervals cover selected C2C checkpoints, rather than Draft-KV’s main results (Appendices D.1, B.3, G).
- **Author-stated control limits:** Adapter-only averaging isolates a receiver-conditioned component, not a unique parameter decomposition. The self-donor diagnostic uses a subset; prompt-KV and draft-KV ablations do not equalize length or computation (Appendices B.3, D.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Scaling requires retraining for each sharer; it does not establish an interchangeable interface. Evaluation covers selected Qwen/Llama pairs and primarily option selection or short-answer generation (Appendices C, E).
- **Analyst inference:** Randomly partitioned annotated gold evidence is a controlled collaboration setting; it does not establish performance with retrieved, noisy evidence. Private mismatch harm is substantial, and some matched configurations underperform receiver-only (Table 1; Appendix C.4).
- **Comparison boundary:** Six main-table C2C configurations are author reproductions, with reproduction checks provided. DLC is assessed only through published scaling numbers, without intervention experiments (Appendix G).

## Open research questions
- **Can the receiver reject plausible but incorrect same-question drafts?** The guard addresses unrelated questions only. Next experiment: inject controlled factual errors into otherwise matched drafts and measure correction, acceptance, and receiver-only harm.
- **Does the channel transmit reasoning beyond answer identity?** Pairing tests show specificity, while the case studies leave answer-identity effects ambiguous (Appendix H.3). Next experiment: permute option order independently and compare reasoning-only versus conclusion-only packets.
- **How much scaling benefit survives equalized message budgets?** Draft lengths vary with model size, and the weakest sharer’s length and capability remain difficult to separate (Appendix E). Next experiment: compare fixed-length drafts and matched inference budgets.
- **Can one interface generalize across sharers?** Each model pair currently receives separate training. Next experiment: train a shared bridge across compatible sharers and evaluate an unseen checkpoint without retraining.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and numerical findings; **medium** in the stronger mechanistic interpretation.

Material information not supplied includes Draft-KV multi-seed variability and main-result confidence intervals, robustness to same-question false drafts, controlled answer-identity ablations, equal-budget message comparisons, total training GPU-hours, and independent verification of the claimed released artifacts.
