# FactorEngram: Factorized N-gram Memory with Basis-Level Gating for Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35578) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35578)


## One-sentence takeaway
FactorEngram combines shared dictionary coefficients with context-dependent basis gates, reporting substantial 8K retrieval gains over reproduced Engram baselines at two backbone scales, but mixed advantages on other metrics (§§3–4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Lookup memory supplies learned representations of recurring token patterns, reducing reliance on repeated reconstruction by Transformer layers. The authors identify two weaknesses in Engram: scalar gates cannot selectively activate components of a polysemous pattern, and hashed embeddings share parameters through collisions rather than semantic similarity (§1).

The proposed gap is a joint design for component sharing and contextual selection. Semantic sharing is the authors’ motivation; direct evidence that learned components correspond to related meanings is not supplied.

## Approach and contributions

- **Factorized memory:** Direct unigram indexing and hashed bigram/trigram lookups retrieve coefficients over a dictionary shared within each insertion layer. An L1 penalty encourages sparse coefficients (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Basis-level gating:** A projected hidden-state query scores each dictionary vector. Sigmoid gates modulate individual coefficients, using the same dictionary for relevance scoring and reconstruction (§3.2).
- **Integration and controlled studies:** Reconstructed memory passes through a projection and short causal convolution before residual addition. Experiments examine unigram coverage, sparsity, gating, and insertion placement while preserving the backbone’s attention and FFN modules (§§3.3, 4.3–4.4).

The contribution is this combined representation and gating design; the paper acknowledges prior factorized memory and fine-grained gating approaches (§5).

## Evidence and main results

All numbers below are author-reported in the supplied prose; the referenced tables’ cells are absent.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better language modeling and downstream average at smaller scale | 340M backbone, 30B training tokens, 8K context: Transformer → FactorEngram WikiText perplexity **23.19 → 21.29**, LAMBADA perplexity **24.90 → 21.69**, downstream average **+1.76 percentage points** | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Large retrieval gains over Engram | Same 340M setting: NIAH-2 **+41.5 points**, NIAH-3 **+43.3 points** versus reproduced Engram | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Larger-scale gains are mixed | 1B backbone, 120B training tokens, 8K context: NIAH gains **9.3–27.6 points** over Engram; downstream average slightly below Engram and WikiText perplexity comparable to Transformer | §4.2; Table 1 |
| Factorization/gating gains persist with unigram coverage matched | 340M, 8K: Engram (+ unigram) → FactorEngram WikiText perplexity **22.32 → 21.29**, LAMBADA **24.42 → 21.69**; downstream average **+0.92 points**; NIAH-2/3 **+36.9/+29.1 points** | §4.3; Table 2 |
| Basis-level gates outperform scalar gates | 340M, 8K, factorization retained: replacing basis gates with scalar gating drops downstream average **49.82% → 48.15%** and NIAH-3 **58.3% → 9.4%** | §4.3; Table 2 |
| Moderate sparsity and placement matter | In 340M, 8K studies, λ=10⁻³ is best except WikiText perplexity; λ=10⁻² worsens every metric versus no regularization. Before-attention insertion performs best overall; layers 10 and 12 are selected | §§4.3–4.4; Tables 3–4; Figure 2 |

These comparisons support performance benefits in the tested settings, especially synthetic retrieval, and show sensitivity to gating and placement. They do not establish universal downstream superiority, semantic interpretability of dictionary bases, or improved runtime efficiency. The 340M and 1B labels describe **backbones**: their configurations additionally use 1B and 2B lookup-table parameters, respectively (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is limited to 340M and 1B backbones; larger-scale benefits remain untested (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Training uses FineWeb-Edu and evaluation is restricted to the listed benchmarks and 8K contexts, limiting demonstrated transfer across data distributions and context lengths (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Benchmark gains do not directly validate the proposed semantic sharing or polysemy mechanism; no basis interpretation or controlled word-sense experiment appears in the supplied text.
- **Evidence gaps:** Table bodies and numerical figure data are missing. Engram reproduction details, parameter/compute matching, uncertainty estimates, and NIAH variant definitions cannot be assessed fully.
- **Efficiency gap:** No latency, throughput, hardware-memory, or compute measurements are supplied, despite the efficiency motivation and large lookup tables.

## Open research questions

- **Do gains persist at larger scales?** The authors explicitly leave this open (§6). Train larger backbones with matched memory budgets and compare retrieval, perplexity, and downstream accuracy.
- **Do shared bases capture reusable semantics?** This motivates the design but lacks direct validation (§1). Test controlled polysemous contexts and inspect shared coefficients and gates across related patterns.
- **What is the performance–cost tradeoff?** Lookup parameter counts are given, but serving costs are absent (§4.1). Compare models under matched training compute and inference-memory budgets while measuring latency and throughput.
- **Does retrieval generalize beyond the tested setting?** Evaluation uses three 8K NIAH variants (§4.1). Test longer contexts and natural-document retrieval with distractors.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and prose-reported comparisons are clear, but complete result/configuration tables, exact NIAH protocols, training and reproduction details, statistical variability, resource measurements, and code/checkpoint availability are unavailable in the supplied text.
