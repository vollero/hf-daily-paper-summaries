# PyroAdapt: Adapting Wildfire Prediction under Spatial Heterogeneity and Temporal Shift

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.12435) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12435)


## One-sentence takeaway
PyroAdapt improves retrospective wildfire location ranking by adapting a pretrained model on retrieved historical examples, capturing about 344 additional neighborhood-smoothed positive cell–days at California’s fixed daily monitoring budget, while online forecasting remains untested.

## Problem and motivation
Wildfire occurrence prediction combines rare positive labels, changing environmental conditions, and spatially varying predictor–fire relationships. The authors identify a gap between historically trained risk scores and deployment-year conditions: handling class imbalance alone does not align adaptation with daily location prioritization under limited monitoring capacity (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Pretrain–retrieve–rank adaptation:** Focal-loss pretraining initializes a TabNet model; complete unlabeled target-period covariates retrieve a deduplicated union of historical nearest neighbors; historical labels then supervise ranking adaptation without target labels or focal supervision (§4.1).
- **Selective ranking and objective analysis:** A unified score-gap loss compares direct ranking’s zero target, RDPO’s frozen reference gap, and selective ranking’s nonnegative reference gap. Selective ranking targets correction of reference errors while retaining positive reference margins. The authors derive gradient-allocation results and a finite-sample avoidable-miss bound for direct and selective ranking (§4.2; Appendix A).
- **Spatial daily prioritization:** California prediction incorporates terrain, ecological embeddings, and prior-year fire rates. Same-day positive–negative pairs remove additive date offsets and focus training on differences between locations (§4.3).

These contributions combine established components into an adaptation framework; the theory does not guarantee improved performance on unseen years.

The evidence supports retrospective ranking gains under the evaluated information access and budgets. It does not establish online readiness, ignition prediction, calibrated probabilities, or equal-budget severe-event discrimination. Selective ranking’s small advantage over other ranking objectives is not uniform across seeds or larger budgets (Appendix D.6; Table 22).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated information constraint:** Adaptation sees the entire target year’s covariate distribution. Issue-time adaptation using only available inputs is unevaluated (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated evaluation scope:** California tests one year on historically observed cells. Unseen-region transfer and individual spatial-feature contributions are not isolated; reported uncertainty concerns training seeds conditional on evaluated years (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated attribution limits:** Ranking methods stop after different update counts. Retrieval is isolated only under continued focal, leaving its necessity and interaction with ranking unresolved. Yosemite’s RDPO comparison also changes candidate definitions (Appendices B.3, F).
- **Author-stated target limitations:** Smoothed positives can represent neighboring activity and repeated observations of one fire. High-DM results use unmatched alarm rates, and probability calibration is untested (Appendices D.1, F).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate improvements cannot establish equitable prioritization across regions; regional audits are needed, consistent with the authors’ sparse-support concern (Ethics Statement).

## Open research questions
- **Do gains survive online information access?** Annual-batch retrieval uses future target covariates. Test rolling adaptation windows restricted to information available at each issue date (Appendices E–F).
- **Does retrieval specifically benefit ranking adaptation?** The selection ablation uses continued focal only. Compare KNN, matched random, and full-history ranking adaptation at equal update budgets (Appendix F).
- **Can the method generalize geographically?** Current evaluations reuse historical locations. Test spatial blocks or held-out ecoregions with explicit historical-label-access and unknown-category rules (Appendix E).
- **Are high-DM gains retained at equal alarm budgets?** Existing severity diagnostics use policy-specific thresholds. Measure high-DM capture under matched daily selection budgets (Appendix F).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported protocol and numerical comparisons; external and operational validity remain unresolved.

Material information unavailable includes released code/configuration access, online evaluation, unseen-region results, regional performance audits, calibration results, spatial-feature ablations, and historically tuned retrieval/loss sensitivity studies.
