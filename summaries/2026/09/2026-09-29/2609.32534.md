# DepthBench: Measuring How Residual Connections Enable More Computational Depth

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32534) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32534)


## One-sentence takeaway
Under approximately matched parameter and token budgets, Full AttnRes—and HC at smaller scales—benefits from deeper, narrower language models, with layer diagnostics suggesting greater depth utilization but measured prefill costs increasing (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Adding Transformer layers can increase architectural depth without making later layers computationally useful. The authors argue that prior normalization and residual designs were evaluated under differing model shapes, recipes, and system settings, leaving their depth benefits confounded. DepthBench asks which architectures benefit when parameters are reallocated from width to depth under controlled training conditions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** Compare ten existing architectures on a shared LLaMA-like backbone, varying width–depth aspect ratio under approximately fixed total parameters. Additional suites control backbone parameters and test multiple model scales; training uses FineWeb-Edu and architecture-specific learning-rate selection (§2, Tables 1–4).
- **Comparative findings:** Establish contrasting shape preferences: Full AttnRes and smaller-scale HC favor deeper models, while Pre-LN and most other variants show weaker or unfavorable trends (§3).
- **Mechanistic diagnostics:** Combine representation distances, layer skipping, layer swapping, residual-path analysis, pruning, and LogitLens. The authors adapt perturbation-score aggregation to emphasize later layers and investigate possible depth bottlenecks in Block AttnRes and mHC (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Reusable assets and cost evaluation:** Report releasing code and checkpoints across model shapes, alongside measured prefill efficiency comparisons (§4.4). The contribution is a benchmark and analysis, rather than a new residual architecture.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Depth hurts Pre-LN but helps HC and Full AttnRes in the main comparison | Approximately 400M models trained on 8B tokens: moving from 16 to 32 layers increases Pre-LN validation loss **2.759 → 2.782**, while Full AttnRes improves **2.751 → 2.718** and HC **2.729 → 2.699**. | §3, Fig. 2; training budget §2.3 |
| Favorable trends survive backbone control | HC and Full AttnRes continue improving toward extreme deep–narrow shapes under both total-parameter and backbone-parameter matching, including cases where total size decreases. | §3, Fig. 2b–c |
| Larger-scale support is stronger for Full AttnRes | Full AttnRes’s favorable trend persists across the smaller-scale suite and at approximately 1.6B parameters. HC’s shallowest 500M configuration diverges, and its 1.6B results lack clear improvement with depth. | §3, Fig. 3 |
| Domain prediction generally improves | Deeper HC and Full AttnRes models generally lower average teacher-forced NLL on coding, STEM, and math task pairs; the authors describe STEM and math trends as especially consistent. | §3, Fig. 4 |
| Later layers show stronger perturbation effects | For 32-layer models, approximately **1% / 16% / 7%** of late-layer causal scores exceed **0.45** for Pre-LN / Full AttnRes / HC; approximately **8% / 46% / 35%** of permutation scores exceed **0.15**. Aggregation covers the last three quarters of the network. | §4.1, Fig. 6, Eqs. 5–8 |
| Derived variants exhibit possible bottlenecks | Across main-sweep aspect ratios, composed residual maps have effective rank **1.44–1.65** for mHC versus **2.53–2.81** for HC. Under the fixed-boundary Block AttnRes schedule, the authors identify an initial ineffective segment of **8 layers in the 32-layer model**. | §4.3, Figs. 9–10 |
| Deeper shapes increase measured prefill cost | At approximately 400M parameters, sequence length **4096**, BF16, on A100-SXM4-40GB: reducing aspect ratio **76.0 → 9.1** raises reported prefill FLOPs roughly **3.0 → 4.4 TFLOPs per sequence** and KV cache by **more than 2×**. Peak-memory and GPU-hour measurements use batch size **8**. | §4.4, Fig. 11 |

The evidence supports architecture-dependent width–depth preferences and associates favorable scaling with stronger cross-layer dependence and order sensitivity. It does not establish a universal causal mechanism, equal-compute superiority, or improved generated-answer accuracy: domain evaluation primarily measures teacher-forced NLL. Single-layer pruning also shows that HC’s later layers need not be individually indispensable (§4.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported:** HC becomes less stable as scale increases and may need finer learning-rate tuning. Forward-cost measurements do not directly establish training wall-clock costs (§§3, 4.4).
- **Author-reported:** Representation diagnostics depend on learning rate. Figure 5 uses a common rate that is suboptimal for LNS and excludes DeepNorm because it explodes at that rate (Fig. 5; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—budget fairness:** Parameter matching is approximate, and architecture overhead is uneven: at the reference shape, MoDA adds 48.2M parameters and mHC adds 4.72M relative to Pre-LN. Fixed parameters and tokens also do not imply fixed FLOPs (§4.4, Table 6).
- **Analyst inference—tuning fairness:** Block AttnRes inherits Full AttnRes’s selected learning rate; mHC’s learning-rate sweep uses a different initialization from the main results (Fig. 12).
- **Analyst inference—scope and mechanism:** Evaluation uses one pretraining corpus and backbone family. Block AttnRes’s diagnosis concerns its chosen boundary schedule; mHC’s rank findings suggest, but do not experimentally isolate, the cause of weaker scaling (§2, §4.3).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Main performance comparisons lack reported multi-seed uncertainty; document-bootstrap intervals in Figure 10 quantify transport-statistic uncertainty rather than training variability.

## Open research questions
- **Does deeper Full AttnRes remain preferable under equal compute or elapsed time?** Parameter matching leaves efficiency differences unresolved (§4.4). Next experiment: compare shapes under matched training FLOPs and GPU-hours.
- **Can tuned HC recover consistent larger-scale depth gains?** Instability and ambiguous larger-scale trends remain (§3). Next experiment: repeat larger-scale shape sweeps with per-shape learning-rate tuning and multiple seeds.
- **Can efficient variants preserve useful cross-depth access?** The proposed Block AttnRes and mHC mechanisms remain incompletely isolated (§4.3). Next experiment: independently vary block schedules and residual constraints while measuring loss, rank, and perturbation sensitivity.
- **Do NLL gains improve generated task solutions?** Figure 4 evaluates predictive likelihood rather than solution success. Next experiment: evaluate generated code and math answers from the same checkpoints using explicit correctness metrics.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main smaller-scale comparisons and their qualifications, but broader computational-depth claims remain inferential.
