# Why Deterministic PRM Guidance Underperforms in Discrete Diffusion Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35472) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35472)


## One-sentence takeaway
On Dream-7B math and code benchmarks, independent sampling with a task-trained outcome verifier beats deterministic intermediate-state PRM guidance at approximately matched inference compute, with diagnostics implicating both premature pruning and weak final selection (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Discrete diffusion language models expose partially masked solutions during generation, creating opportunities to guide reasoning before completion. However, these scattered token states differ from the ordered prefixes used by autoregressive PRMs. The authors ask whether intermediate scoring actually improves accuracy once its inference cost is included, and whether failures arise from damaged candidate pools or poor final selection (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Matched-compute evaluation:** Charge denoising and reward scoring in candidate-level forward passes. Compare independent sampling, majority voting, outcome reward model (ORM) reranking, and segmental guidance that branches into candidates and retains the PRM’s top choice (§3).
- **Failure decomposition:** Use mask-bucket ROC-AUC, answer diversity, oracle pool accuracy, shared-pool reranking, and a sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) control to separate pruning damage from terminal scoring failures (§§4–5.3).
- **Scorer diagnostics:** Compare bidirectional and causal scorers, change causal pooling, retrain on final states, and test fresh-rollout supervision. Here, “PRM” means an intermediate-state value model supervised by eventual correctness, rather than annotated reasoning steps (§§3.1, 5.1, 5.4).
- **Reported artifacts:** Release a scored snapshot corpus, PRM adapters/checkpoints, and training/evaluation tools (App. G). The paper is primarily diagnostic; its contribution is the controlled comparison and failure analysis.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| ORM reranking wins at headline budgets | Dream-7B GSM8K accuracy: ORM **75.13%** versus guided PRM **65.18%** at N=K=8; **82.71% versus 70.02%** at 32. Guided/ORM costs are 1,040/1,032 and 4,160/4,128 forward passes. | Table 1; §3.2 |
| Ordering extends to task-trained math and code verifiers | MATH500, N=K=8: ORM **30.65%**, guided PRM **20.80%**. On 257 held-out MBPP tasks: **63.04% versus 50.88%**; finished-program PRM reranking reaches **65.47%**, with its difference from ORM including zero. | Table 2; Apps. B.2, C.2 |
| Early states provide weaker discrimination | GSM8K bidirectional PRM ROC-AUC declines from **0.77** in the least-masked bucket to **0.54** in the most-masked bucket. Fresh-rollout labeling does not eliminate the decay. | §5.1; Fig. 3(a); App. B.3 |
| Pruning damages the reachable pool | GSM8K, K=N=8: guided hybrid pool Oracle@8 **67.30%**, versus **81.05%** for independent samples; unique answers average **1.75 versus 4.31** per problem. | Table 3; §5.2 |
| Restoring diversity leaves a selection bottleneck | SMC at 1,040 passes restores Oracle@8 to **77.89%**, but cross-mask PRM selection achieves **65.48%** with weighted answer voting or **66.34%** with the top particle. | §5.3; App. B.1 |
| Final-state training closes the shared-pool scoring gap | Same GSM8K candidates, N=8: final-state PRM **75.40%**, ORM **75.13%**, cross-mask PRM **42.84%**. At N=32: **82.79%, 82.71%, 65.35%**, respectively. Specialist–ORM difference intervals include zero. | Table 2; Apps. B.2, C.2 |
| Causal readout explains much of its deficit | On 42,208 GSM8K final trajectories, causal ROC-AUC increases from **0.6147** with mean pooling to **0.7274** with last-token pooling; bidirectional mean pooling reaches **0.7759**. | App. C.3 |
| Advantage survives a wall-clock control | GSM8K ORM@6 achieves **72.40%** in approximately **126 seconds/problem**, versus guided PRM@8 at **65.18%** in approximately **156 seconds/problem**. | §4; App. A.4 |

These results support the authors’ decomposition within the tested settings: guidance loses correct candidates, while cross-mask training can also weaken final ranking. MBPP particularly supports a guidance failure because its PRM remains competitive when judging completed programs. The evidence does not establish that all PRM-guided diffusion samplers underperform. The theoretical results explain possible failure mechanisms; they do not prove that the empirical scorer reaches an information ceiling or uniquely identify the architectural cause (App. F.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
**Author-stated:** The central comparison focuses on Dream-7B, math/code tasks, and fixed-interval deterministic pruning. Only one SMC control is evaluated; adaptive guidance, lookahead, diversity kernels, and hybrid terminal selection remain untested. LLaDA confirms the bidirectional–causal comparison, but lacks a task-specific ORM comparison. Sampler invariance, step-level supervision, and alternative readouts remain unresolved (App. E.1).

The ORM advantage depends on task-specific training. The GSM8K-trained ORM fails to transfer to MATH500; that transfer experiment also uses different samplers and explicitly cannot establish PRM-versus-ORM ordering (App. D.3).

**Analyst inference:** Final-state specialization is not isolated from every training change: the specialist control also removes the step embedding and uses the ORM protocol. Its parity supports that combined recipe, rather than attributing the entire gain solely to training-state selection (Apps. B.2, C.5). Headline confidence intervals compare individual guided runs, whereas headline point estimates average runs; those quantities should not be conflated (App. C.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can preserved diversity plus a final-state verifier beat independent reranking?** SMC repairs much of the pool, but ORM selection on that pool is unreported. Next experiment: compare SMC/top-M plus ORM against independent ORM reranking while charging terminal scoring.
- **Can uncertainty-aware or adaptive guidance avoid harmful early cuts?** Fixed schedules and limited retention controls leave this unresolved. Next experiment: compare calibrated resampling and mask-dependent checkpoints at equal compute (App. E.1).
- **What causes the residual bidirectional advantage?** Readout changes narrow it, while context controls do not uniquely distinguish remaining hypotheses. Next experiment: test appended aggregation tokens and a permutation-trained scorer under matched training (Apps. E.2–E.4).
- **Does the main ranking generalize across backbones and samplers?** LLaDA lacks the ORM comparison, and sampler sensitivity is untested. Next experiment: train LLaDA-specific verifiers and repeat the compute-matched comparison across sampling settings (App. E.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the reported comparisons; **medium** for broader causal and generalization claims.

Material omissions include exact SMC tempering/ESS settings in the supplied prose, detailed MBPP verifier-training splits and grading configuration, ORM results on guided/SMC pools, and a LLaDA-specific ORM comparison. Artifact availability and executability are reported by the authors but not independently verified here.
