# Beyond Teacher Assignment: Domain-Normalized Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35347) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35347)


## One-sentence takeaway
Rescaling each teacher’s token-level feedback by its domain’s measured dispersion improves matched multi-teacher distillation across three Qwen3.5 sizes, primarily through mathematics gains, although well-chosen fixed weights often perform comparably.

## Problem and motivation
Domain specialists can teach a shared language model through on-policy distillation: the student generates responses, and the specialist matching each prompt scores its tokens. The authors identify a gap between **selecting the appropriate teacher** and **controlling its influence on the update**.

In their Qwen3.5 experiments, label routing fails to outperform the strongest single-teacher student and transfers little mathematics improvement. Instruction-following (IF) feedback has substantially greater dispersion and dominates an initial-student gradient probe, motivating explicit feedback calibration (§§1–3; Appendix D.5).

## Approach and contributions
- **Domain normalization:** For each batch, compute population standard deviations of teacher-minus-student rollout log-probabilities within each domain and across all valid response tokens. Apply \(w_d=\mathrm{clip}(\sigma_{\mathrm{all}}/\sigma_d,0.25,4)\) to that domain’s actor-side distillation advantages. Degenerate statistics receive weight one (§2; Appendix D.1).
- **Minimal intervention:** Preserve teacher routing, prompt allocation, advantage signs, response-averaged loss, and the clipped OPD objective. Statistics reuse existing scores, requiring no additional teacher forward pass. Equal weights recover label-routed MOPD (§2).
- **Diagnosis and controls:** Matched experiments separate routing from feedback weighting. Fixed-weight controls suggest that attenuating IF contributes more than amplifying mathematics alone; continuous re-estimation is not consistently necessary (Appendices C.6, D.5).

The rule equalizes unclipped rollout-signal dispersion; it does **not** equalize gradient norms or fix the overall loss scale (§2).

## Evidence and main results
“Total” equally averages six benchmark accuracies; sampled-answer averages estimate single-answer accuracy, not pass@N. Unless specified otherwise, results use 80 student updates and seed 42. Size-ordered results below are **9B / 4B / 2B**; differences are percentage points (pp).

The evidence strongly supports improving the tested label-routed recipe. It does not establish universal superiority over capability-integration methods, or that batchwise adaptation itself is essential. Gradient diagnostics support the authors’ mechanism interpretation but are descriptive rather than a complete causal account.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** One expert pool per size; student-seed replication does not cover retraining specialists. The earlier Qwen3 result shows no clear benefit (§5).
- **Unresolved design choices:** Clipping bounds, domain proportions, and scale estimators are not separately ablated. IF frequently reaches the lower bound, preventing full dispersion equalization (Appendix D.4).
- **Statistical and reporting limits:** Most intervals condition on a particular expert pool and student seed. The main-text 16K cap was selected after inspecting both caps; both are disclosed (Appendices A.3–A.4).
- **Mechanism limits:** Gradient probes omit optimizer state and policy clipping effects. Shorter answers accompany gains, but their causal role is unresolved (Appendices C.4, D.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Because SFT and merging use different budgets, their higher scores do not establish comparative compute efficiency. No measured latency or training-cost advantage is established.

## Open research questions
1. **When is adaptive weighting necessary?** Fixed calibration matches DN-MOPD in several settings. Compare batchwise, first-batch, and globally fixed weights across independently retrained expert pools and changing domain mixtures.
2. **Which normalization design is robust?** Bounds and statistics remain unablated. Vary clipping thresholds and dispersion estimators while measuring accuracy, stability, and domain-gradient contributions.
3. **Does the gain persist under matched compute and convergence?** Longer training preserves the lead over Label, but convergence and cross-recipe efficiency remain unresolved. Compare OPD, SFT, and merging under explicit compute budgets and longer training schedules.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported matched-comparison findings; **medium** in the proposed mechanism and broader applicability.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes variation across retrained expert pools, controlled cross-family replication, normalization-design ablations, convergence behavior, and matched-compute comparisons. Authors report released code and records, but their contents are not supplied here; teacher and student checkpoints are described as forthcoming.
