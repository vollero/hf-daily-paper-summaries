# Fewer Tokens, More Self-Teaching: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Extreme Visual Token Reduction

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32353) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32353)


## One-sentence takeaway
LT-OPD trains a compressed multimodal model on its own generated prefixes using a full-token self-teacher, raising Qwen3.5-4B’s average retained benchmark performance from 68.6% to 82.3% at 5% visual-token retention, with modest measured latency improvement (Tables 2 and 5).

## Problem and motivation
Extreme visual-token pruning removes useful image evidence and changes the response prefixes a model encounters during inference. The authors argue that training on fixed responses misses these altered generation states, limiting adaptation under severe compression (§§1–2, Figure 1).

The practical motivation is reducing multimodal inference costs under resource constraints. The identified gap concerns how a compressed model learns after pruning, beyond choosing which tokens to retain.

## Approach and contributions
- **On-policy self-distillation:** A low-token student generates responses; a frozen, full-token copy of its pretrained initialization supplies next-token distributions on those same student-generated prefixes. Training minimizes Jensen–Shannon divergence, without differentiating through sampling (§3.2).
- **Budget curriculum:** Training starts with more visual tokens, progressively reduces retention, then holds the target budget to adapt directly to extreme compression (§3.3, Appendix H).
- **Training mixture and evaluation:** The authors construct LT-14K from existing single-image question sources and evaluate adaptation across model sizes, three model families, and multiple task categories (§3.4, Tables 2–4, Appendix A).

The contribution is applying existing on-policy distillation principles to extreme visual-token reduction with a budget curriculum. CDPruner supplies token selection; it is not introduced by this paper. Implementation updates the student’s language model, vision encoder, and native merger, using truncated vocabulary support plus an aggregated probability tail (Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Main recovery | Qwen3.5-4B at 5% retention: average retain ratio rises from CDPruner’s 68.6% to 82.3%; DivPrune and curriculum + fixed-prefix JSD both reach 77.6%, EPIC 73.7%, and DAPO 78.2%. | Tables 2 and 6 |
| On-policy training contributes most of the observed gain | At 5% evaluation retention, OPD without curriculum reaches 81.5%, versus 82.3% with curriculum; curriculum + fixed-prefix JSD reaches 77.6%. | Table 2 |
| Target-budget training matters | Removing the final 5% holding stage reduces average retain ratio from 82.3% to 79.5%, under the same 175-update budget and initialization seed. | Appendix H, Table 13 |
| Gains extend across tested backbones | At 5% retention: Qwen3.5-9B improves from 71.1% to 81.4%; GLM-4.6V-9B from 73.8% to 86.3%; LLaVA-OV-1.5-4B from 71.8% to 84.1%. Each ratio uses its own full-token reference. | Tables 3–4 |
| Resource reductions exceed latency gains | Qwen3.5-4B: reported KV cache falls from 26.59 to 3.93 MB; prefill FLOPs from 6.002 to 0.878 T; latency from 1331.510 to 1293.691 ms/sample, a 1.029× speedup. Measurements use a selected TextVQA subset and shared 128-token reference replay. | Table 5, Appendix E |
| JSD is not the best aggregate objective | At 5% retention, reverse KL reaches 82.8% average retain ratio, versus 82.3% for JSD and forward KL; JSD scores higher on the two perception-heavy benchmarks. | Appendix G, Table 12 |
| Limited multi-image/video transfer | At 5% retention, LT-OPD scores 52.0 on MuirBench and 55.6 on MVBench, versus EPIC’s 44.0 and 52.2; their two-benchmark average retain ratios are 78.3% and 69.8%. MVBench uses uniformly sampled frames. | Appendix I, Table 14 |

These results support substantial recovery of benchmark performance after extreme pruning. “Average retain ratio” is an equally weighted mean of benchmark scores normalized by their full-token references, not pooled accuracy (§4.1). It does not establish full capability recovery or large real-world latency gains.

Two narrative claims require qualification: Table 4 leaves LLaVA’s V*Bench score unchanged despite wording suggesting consistent improvements, and Table 14 does not support §4.7’s claim of over 12 percentage points improvement over EPIC.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-described difficulty:** Extremely compressed students can initially generate degraded trajectories; the curriculum is intended to stabilize learning (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Training uses single-image questions, and transfer evidence covers only two additional multi-image/video benchmarks. Broader video generalization remains unestablished (Appendices A and I).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency validity:** Latency uses selected long-answer TextVQA examples and fixed, ground-truth-conditioned decoding replay. It does not measure unrestricted generation with each model’s own response lengths. Appendix E also defines KV storage specifically for full-attention layers.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** No repeated training seeds or confidence intervals are reported. The small aggregate curriculum gain therefore lacks an estimate of run-to-run variability.
- **Analyst inference—comparison completeness:** The supplied text does not specify RL baseline rewards or detailed configurations, alternative teacher interpolation coefficients, or a controlled comparison of token selectors within LT-OPD.
- **Analyst inference—mechanism:** Fixed-prefix ablations support the proposed training choice, but do not directly quantify inference-prefix distribution mismatch or isolate it as the cause of recovery.

## Open research questions
- **How robust is the curriculum benefit?** Its reported gain lacks repeated-run uncertainty. Repeat fixed-budget and curriculum training across seeds with matched compute.
- **Which distillation objective best fits each task?** Reverse KL leads the aggregate while JSD favors perception-heavy benchmarks. Compare objectives across backbones and task mixtures under matched training settings.
- **Does recovery depend on the token selector or target budget?** LT-OPD primarily uses CDPruner and evaluates the extreme target setting. Cross multiple selectors with multiple target budgets.
- **What speedup occurs during natural generation?** Efficiency evidence uses fixed-reference replay. Measure free-generation latency, response length, quality, and memory across batch sizes and image resolutions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, evaluation protocols, and substantial comparative evidence, but some prose overstates its tables.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, detailed RL baseline configurations, teacher-variant coefficients, the procedure supporting the authors’ no-leakage claim, and verified availability of code, LT-14K, or trained checkpoints.
