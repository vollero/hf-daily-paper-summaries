# Self-Play Search Distillation for Large Language Model Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30936) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30936)


## One-sentence takeaway
SPSD converts executable board-game search into offline LLM supervision, improving Qwen3-4B-Base’s reported mathematics mean from 24.1% to 36.6% and held-out-game win rate from 15% to 45%, although transfer varies by backbone and training details are inconsistent (Table 1; §G.1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors seek reasoning supervision that exposes alternatives and consequences beyond a student LLM’s current capabilities. Human process labels are costly, while student-generated supervision inherits student errors. Executable games offer independently replayable transitions and outcomes, but ordinary action logs omit the comparisons underlying decisions (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Offline search-to-language framework:** Separately trained EfficientZero experts generate self-play decision records containing selected actions, visit rankings, values, and retained branches. Replay checks state consistency and narrated transitions before materialization (§§4–4.1).
- **Grounded supervision interface:** Records become move-choice traces plus six state-question tasks. Main-corpus traces retain at most one alternative and one opponent reply; numerical telemetry selects evidence but does not appear in the trace. Decision states are separated across row families (§4.2; Appendices A–B).
- **Post-training and transfer study:** The authors compare SFT, privileged-trace on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), and heuristic RuleBot-Distill, then evaluate unseen games, rule variation, and mathematics. Search information remains hidden at inference (§§4.3–5).

The contribution is this replay-grounded offline interface and its evaluation; EfficientZero and OPSD are adopted components.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest main improvement occurs on Qwen3-4B-Base | Baseline → OPSD: six-suite mathematics mean **24.1% → 36.6%**; held-out-game win **15% → 45%**, reported FIDE **15% → 39.9%**, legality **65% → 75.1%** across 15 held-out games. Mathematics combines pass@1 on three suites and average accuracy over 32 responses on three competition suites. | Table 1; §5 |
| Search supervision exceeds the heuristic control on that backbone | RuleBot-Distill → OPSD: mathematics mean **33.9% → 36.6%** and held-out-game win **35% → 45%**, using the same student-facing interface and OPSD procedure. | Table 1; §§4.3, 6 |
| Transfer depends on model condition | Thinking-enabled Qwen3-8B: mathematics mean **74.9% → 75.0%**, win **53.7% → 62.7%**. Llama-3.1-8B: mathematics mean **19.5% → 19.2%**, win **45% → 50%**, matching RuleBot’s game result. | Table 1; §6 |
| Rule variation improves reported transfer | At 1,000 steps, variant versus base curriculum on Qwen3-4B-Base: FIDE **42.4% versus 39.9%**, legality **77.3% versus 75.1%**, mathematics mean **39.2% versus 36.6%** under the stated fixed optimization budget. | Figure 3; §6 |
| Action agreement supplies behavioral evidence | Wins concentrate where selected-action values and agreement with fresh 50-simulation MCTS are high; SPSD shows stronger coupling than Gemini 3.1 Pro. | Figure 4; §6 |
| Objective choice materially changes outcomes | In a separate evaluation configuration, fixed-teacher generalized-JSD β=0.25 reaches **31.9%** mathematics mean, whereas forward KL alone reaches **4.0%**; these values are explicitly not comparable directly with Table 1. | Tables 9–10; §G.1 |

These results support conditional transfer from game supervision to the reported mathematics benchmarks. Search agreement is correlational evidence of decision alignment; it does not establish that the LLM reproduces MCTS internally or isolate which supervision component causes transfer.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author qualification:** Gains depend on backbone; legality and longer reasoning alone are insufficient (§6). No dedicated limitations section appears.
- **Analyst observation—objective inconsistency:** §4.3 and Table 4 specify forward KL, but Tables 9–10 identify generalized-JSD β=0.25 as the main configuration and report collapse with forward KL alone.
- **Analyst observation—metric ambiguity:** Some Table 1 FIDE scores are below corresponding win rates. That is unexplained under §5’s stated loss/draw/win scoring if both metrics aggregate the same episodes.
- **Analyst observation—causal isolation:** RuleBot and SPSD differ in trajectory source, action quality, and justification. The supplied results do not separately ablate alternatives, replies, state questions, or value-based evidence gates.
- **Analyst observation—verification scope:** Replay verifies transitions and terminal claims, not expert optimality. The supplied text provides no direct human comparison establishing that these particular experts are superhuman.
- **Analyst observation—reproducibility:** Table 8 gives different trace depth and alternative limits from the main corpus, without corresponding variant results resolving their use. Repeated-seed uncertainty, game opponent details, and artifact release locations are unavailable.

## Open research questions
- **Which components drive mathematics transfer?** Their effects are bundled. Run matched-corpus ablations removing alternatives, replies, and state questions individually.
- **Does stronger search improve supervision?** The main corpus uses a fixed search budget without reported budget comparisons. Vary simulations while holding states, corpus size, and student training fixed.
- **How robust are gains across runs and backbones?** Reported transfer is heterogeneous and lacks repeated-run uncertainty. Repeat matched conditions across seeds and model families.
- **How much does environment verification depend on implementation fidelity?** Appendix E describes model-generated environments with cross-validation. Compare transitions and outcomes against an independently implemented rules reference.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central reported gains and corpus construction are clear, but objective and metric inconsistencies weaken interpretation. Missing material information includes reconciled training settings, game opponent and aggregation details, repeated-run uncertainty, isolated component ablations, direct expert-strength validation, and code/data/model release availability.
