# Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35279) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35279)


## One-sentence takeaway
An audit of homogeneous LLM debate shows why interventions must count both harmful collapses prevented and useful corrections lost: a held-out probe-gated freeze saved 29 collapses but discarded 108 corrections across 6,525 debates (Table 7).

## Problem and motivation
Final accuracy hides two opposing effects of debate: an initially correct majority can become wrong, while an initially wrong majority can recover. Generic answer-change rates also omit this direction. The authors identify a reporting gap: evaluations need transition counts, conditional denominators, onset timing, and explicit intervention utility to distinguish productive revision from harmful convergence (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Transition-accounting protocol:** Three same-model agents answer independently, then debate for three rounds. Rule-based coding assigns preserved, collapse, correction, or unrepaired outcomes and records the first correct-to-wrong majority switch (§3.1).
- **Signed intervention replay:** A freeze returns the saved initial majority. Its utility is weighted collapses prevented minus corrections lost; tuning and evaluation are separated through leave-one-model-out splits (§3.3).
- **Behavioral screen and empirical audit:** Eight probes cross four argument strengths with two social-pressure conditions. Total answer-change rate, αtot, serves as model-family triage; round traces diagnose individual trajectories. The authors explicitly do not claim a new deployable controller (§§3–6).
- **Reusable reporting assets:** The authors describe schemas, parsers, provenance and cost audits, release flags, and zero-API rebuild scripts, with some prompts, transcripts, and matrices gated (Table 1; §G.6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Collapse often begins early | Across 6,925 MMLU-Pro debates, 253 collapses: 149 began in Round 1 (58.9%), 47 in Round 2, and 57 in Round 3. | §5.1; Table 6 |
| Probe screen ranks family risk, with limited capability-adjusted evidence | Across seven family means from 14 model rows, αtot versus conditional collapse: Spearman ρ=0.893, exact two-sided p=0.0123. Capability pressure: ρ=0.821; screen partial after initial accuracy: ρ=0.767, p=0.0877. | §§4.2–4.3; Tables 3–4, 23 |
| Round 1 features improve diagnostic discrimination | Pooled out-of-fold AUC on 6,925 debates rises from 0.669 to 0.768; lift 0.099, debate-index bootstrap 95% CI [0.080, 0.118]. | §5.1; §E.1 |
| Probe-gated freeze harms equal-weight utility | Frozen leave-one-model-out replay on 6,525 local-model debates prevents 29/251 collapses but loses 108/804 corrections: net −79, accuracy delta −1.21 percentage points. | Tables 7, 37 |
| Simple Round 1 gates also lose more corrections than they save | On a separate 1,255-trace cohort, majority-change freeze prevents 56 collapses and loses 69 corrections, net −13; learned leave-one-model-out stump prevents 2 and loses 31, net −29. | Table 7; §E.7 |
| Cross-benchmark accounting exposes hidden turnover | On 198 GPQA Diamond questions, Mistral Small 4 records 20/120 collapses and 21/78 corrections, net +1; excluding four invalid initial-majority cases changes net to −1. | Table 30; §G.3 |
| Private revision comparison depends materially on tie scoring | In twelve post-hoc MCQ settings with shared initial answers and 2,000 questions each, private revision trails peer debate by 1.44–4.81 percentage points under binary scoring; expected tie scoring narrows differences to −1.62 through +0.38 points. | §G.5; Table 43 |

The evidence supports separating diagnosis from control and exposing opposing transitions behind final accuracy. It does not establish causal mechanisms, calibrated individual-question prediction, broad screen transfer, or debate superiority over compute-matched solo reasoning. Replay cohorts differ, so their policy rows are not matched comparisons (§§4.4, 6–7).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope:** The primary study covers homogeneous, closed-book MCQ debate. Mixed panels, numeric-answer tasks, and additional benchmarks are post-hoc or feasibility checks; tool use, retrieval, asymmetric roles, and unrestricted generation remain untested (§7; §G.3).
- **Small, revised inferential cohort:** The preregistered 18-model target became 14 rows across seven families, including six Qwen variants. Family aggregation addresses dependence, but the capability-adjusted family result remains non-confirmatory (§A.3.1; §C.3).
- **Parsing and aggregation sensitivity:** Alphabetic tie-breaking affects some outcomes. Removing the last-letter fallback changes transition membership in 51/966 strictly labeled open-trace rows; many other rows lack strict labels (§A.3.3).
- **Diagnostic validity:** The AUC baseline includes gold-dependent initial correctness. Its reported intervals resample debate indices rather than the preregistered question/model clusters; clustered recomputation is unavailable from the open tier (§E.1). **Analyst inference:** these results should not be read directly as deployable online performance.
- **Reproducibility boundaries:** The probe-gated replay is released only as an aggregate; exact Round 1 matrices are gated. Several probe records omit temperature, reply-temperature robustness is unresolved, and provider reruns may drift (§§G.2, G.6).
- **Solo comparison remains incomplete:** Private revision retains debate framing and call count; longer single-agent reasoning budgets were not tested (§G.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does probing add useful selection value beyond capability and disagreement?** The adjusted family result is inconclusive. Next experiment: prospectively evaluate more independent families on common question pools, comparing trace-allocation decisions and costs (§§4.3, G.1).
- **Can a controller preserve corrections while preventing collapses?** Tested freeze gates have negative equal-weight utility. Next experiment: compare correction-aware gates and cheap disagreement baselines on identical held-out rows with prespecified weights (§§6, E.9).
- **How does peer debate compare with compute-matched solo reasoning?** The follow-up does not test longer solo budgets. Next experiment: branch from shared initial answers and match total token budgets across peer debate, private revision, and extended solo reasoning (§G.5).
- **How robust are findings to coding choices?** Parser and tie sensitivities materially affect some rows. Next experiment: recode the complete cohort under multiple parsers and tie rules, reporting transition and policy-utility stability (§A.3.3; §G.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the paper’s central accounting lesson; **medium** in the screen’s practical triage value.
