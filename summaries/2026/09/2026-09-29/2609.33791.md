# Do We Really Need KL Divergence for On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33791) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33791)


## One-sentence takeaway
On the tested math and code tasks, binary teacher-directed token rewards achieve results close to reverse-KL on-policy distillation, while disagreement-aware supervision from two teachers improves over domain-routed multi-teacher distillation (Tables 2–5).

## Problem and motivation
On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its own generated sequences using teacher supervision. The authors question whether its standard reverse-KL reward needs the magnitude of teacher–student disagreement, or whether the direction alone suffices. They also address potential capability conflicts when multi-teacher OPD routes each sample to only one domain-specific teacher (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions

- **BinaryOPD:** Replace the sampled-token log-probability ratio with +1 when the teacher assigns higher probability, −1 when it assigns lower probability, and zero at equality; optimize with a token-level policy-gradient objective (§4.1).
- **Disagreement interventions:** Partition tokens by teacher–student log-ratio and selectively reward or reverse their directions. These experiments identify high-disagreement tokens as especially consequential in the tested settings (§5).
- **C-MOPD:** Consult every teacher for each token. Follow unanimous directions; otherwise follow the domain teacher only when opposing teachers’ disagreement stays below a threshold, assigning zero reward for stronger conflicts (§6.1).
- **Supporting investigations:** Teacher-representation similarity motivates a repeated-feedback explanation, and correctness-based masking tests whether directional rewards must align with verifier feedback (§4.3; Appendices A–B).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| BinaryOPD closely matches reverse-KL OPD | Across five math pairs, six-benchmark Avg@8 scores are close; Qwen3-4B-Non-Thinking reaches 65.1 versus 64.1, while Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct reaches 17.9 versus 18.2. | Table 2 |
| Code results are also close, slightly lower in aggregate | Three-benchmark Avg@8: BinaryOPD versus OPD is 55.2 versus 55.4 for Qwen3-4B, and 50.6 versus 51.0 for DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. | Table 3 |
| Sparse positive-direction supervision can preserve performance | For the Qwen3-4B math pair at ε=0.8, rewarding only teacher-favored high-disagreement tokens retains less than 1.5% of tokens and achieves six-benchmark Avg@8 of 64.5%, versus OPD’s 64.1%. Rewarding every token positively yields 40.3%. | §5.2; Table 4 |
| Direction errors on high-disagreement groups disrupt training | Selectively adding the opposing high-disagreement group causes failure in positive and negative reward experiments; low-disagreement tokens can receive opposing directions with some degradation. | §5.2; Figures 2–3, 9–10 |
| C-MOPD improves over routed MOPD | With a Qwen3-4B student and math/code teachers, ε=0.8 improves every listed benchmark: LiveCodeBench v6 Avg@8 rises from 27.9% to 32.9%, and AIME25 from 52.5% to 53.9%. | Table 5 |
| Strict consensus suppresses updates | C-MOPD at ε=0.0 eventually assigns zero reward to over 90% of tokens and delivers results close to MOPD. | §6.2.2; Figure 4 |
| Verifier-aligned masking has no consistent advantage | For DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/JustRL math, six-benchmark Avg@8 is 55.1 for Verifier-Conflicted versus 54.4 for Verifier-Aligned; Qwen3-4B code averages are both 55.0. | Appendix B; Tables 7–8 |
| Representation similarity supports the feedback hypothesis | Among 61,440 states collected during Qwen3-4B math OPD, 18.5% have a different-prompt nearest neighbor with teacher-hidden-state cosine similarity ≥0.99. | Appendix A; Table 6 |

These results support replacing reverse-KL reward magnitudes in the evaluated settings. They do not establish universal equivalence, statistical superiority, or that representation similarity causally explains BinaryOPD’s success. The verifier experiments preserve teacher-directed updates; they do not test arbitrary reinforcement of incorrect answers.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors provide no dedicated limitations section. They explicitly qualify Appendix A: stable geometry in a fixed teacher representation does not imply an unchanged exact state distribution.

**Analyst inferences from the reported scope:**

- Evaluation covers math and code; C-MOPD uses one student and two teachers (§6.2.1).
- Tables report sampling averages without training-seed variability, confidence intervals, or significance tests.
- C-MOPD changes both teacher coverage and reward formulation relative to MOPD; no ablation isolates their contributions (§6).
- Claims that updates avoid harming other capabilities exceed the direct evidence: the paper evaluates benchmarks rather than proving capability preservation.
- Sparse rewarded tokens do not establish computational savings; teacher inference costs and wall-clock comparisons are absent.
- State-space redundancy is observational evidence from one math setting, not a causal test of repeated-feedback necessity (Appendix A).

## Open research questions

- **Does BinaryOPD depend on repeated state visitation?** Appendix A provides similarity evidence only. Vary prompt diversity and revisit frequency while comparing binary and log-ratio rewards.
- **Which component drives C-MOPD’s gains?** Teacher coverage and reward design change together. Compare routed binary rewards, all-teacher magnitude rewards, and consensus masking under matched budgets.
- **How broadly does disagreement-aware supervision transfer?** Sparse-token interventions emphasize math, and C-MOPD evaluates two teachers. Test additional domains and teacher counts with threshold sweeps and repeated training seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons; **medium** in the proposed mechanism and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes training-seed uncertainty, complete optimizer and update specifications, checkpoint-selection rules, per-method compute costs, causal mechanism ablations, and verification of the announced code’s contents or completeness.
