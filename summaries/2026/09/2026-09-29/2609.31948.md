# Duplex-MPE: Benchmarking Multi-Party Interaction in Full-Duplex Dialogue

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31948) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31948)


## One-sentence takeaway
Duplex-MPE evaluates selective participation in continuous multi-party speech, showing that frequent assistant speech can conceal poor answer accuracy, inappropriate interruptions, and failure to stop after human resolution (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
An assistant sharing a conversation must track every participant while deciding whether to answer, remain silent, or stop because someone else has resolved a request. The authors identify a gap between benchmarks centered on a designated user and shared conversations where any participant can supply context, request assistance, or make an answer unnecessary (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Paired benchmark:** 2,000 synthetic English scenarios, each with three or four humans and one assistant, produce 4,000 explicit/implicit addressing versions with identical gold answers. Models receive a spoken duty instruction and continuous audio without transcripts, speaker labels, or turn boundaries (§§3.1–3.4; Ethics statement).
- **Separate behavioral measures:** Fresh-onset response rate, conditional answer accuracy, silence preservation, and answering-window yield distinguish initiation, content, restraint, and stopping. Response presence is a diagnostic rather than an additional capability score (§3.5).
- **Construction and scoring pipeline:** Claude Opus 5 generates scripts and supplies semantic judgments; Qwen3-TTS synthesizes human turns; Silero VAD measures speech timing; Qwen3-ASR-1.7B transcribes outputs. Sampled human review validates construction and scoring (§§3.2–3.5). Asset release is promised, not established as completed (Reproducibility statement).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Speech presence can conceal failure to initiate appropriately | Explicit condition, 2,000 requests: Freeze-Omni response presence **0.9955**, fresh-onset rate **0.2525**; MiniCPM-o **0.9545** and **0.9500**, respectively. | §4.1 |
| Similar response coverage can conceal radically different accuracy | Explicit condition: MiniCPM-o conditional answer accuracy **0.4730**, versus FLM-Audio **0.0011**, despite response presence **0.9545** versus **0.9505**. FLM-Audio answered **2/1,901** response-present requests correctly. | §4.1 |
| High response presence need not mean selective participation | Explicit condition: MiniCPM-o silence preservation **0.9242**, versus Freeze-Omni **0.0002**; Freeze-Omni passed only **5/20,360** silence-requiring windows. | §4.1 |
| Addressing affects the transcript reference, but no speech-system difference is detected | Across 2,000 pairs, Gemini response decisions: **0.9470 explicit**, **0.3040 implicit**, difference **64.3 percentage points**, exact McNemar **p < 10⁻³⁷¹**. Speech-system response-presence differences range **−1.75 to +0.60 points**; all **p ≥ 0.2365**. | §4.2; A5.1–A5.2 |
| Stopping scores depend on which events qualify | Explicit answering-window yield: MiniCPM-o **0.596**, eligible **n=1,633**; Moshi **0.192**, **n=219**; FLM-Audio **0.008**, **n=977**; Voila **0.848**, **n=620**. Freeze-Omni has only **one** eligible event, so its rate is withheld. | §4.3 |
| Sampled semantic scoring agrees closely with human review | Final reviewer labels agree with Opus on **99/100** answer judgments and **98/100** acknowledgement/intrusion judgments; all **200** checked ASR transcripts preserve meaning. | §A3.4, Table 11 |
| Silence ranking survives reported robustness checks | Ordering is unchanged across duration thresholds **0, 100, 200, 300, 500 ms**; scenario-bootstrap **95% CI** places MiniCPM-o ahead of Voila by **8.25–9.58 percentage points** under explicit addressing. | §4.5; A7.1 |

These results support measuring participation capabilities separately. They do not establish why models fail, equivalence between addressing conditions, or overall stopping superiority from conditional yield alone. Gemini receives privileged transcript and speaker information and is explicitly not a speech-model upper bound (§§3.4, 4.2–4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Synthetic English scenes do not represent real populations, room acoustics, or social norms and may inherit generation and synthesis biases (Ethics statement).
- **Author-stated intervention limit:** Final paired versions sometimes differ beyond the request, including context, N4 content, and audio; differences cannot be attributed solely to naming Aria (§3.3; A2.4).
- **Denominator limitation:** Accuracy excludes silent requests; yield excludes premature speech, nonresponses, and answers finished before resolution. Models therefore receive different conditional evaluation subsets (§3.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Using Opus for both construction and judgment creates possible shared biases. Sampled human agreement supports local reliability but does not establish benchmark-wide validity (§A3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Request-time trends are observational across different scenarios, so they do not isolate conversation length as the cause of degradation (§A3.6).

## Open research questions
- **Does performance transfer to natural conversation?** Synthetic-only evidence leaves this open. Next experiment: evaluate matched human-recorded dialogues across room acoustics and speaker configurations.
- **What explains weak addressing effects in speech systems?** The authors allow both successful contextual inference and failure to recognize addressing cues. Next experiment: independently vary names and contextual cues while holding surrounding audio fixed (§4.2).
- **Do models stop because a request is resolved, or merely because someone speaks?** Reported N4 outcomes do not isolate that mechanism. Next experiment: compare matched resolving and non-resolving human interruptions (§3.5; A4).
