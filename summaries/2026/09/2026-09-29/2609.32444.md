# Rethinking Training-Inference Mismatch in LLM Reinforcement Learning: Where It Arises and How to Correct It

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32444) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32444)


## One-sentence takeaway
Calibrated importance sampling (CIS) uses token-confidence-dependent caps to control training–inference probability mismatch and achieves the highest mean five-benchmark math accuracy across three evaluated MoE models, although baseline tuning and broader generalization remain unresolved (Table 1; §D.6).

## Problem and motivation
Decoupled RLVR systems generate tokens with an inference engine and compute gradients with a training engine. Numerical differences can make their token probabilities disagree despite identical parameters, creating an off-policy correction problem that is amplified in MoE models (§§1–2).

Exact importance ratios can produce large gradient variance. Existing fixed caps or masks introduce bias, but the authors identify a further problem: the ratio combines engine discrepancy with token confidence, so a fixed ratio threshold treats equivalent discrepancies differently across confidence levels (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Mismatch characterization:** The authors derive the exact identity \(k=p+(1-p)e^\varepsilon\), where \(p\) is training-side token probability and \(\varepsilon=\operatorname{logit}(p)-\operatorname{logit}(q)\). Measurements show that displacement varies much less with confidence than the ratio does; routing disagreement is associated with heavier perturbation tails (§3.1; §§A.3–A.6).
- **Confidence-aware correction:** CIS truncates positive displacement, yielding \(f=\min\{k,1+\lambda\max(1-p,\kappa)\}\). The numerical floor prevents clipping at a scale below stored-log-probability resolution; the weight is detached and requires raw sampling-step inference log-probabilities (§3.3, Algorithm 1).
- **Theory and validation:** The authors bound the correction’s second-moment contribution while exposing truncation bias. Experiments compare correction methods and test confidence dependence, lower-side clipping, and numerical flooring. The theory supports bounded truncation generally; it does not establish CIS’s superiority over other bounded caps (§3.3; §§4.2–4.4; §D.6).

These results establish consistent mean improvements under the supplied training recipe and support the proposed clipping design. They do not establish statistically separated wins throughout, superiority against equally tuned baselines, or universal benefits across architectures and engines. The bias diagnostic uses logit gradients rather than the full training-gradient payload (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only MoE models receive RL training; dense-model evidence is diagnostic. Other kernels and numerical formats may require retuning (§D.6).
- **Author-stated comparison limitation:** CIS hyperparameters were selected on Qwen1.5-MoE and transferred, while baselines use reference defaults. Comparable baseline sweeps and combinations with routing replay or FP16 remain untested (§D.6).
- **Author-stated correction boundary:** Neither CIS nor exact token ratios correct the prefix distribution. Policy lag is not modeled separately, and stale tokens constitute less than 1% of these runs (§D.6).
- **Author-stated theoretical boundary:** Bounds assume bounded token gradients and independence, with a covariance-based effective-sample-size extension. Any bounded cap admits similar variance control (§D.6).
- **Author-stated causal limitation:** Routing analyses are observational; identical-route replay is needed to isolate routing’s causal contribution (§A.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence remains narrow because all RL uses GSM8K, and broader knowledge/code evaluation covers only Qwen1.5-MoE (§§C.2, D.3). The “Exact ratio” experimental baseline is also numerically clamped, rather than fully unbounded (Table 8).

## Open research questions
- **Does CIS retain its advantage under equally tuned comparisons?** Baseline sweeps are absent (§D.6). Next experiment: give each operator the same tuning budget and evaluate on a separate selection split.
- **How does CIS interact with routing replay or FP16?** Their combination is left to future work, and routing causality remains unresolved (§§A.4, D.6). Next experiment: factorial comparisons of CIS, route replay, and precision changes.
- **Can correction handle substantial policy lag and prefix shift?** These effects fall outside the modeled target (§D.6). Next experiment: vary rollout staleness and response length while comparing conditional and sequence-level corrections.
- **Does the confidence-aware cap transfer beyond math-trained MoEs?** Dense RL and broader training domains are missing (§D.6). Next experiment: matched dense/MoE runs on math and code rewards, with measured displacement distributions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method, settings, and results; broader superiority remains uncertain.

Material information unavailable includes equally tuned baseline results, dense-model RL outcomes, causal routing interventions, substantial-staleness evaluations, and measured effective sample sizes for correlated gradients.
