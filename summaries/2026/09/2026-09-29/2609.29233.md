# Post-Training Leaves Behavioral Shadows on Unrelated Decisions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29233) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29233)


## One-sentence takeaway
In a shared-ancestor setting, training on 5,664 task-unrelated, single-word teacher responses improves HumanEval+ pass@1 by 5.34 percentage points over an exact nuisance-matched control, although transfer is unreliable outside the strongest tested setting (§5.1; Table 1; Appendix H).

## Problem and motivation
Post-training can change a model’s choices on inputs unrelated to its training task. The authors ask whether these changes reveal capability-relevant information about a private update—and whether another model can learn from them without receiving target-task examples, teacher probabilities, or updated parameters.

The authors identify a gap in prior subliminal-learning work: it largely studies traits or preferences using extensive teacher outputs. Their focus is capability transfer through actively selected, individual word choices (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Active Taskless Distillation (ATD).** Using a public ancestor’s probabilities, select unrelated prompts where two ordinary words have nearly equal probability and one is the unrestricted top choice. Fix the queries before contacting the teacher; retain its greedy response only when it belongs to the candidate pair. Train a student initialized from the ancestor using response-only, full-vocabulary cross-entropy (§§3–4).
- **Controlled capability-transfer evidence.** Compare correct prompt–word pairings with controls that disrupt correspondence. The strongest control preserves the completion multiset and ancestor-disagreement counts within difficulty bins, while forcing half the labels to differ from the teacher’s (§5.1; Appendix C).
- **Characterization of the transferred information.** Test query selection, source specificity, composition, teacher-update strength, and failure cases. Functional diagnostics support structured transfer, but do not consistently imply downstream improvement (§§5.4–5.5; Appendices D–H).
- **Exploratory extensions.** Evaluate a reference-relative pair-margin objective and multi-token observations; neither establishes universal superiority over the basic method (§6).

## Evidence and main results
All differences below are percentage points unless identified as functional metrics.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strongest coding result | Qwen2.5-1.5B, four seeds: HumanEval+ greedy pass@1 is **51.22% versus 45.88%** for the exact control; **+5.34 pp**, 95% CI **[1.22, 9.60]**. Training uses 5,664 retained responses from 17,858 teacher queries. | §5.1; Table 1; Appendix B |
| Acquisition robustness | Five acquisitions × three training seeds: mean HumanEval+ signal–exact-control effect **+4.80 pp [1.18, 8.74]**; all 15 pairs positive. Bootstrap resamples acquisition, seed, and task. | §5.2; Table 6 |
| Adaptation robustness | Reacquired, matched teacher/student settings yield HumanEval+ gains over exact controls: rank-32 LoRA **+6.55 [2.59, 10.82]**, rank-64 **+9.15 [4.88, 13.87]**, full-parameter training **+4.67 [1.22, 8.54]**. | Figure 2 |
| Task breadth | Separate Qwen2.5-1.5B teachers: gains over label-shuffle controls across seven endpoints range from **+0.81 pp** on RACE-high accuracy to **+5.03 pp** on HumanEval+ pass@1; all reported 95% intervals exclude zero. | Table 2 |
| Limited cross-model evidence | Additional Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Llama-3.2-1B lineages have positive mean HumanEval+ gains over shuffled controls, but **each confidence interval includes zero**. | Figure 3 |
| Active selection helps | HumanEval+ active-minus-passive pass@1: **+4.27 pp [0.30, 8.38]** at equal teacher-query budgets; **+5.03 pp [1.52, 8.84]** at equal training-row counts. | Table 3a |
| Structured functional transfer | A 50/50 math/code mixture improves teacher-direction cosine over shuffle by **0.046 for math** and **0.062 for code**, positive in all three seeds. Update-strength projection ordering is recovered on code and SciQ trajectories; endpoint scores cannot reliably rank checkpoints. | Table 9; Appendix G |
| Wider observations can help | Qwen2.5-0.5B, 20-word observations, three seeds: GSM8K exact match **21.23% versus 14.86%**, **+6.37 pp [4.37, 8.42]** over the exact control. HumanEval+ and ScienceQA intervals include zero. | §6.2; Table 5 |
| Transfer has clear failures | Memorized-solution and substitution-cipher teachers do not improve students over their bases. On secondary MBPP+, primary-acquisition signal–exact-control pass@1 is **+0.33 pp [−2.25, 2.91]**. | §5.5; Tables 3b, 13 |

The strongest evidence supports a causal role for teacher-specific prompt–response correspondence within the tested coding protocol: matched controls address word frequencies, training budget, and generic carrier fine-tuning. Execution-based evaluation establishes actual benchmark solution gains.

It does **not** establish recovery of the private parameters, general transfer across unrelated ancestors, or broad acquisition of new knowledge. Source composition and strength recovery are principally functional-diagnostic findings. The authors’ interpretation that ATD elicits capabilities already available to the student remains an interpretation of the failure cases, rather than a demonstrated mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** ATD relies on a known ancestor and actively constructed carriers. Incompatible-ancestor experiments fail, but tokenizer, family, and scale differences confound attributing failure specifically to ancestry (Appendix H).
- **Author-stated uneven transfer:** Additional-model intervals include zero, and MBPP+ recovery is negligible. The proposed low-bandwidth explanation for weak-gap failures is not conclusively established (Figure 3; Appendix H).
- **Author-stated diagnostic limits:** Functional alignment can precede statistically significant endpoint gains, but unequal statistical power prevents concluding that alignment is acquired before capability (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—control strength varies:** Generalization experiments mostly use side-label shuffling, which does not preserve the exact completion multiset. Their identification is consequently less stringent than the primary coding comparison (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—cost accounting:** The retained response count understates teacher calls, and total public-model search cost is unspecified. “Single-word supervision” therefore does not itself establish end-to-end efficiency (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—extension comparisons:** The multi-token experiment changes model scale relative to the flagship experiment; the supplied results do not isolate observation width through a matched single-token comparison (§6.2).

## Open research questions
1. **How necessary is shared ancestry?** Existing failures change several factors together. Next experiment: vary teacher–student ancestry while matching tokenizer, architecture, and scale as closely as possible.
2. **What determines whether functional transfer becomes task improvement?** Composition and strength ordering appear more clearly in diagnostics than endpoint scores. Next experiment: sweep carrier quantity at fixed teachers and jointly measure projection, alignment, and task gains.
3. **Does a wider response improve transfer per teacher token?** The multi-token results lack an isolated width comparison. Next experiment: compare response lengths on the same lineage, carriers, and controls under equal query and token budgets.
4. **Does ATD recover new knowledge or primarily elicit existing capability?** Memorization and cipher failures cannot fully distinguish these explanations. Next experiment: manipulate students’ prior exposure to a controlled skill while holding teacher improvement and acquisition protocol fixed.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the primary within-lineage result as reported; **medium** in broader capability-transfer and mechanism claims.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes the full carrier-generation procedure and vocabulary/filter specification, complete optimization hyperparameters, public-model search and compute costs, numerical confidence bounds for the additional-model experiments, and an isolated single-token versus multi-token ablation.
