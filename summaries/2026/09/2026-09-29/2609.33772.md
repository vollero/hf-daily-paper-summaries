# Skill2Env: Capability-Oriented Environment Synthesis from Skills for General Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33772) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33772)


## One-sentence takeaway
Skill2Env converts reusable skills into capability-targeted executable tasks and hardens them using solver feedback; fine-tuning one backbone on 1.5K selected trajectories improved its reported scores across seven agent benchmarks.

## Problem and motivation
Executable agent training requires coordinated task instructions, tools, workspace state, and evaluators, making manual construction difficult to scale. Skills supply procedures and resources but do not directly specify challenging, executable tasks. The authors identify a gap in translating explicit agent capability demands into environment designs, beyond existing skill-based synthesis approaches (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Capability-oriented synthesis:** Five demands—environment understanding, planning, skill usage, long-horizon consistency, and error recovery—are operationalized through an initial pool of 100 reusable difficulty patterns (§4.2; Appendices A–B).
- **Blueprint-guided construction:** An author-side blueprint coordinates objectives, challenges, facts, information boundaries, execution requirements, and acceptance criteria. It guides construction of instructions, runtime, workspace, and weighted evaluators combining deterministic checks with LLM judging (§4.2).
- **Iterative Task Hardening:** Validated solver traces identify easy or bypassed challenges. Tasks scoring above 0.7 receive coordinated blueprint and environment revisions; newly identified challenges can expand the pattern pool (§§4.3, 5.1).
- **Training demonstration:** The pipeline produced 2,963 executable tasks. DeepSeek-V4-Flash generated teacher trajectories; 1.5K with reward above 0.9 were used to fine-tune Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (§§4.3, 5.1). This demonstrates training utility, while the claimed methodological distinction is explicitly connecting capability demands to construction and hardening.

Table 1’s gains use unrounded scores, explaining apparent discrepancies with displayed endpoints.

The evidence supports transfer from the selected synthetic supervision to multiple benchmarks and harnesses for the tested backbone. The authors interpret this as improvement in reusable capabilities (§§5.2–5.4); qualitative trajectories support that interpretation but do not independently measure each capability. The experiments do not isolate the contributions of blueprints, individual difficulty patterns, teacher supervision, or trajectory filtering.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied paper has no dedicated limitations section. It explicitly acknowledges remaining headroom relative to frontier models (§5.2).

**Analyst inferences grounded in the reported design:**

- **Limited causal attribution:** No component-removal ablations separate capability targeting, blueprint construction, validation, and training-data selection (§5).
- **Restricted model coverage:** Transfer training uses one backbone, and hardening uses a fixed solver; generality across model families is untested (§5.1).
- **Sampling uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated SFT runs are reported. Hardening-stage training uses different random qualifying samples, so task composition can influence the comparison (§5.5).
- **Evaluator validity:** Subjective rubric items use an LLM judge; human agreement or judge calibration is not reported (§§4.2, 5.1).
- **Unequal comparisons:** FACET supplies only a source-reported terminal result on a different backbone, limiting a controlled synthesis-method comparison (§5.1; Table 1).
- **Scope and reproducibility:** Skills must fit provisionable Linux environments. Full synthesis costs, prompts, collection-wide validation rates, and confirmed release contents are unavailable (§4.1; p. 1).

## Open research questions
- **Which synthesis components drive transfer?** Their effects are not separately tested. A next experiment could compare skill-only generation, blueprint generation, and capability-pattern generation with matched teacher, trajectory count, and training settings.
- **Does hardening help different solver families?** Diagnosis is conditioned on one fixed solver. Harden matched tasks with several solvers, then evaluate difficulty and transfer on held-out model families.
- **How reliable are the generated evaluators?** Consistency validation is described without aggregate external auditing. Human-audit a stratified task sample and compare deterministic and LLM rubric judgments against expert assessments.
- **Why do later stages help some benchmarks while hurting others?** Appendix D shows uneven changes. Repeat stage-specific SFT with multiple samples and seeds, controlling task domains and trajectory lengths.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly specifies the method, protocols, and reported results; causal attribution and reproducibility remain less certain.

Material missing information includes component ablations, uncertainty estimates, repeated training runs, synthesis and validation costs, evaluator reliability audits, benchmark-overlap checks, complete generation prompts, and the GitHub URL with confirmed released assets.
