# TraceDance: An Automated System for Building Agent Behavior Benchmarks from Real-World Agent Deployment Traces

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33295) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33295)


## One-sentence takeaway
TraceDance converts observed deployment failures into targeted next-turn agent benchmarks, with promising construction validity but limited evidence about subsequent execution or real-world failure frequency.

## Problem and motivation
Task completion can conceal undesirable behavior, such as leaking credentials or bypassing checks. The authors identify a gap between fixed behavior benchmarks and trace-auditing tools: developers can find deployment problems but lack an automated way to turn arbitrary behavior requests into reusable evaluations of other models (§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trace-to-benchmark construction:** A natural-language behavior query selects a reviewed specification or triggers the **Anchor Synthesis Loop**, which generates and revises retrieval code, confirmation criteria, a context-cut contract, and a grading rubric (§3.2).
- **Anchor-and-Confirm:** CPU-based programmable anchors retrieve candidate events; a cheaper LLM confirms whether they exhibit the requested behavior. Custom anchors undergo safety checks, code review, trial execution, and retrieval-quality checks (§3.2–3.3).
- **Decision-point continuation:** Models receive the recorded context immediately before an action, failure response, or claim. Only their next assistant turn is graded, without executing tools, replaying the environment, or requiring a reference answer (§3.1).
- **Empirical evaluation:** The paper tests construction, human validity, grading reliability, and behavior-specific differences across models. These support the proposed evaluation workflow; recursive self-improvement remains a proposed application (§5–6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Construction usually succeeds | Built benchmarks for 102/107 build-target queries (95.3%); correctly rejected all 32 rejection-target queries; produced 107 benchmarks and 4,125 instances. | §5.2; Table 21 |
| Query interpretation is imperfect | On 100 annotated queries, interpretations were rated accurate for 78% and partially correct for 17%. | §5.2 |
| Sampled validity is encouraging | Both annotators confirmed the requested behavior in 84/100 sampled instances; both rated rubric quality at least 4/5 in 90/100. | §5.3 |
| Grading agreement needs qualification | On 84 mutually validated instances, panel–human agreement was 81.0%, with κ=0.40 versus human–human κ=0.31. Always predicting failure would obtain 83.3% raw agreement; panel pass precision was 44.7% and recall 60.7%. | Appendix C.2; Table 14 |
| Models struggle under the specified rubric | Across 107 benchmarks and 4,065 instances with valid responses from all nine models, mean pass rate was 26.7%; model rates ranged from 22.9% to 33.5%. Pass requires mean judge score ≥4/5; benchmarks receive equal weight. | Tables 2, 20; Appendix A.3 |
| Required checks are especially difficult | Across 102 single-behavior benchmarks, mean pass rates were 67.9% for valid calls and 8.1% for checking first. Lowering the threshold to ≥3 raised these to 88.9% and 26.8%, preserving their order. | Tables 17, 20, 24 |
| Overall rank hides specific strengths | On catalog-based error-guided correction benchmarks, Kimi-K3 scored 57.8% versus Opus 4.8’s 27.8%, despite overall rates of 23.3% versus 33.5%. | Figure 4c; Table 2 |
| Construction still uses substantial LLM resources | Successful queries averaged 128,297 CPU session scans, 706 reviewed candidates, 552 LLM calls, and 25.14 million input/output tokens, excluding evaluation and grading. | Table 23 |

The evidence supports constructing useful diagnostic tests and identifies weaknesses under their rubrics. It does not establish deployment failure prevalence, successful execution of passing responses, or improvements from using TraceDance in training. Ranking uncertainty also matters: adjacent models within the reported middle and lower groups were not significantly separated (Appendix C.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limits:** One-turn grading cannot capture later recovery or execution failure. Failure-selected contexts do not measure deployment prevalence, and avoiding the target behavior alone may still fall short of passing (Appendix F).
- **Construction and grading errors:** Human validation is incomplete, rubric boundaries can be ambiguous, and judges score more leniently than humans. Judge-removal checks preserve the two overlapping judge/models’ ranks, but do not eliminate all grading uncertainty (Appendix C.2).
- **Scope and reproducibility:** Behaviors need programmable retrieval signals. Traces cover two harnesses, come primarily from Doubao Seed models, and remain private; exact benchmarks cannot be publicly rebuilt (§3.1; §4.1; Appendix C.4; p. 10).
- **Analyst inference—selection and validation:** Catalog-oriented, model-drafted, manually reviewed queries provide weaker evidence for unrestricted developer requests. Behavior groups were formed after inspecting family results, although alternative groupings were tested (Appendices C.1, E.1).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency evidence:** Workload counts do not establish retrieval recall or savings against an exhaustive-review baseline. The supplied experiments report no component-removal ablation of Anchor-and-Confirm or the synthesis loop.

## Open research questions
- **Does next-turn performance predict subsequent outcomes?** Execution is unobserved. Compare continuation scores with replayable multi-turn tasks, including delayed recovery and harmful execution.
- **What behaviors do anchors miss?** Retrieval recall is unreported. Exhaustively annotate a manageable trace subset and compare retrieved occurrences against that reference set.
- **Does construction generalize to new users and harnesses?** Queries and deployment sources are constrained. Test independently collected developer requests on held-out harnesses and source-model families.
- **Can these benchmarks improve agents without rubric overfitting?** The proposed improvement loop is unevaluated. Train on discovered weaknesses, then assess fresh traces and independent task-outcome tests.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and experimental summary; **medium** in broader validity and generalization.

Material unavailable information includes the private traces and exact reproducible benchmarks, full source-model composition, retrieval recall, comparative construction cost or latency, complete prompt schemas, and evidence of downstream execution or self-improvement benefits.
