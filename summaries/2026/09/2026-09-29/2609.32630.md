# ExpVoyager: Direct Experience Navigation for Dynamic Agent Skill Synthesis

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32630) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32630)


## One-sentence takeaway
ExpVoyager synthesizes task-specific guidance by actively inspecting raw agent trajectories, improving aggregate success rates across three text-based interactive benchmarks while adding substantial task-time token cost (Table 1; Table 5).

## Problem and motivation
Agents need reusable procedural knowledge from past successes and failures. The authors argue that constructing fixed skills before future tasks are known can discard useful details, while similarity-based trajectory retrieval can miss relevant local evidence or return irrelevant material (§§1–2).

Their preliminary ALFWorld analysis supports this motivation, but measures preservation against a selected, execution-verified set of “oracle” skills rather than all potentially useful knowledge (Appendix B.4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task-conditioned skill synthesis:** A curator investigates past experience for the current target and produces a `skill.md` supplied as context to a frozen executor; model weights are unchanged (§§3.1, 3.4).
- **Navigable Interface:** Regex searches access complete trajectory-level or step-level records, including actions, observations, reasoning, and outcomes. Linked trajectory inspection supplies broader chronological context (§3.2; Appendix B.3.1).
- **Navigation State:** The curator maintains transferable knowledge and unresolved procedural questions, updating both after each observation. Source-specific facts remain conditions to verify during execution (§3.3).

The authors’ claimed novelty is the combination of structured experience access and explicit knowledge-state management, beyond simply synthesizing skills at test time (§5).

These results support aggregate improvements within the tested environments and configurations. They do not establish universal superiority: individual subtasks and execution-step metrics have exceptions (Tables 1, 4), and fewer execution steps do not imply lower overall computational cost.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed failures:** Final synthesis can lose correct ordering or turn a valid precondition into an invalid command; execution can fail to recheck conditions after state changes (Appendix C.2.2).
- **Author-qualified oracle evidence:** Oracle skills are neither unique nor exhaustive; task-specific shortcuts remain possible despite repeated verification and manual review (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited scope:** Three text-based benchmarks and fixed offline pools do not establish transfer to other environments or substantially larger corpora (Appendix B.1; Figure 5).
- **Analyst inference—ablation confounds:** Removing the interface substitutes terminal access; removing state substitutes summarized history. These comparisons do not fully isolate structure from differences in context handling or computation (Appendix B.6).
- **Reporting inconsistency:** The synergy prose claims only one performance exception, but Table 6 shows two Trace2Skill combinations below standalone. The prose also simplifies the budget optimum, whereas ScienceWorld peaks at 25 rounds (Table 5).

## Open research questions
- **Can synthesis preserve established constraints reliably?** Correct navigation evidence still yields incorrect skills. Compare current synthesis with explicit constraint validation before execution (Appendix C.2.2).
- **Can navigation adapt during execution?** Product revisits invalidate previously satisfied conditions. Compare pre-execution synthesis with curator updates triggered by live state changes (Figure 20).
- **Can adaptive stopping improve the cost–success tradeoff?** Longer navigation sometimes reduces success. Test evidence-based stopping against fixed round budgets, measuring SR and E2E tokens (Table 5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, prompts, benchmark settings, and consistent aggregate improvements. Material gaps include a usable code-release address, annotation agreement statistics, latency and monetary costs, formal significance tests, and an explicit definition of the small uncertainty values printed beside Table 1 results. Exact coordinates in several retrieved figures are also unclear.
