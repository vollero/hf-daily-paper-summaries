# Can We Trust the Teacher? Decoupled Credit Direction-Magnitude for Self-Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34848) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34848)


## One-sentence takeaway
DCSD separates reasoning-step credit direction and magnitude from teacher scores, achieving the strongest reported aggregate reasoning accuracy while leaving its practical alignment with oracle local credit only conditionally established.

## Problem and motivation
Outcome-based reinforcement learning supplies reliable final-answer feedback but cannot distinguish helpful, harmful, and redundant steps within a response. On-policy self-distillation supplies dense teacher feedback, but the authors argue that using one teacher signal for both update direction and strength propagates judgment errors and sensitivity to privileged-context presentation. Outcome-anchored alternatives preserve stability while preventing local directions from reversing the trajectory’s sign (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Credit analysis:** The authors derive directional agreement between a success-conditioned student policy’s log-ratio and oracle local advantage, distinguish their magnitude scales, and decompose practical teacher deviations into judgment and preference components (§2; Appendix B).
- **Independent step credit:** DCSD detects steps through hidden-representation transitions, selects direction from changes in the correct answer’s likelihood margin against competitors, and assigns relative magnitude through marginal log-determinant representation gain. Inconclusive probes fall back to trajectory direction (§§3.1–3.3; Appendix C).
- **Constrained teacher allocation:** Clipped, positive teacher weights distribute established credit among tokens within each step. Normalization preserves step direction and total magnitude; the resulting detached credits drive a clipped policy objective (§3.4; Appendix C.4).

The direction guarantee requires bounded readout and candidate-coverage error. The magnitude theorem uses an auxiliary Gaussian evidence model and establishes information envelopes and moment bounds, rather than recovery of each realized oracle advantage (§3; Appendices D.2–D.4).

These results support improved aggregate performance in the evaluated settings, alongside shorter generation than RLCSD and additional training computation. They do not establish universally correct local credit: reference-model agreement is a proxy, and empirical overrides are not automatically covered by the direction theorem. The reported base-model gains remain internally inconsistent.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ explicit qualification:** Probe error is not evaluated online; empirical thresholds alone do not certify oracle-consistent overrides (Appendix C.2).
- **Theoretical scope:** The magnitude interpretation depends on Gaussian evidence and posterior-value assumptions. It bounds expected oracle-credit quantities rather than identifying realized contribution magnitude (Appendix D.3).
- **Authors’ stated failure boundary:** Visual perception and spatial errors may explain the ZeroBench regression and cannot be resolved by credit calibration alone (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No reported ablations isolate direction probing, information magnitude, segmentation, teacher allocation, or general credit attenuation.
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Multimodal baselines are imported from RLSD rather than retrained here. Main accuracy tables lack confidence intervals or repeated-seed results; bootstrap intervals cover only reference-model diagnostics (§4.1; Appendices E.2–E.4).
- **Case-study boundary:** Figures 9–12 explicitly show that stable signs can remain semantically inappropriate or have excessive magnitude. Their displayed quantities are trained-model log-probability differences against Base, rather than direct measurements of constructed policy credit (Appendix F).

## Open research questions
- **How often do overrides match actual continuation-value changes?** The probe’s error budget is unmeasured. Estimate success probabilities through repeated continuations at sampled boundaries and compare their direction with probe overrides.
- **Which component produces the gains?** Component ablations are absent. Compare direction-only, magnitude-only, uniform token allocation, and full DCSD under matched update scales and compute.
- **Does representation novelty track useful reasoning rather than style or length?** The magnitude theorem is model-dependent, and stylistic case studies remain imperfect. Insert controlled repetitions, formatting changes, and useful intermediate steps, then measure information gain and assigned credit.
- **Does DCSD transfer to longer or interactive decisions?** The conclusion proposes these extensions without evaluating them. Test multi-turn tasks with verifiable outcomes and compare credit quality and computational overhead (§5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial method, proof, and experimental detail, but conditional guarantees, proxy diagnostics, and inconsistent headline gains limit stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes component ablations, main-result uncertainty across seeds, measured probe calibration error, empirical validation of the Gaussian evidence assumptions, exact representation-layer/preprocessing and several detector/probe settings, and an explicit code or checkpoint release.
