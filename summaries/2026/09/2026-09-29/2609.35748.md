# Improving Test-Time Scaling with Adaptive Looped Transformers

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35748) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748)


## One-sentence takeaway
TaH2 jointly learns token-specific iteration depth and backbone updates, achieving a reported 53% steeper AIME accuracy–compute slope and 3.4 percentage points higher accuracy at matched decoding compute than its non-looped baseline in the 1.7B post-training setting (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Repeatedly applying shared Transformer layers increases effective depth without duplicating backbone parameters, but still costs computation. The authors ask whether this improves accuracy as output-token budgets grow—a question that parameter-matched or per-token-FLOPs comparisons do not resolve.

Their post-trained Ouro and Huginn adaptations improve accuracy faster with increasing decoding compute, yet remain below the non-looped baseline at matched compute. Token-level analysis suggests wasted iterations: many tokens gain little or suffer higher prediction loss (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluate response-level scaling:** Compare accuracy against total decoding FLOPs while varying output-token cutoffs, using a common pretrained checkpoint and post-training data (§3).
- **Jointly learn depth and predictions:** TaH2 trains the backbone and token-level continuation decider together. Online labels measure whether another iteration reduces target-token loss; stopped tokens receive a no-gradient lookahead during training (§4.2).
- **Prioritize meaningful iteration gains:** Labels retain a specified fraction of total positive gain, while cost-sensitive supervision emphasizes decisions farther from the gain cutoff. Training follows the decider’s own routes (§4.2; Appendix D).
- **Integrate recurrent computation:** A learned updater reinjects token embeddings, extended duo-causal attention exposes eligible earlier-depth states, and stopping probabilities mix predictions across executed depths (§4.1). These components build on prior designs; the central methodological distinction is their integration with joint online depth supervision.

## Evidence and main results
All results below are author-reported; accuracy differences are percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Steeper accuracy–compute slope | At 1.7B, TaH2 with maximum depth \(M=2\) achieves **2.74 points per doubling** of decoding FLOPs versus **1.79** for Standard, a reported **53% improvement**; mean AIME24–26 avg@32, output cutoffs 4K–16K. | Fig. 1a; §§3.2, 5.2 |
| Higher accuracy at matched compute | With evaluation extended to 32K, TaH2 reaches **15.4%** versus Standard’s **12.0%**, both at **191.7 TFLOPs per response**; mean AIME24–26. | §5.2; Fig. 4b |
| Increasing trained depth improves benchmark averages | Across ten benchmarks at 1.7B with default 32K output limits, TaH2 averages **40.6% at \(M=2\)** and **42.5% at \(M=8\)** versus Standard’s **37.7%**; decoding FLOPs/token are **1.21×** and **2.37×** Standard. Metrics vary by benchmark. | Tables 1, 8 |
| Gains extend to larger backbones | Across nine benchmarks, TaH2 \(M=2\) averages **49.6% versus 46.4%** at 4B and **58.5% versus 56.1%** at 8B, outperforming Standard on every listed benchmark. Training uses original teacher responses and two tokens per parameter. | §5.3; Table 3 |
| Runtime overhead remains material | On AIME26, 1.7B TaH2 \(M=2\) uses **1.22× decoding FLOPs/token** and **1.34× / 1.30× end-to-end latency** versus Standard at batch sizes **1 / 4**, respectively; single A800-80GB, 32K output limit. | Table 2; Appendix A.3 |
| Cost-sensitive supervision matters in ablation | Replacing cost-sensitive weights with uniform weights reduces mean AIME24–26 accuracy from **15.5% to 11.9%** for 1.7B, \(M=2\), 32K evaluation. | Table 4; §5.4 |
| More inference iterations alone do not improve accuracy | For the checkpoint trained at \(M=8\), raising inference ceiling from **8 to 12** leaves mean AIME24–26 avg@32 at **16.2%**, while mean depth rises from **2.22 to 2.72**. | Appendix B.2; Table 12 |

The evidence supports improved inference accuracy–compute trade-offs in the tested post-training setting. It does not establish lower total training-plus-inference cost, universal superiority over looped architectures, or gains from indefinitely increasing inference depth. The larger-model experiments establish accuracy improvements, rather than repeating the complete scaling analysis.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Training is more expensive than standard SFT. At 1.7B, TaH2 costs **1.87× Standard at \(M=2\)** and **5.17× at \(M=8\)** under the paper’s algorithmic FLOPs accounting (Table 10). Only SFT is studied; reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation remain future work (§6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Ouro and Huginn are architectural adaptations, not their released checkpoints; Ouro’s first-stage objective is modified to avoid gate collapse. Conclusions therefore concern these post-training implementations (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** TaH2-fixed also changes attention and prediction aggregation, so it does not isolate adaptive stopping alone (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—statistical and selection uncertainty:** Main accuracy results lack confidence intervals or repeated-training-seed variability. The 1.7B teacher is selected using AIME performance, reducing independence between recipe selection and headline evaluation (Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference—deployment scope:** FLOPs exclude prefill, elementwise operations, memory traffic, and serving overhead. Runtime evidence covers one GPU type and small batches; it does not establish broader deployment efficiency (Appendices A.3–A.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does TaH2 win under equal training compute?** Current runs match data and recipes but incur different training costs. Next experiment: give Standard additional SFT compute and compare resulting inference frontiers.
- **Does immediate token-loss improvement predict useful depth during free generation?** Alignment is measured on teacher-forced validation tokens (§5.5). Next experiment: compare predicted gains with continuation-level answer improvements on model-generated prefixes.
- **Can depth extrapolation become productive?** Increasing inference depth beyond the trained ceiling adds compute without improving mean accuracy (Table 12). Next experiment: train with varied depth ceilings and evaluate unseen ceilings.
- **Do gains survive other backbones and larger serving batches?** Evidence uses Qwen3 and limited runtime settings. Next experiment: repeat matched-compute and latency evaluations across model families and batch sizes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, ablations, compute accounting, and evaluation protocols, but supports a bounded empirical conclusion.
