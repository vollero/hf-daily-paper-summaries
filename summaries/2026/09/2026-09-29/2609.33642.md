# Learning to Learn from Context: Synthetic Training from Perturbed Public Documents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33642) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33642)


## One-sentence takeaway
Training a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B student on synthetic reasoning traces from perturbed public documents raises CL-bench task accuracy from 13.7% to 22.8%, then to 24.6% with rubric-reward RL, although transfer gains vary by task (Table 1; Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
The paper addresses learning and applying document-specific rules, facts, and procedures rather than recalling pretrained knowledge. The authors argue that long-document supervision is expensive to annotate, while public documents risk rewarding memorization and fully synthetic contexts often lack real documents’ complexity (§1).

Their proposed gap is a scalable way to obtain document-dependent reasoning supervision from existing public texts without human annotation.

## Approach and contributions
- **Perturb real documents:** GLM-5.2 renames entities, changes numbers, renumbers sections, and shuffles lists to discourage reliance on memorized content (§2.1).
- **Generate and filter supervision:** Generate questions with document-specific rubrics, then compare teacher answers with and without context. Admit samples only when rubric pass rates satisfy \(p_+\geq0.60\), \(p_-\leq0.30\), and \(p_+-p_-\geq0.40\); rejected questions receive one regeneration attempt (§§2.2–2.4).
- **Reuse supervision across training stages:** Preserve the teacher’s native reasoning traces for SFT and reuse question–document–rubric artifacts for GSPO with rubric-level rewards. The resulting corpus contains 9,625 samples from 3,515 documents (§§2–3.1).
- **Empirical contribution:** Evaluate context-learning gains, filtering, data scaling, reward variants, teacher dependence, and transfer to fictional contexts (§§3–4; Appendix C).

The distinctive contribution is this combined pipeline; the paper acknowledges prior entity-substitution and synthetic-supervision methods (§5).

These results support the effectiveness of the combined training recipe for the tested student and benchmarks. They do not establish that rewriting alone causes the gains, that memorization is eliminated, or that performance will continue scaling beyond the tested corpus. Matching a larger model on CL-bench also does not establish comparable overall capability: the student remains behind that teacher on CL-bench Life (Table 1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors acknowledge imperfect rewriting:** document consistency is not guaranteed (§2.1). The rewriting analysis uses different generated questions across conditions, limiting causal interpretation (Appendix C.2).
- **Authors acknowledge judging risk:** they modify brackets in the official CL-bench judge prompt to avoid moderation failures and cannot guarantee identical judge behavior (reproducibility statement, p. 9).
- **Authors report RL tradeoffs:** longer outputs accompany increased failures on anti-hallucination rubrics; the stated “about 2%” increase lacks a clearly specified denominator or distinction between relative and absolute change (§4.3). ARC-AGI performance also falls after RL (Table 2).
- **Authors acknowledge scope and labeling limits:** two agent-style subcategories are omitted, provenance labels are unofficial, and some evaluation results are missing because of execution failures (Appendices A–B; §4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** experiments use one student architecture, and no repeated-run uncertainty estimates are supplied. Generalization across students and statistical robustness therefore remain unresolved (§3.1; result tables).
- **Analyst inference:** the filtering ablation supports the admission rule as a package, but does not isolate context dependence from answer quality, since admission also requires a sufficiently good contextual answer (§2.4; §4.1).

## Open research questions
1. **How much does rewriting contribute independently of filtering?** No matched downstream rewriting ablation is supplied. Run a factorial comparison of original versus rewritten documents and filtering on versus off, controlling sample count and teacher.
2. **What makes a teacher effective for this student?** Teacher benchmark rank does not predict student rank, while output styles differ (§3.2.1; Appendix C.1). Compare teachers with matched reasoning lengths and answer-quality distributions.
3. **Can RL improve task completion without increasing verbosity or hallucination?** Both tested reward modifications reduce target performance (§4.3). Test explicit grounding rewards and evaluate task accuracy, anti-hallucination failures, output length, and reasoning transfer together.
4. **Do gains extend to other students and agent-style contexts?** Only one student is trained, and relevant conversation-form categories are omitted (§3.1; Appendix A). Replicate across student sizes and add held-out workflow and operational-procedure tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed recipes, results, and ablations, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain limited.

Material missing information includes public dataset/code/checkpoint availability, exact generation and gap-judge prompts, the gap judge’s identity, complete RL rollout settings, compute and synthesis costs, independent rewrite-consistency checks, training–evaluation overlap audits, and repeated-run uncertainty estimates.
