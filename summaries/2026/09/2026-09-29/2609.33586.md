# Approximating Softmax in Pretrained LLMs: Model Sensitivity and Kernel Acceleration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33586) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33586)


## One-sentence takeaway
Rowmax-H15 coarsens attention exponentials without retraining, accelerating B200 FP8 attention-forward calls by 12.4% at causal 8K while separately increasing BF16 perplexity by 0.091–0.492% across five models at 2K (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask which softmax properties frozen language models actually require, and whether relaxing them can accelerate fused attention. Their motivation is the throughput imbalance between matrix multiplication and exponential evaluation on B200. The identified gap is model-level evidence: small elementwise approximation error does not directly establish acceptable language-model behavior (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled sensitivity experiments:** Change support, relative weighting, grid resolution, allocation, reconstruction, and exponential evaluation separately in frozen decoder-only models. Evaluation primarily uses paired WikiText-103 next-token NLL (§3; Table 1).
- **Allocation and distortion findings:** Test whether resolution near the row maximum matters and whether matched aggregate attention divergence predicts downstream loss (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Kernel specialization:** Rowmax-PoT rounds row-relative log weights to powers of two; Rowmax-H15 uses `{1, 1.5} × 2^k`, with half-octave input rounding and integer bit operations, inside FlashAttention-4 (§§4.4–5.2).

The contribution is the controlled characterization and its fused-kernel realization. The authors explicitly acknowledge that row-maximum-relative quantization and related bit-level exponentiation constructions already exist (Appendices B.1, E).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Support can shrink, but aggressive pruning fails | Qwen2.5-1.5B, WikiText-103 at 2K: retaining the largest half with softmax weights raises perplexity 0.131%; nominal 1% retention raises it 90.478%. | §4.1; Fig. 1A |
| Relative weights matter | Qwen2.5-1.5B single-layer control: uniform weighting increases NLL at all 28 target layers with identical mean-threshold supports; every pointwise 95% interval excludes zero. | Fig. 1B; Appendix D.1 |
| A coarse grid preserves average NLL closely | Full-support, nearest-boundary grid with `K=32, R=4`: perplexity increases 0.052–0.852% across ten models at 2K; Gemma’s interval spans 1%. | §4.1; Table 3 |
| Resolution placement beats unweighted error | Qwen2.5-1.5B, fixed `K=21`, changing `R=1→4`: unweighted RMS log-weight error rises 27.9%, probability-weighted error falls 29.2%, and ΔNLL falls 71.7%. Allocation favors `R=4` in all ten point estimates, with nine intervals excluding zero. | §4.2; Fig. 2; Table D1 |
| Matched scalar distortion leaves model-dependent losses | At calibration target `J₀`, flattening-minus-sharpening NLL is resolved positive in seven models, negative in two, and unresolved in one. Aggregate JSD is matched on calibration blocks only. | §4.3; Table D2; Appendix A.7 |
| Kernel fidelity loss is small and measurable | Direct BF16 Rowmax-H15 versus stock FA4, five models, 97 WikiText-103 2K blocks: perplexity rises 0.091–0.492%; all ΔNLL intervals lie above zero. | Table 2; §5.4 |
| FP8 attention calls accelerate | B200, batch 1, 16 heads, head dimension 128, 8K: stock→patched latency is 0.2063→0.1835 ms causal (+12.4% speed-up), and 0.4879→0.3879 ms non-causal (+25.8%). | Table 2; Appendix B.5 |
| Board energy decreases | FP8 causal 16K, one sustained loop per kernel: 742.5→680.3 mJ per forward, −8.4%, at essentially unchanged mean board power; no confidence interval. | Table B5f |

These results support tolerance to selected approximations and attention-kernel acceleration in the measured settings. They do **not** establish a jointly measured FP8 speed–fidelity tradeoff, full-model acceleration, output equivalence, or universal tolerance across models and tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Primary evidence is WikiText-103 NLL. Zero-shot checks evaluate characterization operators, not Rowmax-H15; they do not establish broad capability preservation (§§6–7).
- **Quality–performance mismatch:** Kernel fidelity is measured on BF16 at 2K, whereas headline acceleration uses FP8 at longer contexts. FP8 semantic simulation omits FP8 operand quantization and uses different rescale granularity (§5.4; Appendix B.2).
- **Measurement constraints:** No locked GPU clocks or performance counters; timing spread is not a confidence interval. Energy comes from one loop per kernel. Call-latency gains are negative at 2K (§7; Appendices B.3–B.5).
- **Statistical boundaries:** Intervals are unadjusted for multiplicity; model sign counts are descriptive. JSD matching is not rechecked on the complete evaluation set and does not match its layer/head/row distribution (Appendices A.7, A.9).
- **Analyst inference:** Practical deployment benefit remains uncertain because full-model prefill and decode were not measured, and a small mean NLL change does not guarantee similar predictions (Appendices B.5, C.4).

## Open research questions
- **Does FP8 kernel fidelity remain acceptable at timing lengths?** Direct FP8 quality measurements are missing. Next experiment: evaluate stock and patched FP8 kernels with interpretable operand quantization at 8K and 16K.
- **Can finer input rounding improve the kernel tradeoff?** Quarter- and eighth-octave variants improve offline NLL but lack FA4 measurements. Next experiment: implement both and measure paired fidelity, latency, and energy (Appendix B.2).
- **What explains opposite responses at matched divergence?** Aggregate matching and mass restoration do not isolate a universal mechanism. Next experiment: match divergence by layer and head, verify it on evaluation blocks, and compare downstream NLL (Appendices A.7, D.4).
- **Do attention gains reduce full-model runtime?** Only attention forward was timed. Next experiment: benchmark complete prefill and decode across context lengths and model families (Appendix B.5).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported findings within their stated settings; broader deployment conclusions remain unestablished.

Material missing information includes direct FP8 kernel fidelity, long-context Rowmax-H15 fidelity, full-model runtime, counter-verified bottleneck mechanisms, repeated energy uncertainty, and a public release location—the supplied text promises code and results but does not establish their availability.
