# CompoWorld: Compositional Environment Scaling for General Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33665) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33665)


## One-sentence takeaway
CompoWorld trains tool-using agents on verified workflows composed from reusable services, improving its Qwen3.6-35B-A3B backbone across eight benchmarks, with a fixed-budget comparison supporting composition’s value over isolated-environment training.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a gap between generating tasks within individual environments and training agents to carry information and constraints across services. Real workflows require coordinated state changes, so success on individual tools may not translate into complete cross-application execution. Their proposed scaling axis is the composition of reusable services and task-specific dependencies (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Reusable hybrid services:** Coding agents convert public MCP specifications into typed, stateful mock services with unified interfaces. Implementations undergo execution, repair, and separate adversarial testing; an LLM world model handles unavailable or unverified tools (§4.1).
- **Verified workflow composition:** A random walk selects services, introduces information dependencies, and requires revisits. A task-authoring agent constructs initial states, instructions, and executable rubrics, then probes a reachable solution accepted by the verifier (§4.2).
- **Completion-focused training:** Successful trajectories provide SFT data. During GRPO, rubric criteria receive greater weight when fewer rollouts satisfy them, emphasizing remaining completion gaps (§4.3). GRPO itself is adopted rather than introduced.
- **Training corpus and evaluation:** The authors construct 448 services exposing 10,130 tools and train one backbone with 3K SFT trajectories followed by 1K RL tasks (§§4.1, 5.1). Asset release status is not established in the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Strongest gain in complete workflow execution | AutomationBench 1.0.6 pass rate: **10.33% → 32.33%**, **+22.00 points**; compared with GPT-5.4 **27.67%**, Opus 4.6 **25.50%**, and Occamy-1.0 **27.60%**. | Table 1; §§5.1–5.2 |

| Composition helps under a fixed trajectory budget | With **1K SFT trajectories**, composed versus single environments yield τ3-Banking pass rates **17.53% vs. 9.62%** and AutomationBench pass rates **33.33% vs. 12.83%**; all four evaluated benchmarks favor composition. | §5.3.2; Fig. 5(a), p. 10 |

| Reward reweighting helps in the shown runs | At step **140**, approximate smoothed evaluation scores are **0.76 with reweighting vs. 0.67 without**. Authors report RL adds **1.2 points on average** across eight benchmarks, with mixed individual effects. | Appendix A, Fig. 6; §5.3.3 |
| Simulation is a minority component | **7.1% of tools** use world-model simulation; approximately **10% of samples** invoke at least one simulated tool. | Appendix B, p. 16 |

The evidence supports useful transfer from composed synthetic workflows and a composition benefit under the reported fixed-budget SFT comparison. It does not establish universal frontier-model superiority, faithful execution of real services, or reliable generalization to explicitly held-out service combinations. The average benchmark gain combines heterogeneous metrics, and substantial partial-credit performance still accompanies a low complete-success rate.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Finite tests cannot capture all real-service business rules and boundary cases. Manual spot checks and downstream improvements do not prove every generated environment accurate (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** Simulation errors can propagate through affected workflows despite limited simulation usage (Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** Reward reweighting supplies no distinguishing signal for a criterion failed by every rollout (§4.3; Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Gains mainly come from SFT; RL sometimes reduces benchmark performance, and transfer to personalized long-term assistance is limited (§§5.2–5.3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The composition comparison controls trajectory count and SFT procedure, but richer task complexity and diversity remain bundled with composition (§5.3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons with five specialized models use source-reported results rather than a unified rerun (§5.1). No uncertainty intervals or repeated-seed results are supplied.
- **Analyst inference:** Figure 6’s evaluation-set construction and score definition are insufficiently specified to assess the reward ablation fully.

## Open research questions
1. **Does training generalize to unseen compositions?** No explicit held-out combination protocol is reported. Next experiment: reserve service combinations and dependency structures while retaining their constituent services in training.
2. **Which compositional feature drives gains?** Dependencies, revisits, complexity, and diversity change together. Next experiment: match tool exposure and trajectory length while separately varying dependencies and revisits.
3. **How much does simulation fidelity affect learning?** Usage proportions are reported, but no simulation-removal ablation appears. Next experiment: compare matched deterministic-only and hybrid corpora, auditing simulated transitions against reference executions.
4. **How robust is completion-focused reward?** The shown comparison lacks repeated-run uncertainty and a fully described evaluation set. Next experiment: repeat matched RL runs and report both rubric scores and complete-success rates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method and reported results, but causal and reproducibility claims remain limited.

Material missing information includes confirmed asset availability, synthesis cost and rejection rates, quantitative environment-fidelity audits, explicit composition holdouts, benchmark-contamination checks, repeated-run uncertainty, and full details of the reward-ablation evaluation set.
