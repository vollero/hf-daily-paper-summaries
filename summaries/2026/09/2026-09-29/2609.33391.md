# Beyond Timestamps: Decision-Aligned On-Policy Distillation for Long-Horizon Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33391) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33391)


## One-sentence takeaway
ALIGNOPSD aligns privileged feedback across sibling rollouts and redistributes outcome credit over adaptive decision spans, outperforming GRPO and StepOPSD on all eight reported aggregate comparisons across three benchmarks and two backbones (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Sparse terminal rewards provide little guidance about which intermediate decisions helped or harmed a long-horizon agent. Dense privileged self-distillation feedback can help, but the authors identify **Decision–Timestamp Mismatch**: corresponding decisions may occur at different turns across rollouts, and one decision may span several turns within a rollout. Consequently, timestamp-local feedback can misalign both the scoring context and the duration over which credit is assigned (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Decision-aligned supervision rectification:** A frozen encoder matches student and privileged thinking traces across same-task sibling rollouts, subject to action-consistency filters. The teacher re-scores the same student response under matched privileged histories; match confidence controls mixing with local feedback. Unmatched turns retain local feedback (§2.2).
- **Adaptive hierarchical credit assignment:** Changes in correspondence profiles define variable-duration spans. Rectified evidence allocates the trajectory advantage first across spans, then across their turns, while preserving its token-weighted mean (§2.3; Appendix E.3).
- **Mismatch diagnostics and evaluation:** The paper measures temporal displacement, examines the thinking-based matching surrogate, and compares ALIGNOPSD with agent RL and self-distillation baselines. Its claimed distinction is aligning supervisory contexts before constructing fine-grained credit (§4; Appendices C.1–C.3).

The teacher and student share model parameters. Privileged guidance and auxiliary alignment components are used only during training; optimization adds no auxiliary distillation loss (§§2.1–2.3).

The results support improved benchmark performance and contributions from both stages in the WebShop 7B ablation. The authors report first place in six of eight aggregate comparisons (§3.2). These results do not establish semantic correctness of individual matches, exact recovery of decision boundaries, or statistically reliable performance differences across training seeds.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Thinking-correspondence diagnostics do not measure semantic precision; high retained similarity partly follows from the selection rule (Appendix C.2).
- **Author-stated:** Functional-span annotations are available only as aggregates, so the paper reports no boundary F1 and makes no claim of exact semantic-goal recovery (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The annotation protocol imposes the same span-duration bounds used by the method, limiting its independence as evidence for natural decision duration (Table 7; Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Component ablations cover only WebShop 7B, while evaluation uses two scales of one backbone family. Transfer of the component-level conclusions remains untested (Tables 1–2).
- **Analyst inference:** Main-result seed counts and across-seed uncertainty are unavailable. Figure 3 shading represents local temporal variation, not seed uncertainty.
- **Analyst inference:** Matched optimizer and rollout budgets do not establish matched compute cost: correspondence and re-scoring are additional overhead, without reported wall-clock or memory measurements (Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does matching identify functionally valid contexts?** Similarity diagnostics do not establish semantic precision. Next experiment: independently label retained and rejected pairs, then relate match correctness to training gains.
- **Do adaptive spans recover useful decision boundaries?** Only aggregate annotation comparisons are available. Next experiment: obtain per-trajectory boundary labels without matching duration constraints and evaluate boundary agreement and downstream performance.
- **Do both stages help beyond WebShop 7B?** Controlled component ablations are confined to that setting. Next experiment: repeat rectification and allocation ablations on ALFWorld and Search-QA at both scales.
- **Are gains retained under equal compute budgets?** Additional training overhead is acknowledged but unquantified. Next experiment: compare performance against elapsed training time and total compute, including matching and teacher re-scoring.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reports benchmark and ablation results, but leaves material validity questions unresolved.

Missing information includes main-result seed counts and uncertainty, exact evaluation manifests and sample counts, the precise checkpoint-selection rule, full optimizer settings, measured training overhead, per-trajectory semantic annotations, and verified contents of the claimed code release.
