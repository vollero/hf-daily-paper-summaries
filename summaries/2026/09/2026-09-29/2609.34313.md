# ControlScope: Workflow Revision and Reliability in LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34313) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34313)


## One-sentence takeaway
ControlScope finds that broader workflow revision can repair inefficient or incorrect agent programs, but its reliability depends on reviewer configuration, edit selection, and whether later reviews allow useful repairs to finish (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation
An agent executing generated code may need to correct a tool argument, replace a faulty loop, or continue a useful repair without interruption. The authors identify a gap between **having permission to repair** and **selecting and completing a successful repair**: broader authority expands available actions but need not improve the deployed policy (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Nested revision comparison:** KEEP continues the existing program; ARG additionally edits the next primitive call’s data arguments; FULL additionally replaces the unfinished workflow. Branches share the initial public state, model, APIs, and environment permissions. Ordinary planning remains available in every condition (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Mechanism diagnostics:** Matched-prefix replay distinguishes repair availability, actual selection, and subsequent execution. Retained replacements are tested both alone and with ordinary planning, keeping these endpoints separate (§3.3).
- **Empirical scope and timing study:** Sustained and one-time reviews are evaluated on filesystem tasks, ALFWorld, and AppWorld, with additional workload confirmation, shared-edit replay, and execution-protection experiments (§§4–6). These are comparative evaluation contributions; the text does not establish a new trained model or a public asset release.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Filesystem gains depend on configuration | On the same 20 tasks, three reasoning-reviewer panels achieve FULL success of 15–16 versus KEEP’s 13; four fast panels achieve 10–13 versus 13. Panels reuse tasks and some KEEP trajectories. | Table 1; §5.1 |
| ALFWorld effects reverse across cohorts | With common recovery, fast KEEP/ARG/FULL scores are 85/86/87 on 87 tasks across 52 scenes, versus 134/134/127 on 134 tasks across four scenes. | §5.2; Appendix C.3 |
| Reasoning review adds substantial logged work | On the 87-task ALFWorld panel, FULL achieves 87 successes versus KEEP’s 85, with 1,261,096 versus 42,481 logged output tokens: 29.7 times as many. FULL uses 1,124 versus 1,336 environment actions. | §5.2; Table 7 |
| AppWorld net differences are small | Normal-split task completion is 144/141/145 for KEEP/ARG/FULL over 168 tasks; challenge completion is 270/272/271 over 417. FULL–KEEP paired gains are +0.60 and +0.24 percentage points, with template-bootstrap 95% intervals of [−2.38, 3.57] and [−2.40, 2.88]. FULL also renews block quota. | Table 2; §5.3 |
| Early local authority does not change observed outcomes | ARG and FULL have identical terminal outcomes in all 216 paired draws at 36 frozen states covering 19 filesystem tasks. Of 432 decisions, 391 continue unchanged, including fallbacks. | §5.4; Table 6 |
| Fresh confirmation reproduces a batching rescue | Across six new student-subset/task–program pairs per reviewer mode, KEEP/ARG/FULL score 5/5/6. In the discriminating fast run, FULL succeeds in 18 calls after a shared 13-call prefix; KEEP and ARG exhaust 100 calls. | §6.1; Table 3 |
| Broader authority can miss a cheaper shared edit | A selected ARG batch-input patch succeeds in 11 calls; sampled KEEP and FULL both succeed in 25. Replaying that patch under either scope gives matching successful traces. | §6.1; Appendix C.1 |
| Later revision can interrupt viable repairs | In two failed runs of one file-time classification task, retaining actual replacements reaches the goal for 49/71 and 46/62 candidates within the original budget. These candidates have correlated prefixes. | §6.2; Figure 3 |
| Simple protection does not improve panel success | On 20 fresh filesystem source programs, continuous/protected FULL score 14/13. Five-call protection reduces logged output by 19.4%, with zero paired wins and one loss. Retaining only the first replacement separately loses three successes across 23 replacement-producing runs. | Appendix B.2; Appendix C.2 |

The evidence supports **configuration-dependent benefits and concrete repair mechanisms**, rather than a general advantage for unrestricted rewriting. Candidate replays establish that particular successful continuations were available; they do not establish that a policy reliably selects them. Likewise, local ARG–FULL equality does not establish equivalence under sustained review.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-qualified scope:** Filesystem panels repeatedly measure a small shared task set; uncertainty is based on seven categories. Replay candidates from one task are correlated, and interruption findings remain case-level (§§4, 6.2).
- **Author-disclosed confounding:** AppWorld V1 combines broader revision with renewed request allowance. Reviewer panels also differ in tool-choice settings, error handling, and sometimes replacement boundaries (§§4–5; Appendix A.2).
- **Author-disclosed selection constraints:** Filesystem tasks incompatible with Python are excluded; local boundaries follow restrictive eligibility rules. Workload confirmation follows exploratory selection and reuses the original student-record pool (Appendices B–B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond the served model and evaluated task families remains unestablished. Logged tokens include reused responses, so they measure represented model work rather than directly establishing billed cost (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** The midpoint schedule requires a completed baseline trajectory, limiting its direct use as an online scheduler (Appendix B.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does sustained FULL help under equal AppWorld quotas?** V1 favors FULL through quota renewal; V2 tests local continuations. Run sustained official-test comparisons with shared remaining quotas.
- **Can a reviewer reliably select the smallest sufficient repair?** Shared-edit and pagination diagnostics show successful available edits being overlooked (§6.1; Appendix A.2). Compare scope-selection policies on frozen states with verified candidate outcomes.
- **When should execution be protected from review?** Useful repairs are interrupted in individual cases, but tested retention rules lose successes (§6.2). Compare adaptive protection against continuous review and fixed protection on fresh task families.
- **How broadly do the effects generalize?** Outcomes vary with source program, reviewer configuration, and cohort (§5). Repeat matched-budget comparisons across additional models and independently selected tasks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons and their stated qualifications; **medium** in broader design implications.
