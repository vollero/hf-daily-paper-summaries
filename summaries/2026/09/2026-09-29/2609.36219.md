# LeRF: Learning Reference Coordinate Frames for Perspective Taking Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36219)


## One-sentence takeaway
LeRF improves viewpoint-dependent visual reasoning by training VLMs to selectively predict and render reference frames, with gains strongest for imagined viewpoints and a measurable trade-off on camera-view questions.

## Problem and motivation
VLMs often answer spatial questions from the camera’s viewpoint even when asked to adopt another entity’s or an imagined observer’s perspective. The authors identify two gaps in existing approaches: implicit viewpoint transformation is unreliable, while explicit 3D reconstruction introduces perception errors and additional pipeline complexity (§1–2).

LeRF targets spatial question answering from static images. Robotics and embodied interaction motivate the work, but the paper does not evaluate deployment in those domains.

## Approach and contributions
- **Projected reference-frame representation:** Predict an origin and front, left, and up axis endpoints directly in image coordinates, then render directional overlays without reconstructing a complete 3D scene. A deterministic renderer handles certain foreshortened axes with depth symbols (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Selective frame construction:** Given an image and question, the VLM chooses whether to invoke the renderer. During subsequent reasoning, numerical frame parameters are removed from the history, leaving the rendered cues and reference-entity description (§3.2).
- **Two-stage learning and diagnostics:** Pose-derived supervised fine-tuning teaches grounding, frame prediction, and tool routing; GRPO then rewards final-answer correctness while excluding frame-prediction tokens from the policy-gradient loss. Perturbation and rendering ablations test how the resulting model uses frames (§3.3–3.4, §4.3).

The contribution combines learned frame construction with visual reasoning; it does not introduce a new perspective-taking benchmark.

The results support improved performance on the evaluated viewpoint-changing tasks and sensitivity to visual frame cues. Among the open-source methods listed in Table 1, LeRF-9B leads five non-camera categories and ranks second on Multi-Object; this supports the authors’ ranking claim within that comparison set.

The evidence does not establish that rendering explains all gains: most overall improvement survives without overlays. Nor does projected-axis accuracy establish complete 3D understanding. The latency table measures inference time and output length, rather than total computation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Frames omit metric depth, scale, and complete scene geometry; precise distances and complex or heavily occluded relations may require richer representations (Appendix D.1).
- **Author-stated:** Ambiguous orientation, occlusion, incorrect grounding, frame errors, and downstream counting or reasoning failures remain. Categories without meaningful intrinsic directions are excluded from supervision (Appendices B.3, D.1–D.2).
- **Author-stated:** Dynamic scenes and temporal frame tracking remain unexplored (§6; Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is bounded by two Qwen3.5 model sizes and selected benchmark categories. The supplied results report no uncertainty intervals or repeated-run variability.
- **Analyst inference:** The ablations demonstrate benefits from SFT initialization, but do not isolate frame supervision from grounding supervision, routing examples, or other SFT effects. Perturbation establishes sensitivity, not universal correct use of frames (§4.3).
- **Comparison qualification:** OrientAnything-V2 receives ground-truth origins and intrinsics for projection; SpatialReasoner is evaluated without thinking because it does not support it (§4.1; Table 1 caption).

## Open research questions
1. **Can LeRF preserve camera-view accuracy while improving other perspectives?** Table 1 shows an Ego regression despite selective routing. A next experiment could vary no-tool supervision and training-task balance, measuring accuracy and routing jointly.
2. **Which SFT component produces the benefit without overlays?** Table 3 leaves the source of this improvement unresolved. Matched ablations could separate grounding, axis prediction, and routing supervision.
3. **How robust is reasoning to realistic frame uncertainty?** The paper tests one rotation intervention and presents qualitative failures. A next experiment could sweep origin and axis errors, stratified by occlusion and orientation ambiguity.
4. **Can frames remain reliable over time?** Video is explicitly unexplored. A next experiment could evaluate temporal tracking, viewpoint changes, and accumulated errors on annotated sequences.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons and method description; lower in broader causal and generalization claims.

Material missing information includes total SFT sample counts and dataset proportions beyond the no-tool share, benchmark decontamination procedures, repeated-run uncertainty, the latency evaluation sample composition, and verifiable code/model download locations or release status.
