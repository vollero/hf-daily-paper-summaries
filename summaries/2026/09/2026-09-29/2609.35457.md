# How Far Are We from Removing the Visual Encoder? Scaling Laws for Encoder-Free Multimodal Pretraining

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35457) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35457)


## One-sentence takeaway
Encoder-free models improve multimodal validation loss faster with compute but remain behind in measured experiments; parity near 10²² decoder FLOPs is an extrapolation conditional on a fixed-size encoder baseline and shared loss floor (§3.2; Appendix B).

## Problem and motivation
Pretrained visual encoders supply useful image representations, while encoder-free models must learn them inside the language decoder. The authors ask how this changes compute efficiency and optimal allocation between model capacity and training tokens. Their identified gap is a controlled scaling comparison that also examines how the decoder compensates for the missing encoder (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled scaling comparison:** Match 11 sparse MoE decoder sizes, spanning 1.1B–44B total parameters, with identical data, optimization, pixels, and visual-token counts. Compare raw-pixel projection against a jointly trained, pretrained SigLIP 2 encoder of fixed size (§2.3; Appendix A).
- **Allocation and parity estimates:** Fit separate text and multimodal IsoFLOP laws, then analyze equal-loss compute efficiency under optimal allocation, overtraining, and individual multimodal topics (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Mechanistic evidence and robustness:** Examine attention masks, representation changes across layers, and expert routing; check alternative fitting, held-out forecasts, loss-floor sensitivity, compute accounting, and downstream benchmarks (§3.3; Appendices B–G).

The contribution is the comparative scaling study and its findings; encoder-free architectures themselves are established prior work (§4).

## Evidence and main results
Unless stated otherwise, compute below counts decoder FLOPs; efficiency ratios divide encoder-based cost by encoder-free cost at equal loss.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Removing the encoder favors larger multimodal decoders | In \(M_{\mathrm{opt}}\propto C^a\), multimodal \(a\) is 0.570 encoder-free versus 0.464 encoder-based; conditional central 80% intervals are [0.546, 0.595] and [0.458, 0.472]. Text exponents are 0.427 versus 0.422. | §3.1; Table 4 |
| Multimodal loss improves faster, but measured efficiency remains worse | Across fitted multimodal budgets of 10²⁰–10²¹ FLOPs, loss exponents are 0.3778 versus 0.2998. Observed mean compute-efficiency ratio is 0.470; model-efficiency ratio is 0.789. | Fig. 4; Appendix A.5 |
| Text scaling is nearly matched | Text loss exponents are 0.0973 versus 0.0979; observed mean compute and model efficiency ratios are 0.985 and 0.997. | Fig. 4 |
| Parity is predicted beyond the measured range | Compute-optimal crossover: 6.1×10²¹ FLOPs, conditional 80% interval [4.2×10²¹, 1.0×10²²]. Under 5× overtraining: 1.2×10²², interval [8.4×10²¹, 2.0×10²²]. | §§3.2.1–3.2.2; Appendix B.4 |
| Short-range forecasting has supporting evidence | Fitting multimodal optima through 4×10²⁰ FLOPs and forecasting at 10²¹ gives signed loss errors of −0.01% encoder-free and +2.19% encoder-based. | Appendix B.2; Fig. 13 |
| Decoder probes suggest visual specialization | Encoder-free visual states change earlier, visual expert routing is more concentrated, and bidirectional visual attention becomes more beneficial with compute. These are diagnostic observations. | §3.3; Figs. 8–10, 16 |
| Downstream averages improve without reaching parity | At about 100B training tokens, the 33B-A2.2B models average 51.2 encoder-free versus 57.7 encoder-based across 11 benchmarks under matched 3-shot evaluation without further training. | Appendix G; Table 6 |
| Full compute accounting preserves the exponent ordering | Including front-end training cost gives multimodal loss exponents 0.378 encoder-free versus 0.362 encoder-based. A numerical crossover is not refitted. | Appendix C; Table 5 |

The evidence supports faster multimodal improvement and a shift toward larger decoders within this architectural setting. It does **not** demonstrate an observed crossover or downstream parity. Topic fits predict earlier parity for STEM and later parity for perception-heavy tasks, but all evaluated topics remain less compute-efficient without an encoder (§3.2.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The encoder remains fixed while the decoder scales. Joint encoder–decoder optimization could produce a different comparison (Appendix A.1).
- **Author-stated extrapolation assumptions:** The common loss floor is structurally imposed and not identified reliably by the finite observations. Crossover timing is more sensitive to it than the ordering of scaling exponents (Appendix B.3).
- **Conditional uncertainty:** Bootstrap intervals describe residual fitting uncertainty. Overtraining multipliers are held fixed during resampling; these intervals do not establish robustness to alternative architectures or mixtures (Appendix B.4; analyst interpretation).
- **Limited ablation coverage:** Hyperparameters are not retuned for causal attention, its largest multimodal optimum is extrapolated, and some topic optima also require extrapolation (Appendices E.2, F).
- **Analyst inference:** Attention, cosine similarity, and routing concentration support the proposed adaptation mechanism but do not causally establish expert specialization (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Downstream averages combine different benchmark metrics, and individual task results are uneven; the aggregate trend should not imply universal task-level improvement (Appendix G; Table 6).

## Open research questions
- **Does parity actually occur near the forecast?** No crossover is measured. Run matched IsoFLOP sweeps around the predicted region, evaluating both validation loss and downstream tasks.
- **Does the result survive joint encoder scaling?** The baseline fixes encoder capacity. Sweep encoder and decoder sizes under equal total training FLOPs (Appendix A.1).
- **Can decoder design reduce the early visual-learning delay?** The proposed bootstrapping mechanism remains a hypothesis. Ablate dedicated visual experts or early visual-processing layers at matched compute (§3.3).
- **How dependent are the laws on the data mixture?** Only one fixed mixture is studied. Repeat allocation and crossover fits across text/multimodal proportions and perception-topic weights (Appendix A.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the measured trends with multiple checks; the headline parity budget remains conditional extrapolation.

Material missing information includes exact corpus identities and sizes, full model-by-model hyperparameters and architecture configurations, training-seed variability, released code/checkpoints, and direct experiments at the predicted crossover.
