# WaveFront Decoding: Parallelized Self-Speculative Decoding for Looped Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-29.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23033) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23033)


## One-sentence takeaway
Wavefront Decoding batches drafting and verification across recurrence depths without additional training, delivering reported end-to-end speedups of 2.42× on Ouro-2.6B and 3.54× on Huginn-3.5B over autoregressive decoding across six Spec-Bench categories (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
Looped language models reuse a shared block repeatedly, saving parameters but requiring sequential recurrent calls for each generated token. Shallow recurrence outputs can provide drafts, yet the authors’ draft-then-verify (DtV) baseline separates drafting from verification, leaving opportunities to batch their shared computation unused (§§1–2).

The paper addresses this scheduling gap. Its motivation is lower decoding latency, rather than reduced computation per fully verified token (§3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Continuous wavefront scheduling:** WFD batches token states at different positions and recurrence depths in one shared-block call. Shallow states draft new tokens while older states advance toward full-depth verification. Rejections commit the full-depth correction and flush subsequent speculative positions (§3.1, Algorithm 1).
- **Scheduling cost analysis:** Under full acceptance and approximately constant small-batch call latency, a finite wavefront reaches DtV’s infinite-draft-length latency limit. WFD also bounds the speculative positions discarded after rejection (§3.2).
- **KV-traffic analysis and approximate extension:** The authors identify mixed-depth KV accesses as a long-context bottleneck and evaluate WFD with cross-recurrence KV sharing. Sharing itself comes from prior work; its combination with WFD is explicitly approximate (§3.3).

Native shallow-recurrence drafting is also attributed to prior work; the new contribution is principally the concurrent schedule (§2.2).

These results support a scheduling advantage on the tested checkpoints and hardware. They do not establish universal speedups or lossless behavior for the KV-sharing extension. The authors attribute unshared-cache bf16 accuracy differences to numerical effects; fp32 token agreement supports that explanation in the tested settings (§4.3, Appendix B.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers two public checkpoints on one GPU. Adaptive depths, tree drafting, and distributed execution remain unevaluated (§6, Appendix D).
- **Author-stated performance boundary:** Gains decrease with user batch size; without sharing, long contexts increase mixed-depth KV traffic (Appendices B.1–B.2).
- **Author-stated approximation:** Shared KV states can be observed at different update stages than in sequential AR. Naive sharing failed to preserve Ouro’s baseline accuracy (§§3.3, 4.3).
- **Analyst inference—limited quality evidence:** Shared-cache accuracy is assessed on two mathematics benchmarks; this does not establish comparable quality across all six throughput categories.
- **Analyst inference—unclear long-context claim:** Appendix B.2 reports shared-cache results for Ouro, including approximately 3.2× speedup at 64k context, while §4.3 and Table 8 say Ouro retains its original cache and does not admit sharing. The supplied text does not resolve this inconsistency.
- **Analyst inference—measurement uncertainty:** No repeat-run variability or confidence intervals are supplied. Static-width CUDA-graph timing does not establish performance for the complete dynamic rejection-and-regrowth scheduler.

## Open research questions
- **Can adaptive draft depth improve latency?** Appendix B.3 shows a draft-accuracy/parallelism trade-off, but adaptive policies remain future work. Compare an online depth policy against tuned fixed depths on identical workloads.
- **How broadly does KV sharing preserve quality?** Sharing breaks token equivalence, and quality evaluation is narrow. Measure matched-cache token agreement and task quality across the non-mathematical Spec-Bench categories.
- **How much benefit survives fully optimized dynamic execution?** CUDA-graph results use static steady-state shapes. Benchmark the complete scheduler, including rejection and refill, against optimized AR and DtV.
- **Does distributed P/R/C execution help?** Appendix D.1 proposes disaggregation without measurements. Compare single-device and distributed execution while measuring communication cost and end-to-end latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Main throughput results and scheduling details are explicit, but the Ouro KV-sharing inconsistency limits confidence in the long-context claims.

Material information unavailable includes timing variability, statistical uncertainty for accuracy differences, exact benchmark sampling details, a resolved Ouro long-context cache configuration, broad quality evaluation of sharing, and measured adaptive or distributed execution results.
