# CRISP: Cliff-awaRe Input-adaptive Sparse Prefilling with Structural-Mass-Motivated Routing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01925) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01925)


## One-sentence takeaway

CRISP accelerates long-context LLM prefilling by routing attention heads with an inexpensive structural-mass proxy and replacing cumulative coverage on structured heads with a sink-aware noise-floor threshold, improving retrieval accuracy while reaching up to 5.30× attention speedup at 512K tokens.

## Problem and motivation

Long-context prefilling is bottlenecked by quadratic self-attention. Existing fixed or offline sparse patterns cannot adapt fully to input-dependent attention structure, while FlexPrefill’s dynamic approach incurs extra routing computation and selects tokens using cumulative attention-mass coverage.

The authors identify two gaps: FlexPrefill’s Jensen–Shannon divergence (JSD) router requires a pooled matmul, softmax, and divergence calculation; and cumulative coverage can either stop within a dominant attention sink or cross the signal–noise boundary and collect \(O(n)\) background tokens (§§1–4; Figure 1).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Structural routing proxy:** \(C_{\text{struct}}\) measures proxy-attention mass at the first 128 sink tokens and last 128 recency tokens. Heads above a threshold enter the Vertical-Slash (VS) path; others use Pooled Estimation (PE). It reuses the existing proxy attention map (§3.2; Algorithms 1–2).

2. **Mass-cliff analysis:** The paper models VS attention as sinks, task-relevant signal, and near-zero background mass, arguing that residual cumulative coverage requires \(O(n)\) noise tokens as context grows (§§4.1–4.2; Appendix C).

3. **Sink-aware selection:** For each vertical and slash block distribution, CRISP retains blocks whose mass exceeds \(\alpha\) times the mean residual mass after excluding the always-retained first and last blocks. The number of selected blocks therefore adapts to the observed distribution rather than a fixed coverage target (§4.3; Algorithm 3).

4. **Training-free integration:** CRISP preserves exact softmax attention within selected blocks and retains FlexPrefill’s cumulative selection for diffuse PE heads (§4.3; Algorithm 4).

These results establish favorable accuracy–latency tradeoffs against the tested sparse baselines on the reported models and benchmarks. They do not establish universal superiority across architectures, end-to-end serving latency, decoding, hardware platforms, or models above 8B. Scores exceeding dense attention are empirical outcomes; the paper’s explanation that sparse selection produces a “cleaner” representation is an author interpretation, not directly isolated by the experiments.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** \(C_{\text{struct}}\) depends on sink and recency concentration observed in current architectures; sink-suppressing or gated architectures may require a different proxy (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Binary VS/PE routing may mishandle hybrid heads, and CRISP primarily improves the VS path (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation is principally limited to two 7–8B model families, prefilling, and established context ranges; models above 8B and autoregressive decoding remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Llama RULER loses 0.40 points versus FlexPrefill-0.95 because some aggregation-relevant blocks may fall near the threshold (§5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The theoretical \(O(n)\) result assumes a simplified fixed logit-gap model and a positive residual coverage requirement; its applicability to arbitrary empirical head distributions is not proven (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Latency is attention-only on one H100, so routing gains may translate differently to end-to-end systems (§5).
- **Analyst inference:** PE-path evidence is narrow: its mass-profile analysis uses one Llama model and one input, with diffuse heads identified by top-1 mass rather than recomputed routing (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** The text contains reproducibility ambiguities. Appendix A says CRISP eliminates the dual-direction decomposition, whereas §4.3 and Algorithm 3 explicitly retain it. Appendix E also claims InfiniteBench threshold spread below 0.7 points across both families, but Table 7 reports a 2.10-point Qwen spread.

## Open research questions

1. **Can routing work without architectural attention sinks?** The current proxy assumes sink/recency concentration. Test CRISP against entropy-, learned-, or distribution-shape proxies on sink-suppressed and gated-attention models.

2. **Would soft routing improve hybrid and aggregation heads?** Binary routing can discard non-VS structure and slightly hurts Llama RULER. Compare probabilistic VS/PE mixtures or confidence-weighted unions against the current router at matched latency.

3. **Does the mass cliff persist during decoding?** The paper evaluates prefilling only. Measure per-step attention distributions and quality–bandwidth tradeoffs under causal decoding across generation lengths.

4. **How well do the gains scale across systems and model sizes?** Evidence above 8B and beyond one H100 is absent. Run end-to-end serving experiments on larger models and multiple accelerators, reporting memory, throughput, and time-to-first-token.
