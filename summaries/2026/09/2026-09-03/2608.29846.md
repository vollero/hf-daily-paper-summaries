# Influence-Directed Distillation: Solving the Diversity Bottleneck in Sampled-Token On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29846) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29846)


## One-sentence takeaway

IDA-OPD selectively shrinks sampled-token distillation updates predicted to contract student entropy, improving pass@k across evaluated math and code tasks without requiring full-vocabulary teacher probabilities.

## Problem and motivation

Sampled-token on-policy distillation (OPD) reduces teacher-query cost by using only the teacher probability for a student-sampled token, but may improve pass@1 while leaving pass@k stagnant—a “diversity distillation failure.” The authors argue that existing diversity remedies either require costly top-K/full-distribution teacher information or inflate entropy indiscriminately, without identifying which individual updates cause entropy loss (Sections 1–2).

Their analysis finds that an update’s entropy effect is not determined by the teacher–student log-probability gap alone: it also depends on the student’s local token distribution. Moreover, aggregate entropy contraction is reported to concentrate around numerous low-discrepancy teacher–student positions rather than solely at high-disagreement positions (Figures 1–2, Sections 3–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Token-level diagnostic:** First-Order Local Entropy Influence is defined as \(I_H(y)=A_yD_y\), combining sampled-token advantage \(A_y=\log q_y-\log p_y\) with a student-distribution-dependent entropy-direction factor \(D_y\). Under the paper’s local logit-step analysis, \(\Delta H=\eta I_H(y)+O(\eta^2)\) (Theorem 1, Section 3.2).

- **Selective distillation rule:** IDA-OPD retains entropy-expanding updates unchanged. For entropy-contracting updates, it replaces \(A_y\) with \(w_yA_y\), where \(w_y=|q_y-p_y|/(q_y+p_y)\). Thus, corrections are attenuated most strongly when teacher and student already agree, while their direction is preserved (Equations 2–4, Section 4.1).

- **Efficiency claim:** The method transfers one teacher scalar per position, \(O(L)\), and computes its entropy term from already-materialized student logits; the authors contrast this with selected-position top-K teacher distributions of \(O(L_{\mathrm{sel}}K)\) for EOPD and AOPD (Section 5.7).

- **Empirical contribution:** Experiments cover two math model scales and one code setting, with component and shrinkage-shape ablations plus entropy analyses (Sections 5.1–5.6).

These results support the authors’ claim that IDA-OPD improves sampled-output success and avoids some entropy contraction in the tested Qwen3 math and code settings. They do not establish that entropy is equivalent to semantic or solution-path diversity, that the method generalizes beyond the tested model family and tasks, or that its theoretical approximation remains accurate for arbitrary optimizer steps.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-acknowledged theory-to-training gap:** Theorem 1 concerns an idealized, first-order, local logit-space step, whereas actual training uses shared parameters and AdamW. Figure 4a offers empirical directional validation but not a general theoretical guarantee (Section 5.5).

- **Analyst inference—narrow model coverage:** All reported students and teachers are Qwen3 non-thinking variants, so architecture- and family-level generalization is not established (Section 5).

- **Analyst inference—limited domains and ablations:** Code is evaluated in only one 4B setting, while the detailed gate and shrinkage ablations are limited to the 4B math setting and two AIME datasets (Tables 2–4).

- **Analyst inference—diversity proxy validity:** The study treats pass@k and policy entropy as diversity-sensitive indicators but does not directly measure distinct reasoning strategies, semantic diversity, or code-solution diversity (Sections 5.2 and 5.6).

- **Analyst inference—statistical uncertainty:** The supplied text describes a variance-reduced pass@k estimator but reports no confidence intervals, repeated-training variability, or significance tests (Section 5; Tables 1–2).

- **Analyst inference—cost comparison is asymptotic:** Section 5.7 provides information-complexity claims, but the supplied text contains no measured wall-clock time, memory usage, throughput, or end-to-end cost table.

## Open research questions

1. **Does \(I_H(y)\) remain reliable across architectures and optimizer regimes?** The evidence is confined to Qwen3 models and AdamW training. A next experiment could measure influence-sign agreement with realized entropy changes across several model families, scales, learning rates, and optimizers.

2. **Does preserved token entropy correspond to meaningfully different solutions?** Pass@k cannot distinguish genuine strategy diversity from superficial sampling variation. A next experiment could cluster generated proofs or programs by semantic strategy and compare diversity, correctness, and entropy jointly.

3. **How does IDA-OPD behave outside reasoning-focused tasks?** Only competition mathematics and code are evaluated. A next experiment could apply the same sampled-token budget to instruction following or open-ended generation while measuring quality and diversity.

4. **Is the linear shrinkage rule robust or task-dependent?** It beats three alternatives on two AIME benchmarks in one 4B setting, but broader optimality is unresolved. A next experiment could tune or learn monotone shrinkage functions on held-out tasks and test transfer across domains and scales.
