# SolarWM: Open Data and Scalable Training for Long-Horizon Video World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02886) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02886)


## One-sentence takeaway

SolarWM combines a 1.43M-clip open data engine with a shared three-stage adaptation recipe for four heterogeneous video backbones, producing camera-controllable causal models that the authors qualitatively demonstrate in uninterrupted rollouts up to one hour.

## Problem and motivation

Interactive video world models must preserve visual coherence over long autoregressive rollouts while responding to camera controls. The authors identify two coupled obstacles: datasets provide inconsistent temporal, geometric, semantic, and quality supervision, while video backbones use incompatible representations, attention layouts, and conditioning mechanisms. Existing open systems are described as relying on restricted data or model-specific implementations and often omitting processed data, exact recipes, or complete training pipelines, limiting reproducibility and controlled cross-backbone comparison (Sections 1 and 3.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Reconfigurable data engine:** Converts heterogeneous sources into frame-aligned samples containing video, metric camera poses, per-frame intrinsics, dense captions, quality measurements, and provenance. Source preprocessing is separated from filtering, balancing, temporal windowing, and backbone-specific representations (Sections 5.1–5.7).
- **Open annotated corpus:** Processes 1,425,694 clips from 10 source datasets into 14 independently selectable owners, including three Clean Plate derivatives; accepted and rejected samples and their selection reasons are retained (Table 4; Sections 5.3 and 5.5).
- **Backbone-native framework:** Applies shared data, camera-conditioning, optimization, and rollout interfaces to Wan2.2-5B, Wan2.2-14B, LTX-2.5, and MiniMax-H3 while retaining route-specific latent and conditioning contracts (Section 6; Table 5).
- **Three-stage training:** Uses bidirectional camera-conditioned adaptation, teacher-forced AnyFlow causal initialization, and distribution-matching distillation on self-generated trajectories (Section 4; Figure 2).
- **Release commitment:** The authors state that they will release processed data, annotations, selection records, pipeline, exact recipes, implementations, weights, and inference code (Section 2). The supplied text does not verify that these artifacts are already accessible.

The evidence establishes corpus scale, pipeline structure, model configurations, and the existence of selected qualitative long-horizon generations under the stated settings. It does **not** quantitatively establish state-of-the-art performance, real-time throughput, perceptual quality, camera-control accuracy, temporal consistency, or superiority to baselines: no numerical model-quality metrics, runtime measurements, human evaluation, uncertainty estimates, or matched baseline results are supplied in the experimental section.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **No explicit limitations section:** The authors do not provide a dedicated discussion of limitations.
- **Analyst inference — qualitative evaluation:** Figures 6–11 and narrative judgments are the principal model evidence. Sparse sampled frames cannot reveal all failures within minute- or hour-long videos.
- **Analyst inference — unsupported comparative strength:** The paper claims state-of-the-art performance, but the supplied experiments contain no quantitative baseline table or model metric supporting that comparison.
- **Analyst inference — “real-time” is unquantified:** Hardware, latency, throughput, memory use, and end-to-end interaction measurements are absent.
- **Analyst inference — limited long-horizon model coverage:** The presented minute- and hour-scale results are attributed to the Wan2.2-5B-fast causal student; equivalent long-horizon evidence for the other three routes is not shown.
- **Analyst inference — unclear training evidence:** Optimization budgets, training compute, dataset mixtures per model, convergence curves, and ablations isolating the three stages, data filtering, fused-PRoPE, or backbone-native choices are absent from the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference — possible evaluation selection bias:** The criteria for choosing the displayed trajectories and examples are not specified, and no aggregate success or failure rate is reported.
- **Analyst inference — release status:** Much of the openness claim is phrased as “will release”; the text alone cannot confirm availability, licensing, or executable reproducibility.

## Open research questions

1. **How often does hour-scale coherence actually hold?** Only selected frames from a single illustrated hour-scale setting are provided. A next experiment could evaluate many full rollouts with blinded human ratings and automated camera, identity, and temporal-drift measurements at regular intervals.

2. **Which training stage produces the long-horizon benefit?** The paper describes three stages but reports no stage-by-stage ablation. A controlled experiment could compare bidirectional-only, teacher-forced AR, and AR-plus-DMD checkpoints under identical data, compute, and rollout trajectories.

3. **Does the recipe transfer equally well across all four backbones?** Cross-backbone 10-second qualitative evidence is shown, but long-horizon causal results focus on the 5B route. The same minute- and hour-scale evaluation suite could be run across all four models with matched sampling settings and compute reporting.

4. **What is gained by the corpus design and source-aware filtering?** No comparison against naïve mixing, uniform filtering, removal of rejected-data provenance, or individual source subsets is reported. A factorial data ablation could measure camera adherence and rollout stability for alternative mixtures and tier policies.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for describing the architecture, corpus, stated settings, and reported qualitative demonstrations because the supplied text is complete and detailed. **Confidence is low for comparative effectiveness** because the paper provides no quantitative model-performance results.

Material missing information includes training compute and schedules, optimization counts for each stage, inference hardware and speed, quantitative quality/control metrics, baseline evaluations, statistical uncertainty, systematic failure cases, ablations, selection protocol for qualitative examples, licensing details, and confirmation that all promised release artifacts are operational.
