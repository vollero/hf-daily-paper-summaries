# Autoregressive Mosaics: Probing 2D Spatial Reasoning in Text-Only Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30751) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30751)


## One-sentence takeaway

AM-Bench finds that eight text-and-code-only LLMs can accurately transcribe specified geometry into drawing code, yet differ markedly in open-ended 2D composition, with performance also shaped by the output medium and layouts apparently determined incrementally during generation.

## Problem and motivation

Programmatically generated images do not reveal whether an LLM possesses an internal 2D layout, merely translates explicit spatial descriptions into code, or is constrained by the drawing medium. Existing recognition and generative evaluations conflate these factors. The authors therefore seek to separate spatial composition from spatial expression and test whether a layout representation exists before drawing begins (Introduction; Fig. 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **AM-Bench:** A benchmark based on programs rendered as 24×24 mosaics through a deterministic six-primitive canvas API. Its translation task supplies complete geometry, while its layout task requires models to infer geometry from underspecified prompts (Secs. 3.1–3.3).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Translation uses exact-area per-part IoU (PIoU); layout uses two VLM judges over five rubric dimensions, with pairwise human validation (Secs. 3.2–3.3; Appendix C).
- **Output-medium ablation:** The same 150 layout prompts are generated through canvas code and raw SVG, with SVG rerasterized to 24×24 for resolution-matched scoring (Sec. 4.2).
- **Representation and intervention analysis:** Linear probes decode 6×6 occupancy maps from pre-generation residual-stream activations, followed by code-prefix coordinate interventions testing sensitivity to the evolving geometric state (Sec. 4.3; Appendix D.2).
- **Released assets:** The authors state that prompts, generations, executor, evaluation pipeline, translation scorer, and experimental code will be released, although the supplied review copy removes the project link (Sec. 2; Appendix F.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Translation was nearly solved by every evaluated model. | Across 145 references and 1,740 attempts per model, mean PIoU ranged from 0.929 to 0.995; every model had median PIoU 1.000 and exceeded the preregistered 0.6 gate. Overall, 98.1% of 13,920 attempts passed versus a random-placement mean PIoU of 0.072. | Tables 1 and 5; Sec. 4; Appendix B.2 |
| Open-ended layout scores varied substantially despite strong translation. | Under Judge 1, overall mean layout scores ranged from 3.57 for GLM-4 32B to 1.75 for CodeLlama 34B; Judge 2 ranged from 4.33 to 2.87 and produced the identical model ranking, Spearman ρ=1.0. | Table 2; Sec. 4.1 |
| The judges agreed on ranking but differed in absolute scores and had noisy sample-level agreement. | Judge 2 was 0.90 points more lenient overall; per-sample Pearson correlations were 0.43–0.54 and Spearman correlations 0.42–0.57. Two human annotators agreed on 70.6% of 350 pairs (Krippendorff’s α=0.60); on agreed directional pairs separated by a judge, agreement with humans was 82.4% for Judge 1 and 76.7% for Judge 2. | Table 2; Sec. 4.1; Appendix C.2 |
| SVG improved measured layout performance. | With the same 150 prompts, sampling schedule, rubric, judge framing, and 24×24 resolution matching, SVG improved the pooled score by 0.37, 95% CI [0.26, 0.47]; all eight deltas were positive and six were individually significant. | Sec. 4.2; Fig. 4; Table 6 |
| Pre-generation activations contained a coarse, largely shared layout signal. | Ridge probes predicting each model’s 6×6 consensus occupancy exceeded a refitted TF-IDF baseline for all eight models: mean ΔR²=+0.164, range +0.130 to +0.205, all BH-adjusted p≤0.0004. For GLM-4 32B and Gemma 2 27B, shared-layout R² was 0.550 and 0.573, while model-specific R² was 0.005 and −0.005. | Sec. 4.3; Table 3; Appendix D.2.1 |
| Generation responded to changes in the existing geometric code state. | Among eligible completed generations, moving a statement into an unused region raised continuation rates from 43.8% to 84.6% for GLM-4 32B and from 2.5% to 41.2% for Gemma 2 27B; both BH-FDR p=0.0007. The study used 731 generations and 26,316 continuations across four arms. | Sec. 4.3; Appendix D.2.2 |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that layout differences are not explained by gross inability to transcribe coordinates into the tested canvas API, and that measured performance depends on the output medium. The probing results establish linear decodability, not causal use of the decoded representation. Likewise, the code intervention demonstrates sensitivity to textual geometric state but does not show that a particular internal activation representation causes that behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Only eight open-weight models from 8B–34B were evaluated; larger and closed-weight models remain untested, and the roster is not a controlled scaling-law sweep (Sec. 5; Appendix F.2).
- AM-Bench covers small allocentric 2D mosaics, not general spatial competence, depth, occlusion, metric egocentric continuity, or embodied behavior (Sec. 5; Appendix F.2).
- Layout evaluation depends on VLM judges. They agree on model ranking but differ systematically in absolute scores, while human inter-annotator agreement is only moderate (Sec. 4.1; Appendix C.2).
- Shared-versus-specific probing and the causal intervention cover only two models. The intervention changes code rather than internal activations (Sec. 4.3).
- The translation difficulty ladder and icon stress test were run only on the original two-model pilot; periodic patterns and tilings were not tested (Appendices B.2 and F.2).
- Numeric tokenization remains a candidate confound, although strong translation performance argues against gross coordinate-generation failure (Appendix F.2).
- Seventeen of 145 translation references required simplification, introducing documented target noise (Appendix F.2).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Because layout prompts are synthetically generated and the held-out human prompts are excluded from the released suite, robustness to naturally authored prompt distributions is not established.
- The claim that SVG gains reflect prior exposure is explicitly a hypothesis; the experiment establishes a medium effect but does not distinguish memorization, syntax familiarity, expressiveness, or other medium-specific causes.
- Translation controls precise transcription of supplied layouts, but may not cover every code-generation skill needed for independently devised layouts, especially periodic construction.
- The human comparison is too small after agreement filtering to confirm the reported cross-tier inversion or cross-model differences; the paper itself labels these secondary analyses underpowered.

## Open research questions

1. **Do the findings generalize across scale and model access regimes?** Larger and closed-weight systems were not tested. A next experiment could apply the same translation, layout, and probing protocol to a controlled within-family scaling series.

2. **What property of SVG produces the performance gain?** The current ablation changes the representation and its likely training exposure together. Compare several equally expressive, resolution-matched drawing languages while controlling syntax frequency and using held-out rare compositions.

3. **Does the decoded pre-generation signal causally guide drawing?** The intervention targets textual code state rather than activations. Activation patching or steering at probe-selected layers could test whether altering the decoded occupancy signal changes subsequent geometry.

4. **Does translation remain easy for periodic and more general spatial structures?** Repeating grids were omitted from the stress-test ladder, while patterns and symmetry appear challenging in layout generation. Add controlled periodicity rungs and run the expanded ladder across all eight models.
