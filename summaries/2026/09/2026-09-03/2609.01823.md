# Kirin: Animal Motion Generation from In-the-Wild Video

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01823) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01823)


## One-sentence takeaway

Kirin turns in-the-wild quadruped videos into a large aligned video–text–3D-motion dataset and trains an image-and-text-conditioned diffusion model whose generated motions show stronger fidelity and alignment than AniMo, though with reduced diversity.

## Problem and motivation

Large-scale animal motion modeling is constrained by the difficulty of capturing animals—especially wild or endangered species—in controlled motion-capture environments. Existing datasets are small, cover few species, lack 3D motion, or rely on artist-authored actions that inadequately represent natural behavioral variation. The authors target this gap by reconstructing scalable 3D motion directly from monocular Internet videos and using it for conditional motion generation and mesh animation (Introduction, pp. 2–3; Sec. 2.1, p. 4).

## Approach and contributions

- **AiM3D dataset:** The authors reconstruct 29,979 SMAL-based quadruped motion sequences from AiM videos and generate six descriptions per clip, producing 179,874 captions aligned with videos and motions across 23 categories (Sec. 3.1, pp. 6–8; supplementary Sec. 1 and Table 5, pp. 20, 26).
- **Motion reconstruction:** AniMer initializes per-frame shape and pose; sequence-level optimization improves 2D keypoint alignment and rotational smoothness. SpatialTrackerV2 separately estimates global translation, which is scaled into SMAL coordinates and combined with articulated pose (Sec. 3.1, pp. 6–8; Fig. 10, p. 25).
- **Conditional generation:** An MDM-derived diffusion model fuses frozen DistilBERT text features, frozen DINOv3 image features, and timestep embeddings through a transformer decoder. Per-modality dropout permits text-only, image-only, or combined conditioning (Sec. 3.2, pp. 8–9; Fig. 2, p. 4).
- **Automatic animation:** A single-image 3D model supplies a textured T-pose mesh; SMAL fitting, skinning-weight transfer, and linear blend skinning rig and animate it with generated motion (Sec. 3.3, pp. 9–10).
- **Claimed novelty:** The authors describe AiM3D as the first large-scale aligned video–text–motion quadruped dataset and their model as the first animal motion generator jointly conditioned on text and images (Introduction, p. 3; supplementary Table 5, p. 26).

The quantitative evidence supports improved conditional-generation fidelity and text–motion alignment against the tested AniMo baseline, plus a favorable reconstruction speed–accuracy tradeoff against feed-forward systems. It does not establish universal state of the art: only one tailored generation baseline is evaluated quantitatively, mesh-animation evidence is qualitative, and SMALify remains more accurate than Kirin’s reconstruction. The paper’s statement that both Kirin variants outperform the baseline “across all metrics” is not literally supported by Table 1’s diversity values, where higher is marked better and AniMo is closer to or above the ground-truth value.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- One shared model lacks animal-specific structural priors despite large differences in species scale and proportions (supplementary Sec. 10, p. 26).
- Tail motion is not explicitly modeled because source footage often hides tails and pseudo-ground-truth keypoints omit tail joints (supplementary Sec. 10, pp. 26–27).
- Monocular depth ambiguity and reconstruction errors can cause foot sliding, floating, or other physically implausible artifacts; no explicit physical constraints are imposed (supplementary Sec. 10, p. 27).
- Frontal, overhead, occluded, and unstable-camera footage degrades limb reconstruction and can contaminate generation training data (Sec. 3.1, p. 8; supplementary Sec. 10, p. 27).
- Post-optimization during rigging can introduce skeleton-to-mesh misalignment (supplementary Sec. 10, p. 27).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text:**

- Dataset supervision is noisy: the authors estimate only 86% of reconstructions and 90% of caption samples are correct from a 100-sample validation, so training and test labels are not equivalent to motion-capture ground truth.
- The image-conditioning ablation changes both training and evaluation conditions, but the supplied text reports no tests isolating encoder choice, fusion strategy, modality-dropout probability, or guidance scale.
- Quantitative generation comparison is narrow because AniMo is the only evaluated tailored baseline; the Puppeteer comparison is visual rather than metric-based.
- Reproducibility is incomplete in the retrieved text: the global-translation scale is documented, but generation training duration, optimizer settings, model size, guidance scale, and compute requirements are not reported.
- “State-of-the-art” reconstruction should be qualified: Kirin leads the fast baselines, not the slower SMALify method, in Table 3.

## Open research questions

1. **Can category-aware anatomy improve cross-species realism without fragmenting the dataset?** The shared skeleton and generator lack species-specific priors. A next experiment could compare the current model with category embeddings and adaptive skeleton representations, reporting per-species motion metrics and human judgments.

2. **How much do reconstruction errors propagate into generation?** The dataset contains acknowledged occlusion, depth, and jitter failures, but no quality-stratified training ablation is reported. Train identical generators on all data, confidence-filtered data, and a manually verified subset, then compare fidelity, physical artifacts, and generalization.

3. **Can explicit physical constraints reduce foot sliding and floating?** The present system has no physics-aware objective. Add contact labels or a physics-based refinement loss and evaluate foot-contact consistency, penetration, and trajectory stability alongside the existing metrics.

4. **Can tails and other species-specific structures be modeled reliably?** Tail joints are absent from pseudo-ground truth even though tails carry behavioral information. A plausible experiment is to annotate a multi-species tail subset and compare dedicated tail dynamics with the current fixed-SMAL representation.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the architecture, dataset scale, reported metrics, and documented limitations because the supplied text includes the complete main paper and supplementary material.
