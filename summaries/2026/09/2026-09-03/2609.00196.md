# WHALE: A Simple Recipe for Joint Harness-Weight Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00196) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00196)


## One-sentence takeaway

WHALE alternates model fine-tuning with executable-harness search and, on three tool-using tasks, achieves higher best mean@8 accuracy than weight-only, harness-only, and matched prompt–weight adaptation.

## Problem and motivation

Agent performance depends on both model parameters and the executable harness controlling prompts, tools, observations, parsing, errors, and termination. Optimizing only one can leave the other as the bottleneck, while updating either changes which counterpart is effective. Prior joint-adaptation work considered weights and textual prompts, but not broader executable harness programs; the authors identify coordinating these components—especially their different update timescales and switching schedule—as the central gap (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Alternating optimization:** WHALE fixes the harness while updating model weights, then fixes the updated model while searching for a better harness, allowing repeated co-adaptation (Section 4, Eq. 4).
- **Concrete recipe:** The experiments instantiate weight updates with online rejection-sampling fine-tuning on verifier-accepted rollouts and harness search with Meta-Harness’s proposal–evaluation–selection loop (Sections 3.1–3.2).
- **Adaptive scheduling:** A patience-based variant switches phases after a minimum duration when the phase’s training signal stops improving, without consulting validation data (Section 4; Appendix A.1).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Budget-matched comparisons cover weight-only, harness-only, prompt-restricted Fast–Slow Training (FST), stagewise optimization, and multiple alternation schedules across search QA, mathematical reasoning, and chess puzzles (Sections 5–6).
- **Empirical diagnosis:** The study distinguishes harness-dominant and model-dominant regimes and reports that a small update to one component can unlock improvements from the other (Section 6.1).

These results establish strong best-so-far accuracy and rollout-efficiency advantages for this specific RSFT-plus-Meta-Harness implementation on the tested models, domains, and verifier-defined tasks. They do not establish convergence to a global joint optimum, superiority for other optimization operators or larger models, or total compute efficiency because harness-proposer compute is excluded. The paper’s return rule also selects the pair with the best test accuracy, so the reported best points do not represent a deployable validation-based selection procedure (Algorithms 1–2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The conclusion leaves other weight-update and harness-search algorithms, larger models, and additional domains to future work (Conclusion, pp. 9–10).
- **Author-stated accounting boundary:** Rollout comparisons exclude proposer compute during harness search (Section 6.1), so they measure target-agent rollout efficiency rather than full computational cost.
- **Analyst inference — test-set selection:** Algorithms 1–2 return the pair with the best accuracy on `Dtest`. This risks optimistic best-point reporting and leaves practical checkpoint selection without test access unestablished.
- **Analyst inference — limited generality:** Results use only Qwen3.5-2B/4B, one weight optimizer, one harness optimizer, and three environments; transfer to other scales, operators, and agent settings is unsupported.
- **Analyst inference — stochastic reliability:** The supplied text does not report repeated-run variance, confidence intervals, or statistical tests. It is therefore unclear how robust schedule rankings and smaller margins are to sampling or proposer randomness.
- **Analyst inference — verifier dependence:** Training, harness scoring, and testing share fixed domain verifiers. SearchQA additionally depends on an LLM judge, while Math and chess use strict reference matching; sensitivity to alternative evaluators is not tested (Appendix B.3).
- **Analyst inference — ablation coverage:** Schedule ablations and adaptive WHALE are evaluated only on SearchQA and Math, not chess (Section 6.2), and no experiment separates the effects of individual harness-edit categories.
- **Analyst inference — budget matching:** Target-agent update budgets are matched, but the methods do not have equal total computation because executable-harness proposals require an external proposer.

## Open research questions

1. **Does WHALE generalize across optimization operators and model scales?** The paper uses only RSFT, Meta-Harness, and 2B/4B Qwen models. A next experiment could cross multiple weight optimizers and harness-search algorithms over several model sizes under matched end-to-end compute.

2. **Can checkpoint and phase switching avoid test-set selection?** The algorithms report the pair with best test accuracy, while adaptive stopping uses training signals only. A next experiment could reserve validation data for checkpoint selection and compare training-signal, validation-based, and fixed-budget policies on a held-out test set.

3. **Which executable harness components produce the gains?** The search space jointly exposes prompts, tool formatting, feedback, parsing, retrieval or execution behavior, and termination. A next experiment could restrict edits to one component family at a time and measure accuracy, rollout cost, and behavioral metrics.

4. **Are the improvements reproducible under proposer and sampling randomness?** No multi-seed uncertainty is supplied. A next experiment could repeat each schedule with independent model sampling and proposer sessions, reporting confidence intervals and the frequency of destabilizing harness selections.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, experimental setup, and reported numerical results because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variance or significance tests, end-to-end compute and monetary cost including the proposer, validation-based checkpoint-selection results, results for adaptive scheduling on chess, comparisons using other models or update operators, and component-level harness ablations.
