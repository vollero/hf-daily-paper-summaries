# FoldingAgent: Inferring Parametric Origami Procedures from Demonstration Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00377) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00377)


## One-sentence takeaway

FoldingAgent uses a zero-shot vision-language agent, an origami simulator, visual verification, and rollback to convert manually selected video keyframes into executable Pureland-origami programs, outperforming simpler VLM variants on 27 captured sequences.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses the semantic gap between human-oriented origami demonstrations and the explicit geometric representations required by computational systems. Given video keyframes, the task is to infer both the paper’s layered geometric state and the executable action sequence that produced it. This is difficult because hands and paper layers cause occlusion, folds have long-range effects, and early mistakes propagate through later steps. The authors argue that prior vision methods largely classify states or predict isolated creases rather than recover complete, sequential programs (Introduction; Sec. 2.1).

## Approach and contributions

- **New reconstruction task:** Infer a procedural folding program directly from demonstration keyframes, including intermediate states and transition actions (Sec. 4).
- **Parametric origami representation:** Extend FOLD with face orientation and explicit layer ordering; represent actions through `add_vertex`, `fold`, `unfold`, `rotate`, and `flip` primitives (Secs. 4.1–4.2; Fig. 3).
- **Agentic reconstruction framework:** A pretrained VLM proposes actions and interacts with a deterministic simulator, renderer, movement viewer, separate VLM critic, checkpoints, and rollback mechanism (Sec. 4.3; Fig. 2).
- **Bounded recovery:** Limit exploration to five transition attempts per keyframe, then use a selector model to commit the best explored state; total execution is capped at 300 tool calls (Secs. 4.3 and 5.2.3).
- **PurelandFold benchmark:** Curate 27 self-captured Pureland sequences with keyframe-level actions, topology, geometry, crease assignments, face orientations, and layer structure (Sec. 5.2.1; Appendix C).

These results support the authors’ claim that the structured state/action space provides a large validity gain and that agentic rollback improves completion and final-state fidelity over the tested internal variants. They do not establish superiority over a directly comparable prior end-to-end method, because the authors report that none exists. Evaluation is confined to 27 controlled, self-recorded Pureland sequences, and the backbone comparison covers only five sequences. Because several metrics are reimplemented from descriptions without released reference code, exact comparability to prior reported values is not established (Appendix E.3).

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Performance depends strongly on the VLM; heavy hand or layer occlusion remains difficult (Sec. 6; Fig. 12).
- Simultaneous compound actions, such as rotating while flipping, violate the sequential action-space assumptions (Sec. 6).
- Only Pureland origami is supported; advanced techniques require a broader action space and potentially stronger visual reasoning (Sec. 6).
- The critic can approve incorrect states, with an 88% reported success rate (Sec. 5.1).
- When all five explored candidates are poor, the selector must still commit one, potentially propagating errors (Appendix F.1; Fig. 12).
- Long runs are costly and can fail through thinking-token exhaustion (Sec. 5; Appendix B).

**Analyst inferences:**

- External validity is narrow: videos were captured with an iPhone 12, ring light, daylight, and high-contrast black background rather than sampled from noisy online demonstrations (Appendix C).
- Manual keyframe extraction is an unautomated dependency, so the paper does not evaluate full raw-video-to-program inference (Sec. 4; Appendix C).
- The main comparison is an internal ablation ladder using the same VLM, not a direct comparison with an established task-specific system (Sec. 5.2.3).
- PurelandFold appears to be used entirely for evaluation; the supplied text does not specify a formal train/validation/test split. The system is zero-shot, but dataset-level selection effects remain unclear.
- Metric reproducibility carries risk because TSS, GS, CS, FFS, and CPD were independently implemented from published descriptions whose authors did not provide code (Appendix E.3).
- No isolated ablation of rollback, movement viewing, checkpoint overview, selector behavior, or exploration budget is reported in the supplied tables; their individual contributions therefore remain unresolved.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the method operate directly on untrimmed, uncontrolled online videos?** Manual keyframes and controlled capture leave this untested. A next experiment could add automatic temporal segmentation and evaluate on naturally sourced videos with clutter, camera motion, and varied lighting.

2. **Which agentic components produce the accuracy gains?** The reported ablations combine multiple capabilities. A factorial ablation could independently remove rollback, critic calls, movement filmstrips, checkpoint overview, and bounded selection while holding the backbone and budget fixed.

3. **Can richer folding techniques and simultaneous actions be represented reliably?** The five sequential primitives exclude compound actions and non-Pureland folds. A next experiment could extend the simulator with a validated complex-fold vocabulary and annotate a benchmark containing those transitions.

4. **How robust are results to verifier errors and budget choices?** Critic mistakes and forced selection from poor candidates can propagate. A next experiment could vary the critic, attempt budget, and acceptance policy while measuring accuracy, cost, and unrecoverable-error frequency.
