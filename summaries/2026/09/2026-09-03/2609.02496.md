# Debias-SparseGPT: Bias-Aware Pruning for Large Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02496) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02496)


## One-sentence takeaway

Debias-SparseGPT modifies SparseGPT’s second-order pruning objective with representations from demographic contrast pairs, often reducing pruning-induced bias without materially changing general-task performance or pruning efficiency, though gains are not universal under aggressive 2:4 sparsity.

## Problem and motivation

Post-training pruning makes LLM deployment cheaper, but can amplify demographic disparities even when aggregate accuracy is preserved. The authors identify a methodological gap: existing LLM pruning algorithms optimize weight importance and output reconstruction without directly constraining changes between pro- and anti-stereotypical inputs (§1–§2.2; Table 1).

The paper targets representational bias in causal question answering, operationalized primarily as failure to select an “Unknown/Not stated” answer when demographic information does not justify a group-specific response (§2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Bias-aware objective:** The method reconstructs layer outputs for paired pro- and anti-stereotypical inputs while additionally penalizing changes in their representation difference, \(\Delta X=X_0-X_1\) (§3.1, Eq. 2).
- **Modified second-order pruning:** It augments the input-space Hessian with \(2\Delta X\Delta X^\top\). This Hessian affects both pruning-mask saliency and compensation updates for retained weights (§3.1, Eqs. 3–5; Algorithm 1).
- **Efficiency preservation:** Because the extra term is accumulated into the existing Hessian, the method retains SparseGPT’s \(O(d^3_{\text{hidden}})\) computational complexity and layer-wise \(O(d^2+nd)\) memory complexity (Table 1; §5.3).
- **Broad empirical study:** The evaluation covers nine 3.8B–9B base or instruction-tuned LLMs, unstructured 25%/50% sparsity, and semi-structured 1:4/2:4 sparsity (§4; Tables 7 and 13).
- **Calibration analysis:** The authors test how contrast-pair category and additional long-context UltraChat examples affect aggressive 2:4 pruning (Appendix E, Tables 14–15).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large UnQover gain for LLaMA | At 1:4 sparsity, Debias-SparseGPT scored 60.46% versus 35.60% for SparseGPT; MMLU was 59.76% versus 59.11%. The UnQover gain was significant at \(p<0.01\). | Table 2; §5.1 |
| Improved Qwen fairness-performance trade-off | At 1:4 sparsity, Qwen DTO fell from 0.311 to 0.291; UnQover rose from 70.60% to 74.41% and MMLU from 67.35% to 67.73%. | Table 2 |
| Improvements across additional model families were generally smaller | At 1:4 sparsity, all six additional models improved over SparseGPT on UnQover and DTO; examples include Phi-4-Mini, 43.36%→48.51%, and DeepSeek, 17.66%→20.08%. | Appendix D, Table 11 |
| Benefits were not universal across sparsity settings | With StereoSet-only 2:4 pruning on Qwen, Debias-SparseGPT reduced UnQover from 28.45% to 24.94%, MMLU from 50.41% to 48.16%, and worsened DTO from 0.616 to 0.645, despite improving BBQ from 64.10% to 66.70%. | Table 3 |
| Richer calibration repaired much of the 2:4 degradation | Adding 256 UltraChat examples to StereoSet raised Debias-SparseGPT’s Qwen MMLU from 48.16% to 54.17%, UnQover from 24.94% to 47.26%, and lowered DTO from 0.645 to 0.494. | Table 4; Table 12 |
| Calibration composition materially affected results | Under Qwen 2:4 pruning, 64 UltraChat examples produced the best reported DTO, 0.486, while 256 produced the best PPL, 13.37, and MMLU, 54.17%. Religion-only StereoSet pairs yielded DTO 0.453. | Appendix E, Tables 14–15 |
| Sparse inference efficiency was preserved | On an A100 with Qwen 2:4 sparsity, both methods achieved 73.05 tokens/s and estimated 0.0376 kg CO₂e/Mtoken, versus 27.54 tokens/s and 0.0998 for the dense model; Debias-SparseGPT had lower DTO than SparseGPT, 0.494 versus 0.522. | Table 5; §5.3 |
| Supplementary safety results favored the method | At 1:4 sparsity, unsafe rates were lower than SparseGPT for three models on both evaluated benchmarks; Vicuna HarmBench fell from 0.510 to 0.425. | Appendix F, Table 16 |

The evidence supports improved bias metrics in many 1:4 and less-restrictive settings, with similar perplexity and general-task accuracy to SparseGPT. It also demonstrates equal measured sparse-inference efficiency on one Qwen/A100 configuration.

It does **not** establish uniform improvement: several model–sparsity–benchmark combinations regress, especially StereoSet-only 2:4 pruning. CrowS-Pairs effects are mixed, and the experiments do not show that higher “Unknown” accuracy captures all relevant forms of fairness. Reported paired significance testing applies only to dagger-marked comparisons.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Calibration and evaluation are English-only, so multilingual generalization is untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** The main evaluation emphasizes representational bias rather than the full range of toxicity, harmful generation, robustness, or other safety dimensions. Appendix F is limited to three models, 1:4 sparsity, and two benchmarks.
- **Author-stated:** Aggressive 2:4 sparsity substantially degrades perplexity and is highly sensitive to calibration-set size and composition; theoretical error bounds for the estimated Hessian are absent.
- **Author-stated:** Learned sparsity patterns receive only an initial analysis; Appendix G finds changes concentrated in attention output projections but does not explain causality.
- **Analyst inference:** “Consistent” improvement should be interpreted cautiously. Table 13 contains UnQover regressions for Qwen at 2:4 and Vicuna at 50%, along with mixed BBQ and CrowS-Pairs outcomes.
- **Analyst inference:** The comparison does not isolate mask selection from weight compensation, since the bias-aware Hessian changes both; a component ablation is not reported.
- **Analyst inference:** Reliance on “Unknown/Not stated” accuracy may confound stereotype avoidance with a general tendency to abstain. No separate abstention-overuse analysis is supplied.
- **Analyst inference:** Efficiency measurements cover one model, one GPU type, and one sparsity regime; broader hardware and end-to-end pruning-cost results are not established.

## Open research questions

1. **Which part of the method produces the fairness gains?** The Hessian affects both mask construction and weight reconstruction, but these effects are not separated. An experiment could use the bias-aware term in only one component at a time.

2. **Does improved unknown-answer accuracy reflect genuine debiasing or increased abstention?** The reported metrics do not measure inappropriate abstention on answerable questions. A matched evaluation could jointly measure justified abstention, unjustified abstention, and group-conditioned task accuracy.

3. **How robust is the method to calibration distribution and language?** Tables 14–15 show strong sensitivity to calibration size and category, while all primary data are English. A factorial experiment could vary language, demographic category, corpus diversity, and pair count across fixed models and sparsity regimes.

4. **Can aggressive structured pruning be made reliable?** StereoSet-only 2:4 results sometimes favor SparseGPT, whereas richer calibration reverses the outcome. Repeated calibration subsampling with confidence intervals could test whether the improvements are stable or dependent on particular examples.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, experimental settings, and reported table values because the supplied text includes the full main paper and appendices.

Material missing or insufficiently specified includes repeated-run variability, confidence intervals or effect sizes beyond selected paired \(t\)-tests, significance results for most metrics, component-level ablations, inappropriate-abstention rates, multilingual evidence, broader hardware measurements, and the practical wall-clock cost of constructing the paired calibration Hessian.
