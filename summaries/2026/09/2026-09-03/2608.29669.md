# Wasserstein-Barycentric Interaction Fields for Spatial Factor Models: Evidence from Language-Model Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29669) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29669)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors construct a directed peer-interaction field by jointly reconstructing each firm’s pre-period news-embedding distribution from aligned peers, and report that it fits subsequent return dependence better than equal-support and pairwise-distance alternatives while remaining conditionally informative alongside news co-mentions.

## Problem and motivation

Spatial asset-pricing models generally treat the interaction matrix \(W\) as an externally supplied object, leaving both the origin of peer weights and the economic meaning of spatial feedback unexplained. Existing geographic, network, and text-based approaches often reduce a firm to a point or make observed links primitive, potentially discarding the dispersion and multimodality of its information footprint.

The authors address this gap by representing each firm as a distribution of article embeddings, constructing \(W\) from how combinations of peers reconstruct that distribution, and deriving the spatial coefficient from a quadratic exposure-adjustment problem rather than assuming it directly in returns (Introduction; Sections 2–4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Distribution-valued firm representation:** Each firm is represented by an equal-mass empirical distribution of article embeddings instead of a centroid or scalar text feature (Sections 2 and 4).

2. **Target-anchored barycentric field:** For each target, the method first computes target-specific optimal-transport assignments to every peer, then chooses nonnegative, unit-sum peer weights that jointly reconstruct the target cloud. Repeating this produces a directed, row-stochastic, zero-diagonal field \(W^\flat\) without a kernel bandwidth (Definition 4; Equations 11–12; Figure 2).

3. **Economic spatial closure:** A quadratic objective balancing departure from stand-alone exposure against peer misalignment yields
   \[
   B=\rho WB+(1-\rho)\xi,\qquad \rho=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},
   \]
   making \(\lambda\) a model-implied relative penalty index (Definition 2; Theorem 1).

4. **Multiple interaction channels:** The framework admits multiple fields—empirically, the barycentric and persistent news co-mention fields—with separate coefficients and penalty indices (Definition 3; Theorem 3).

5. **Dispersion theory:** Under explicit carrier, transmission-slack, stationarity, and stability assumptions, the authors bound how much cross-sectional exposure dispersion can survive peer adjustment (Equation 14; Theorem 5; Corollary 3).

6. **Out-of-window empirical evaluation:** Text from 2018–2022 constructs frozen fields, which are evaluated using working-model spatial QMLE on 885 aligned trading days in 2023–2026 for 52 firms (Section 6.5).

The results establish that, within the supplied geometries and working likelihood, the barycentric field organizes conditional return dependence and adds fit beyond the tested support-only, pairwise-distance, and co-mention alternatives. They do **not** establish causal peer effects, actual managerial adjustment costs, identification of latent exposures, or a geometry-invariant structural parameter. The cross-geometry likelihood rankings are descriptive rather than formal likelihood-ratio tests.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated—identification:** Freezing the field prevents evaluation returns from constructing their own peer weights, but omitted industry, technology, attention, and reporting-selection factors may affect both text and returns. Structural interpretation requires an unverified return-bridge restriction (Sections 6.2 and 8).

- **Author-stated—working likelihood:** The spherical, homoskedastic disturbance used by QMLE is not implied by the exposure model; the implied innovation in Equation 19 is generally cross-sectionally correlated (Sections 6.1–6.3).

- **Author-stated—representation dependence:** Results depend on the embedding model, ground metric, candidate universe, balanced-cloud truncation, and reconstruction rule. Tied transport assignments may produce different fields under alternative optimal selections (Section 8).

- **Author-stated—external validity:** The 52-firm sample is a large-cap, survivor-conditioned intersection with continuous heavy news coverage and is not representative of listed firms. Technology is disproportionately represented (Section 6.5; Table 1; Section 8).

- **Author-stated—lost information:** Truncating every cloud to 128 articles removes firms’ true news-volume information and may not preserve annual article composition (Sections 6.5 and 8).

- **Author-stated—latent dispersion assumptions:** The carrier map, noncollapse constant \(L\), and transmission slacks \(\tau_i\) are not estimated, so the spatial-dispersion certificate is conditional and not numerically calibrated (Section 5; Section 8).

- **Analyst inference—reproducibility risk:** The code, derived fields, and complete regeneration archive are only promised upon acceptance, while provider-hosted articles, embeddings, and market data are not redistributed (Data availability statement).

- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** The paper tests several carefully chosen comparators and representation variants, but does not report experiments with unbalanced transport, weighted clouds, alternative ground metrics, or alternative tied-assignment selections; the authors themselves identify these as useful future checks (Section 8).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the field remain useful in broader and changing firm universes?**  
   The current sample contains 52 survivor-conditioned, heavily covered large-cap firms. A next experiment could reconstruct fields in rolling ex ante windows while admitting firm entry and exit across additional market segments.

2. **How sensitive is the field to transport and cloud-design choices?**  
   Equal-size clouds, squared Euclidean embedding cost, and one deterministic optimal assignment are maintained choices. A factorial comparison could vary balanced versus unbalanced transport, article weighting, ground metrics, and tied-assignment rules on identical evaluation dates.

3. **Can the adjustment coefficient receive a causal or structural interpretation?**  
   The return-bridge quasi-score condition is assumed rather than demonstrated, and text may share omitted drivers with returns. A plausible next experiment would use an externally timed information shock or exclusion design that shifts peer information geometry without directly shifting target returns.

4. **How many interaction channels can be separated reliably?**  
   The two fitted spatial lags correlate at 0.897810, and their bootstrap coefficients correlate at \(-0.683028\), already making total feedback easier to estimate than its allocation. Adding candidate fields while reporting joint regions, boundary behavior, and induced-signal collinearity would test where channel decomposition becomes weakly identified.
