# Sparse Readout Prism: Explaining Logit-Lens Scores in Features Instead of Tokens

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01936) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01936)


## One-sentence takeaway

Sparse Readout Prism factorizes a language model’s unembedding weights into sparse features, yielding corpus-independent decompositions of lens scores and showing that fitted lenses can report different token languages for the same state while retaining the same dominant readout feature.

## Problem and motivation

Lens methods decode intermediate language-model states into tokens, but the resulting ranking reflects both the state and the readout used to score it. Token identity is also an ambiguous explanatory unit: one token row can cover multiple senses, while different tokens—including translations—can share underlying readout structure (Introduction, pp. 1–2; Figure 1).

The authors identify an additional confound for fitted lenses: maps learned on different corpora can produce different token readings from an identical hidden state. They call this **corpus conditionality** and argue that token rankings alone cannot distinguish properties of the state from properties introduced by the fitted instrument (§5, pp. 8–10).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Sparse Readout Prism (SRP).** The method fits a TopK sparse autoencoder directly to the rows of a model’s unembedding matrix. Each row is represented by sparse coefficients over a shared overcomplete dictionary, a shared offset, and a residual (§3.1, pp. 3–4). Reported operating points use \(k=256\) and 32× expansion (§3.1).

2. **Additive score explanations.** A selected token logit or linear contrast is decomposed into signed feature contributions plus an explicit residual, in original logit units. For zero-sum contrasts, the shared offset cancels (§3.2; Figure 2; Appendix A).

3. **Common coordinates across readings.** One weight-derived dictionary serves every context, layer, and lens for a given model, allowing comparisons without fitting a new explanatory basis to each activation corpus (§3.3).

4. **Corpus-conditionality study.** The authors hold hidden states fixed while varying the language used to fit Jacobian lenses or ridge translators, then compare both token outputs and dominant SRP features (§5.1–§5.3).

5. **Released assets.** The text reports released code, dictionaries for eight models, contrast banks, prompts, and label-audit materials (p. 1; Appendix F.3, pp. 26–28).

The evidence supports SRP as a comparatively faithful, locally additive description of selected linear readout scores and demonstrates corpus conditionality in the tested fitted lenses. It does not establish that the learned features are canonical, that they identify upstream circuits, that fitted-lens corpus conditionality generalizes to all models or languages, or that readout-side edits reliably improve generation-level behavior.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Source truncation:** The supplied paper text omits 138,833 characters from the middle of the appendices. This digest therefore cannot claim complete coverage, and some detailed tables, ablations, protocols, and results are unavailable.

- **Author-stated basis sensitivity:** Individual directions depend on dictionary width, sparsity, training recipe, and seed. Cross-seed experiments recover related row groups rather than identical directions (Limitations; §4.4).

- **Author-stated compute cost:** Each model needs a one-time SAE fit of roughly 6–12 NVIDIA A40 GPU-hours; larger readouts require about 40 GB for training (Limitations; Appendix F.3).

- **Author-stated empirical scope:** The central cross-lens study uses only Qwen3.5-9B, English–Chinese and English–German, two fitted-lens constructions, and limited fitting-corpus budgets (Limitations; §5).

- **Author-stated interpretation boundary:** SRP explains selected readout scores, not the circuits that produce them or full generation behavior. Feature labels derived from top unembedding rows can inherit tokenizer and vocabulary geometry (Limitations; Appendix O.1).

- **Author-stated nonlinear-readout boundary:** The main baseline and intervention comparisons exclude Gemma-4 because its post-readout softcap is nonlinear; Gemma fidelity is assessed separately (§3; §4 setup).

- **Analyst inference:** The strongest causal evidence is local direction ablation with no new forward pass (§4.3). It validates score sensitivity on the readout side, but not that an SRP feature is a causal computational mechanism upstream.

- **Analyst inference:** The word-sense result uses features selected from labeled training contexts and trails a full-state probe, so it supports compact summarization more directly than uniquely superior sense representation (§4.5).

- **Analyst inference:** The readout-edit findings are not robust across models, and the reported Qwen3.5-2B edit has substantial off-target regression and KL cost (Appendix N–N.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Do corpus-conditional token shifts persist across more models, scripts, and fitted-lens objectives?** The controlled study is restricted to Qwen3.5-9B and two language pairs. A next experiment could repeat the frozen-state protocol across several model families, language typologies, and corpus-matched lens methods.

2. **Can readout features be aligned reliably across models or training checkpoints?** The present basis is model-specific, and individual directions vary across seeds. A next experiment could benchmark cross-model dictionary alignment using held-out row groups, contribution agreement, and intervention transfer.

3. **How do SRP features relate to upstream activation features and circuits?** The paper decomposes the readout side and only outlines joint activation/readout factorization. A next experiment could pair layerwise activation dictionaries with SRP and test whether aligned feature pairs predict causal patching outcomes.

4. **Can feature-directed edits achieve consistent lexical control with low collateral damage?** Matched-KL benefits appear model-dependent. A next experiment could optimize sparse combinations of SRP directions on validation prompts and evaluate held-out generation quality, off-target behavior, and safety-filter interactions.
