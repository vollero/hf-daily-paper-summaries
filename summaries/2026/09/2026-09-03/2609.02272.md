# PaperCompiler: Faithful Paper-to-Code Generation via Repository-Level Specification Compilation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02272) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02272)


## One-sentence takeaway

PaperCompiler improves repository-level paper-to-code fidelity by compiling paper-grounded evidence into explicit requirements, file ownership, cross-file dependencies, and anti-degradation constraints before generating code.

## Problem and motivation

Research papers often omit implementation-critical details while describing methods at a high level, making faithful repository synthesis difficult. Existing paper-to-code agents typically pass free-form plans or summaries between stages; the authors argue that downstream coding agents can weaken, reinterpret, or lose these requirements, causing algorithmic simplification and cross-file inconsistency (Introduction, pp. 1–3; Fig. 1).

The identified gap is an intermediate representation that preserves provenance and uncertainty while binding each methodological requirement to the repository component responsible for implementing it.

## Approach and contributions

- **Paper Grounding:** Builds a structured implementation blueprint covering method scope, execution flow, data, training, and evaluation. Every item records its source location, evidence status, and implementation role; long or format-sensitive material is preserved verbatim in a reference registry (Section 3.1, pp. 4–5).
- **Specification Compilation:** Reconciles grounded items into enforceable behavioral requirements, including prohibited method-degrading substitutions. It assigns primary file ownership, producer–consumer relationships, interfaces, and artifact flows, then creates localized file contracts (Section 3.2, pp. 5–6).
- **Constraint-Guided Generation:** Generates files in dependency-compatible order using their local contracts, committed upstream code, and downstream compatibility requirements, while leaving unspecified local engineering choices flexible (Section 3.3, p. 6; Algorithm 1, p. 12).
- **Empirical contribution:** Evaluates the method on 90 Paper2CodeBench papers, a ten-paper comparison with additional end-to-end systems, critique-level failure analysis, and a nine-paper component ablation (Sections 4.1–4.4).

The evidence supports improved model-judged repository fidelity under the paper’s controlled setup, with the strongest improvement when author repositories inform evaluation. Ablations support the value of reconciliation and file-level contracting, although the nine-paper, single-generation design makes those estimates preliminary.

It does not establish full reproduction of reported experiments, universal executability, formal correctness, or generalization across model families and generation seeds. The supplied text also does not report statistical significance tests or human evaluation independent of the model-based judges.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The system relies mainly on text-parsed content and may miss information contained in diagrams, complex figures, and visual examples (Appendix A, p. 11).
- Missing details may be supplied from the LLM’s parametric knowledge, especially when external tools, proprietary APIs, simulators, conventions, or auxiliary resources are unavailable (Appendix A).
- Evaluation measures repository-level fidelity, not complete reproduction of experimental results or executability across all repositories, seeds, and model families (Appendix A).
- Specifications guide generation but provide no formal correctness guarantee (Appendix A).
- Fine-grained interface alignment remains problematic: API/schema critiques increase from 2.3% to 4.0% (Section 4.3; Table 3).
- The SEABO case shows that preserved modular topology need not yield executable external-protocol integration: its runner uses dummy data without a D4RL path and does not implement the state-only training regime (Appendix D.4, pp. 14–15).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The primary results depend on LLM judges, including the reference-based protocol; without reported independent human assessment, evaluator bias remains a validity risk.
- The controlled full-benchmark comparison is chiefly against PaperCoder. AutoP2C and AutoReproduce are compared only on ten papers, limiting conclusions about relative performance against those systems.
- The ablation uses nine papers, one generated repository per paper–variant pair, and reused upstream artifacts where possible. This limits precision and may leave generation variance insufficiently characterized.
- Reference-free scores saturate near the top and sometimes favor ablations, indicating that those protocols may not discriminate method fidelity well (Section 4.4).
- PaperCompiler requires substantially more tokens than PaperCoder, so the fidelity gains are accompanied by higher computational cost; the evidence does not isolate every effect of added computation from architectural effects.

## Open research questions

1. **Can multimodal grounding preserve implementation details found only in figures and diagrams?** This remains open because the current system primarily consumes parsed text. A next experiment could compare text-only and multimodal grounding on papers annotated for figure-exclusive requirements.

2. **Do compiled specifications improve actual execution and result reproduction?** The reported evaluations do not establish end-to-end executability or recovery of published results. A next experiment could run generated repositories under fixed environments and measure test completion plus deviation from reported metrics.

3. **How robust are the gains across code models and generation seeds?** The controlled experiments use o3-mini, while the limitations explicitly exclude broad claims across model families and seeds. A factorial study across several backbones and repeated seeds could measure interaction effects and variance.

4. **How can interface mismatches be reduced without weakening method fidelity?** API/schema errors increase despite improvements elsewhere. An experiment could add interface-level static checks or execution tests and measure both schema failures and reference-based fidelity.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported results because the supplied text includes the complete methodology, tables, ablations, appendices, and stated limitations.

Material missing information includes statistical significance or confidence intervals; detailed judge prompts and aggregation rules beyond the excerpts; independent human evaluation; execution success rates; full reproduction of reported scientific results; repeated-generation variance; results across alternative model families; and evidence of public release for PaperCompiler’s code or intermediate artifacts.
