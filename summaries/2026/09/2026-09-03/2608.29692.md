# Portfolio Risk Bounds without Cross-Asset Return Covariances: Distributional Fields from Language-Model Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29692) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29692)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors derive a conditional upper bound on long-only portfolio variance from Wasserstein distances between firms’ news-embedding distributions and show descriptively that the resulting covariance-free allocation ranks near the lowest 1% of in-sample variance across four specified portfolio populations.

## Problem and motivation

Covariance-based portfolio optimization becomes unreliable when the return history is short relative to the number of assets, because the sample covariance matrix is noisy or singular and optimization is especially sensitive to poorly estimated directions. Existing shrinkage and factor approaches impose structure but still derive cross-asset information from joint returns.

The authors instead ask what observable firm information can rule out about portfolio risk before estimating cross-asset return covariances. Their identified gap is a portfolio-level mechanism that converts distributional separation between firms into a coherent risk restriction: pairwise covariance envelopes alone may correspond to mutually incompatible couplings and therefore cannot simply be combined into an \(n\)-asset portfolio model (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Conditional risk certificate.** Each firm is represented by the empirical distribution of its article embeddings. An antilipschitz common carrier and bounded firm-specific slack translate observed Wasserstein-2 separation into lower bounds on latent systematic-exposure separation. A single coherent joint exposure law then yields an upper bound on systematic portfolio variance (Theorems 1–3, §3).

2. **Sharp and tractable formulations.** Weighted multi-firm transport dispersion provides the sharp certificate, while a quadratic weighted-pairwise relaxation gives a computationally simpler decision objective. The sharp version preserves joint compatibility and applies slack at the aggregate level (§3.3).

3. **Allocation rule without cross-asset covariance.** The portfolio minimizes the certified variance cap using information geometry and, for raw capital weights, marginal volatility scales. Under zero firm-specific slack, the carrier scale changes the certified reduction but not the normalized risk-weight allocation (Corollary 1, §4).

4. **Descriptive empirical evaluation.** The zero-slack rule is constructed from frozen news embeddings and only afterward evaluated with the 2018–2022 return covariance. The paper compares it with capped random portfolios, equal risk weighting, and the ex post long-only GMV portfolio (§§5–6).

5. **Formal verification.** Displayed algebraic bounds and identities are machine-checked in a pinned Lean 4/mathlib v4.31.0 workspace, although the formalization does not validate the economic transmission assumptions or empirical proxy (Appendix A).

The evidence establishes that, for this fixed universe, period, representation, and set of comparison laws, the constructed allocation has unusually low conventionally measured in-sample variance. It also supports the internal mathematical validity of the certificate conditional on its assumptions.

It does not establish out-of-sample performance, identify the information-to-exposure mechanism, recover a covariance matrix, show that the method reaches GMV performance, or demonstrate superiority under portfolio populations other than those reported.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated—conditional identification:** The certificate relies on the carrier, slack, coherent-joint-law, and return-bridge restrictions. News text does not identify or calibrate these assumptions (§7).

- **Author-stated—in-sample evaluation:** Both information geometry and variance evaluation use 2018–2022 data. The expanding-cutoff analysis is not a prospective backtest (§§5.2, 5.4, 7).

- **Author-stated—representation dependence:** Article selection, model family, embedding width, input limits, and encoder vintage affect the geometry. The sensitivity results do not demonstrate representation invariance (§§5.5, 6.5, 7).

- **Author-stated—restricted comparison populations:** Percentiles depend on four stylized Dirichlet-capped laws and need not generalize to market-cap, factor-tilted, or historically observed allocation populations (§7).

- **Author-stated—external validity:** The 52-firm universe is a coverage-screened survivor panel rather than a changing historical index universe; news coverage is not shown to be comprehensive, and ticker assignment is not manually validated at article level (§§5.2, 7).

- **Author-stated—implementation gap:** The experiment standardizes assets so normalized risk and capital weights coincide. Practical capital implementation requires marginal volatility estimates, and this gap is not quantified (§7).

- **Analyst inference—limited calibration evidence:** Because \(L\), firm-specific slack, and the residual budget \(\delta\) are not empirically calibrated, the reported variance rankings evaluate the zero-slack allocation rather than the numerical tightness or coverage of the theoretical bound.

- **Analyst inference—missing mechanism ablations:** The cross-sector attribution does not establish whether embeddings add information beyond sectors. A sector-label or sector-neutral geometry ablation is not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Does the allocation retain low variance prospectively?**  
   This remains open because all reported return evaluation is in-sample. A next experiment could freeze the encoder, article cutoff, transport matrix, and weights at each rebalance date, then evaluate subsequent returns.

2. **Can the transmission assumptions be calibrated or tested?**  
   The text does not identify \(L\), firm-specific slack, or the return bridge. A next experiment could estimate exposure proxies from a separate period and assess whether observed embedding distances reliably lower-bound exposure distances.

3. **Which measurement choices drive useful geometry?**  
   Encoder and width results are non-monotonic, while ground-metric and cloud-construction alternatives are not reported. A prespecified factorial experiment could vary encoder, metric, cloud balancing, and article source with held-out return evaluation.

4. **Does the certificate add value to covariance estimation?**  
   The paper does not integrate its bound into shrinkage or constrained covariance estimators. A next experiment could impose certificate-derived constraints on a covariance estimator and compare out-of-sample risk with unconstrained shrinkage and factor baselines.
