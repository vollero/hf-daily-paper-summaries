# NeoMME: A Single-Tower Multimodal-Native Multilingual Foundation Encoder for Efficient Fine-Tuning and Inference

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01657) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01657)


## One-sentence takeaway

NeoMME replaces separate vision and language towers with a shared bidirectional encoder and, after retrieval fine-tuning, offers competitive visual-document retrieval at 260M–800M parameters alongside strong encoding and storage efficiency.

## Problem and motivation

Visual-document retrievers commonly inherit a pretrained vision tower and causal language decoder from generative vision-language models, creating parameter and compute overhead for non-generative representation tasks. The authors target a smaller, unified alternative that can encode multilingual text and raw images, support high-resolution documents and long contexts, and produce indexable retrieval representations through one backbone.

They identify a specific gap: among the reviewed systems, none combines continuous raw image patches, a vision-tower-free shared bidirectional Transformer trained from scratch, image-conditioned masked-text diffusion, and both dense and late-interaction visual-document retrieval (Sections 1 and 2.2, pp. 2–4).

## Approach and contributions

- **NeoMME backbone:** Two models—262.9M and 793.7M parameters—map text tokens and non-overlapping \(32\times32\) RGB patches through modality-specific projections into one shared bidirectional Transformer. Both support 16,384 tokens and dynamic-resolution images (Sections 3.2–3.3; Table 1, pp. 6–8).

- **Multimodal pretraining:** The models are trained from random initialization using masked discrete-diffusion text denoising. Image patches remain visible for multimodal samples, conditioning masked-text recovery; there is no image-reconstruction objective (Sections 4.1–4.3, pp. 9–11).

- **Dual-head retrieval adaptation:** NeoMME-Retriever jointly trains a 128-dimensional per-token late-interaction head and a mean-pooled dense head with Matryoshka widths. One backbone pass produces both representations (Sections 5.2 and 5.4, pp. 15–17).

- **Deployment-oriented efficiency:** Hierarchical token pooling and asymmetric quantization reduce late-interaction storage, while released fused Late-Interaction Kernels compute exact MaxSim without materializing the full similarity tensor (Sections 5.5 and 5.9, pp. 17, 20–21).

- **Released assets:** The authors report Apache-2.0 releases of pretrained and retrieval checkpoints, pre-decay checkpoints, Hugging Face Transformers support, and retrieval-compatible tooling (Abstract and Sections 1, 4.4, pp. 1–3, 12).

The evidence establishes that the released architecture can be adapted effectively to visual-document retrieval, that late interaction is important for the reported quality, and that encoding and stored-representation costs can be reduced under the tested protocols. It does not establish architectural superiority under matched pretraining data, compute, supervision, or evaluation code; nor does it demonstrate end-to-end search latency or quality on a unified corpus mixing text and images.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors explicitly identify:

- Pretraining scale is limited to about 524B packed tokens per model, including roughly 290B text-only tokens, versus about 2T text tokens reported for ModernBERT; accounting methods also differ (Section 7, p. 24).

- No objective directly predicts pixels, image tokens, or global image representations. Frozen natural-image classification is weak, averaging 13.2 accuracy across ten tasks (Sections 6.2 and 7, p. 24).

- Retrieval supervision is much smaller than for a cited state-of-the-art text retriever: about 430K sampled text-query and 850K image-query examples versus hundreds of millions of contrastive examples for mLateOn (Section 7, p. 24).

- Mixed text-and-page retrieval is trained for but not evaluated, and visual retrieval data covers substantially fewer languages than the text stream (Sections 5.3 and 7, pp. 16, 24–25).

- Major architecture, data, scale, and training decisions lack matched ablations; query-expansion tokens are also not isolated (Section 7, p. 25; Appendix A.3, p. 40).

- Web and generated data may carry social, geographic, topical, and linguistic biases; iterative text sampling has not received a dedicated safety evaluation (Section 7, p. 25).

Analyst inference: several headline comparisons mix results from the authors’ evaluation with MTEB or model reports, so differences may reflect harness or reporting variation. The authors partially mitigate this by aggregating the same eight public ViDoRe v3 tasks, but the comparisons are not controlled training studies (Sections 5.6–5.7, pp. 17–19).

Analyst inference: the efficiency results measure model-side encoding on deterministic square images rather than complete page ingestion, and the compression study excludes ANN routing, filesystem overhead, and optimized binary-scoring latency. Consequently, the reported gains should not be interpreted as end-to-end serving gains (Sections 5.9, D.1, and D.2, pp. 21, 49–51).

## Open research questions

1. **Which design choice drives the retrieval gains?** The paper does not isolate the shared tower, diffusion objective, patch size, attention schedule, or data mixture. A matched-compute factorial ablation could replace one component at a time and evaluate ViDoRe, BEIR, and image-sensitivity probes.

2. **Would a direct visual objective improve general visual representations without harming retrieval?** Weak frozen natural-image transfer may result from predicting only text. A controlled pretraining study could add masked-image, pixel-reconstruction, or image-level contrastive losses and compare frozen probes, fine-tuned image tasks, and visual-document retrieval.

3. **How well does one embedding space rank mixed text and page-image corpora?** The training mixture supports both document types, but this joint setting is unevaluated. A next experiment could build one candidate index containing text chunks and page screenshots and report modality-stratified recall and nDCG.

4. **Do compression gains survive complete large-corpus serving?** Current results omit routing, index construction, filesystem overhead, and optimized binary kernels. An end-to-end benchmark could measure recall, latency, throughput, memory, and storage across corpus sizes and pooling/quantization settings.
