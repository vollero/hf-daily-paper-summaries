# It Takes Two to Match: Co-Evolving Generative Retriever with Reinforcement Learning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00638) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00638)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoGR jointly trains query- and item-side LLMs to generate directly matchable keyword representations, achieving higher validation F1 than the tested sparse, dense, and generative baselines on two product-search datasets.

## Problem and motivation

First-stage retrieval must balance recall and precision because omitted relevant items cannot be recovered downstream, while irrelevant candidates burden later ranking. Existing LLM-assisted approaches generally improve only the query representation and still delegate matching to a separate retriever. The authors target the gap of jointly learning query- and item-side representations while retaining compatibility with inverted-index infrastructure (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Direct generative lexical retrieval:** Separate LLMs generate compact keyword sets for queries and items; overlap determines candidate retrieval, followed by BM25 ranking over generated keywords (Section 2.1).
- **Aligned initialization:** SFT item targets come from base-LLM keywords, while query targets are the most frequent keywords pooled from relevant items, creating initial cross-side overlap (Section 2.2; Algorithm 1).
- **Co-evolving RL:** Query and item generators are updated alternately with GRPO against the opposite side’s frozen index (Sections 2.3; Figure 2; Algorithm 2).
- **Shared but asymmetric rewards:** Query rollouts receive retrieval F1; item rollouts receive their counterfactual marginal effect on aggregate query-side F1, with efficient computation restricted to affected queries (Equations 2.2–2.3; Appendix B.3).
- **Empirical analysis:** The paper compares against ten baselines and studies training-design choices, alternating dynamics, keyword evolution, and added input information (Sections 4.1–4.5).

These results establish superiority within the tested validation settings and support the usefulness of co-training, SFT initialization, separate generators, and the marginal reward. They do not establish statistical significance, production impact, generalization beyond unseen queries in a fixed item universe, or superiority on broader document-retrieval domains.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Ranking remains BM25-based; stronger ranking methods are left for future work (Conclusion).
- **Author-stated:** Optimization targets relevance F1 rather than business outcomes such as irrelevant-ad rate or revenue (Conclusion).
- **Analyst inference:** Evaluation covers only two commercial-search datasets, and WANDS has just 50 evaluation queries; breadth and uncertainty are therefore limited (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** The internal gold labels come from an unspecified internal LLM judge, and both datasets treat unlabelled pairs as irrelevant, which may introduce label error (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Queries are split, but the complete item universe is retained across training and validation, so unseen-item generalization is not tested (Section 3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Appendix E says checkpoints are selected by validation F1 and results are reported on that validation set. Without a separate test set, this may yield optimistic estimates.
- **Analyst inference:** No confidence intervals, multiple-run variance, or significance tests are reported in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference:** Training requires two LLM generators and eight NVIDIA B200 GPUs; latency, index-update cost, and production-scale efficiency are not evaluated (Section 3.3; Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Code, trained checkpoints, and the internal dataset are not stated to be released, limiting full reproducibility.

## Open research questions

1. **Does CoGR generalize to new items or changing catalogs?** Only queries are held out while the item universe remains fixed. A next experiment could split by both queries and items or simulate catalog insertions after training.

2. **Are the gains robust across domains and random seeds?** Evidence comes from two commercial-search datasets without uncertainty estimates. Multi-seed experiments on additional public retrieval collections could report confidence intervals and significance tests.

3. **Can the marginal reward optimize real downstream utility?** The current objective is F1, while the authors identify revenue and irrelevant-ad rate as future targets. An offline replay or controlled online experiment could compare F1-trained and business-objective-trained variants.

4. **What is the quality–cost trade-off at production scale?** The paper gives training hardware and an efficient reward computation but not serving latency or index-refresh measurements. A scale study could vary catalog size, keyword budget, refresh frequency, and ranking method while measuring quality, memory, and latency.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported in-paper results.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, repeated-run results, a separate held-out test set, detailed internal-labeler validation, production latency and index costs, unseen-item evaluation, public release status, and empirical results for downstream business objectives.
