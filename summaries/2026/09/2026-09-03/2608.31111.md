# Aspire: Can Models Self-Evolve from Vague Goals?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-03.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31111) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31111)


## One-sentence takeaway

Aspire shows that LLM agents can operationalize vague capability goals and execute model- or harness-update loops, but above-base weight gains are rare and unstable, while all evolved harnesses underperform an engineered reference.

## Problem and motivation

Existing self-evolution systems generally receive a human-defined task, metric, and evaluator, leaving them to decide only how to optimize. The authors argue that autonomous learning must also determine what to improve and how to verify improvement from a broad natural-language goal.

Aspire targets this missing “goal operationalization” problem: agents must diagnose gaps, select or synthesize data, choose updates, build validation signals, and decide when to stop without seeing the downstream evaluation tasks. This matters because improvements on an agent-created proxy may not transfer to the intended capability (Introduction; Figure 1, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Problem formulation:** Formalizes vague-goal-driven self-evolution and target operationalization as jointly deciding what and how to optimize (Sections 1 and 3.1).
- **Benchmark:** Introduces Aspire, whose sealed evaluator contains 520 expert-authored items across six capability goals. Agents receive no item-level feedback and, depending on the protocol, either no intermediate score or bounded aggregate scores (Section 3.2; Figure 2; Table 3).
- **Unified evolution environment:** Supports both model-weight updates and harness edits through controlled data, SFT, GRPO, LoRA, validation, branching, verification, and rollback operations (Section 3.3; Figures 3; Table 1).
- **Empirical analysis:** Compares vague goals with explicit tasks, tests final-only and adaptive weight evolution, and evaluates one-step harness evolution while recording trajectories and provenance (Section 4).
- **Scope:** Aspire introduces a benchmark and environment, not a new training algorithm (Section 5).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Vague-goal systems scored lower in aggregate than explicit-task references. | Claude Opus 4.8: **27.07% vs. 32.90%** official reference; GPT-5.6: **29.58% vs. 36.23%**, weighted over seven PostTrainBench tasks. These are not prompt-only causal pairs. | Section 4.2; Figure 4a; Table 4 |
| Vague goals were associated with more deliberation and less active training work. | Across 48 matched Claude Opus 4.8 pairs: **+2,109 seconds** decision time, **+0.61 GPU-idle hours**, and **−1.27 active hours** per pair; LoRA use rose from **24.1% to 89.8%**. | Section 4.2; Figure 4b; Appendix B |
| Final-only weight evolution rarely beat the base model. | Across 24 runs, Qwen3.5-4B had **0/6** positive Avg@2 outcomes and Qwen3.5-9B had **1/6**. The sole positive pair raised scientific reasoning from **45.33 to 48.00**, or **+2.67 points**; only **3/24** individual checkpoints exceeded base. | Section 4.3; Figure 5; Table 5 |
| Adaptive search usually produced checkpoints but almost never retained improvement. | Of 30 configuration–goal cells, **28** produced an evaluated checkpoint, **21** an eligible checkpoint, and **1** a retained improvement. Terra raised Qwen3.5-4B mathematics from **17.86 to 20.10**. | Section 4.3; Figures 6–7; Appendix C.4 |
| Local progress frequently remained below the base model. | Among 62 consecutive transitions in 22 cells, **28 rose, 13 tied, and 21 fell**. Qwen3.5-4B mathematics rose **0.79→1.75→2.38**, still far below the **17.86** base. | Section 4.3; Figure 7b–c |
| Numeric-label training coincided with output-format collapse. | Five final checkpoints trained on **21,000 single-digit targets** emitted single digits for all **279** corresponding evaluation outputs; scores were **0, 0, 0, 6.141, and 0**. The authors explicitly do not claim causality. | Section 4.3; Appendix C.3 |
| Every valid evolved harness underperformed Qwen-Agent. | Qwen-Agent scored **28.64 task macro / 27.65 example micro**; successors scored Luna **19.32/18.33**, Terra **20.76/20.14**, and Sol **27.22/25.97**. Each frozen harness was executed three times on the same 20 writing items. | Section 4.4; Table 2 |
| Narrow self-validation did not predict hidden-set performance. | Luna improved its own checklist from **7/8 to 8/8**, yet obtained a **19.32** task-macro hidden score versus the reference’s **28.64**. | Section 4.4; Appendix D |

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that completing an autonomous update loop is substantially easier than obtaining a retained, target-aligned gain. It also documents proxy mismatch, regression, and checkpoint-selection failures.

It does **not** demonstrate general impossibility of vague-goal self-improvement. The retained adaptive gain was selected using repeated aggregate feedback on the same evaluation slice, without a separate confirmation slice; adaptive cells have one run each; harness differences lack reported per-execution dispersion; and most evaluations cover only the targeted goal rather than preservation of unrelated capabilities.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Results are bounded to six goals and the coverage and scoring quality of their expert-authored items (Conclusion).
- The adaptive experiment has one canonical run per configuration–goal cell, so it is not a multi-seed estimate (Conclusion; Appendix C.6).
- Content-bearing traces are available only under controlled access; the portable artifact omits raw messages, outputs, hidden items, and per-item judge reasons (Appendix C.5).
- Repeated adaptive scores use the same fixed evaluation slice, so the sole retained gain is feedback-guided selection rather than independently replicated transfer (Section 4.3).
- RQ3 tests only one-step harness generation on one goal, not recursive harness evolution; execution dispersion is unreported (Section 4.4; Table 2).
- The composite logic/reliability/instruction-following goal does not identify its three components separately (Section 3.2).
- “OOD” means independently authored and excluded from agent training and validation, not necessarily absent from model pretraining (Section 3.2).
- Goal-specific evaluations do not assess preservation of unrelated capabilities (Section 4.3).

**Analyst inferences**

- Causal attribution is limited because RQ1 reference systems may differ in metadata beyond the goal contract, as the authors acknowledge.
- Comparisons between decision-model scales are confounded by simultaneous changes in decision model, base model, and base score.
- Repeated evaluation on fixed adaptive slices creates selection-overfitting risk even without item-level access.
- Reproducibility cannot be fully assessed from the supplied text because complete action schemas, evaluator artifacts, raw trajectories, and per-run harness variance are unavailable here.

## Open research questions

1. **Can gains selected through aggregate feedback generalize to a fresh hidden slice?**  
   The only retained adaptive gain lacks an independent confirmation set. A next experiment could select checkpoints on one sealed slice and evaluate once on a separately authored, untouched slice.

2. **Which operationalization choices cause improvement or regression?**  
   Current trajectory evidence is descriptive, and agents change data, algorithms, and hyperparameters together. A factorial ablation could independently vary proxy-data alignment, update method, validation breadth, and stopping policy.

3. **Can repeated self-evolution retain both target and unrelated capabilities?**  
   Reported runs keep the decision model fixed and evaluate mainly one goal. A multi-round experiment could promote descendants while periodically measuring all six goals plus unrelated control tasks.

4. **Does diverse self-validation improve harness transfer?**  
   Luna’s narrow checklist encouraged proxy specialization, while Sol’s broader testing still did not beat the reference. A controlled study could compare narrow versus stratified validation suites across multiple harness-creation seeds and report execution variance.
