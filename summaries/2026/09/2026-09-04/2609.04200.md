# Principia: Relational Physics Tests for Video Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04200) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04200)


## One-sentence takeaway
Principia evaluates paired-object physical relationships across eight Newtonian phenomena, finding a best generator score of 0.419 and best VLM violation-detection agreement of 0.661, subject to measurement and protocol ambiguities (Tables 3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Visually convincing motion can violate physics, while absolute measurements in generated videos depend on potentially unknown scale, timing, and camera calibration. The authors propose evaluating relationships between objects within the same scene instead. They identify a gap between subjective plausibility judgments, matching a single reference trajectory, and quantitative tests spanning multiple physical laws (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled relational benchmark:** Principia extends a prior two-object gravity protocol to eight phenomena covering translational, rotational, collisional, and oscillatory dynamics. The final conditioning set contains 401 recorded scenes plus 128 appearance augmentations, totaling 529 (§2; Appendix A.1).
- **Image-space consistency metric:** SAM3 trajectories supply event times and displacements. For quantities expected to agree, the score is \(S=1-|F_1-F_2|/(|F_1|+|F_2|)\); projectile and momentum tests instead score correct distance ordering. These measurements avoid absolute metric calibration under the specified experimental assumptions (§4.1; Appendix A.2).
- **Synthetic violation testbed:** Principia-Synth uses Isaac Sim to construct physically consistent and deliberately inconsistent videos for VLM PASS/FAIL evaluation (§3.2; Appendix B).
- **Comparative evaluation:** The authors test six generators and four VLMs. They state that first-frame inputs and scenario prompts are released, while full recorded videos are withheld (§§4–5).

These results establish failures on the tested relational constraints. They do not establish complete physical understanding when a model passes, statistical independence between visual quality and physics, or architectural causes of failure. Generator consistency scores and VLM classification agreement measure different tasks and are not directly comparable.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** The benchmark excludes fluids, soft-body deformation, and thermodynamics. Hallucinations, missing objects, and severe deformation are not automatically separated from physical violations (§5).
- **Protocol ambiguity:** §4.1 describes filtering nonconforming videos before scoring, whereas Appendix A.6 assigns videos below the directional-consistency threshold a score of zero. Failure rates and their contribution to overall scores are unavailable.
- **Analyst inference—measurement validity:** The spring invariant concerns equilibrium extension, but Appendix A.2 measures the first oscillation’s maximum extension and estimates mass from cube dimensions. The supplied text does not reconcile these choices or quantify their error.
- **Analyst inference—camera claims exceed validation:** Calibration independence does not establish robustness to arbitrary camera geometry or movement. Appendix A.8 acknowledges static-camera requirements, and Table 6 shows sensitivity beyond the blanket “unaffected” description.
- **VLM evaluation uncertainty:** §4.4 describes mixed real and synthetic inputs, while Appendix B emphasizes Principia-Synth. Sample counts, class balance, and uncertainty estimates are absent, limiting interpretation of “near chance.”
- **Reporting and reproducibility:** Appendix A.7 prose gives some scaling changes inconsistent with Table 3/Figure 7. Compute totals also differ between §5 and Appendix A.5. Withheld real videos constrain independent validation. The authors’ contamination-robustness claim is not experimentally tested.

## Open research questions
- **How much generator failure comes from relational errors versus basic motion or tracking failures?** The reported scores do not separate these sources. Next experiment: manually audit a stratified sample and report directional failures, tracking errors, and relational scores separately.
- **How reliable are the measurements under controlled perturbations?** Camera sensitivity and the spring measurement mismatch remain unresolved. Next experiment: compare extracted scores with known simulator quantities while varying camera transforms, release conditions, and spring measurement definitions.
- **Do VLMs fail at temporal perception or applying physical relationships?** Binary judgments alone cannot distinguish these causes. Next experiment: compare full-video input with tracked trajectories and explicit measured quantities on the same balanced scenes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The main tables clearly support weak performance on these tests, but interpretation is limited by protocol inconsistencies.

Missing material information includes VLM dataset size and class balance, model-specific failure counts, complete seed allocation, tracking/event-detection accuracy, uncertainty on model comparisons, exact VBench evaluation details, and confirmed availability of scoring code and synthetic assets.
