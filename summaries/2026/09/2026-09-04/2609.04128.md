# Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04128) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04128)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors’ off-policy environment evolution pipeline and lineage curriculum outperform implemented co-evolution and ensemble baselines on Terminal-Bench 2.1 for two Qwen models, although broader generalization and compute efficiency remain unestablished (§5.5).

## Problem and motivation
Terminal-agent RL needs executable tasks whose verification outcomes distinguish successful from unsuccessful trajectories. The authors argue that tasks synthesized from scratch become too easy, while environments generated from a training agent’s failures depend on that agent’s weaknesses and eventually provide fewer learning signals. They propose increasing task difficulty independently of target-agent rollouts (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Difficulty formulation:** Decompose trajectory difficulty into execution length, scenario novelty, and skill rarity. Replace model-dependent probabilities with a reference distribution grounded in broad world knowledge; the authors suggest estimating it through web research (§3).
- **Verified environment evolution:** A proposer edits a scenario–skill sequence, a reviewer checks the plan, and a modifier updates the environment. Acceptance requires reference-solution success, no-op failure, and quality checks. Mutation effort controls edit scope; rejected directions trigger fallback to another direction (§4.1).
- **Lineage curriculum:** The Evolution-Lineage scheduler presents earlier environments first, advancing when success exceeds six of eight rollouts. It supports GRPO training after rejection-sampling fine-tuning (§§4.2, 5.4).

These results support the combined pipeline in the reported terminal setting. Comparisons hold synthesis model and environment count constant (§5.5), but do not establish equal total compute. Peak-checkpoint results and early scheduler dynamics do not isolate every component’s contribution or demonstrate universal model-independent difficulty.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated measurement limit:** Zero observed pass rates prevent reliable assessment of additional difficulty, motivating the generation cap (§5.2).
- **Author-stated incomplete scope:** SWE agents, computer-use agents, and settings where the same model constructs and learns from environments remain future work (§6; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—partial policy independence:** Evolution avoids target-agent rollouts, but seed selection uses Claude Opus 5 pass rates and turn counts. The complete pipeline therefore retains model-dependent filtering (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference—difficulty proxy uncertainty:** The reference distribution is conceptual; a reproducible estimator and calibration against observed difficulty are not supplied. Longer realized trajectories need not uniquely measure intrinsic task difficulty (§§3, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference—attribution and uncertainty:** Direction ablations measure environment difficulty rather than downstream benchmark gains. Scheduler comparisons cover early training only, and benchmark uncertainty intervals and independent training repetitions are not reported (§§5.3–5.5).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Exact prompts, rubrics, repair budgets, total training-environment count, and construction/training costs are unavailable. Oracle success and no-op failure alone cannot establish comprehensive verifier correctness (§§4.1, 5.5).

## Open research questions
- **Does difficulty transfer beyond the tested model set?** Cross-model rollout evidence is limited to three evaluators. Next experiment: test fixed lineages on additional model families and compare difficulty rankings across generations.
- **Which components drive benchmark gains?** Existing ablations do not separate each evolution direction’s downstream benefit from scheduling. Next experiment: compare direction-specific and mixed lineages with and without the scheduler under matched compute.
- **Does evolution improve learning per unit cost?** Environment count is controlled, but total cost is unreported. Next experiment: compare benchmark gains at matched synthesis-plus-training budgets.
- **Can evolution remain useful beyond observed zero-pass generations?** Pass-rate measurement saturates (§5.2). Next experiment: test stronger solvers and reference-solution execution measures on those generations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the method description and reported benchmark comparisons, but causal and generalization claims have limited support.

Material missing information includes exact headline-gain baselines, statistical uncertainty, independent training repetitions, complete implementation and RL hyperparameters, compute costs, detailed seed composition, reference-distribution estimation, and public availability of code, environments, or checkpoints.
