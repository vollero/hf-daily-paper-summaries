# Why Gated DeltaNet Survives 4-Bit Quantization: NVFP4 W4A4 for the Recurrent Half of a Hybrid 27B LLM

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04098) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04098)


## One-sentence takeaway
On Qwen3.8-27B, the authors report that NVFP4 quantization of all backbone linear projections preserves measured task accuracy while reducing memory and prefill time, with replay experiments showing that Gated DeltaNet’s gates and recurrence suppress quantization error (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
Public quantizations examined by the authors protect Gated DeltaNet (GDN) projections with higher precision, motivated by possible recurrent error accumulation. Since GDN occupies most layers in this hybrid model, those exemptions leave substantial efficiency gains unrealized. The paper investigates both whether GDN can tolerate W4A4 and why it does so (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Calibration-only quantization:** Minima applies NVFP4 W4A4 to all 496 backbone linear projections, including GDN gates, using 128 calibration samples of 32K tokens. Embeddings, the language-model head, convolutions, norms, and gate parameters remain higher precision (§3; Appendix B.4).
- **Mechanistic analysis:** Captured-activation replay, isolated projection quantization, FP32 recurrence experiments, and positional loss analysis support an explanation involving local block scaling, gate nonlinearities, and corrective state updates (§5).
- **Serving corrections:** The authors repair incompatible scales across fused projections and identify serving-path and prompt-template confounds (§6).
- **Calibrated KV cache:** Static FP8 KV scales reduce long-context perplexity loss without a measurable throughput cost; the authors identify Minima+scales as the released checkpoint (§§3, 7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Task accuracy is broadly retained | Authors report Minima’s five-task average is 0.52 percentage points below BF16 and no task comparison is CI-separated; AIME and GPQA use four seeds, LiveCodeBench one. | Abstract; §3; §4, Table 1 caption |
| Smaller weights and faster prefill | Minima uses 17.5 GiB of weights, reported as 2.9× smaller than BF16; single-request 32K TTFT falls from 6.90 s to 4.03 s on one RTX PRO 6000, TP=1, FP8 KV. | Abstract; §§3–4, 8 |
| Efficiency gains depend on phase | At 8K, prompt throughput exceeds Unsloth/RadixArk by 14–19%; decode trails RadixArk by 2–4%. | §§4, 9 |
| Quantization still increases perplexity | WikiText-2 Minima–BF16 PPL gaps are +0.72 at 4K and +0.49 inside a 32K request, under the common FP8-KV regime. | §§3–4 |
| Gates have low output sensitivity | Quantizing only gate projection a or b gives median layer-output errors of 2.1% and 2.6%, despite respective GEMM errors of 11.0% and 8.5%; 96 layer/sequence replays, 8K tokens each. | §5.2; Table 3 caption |
| Recurrent error plateaus | Across five layers in 32K FP32 lockstep runs, full-Minima relative state error is 12.96% at token 256 and 12.31% at token 32,768. | §5.3; Figure 1 |
| Positional loss gap decreases | With matched FP8 KV, Minima–BF16 mean NLL difference falls from +0.081 nats in the first half of the 32K window to +0.011 in the second. | §5.4; Appendix B.1 |
| KV calibration recovers most of its penalty | Minima PPL@32K improves from 10.84 to 10.50, recovering 83% of the +0.41 uncalibrated FP8-KV penalty; throughput differs by at most 0.4%. | §7 |
| Retrieval remains saturated | All four compared models score 100% on RULER single- and multikey retrieval at 32K and 64K. | Table 1 caption |

These results support useful quantization tolerance under the tested configuration, alongside a plausible architectural explanation. They do not establish statistical equivalence, universal recurrent stability, or superiority on perplexity. The recurrence experiments isolate injected quantization effects using fake quantization and FP32 trajectories; they do not reproduce every aspect of native serving arithmetic (§5; Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One model family and size, one quantization format, perplexity through 32K, and retrieval through 64K; behavior at 128K or beyond is extrapolation (§9).
- **Author-stated evaluation gap:** Minima+scales inherits Minima’s task scores; only perplexity, retrieval, and throughput were re-measured. The concurrent QUASAR QAT checkpoint was not benchmarked (§9).
- **Author-stated architectural dependency:** Gate protection depends on the softplus/exponential parameterization; direct noise on the decay coefficient can cause substantial state error (§§5.3, 9).
- **Analyst inference:** Community recipes differ in attention, MLP, and head precision as well as GDN precision, so whole-model comparisons do not isolate the efficiency contribution of GDN alone (§3; Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Saturated retrieval scores and sparse seed coverage limit sensitivity to small regressions. WikiText’s reported context inversion also restricts interpretation of shrinking positional loss gaps (§§4, 6; Appendix B.4).

## Open research questions
1. **Does stability persist beyond the measured windows?** Longer contexts remain untested (§9). Extend lockstep analysis and end-to-end retrieval/perplexity to 128K and beyond.
2. **Does gate protection generalize across architectures?** The evidence covers one parameterization and model (§9). Repeat projection replay on other recurrent mixers, including linearly parameterized decay gates.
3. **What is GDN quantization’s isolated contribution?** Compared checkpoints differ in several precision choices (Appendix B.4). Hold all other tensors and serving settings fixed while changing only GDN precision.
4. **Does the released scaled checkpoint preserve task accuracy directly?** Its task scores were inherited (§9). Re-run the complete matched-seed task suite on Minima+scales.

## Confidence and missing information
