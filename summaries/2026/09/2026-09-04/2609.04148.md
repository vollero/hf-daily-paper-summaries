# Terminal-Universe: Turning Agent Trajectories into Scalable Terminal Environments

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04148) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04148)


## One-sentence takeaway
Terminal-Universe reconstructs reusable workspaces from recorded agent trajectories and synthesizes verified training interactions, improving Qwen3.5-27B by 11.9 percentage points on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 13.8 points on EvoCode-Bench v2 MT@4 under the reported settings (§5, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Recorded terminal-agent trajectories are plentiful, but executable environments for generating and checking further training interactions remain scarce. The authors identify an underused resource: file operations in existing trajectories reveal workspace contents that can support reconstruction without the original repository. Unlike a fixed demonstration, a recovered environment can support new tasks, stronger solver rollouts, and executable verification (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory-based reconstruction:** Replay observed file operations to recover pre-edit contents, exclude agent-created solution files, and use an agent to supply missing context. A separate read-only judgment filters for task sufficiency; reconstruction remains approximate (§3.1).
- **Environment reuse:** Recover original user intent or generate new single-workspace tasks. Cross-workspace synthesis mines directional capability gaps and pairs a writable target with a read-only reference (§3.2).
- **Interactive task expansion:** A simulated user extends sessions, requests corrections, and changes requirements while tracking active constraints. Private round-level tests guide subsequent feedback (§3.2).
- **Verified supervision:** Agent-authored tests filter Single-WS and Cross-WS solutions. Multi-Round records retain recovered intermediate failures but trim trailing failed rounds; Intent Recovery remains unfiltered. The final mixture excludes Intent Recovery (§3.3, §4.3, Appendix D).

The evidence supports improved benchmark performance for the tested student and useful contributions from completion and interactive supervision. MT@4 credits consecutive successful rounds reached by any of four runs; it is not single-run session success. These experiments do not establish exact reconstruction fidelity or equal-compute superiority over other synthesis methods.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Reconstruction cannot recover unseen project-specific content reliably; standardized containers may mishandle specialized dependencies. Source trajectories bound language and domain coverage (§3.1, Appendix B.2, §7).
- **Author-stated:** One teacher generates tasks, solutions, and verifiers, creating correlated capability gaps and potentially shared mistakes (§7).
- **Analyst inference:** “Task-sufficient” should not be interpreted as independently validated executability. The judge checks project context rather than build success. Manual inspection found unnecessary substantial additions in eight of 30 completions and no solutions in that sample; this leaves population-level fidelity and leakage uncertain (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Attribution is incomplete: re-solving changes the teacher policy, and breadth/depth additions increase record counts. Cross-method comparisons also vary base models, scaffolds, and inference settings (§6.1, Tables 7–9, Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is limited to one fine-tuned base model and the reported terminal/coding benchmarks. Workspace composition is heavily Python-dominated, and the Full Mixture does not outperform every specialized mixture on every metric (Figure 5, Tables 3, 7, 9).

## Open research questions
1. **How faithful are completed environments?** Context judgments and the small manual audit leave this unresolved. Compare reconstructions against available original snapshots using build checks, behavior tests, and independent solution-leakage review.
2. **Would independent verifiers improve supervision?** Shared teacher errors remain an acknowledged risk. Hold tasks and solutions fixed, then compare same-teacher tests with independently authored tests and a human-audited subset.
3. **Do breadth and depth gains survive equal token or compute budgets?** Mixture experiments add records, and interaction lengths differ substantially. Compare Single-WS, Cross-WS, and Multi-Round allocations under matched training tokens and generation cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and within-paper comparisons; more limited in fidelity and broad generalization.

Material missing information includes public artifact availability, total generation and training cost, independent verifier accuracy, human calibration of sufficiency judgments, statistical significance tests, and an explicit EvoCode-specific contamination procedure. Some extracted tables also leave the meaning of uncertainty annotations unclear.
