# Last Translation Benchmark

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04173) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04173)


## One-sentence takeaway

LTB pairs difficult human-contributed translations with example-specific verification rules, exposing substantial model failures and producing rankings more consistent with human judgments than generic evaluation methods on its selected challenge set (§§3.2–3.3).

## Problem and motivation

The authors argue that standard translation benchmarks are approaching saturation, while automatic metrics and generic LLM judges miss consequential errors; human evaluation adds cost, subjectivity, and reproducibility problems. They seek a challenging benchmark with interpretable success criteria that identifies specific weaknesses rather than measuring average translation quality (§§1, 1.3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Live challenge benchmark:** LTBv1 contains 3,456 accepted examples across 109 languages, including text and multimodal inputs. LTBv1-eval selects 911 text-only examples for difficulty, output diversity, and language-pair balance (§3).
- **Example-specific verification:** Contributors supply a reference translation and handcrafted rules. An LLM checks each rule separately; an example passes only if every rule passes. Acceptance requires a passing human reference, at most two passing automatic translations, and review by a contributor fluent in the relevant languages (§2).
- **Difficulty taxonomy:** Linguists inductively develop a multilabel taxonomy of input-side difficulties, then an LLM annotates the full dataset. Categories cover linguistic phenomena, cultural knowledge, constraints, and model-output blockers (§3.4).
- **Evaluation and expansion studies:** The paper compares verification with generic judges, metrics, and human assessment; tests rule-assisted translation; and pilots transferring examples to new target languages (§§3.2–3.3; Appendix C).

| Leading models leave substantial headroom | On LTBv1-eval, using Gemma 4 verification, all-rules pass rates are 43.8% for Gemini 3.1 Pro, 34.4% for GPT-5.6 Sol, 2.9% for Google Translate, and 99.1% for contributor references. | Verification yields more consistent rankings | Average within-method Kendall τb is reported as 86.9 for verifiers, 71.3 for generic judges, and 35.1 for metrics, on the table’s displayed scale. | Independent human scoring favors contributor references | Across 317 examples, 19 language pairs, and 22 annotators, reference quality scores rise from 81.4 without rules to 90.8 with rules; Gemini 3.1 Pro scores fall from 77.0 to 68.1. | Verification reduces most measured self-preference | Gemma 4 self-bias is 8.9% under verification versus 28.2% under generic judging. Gemini 3.1 Pro remains self-disfavoring: −8.9% versus −8.3%. | §3.3, Table 5 |
| Automatic transfer still needs review | A pilot transfers 100 source examples across five target languages. Automatic filters retain 48%; manual review of retained examples reports 64.1% acceptable, 29.4% rejected, and 6.5% uncertain. | Appendix C, Table 7 |

The evidence supports targeted failure detection and stronger ranking agreement within this benchmark. It does not establish average real-world translation quality, comprehensive correctness of passing translations, or human-level translation ability. The rule-assisted result uses privileged information that may reveal the required interpretation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Examples deliberately target weaknesses; rules and taxonomy labels are incomplete measures of translation quality (§§1.3, 3.4).
- **Coverage:** The full dataset is 94% textual and 73% non-English→English; the main evaluation is text-only. Multimodal examples establish breadth of cases, not aggregate multimodal performance (§3; Main Table 2).
- **Reference and evaluation dependence:** Contributors author both references and rules. Independent human evaluation partially addresses this, but the authors acknowledge differing judgments about rule importance (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison risk:** Excluding unsupported languages can give models different evaluation subsets, complicating direct ranking comparisons (Main Table 2 caption).
- **Analyst inference—validation gaps:** Ranking agreement does not establish per-rule accuracy. The supplied text provides no confidence intervals, rule-level verifier error analysis, or agreement measurements validating the full LLM-generated taxonomy.
- **Author-stated realism tension:** Some submissions are unusual, deliberately confusing constructions reflecting the collection process’s gamified nature (§3.4).

## Open research questions

- **Can models discover the crucial constraints themselves?** Synthetic rules remain far below human rules in Table 3. A next experiment could compare failure-specific rule discovery with matched-budget extra reasoning on unseen examples.
- **How robust are individual verification decisions?** Ranking stability leaves rule-level mistakes unresolved. Independently adjudicate a stratified sample of passes and failures across languages, phenomena, and verifiers.
- **Do findings extend beyond short text?** The main evaluation excludes multimodal inputs. Construct balanced audio, image, video, and longer-text subsets, then repeat verifier and human comparisons.
- **Which difficulties transfer reliably across languages?** Appendix C finds target-dependent invalid rules. Test taxonomy-conditioned transfer with independent review and explicit checks for target-language applicability.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the benchmark design and reported comparisons, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing details include exact Table 3 aggregation, per-model evaluated sample counts, detailed evaluation-subset selection, decoding and verifier settings, uncertainty estimates, annotation agreement, taxonomy-label validation, and aggregate multimodal results. Original audiovisual inputs are also unavailable in the text extraction.
