# Compile by Training: Turning Natural-Language Specifications into Local Neural Functions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04199) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04199)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors refine PAW-generated adapters using teacher-synthesized examples, raising semantic accuracy on FuzzyBench-Hard from 22.4% to 83.6% while increasing reported compilation time from 3.5 to 50.9 seconds (§5.2).

## Problem and motivation
Recurring text functions can be difficult to implement with rules, while repeated remote-model calls incur latency, cost, and provider dependence. PAW already compiles specifications into adapters, but its one-pass compiler allocates fixed computation to every function; the authors investigate whether specification-specific training improves correctness while preserving reusable local execution (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Specification-specific compilation:** Teachers generate input–output examples in validated JSON batches. Training refines a PAW-initialized LoRA adapter for a frozen Qwen3-0.6B interpreter; the packaged program contains the adapter, runtime prompt, specification, and interpreter metadata (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Interactive build service:** Teacher synthesis, model loading, and training overlap. Persistent jobs, GPU workers, cached supervision, and artifact storage support background compilation (§4).
- **Evaluation and composition demonstrations:** The paper compares correctness against PAW’s fast compiler and demonstrates website assistance, language-to-action programs, and bidirectional prose-style rewriting (§§5–6).

The contribution is this compilation workflow and its deployment; synthetic supervision, LoRA, and PAW’s program representation are inherited techniques (§7).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher benchmark correctness | Mean LEM rises from 0.224 for amortized PAW to 0.836 for compile by training: +0.612 absolute on FuzzyBench-Hard. | §5.2; Fig. 4 |
| Longer compilation | Reported comparison: 50.9 seconds versus 3.5 seconds for fast PAW. A cold compile of one representative specification takes 50.9 seconds on B300, 68.2 on H200, and 99.2 on an unspecified RTX GPU. | §§5.2, 5.4 |
| Mixed teachers improve development results | With 3,600 unique pairs expanded to 6,400 examples, mean LEM is 0.746 for mini-only supervision versus 0.851 for a 2:1 mini/GPT-5.5 mix; batch 64, learning rate 2×10⁻⁴, 100 steps. | Table 1; Appendix C |
| More unique supervision can help | Separate development sweep: mean LEM is 0.821/0.836/0.836/0.866 for 1,440/2,400/3,600/7,200 unique pairs; batch 48, learning rate 2×10⁻⁴, 100 steps. | Table 1; Appendix C |
| Grader agrees with author labels | GPT-5.5 judge accuracy is 0.977 and Cohen’s κ is 0.946 against 128 author labels. | §5.1; Table 3 |
| Limited concurrency evidence | Four concurrent compile jobs all complete, with mean queue wait of 1.01 seconds. | §5.4 |
| Application feasibility | Avatar program matches expected action structure on 43 of 44 hand-authored instructions; website helper deploys 30 programs, including 28 active routing nodes. | §§6.1–6.2 |

These results support improved judge-assessed correctness on the selected benchmark and demonstrate executable composition. They do not establish general benchmark superiority, guaranteed correctness, or measured runtime cost savings. Deployment activity demonstrates use, rather than independently measured application quality.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Synthetic supervision can inherit teacher errors; correctness-critical applications need validation or deterministic control paths. Systematic user studies remain future work (§9).
- **Analyst inference:** Selecting specifications where fast PAW has no exact matches limits generalization of the reported advantage to broader tasks (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** GPT-5.5 supplies supervision and judges outputs. The author-label validation supports grader reliability on its sample, but does not eliminate potential evaluation dependence (§3.1; Appendices A–B).
- **Analyst inference:** No warm-start ablation isolates initialization’s contribution, and no sequential-versus-overlapped timing comparison quantifies the streaming design’s benefit. Latency and load evidence cover limited settings (§§4–5).

## Open research questions
- **Does the gain extend beyond baseline failures?** The main benchmark is selectively constructed. Evaluate both compilers on the full benchmark with per-task results and uncertainty estimates.
- **How much does PAW initialization contribute?** Its effect is not isolated. Compare PAW initialization with standard LoRA initialization under identical supervision and training budgets.
- **When does compilation repay its upfront expense?** Runtime latency, memory, monetary cost, and invocation break-even points are unreported. Measure these against repeated remote calls across representative workloads.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported comparisons are clear, but their broader validity is uncertain.

Material missing information includes benchmark and development-set sizes, task composition and split relationships, repeated-run uncertainty, per-task outcomes, independent application-quality evaluation, runtime resource measurements, monetary costs, exact RTX hardware, and a fully specified public optimization recipe.
