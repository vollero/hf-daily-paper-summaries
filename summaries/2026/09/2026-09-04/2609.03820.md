# Select, Compress, Reinvest: A Controlled Study of Visual-Token Allocation in Long-Video MLLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03820) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03820)


## One-sentence takeaway
In matched long-video QA experiments, query-aware frame selection improves accuracy, and reinvesting spatial compression savings in additional frames yields further gains at audited equal-or-lower answerer token cost on LongVideoBench’s long bins (§§5.1–5.4).

## Problem and motivation
Long-video models must answer questions from a small subset of available frames. The authors ask whether limited visual tokens should buy better timestamps, sharper frames, or more temporal coverage. Prior systems change scorers, selection rules, resolution policies, and answering pipelines together, making their individual contributions difficult to identify (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled selection comparison:** Six training-free rules share cached LongCLIP embeddings from a 1 fps pool, question-stem-only scoring, and an answering harness. Unmodified Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) selects frames correlated with the remaining query residual while suppressing previously covered directions (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Allocation decomposition:** Paired experiments separately change timestamps, compress fixed timestamps, and reinvest savings in more frames. The contribution is controlled evidence and token auditing, rather than a new pursuit algorithm or the first observation that additional compressed frames can help (§§3.4, 5.4).
- **Robustness and evaluation diagnostics:** Scorer swaps, answerer transfers, residual analysis, and cross-selector frame-overlap checks expose generalization limits and an implementation error hidden by aggregate accuracy (§§5–6).

## Evidence and main results
Accuracy changes below are percentage points (pp).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| OMP improves equal-frame-budget accuracy | Qwen3-VL-8B, eight frames: OMP versus uniform accuracy is .6223 versus .5654 on LongVideoBench; .6222 versus .5637 on Video-MME; .4635 versus .3454 on LVBench. Gains: 5.69, 5.85, and 11.81 pp. | Table 2 |
| Selection can substitute for frame count | LongVideoBench 3600 s bin: OMP-8 reaches .5461 versus uniform-16 at .4770, +6.9 pp, p=.0011. The comparison excludes selection preprocessing cost. | §5.1 |
| Classical OMP is competitive with the strongest matched selector | LDDR stage-1 accuracy differs from OMP by +0.97 pp on LongVideoBench, −0.29 on Video-MME, and +0.58 on LVBench, with Qwen at eight frames. | Table 2 |
| Fixed-timestamp compression has small observed effects | Qwen aggregate accuracy changes are +0.30, −0.44, and +0.39 pp on LongVideoBench, Video-MME, and LVBench. On pooled LongVideoBench long bins, n=976, the difference is +0.72 pp, 90% CI [−0.60,+2.03]; post-hoc equivalence passes ±3 pp but misses ±2 pp. | Table 4; §5.3 |
| Reinvestment improves accuracy with audited token cost | Qwen, pooled LongVideoBench long bins: eight full-resolution OMP frames versus sixteen compressed frames gives .5820 versus .6055, +2.36 pp, p=.0346. Token ratios are 0.996 and 0.984 in the 600 s and 3600 s bins. | §3.4; Table 5; Figure 4 |
| Scorer robustness is encouraging but bounded | On LongVideoBench-600 s, n=412, uniform < top-k < OMP under both LongCLIP and SigLIP. OMP’s scorer difference is +0.97 pp, p=.73; its approximate 90% interval spans [−3.6,+5.6] pp. | §5.2; Table 3 |
| Answerer transfer depends on benchmark | InternVL3-8B gains from OMP versus uniform are +0.11 pp on long Video-MME, p=1.000, but +10.14 pp on LVBench, p=1.2×10⁻¹³, using identical timestamps and tile budgets across selector arms. | Table 6 |

These results support within-harness benefits from selection and reinvestment. They do not establish end-to-end compute savings, universal scorer invariance, or an OMP-specific reinvestment advantage. The authors’ ranking of allocation decisions synthesizes separate contrasts rather than a complete factorial experiment (§§1, 5.1–5.4).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated comparison boundaries:** AKS uses a substituted scorer; FOCUS⋆ tests a schedule replay rather than its original online scoring pipeline; LDDR-select tests stage 1 only. MDP3 and Q-Frame are absent (§§3.3, 7).
- **Author-stated statistical limits:** Equivalence margins are post hoc, tests lack multiplicity correction, and the selector-by-budget interaction cannot resolve effects near one percentage point. Small compression differences outside the tested pooled settings are not equivalence evidence (§7).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** Token parity is audited only for the LongVideoBench long bins. Selection preprocessing cost is unmeasured; subtitles are disabled; scorer and mechanism checks cover limited settings (§§3.4, 5.1, 7).
- **Exploratory mechanism evidence:** Residual geometry is observational. The failure audit is purposive, model-assisted, unblinded, and lacks inter-rater agreement or a matched control; it cannot establish population prevalence or OMP-specific failure patterns (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** The proposed candidate-pool threshold is not causally established: duration bins also differ in content and questions, and the authors did not run controlled truncation (§5.1).

## Open research questions
- **Do savings survive end-to-end accounting?** Pool decoding and encoding costs were not measured. Benchmark total latency, memory, and compute for single-query versus repeated-query videos (§5.1).
- **Does the selector ordering hold with different scoring principles?** Both tested encoders use contrastive image-text objectives. Repeat matched comparisons with ITM or attention-based scorers (§5.2).
- **Can relevance constraints improve later OMP picks?** The audit motivates this, but tested variants do not establish an improvement. Evaluate a prespecified relevance-constrained selector on held-out questions with a blinded failure audit (§5.5; Appendices C–D).
- **How much does reinvestment depend on selection?** The interaction is underpowered. Run a larger selector-by-frame-count-by-resolution experiment with audited token costs and prespecified effect thresholds (§5.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the main paired comparisons and explicitly bounds several claims. Material missing information includes end-to-end resource measurements, token audits outside LongVideoBench’s long bins, fully specified prompts and parsing details, some GPT per-item records, and independent verification of the reported release.
