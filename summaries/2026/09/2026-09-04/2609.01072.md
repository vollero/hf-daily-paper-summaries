# Let Confidence Change, Not the Prediction: Prediction-Preserving Repair for Post-hoc Calibration

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01072) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01072)


## One-sentence takeaway
CORD repairs a fitted calibrator’s probability vectors to recover every original top-1 prediction, with lower average ECE, NLL, and Brier scores across the evaluated image-classification datasets, although individual comparisons can worsen (Proposition 1; Tables 1, 7–9).

## Problem and motivation
Multiclass calibration can change both confidence and the predicted class. Accuracy measures the net correctness effect of these changes, allowing beneficial, harmful, and incorrect-to-incorrect revisions to obscure their total frequency. The authors use Top-1 Prediction Change Rate (TPCR) to expose that frequency (Introduction; Equation 10).

The identified gap is how to retain the original prediction without restricting calibrator fitting or returning only a confidence attached to a separately selected label. CORD instead produces a complete probability vector whose own argmax recovers the original decision.

## Approach and contributions
- **Post-fit vector repair:** CORD adjusts the probability mass on the original top-1 class while preserving the calibrated conditional distribution over all remaining classes. A lower bound on that mass guarantees strict top-1 preservation. For a fixed original-class mass, this reconstruction uniquely minimizes KL divergence from the calibrated vector (Equations 2–4).
- **Coordinated mass selection:** Local references use the calibrated original-class probability, averaged with the original classifier’s probability when the prediction changed. A constrained Bernoulli–KL objective coordinates repairs to retain the calibration split’s mean calibrated original-class mass whenever feasible (Equations 5–7).
- **Compact deployment rule:** One shared scalar, solved by bisection, determines repairs for new inputs. The adapter requires output pairs but no additional labels, supervised map, or user-tuned hyperparameter; its prediction-preservation guarantee extends to new inputs (Algorithm 1; Proposition 1).
- **Evaluation contribution:** The paper separates prediction changes from accuracy changes and tests repair choices, corruption robustness, calibration-set size, and fit-time preservation baselines. The claim that CORD is the “first” such adapter is the authors’ novelty claim, not independently established here.

The evidence supports exact decision preservation and average scoring improvements in these settings. It does **not** establish universally better calibration, improvement for every classifier–calibrator pair, or dominance over fit-time methods. Tables 7–8 contain degradations, and Tables 4 and 11 show mixed baseline rankings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Structural boundary:** Mean preservation is guaranteed on the calibration split only. For boundary probabilities, conditional-distribution preservation applies exactly to the internally stabilized calibrated vector (Proposition 1; Appendix, p. 10).
- **Preservation tradeoff:** Restoring original predictions also reverses beneficial calibrator revisions. The authors explicitly treat preservation as a deployment choice (Conclusion).
- **Analyst inference—aggregate sensitivity:** Large Matrix Scaling failures and repair gains on CIFAR-100 can strongly influence dataset averages; Table 8 shows much smaller gains for several other calibrators.
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Experiments cover image classification and CIFAR corruption shifts. Dense Matrix Scaling, Structured Matrix Scaling, and Dirichlet calibration are omitted on ImageNet for parameter-count reasons; fit-time baseline comparisons are confined to CIFAR-10 (Experimental Setup; Tables 4, 11–12).
- **Analyst inference—selection risk:** Table 4 selects the best direct Base separately by classifier and metric. This is a comparison summary, not evidence for a deployable selection procedure on unseen data.
- **Design choice:** Equal local-reference weighting follows symmetry, not a demonstrated universally optimal scoring objective; alternative weights lead different metrics in Table 6.

## Open research questions
- **When does repair worsen scoring quality?** Individual degradations remain despite positive averages. Next experiment: stratify paired score changes by calibrator, TPCR, and beneficial versus harmful revisions.
- **How reliably does the shared scalar transfer under other shifts?** Mean preservation is only guaranteed on the construction split, and shift tests use CIFAR-C. Next experiment: hold the scalar fixed under controlled class-prior changes and measure mean discrepancy, ECE, NLL, and Brier.
- **Can alternative objectives or broader preservation constraints improve results?** The conclusion leaves richer objectives and extensions beyond top-1 open. Next experiment: compare alternative mass-selection objectives or top-k constraints under matched output pairs and evaluation splits.
