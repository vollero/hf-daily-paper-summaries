# Locked at the Entrance, Open Inside: Where RLVR Narrows the Solution Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29188) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29188)


## One-sentence takeaway
On Countdown, the authors find that RLVR reduces access to alternative solution openings while preserving substantial ability to complete them when supplied, with related early-diversity losses on mathematical benchmarks (§§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
RLVR can improve single-sample accuracy while reducing solution diversity, limiting the benefit of repeated sampling. The authors identify a diagnostic gap: aggregate answer counts and sampled trace clusters conflate failure to **initiate** a solution family with failure to **execute** it after initiation (§1). Distinguishing these failures could guide more effective diversity recovery.

## Approach and contributions
- **Access–execution decomposition:** Exhaustively enumerate Countdown solutions, canonicalize equivalent expressions, and group them by first operand and operator. Compare free sampling with solver-constructed entrance prefixes that leave subsequent arithmetic unspecified (§3).
- **Localization evidence:** Combine teacher-forced likelihood changes, matched-prefix completion, and recovery of previously observed but subsequently unsampled families across PPO and GRPO (§5).
- **Targeted recovery:** Evaluate reasoning-phase logit mixing, checkpoint sampling, layer interpolation, and allocation across feasible entrances. The contribution is their entrance-based diagnosis and evaluation; checkpoint composition itself is acknowledged prior work (§§2, 6).
- **Transfer and training controls:** Examine early-calculation diversity on larger mathematical reasoning models and compare supervised, distilled, and staged alignment settings (§7; Appendices F–G).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage contracts during RLVR | PPO steps 50→275: mean canonical solution coverage **0.337→0.111**, a reported **67%** decline; 150 Countdown problems, 320 samples each. GRPO steps 25→450: reported **43%** decline on 135 problems, same sample count. | §4; Appendix B.9 |
| Contraction persists on problems solved at both endpoints | On 58 PPO problems solved at steps 50 and 275, coverage falls **0.564→0.286**, with 320 samples per problem. | Appendix B.5 |
| Likelihood shifts concentrate near the entrance | PPO steps 25→275, 1,530 matched valid continuations: entrance NLL increases **4.496** versus **0.280 nats/token** downstream. GRPO steps 25→450, 232 continuations: **8.08** versus **0.71 nats/token**. | §5; Appendix C.3 |
| Minimal prefixes recover designated-family completion | PPO step 275: **0.018→0.212** without versus with a minimal entrance, over 150 problems with 8–16 continuations per prefix. GRPO step 450: **0.104→0.188**, with 16 continuations per condition and problem. | Table 3A; Appendix D.3 |
| Previously sampled families remain executable | For 28 families absent from 320 late-policy free samples, supplied-entrance completion is **0.475→0.529** at PPO steps 50→275; 64 continuations per cell. Paired change: **+0.054**, 95% CI **[−0.080, 0.190]**. | Table 3C; Appendix C.6 |
| Layer interpolation recovers coverage | Midpoint interpolation of blocks 20–28 between PPO steps 50 and 275: coverage **0.111→0.152**, reported **37%** relative gain; paired change **+0.041 [0.020, 0.069]**. Observed pass@1 rises **0.278→0.305** on 100 confirmation problems, 64 samples each. | §6; Appendix E |
| Larger models show early-diversity loss | Across six math benchmarks, Qwen base→SimpleRL first-calculation entropy falls a reported **30% at 7B** and **41% at 14B**. Macro pass@64 changes **0.754→0.760** at 7B and remains **0.774** at 14B; 64 samples per problem. | §§3, 7; Appendix G.1 |
| Multi-solution SFT preserves more coverage at lower accuracy | On 135 Countdown tasks, 320 samples each, SFT with four demonstrations per training problem reaches coverage **0.709**, pass@1 **0.333**, versus late GRPO **0.268**, **0.429**. | §7; Appendix G.3 |

The evidence supports an access bottleneck in these Countdown runs and shows that many unsampled families remain recoverable. It does not establish zero probability for missing families or universal preservation of execution. The larger-math results use structural proxies rather than exhaustive solution coverage. Accuracy improvement also has a substantial execution component: the authors’ observational decomposition attributes most of the solve-probability increase to improved conditional execution (Appendix C.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Exact decomposition depends on Countdown enumeration. First calculations on other benchmarks are proxies, and externally supplied entrances measure a different conditional quantity from naturally selected entrances (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated training restrictions:** PPO uses one rollout per prompt and no actor KL penalty; the other run is an externally trained GRPO series; SFT uses LoRA (Appendix A).
- **Author-reported exceptions:** Recovery is weaker in the deeper access tail, longer horizons introduce execution constraints, and GRPO continuation after diverse SFT again produces low rollout entropy (Appendices C.6, F.4, G.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Different model families, curricula, supervision, and operating accuracies prevent the staged-alignment and SFT comparisons from isolating one causal protection mechanism.
- **Internal reporting inconsistencies:** The “more than fiftyfold” PPO accuracy increase in §4 conflicts with Appendix B.7’s untruncated pass rates of 0.0513→0.2851 at the same endpoints. The conclusion reports 38% coverage recovery versus 37% in §6. Section 7 also associates Table 26 with a 7B comparison, while Appendix G.2 labels it 1.5B.
- **Analyst inference:** The local gradient surrogate motivates entrance concentration but does not establish its inevitability across RLVR configurations (Appendix B.2).

## Open research questions
- **Which training choices cause entrance collapse?** The PPO configuration and public GRPO replication do not isolate rollout-group or KL effects. Next experiment: vary these factors with matched initialization, training budget, and multiple seeds.
- **Can entrance allocation work without an exhaustive solver?** The demonstrated allocation method requires known feasible openings. Next experiment: compare learned entrance proposals with free sampling under matched token budgets on mathematical benchmarks.
- **What preserves diversity through continued RLVR?** Staged models preserve early diversity, yet GRPO after multi-solution SFT re-concentrates. Next experiment: compare SFT→RLVR and SFT→DPO→RLVR from the same model while measuring coverage, entropy, and accuracy throughout.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial convergent evidence, but several claims require narrower interpretation than the headline conclusions.

Material gaps include missing numerical bodies for many referenced tables, unresolved reporting inconsistencies, multi-seed training variability, complete public GRPO training details, and verified repository contents. The supplied continuation experiment reports rollout entropy without corresponding final held-out coverage and accuracy.
