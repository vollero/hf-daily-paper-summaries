# The Missing Temporal Link: Temporal Context Routing for Script-Driven Audio-Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02367) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02367)


## One-sentence takeaway
Temporal Context Routing explicitly routes timed script prompts into video and audio cross-attention, substantially improving shot timing on the authors’ evaluation while providing smaller dialogue-timing gains over matched temporal operators (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Audio and video can remain synchronized with each other while jointly missing a script’s requested cuts and dialogue intervals. The authors attribute this gap to encoding timestamps as text without connecting them to either modality’s temporal coordinates. Script-driven production also requires independently timed shots and dialogue, since speech can span a cut (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Temporal routing:** TCR removes timing fields before text encoding, maps prompt intervals to tokens, and adds a duration-normalized, negative quadratic timing bias to both video–text and audio–text attention logits. Shot and dialogue intervals remain independently controllable, including when they overlap (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Backbone adaptation:** Implemented on LTX-2.3, TCR leaves self-attention and audio–video attention unchanged. The routing operator adds no learnable parameters, but training updates LoRA adapters using the original joint flow-matching objective (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Refined supervision:** A pipeline constructs multi-shot clips, obtains structured annotations from Gemini, corrects cuts with PySceneDetect, and aligns dialogue with WhisperX. Refined timestamps use a 0.1-second grid (§3.4).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Alongside comparisons with existing generators, the authors compare routing against hard interval masks and Gaussian Interval RoPE under matched training conditions (§4.3; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results
All main-table results use 200 test scripts, one output per model per script, at 704×1280 and 24 fps without first-frame conditioning (§4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large improvement over the original backbone | TCR versus LTX-2.3: Shot Boundary MAE **0.042 vs 1.11 s**; Shot IoU **0.957 vs 0.532**; exact shot-count accuracy **93.0% vs 36.0%**; Dialogue Acc@0.5s **84.1% vs 28.3%**. | Table 1 |
| Matched operators narrow the dialogue advantage | TCR / hard mask / Gaussian Interval RoPE: Shot Boundary MAE **0.042 / 0.108 / 0.113 s**; Dialogue Acc@0.5s **84.1% / 83.7% / 82.8%**, using the same backbone, timing map, training data, and optimization. | Table 2; Appendix A |
| Refined labels materially affect timing | Refined versus coarse TCR supervision: Shot Boundary MAE **0.042 vs 0.375 s**; Dialogue Acc@0.5s **84.1% vs 37.6%**. | Table 2 |
| Quality and synchronization results are mixed but competitive | TCR versus LTX-2.3: IQ **0.7032 vs 0.6819**, AES **0.5477 vs 0.5354**, WER **8.48% vs 14.0%**, Sync-C **2.78 vs 2.55**; offset accuracy is slightly lower, **30.5% vs 31.2%**. | Table 1 |
| Human preference favors TCR | With ties included, overall preference for TCR is **72.3%** against LTX-2.3 and **83.9%** against JoyAI-Echo; **28 participants**, **16 cases**, eight cases per comparator. | §4.2; Figure 5; Appendix D |

The matched comparisons support an advantage for TCR’s shot-timing operator within this training setup. The larger end-to-end gains combine routing with adaptation and refined supervision. Dialogue Acc@0.5s requires both onset and offset errors to meet the threshold and counts unmatched prompts as failures; shot MAE instead averages matched shot pairs (Appendix E). These results do not establish universal quality preservation or a statistically reliable dialogue advantage over the hard mask.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper provides no dedicated limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences from the reported setup**:

- **Restricted generalization evidence:** Training uses two short-drama collections, events focus on dialogue, and TCR is implemented on one backbone. Broader domains and non-speech timing remain untested (§§3.1–3.2, 4.1).
- **Evaluation selection and measurement:** Test scripts must pass a Gemini–detector shot-count consistency check, and generated cuts are evaluated with the same frozen detector. This restricts the evaluated population; matched-pair shot MAE also excludes unmatched shots from its average, making shot-count accuracy essential context (Appendix E).
- **Limited uncertainty reporting:** Figure 6 includes prompt-bootstrap intervals for shot metrics across checkpoints, but the main tables lack uncertainty estimates, particularly for the small dialogue differences. Inference specifies one seed, and human evaluation covers few distinct cases (Appendices B, D, G).
- **Incomplete component isolation:** Routing strength is justified analytically without a reported sweep; video-only versus audio-only routing is not separately ablated. “Intervals as text” is explicitly an auxiliary, unmatched reference (Table 2; Appendices F–G).

## Open research questions
- **Does TCR transfer beyond dialogue-centric drama?** The reported scope is narrow. Test matched operators on held-out genres and timed music or sound effects.
- **How robust is routing to inaccurate or infeasible schedules?** Experiments assume supplied intervals. Perturb timestamps and compress dialogue windows, measuring timing, intelligibility, and script fidelity.
- **Which design choices drive the remaining advantage?** Fixed routing strength and combined modality routing leave attribution incomplete. Sweep routing strength and independently enable each modality’s routing across multiple seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the mechanism and reported within-setting gains, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes collection identities and language composition, clip-duration distribution, hardware and compute costs, complete auxiliary-reference training details, multi-seed results, uncertainty for main-table dialogue gains, and confirmed availability of code, weights, and evaluation data.
