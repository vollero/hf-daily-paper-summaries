# Percolation Dynamics in Optimization : Variance Cascades and Discrete Scale Invariance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02373) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02373)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors model neural-network simplification as symmetry-driven percolation with geometrically spaced variance peaks, supported most strongly by an AdamW grokking experiment, while the theory remains conditional and benchmark evidence is mixed.

## Problem and motivation
SGD can steer networks toward invariant sets corresponding to simpler subnetworks, but the authors identify a gap in explaining how this simplification unfolds over time. They seek a structural account of abrupt training changes, including delayed generalization, that smooth descriptions of optimization may miss (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Topological model:** Represent subnetworks approaching shared invariant sets as equivalence classes in a Reeb graph that records merges and splits; coarse-grain this graph into monotonic percolation under timescale separation (§§3–4).
- **Conditional scaling theory:** Derive block-merge jumps and a discrete scale invariance (DSI) factor, \(\lambda(n)=n^\sigma\), assuming an ensemble mean-field growth relation. For fixed merge multiplicity, persistent large-network jumps require merging components proportional to network size (Theorems 4.4, 4.7; Appendix C.25).
- **Adaptive-optimizer extension:** Extend trapping and DSI to Adam/AdamW using joint parameter–moment states, truncated heavy-tailed gradients, and bounds on preconditioner errors, valid before specified stopping times (§5; Appendix D).
- **Empirical diagnostic:** Measure effective-rank variance across seeds, detrend log-variance, and test peak sequences against phase-randomized spectral surrogates. This is an analysis framework; no improved optimizer is demonstrated (Appendix F.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Discontinuity depends on component scaling | For an \(n\)-way merge of size-\(i\) components, \(\Delta\mathcal O=(n-1)i/N\); fixed-\(n\) jumps persist when \(i=\Theta(N)\), but vanish for \(i=O(1)\). | Theorem 4.4; Corollary C.18 |
| Idealized scaling matches its construction | Forced \(K=3\) spline coalescence at times 700 and 1400 gives \(\lambda=2.00\), using a two-point fit. | Appendix E.3; Figure 3 |
| Unconstrained toy optimization shows merging and reversal | \(K=6\) GELU network, SGD, MSE: two condensation peaks give \(\lambda\approx2.00\); changing both target signal and learning rate at \(t=3000\) produces fragmentation. | Appendix E.4; Figures 1, 3 |
| Grokking provides the strongest reported null-test evidence | AdamW Transformer, modulo 17, 15 initializations, 30,000 epochs, learning rate 0.003, decay 0.5: 3 peaks, \(\lambda=2.11\), \(R^2=1.00\), spectral-null FPR 0.1%. | Appendix F.3; Figure 12 |
| Tabular evidence is mixed | Across 20 initializations: Heart Disease—4 peaks, \(\lambda=1.71\), \(R^2=0.98\), FPR 4.9%; Abalone—6 peaks, 1.28, 0.97, 15.6%; German Credit—4 peaks, 1.99, 0.86, 80.2%. | Table 1 |
| Vision evidence is weaker | FashionMNIST, 15 initializations: 3 peaks, \(\lambda=1.57\), \(R^2=1.00\), FPR 10.7%. MNIST has only 2 peaks, without an extended cascade. | Appendix F.1, F.3; Figures 10–11 |

The empirical pipeline uses 1,000 spectral surrogates and 500 bootstrap resamples per configuration (Appendix F.1). Results support structured variance sequences in some settings, especially grokking; German Credit demonstrates that apparently geometric peaks can also arise under the null. These observations do not establish that symmetry-driven merges cause generalization, identify actual merge multiplicities, or validate universal DSI across architectures.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Practical-width discontinuities, curved invariant manifolds, extreme hyperparameters, and systematic large-model adaptive-optimizer validation remain open (§8).
- **Limited adaptive diagnostics:** The tracked grokking coordinate pair had not merged; the gradient tail index was not directly estimated. The reported diagnostics therefore check only part of the extension’s assumptions (Remark D.28).
- **Analyst inference—measurement gap:** Largest-component fraction becomes effective rank, while theoretical distances from critical edge density become empirical training-time peak sequences. The supplied derivations do not clearly establish the required density-to-time mapping (Theorem C.25; Appendices E.2, F.1).
- **Analyst inference—mathematical overstatement:** Equation (35) gives a finite relative-variance expression for positive pre-transition order parameter, so it supports a peak rather than literal divergence. Also, weak cross-covariance is treated as conditional independence without a quantified approximation error (Assumption 3.2; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—validation limits:** The forced toy schedule and two-point fits offer limited independent evidence for scaling. Higher-order grokking merges are inferred under assumed exponents rather than directly observed (Appendices E.3, F.2).

## Open research questions
- **Do jumps survive increasing width?** Practical component scaling is unresolved (§8). Sweep network width and directly measure merge sizes and normalized jump amplitudes.
- **Do spectral peaks identify actual symmetry merges?** Effective rank substitutes for graph connectivity (Appendix F.1). Track neuron equivalence and spectral changes together, then perturb permutation symmetry.
- **Can DSI predict transitions prospectively?** The paper reports fitted cascades but no held-out forecasting evaluation. Fit early peaks and evaluate subsequent transition-time predictions against spectral-null and non-DSI baselines.
- **When does the AdamW extension apply?** Diagnostics are preliminary (Remark D.28). Sweep optimizer settings while measuring tail moments, correlation times, preconditioner errors, and basin escapes.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported results and conditional framing, but theoretical-to-empirical links remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes released code and raw trajectories, complete architecture and data-split specifications, exact peak-selection and smoothing rules, numerical bootstrap uncertainty, direct merge observations, and comprehensive checks of theoretical assumptions. Tabular task labeling is also inconsistent between §6 and Figure 2.
