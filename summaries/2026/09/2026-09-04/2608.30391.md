# Using Grounded Theory for Agent Behavior Analysis at Scale

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30391) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30391)


## One-sentence takeaway
AutoTraceGT automates grounded-theory coding to produce auditable agent-behavior taxonomies that recover many human-identified failure patterns and sometimes improve failure prediction, although reporting inconsistencies weaken confidence in exact results.

## Problem and motivation
Outcome labels and action counts reveal whether agents succeed but offer little explanation of their behavior. Manual trajectory analysis is expensive, while predefined behavioral classifiers can miss unfamiliar patterns. The authors propose inductive, iterative qualitative analysis with an explicit stopping rule to address this gap (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Automated grounded-theory pipeline:** OpenCode labels trajectory episodes with evidence-linked behavioral codes; AxialCode groups codes and relationships; Manage revises a persistent codebook; TheoreticalCode synthesizes an explanatory narrative (§3).
- **Auditable iteration and stopping:** Category memberships, quotations, memos, and revision logs connect interpretations to trajectories. Stopping depends on sufficiently few additions over consecutive rounds. Although the framework supports adaptive sampling, the main experiments use disjoint uniform random sampling (§3; App. A.6).
- **Interpretive and predictive reuse:** Codebooks provide behavioral explanations and binary category-presence/co-occurrence features for GLMs and FLAML failure classifiers. Evaluation includes human-taxonomy comparisons, human audits, stability studies, and ablations (§4; App. A.7).

The contribution is the operationalization and evaluation of grounded theory for agent trajectories; grounded theory itself is an existing methodology. The authors’ claim of being the first such end-to-end pipeline is not independently established by the supplied text.

These results support useful, inspectable behavioral descriptions and complementary predictive features within the evaluated corpora. They do not establish causal failure mechanisms, universal superiority over baselines, or complete discovery of behavioral structure. Human review strengthens the coverage evidence but is limited in scope.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Shared LLM biases may persist despite model agreement; judging and feature annotation depend on LLM interpretation. The method requires many calls and targets offline English-language, single-agent benchmark analysis. Algorithmic saturation is only an approximation of methodological saturation (p. 12).
- **Scope of robustness evidence:** The appendix includes prompt, sampling, merge-policy, and cross-family checks, but several concern only one dataset/backbone. Cross-family evaluation uses Gemini-3-Flash only on SWE-Agent (App. A.7).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** The headline claims **7,500+ trajectories**, whereas Table 4 lists **6,180 in total** (analyst sum), including only **200** failure-annotated trajectories. The text does not reconcile these counts with its approximately 500-per-benchmark discussion (§4.2.1).
- **Ambiguous saturation implementation:** Algorithms define an addition **count**, while App. A.6–A.7 describe a fractional addition **rate** with threshold **0.2**. The actual denominator and implemented rule are unclear.
- **Unclear coverage denominators:** Several Table 1 recall percentages do not correspond to integer fractions of the listed human-category counts. Exact aggregation cannot be reconstructed from the supplied definitions.
- **Analyst inference—protocol reproducibility:** Prediction experiments explicitly claim labels are hidden from coding agents, but the general axial-coding method and supplied prompt expose outcome status (§3; App. A.7–A.8). A prediction-specific prompt or implementation is needed to reconcile these descriptions; this discrepancy does not itself prove leakage.
- **Analyst inference—causal validity:** Narrative agreement and GLM associations cannot establish that intervening on the identified behaviors would improve success.

## Open research questions
1. **Does category-addition stopping miss rare behaviors or evolving relationships?** The authors acknowledge that saturation should incorporate category density and relationship stability. Continue coding held-out batches after stopping and measure newly discovered, human-validated structure.
2. **How stable are findings across model families and languages?** Current cross-family testing is narrow and all evaluated logs are English. Repeat matched-corpus induction across several families and multilingual logs with blinded human review.
3. **Can the discovered behaviors guide effective interventions?** Training and process rewards are proposed, not tested. Randomize interventions such as verification before task closure and compare task success and behavioral changes.
4. **When do grounded-theory features outperform simpler alternatives?** Gains vary by corpus and feature combination. Run repeated, resource-matched comparisons against few-shot induction and clustering with uncertainty estimates.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes substantial methodological detail, ablations, and human checks, but several numerical and procedural ambiguities remain.

Material missing information includes reconciled corpus counts, precise coverage aggregation, the implemented saturation calculation, prediction split sizes and uncertainty estimates, prediction-specific label-hiding prompts, full AutoML settings, and complete measured usage for downstream coding stages.
