# PACE: Towards Surfacing Hidden Conflicts in User Requests

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-04.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03293) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03293)


## One-sentence takeaway
PACE benchmarks hidden conflicts in personal knowledge bases, and PACEMAKER improves evidence-grounded feasibility judgments across three model configurations while leaving substantial retrieval and reasoning errors (Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
An ordinary request can conflict with commitments, personal constraints, or surrounding conditions recorded elsewhere in a user’s knowledge base. Assistants must retrieve and combine those facts while avoiding unnecessary refusal. The authors identify a gap between this setting and prior evaluations centered on explicit risks, supplied context, or preference following (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **PACE dataset:** Synthetic persona-grounded requests paired with atomic KB facts, gold evidence, feasibility labels, and rationales. It contains 3,249 queries across 185 profiles and 376,448 facts, covering temporal, personal, and state constraints with both conflict and non-conflict cases (§3; Tables 1, 6).
- **PACEMAKER framework:** A training-free pipeline uses specialized LLM agents to plan conflict probes, generate counter queries, and filter evidence. Dense/BM25 retrieval and weighted rank fusion produce seeds; breadth-first traversal over a similarity graph expands evidence before final filtering and answer generation (§4; Appendix B).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Comparisons examine retrieval, decision-plus-rationale quality, evidence coverage, component removal, structured retrieval alternatives, judge agreement, and computational cost (§§5–7; Appendices C–D). Code and dataset availability is stated on page 1; it was not independently verified here.

PASS requires both the correct decision and a matching core rationale; it is not pure decision accuracy (§5.1). These results support improved benchmark performance, not reliable real-world task execution. Full-evidence cases score better in Figure 3, but that observational grouping does not isolate a causal effect of evidence completeness.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers feasibility judgments rather than completed scheduling, recommendations, or planning; specialized training remains unexplored (Limitations, p. 10).
- **Analyst inference—label quality:** Table 12’s temporal example claims insufficient time, although its listed durations total 175 minutes within a 270-minute window. Travel to the initial workout is unspecified, so the stated conflict is not established by the supplied facts. Its personal-dislike and garage-access examples also appear inconsistent with Appendix E’s exclusion rules.
- **Analyst inference—generalization and evaluation:** Synthetic construction and judging both use GPT-5.4-mini. Human checks and an alternative judge offer partial validation, but the alternative judge only evaluates Qwen PACEMAKER outputs, leaving comparative ranking robustness untested (Appendices C, D.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** GraphRAG supplies synthesized intermediate context and experiences timeouts; its interface differs from ranked-fact retrieval. Component ablations cover only GPT, with no reported uncertainty estimates for small differences (Appendices D.5–D.6).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Appendix B specifies per-view retrieval K=10, whereas Table 14 labels default K=20 without clearly reconciling the notation. Cost measurements cover one KB; HippoRAG 2’s reported cold-start total also does not equal its listed indexing-plus-retrieval times (Appendix D.7; Table 25).

## Open research questions
- **Does performance transfer to real, evolving personal records?** Only synthetic, reference-date scenarios are tested. Next experiment: evaluate consented longitudinal KBs with updates and stale facts.
- **How should preferences, hard constraints, and user overrides affect refusal?** Human disagreements and example inconsistencies leave this boundary unsettled. Next experiment: independently adjudicate matched cases that vary constraint strength and explicit user intent.
- **Does complete evidence itself improve judgments?** Figure 3 compares different coverage groups. Next experiment: randomly remove or restore decisive facts within the same queries.
- **Do retrieval gains survive task execution?** The benchmark stops at responses. Next experiment: add executable scheduling or reservation environments and measure completion alongside inappropriate actions and unnecessary refusals.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive methods, prompts, and results, but contains internal inconsistencies. Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, significance tests, a clearly separated hyperparameter-selection split, independent label audits beyond the sampled cases, real-user evaluation, and verification of released artifacts.
