# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-09-10

## Executive summary

- **Research-agent training benefits from corrected outcome predictions in the reported experiments.** WMRL (`2608.12564`) uses real-execution anchors to calibrate predicted rewards, reporting 3.1–3.4× lower AutoResearch GPU-hours and higher benchmark averages than same-scale GRPO. Its robotics experiment still consumes completed rollout images.
- **Agent improvement depends on what gets corrected.** AgentGrad (`2609.08572`) constructs localized prompt repairs; harness–model adaptation (`2609.09134`) finds that full expert imitation degrades evolved-harness performance, while single-turn correction provides a modest average gain.
- **Executable scores need careful interpretation.** SWE-Bench Pro Verified (`2609.08149`) addresses observed answer leakage and specification defects; Φ-Bench (`2609.10226`) gates infrastructure rewards on correctness and integrity. Neither supports treating every reported percentage as a task-resolution rate.
- **Research evaluation separates selection, intervention, and discovery claims.** SAEScientist-Bench (`2609.09113`) finds a larger expert-reference gap in steering than activation selectivity. DCP (`2609.09219`) separately audits utility, outcome recovery, and feedback benefit without establishing historical novelty.
- **Control-grounded generation shows progress within restricted tests.** Programmable World Model (`2609.10540`) improves coarse state-to-video agreement; SyncWorld (`2609.09155`) improves average cross-view consistency using setup calibration. Entity-specific persistence and accumulated simulation error remain insufficiently tested.
- **Reasoning supervision and rollout structure both matter.** Endpoint retention (`2609.07103`) improves several post-training averages, while DATPO (`2609.08650`) expands finite-sample answer coverage at higher reported training time. These results do not establish that omitted reasoning is internally reconstructed or that additional answers represent distinct valid strategies.
- **Domain benchmarks expose different bottlenecks.** Python access improves the tested WearableQA configuration (`2609.05405`); StochBench (`2609.09264`) distinguishes checked proofs from faithful formalization; RESCUE-BENCH (`2609.09657`) measures agreement with recorded support annotations rather than intervention effectiveness.
- **Quality, fidelity, and efficiency remain separate outcomes.** OracleZoom (`2609.06490`) improves observable reference consistency without verifying unseen detail; neural speech decoding (`2609.10296`) improves selected semantic metrics while worsening WER; DF26 (`2609.07369`) documents detector degradation alongside successful generator-specific detection and retraining.

## Main results worth noting

- **Corrected reward prediction has a concrete compute comparison — `2608.12564`.** At 4B, WMRL reports 286 GPU-hours versus GRPO’s 883, with MLE-Dojo/DSBench percentile averages of 16.4/28.8 versus 15.2/25.7. At 9B, the corresponding figures are 349 versus 1,174 GPU-hours and 21.6/32.8 versus 18.8/31.2. Scores use per-task avg@8 followed by category averaging. Pure predicted-reward training underperforms GRPO at both scales. The result supports the complete correction recipe within selected sandbox-feasible tasks; statistical significance is unreported.

- **DCP reports unusually explicit uncertainty for a tightly scoped claim — `2609.09219`.** In each complete SQLite-Web and virtual-catalyst audit, 0/96 qualified recoveries yield a one-sided recovery-probability bound of 0.0468 at α=0.01. Separately, truthful versus neutral feedback produces 30/30 versus 0/30 recoveries in each audit, with paired 99% effect CI [0.6379, 1.0]. These are registered episode- and checkpoint-conditional findings, not impossibility, historical novelty, or independently issued certification.

- **Harness fit changes the outcome of imitation — `2609.09134`.** Across seven enterprise tasks, Qwen’s evolved-harness mean success falls from 78.0% to 63.1% after full expert imitation, with every task regressing. Local correction raises the mean to 79.7 ± 0.67% from 78.0 ± 0.97%, mean ± SEM over three runs, but slightly reduces two task scores. The large imitation regression is more directly supported than the proposed planning-fit mechanism.

- **Tool access addresses a measured computational bottleneck — `2609.05405`.** GPT-5.4’s WearableQA accuracy is 51.2% with row input, 51.5% with CSV, 51.9% with Markdown plus statistics, and 71.3% with a fixed-representation Python agent. This comparison supports computational access for this model and ten-option benchmark; it does not establish clinical usefulness or generality across models.

- **High state scores can still leave identity unresolved — `2609.10540`.** PWM’s 94% Count Accuracy versus LingBot-World-V2’s 40.75% uses 400 sampled frames from 50 clips. Its 98% State Accuracy versus 8% checks whether any dead character appears in at least one of three post-transition frames for each of 50 events. These are strong coarse-agreement results under the benchmark, with different control interfaces across systems; they do not establish correct victim identity or lasting state persistence.

- **Post-training accuracy and coverage require different accounting — `2609.07103`, `2609.08650`.** Middle-token masking raises Qwen3-1.7B-Base GRPO mean accuracy from 25.8 to 34.5 across AIME24, MATH500, and GPQA-Diamond after 100 DAPO-17k training steps (`2609.07103`). DATPO’s Qwen3-4B five-benchmark mean pass@k is 60.4% versus GRPO’s 58.3%, with k=8 on MATH500 and 64 elsewhere (`2609.08650`). DATPO’s separate timing table reports 60.3 hours versus GRPO’s 26.3 on one A100, without explicitly identifying the timed model. These figures cannot be combined into a common improvement ranking.

- **Two efficiency studies illustrate different evidential standards — `2609.06806`, `2609.05463`.** HYBRIDAL’s variants pass pooled macro-F1 non-inferiority against retraining, fine-tuning, and their per-observation best at margin 0.010 across three encoders, six datasets, and five seeds. Time savings versus retraining come with higher raw NLL (`2609.06806`). The caching paper’s 89.3% Redis hit rate instead measures internal lookups across six days; it does not quantify avoided inference or user-visible latency savings (`2609.05463`). This distinction is editorial, not a shared-method claim.

- **Representation quality does not settle output fidelity — `2609.10296`.** On 172 held-out sentences from one listener, semantic decoding improves BLEU-1 to 0.100 ± 0.008 versus BrainECHO’s 0.061 ± 0.008, while WER worsens to 1.925 ± 0.100 versus 1.018 ± 0.030, mean ± SD. The semantic/overlap improvement should be read alongside substantial transcription error.

- **DF26’s evidence qualifies its title — `2609.07369`.** FF++-trained DFD-FCG drops from 94.3% AUROC on CDFv3 to 48.2% on DF26 in Table 5, but GenD-PE retains 69.7% pooled DF26 AUROC. Training GenD-PE on HunyuanVideo I2V official statements yields 96.8%, 95.6%, and 93.1% AUROC on Grok, Veo, and Wan 2.6, with official statements excluded from evaluation. The supported finding is substantial distribution-shift failure for many checkpoints, alongside evidence that detection can improve.

## Open research directions

These are proposed tests motivated by limitations in the supplied analyses, not claims that the wider literature lacks solutions.

1. **Separate reward improvements from the rest of the training recipe — `2608.12564`, `2609.11042`, `2609.09187`.** Run within-paper factorial comparisons holding data, initialization, rollout policy, and compute fixed: real-anchor frequency for WMRL; binary versus assertion-based rewards for T1; DPO, strategy GRPO, draft GRPO, and reward mixtures for AgenticGen. Evaluate independent outcomes rather than only the reward models used in training.

2. **Test localized correction under matched supervision — `2609.08572`, `2609.09134`.** Compare single-turn repairs, expert continuations, and complete expert trajectories using matched examples and training tokens. For AgentGrad, include paired-agent interventions on failures unresolved by any single agent, reporting hint-free held-out performance and unresolved-case coverage.

3. **Strengthen the evidence behind research and engineering success — `2609.09113`, `2609.09219`, `2609.08149`, `2609.10226`.** Rebuild SAE expert references without evaluation-text reuse and randomize steering-feedback access; sweep DCP challenger budgets against frozen outcomes; test fixed code patches against independently authored hidden workloads. These separate feedback effects, recoverability, and evaluator-specific success.

4. **Measure physical correctness beyond short rendered clips — `2609.10540`, `2609.09155`, `2609.10522`, `2609.00369`, `2609.08965`.** Use task-specific stress tests: identity-preserving death after occlusion, repeated imagined action chunks with contact-state checks, transfer to an arm absent from training, captured human–furniture interaction, and reactive traffic. Report failure rates and total execution latency alongside each system’s existing metrics.

5. **Disentangle supervision, context access, and exploration — `2609.07103`, `2609.08650`, `2609.05779`.** Within matched training budgets, independently vary middle-token losses and context retention, DATPO allocation and branching topology, and file/edit targets with corrected schedules and compiler feedback. Measure held-out correctness and valid solution diversity; do not infer internal reasoning from retained answer suffixes or trajectory length alone.

6. **Audit whether labels represent the intended domain task — `2609.05405`, `2609.06703`, `2609.09264`, `2609.09657`.** Obtain independent clinician review of wearable interpretations; exhaustively annotate patents including unqualified records; review matched direct/abstracted Lean targets; and allow experts to identify multiple acceptable support strategies. Each test distinguishes consistency with construction rules from broader validity.

7. **Measure efficiency over the complete workload — `2609.06702`, `2609.06806`, `2609.10355`, `2609.05463`.** Match hardware for PARSER, match retraining duration for switching schedules, include video/audio encoding and selection costs, and replay caching workloads with layer ablations and freshness checks. Report latency distributions, total resource use, and retained task quality.

8. **Validate fidelity against independent targets — `2609.06490`, `2609.10296`, `2609.07369`.** For super-resolution, withhold actual high-resolution image-pyramid targets and test whether projected metrics predict reconstruction. For MEG decoding, use held-out participants/topics and shuffled-signal controls. For authenticity detection, hold out generators and source clips while matching scenario and generation mode.

9. **Test behavioral audits on independently validated outcomes — `2609.08126`, `2609.02771`, `2609.10060`.** Audit removal of goals and hints in SchemeArena and distinguish covert intent from successful action; compare category-specific versus aggregate refusal rewrites; evaluate reference-relative bias metrics on ordinary held-out fine-tunes with dependence-aware statistics. Include benign controls and utility measures.

10. **Connect sparse-recovery guarantees to feasible estimators — `2509.01809`.** Compare exhaustive constrained estimation and practical algorithms on identical manageable sparse Gaussian instances, measuring exact recovery, fractional error, and runtime separately. Decreasing-retention experiments could probe where fixed-ψ asymptotic predictions stop being informative, without treating simulations as a proof.

## Coverage and uncertainty

**All 32 supplied current papers were analyzed and included.** All three downstream stages—taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical connections—were available. The historical input contained 50 candidate evidence blocks; eight supported cross-day edges are retained here. The digest uses only supplied evidence and catalog URLs, with no external browsing.

**Collection failures affected the Hugging Face Markdown route for 18 papers:** `2608.12564`, `2609.10522`, `2609.11042`, `2609.05405`, `2609.09113`, `2609.09219`, `2609.00369`, `2609.06702`, `2609.10226`, `2609.07103`, `2609.06806`, `2609.08126`, `2609.09187`, `2609.09657`, `2609.10296`, `2609.08650`, `2609.07369`, and `2609.08965`. The reported reasons were HTML responses or content lacking full paper text. Every affected paper nevertheless has a supplied analysis; these route failures are not counted as missing analyses.

**Partial evidence remains material:**

- Current source text is explicitly truncated for `2609.10355` and `2609.09657`.
- Missing numerical table bodies, plotted values, or figure details especially constrain `2609.08572`, `2609.08149`, `2609.09155`, `2609.06703`, `2609.05779`, `2609.05463`, `2609.02771`, and `2609.10060`. Specific figure or qualitative evidence is also incomplete for `2609.10226` and `2609.10296`.
- Historical evidence is supplied as service-truncated cached analyses. No `listing_summary` evidence was supplied. Only visible endpoint content supports retained edges; candidate-selection completeness is unknown.
- Artifact availability is frequently author-reported or unspecified. Code, datasets, checkpoints, and audit bundles were not independently inspected.

**Numerical inconsistencies are not silently resolved.** Pro Verified’s paired counts imply a different baseline accuracy and score drop from its prose (`2609.08149`). Additional discrepancies affect secondary Show-Harness results, T1 budgets, WearableQA context ablations, DF26 aggregate detector scores, bias-audit AUC settings, and PlannerForge tuning variance. Sparse-recovery notation also contains possible rendering loss.

Confidence is lowest for broad deployment claims, causal explanations without controlled ablations, comparisons involving unvalidated judges, and results lacking full tables or independent training repetitions. The LibriBrain100 reuse edge and same-day feedback-transfer proposal remain conditional. Unconnected papers are not evidence that no relationship exists; the tables intentionally retain only a sparse, supported set.
