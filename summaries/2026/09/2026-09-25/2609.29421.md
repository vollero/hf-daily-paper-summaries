# Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29421) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29421)


## One-sentence takeaway

Rufus-Air documents an eight-stage post-training pipeline that improves GLM-4.5-Air chiefly on instruction following and agent tasks, while leaving stage-order optimality and complete reproducibility unproven (§§5–6).

## Problem and motivation

Public base models make post-training accessible, but published recipes often omit data preparation, reward design, infrastructure, and intermediate results needed for reproduction. The authors address that documentation gap with an integrated recipe built on GLM-4.5-Air-Base, a mixture-of-experts model with 106B total and 12B active parameters (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions

- **An integrated recipe and empirical account.** The pipeline is SFT → Reasoning RL → Coding RL → Instruction-Following RL → General Agent → Coding Agent → Search Agent → RLHF. Its contribution is the documented combination and engineering experience; GSPO, GRPO, and other underlying methods are adopted from existing work (§§2–4).
- **Data and reward design across capabilities.** SFT uses 9.01M public examples with approximately 27B supervised tokens. Subsequent stages combine solvability checks, stage-specific difficulty filtering, execution rewards, instruction rubrics, search-answer judging, and a public preference reward model. No new human annotation is commissioned, but public synthetic traces and teacher-assisted generation/filtering remain dependencies (§§3.1–3.8).
- **A proposed ordering principle.** The authors place stages according to capability prerequisites and perceived exposure to reward hacking, generally moving from verifiable objectives toward softer rewards. They explicitly present this as a successful ordering, not an experimentally established optimum (§§2.1, 6).
- **Practical infrastructure and local findings.** Rufus-Slime and Rufus-Gym integrate existing components with exact-token multi-turn rollouts, persistent sandboxes, distributed environments, and routing replay. A paired Search Agent experiment supports removing group standard-deviation normalization under noisy rewards (§§3.7, 4).

These results support the effectiveness of the complete recipe and several local stage transitions under the reported settings. They do not isolate every ingredient’s contribution or establish an optimal ordering. The six externally sourced baseline columns are not controlled comparisons; several intermediate evaluations also use different protocols (§5.2; Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated causal limits:** No comprehensive ordering ablation or common benchmark suite across every stage. Coding Agent stopped at its compute budget, and its SWE-bench improvement disappears by the final checkpoint without measurements identifying the responsible later stage (§6; §3.6).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** Different measurements of the same checkpoint appear across stage tables. Evaluations use one judge per benchmark; live-web results depend on the evaluation window, and Tau2 results depend strongly on the user simulator (§5; Appendices E–F).
- **Author-stated contamination and reward risks:** Text and embedding screens cannot exclude all leakage. Search rewards can still reward memorized answers accompanied by incidental tool use (§§3.1, 3.3, 3.7).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** Broad claims about filtering, SFT composition, and infrastructure exceed the controlled ablations reported. Benchmark monitoring during tuning and checkpoint selection also leaves performance on untouched evaluations uncertain (§§3, 6).
- **Analyst inference—reproduction remains demanding:** SFT used 512 H200 GPUs for roughly 13 days; RL requires additional environments and services. The supplied text does not establish release locations for Rufus-specific code, weights, or prepared datasets, and training uses an internal search backend (§§3.7, 4; Table 16).
- **Analyst inference—reuse and safety scope:** One SFT source explicitly restricts commercial use, so public availability does not imply unrestricted reuse. RLHF targets harmlessness, but no dedicated safety evaluation is reported (Table 17; §§3.8, 5.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the proposed stage order outperform alternatives?** The authors did not establish optimality. Compare selected stage swaps and joint training under matched compute, evaluating one fixed suite after every stage (§6).
2. **Which difficulty filters improve learning per unit of compute?** Filters differ across stages without a common controlled comparison. Test static filtering, online filtering, and unfiltered sampling while measuring held-out accuracy per GPU-hour (§§3.2–3.7).
3. **Can later stages preserve software-engineering gains?** The Coding Agent gain disappears before release, with missing intermediate measurements. Evaluate SWE-bench after each later stage and test replay or retention constraints (§3.6).
4. **Can search rewards enforce evidence-dependent answers?** Successful tool calls need not support the final answer. Test evidence-grounding rewards using withheld, corrupted, or contradictory tool observations (§3.7).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The text provides substantial stage-level evidence and unusually explicit caveats, but causal and reproducibility claims remain less secure than the reported benchmark comparisons.

Material missing information includes confirmed Rufus-specific artifact availability, complete compute and cost accounting, comprehensive component/order ablations, training-seed variability and confidence intervals, a consistent evaluation matrix across checkpoints, and dedicated safety measurements.
