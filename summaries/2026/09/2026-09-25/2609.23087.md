# Neural Spectral Capacity: Measuring and Designing Architectures from Network Specification Alone

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23087) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23087)


## One-sentence takeaway
NSC scores architectures from matrix shapes and initialization variances, enabling exact optimization of an additive proxy that performs well in reported architecture-search and pruning experiments, without guaranteeing optimal task performance (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Parameter counts and FLOPs measure size and compute but cannot reliably distinguish how a budget is distributed across depth, width, attention heads, and FFNs. Existing training-free proxies typically require instantiated models and sampled inputs, then heuristic search. The authors seek a specification-only score that captures architectural structure and supports efficient, exact resource-constrained optimization (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Specification-only spectral score:** Start with the matrix score \(\log\det(I+W^\top W)\), motivated by a linear Gaussian channel. Define its architecture-only counterpart using the Marchenko–Pastur limiting spectrum and matrix dimensions and initialization variance. This is a deterministic proxy definition, not an exact finite-matrix identity (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Architecture aggregation:** Sum contributions from per-head attention projections, output projections, and FFNs; reshape convolutional weights for CNNs. Across-layer summation is an empirical modeling choice, not network-level mutual information (§3.3).
- **Exact proxy optimization:** NSC-DP enumerates network-level configurations and solves additive layer choices by knapsack dynamic programming. It also provides optimal proxy scores across the discretized budget grid (§3.4).
- **Empirical validation:** Evaluate ranking, architecture selection, and subnet pruning; additionally investigate aggregation, initialization conventions, and agreement with trained-weight spectral scores (§4; Appendices C, F–H).

The evidence supports a cheap, useful selection proxy within the evaluated spaces. It does not establish universal ranking superiority, globally optimal accuracy, or training-free production of usable model weights. Reported speedups concern search; some selected models require training or previously trained supernets.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Network-level additivity is empirically motivated. The spectral gradient analysis does not derive task-loss behavior, and DP optimality applies only to the proxy under the specified separable constraints (§3.3; Appendix D.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison fairness:** Transformer-XL candidates differ in parameter count. AutoFormer-Tiny’s stated budget is 5.7M, while Table 4 lists NSC-DP at 5.8M. Several pruning baselines also undershoot the target budget, although W-PCA matches NSC-DP (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Analyst inference—unequal oracle access:** NATS-Bench results select the best true accuracy among NSC’s top ten candidates, versus one candidate for proxy baselines. Exact strict top-one results are not supplied (Appendix F.4; Table 17).
- **Analyst inference—limited uncertainty reporting:** Main search and pruning results lack repeated-run uncertainty. AutoFormer and LoNAS findings depend on inherited supernet weights; GQA/MQA and MoE extensions are proposed without corresponding experiments (§§4–5).
- **Internal reporting issues:** FlexiBERT baseline correlations differ between Tables 3 and 9. Table 1’s product aggregation exceeds summation on both FlexiBERT and AutoFormer-Tiny, contrary to the accompanying claim that no alternative wins on more than one benchmark. Appendix G.2’s across-budget spectral agreement does not establish equivalent rankings among all same-budget candidates.

## Open research questions
- **Does NSC improve quality at exactly matched resources?** Current comparisons include size differences and omit uncertainty. Train repeated NSC-selected and baseline architectures at identical parameter budgets.
- **How much does layer placement matter?** Specification scores can tie across permutations of FFN widths, while trained-weight scores can distinguish them (Appendix G.2). Evaluate multiple same-budget permutations on downstream tasks.
- **Does the method transfer to GQA/MQA and MoEs?** These extensions are asserted structurally but not tested (§5). Compare controlled architecture rankings and selected models in spaces varying head sharing or expert allocation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method and reported results, but comparison and reporting issues limit broader conclusions.
