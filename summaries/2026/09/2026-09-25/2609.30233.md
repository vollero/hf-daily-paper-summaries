# Coding Agents for Generalized Task and Motion Planning Problems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30233) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30233)


## One-sentence takeaway
Off-the-shelf coding agents synthesize reusable policies that outperform supplied planners on simulated task and motion planning benchmarks, with fast execution but evidence limited to fully observed environments and simulator-assisted synthesis (§§I, IV–VI).

## Problem and motivation
Task and motion planning (TAMP) couples discrete choices—such as which object to move—with continuous geometric and physical constraints. Generalized TAMP exploits regularities across instances to reduce repeated planning, but existing approaches require substantial domain-specific engineering. The authors investigate whether coding agents can discover useful strategies without supplied symbolic models, samplers, or manipulation skills (§I).

## Approach and contributions
- **AgenticGenPlan workflow:** Agents receive a task description and simulator interface, then choose experiments, write tests, and revise a Python policy. The main setting provides NumPy and SciPy but excludes environment source code and engineered TAMP components. The final policy is frozen and runs without LLM calls (§§II-C, III-A).
- **Systematic empirical assessment:** The study evaluates three coding-agent configurations against benchmark planners, one-shot generation, and LLMGenPlan, plus source-access variants. Its central contribution is evaluating general-purpose coding agents for constrained physical planning rather than introducing a specialized planning architecture (§§I, III–IV).
- **Behavioral analysis and reported release:** Revision histories illustrate model calibration, targeted testing, and unexpected manipulation strategies. The authors report releasing code and full prompts (§§I, IV-B).

## Evidence and main results
The common protocol uses five synthesis runs per method/environment, 100 shared held-out instances per environment, a $20 model-usage budget for coding-agent and LLMGenPlan synthesis, and a 60-second evaluation timeout (§IV-A). All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Broad evaluation | Seven synthesis methods across 28 environments produce 980 programs and 98,000 evaluation episodes. | Abstract; §I |
| Higher success than supplied planners | On the 16 environments with planners, mean success is 95% for Astra, 82% for Opus, and 56% for Sol, versus 47% for planners. | §I |
| Source access improves aggregate success | Across all 28 environments, Opus rises from 74% to 84%; Astra from 86% to 95%. Source-equipped LLMGenPlan reaches 28%. | §IV-B, source-access analysis |
| Lower per-instance computation | Across 14 environments with planners and multiple object counts, Opus averages 2.1 seconds and Astra 0.5 seconds per instance, versus 29 seconds for planners. Computation excludes synthesis and evaluation-environment advancement. | §§II-C, IV-B |
| Fast action computation, conditional on perfect runs | On 15 environments where all four compared agent/source configurations have a perfect run, main-setting Opus averages 11.7 ms/action, Astra 1.3 ms/action, and Astra + source 50.4 ms/action. Each method uses its own perfect runs. | Table III caption; §IV-B |
| Interaction supports model calibration | In one Opus Shelf run, fitting six kinematic parameters reduces position RMSE from 38.9 to 1.8 mm **on the calibration observations**. | §IV-B; Fig. 3 |
| Dynamic manipulation remains difficult | Dynamic3D mean success is 65% for Astra and 45% for Opus under the common evaluation protocol. | §IV-B |

These results support useful reuse across unseen instances drawn from the synthesis distribution. They do not establish real-world transfer or generalization to unseen task families. Counts exceed those in the original benchmark, but synthesis and evaluation share a distribution; this is not evidence of object-count extrapolation beyond synthesis exposure (§IV-A). Tables I–III contain captions but no numerical bodies in the supplied text, limiting independent inspection of aggregate results.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Full observability, object-centric states, and simulator access leave performance under perception uncertainty untested. Undisclosed training data prevent excluding prior benchmark exposure (§VI).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** Agentic versus LLMGenPlan comparisons change tool access, testing freedom, feedback, and thinking configuration. They support the complete workflow but do not isolate the contribution of autonomous experiment selection (§§III-A–B).
- **Analyst inference—source-access confounding:** Source access supplies information, reusable helpers, and arbitrary-state simulator manipulation together; the observed improvement cannot identify their separate effects (§III-A).
- **Analyst inference—efficiency scope:** Reported execution savings omit synthesis costs, and the per-action comparison selects perfect runs. Neither establishes total lifecycle efficiency across all generated programs (§§II-C, IV-B).
- **Analyst inference—memorization evidence:** Unexpected strategies and substantial revisions support active problem solving but cannot rule out partial reuse of pretrained benchmark knowledge (§VI).

## Open research questions
1. **Which agentic capability drives the gains?** Existing comparisons vary several capabilities together. A next experiment could hold model, reasoning configuration, and budget fixed while independently disabling custom tests and autonomous test selection.
2. **Do policies generalize beyond the synthesis distribution?** Held-out instances share that distribution. Synthesize on restricted object counts and geometries, then evaluate deliberately disjoint ranges.
3. **What accounts for source-access improvements?** Information and executable helpers are bundled. Compare read-only source inspection, helper reuse, and arbitrary-state simulator access separately.
4. **How robust are policies to imperfect observations and physics?** The study assumes fully observed simulation. Evaluate frozen programs under controlled state noise and altered physical parameters before testing hardware transfer.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the protocol and major findings, but detailed verification is limited by missing table bodies and plotted numerical data.
