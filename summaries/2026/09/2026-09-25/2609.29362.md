# Parts-of-Speech as Emergent Categories in SAE Latent Space

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29362) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29362)


## One-sentence takeaway
In LLaMA-3-8B, compact, overlapping groups of SAE latents predict English POS categories and remain active on held-out examples, but do not establish category-exclusive or causal grammatical features.

## Problem and motivation
High probing accuracy shows that linguistic information is recoverable, but does not explain how it is organized. The authors use independently annotated POS categories to test whether SAE representations expose individual grammatical features or distributed feature groups, addressing a gap between intuitive latent interpretations and systematic linguistic evaluation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Localization pipeline:** Extract layer-30 residual activations with a pretrained EleutherAI SAE, align each UD token to its leftmost overlapping subword, and train L1 logistic one-vs-rest probes. Rank positive coefficients and select the smallest group activating on at least 95% of each category’s training tokens (§§3–4).
- **Compact representation analysis:** Combine selected groups into 498 latents and evaluate multiclass POS classification, including random-replacement and dense-representation baselines (§§5.2, 5.4).
- **Stability and specificity analysis:** Evaluate group activation on held-out GUM and controlled sentences, revealing substantial recall alongside cross-category and positional activation. The authors also report supplementary layer and parameter comparisons (Appendices A.1, B.2–B.3).
- **Reported releases:** POS-aligned activations, controlled evaluation data, and probing/localization code (Appendix A.3).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Compact groups support POS prediction | The selected union contains 498 latents; 12% are shared by multiple POS categories. With all selected features, multiclass accuracy/macro-F1 is 0.87/0.76 in cross-validation and 0.89/0.81 in the reported train/test evaluation. | §5.2; Table 3 |
| Feature selection matters | With 0% overlap with the selected set, random replacements yield accuracy/macro-F1 of 0.24/0.16 in cross-validation and 0.23/0.16 in train/test evaluation. | Table 3 |
| SAE probes do not outperform dense probes | Table 2 reports accuracy/macro-F1: SAE 0.88/0.78, layer-30 activations 0.92/0.84, embeddings 0.88/0.78. The table does not clearly identify its evaluation split. | §5.4; Table 2 |
| Held-out activation coverage is high but uneven | On GUM test tokens, target-group recall is 0.994 for NUM, 0.949 for NOUN, 0.889 for SYM, and 0.727 for X. Reported mean distinctiveness is 0.27, with standard deviation 0.07. | §5.3; Table 8 |
| Controlled activation is insufficiently selective | Using any group activation as a POS prediction, Table 1’s average row reports precision 0.192, recall 0.937, and F1 0.318; template-level metrics are micro-averaged. | §5.3; Table 1 |
| Lexical control supports a gap, but its magnitude is unclear | Random word-type labels yield accuracy/macro-F1 0.54/0.42; prose gives real-label scores 0.88/0.97, conflicting with Table 2’s macro-F1 of 0.78. | §5.4; Table 2 |
| Compactness extends to sampled layers | Selected unions contain 530, 501, and 498 latents at layers 2, 15, and 30, respectively. This supports similar group budgets, not identical latent identities. | Appendix A.1 |

The evidence supports predictive, compact, partially shared POS representations. High activation recall does not imply category specificity: a group can activate on its target category and many others. The authors’ claim that lexical identity plays only a minor role is stronger than the control securely establishes, particularly given inconsistent metrics. No causal use of these latents by the language model is demonstrated.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One base model and SAE setup, English POS, restricted controlled constructions, and correlational probe coefficients limit generalization and causal interpretation. Supplementary layer checks partially address layer dependence (Limitations; Appendix A.1).
- **Author-observed confounds:** Groups respond to lexical variation, shared forms, context, and sentence position. Prefixing controlled sentences shifts first-token activations to the new initial token (§5.5; Appendix B.3.1).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation ambiguity:** The text does not establish whether supervised feature selection was repeated within each cross-validation fold. Selecting features before folding could inflate cross-validation estimates (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Analyst inference—metric interpretation:** The defined distinctiveness score normalizes across groups firing on tokens of a given category. It therefore does not directly measure whether that category’s group fires exclusively on that category, despite the prose interpretation (§5.3).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Real-label control scores conflict with Table 2; Appendix A.1 and Table 3 give different layer-30 compact-classifier accuracies without clearly reconciling settings. Table 5’s group summaries also do not match the listed Table 4 counts.

## Open research questions
- **Do these groups affect model behavior?** Prediction alone leaves causal relevance open. Ablate selected groups and matched random groups, then measure changes on controlled morphosyntactic tasks.
- **How much survives lexical separation?** Word-type randomization and simple templates do not isolate contextual abstraction fully. Repeat discovery and evaluation with disjoint word types and ambiguous forms presented in contrasting grammatical roles.
- **How general is the organization?** Sampled layers offer partial evidence within one model. Replicate the pipeline across models, SAE configurations, and typologically diverse annotated languages.
- **Can specificity improve without losing coverage?** Any-latent activation produces many false positives. Compare it with activation thresholds and joint latent patterns on held-out controlled constructions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The compact, overlapping-group finding has several supporting evaluations, but exact comparisons and abstraction claims are less secure.

Material gaps include numeric values embedded only in figures, unambiguous evaluation settings for several classifier tables, confirmation of fold-specific feature selection, variability across random-replacement runs, and reconciled inconsistent metrics. Artifact availability is reported by the authors but was not independently verified.
