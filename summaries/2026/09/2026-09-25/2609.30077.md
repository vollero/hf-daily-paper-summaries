# Rate-distortion optimization for full-reference image quality metrics via stochastic Hessian estimates

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30077) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30077)


## One-sentence takeaway
Stochastic, localized Hessian estimates enable perceptual-metric-driven VVC intra-coding, delivering reported target-metric BD-rate savings of 14.2–36.7% versus SSE-RDO at matched PerceptQPA PSNR costs, without decoder changes (Table 2; §4).

## Problem and motivation
Block-based encoders need distortions that decompose across blocks, whereas full-reference image quality assessment (FR-IQA) metrics generally require the complete reconstructed image. Existing alternatives either specialize in particular metrics or adjust block quantization while leaving other coding decisions driven by squared error (§1; Table 1).

The authors build on prior metric quadratization theory to address the practical gap: estimating a large, image-dependent Hessian and making its resulting distortion usable inside block-wise rate-distortion optimization (RDO).

## Approach and contributions
- **Localized quadratic distortion:** Approximate the target metric near the source image using its Hessian, then retain either its diagonal or block diagonal. The resulting input-dependent quadratic distortion decomposes across coding blocks (§§2–3.1).
- **Practical stochastic estimation:** Estimate those structures through automatic-differentiation Hessian-vector products. For squared-residual metrics, use Gauss–Newton vector-Jacobian estimates that are positive semidefinite by construction; adapt noise smoothing to stabilize neural-metric estimates (§3.1).
- **Encoder integration:** Add an identity regularizer to control the PSNR–target-metric trade-off and derive a Hessian-trace-based rescaling of the SSE Lagrangian under high-rate assumptions. Integrate the distortion into partitioning, prediction, transform selection, and quantization decisions (§3.2; §4).

The quadratic approximation itself comes from prior work; the paper’s contributions concern its estimation, localization, and codec integration (§§1–2).

## Evidence and main results
All main experiments use VTM 23.8, all-intra 8-bit 4:2:0, QPs {22, 27, 32, 37}, and an SSE-RDO anchor. Default estimation uses 256 probes and 8×8 tiles; Kodak selects settings, which are transferred to 41 CLIC images (§4).

These results support improved compression efficiency **under the selected target metric and PSNR trade-off**. They do not establish improved human-rated quality or simultaneous improvement across metrics: for example, SSIM-targeted block RDO worsens CLIC DISTS BD-rate by +5.7% (Table 2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ approximation boundary:** The theory relies on local, high-rate behavior. Block localization agrees in expectation when cross-tile errors are uncorrelated; diagonal localization requires the stronger pixel-wise condition (§§2–3.1).
- **Authors’ locality caveat:** LPIPS and especially DISTS retain little Hessian energy within tiles, making their approximation more dependent on error-correlation assumptions (§4).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization:** Evaluation covers still-image all-intra coding. Inter-frame video, other coding formats, and human preference are not evaluated (§4).
- **Analyst inference—trade-off sensitivity:** Main comparisons use interpolation over three regularization values to match PerceptQPA’s PSNR cost. Gains are conditional on these operating points, with visible cross-metric conflicts (Table 2).
- **Reporting gaps:** Exact regularization values, numerical confidence-interval endpoints, and estimator-run variability are unavailable. The text provides smoothing and probe-budget ablations, but no tile-size sweep or isolated validation of the Lagrangian rescaling (§4).

## Open research questions
- **Can broader localization improve neural metrics?** LPIPS and DISTS have substantial out-of-tile Hessian energy (§4). Compare larger or adaptive tiles at matched PSNR cost, measuring target BD-rate, memory, and runtime.
- **How robust is the approximation at lower rates?** Its justification assumes high-rate errors (§§2–3). Extend the QP range and measure surrogate-versus-target distortion agreement alongside cross-tile error correlations.
- **Do target-metric gains improve perceived quality?** No human study is reported, and metrics sometimes conflict (Table 2). Run blinded preference comparisons at matched bitrate.
- **Does the method transfer to inter-frame coding?** Only all-intra results are supplied (§4). Evaluate temporal prediction with per-frame Hessian estimates, including temporal consistency and total encoding cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and tabulated results; broader perceptual and video generalization remains unestablished.

Material missing information includes exact regularization settings, numerical bootstrap intervals, stochastic-run variability, detailed per-image results, and human or inter-frame evaluation. The linked code was not inspected; analysis relies exclusively on the supplied text.
