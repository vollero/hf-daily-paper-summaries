# WanPE: Towards Cinematic Prompt Enhancement for Modern Text-to-Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30221) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30221)


## One-sentence takeaway
WanPE learns cinematic prompts from video-derived supervision and semantic-consistency reinforcement learning, improving expert video preferences under a fixed Wan3.0 generator, with additional gains demonstrated on two generators after format adaptation (Tables 1–3).

## Problem and motivation
Longer video generation requires prompts that coordinate actions, camera movements, lighting, sound, and transitions while preserving user instructions across shots. The authors argue that conventional forward rewriting produces synthetic descriptions that differ from generators’ video-grounded training captions and can lose requirements during elaboration (§1).

The proposed gap is therefore both structural—learning coherent cinematic organization—and semantic—maintaining subjects, dialogue, event order, and other constraints throughout a plan.

## Approach and contributions
- **Video-grounded reverse supervision:** Caption approximately 1.05M real-world clips into video summaries and timestamped shot descriptions, then use GPT-5.4 with human-request demonstrations to reconstruct compatible user prompts. Fine-tuning learns the prompt-to-cinematic-plan mapping (§2.2; Appendices A–B).
- **Semantic-Consistency GRPO:** Train on approximately 15K human-written prompts, including difficult cases identified through generated-video inspection. A Qwen3.7-Max text evaluator rewards preservation across nine dimensions, including actions, dialogue, camera, and spatial relations (§2.3).
- **WanPEval and scaling study:** Introduce 249 curated requests spanning 5–30 seconds and three instruction granularities; evaluate models from 4B to 397B with text scoring and approximately 11K blind video comparisons involving 60 film-domain experts (§§2.4–3; Appendix C).

The methodological contribution is the combination of reverse-constructed cinematic supervision and constraint-focused GRPO; GRPO itself is an existing method.

## Evidence and main results
Reported preference scores below use the paper’s percentage scale. **S** counts wins plus half of “both good” judgments, divided by valid comparisons; “both poor” receives no credit. Bradley–Terry fitting treats both kinds of joint judgment as ties (§2.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Enhancement improves fixed-generator performance | With Wan3.0, WanPE-397B raises S from 46.08→56.74 at 5 seconds, 33.63→49.91 at 10 seconds, and 30.59→49.43 at 15 seconds. | Table 1 |
| Highest reported short-duration system scores | On the 5–15-second arena, WanPE-397B + Wan3.0 achieves overall S=50.61 and BT=61.85, versus Seedance 2.0’s 43.42 and 54.70; it leads all evaluated systems on both metrics. | Table 1 |
| Large gain at 30 seconds; similar aggregate score to Seedance | In the separate 30-second arena, Wan3.0’s original-request S=9.38 rises to 60.24 with WanPE-397B; Seedance 2.5 scores 59.76. | Table 2, Exp1 |
| Reverse supervision beats tested forward alternatives | Under Wan3.0, reverse-trained WanPE-397B-SFT scores S=49.86, versus 39.49 for Forward Rewriting and 35.17 for Forward-target SFT. Forward-target SFT uses the same reconstructed inputs and training settings. | §3.2; Table 2, Exp2 |
| Transfer works after conversion | In separate 5–15-second comparisons, GPT-5.4-adapted WanPE prompts raise S from 21.11→35.56 on LTX-2.5-Base and 35.92→41.09 on MiniMax-H3-Base versus native enhancers. | §3.3; Table 2, Exp3 |
| SC-GRPO improves text fidelity and downstream preference | Gemini-evaluated semantic scores improve by 18.6–23.3 points across model sizes; 397B rises from 75.5→97.6. In the Wan3.0 video ablation, 397B S rises from 42.70→49.69 after SC-GRPO. | Table 3 |

The fixed-generator ablations support benefits from both training stages. Commercial-system comparisons assess entire pipelines and cannot isolate enhancer quality. Scores depend on each arena’s opponents and should not be compared directly across experiments. The near-equal 30-second scores establish neither statistical equivalence nor a reliable advantage.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied paper has no explicit limitations section. The following are **analyst inferences**:

- **Uncertainty is underreported:** Each method generates one video per request; confidence intervals, repeated-generation variability, and inter-rater agreement are absent. The displayed preference intervals represent alternative credit for “both good,” not statistical uncertainty (§2.4; Tables 5–6).
- **Failure exclusion may affect rankings:** Unsuccessful generations are excluded, but per-system exclusion counts are not reported (§2.4).
- **Text preservation is a proxy:** SC-GRPO rewards textual consistency, not whether pixels and audio realize each requirement. Aggregate video preferences support usefulness but do not establish constraint-level execution (§§2.3, 3.4).
- **Transfer has a conversion confound:** GPT-5.4 reformats prompts, and no conversion-only control isolates its contribution (§3.3).
- **Scope and reproducibility are limited:** Evaluation covers a small curated benchmark through 30 seconds. Exact captioner identity, full training hyperparameters, dataset provenance, and inference costs are unavailable; reporting experiments on 512 GPUs does not establish deployment efficiency (§3; Appendices A–C).

## Open research questions
- **Does reverse supervision help because of grounding or prompt structure?** The forward comparisons do not fully separate these factors. Next experiment: match length, shot schema, and detail density while varying whether targets come from real videos (§3.2).
- **How closely does text fidelity predict rendered fidelity?** The paper reports text scores and aggregate preferences, without constraint-level linkage. Next experiment: annotate whether each requested action, speaker binding, and event order appears correctly in generated videos (§3.4).
- **How much transfer comes from WanPE versus the adapter?** Conversion is part of every transfer result. Next experiment: compare deterministic formatting and GPT-5.4 conversion, including native prompts passed through the same adapter (§3.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The tables consistently support gains in the reported settings, but broader reliability remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty, generation-failure counts, rater agreement, definitions of “Perfect” and “Failure,” complete training and generation settings, captioner identity, full reward prompts, inference latency/cost, and confirmed release availability for weights, training data, and WanPEval.
