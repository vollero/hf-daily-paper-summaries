# OmniEcho: Spatial Audio Understanding for Embodied Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23407) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23407)


## One-sentence takeaway
OmniEcho adds a pretrained spatial-audio encoder to Qwen3-Omni, improving accuracy on the authors’ real-recorded spatial QA benchmark while leaving substantial navigation, stopping, and distance-estimation challenges (Tables 1–4, 16–17).

## Problem and motivation
Embodied agents need to locate sounds outside their camera view and combine acoustic geometry, visual observations, and sound semantics to navigate. The authors identify three gaps: expensive real-world spatial-audio annotation, insufficient geometric consistency in synthetic training data, and difficulty integrating spatial cues into pretrained omni-modal models (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** OmniEchoBench combines spatial QA over 197 recorded scenes and 2,972 questions with 900 navigation episodes across 30 scanned indoor environments. Navigation runs in Habitat using real FOA recordings selected by agent position; it is not a quantitative physical-robot benchmark (§§3.1–3.2, A.4.2).
- **Synthetic supervision:** A shared renderer converts sound stems and source/listener trajectories into first-order ambisonics (FOA). The pipelines create spatial QA and augment existing navigation trajectories with destination sounds, attenuation, and selected acoustic effects (§3.3, Appendix A.1).
- **Model and training:** A spatial encoder processes acoustic features alongside the backbone’s native semantic-audio pathway. Training first aligns sound representations with text, then teaches query-conditioned localization, and finally tunes the language model and spatial projector while freezing the encoders (§4.2).

The distinctive contribution is this combined benchmark, synthesis pipeline, and perception/navigation model; the authors’ broader priority claim is not independently established by the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported spatial QA accuracy | On OmniEchoBench-QA, FOA+vision OmniEcho achieves **28.5%** overall accuracy versus **18.5%** for single-channel+vision Qwen3-Omni and **11.4%** for FOA+vision SO-7B. | Table 1 |
| Both audio pathways help | QA overall accuracy is **28.5%** with both frozen encoders, **19.8%** without the FOA encoder, and **26.2%** without the native audio encoder; updating the FOA encoder during integration also gives **26.2%**. | Table 4 |
| Real-recorded navigation remains difficult | OmniEchoBench-Nav, self-stop and **1 m** success radius: OmniEcho **16.2% SR / 11.5% SPL**; binaural SoundSpaces **5.4% / 3.9%**; text-guided InternVLA-N1 **17.8% / 16.7%**. | Table 2; Appendix A.4.2 |
| Stronger text-guided navigation remains ahead | R2R val-unseen with simulated audio, self-stop and **3 m** radius: OmniEcho **22.2% SR / 16.3% SPL**, versus text-guided CMA **22.0% / 20.0%** and InternVLA-N1 **50.6% / 43.3%**. | Table 3; Appendix A.4.2 |
| Reaching the vicinity often fails to produce completion | Across **900** OmniEchoBench-Nav episodes, **52.89%** enter the **1 m** goal region, but **16.22%** terminate successfully; reported distance-estimation MAE is **2.20 m**. | Tables 16–17 |
| Ground-truth direction changes navigation behavior | On OmniEchoBench-Nav under the same execution/self-stop protocol, GT direction raises SR from **16.2% to 23.7%** and SPL from **11.5% to 22.1%**, while oracle success falls from **52.89% to 34.33%**. | Table 18 |
| Real-data adaptation is promising but comparison needs clarification | Table 15 reports QA overall accuracy **28.5% → 34.9%** after adaptation on **1,000** samples with a scenario-disjoint holdout; its baseline exactly repeats the full-benchmark scores from Table 1. | Appendix A.5.1, Table 15 |

These results support benefits from the spatial pathway on the authors’ benchmark. They do not establish general navigation superiority: guidance modalities, backbones, and baseline compatibility differ. QA accuracy also reflects relatively broad tolerances—**±45°** for angular answers and **±2 m plus correct bearing** for localization—rather than precise geometric reconstruction (Table 13).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged:** Synthetic-to-real mismatch, unreliable distance estimates, inefficient navigation, and stopping remain problems (§5.2, Appendix A.5).
- **Author-acknowledged:** Audio-navigation baselines cannot be fully aligned with the proposed semantic-plus-spatial task. Executing several actions between decisions also means the oracle-success gap cannot be attributed solely to stopping errors (§5.2.2, Appendix A.5.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Nearest-receiver playback approximates continuously changing acoustics; single-source indoor episodes leave robustness to competing or moving navigation targets unestablished. Physical deployment evidence is qualitative (Appendix A.2.2, Fig. 20).
- **Analyst inference:** Missing uncertainty estimates limit confidence in small differences. The adaptation table needs confirmation that both rows use the identical held-out subset.
- **Analyst inference:** Deterministic scoring follows an LLM extraction step, so complete reproducibility also depends on extractor stability and configuration (Appendix A.4.1).

## Open research questions
- **What most limits successful stopping?** Distance errors and action batching are confounded. Test calibrated proximity prediction and shorter replanning intervals independently on identical episodes (Appendix A.5.2).
- **How much real data improves transfer?** The adaptation result suggests benefits but leaves baseline comparability unclear. Run a data-size sweep with fixed scenario-disjoint evaluation and matched pre-adaptation scores (Appendix A.5.1).
- **Does performance survive continuous, competing acoustics?** Navigation uses a single source and discrete recording locations. Evaluate continuous robot recordings with controlled distractors and moving targets (Appendix A.2.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture, benchmark construction, and principal results are described in detail. Material gaps include statistical uncertainty, confirmed artifact availability, an unambiguous adaptation comparison, extractor reliability measurements, quantitative physical-robot evaluation, and inference latency.
