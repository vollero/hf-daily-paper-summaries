# PUBG Ally: A Conversational Embodied Agent as an AI Teammate

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29837) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29837)


## One-sentence takeaway
PUBG Ally combines a distilled on-device language model with a reactive game controller to deliver a voice-enabled teammate whose offline scores approach its teacher’s, while live players report positive recommendation alongside weak combat performance.

## Problem and motivation
An AI teammate must coordinate speech, intentions, and actions while a multiplayer game continues changing during inference. The authors argue that autonomous gameplay competence alone does not establish teammate quality: responsiveness, cooperation, and communication also matter (§1).

The training and evaluation gap is interactive: agent behavior changes what human partners do next, making static dialogue data and offline scores incomplete substitutes for actual play (§§4, 6).

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated teammate architecture:** An event-driven language model queries bounded observation tools, communicates, and issues high-level actions; a stateful behavior tree executes movement, combat, and recovery independently of model latency. Context compaction preserves unfinished plans while discarding stale observations (§3; Appendices B–C).
- **Training from human–agent gameplay:** The authors collected 38,956 sessions from 1,046 participants. A 31B teacher supplies demonstrations and corrected student trajectories; an intermediate 8B model supports off-policy and agentic on-policy distillation into locale-specific, approximately 2B students. No additional task-reward RL objective is used (§4).
- **Player-informed evaluation:** Offline graders, weights, and comparison rules are iteratively revised using blinded gameplay preferences and localized player feedback (§6).
- **Contextual safety and deployment:** Dedicated safety training distinguishes game combat from harmful real-world speech; output filtering and model-triggered memory redaction supplement training. Quantization and adapted speech models enable local deployment in Korean, English, and Chinese (§5; Appendices G–H).

The contribution is principally the integrated system and development process. The authors’ commercial “first” claim cannot be independently established from the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Distilled student approaches teacher on offline capability | Student versus optimized teacher: factual grounding **0.932/0.944**, event response **0.954/0.952**, intent **0.953/0.960**, instruction commitment **0.876/0.889**, trajectory quality **0.902/0.910**. All paired 95% bootstrap intervals overlap on held-out gameplay contexts. | §7.1; Fig. 6 |
| Local inference reduces latency | On RTX 4060-class collection PCs, median LM latency is **0.80 s local versus 2.54 s cloud**; composed single-exchange latency is approximately **1.62 versus 3.36 s**, using the same STT/TTS engines. LM timing covers **11,246 local and 28,336 cloud sessions**. | §7.3; Table 8 |
| Production-like safety remains harder than broad benchmarks | Final model-only harmless response rates: Korean **98.3% benchmark / 84.1% production**, English **99.4% / 99.0%**, Chinese **99.3% / 89.3%**. Production rates after filtering: **91.0%, 99.0%, 92.1%**, respectively. Metric excludes silent trajectories. | Table 6; Appendix K |
| Safety gains accompany more benign refusals | Over-refusal rises from capability-only to final models: Korean **0.4%→5.0%**, English **0.8%→6.1%**, Chinese **0.6%→2.2%**, on curated benign game utterances. | Table 7 |
| In-game safety data improves contextual handling | Korean model-only production harmless rate changes **68.8%→70.5%** with broad synthetic safety data, then reaches **84.1%** after adding real in-game safety data. Final boundary-breached performance remains **63.6%**. | §7.2; Fig. 7 |
| Cloud-versus-student preference is latency-confounded | Initial blinded comparison favors local v1: **69.5% versus 18.8%**, **n=154**. Cloud latency was degraded; a separate comparable-latency retest favors cloud **52.4% versus 31.1%**. Retest sample size is not supplied. | §7.3; Table 10 |
| Only some successive student updates clearly improve preference | v3 beats v2 **42.3% versus 27.6%**, **n=156, p=0.03**; v5 beats v4 **54.8% versus 26.2%**, **n=84, p=0.005**. Other student comparisons are not statistically significant. | Table 10 |
| Positive live recommendation coexists with weak gameplay ratings | Among self-selected survey respondents with verified play, net recommendation is **+25.1 percentage points**, **95% CI [23.4, 26.7]**. Combat skill averages **2.58/5** and command following **2.99/5**. | §8; Fig. 14 |

These results support practical deployment and strong task adaptation within the tested setting. Overlapping capability intervals do not establish equivalence, safety rates are conditional on generated speech, and surveys do not establish improved competitive outcomes or representative population satisfaction.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Live participants self-selected into both the mode and survey; PC bang participants were compensated, experienced adults. Comparisons between these populations are descriptive (§8; Appendix D).
- **Author-stated:** Players reported delayed revives, persistent unwanted goals, inconsistent memory, unfulfilled commands, and distracting speech (§§7.3, 8).
- **Author-stated:** Keyword filtering does not guarantee contextual safety; input redaction depends on correct model detection (§5.3). Safety reference checkpoints are not controlled ablations, and the final Korean recipe combines distillation with knowledge injection (Table 6; Fig. 7).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied results do not isolate the effects of event scheduling, context compaction, teacher corrections, or on-policy distillation. Aggregate system improvements cannot establish each component’s contribution.
- **Analyst inference:** Repeated evaluator refinement against accumulated player feedback warrants a fresh prospective validation set; the supplied text does not quantify held-out preference-prediction accuracy.
- **Coverage:** The source is truncated, including part of the conclusion and intervening material; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions
1. **What causes the gap between strong offline scores and weak gameplay ratings?** Live complaints persist despite teacher-like scores. Test targeted controller and speech–action coordination changes in blinded matches, measuring command completion and player preference.
2. **How much does each training stage contribute?** The reported teacher–student comparison bundles multiple stages. Compare equal-data students with and without trajectory corrections and on-policy distillation.
3. **Does evaluator refinement generalize to new players and builds?** Refinement uses accumulated development feedback. Freeze the suite and prospectively test preference predictions on new participants and unseen model variants.
4. **Can safety recovery improve without increasing over-refusal?** Boundary-breached dialogue remains difficult. Test recovery-focused supervision on held-out multi-turn attacks alongside benign tactical speech.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Architecture and reported comparisons are well documented, but causal attribution and generalization remain limited.

Unavailable or unclear in the supplied text: public release of code, weights, or gameplay data; exact capability and safety evaluation-set sizes; total live-survey denominator; complete training hyperparameters; component-isolating ablations; and the omitted source material.
