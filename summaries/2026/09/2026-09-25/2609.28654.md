# Training Object Permanence in World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28654) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28654)


## One-sentence takeaway
WROP provides synthetic training data and a benchmark for object permanence and solidity, with its fine-tuned model ranking highest among evaluated video-continuation systems, although the study does not isolate the causal benefit of training (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Video generators can lose object identities during occlusion, allow objects through barriers, or fail to connect support removal with falling. The authors argue that these failures undermine broader physical reasoning and identify a shortage of large training resources and structured, three-dimensional video-continuation evaluations for these capabilities (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dataset and benchmark:** WROP contains 150 hand-authored Blender generators across three object-permanence and three solidity families, a 1.5-million-sample training corpus, and a 300-question exam. Samples include context and target videos, prompts, trajectories, and scene metadata. Motion uses authored keyframes rather than a physics solver (§3).
- **Training baseline:** PWM-WROP fine-tunes the 16B Cosmos3-Nano model for one epoch on an earlier rendering of the same generators. Architecture and tokenizer are unchanged; supervision uses videos and prompts, without trajectory or task-label supervision (§4.1).
- **Evaluation:** Fourteen systems are compared using blind human preferences and secondary reference-video similarity metrics, with explicit distinctions between continuation, reference-to-video, and editing interfaces (§4).
- **Engineering asset:** The authors report releasing PWM, a native-PyTorch Trainium2 training stack, alongside data, weights, outputs, and scores; Appendix B documents optimizations and correctness checks.

| Performance varies by physical concept | Among 14 models, PWM-WROP ranks **first** on static occlusion but **eighth** on collision; family rankings have wide uncertainty, especially solidity families with **36–44 total judgments each**. | Figure 6; Appendix C, Table 6 |

These results establish strong comparative performance on WROP, particularly for continuation and occlusion tasks. They do **not** establish a controlled improvement over the untuned base model on the exam, generalization to unseen generators or real scenes, or human-like internal representations. Human ratings jointly assess prompt alignment, motion, and physical consistency; automatic metrics measure reference similarity rather than physical correctness (§§4.4–4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged confounds:** Architectures and interfaces differ, preventing attribution of performance gaps to training alone. Native resolutions also differ, and target-fit metrics can reward static scene reproduction (§§4.2, 5.1.1, 5.3).
- **Author-acknowledged uncertainty:** Family-level comparisons are sparse and have wide confidence intervals; ranks indicate tendencies rather than established differences (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Training and examination use the same generators. No held-out-generator or real-video evaluation is reported, leaving transfer beyond these task templates unresolved (§§3.2, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference—physical validity:** Ground truth is analytically authored animation; automated audits check files and schemas, while manual inspection covers representative samples. This does not independently establish physical correctness across the entire corpus (§3.3).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Structural and camera variation described in §3.1 contrasts with their preservation in the shared generation driver (§3.3). The universal 60-frame split description also conflicts with two 90-frame evaluation items (§4.3). These differences need clarification for reproduction.

## Open research questions
- **Does WROP training itself improve physical reasoning?** The main exam lacks an untuned Cosmos3-Nano control. Evaluate base and fine-tuned checkpoints with identical inference settings and blinded judgments.
- **Do learned constraints transfer beyond generator templates?** Training and exam share generators. Hold out entire generators and structural configurations, then test transfer to separately constructed scenes and real videos.
- **Why is collision performance weaker?** Family rankings suggest uneven capability, but uncertainty is substantial. Expand collision evaluation and compare balanced training with added contact or trajectory supervision.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The main results and evaluation settings are explicit, but causal and generalization claims remain weakly supported.

Material missing information includes a controlled base-model exam comparison, train/exam seed-disjointness details, precise structural randomization coverage, full fine-tuning hyperparameters and total compute, and a reconciled account of exceptional clip lengths. Release availability is asserted by the authors and was not independently checked.
