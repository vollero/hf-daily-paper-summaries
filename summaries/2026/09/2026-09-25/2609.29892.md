# Qwen-Planner-Agent: A Closed-Loop AI-for-AI Framework for Real-World Mobile Planner Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29892) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29892)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that combining planner training, competence-adaptive reinforcement learning, and a memory-and-skills harness achieves the highest observed MobilePA-Bench score, while evidence for sustained autonomous improvement remains limited.

## Problem and motivation

Mobile assistants must coordinate applications, track changing state, recover from failures, and verify completion across long workflows. Real-device interaction is costly and difficult to parallelize, limiting training and evaluation. The authors address how execution feedback can guide coordinated improvements to data, model training, and runtime support rather than treating these as separate development stages (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Integrated development loop:** AI-assisted task construction, trajectory diagnosis, and data reweighting connect to training and harness revisions. Programmatic sandboxes, LLM simulations, and selected real-device sessions share structured action, feedback, and verification records. Dataset releases and consequential updates retain human review (§§2.1–2.3).
- **CARE optimization:** Following supervised training that masks erroneous action turns, online RL switches between progress rewards, success-only rewards, and efficiency penalties according to sampled-group success. A success-derived floor on advantage normalization limits amplification of small efficiency differences; a bounded LLM controller configures scheduling parameters (§2.4).
- **Planner–harness adaptation:** A runtime supplies tool-conditioned skills, persistent memory, and execution feedback. Offline development alternates policy training with LLM-edited harness instructions. Model parameters stay fixed during serving; the authors explicitly frame autonomous co-evolution as a future development pathway (§2.5).

The distinctive contribution is this integration and CARE’s competence-dependent optimization, rather than a new visual screen-grounding architecture (§4.2).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Highest observed mobile Overall score | On MobilePA-Bench, with 1,700+ executable tasks, 200+ tools, and 13 domains, the 27B agent scores **77.05%**, versus **67.22%** for its Qwen baseline and **76.84%** for GPT 6 Astra. | §3.1, Table 1 |
| Harness improves fixed planners | Holding trained checkpoints fixed, Overall rises **71.90% → 77.05%** for 27B and **64.79% → 69.91%** for 35B-A3B on MobilePA-Bench. | §3.2.2, Table 1 |
| CARE reduces generated output | Across four training environments, full CARE uses **32.5% fewer output tokens** than Vanilla RL at the final shared plotted step, with comparable smoothed training accuracy. Removing calibration shortens outputs further but lowers accuracy. | §3.2.1, Fig. 6 |
| Alternating updates improve reported scores | Using a separate 27B checkpoint, MobilePA-Internal Overall progresses from model-only **82.67%**, to harness baseline **84.23%**, to co-evolution **88.50%** after four iterations. MCPMark scores are **38.00%**, **42.26%**, and **46.98%**, respectively, after three iterations. | §§3.2.3, Table 2, Fig. 7 |
| Memory helps beyond direct-context capacity | For the same 27B planner, BEAM average rubric scores rise **42.01% → 72.20%** at 500K history, **40.12% → 73.71%** at 1M, and **21.99% → 67.24%** at 10M. Direct inference uses a 256K budget with earliest-history truncation; the harness processes full histories. | §3.3, Table 3 |
| Non-mobile gains are uneven | Across seven agentic benchmarks, arithmetic mean scores rise **57.74% → 62.31%** for 35B-A3B and **67.91% → 69.29%** for 27B. Across three general-capability benchmarks, means decrease **90.35% → 89.92%** and **90.05% → 89.70%**, respectively. | §3.4, Table 4 |
| Low estimated output-token cost | Estimated cost is **$2.41 per 1,000 tasks**, versus **$3.06–$67.76** for compared models, including thinking tokens but excluding input, tools, device execution, and harness processing. | §3.1, Fig. 1 |

The paired checkpoint comparisons support a benefit from the combined harness. CARE’s plotted results support reduced output length during training. These findings do not establish statistically significant superiority over the nearest competitor, lower total operating cost, or equal-resource superiority in memory evaluation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Simulations can produce inconsistent states, programmatic environments cover only implemented transitions, and real devices have costly interactions and difficult resets (§2.2). Sustained autonomous co-evolution is explicitly not established (§2.5.4).
- **Analyst inference—uncertain attribution:** Co-evolution lacks a matched-budget continued-training baseline with a fixed harness. Its gains therefore do not isolate the benefit of alternating revisions from additional training. The evolve set guides revisions; independent final-test status for Table 2 is not clearly specified (§3.2.3).
- **Analyst inference—incomplete ablations:** The harness comparison combines skills, memory, context, and feedback. Separate contributions of these components, the data flywheel, backend mixture, and LLM reward controller are not isolated (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** No confidence intervals or repeated-run variability accompany the main ranking. Memory comparisons differ in accessible history and processing cost; externally reported Mem0 results are reference points rather than matched experiments (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Author-stated security boundary:** User-level memory authorization depends on trusted upstream services. Additional controls are described as requirements or recommendations, and reducing identifying details does not guarantee anonymity (§2.5.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Does alternating harness revision outperform equally funded model training?** Current baselines do not isolate this effect. Compare fixed-harness RL, harness-only editing, and alternating updates under equal budgets on an untouched test set.
2. **How much does real-device experience contribute?** Backend proportions and separate transfer results are unavailable. Vary the training mixture and evaluate identical tasks on live devices with controlled execution disturbances.
3. **Does CARE improve total execution efficiency?** Evidence centers on training output length and output-token estimates. Measure held-out success, tool calls, latency, and complete operating costs under matched execution budgets.
4. **Which memory mechanisms justify their overhead?** Gains concentrate in histories exceeding direct-context budgets, while shorter-history results vary. Ablate consolidation, source verification, and retrieval under matched processing budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the architecture and reports useful paired comparisons, but causal attribution and reproducibility remain incomplete.

Material missing information includes exact backbone identities, training-data quantities and backend proportions, concrete training and CARE configurations, controller/editor identities and prompts, repeated-run uncertainty, independent co-evolution test details, complete cost accounting, memory valid-execution coverage, and confirmed public availability of implementation artifacts.
