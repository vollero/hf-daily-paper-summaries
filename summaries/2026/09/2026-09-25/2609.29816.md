# AV-GRPO: Modality-Anchored Decoupling Diffusion Reinforcement Learning for Joint Audio-Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29816) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29816)


## One-sentence takeaway
AV-GRPO alternates reinforcement-learning updates with one modality’s complete denoising trajectory fixed, improving most reported audio-video generation metrics on LTX-2.3, although component-level attribution and training-budget comparability remain unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Joint audio-video generators can produce perceptual artifacts, miss prompt details, or generate poorly synchronized streams. The authors argue that jointly updating both modality towers with aggregated rewards obscures credit assignment, increases memory requirements, and compares synchronization scores across counterparts of unequal difficulty (§1). Different audio and video optimization dynamics further undermine a shared training configuration.

## Approach and contributions
- **Anchored conditional optimization:** Candidates within a group share one complete audio or video trajectory. Only the other modality varies, making synchronization comparisons conditional on a common counterpart; the constant anchor reward drops out (§2.2).
- **Trajectory locking and alternating tower freezing:** Anchor states remain fixed during sampling and policy updates. Only the target tower trains, and the roles periodically switch, reducing backward-pass requirements and directing the reward signal to the active tower (§2.2).
- **Modality-specific stabilization:** Adaptive Noise Clipping bounds stochastic increments; audio and video use different noise strengths and objectives. Video reweighting follows LongCat-Video, reward normalization follows GDPO, and a CLAP guardrail addresses observed audio reward hacking (§§2.3–2.4; Appendix D). These are adaptations within the framework, not all independently new methods.
- **5DAV training asset:** The authors construct 5,760 prompts across 288 combinations of semantic hierarchy, sound source, synchronization difficulty, temporal complexity, and instruction granularity (§3.1).
- **Idealized theoretical analysis:** Appendix A establishes joint optimality and convergence for exact conditional Gibbs kernels under explicit compatibility, contraction, and non-interference assumptions.

The tables support broad improvements in reported automatic metrics across both benchmarks, with explicit tradeoffs between LoRA and full fine-tuning. They do not establish statistical significance, human preference gains, or the independent contribution of every proposed mechanism.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-observed failures:** Audio optimization can improve quality and synchronization rewards while sacrificing CLAP; the guardrail addresses this behavior. The authors also report video overexposure and instability under unsuitable objectives or noise strengths (§2.4; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** CLIP, CLAP, and DeSync appear in both optimization and evaluation, so improvements on these metrics are not fully independent validation. Other reported metrics broaden the evidence, but no direct human study is supplied (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference—unclear budget matching:** Appendix B describes a 480-step learning-rate schedule, a 1,440-step AV-GRPO run, and 480 GDPO steps. Its data-exposure explanation is difficult to reconcile with those counts, leaving compute and exposure matching unclear.
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** Reported ablations cover data and alternation, but do not separately quantify trajectory locking, freezing, noise clipping, reweighting, or the CLAP guardrail (§3.4).
- **Explicit theoretical boundary:** The guarantee concerns idealized exact kernels and matching alternating inference. It does not establish convergence of practical finite-step GRPO training or ordinary joint sampling (Appendix A.12).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Post-training experiments use one backbone and one clip configuration; transfer to other architectures or longer sequences is untested in the supplied text (§3.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Which mechanisms drive the gains?** Component effects remain unseparated. Run matched-budget ablations of anchoring, freezing, noise clipping, and modality-specific objectives.
2. **Do improvements survive independent perceptual evaluation?** Several evaluation metrics are also rewards. Run blinded human comparisons and synchronization tests using evaluators excluded from training.
3. **Does the method transfer beyond the tested setting?** Only LTX-2.3 at a fixed clip configuration is evaluated. Repeat post-training on another interacting-tower model and longer clips, measuring quality, synchronization, memory, and runtime.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and headline table results are explicit, but causal and reproducibility claims have weaker support.

Material missing or ambiguous information includes numerical Figure 3 results, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variability, evaluation sample counts, precise GDPO budget matching, measured efficiency savings, LoRA rank, noise-clipping and CLAP-guardrail thresholds, prompt-generation/validation procedures, and an accessible code/data release address.
