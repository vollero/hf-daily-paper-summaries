# RGBD20K: A Large-Scale Benchmark for RGB-D Semantic Segmentation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29028) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29028)


## One-sentence takeaway
RGBD20K combines 20,000 reannotated RGB-D pairs across 160 indoor semantic classes with a score-guided fusion baseline that achieves 46.3% mIoU versus GeminiFusion’s 45.4% on its test set (Table I; Table III).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that RGB-D segmentation benchmarks constrain progress through limited semantic coverage, insufficient training data, and noisy pixel labels. Their target gap is fine-grained indoor segmentation with carefully refined boundaries, rather than the reconstruction-oriented objectives of larger existing collections (§§I–II).

## Approach and contributions
- **Curated benchmark:** RGBD20K unifies seven existing sources into 20,000 aligned RGB-D pairs, covering 160 classes and 75 scene types. Its stratified split contains 18,000 training and 2,000 testing pairs, with every class represented in both subsets (Tables I–II; §§III-C, III-E).
- **Annotation refinement:** Manual labeling and repeated inspection use hierarchical categories, depth-aware overlap ordering, and object–part relationships. The authors also construct SUN RGB-D† with refined annotations while retaining its original category setting (§§III-D, V-B).
- **Score-purified fusion (SPF):** Building on GeminiFusion, SPF learns reciprocal reliability scores to weight within-modality and cross-modal features before forming attention keys and values. A hierarchical encoder and SegFormer decoder produce segmentation masks; the new component is the fusion module (§§IV-A–B).
- **Benchmarking:** The paper compares representative segmentation methods and tests SPF’s projections, scoring, noise components, and score-head architecture (Tables III–VI).

The evidence supports SPF’s superiority **among the reported methods and settings**, and shows that annotation changes materially affect evaluation. It does not isolate improved training from changed test labels in the full reannotation comparison, or establish generalization to unseen environments. Scores across datasets with different taxonomies are not directly comparable measures of difficulty.

## Limitations and validity risks
The authors provide no dedicated limitations discussion. The following are **analyst inferences from the reported scope and omissions**:

- **Split independence is unclear:** RGBD20K incorporates SUN RGB-D and tracking-oriented sources, but the stratified split description does not specify scene/sequence isolation, deduplication, or preservation of source benchmark partitions. This leaves overlap risks unresolved, without establishing that leakage occurred (§III-E; Table II).
- **Generalization claims exceed the experiments:** Evaluation uses indoor, fixed-category benchmarks; cross-dataset transfer, unseen classes, controlled depth corruption, and RGB-only comparisons are not reported (§V).
- **Annotation quality lacks independent measurement:** Manual refinement and illustrative examples are described, but annotator agreement and independent boundary-quality audits are absent. Higher scores against revised labels alone do not establish label correctness (§III-D; Fig. 5; §V-B).
- **Comparisons do not isolate capacity or data effects:** SPF has more parameters than GeminiFusion; no matched-capacity control or controlled dataset-size/taxonomy ablation is shown. Baselines also use different backbones (Table III).
- **Uncertainty and efficiency remain unmeasured:** No repeated-run variability, latency, or FLOPs are reported. The noise discussion emphasizes keys, while the equations include noise in both keys and values, leaving an implementation ambiguity (§IV-B; §V-C).

## Open research questions
- **Does RGBD20K improve transfer?** Separate benchmark scores do not demonstrate transfer. Train with and without RGBD20K, then evaluate on a source-disjoint indoor target with compatible labels.
- **Does SPF specifically resist unreliable depth?** Component ablations do not test the claimed noise-suppression mechanism directly. Compare SPF and GeminiFusion under controlled depth noise, missing pixels, and misalignment.
- **Which dataset changes drive learning gains?** Scale, taxonomy, and annotation quality change together. Hold model and independently audited test labels fixed while varying training-set size and annotation refinement.
- **How well are rare classes learned?** The dataset is explicitly long-tailed, but frequency-stratified results are absent. Report per-class and frequency-group IoU across methods.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Dataset specifications and aggregate results are explicit, but broader robustness and generalization claims remain unverified.

Material missing information includes split manifests and overlap controls, independent annotation audits, per-class scores, repeated-run uncertainty, full training/preprocessing settings, runtime measurements, and verified contents of the announced dataset/model release.
