# Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29845) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29845)


## One-sentence takeaway
Averaging two text streams’ embeddings preserves some next-token information in pretrained Transformers, and targeted training improves preservation, but separate decoding remains substantially weaker than independent inference.

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether approximate linear structure inside Transformers extends to their end-to-end predictions: can one mixed input preserve the outputs of two independent contexts? This could support shared computation and smaller caches. Their identified gap is evidence for *intrinsic* superposition in ordinary pretrained models, rather than multiplexing enabled by dedicated architectural machinery (§§1, 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Measure intrinsic superposition:** Average aligned token embeddings from unrelated texts, then compare the mixed output with the arithmetic mean of independent next-token distributions using ranks, distributional distances, and hidden-state additivity (§2).
- **Investigate its origins:** Track Pythia checkpoints and replace attention weights with unrelated-text or position-permuted weights to examine training dynamics and the contribution of attention structure (§§2.4, 3).
- **Improve preservation:** Train a student on mixed embeddings to match the averaged predictions of a frozen teacher (§4).
- **Attempt separate decoding:** Joint Contrastive decoding combines shared backbone logits with positive guidance from each stream’s small-model logits and negative guidance from the other stream. Additional separation variants and performance benchmarks appear in Appendix G (§5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Pretrained mixing preserves some preferred tokens | For illustrated Pythia-2.8B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Qwen2.5-3B models, approximately 30–40% of single-stream preferred tokens remain top-10; 50–60% remain top-50. | §2.2; Fig. 1 |
| Mixed distributions are closer to their target than unrelated predictions are to each other | On FineWeb, Pythia-2.8B at context length 32 has KL 1.86 and normalized KL ratio 0.42. KL/JS use temperature 1.5. The denominator compares independent outputs on unrelated contexts. | Table 1; Eq. 3; p. 4 footnote |
| Hidden-state additivity decreases during pretraining | Across plotted Pythia checkpoints, layer-averaged normalized additivity error increases with training. | §2.4; Fig. 2 |
| Fine-tuning particularly improves content-position agreement | Qwen2.5-3B mixed-input median rank of the vanilla preferred token improves from 284 to 5 on content positions; top-1 agreement rises from 4.2% to 22.8%. Predictable-position median rank worsens from 6 to 8. | Table 2 |
| Aggregate rank survival does not imply task success | On 200 LAMBADA prompts truncated to 128 tokens, Qwen2.5-3B accuracy is 73% vanilla, 0.5% with donor attention, and 2.25% with embedding mixing. | §3; Table 4 |
| Guided decoding helps but trails the guide alone | Llama-3.2-3B with a 1B guide improves mean mixed-stream LAMBADA accuracy from 0.182 to 0.430; independent small-model accuracy is 0.540. FineWeb token Jaccard overlap falls from 0.094 to 0.067. | Table 5 |
| Distillation sacrifices single-stream quality | Qwen2.5 FineWeb PPL is 12.44 for the independent 3B model, 65.52 after tuned distillation, and 19.31 for Joint Contrastive guided inference. | Table 8 |
| Speed gains depend on the comparison | Pythia-2.8B throughput is 109.6 tokens/s for two-head separation, 79.4 guided, 113.1 independent batch-size-two, and 56.0 sequential independent inference; 100 runs, prompt and generation lengths both 128. | Table 9 |
| Three-stream approximation degrades | For Pythia-2.8B at context length 512, normalized KL rises from 0.37 with two streams to 0.47 with three. | Table 11 |

These results support partial information preservation and its trainability. They do not establish lossless independent computation or a quality-matched throughput advantage over batching. Rank metrics largely track agreement with a model’s own predictions, while low token overlap alone does not establish coherent or correct separation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation is predominantly monolingual and text-only, with relatively short contexts; longer contexts and multimodal mixing remain untested (§8).
- **Author-acknowledged tradeoffs:** Distribution matching damages single-stream prediction, and reliable disentangling remains open (§§4–5). “Lightweight” is relative to pretraining: a Pythia-2.8B distillation run required approximately 114 hours on two A100 80GB GPUs (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—architectural attribution:** Increasing hidden-state error during Pythia pretraining supports an initial bias toward additivity, but does not alone prove useful semantic processing at initialization or isolate which architectural components cause it.
- **Analyst inference—target mismatch:** Training targets an arithmetic probability mixture, whereas the decoding obstruction assumes averaged logits and hence a geometric probability mixture. The paper does not establish that this latter assumption explains the trained decoder’s remaining errors (§§4–5).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** “Ground-truth” sometimes means the model’s argmax prediction. Section 4’s claimed KL reduction to 0.27 points to a nonexistent fine-tuned row in Table 1. Section 2.3 and Fig. 4 disagree on whether early-position divergence is higher or lower.
- **Comparison and reproducibility gaps:** Table 5 changes Pythia backbone size between pretrained and Joint Contrastive rows. Appendix H supplies examples rather than a complete reproducible content-token classifier. Most quality results lack uncertainty estimates.

## Open research questions
- **Can separation outperform independent batching at matched quality?** Current guided accuracy trails the small guide alone. Benchmark accuracy, latency, throughput, and peak memory jointly across guided, two-head, and independent decoding.
- **Can training retain ordinary language-model quality?** Distillation substantially increases single-stream PPL. Sweep mixtures of ordinary language-model loss and superposition loss to measure the tradeoff.
- **Which architectural components cause useful superposition?** Checkpoint trends and attention patching do not fully isolate causality. Compare controlled architectural ablations using both additivity and content-sensitive prediction metrics.
- **Does recoverable separation scale beyond the tested regime?** Three-stream evidence concerns distributions rather than successful decoding. Test separate generation with more streams, longer contexts, and multilingual pairs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Multiple controls and quantitative tables support partial superposition, but stronger semantic and efficiency claims exceed the demonstrated results.

Material missing information includes released code/checkpoints, exact evaluation splits and most sample counts, complete token-stratification rules, uncertainty for most quality metrics, precise throughput hardware/settings, and reconciliation of inconsistent metric terminology and cross-references.
