# ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems&#x27; Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-25.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30199) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30199)


## One-sentence takeaway
ExplorationBench shows that AI systems can learn unfamiliar executable rules through experimentation and apply them to held-out tasks, but success varies sharply between exploration trajectories and does not follow reliably from correctly reporting the rules.

## Problem and motivation
Static evaluations struggle to separate recalled knowledge from knowledge acquired through exploration. Real scientific discoveries pose the opposite problem: novelty may be clear, but verification can be expensive or unavailable. The authors identify a gap in evaluating the complete sequence of choosing experiments, discovering rules, and applying them to unseen tasks after interaction ends (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Two verifiable synthetic worlds:** AlienCode provides 31 discovery targets and 70 tasks; AlienLogic provides 24 targets and 70 tasks. Hidden semantics contradict a flawed manual and familiar conventions. Programs and proofs receive executable grading; designated unprovable goals use bounded certification (§3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Exploration-to-transfer protocol:** Systems receive shared worked examples, explore for four rounds, and undergo tool-disabled testing in discarded conversation copies at each milestone. Parameters remain fixed, and exploration history stays within one context window (§3.1).
- **Separate process diagnostics and controls:** The benchmark distinguishes probe selection, reported rules, and held-out performance. Autonomous exploration is compared with replayed probes, fixed probes, deliberation without feedback, direct answering, and complete-rule access (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Empirical characterization:** Ten systems run three trajectories per sandbox, exposing differences between attainable performance, typical performance, rule reporting, and reliability (§4).

## Evidence and main results
Reported accuracies average three answers per held-out question over 70 tasks per sandbox. Best@3 selects the highest final accuracy among three complete autonomous trajectories; controls generally use one trajectory per system.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback enables substantial acquisition | Claude Opus 5’s selected AlienCode trajectory rises from 3.8% at M0 to 87.6% at M4; without-tool M4 accuracy across systems is 0.5–11.0%. | Table 1; §4.2.1, Finding 1 |
| Choosing probes helps in AlienCode | Across ten systems, median M4 accuracy is 66.0% for autonomous Best@3, 40.7% for hindsight replay, and 5.7% for fixed probes; autonomous beats replay for nine systems. AlienLogic’s median autonomous-minus-replay difference is only 0.5 percentage points. | §4.2.1, Finding 2 |
| Rankings depend on the world | Spearman correlation between the two sandbox Best@3 rankings is 0.35. | §4.2.1, Finding 3; Fig. 5 |
| Complete rules affect the worlds differently | AlienCode Best@3 exceeds pre-exploration open-book accuracy for seven systems, but Mean@3 does so for five. In AlienLogic, open-book accuracy is 92.9–97.1%, versus autonomous Best@3 of 58.1–83.8%. | Table 1; §4.2.2, Finding 4 |
| Stating rules does not ensure successful application | AlienCode tasks whose required rules are all correctly reported achieve 70.9% accuracy across 647 task–trajectory pairs at M4. | §4.2.2, Finding 6; Fig. 8 |
| Trajectory variation exceeds repeated-answer noise | Maximum within-system endpoint spread is 72.8 percentage points in AlienCode and 21.0 in AlienLogic; repeated-answer standard deviations are at most 4.7 and 2.9 points, respectively. | §4.2.3, Finding 7; Fig. 9 |
| Further exploration can reduce performance | Six of 30 AlienCode trajectories and three of 30 AlienLogic trajectories finish at least three percentage points below an earlier milestone. | §4.2.3, Finding 8 |

These results support evidence-driven learning within the two constructed worlds. They do not establish real scientific discovery or a general causal advantage from autonomous probe selection: the latter comparison uses selected autonomous maxima against mostly single control runs. The association between discovering key rules and accuracy jumps is explicitly correlational (§4.2.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Deterministic synthetic worlds omit noisy observations, costly or irreversible experiments, open-ended hypotheses, and long research horizons (§6).
- **Author-stated sampling limits:** Three trajectories and three answers per question cannot precisely estimate variability. Best@3 measures attainable endpoints rather than reliability (§§5.1, 6).
- **Analyst inference—comparison strength:** Unequal trajectory selection and observed replay variability weaken claims that choosing experiments alone explains autonomous–replay gaps (§4.2.1; Appendix A.6).
- **Analyst inference—resource comparability:** Similar tool-call budgets do not imply equal compute: reasoning settings are API-specific, and exploration tokens vary by more than an order of magnitude (Table 3; Appendix A.3).
- **Measurement boundaries:** Rule reports are imperfect behavioral proxies, AlienLogic lacks a scored rule-report diagnostic, and “unprovable” means within declared certification bounds (§3.3; Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does autonomous selection outperform replay under equal replication?** Most controls have only one trajectory. Run equally replicated autonomous and replay conditions and compare endpoint distributions.
- **What prevents correct rule knowledge from becoming correct execution?** Covered tasks still fail frequently. Supply verified rules and vary worked execution examples or composition scaffolding while holding tasks fixed.
- **Can systems preserve discoveries during longer exploration?** Some trajectories regress, while others are still improving at the budget limit. Extend the horizon and compare ordinary exploration with periodic rule-validation probes.
- **Do these findings survive noisy feedback?** Both environments are deterministic. Introduce controlled observation noise and measure acquisition, transfer, and regression.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The protocol and headline results are well documented, but causal interpretations and broader generalization are less secure.

Material information unavailable or unclear in the supplied text includes the complete rule inventories and prompts, numerical AlienLogic certification bounds, the procedure for judging rule-report correctness, full sampling and timeout settings, and downloadable code/data availability. Several figure labels are corrupted in extraction; the analysis relies on readable prose and tables.
