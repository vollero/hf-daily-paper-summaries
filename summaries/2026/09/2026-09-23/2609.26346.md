# Blaming Across the Aisle: Political Contrasting and Blame Attribution in the Danish Parliament

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26346) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26346)


## One-sentence takeaway
BlameBERT achieves 0.80 macro-F1 on a curated Danish parliamentary test set, and its corpus predictions associate blame with opposition status, rising rates after 2019, and stronger ideological-extremity effects on the right, without establishing causal mechanisms.

## Problem and motivation
The authors investigate whether parliamentary blame has increased and how it relates to government status and ideology. They distinguish blame—negative evaluation combined with causal responsibility—from general negativity, arguing that parliamentary discourse is understudied relative to social media (§§1–1.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Annotation pipeline and dataset:** Translate Danish sentences into English, apply DEBATE using five hypothesis templates, and map silver labels back to Danish. Five training datasets vary the required agreement across templates (§2.1).
- **BlameBERT:** Fine-tune mmBERT with LoRA and focal loss; select the model trained on unanimous template agreement. The authors claim the first Danish political blame detector and report releasing code, model, and dataset (§§1.3–2.2; p. 1, footnote 1).
- **Political analysis:** Classify 4,938,119 sentences from 13 parties, then fit negative-binomial mixed models to party-month blame counts with sentence-count offsets and party random intercepts. Analyze the full period and 2019–2026 separately, with threshold and topic checks (§2.3; Appendices A.9–A.10).

The evidence supports predictive performance on the constructed test set and observational associations in classifier-derived counts. It does not establish causal political contrasting, actual hostility toward domestic rivals, or broader democratic consequences. The authors interpret the larger recent interaction as intensification, but report no direct test of the difference between periods.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** Translation and DEBATE errors can propagate into BlameBERT; classification uncertainty is not propagated into the statistical models. Blame targets are unidentified (p. 9; §2.3).
- **Author-stated modeling limits:** Ideology is fixed across time and varies across only 13 parties. Government coding groups support parties with opposition, and recent disruptions may confound trends (p. 9).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation selection:** The gold set is enriched using DEBATE labels and excludes annotator disagreements. Reported agreement is 84.8%, κ=0.676; 424 of 500 sentences remain. Performance may therefore misrepresent naturally occurring or ambiguous cases. Choosing among DIAL models using this same test set also weakens its independence (§§2.1–2.2).
- **Analyst inference—baseline comparability:** Generative performance excludes failures; assigning failures to blame instead raises its macro-F1 to 0.79. Table 7 reports totals of 426 and 358, inconsistent with the stated 424 examples and 68 failures. Runtime comparisons use different GPUs (Appendix A.5).
- **Analyst inference—interpretation:** Threshold stability does not establish unbiased prevalence. Appendix A.10 also describes exponentiated logistic coefficients as relative probabilities; these are odds ratios under its stated model.
- **Analyst inference—coverage:** Excluding discontinued parties and unattached members narrows historical representativeness. Sentence-volume continuity checks do not establish stable language or classifier accuracy across the corpus merge (§2.3; Appendix A.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does opposition blame primarily target governing rivals?** Targets are unmeasured. Annotate blame source–target relations and repeat the analysis separately for domestic rivals and external actors.
- **Do trends survive representative measurement validation?** The test set is enriched and agreement-filtered. Evaluate randomly sampled sentences stratified by period and party, retaining and adjudicating ambiguous cases.
- **Has the ideology interaction significantly strengthened?** Separate-period coefficients do not directly test change. Fit a joint model with period × wing × extremity interactions and time-varying ideology.
- **How much does translation affect labeling quality?** No direct translation ablation is reported. Compare translated DEBATE labels with Danish-native labeling on a common human-annotated sample.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Methods and results are extensively documented, but measurement selection and reporting inconsistencies constrain interpretation.

Unavailable in the supplied text: representative population-level classification accuracy, uncertainty intervals for classifier comparisons, resolved baseline sample counts, a direct cross-period interaction test, and blame-target annotations. Public assets are reported as released but were not independently inspected.
