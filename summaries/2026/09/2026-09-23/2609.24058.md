# All-in-One Multilingual Scene Text Recognition with Script-aware Mixture-of-Experts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24058) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24058)


## One-sentence takeaway
ScriptMoE combines multilingual synthetic training data with script-aware sparse decoding to improve average word accuracy over a jointly trained dense baseline, while its recognition upgrade substantially improves a fixed-detector OCR pipeline (Tables 2–3).

## Problem and motivation
Multilingual scene text recognition lacks sufficient real training data outside dominant languages. The authors argue that separate language recognizers complicate deployment, large vision-language models remain costly and uneven across scripts, and a single dense decoder struggles to share capacity across different writing systems (§§1–3.1).

The targeted gap is a compact, unified recognizer with both broad script supervision and specialized decoding capacity.

## Approach and contributions
- **Synthetic supervision:** TextMuSS-10M contains one million synthetic images per script across ten scripts. It combines lexical text, shuffled characters, rare-character expansion, and newspaper sentences with varied rendering. The claimed 229-language coverage denotes character-set representability; it is not a separate accuracy evaluation of every language (§3.2; appendix, pp. 15–16).
- **Script-aware architecture:** A shared SVTRv2 encoder feeds an autoregressive decoder whose feed-forward layers use four routed experts and an always-active shared expert. Each image selects two routed experts; a token-dependent gate combines their output with the shared branch (§3.3).
- **Indirect script supervision:** A separate classifier predicts four script groups from pooled visual features. It shares the router’s input, not its weights, and supplements transcription training with an auxiliary loss (§3.4).
- **Evaluation assets and protocol:** TextMuSS-Bench extends MLT2019 with collected Russian, Thai, and Tibetan images. STR baselines are retrained using the same data and schedule, alongside zero-shot evaluations of existing OCR systems (§4.1; appendix, p. 19).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported average STR accuracy | On TextMuSS-Bench’s 10,899 images, ten-script macro-average word accuracy is **82.06%**, versus **80.75%** for SVTRv2-AR: **+1.31 percentage points** under matched training data and schedule. | Tables 1–2; §4.1 |
| Gains vary across scripts | Versus SVTRv2-AR, word accuracy improves by **2.98 points** on Arabic, **2.40** on Thai, and **1.96** on Tibetan, but falls from **92.27% to 91.67%** on Latin and **87.28% to 86.77%** on Hindi. | Table 2 |
| Recognition upgrade improves pipeline F1 | With the PP-OCRv5 detector retained, CC-OCR total F1 rises from **65.71% to 80.89%**, versus **80.73%** for Qwen3.5-9B. The recognizer is retrained for line-level inputs; CC-OCR scores Latin languages by words and others by characters. | Table 3; appendix, pp. 19–20 |
| Synthetic and real data complement each other | Over the **seven MLT2019 scripts**, average word accuracy is **77.12%** with real data, **79.61%** with TextMuSS-10M alone, and **85.52%** with both. Russian accuracy falls from **68.13% to 61.20%** when real data is added. | Table 4 |
| Components improve reported average accuracy | Ten-script average word accuracy falls from **82.06%** to **81.63%** without script-classification supervision and **81.35%** without the shared expert. Token-level routing scores **81.69%**. | Tables 5, 11 |
| Compactness does not imply faster dense-baseline inference | ScriptMoE stores **45.85M** parameters and activates **41.13M** per image. At batch size **256** on one V100, reported latency is **541.07 ms**, versus **395.92 ms** for SVTRv2-AR; throughput is **473.1 versus 646.6 images/s**. | Tables 8–9 |
| Additional benchmark gains are modest | Reported average accuracy is **86.85% versus 85.93%** on BCTR and **88.95% versus 88.67%** on Union14M-Benchmark, comparing ScriptMoE with SVTRv2-AR. | Table 10; appendix, p. 21 |

These results support improved aggregate recognition under the reported training and evaluation protocols. They do not establish superiority on every script or language, statistically reliable differences between close scores, or measured speed advantages over VLMs. STR scoring lowercases text and removes Unicode symbols and whitespace, so accuracy concerns normalized transcription rather than exact preservation of all visible content (appendix, pp. 19–20).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Synthetic-to-real domain gaps, unresolved Latin–Cyrillic homoglyph confusion, and dependence on detector quality remain (appendix, p. 22).
- **Analyst inference—coverage:** Character-set support for 229 languages exceeds the granularity of the reported script-level evaluations; language-specific reliability remains unestablished (Table 7).
- **Analyst inference—annotation:** Each newly collected script has one expert annotator, followed by morphological review from a non-specialist. Independent linguistic agreement is not reported (appendix, p. 15).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty and mechanism:** Experiments use a fixed seed without reported confidence intervals or repeated-seed variation. Four routing examples illustrate specialization but do not establish population-wide routing behavior (Figure 5; appendix, p. 19).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Retrained STR systems and zero-shot VLMs have different adaptation conditions; PP-OCRv5’s Table 2 average also excludes unsupported scripts. No capacity-matched enlarged dense baseline is reported, limiting separation of specialization benefits from added capacity.

## Open research questions
- **Does image-level routing handle mixed-script lines reliably?** The method assumes few scripts per crop, without a dedicated mixed-script evaluation. Next experiment: compare image- and token-level routing on annotated mixtures with increasing script diversity.
- **Can real Cyrillic supervision resolve homoglyph errors?** Russian performance worsens when the current real-data mixture is added. Next experiment: vary real Cyrillic data and Latin–Cyrillic sampling balance while tracking substitution errors.
- **How much improvement comes from routing versus additional capacity?** Existing ablations change expert structure and parameter allocation together. Next experiment: compare against dense decoders matched for stored parameters and inference cost.
- **Can new scripts be added without degrading existing ones?** Continual script expansion is proposed as future work (§5). Next experiment: add scripts incrementally and measure new-script accuracy, forgetting, and training cost.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons and architecture; **medium** in broad generalization and causal explanations.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, per-language validation across the claimed coverage, comprehensive routing statistics, capacity-matched dense comparisons, independently checked transcription agreement, measured VLM/edge-device efficiency, and verified availability of downloadable datasets and checkpoints.
