# RULER: Instance-aware Rubric Rewards for SVG Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25270) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25270)


## One-sentence takeaway
RULER improves text-to-SVG generation by training on prompt-specific visual rubrics, achieving stronger benchmark rubric scores and human preferences than evaluated baselines, with demonstrated sensitivity to rubric design.

## Problem and motivation
Open-ended SVG generation admits many valid renderings, making reference-based evaluation restrictive. The authors argue that conventional CLIP, Aesthetic, and HPS rewards inadequately capture vector-art quality and can encourage reward hacking. Their proposed gap is a reference-free reward that evaluates both prompt-specific requirements and visual execution (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Evaluation evidence:** Compare a universal rubric evaluator with CLIP and Aesthetic against human judgments, finding substantially stronger correlation and ranking agreement (§3.2).
- **Instance-aware reward construction:** A frontier model generates an ideal SVG and then six prompt-specific criteria covering semantics, visual quality, and style. The ideal SVG supports rubric construction and filtering but is not a reference during rollout scoring. Thus, training requires no externally paired SVG ground truth or human preference labels, but does use synthetic exemplars (Appendices B, F.1).
- **Rendering-based policy optimization:** Frozen Qwen3-VL-8B scores rendered rollouts item by item; a weighted average supplies the GRPO reward for Qwen3-8B. Rubrics are generated offline and cached (§3.4; Appendices C, G).
- **Controlled reward comparisons:** Fixed-backbone experiments compare scalar rewards, universal rubrics, instance-aware rubrics, and rubric-axis ablations (§§4.3–4.5).

The contribution is the SVG-specific reward construction and supporting evidence; GRPO and rubric-based RL are acknowledged existing methods (§2).

These results support improved quality within the evaluated MMSVG distribution. Evaluation uses GPT-5-mini with a universal rubric, separate from the training judge and instance-aware rubrics. Human comparisons add independent evidence, but the narrow lead over VectorFusion and small numerical advantage over DeepSeek-V3 do not establish statistically reliable superiority. DeepSeek-V3 is absent from the final human preference study.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** External generators and judges can transmit biases or miss subtle qualities; rendering and judging increase training cost; fixed quality axes may exclude valid artistic preferences (Limitations).
- **Author-stated scope:** Training and testing remain within MMSVG’s distribution; this is explicitly not an out-of-distribution evaluation (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Separate evaluation and training models reduce direct evaluator reuse, but shared quality dimensions leave potential preference alignment between reward and evaluation (Appendices D.1, H).
- **Analyst inference:** Automated scores condition on successful rendering, so they are not unconditional generation-quality measures. Render rates are supplied separately, and human comparisons count rendering failures as losses (Appendices C–E).
- **Analyst inference:** Five-run results measure decoding variability, not training-seed robustness. Human studies report neither inter-rater reliability nor uncertainty intervals; final-output pairs are judged once (Appendices D, E.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Rubric-S changes several features simultaneously, so its failure does not isolate strictness, missing style criteria, or penalty design. The synthetic-exemplar filtering step also lacks a reported ablation (§4.4; Appendices B, F.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does RULER generalize beyond MMSVG?** Current evaluation remains within the training distribution. Test held-out visual domains and unfamiliar artistic styles with blinded human evaluation.
- **How much do synthetic exemplars and filtering contribute?** They are part of construction but not separately ablated. Compare direct text-to-rubric generation, exemplar-assisted generation, and filtering variants under matched training budgets.
- **Which rubric choices prevent text shortcuts?** Rubric-S changes multiple components and still produces hacking. Vary style coverage, calibration, and text penalties independently, measuring both shortcut frequency and human preference.
- **Can judging become cheaper without losing quality?** The authors identify online judging as a scalability constraint. Compare smaller judges or selective rollout judging while reporting total training cost and final quality.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and reported in-distribution gains, but broader superiority remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes training duration and total online judging cost, independent training-seed results, human-rating reliability and significance estimates, a construction/filtering ablation, and confirmed availability of the promised code and data.
