# RoboFollow: Unveiling the Instruction Following Mirage in Embodied Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25636) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25636)


## One-sentence takeaway
RoboFollow exposes substantial instruction-grounding failures under layout changes and semantic recombinations in evaluated robot policies, even when their in-distribution performance is strong, within the authors’ fine-tuning setup (§§4–5).

## Problem and motivation
High manipulation success can conceal weak language use when the scene already suggests the intended task. Conventional final-state evaluation also mixes semantic errors with motor failures. The authors identify a need for training scenes that support multiple feasible behaviors and evaluations that separately measure task selection and execution (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Language-necessary benchmark:** RoboFollow groups 75 training task labels into six initial-scene configurations across spatial relations, object properties, motion constraints, and elementary logic. It provides 3,750 demonstrations on RoboTwin2.0 and formalizes scene ambiguity as conditional task-label entropy, \(H(T\mid S)\) (§3; Appendix A).
- **Controlled diagnostic protocol:** L0 tests the training distribution; L1 changes layouts or observable states; L2 changes semantic combinations or formulations; L3 combines these changes. These are diagnostic dimensions, not a strict difficulty ordering. Test actions remain within the demonstrated repertoire (§3.1; Appendix A).
- **Stage-wise evaluation and empirical diagnosis:** Intent Score (IS) measures semantic target selection; Execution Score (ES) measures physical completion; Completion Rate (CR) provides an action-dependent binary outcome. Nine VLA/WAM policies and several mitigation strategies are evaluated (§§3.2–5; Appendix B).

The reported IS declines support semantic selection errors as a contributor beyond execution failure. They do not establish that all embodied agents ignore language, isolate architecture from training effects, or prove that scene entropy causes the observed gaps. The authors’ explanations of LangForce and CFG failures are mechanistic interpretations rather than demonstrated causal results.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Simplified geometry, short horizons, and controlled interactions exclude comprehensive coverage of contact-rich manipulation, open-vocabulary tasks, long-horizon planning, and deployment (§7).
- **Author-stated missing control:** Structural training-layout diversity remains untested; demonstration count, paraphrases, and training duration do not substitute for this intervention (§5.3).
- **Author-stated external-validity limits:** Cross-simulator transfer is unestablished, and the real-robot pilot does not control task identity or difficulty (§7; Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The small, default-scene QA probe gives limited evidence about general scene comprehension or its causal relationship to action failures (§5.1; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** IS is inferred from observable behavior, so its separation from motor competence is operational rather than a direct measurement of internal understanding (Appendix B).

## Open research questions

- **Does structural layout diversity close the gap?** This factor is explicitly untested. Train with additional layouts while retaining disjoint evaluation layouts and matching demonstration counts (§5.3).
- **Can grounded intermediate supervision improve comprehension-to-action transfer?** Strong QA performance and QA co-training did not resolve manipulation generalization. Compare explicit object, relation, and waypoint supervision against QA-only co-training under matched conditions (§§5.1–5.2).
- **Does the diagnostic gap persist in matched real-world tasks?** The pilot uses different instruction sets and task compositions. Evaluate paired tasks with controlled semantic and layout changes across independently trained policies (Appendix C.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark design and selected numerical findings are explicit, but the supplied text lacks the bodies of Tables 1–3 and 9. Consequently, full model rankings, detailed mitigation scores, control-panel results, and per-model training hyperparameters are unavailable. Simulation evaluation counts, independent training-run variability, exact stage-scoring thresholds, and QA grading details are also not established in the supplied text.
