# Tri-PvP: Exposing Modality Bias in Omni-Modal Large Language Models through Perceptual-Propositional Evidence Conflicts

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06011) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06011)


## One-sentence takeaway
Tri-PvP finds widespread visual dominance under controlled image–audio–text conflicts, but evidence-form effects vary by model, and an audio-amplifying decoding intervention only partially improves conflict handling.

## Problem and motivation
When modalities disagree, omni-modal models may systematically favor one source. The authors argue that existing evaluations confound **modality** with **evidence form**: photographs and natural recordings convey perceptual evidence, while written or spoken declarations convey propositional evidence. Controlling this distinction helps identify what models favor and assess their reliability under inconsistent inputs (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled benchmark:** Tri-PvP contains 8,000 samples across animal, emotion, environment, and music domains. Each domain supplies 500 conflicting label triples instantiated under four matched image/audio evidence-form combinations; text remains propositional. Perceptual inputs use existing datasets, while propositions become word-card images, synthesized speech, or text (§4.1).
- **Behavioral and representation analysis:** Five models receive open-ended, modality-neutral questions. A judge assigns eight response categories. Input-order experiments and layer-wise logistic probes examine two models more closely (§§4.2–6.2).
- **Diagnostic intervention:** On Gemma4, contrastive decoding combines audio-only and full-input logits as \(z_{\mathrm{CD}}=1.5z_{\mathrm{audio}}-0.5z_{\mathrm{full}}\), with a full-input plausibility filter. This adapts an existing technique rather than introducing a new decoding family (§6.3).

## Evidence and main results

Here, **Perc** denotes perceptual evidence and **Prop** propositional evidence; I/A identify image/audio.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Visual dominance is widespread | With image→audio→text ordering, `BIAS_IMAGE` is the largest category in 18/20 model–condition combinations; both exceptions involve Qwen2.5 with propositional images. | §5.2; Fig. 3; Tables 5–9 |
| Evidence form matters, with exceptions | Qwen2.5 image bias falls from 49.7% to 12.7% when images change Perc→Prop with Prop audio fixed. Gemma4 audio bias rises from 0.9% to 25.4% when audio changes Perc→Prop with Perc images fixed. Conversely, Gemini3 audio bias falls from 4.2% to 2.0% in that latter comparison. | Tables 5, 6, 9 |
| Fully propositional inputs produce the most `NO_BIAS` responses | All five models peak in PropI–PropA, ranging from 1.5% for Qwen3 to 60.1% for Qwen2.5 under the default ordering. | §5.2; Tables 5–9 |
| Order substantially changes individual outcomes | Gemma4’s PropI–PropA image bias is 74.9% for I→A→T, but 17.1% for T→I→A; text bias changes from 5.1% to 62.0%. | Table 6 |
| Output bias is predictable before generation | Most probes exceed 50% chance balanced accuracy within the first two layers; Qwen2.5 image probes peak around 80% under perceptual-image conditions. Gemma4 perceptual-audio probes are weak. | §6.2; Fig. 4 |
| Contrastive decoding partially improves Gemma4 conflict responses | In PropI–PropA, image bias drops 74.9%→38.8% and `NO_BIAS` rises 7.2%→38.6%. Text bias increases in every condition; in PercI–PropA it rises 9.0%→22.5%. | Table 10 |
| General-task performance changes modestly overall | On 1,142 OmniBench items, Gemma4 accuracy changes from 439/1,142 (38.4%) to 427/1,142 (37.4%); CD corrects 141 errors and introduces 153. | Appendix I.3; Table 11 |
| Judge validation supports, but does not independently establish, reliability | Authors report 97.1% agreement on 800 inspected responses. | §4.2; Appendix F |

These results establish behavioral preferences within the constructed conflicts and demonstrate that representations predict subsequent response categories. They do **not** establish causal mechanisms, reliable real-world source selection, or universal evidence-form preferences. The proposed encoder-pretraining explanation is explicitly a hypothesis (§5.2). Likewise, the small aggregate OmniBench decline does not establish capability equivalence across tasks.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Only everyday domains and neutral questions are tested. Scientific inputs, source-reliability cues, and instructions requiring a single answer or source enumeration remain unexplored (Limitations).
- **Author-stated intervention limits:** CD uses one model and fixed coefficients, adds a forward pass, and leaves residual text bias (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—recognition remains a potential confound:** Human verification of unambiguous inputs does not establish equal model recognition accuracy across modalities. Matched unimodal recognition controls are not reported (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—`NO_BIAS` is heterogeneous:** The actual judge prompt accepts refusal alone, broader than the main-text definition. Appendix D confirms that this category mixes refusal, enumeration, and explicit conflict recognition; increases therefore need not mean better evidence reconciliation.
- **Analyst inference—headline claims need qualification:** Gemma4 favors propositional images under default ordering, Gemini3’s audio-bias rates decline with propositional audio, and individual permutations can reverse dominance. The tables support tendencies rather than universal invariance (Tables 6, 9).
- **Analyst inference—validation ambiguity:** Appendix F calls 800 responses 10% of 8,000 judged responses, despite evaluation across five models. The audit’s full population is unclear; independent inter-annotator agreement is not supplied.

## Open research questions
- **Does evidence form affect preference beyond recognition difficulty?** Human filtering does not settle this. Measure matched unimodal accuracy, then repeat conflict comparisons on inputs each model recognizes correctly.
- **What causally produces early bias signals?** Probes establish predictability, not causation. Intervene on identified representations and measure both conflict handling and unimodal competence.
- **Can mitigation improve conflict recognition without encouraging refusal or shifting bias?** Current CD results mix these outcomes. Sweep coefficients across models and evaluate explicit conflict recognition, refusal, and task-specific accuracy separately.
- **Can models weight genuinely unequal sources appropriately?** Tri-PvP assumes no source deserves priority. Introduce controlled corruption and reliability cues, then evaluate whether models favor the demonstrably reliable source.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods, prompts, appendices, and numerical tables, but several broad claims exceed their consistent support.

Material gaps include model-specific unimodal controls, causal interventions, multi-model CD sensitivity tests, probe uncertainty estimates, independent annotation agreement, clarification of the judge-audit denominator, and explicit asset-disjoint probe splitting. The linked release’s contents were not inspected.
