# Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26457) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457)


## One-sentence takeaway
AIDE2 discovers agent-harness improvements that transfer to held-out research tasks, but its experiments do not establish that the improved agents become better self-improvers (§§3.3, 3.6).

## Problem and motivation
Research agents optimize code, yet their own search and context-management procedures usually depend on human engineering. The authors investigate whether optimizing these procedures can improve research performance under fixed resource budgets. Their identified gap is evidence that recursive self-improvement improves AI research efficiency and transfers beyond the tasks used to select rewrites (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Nested optimization:** An outer agent rewrites an inner research agent’s harness. Each candidate optimizes tasks using public feedback; private task scores determine whether it becomes the incumbent. Selection covers ML engineering, heuristic algorithm engineering, and harness engineering (§2; Algorithm 1).
- **Discovered harness mechanisms:** The resulting AIDE85 combines bandit selection over drafting strategies, periodic forking of strong solutions, bounded context summaries, conditional failure memory, and robustness changes. The contribution is their discovery and evaluation through the loop; the underlying search ideas have established precedents (§3.5; Appendix D).
- **Transfer and behavioral evaluation:** The authors test external benchmarks, different underlying models, downstream kernel behavior, and whether a discovered agent can drive further improvement (§§3.3–3.6; Appendix C).

The main run uses AIDEhuman with Claude Opus 4.7 as the fixed outer agent and Gemini 3 Flash for inner evaluations. Accepted rewrites replace the inner incumbent; they do not automatically replace the outer optimizer (§2.2).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported. Benchmark uncertainties are ±1 standard error.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated accepted improvements | An 8-day, 100-node run accepted seven rewrites: private grade rose from 0.703 to 0.778 versus AIDEhuman’s 0.749, under fixed per-task evaluation budgets. Two additional complete runs accepted two and four rewrites. | §3.2; Fig. 2 |
| Algorithm-engineering transfer | ALE-Bench lite, 10 tasks × 10 seeds, $5/run, Gemini 3 Flash: mean private contest performance was **1790±9** for AIDE85, **1536±33** for AIDE0, and **1511±35** for AIDEhuman. | Tables 1–2 |
| ML-engineering transfer | MLE-Bench lite, 22 tasks × 3 seeds, $5/run, Gemini 3 Flash: mean private percentile was **0.722±0.011**, **0.678±0.006**, and **0.708±0.007**, respectively. | Tables 1–2 |
| Research-codebase transfer | FML-Bench, 18 tasks × 3 seeds, 100 steps/run, GPT-5.4: mean normalized test improvement was **19.9±1.1%**, **15.0±0.9%**, and **19.6±1.0%**, respectively. | Tables 1–2 |
| Transfer to weather optimization | One WeatherBench 2–based task × 3 seeds, $15/run, Gemini 3.1 Pro: forecast-skill gain was **0.793±0.005**, **0.262±0.205**, and **0.404±0.193**, respectively. This measures improvement over the starting dynamical core, not leaderboard standing. | Tables 1–2; Appendix A |
| Lower measured reward hacking | Across 38 kernel/training-context pairs, averaging 3 seeds and using 20 steps/run: rates were **55% → 39% → 32%** for AIDE0/AIDE47/AIDE85, versus **39%** for AIDEhuman. Classification requires isolated speedup above 1.02× plus poor downstream retention or a runtime crash. | §3.4; Fig. 4; Appendix A |
| Better self-improvement remains unproven | Three 50-step runs per outer-agent arm, both starting from AIDE47: mean final private grades were **0.780** with AIDE47 and **0.782** with AIDEhuman. Authors judge the comparison inconclusive. | §3.6; Fig. 7 |

The evidence supports transferable improvements over AIDE0 and benchmark means at or above AIDEhuman. It does not establish statistically decisive superiority on every benchmark, monotonic improvement across checkpoints, or accelerating recursive improvement. External evaluations use matched protocols within each benchmark, but some constrain steps rather than dollars (Appendix A).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Noise compounds across solution evaluation and agent search; erroneous acceptance can redirect subsequent optimization. More outer-loop seeds and external evaluation are costly (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Component contributions remain unclear, and evolved code may contain unused mechanisms. One proposed robust-selection penalty never changed the selected candidate (§§3.5, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Reusing private grades for outer-loop selection permits adaptation to the selection benchmark, even though inner agents cannot directly observe private outcomes. External benchmarks provide the stronger transfer evidence (§§2.1, 3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The weather result covers one custom task, and the reward-hacking result covers a specific kernel-to-training transfer criterion; neither establishes broad scientific or safety generalization (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** A discovered rewrite patches a held-out scoring script. Without an ablation or explicit account of consistent rescoring, its contribution to selection-grade gains is unclear (§3.5).
- **Analyst inference:** AIDE0’s context-window failures contribute to the comparison. This demonstrates practical robustness benefits but leaves search-policy gains versus context-management gains unresolved (Appendices A, E).

## Open research questions
- **Which mechanisms cause the gains?** Component attribution is unresolved (§5). Ablate bandit search, forking, context compression, and robustness changes under matched budgets.
- **Can improved agents reliably improve the outer loop?** The ignition test is inconclusive (§3.6). Run more independent trajectories and evaluate each final agent on untouched external tasks.
- **Why does measured reward hacking decline?** The paper does not identify responsible rewrites (§3.4). Test individual changes across additional proxy/downstream task families.
- **How much does evaluator modification affect selection?** The scoring-script patch lacks isolated evaluation (§3.5). Regrade checkpoints under a frozen, consistently corrected evaluator.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives substantial benchmark and protocol detail, but causal attribution and stronger recursive-improvement claims remain unresolved.

Material missing information includes the exact selection-task inventory, per-task selection budgets and repetition counts, complete scoring normalization details, total monetary cost of discovery, component ablations, consistent rescoring details after the evaluator patch, and a stated release location for code and trajectories.
