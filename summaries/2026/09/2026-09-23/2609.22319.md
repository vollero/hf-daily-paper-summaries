# Embedding Physics Priors in Robot Learning: A Survey

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22319) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22319)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey organizes physics priors in robot learning by their placement in data, architectures, or training losses, highlighting potential efficiency benefits alongside limited systematic comparisons and unresolved scaling challenges.

## Problem and motivation
Robot learning faces expensive data collection, complex interactions, and reliability requirements that make purely data-driven generalization difficult. The authors identify fragmented terminology and surveys restricted to particular methods or domains as obstacles to understanding when physics priors help (§§1–1.2).

## Approach and contributions
This is a literature synthesis, not a new learning algorithm.

- **Unified classification:** Adapts and extends an existing taxonomy into physics-guided inputs/data/representations, physics-encoded architectures, and physics-informed losses. Labels can overlap; a decision flow makes classification explicit (§2, Figs. 3–4).
- **Robotics-focused synthesis:** Maps methods to dynamics learning, planning/prediction, control, and estimation, including architectural trade-offs and generative-model approaches (§1.3, Tables 2–7).
- **Reusable literature organization:** Reports a public repository containing reviewed papers, classifications, search methods, tables, and software resources (abstract, §1.1).

The authors claim novelty in the breadth of the combined robotics coverage, rather than in originating the three embedding routes (§1.2, Table 1).

The supplied evidence supports a useful taxonomy and qualitative synthesis of reported findings. It does not establish a pooled effect size, a controlled ranking of embedding routes, or universal improvements in safety or generalization. Results attributed to individual methods are the survey’s summaries of prior work.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Strong priors can restrict expressiveness when real behavior departs from assumed physics (§1).
- **Author-stated:** High-dimensional robots and contact-rich dynamics remain common validation gaps; many architectures require substantial manual design (§§3.6–3.9).
- **Author-stated:** Physics-informed losses depend on differentiable equations, introduce training cost, and may encode inaccurate assumptions (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Corpus frequencies characterize the selected literature, not method effectiveness or exhaustive field coverage. The supplied text describes Google Scholar searches and selected preprints but does not expose the complete screening protocol (§1.1).
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated during §4.3. Detailed physics-guided coverage, software discussion, and later research-direction sections are unavailable; this analysis does not cover the complete survey.

## Open research questions
- **When do combined embedding routes outperform individual routes?** Fig. 1 identifies limited systematic comparisons. A plausible next experiment would compare individual routes and their combinations under matched data and compute budgets.
- **Do benefits persist on complex, contact-rich robots?** §§3.6–3.9 identify scaling and contact as unresolved. A next experiment could compare structured and black-box models across increasing robot complexity, measuring prediction, tracking, and constraint violations.
- **How should learning respond to incorrect physics priors?** §4.1 identifies equation fidelity and loss balancing as risks. A next experiment could progressively perturb assumed dynamics and compare fixed versus adaptive physics-loss weighting.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The taxonomy and visible trade-offs are explicit, but truncation limits coverage.
