# The Tasteful Agent: Measuring and Improving Taste in Long-Horizon Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25804) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25804)


## One-sentence takeaway
Taste-Bench measures hindsight-supported choices within agent trajectories, and the authors report that judgment distillation improves held-out decisions and software-task success when advice uses forks mined from earlier runs on those same tasks (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Long-running agents can choose plausible directions whose costs become apparent only later. The authors argue that end-to-end success benchmarks obscure this decision quality, while expert annotation is expensive. They propose measuring “taste”: choosing the direction with the better eventual outcome before seeing the continuation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Outcome-labeled decision forks:** Extract alternatives from parallel attempts with different outcomes or from an abandoned approach and subsequent recovery within one trajectory. Models see the task, prefix, and alternatives; continuations remain hidden (§§2–3).
- **Taste-Bench:** Release 502 questions spanning engineering and ML research, with automated evidence checks and filters for obvious or unsupported choices. Scoring requires correctness under both candidate orders (§3; Appendix A).
- **Judgment distillation:** Train Qwen3.6-27B LoRA adapters using reasoning generated by the same base model given the supported answer, followed by answer calibration. Evaluate task-disjoint judgment transfer and advice to a fixed executor (§5; Appendix G). The recipe adapts existing context-distillation methods; its application to mined forks is the contribution (§6).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best reported benchmark score remains limited | Across 14 models and 502 questions, GPT-5.6 Sol scores 59.7%; GPT-5.5 scores 59.5%. These are equally weighted engineering/research averages requiring both orders correct. | §4.2; Figure 3 |
| Later evidence predicts lower accuracy | Mean both-orders accuracy across 14 models falls from 62.3% on 158 “in-prefix” questions to 21.0% on 69 “more-work” questions. | Figure 4; Tables 5–6 |
| No clear overall benefit from increased reasoning effort | Lowest-to-highest effort changes pooled accuracy by −0.2 percentage points for Sol and +2.2 for Luna; respective 95% intervals are [−3.2, +2.8] and [−1.6, +6.0]. Luna’s research accuracy nevertheless rises from 43.8% to 54.5%. | §4.4; Appendix D.4 |
| Distillation transfers across source tasks | On 390 pooled out-of-fold engineering questions, Qwen3.6-27B improves from 30.0% to 47.9% both-orders accuracy; two-order mean accuracy rises from 42.7% to 62.4%. | §5.2; Appendix G.3 |
| Advice improves reported executor success | On 41 held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks, reported success is 14.6% without advice, 33.7% with student advice, and 39.0% with correct advice. Forks come from prior runs on those tasks. | §5.3; Appendix H |
| Human agreement is high after outcome disclosure | Of 172 judgments expressing a clear preference, 170 agree with mined labels (98.8%). The 100 reviewed questions include 70 released and 30 rejected items. | Appendix F |

These results support task-disjoint transfer in selecting supplied alternatives and benefits from task-specific advice. They do not establish autonomous fork discovery or comparable gains on tasks without prior trajectories. The stated 25% random baseline assumes independent guesses across the two presentations (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Acknowledged attribution uncertainty:** Execution and environment also affect outcomes (§2). **Analyst inference:** Filtering observational trajectories does not fully isolate decision effects, especially when a detour’s recovery follows additional information.
- **Selected evaluation population:** Only 10.8% of mined candidates survive filtering (§3.3). Human agreement excludes non-preference judgments and follows outcome disclosure; it is not unconditional evidence of label validity (Appendix F).
- **Horizon measurement:** One label-informed model assigns horizons; only 13 questions require “substantial future work,” merged with local-check questions (Appendix D.3). **Analyst inference:** The horizon trend does not isolate horizon from construction or domain differences.
- **Transfer scope and controls:** Training and evaluation share repositories. Reported experiments omit label-only, calibration-only, base-model-advice, and generic-advice-header controls. The advice includes verification instructions, so its benefit cannot be attributed solely to distilled judgment (Appendices G–H).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** A 33.7% binary success rate cannot arise from one run on each of 41 tasks; repetitions or aggregation explaining it are unspecified (§5.3; Appendix H).
- **Author-stated comparison caveat:** SWE-bench Verified reasoning settings are not fully matched, and cross-benchmark correlation intervals are wide (Appendix D.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does judgment generalize to new repositories or research domains?** Existing distillation tests engineering tasks within shared repositories. Next experiment: repository-disjoint and engineering-to-research transfer.
2. **Which training and advice components cause the gains?** Component controls are absent. Next experiment: compare label-only training, distillation without calibration, base-model advice, and verification-only advice.
3. **Can improved judgment guide fresh runs without hindsight-mined forks?** Evaluation supplies alternatives from earlier attempts. Next experiment: generate alternatives online on tasks with no prior trajectories and measure task success.
4. **Do mined labels identify reliably better decisions?** Recorded continuations do not establish expected outcomes under repeated execution. Next experiment: replay both alternatives from matched states across multiple seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Construction and judgment results are extensively documented, but causal attribution and end-to-end interpretation remain limited.

Material missing information includes executor repetition/aggregation details explaining 33.7%, uncertainty for student-advice success, component ablations, and evaluation without prior runs on the target tasks.
