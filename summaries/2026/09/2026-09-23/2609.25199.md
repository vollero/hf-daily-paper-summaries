# Lean Pool: An AI-Maintained Archive of Formalized Mathematics

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-23.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25199) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25199)


## One-sentence takeaway
Lean Pool demonstrates an agent-assisted workflow for maintaining and reusing independently authored Lean formalizations, while its operational evidence leaves mathematical review accuracy and preservation of theorem statements unresolved.

## Problem and motivation
Formal proofs need continued maintenance as Lean and Mathlib change; preserving source alone does not keep results usable. Researchers also need to discover relevant theorems and inspect their assumptions. The author argues that Mathlib’s coverage and human review throughput leave a gap for rapidly archiving completed research formalizations (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Maintained archive:** Pool independently developed projects into a shared Lean/Mathlib environment while preserving attribution, provenance, and project organization. Admission requires completed proofs, restricted axioms, project cards, and Apache-2.0 or MIT licensing (§§2–3).
- **Agent-assisted operations:** Discover projects, repair dependency upgrades, and optimize proofs through workflows checked by compilation, linters, environment audits, and maintainer decisions. LLM reviews assess advertised mathematical claims beyond compilation (§§3, 5–7; Appendices B–D).
- **Discovery and reuse interface:** Connect informal project descriptions to formal statements, source, documentation, and declaration dependency graphs through Exposition, building on existing exposition tools (§§8–9).
- **Operational study:** Analyze historical upgrades, accepted optimizations, retained reviews, and reuse records. The contribution is an archive and its operating workflow; the supplied text does not establish a new proof-generation algorithm (§3).

These records support operational feasibility and concrete reuse examples. They do not establish fully autonomous maintenance, superiority over human maintenance, or reliable detection of mathematical misrepresentation. Most quantitative tables appear only as captions in the supplied text, preventing assessment of effect sizes.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Successful migration establishes compatibility of accepted statements, not equivalence of every declaration before and after repair; auxiliary assumptions changed (Appendix D).
- **Author-stated:** Review agreement measures consistency, not correctness. Retained reports omit unrecoverable executions, and the paused service does not establish coverage of recent imports (Appendix F).
- **Author-stated:** Whole-library optimization measurements use single paired runs. Shared-host background activity and heterogeneous project-level protocols limit comparison; memory definitions also differ across experiments (Appendices A.2, E).
- **Author-stated:** Historical maintenance charges and human labor were not logged. Review price estimates are incomplete and, for quota-based execution, are not cash expenditure (Appendices D, F).
- **Analyst inference:** Studying accepted changes cannot establish the success rate of all attempted optimizations or isolate agents’ contribution from human integration; the supplied evidence lacks that denominator and a controlled comparison.
- **Coverage limitation:** The source is truncated, and most table bodies and figure values are unavailable; this analysis does not cover the complete paper.

## Open research questions
- **Do repairs preserve mathematical meaning?** Appendix D explicitly leaves declaration equivalence unresolved. A next experiment could compare pre/post-upgrade theorem types and independently audit strengthened assumptions.
- **How accurate is LLM mathematical review?** Verdicts and merge outcomes lack independent correctness labels (§7). Evaluate retained reviews against blinded expert judgments, measuring missed issues and false alarms.
- **How much maintenance effort do agents save?** Human labor and historical costs were not logged (Appendix D). Compare matched upgrades under human-only and agent-assisted workflows with complete time, cost, and integration records.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The workflow and its stated caveats are well supported, but quantitative effectiveness is difficult to assess.

Missing information includes most numerical table contents, figure counts, observation dates and configurations referenced by those tables, detailed model assignments, complete operating costs, independent review-accuracy labels, and the collector-omitted source material.
