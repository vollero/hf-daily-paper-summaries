# From Reweighting to Rewriting: Unlocking the Intervention Effects of Influential Samples in Training Data Attribution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02771) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02771)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that rewriting responses of influence-selected SFT examples steers abstention more consistently than reweighting those examples, while safety-refusal gains introduce an over-refusal trade-off (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation

Training data attribution should identify examples useful for changing model behavior, but weak results from deleting or upweighting influential examples confound selection quality with intervention design. The authors ask whether these examples become useful when their supervision changes, rather than merely its weight (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Supervision-level intervention:** Rank examples using influence functions with EK-FAC curvature approximation and a held-out target-response log-likelihood. Replace responses at either ranking extreme with aligned or opposed templates, keeping instructions fixed (§3; Appendix A).
- **Controlled comparison:** Compare rewriting, deletion, and upweighting on identical selections, plus random-selection controls, throughout retraining. The default setting modifies 1,600 of 64,000 Tülu 3 examples, with shared training configurations and fixed data order (§4.1; Appendix H).
- **Selection and mechanism analyses:** Compare alternative selectors, examine local gradient changes, evaluate behavioral specificity, and transfer rankings between models (§5; Appendices B–F).

The contribution is the LLM SFT intervention framework and its empirical comparisons; influence functions, EK-FAC, and influence-guided example editing have prior precedents acknowledged in §2. Rewriting is explicitly **not** a finite realization of the original influence-function prediction (§3.2.2).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Rewriting produces stronger, persistent directional changes | Authors report increased/decreased abstention recall under aligned/opposed rewriting across OLMo2-1B, Qwen3.5-2B, Gemma3-4B, and OLMo2-7B, exceeding matched random rewriting; deletion/upweighting are inconsistent. | §4.2; Figs. 2–3 |
| Influence selection outperforms several alternatives | On OLMo2-1B, IF and absolute IF give the strongest sustained aligned effects; signed IF gives the largest sustained opposed decrease. Probing can lead early under opposed rewriting. | Appendix C.2; Fig. 8 |
| Abstention improves with some over-refusal | Authors report higher F1 than random rewriting for both ranking extremes across all evaluated models, reduced precision, and accuracy near baseline. | Appendix D.1; Table 3 |
| Effects are behavior-specific | Gains concentrate on answer-unknown and false-premise scenarios; influence selection does not consistently beat random rewriting on underspecified context. | §5.3; Appendix D.2; Figs. 7, 9–10 |
| Ranking overlap and transfer are partial | Independently selected sets of 1,600 examples overlap by 41.5% at the top and 49.5% at the bottom between OLMo2-1B and OLMo2-7B. Transferred selections generally outperform random rewriting, with variable effects relative to native rankings. | Appendix F; Figs. 12–13 |
| Safety gains incur a utility cost | On OLMo2-7B during early continued SFT, aligned rewriting improves multiple safety benchmarks but worsens XSTest performance, indicating benign-prompt over-refusal. | §6; Appendix G.2; Table 2 |

These are predominantly **author-reported qualitative results**: the supplied text includes figure captions and discussion but lacks plotted values and table bodies. It supports the distinction between selection value and intervention choice, but does not establish exact behavioral effect sizes, statistical significance, or comprehensive capability preservation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** The method assumes clear aligned/opposed rewriting targets; extension beyond abstention and refusal remains unresolved (§7).
- **Author-stated trade-off:** Abstention precision declines, and safety rewriting causes more substantial over-refusal. The authors suggest the aggregate safety target contributes, without establishing that explanation causally (§6; Appendix D.1).
- **Analyst inference—limited coverage:** Broad selector comparisons and general-capability checks use OLMo2-1B; safety experiments use OLMo2-7B and early training. Their conclusions therefore have narrower support than the main abstention comparison (Appendices C.2, D.3, G.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement limits:** Abstention uses fixed subsets and an LLM judge that ignores answer accuracy. Local influence diagnostics support gradient alignment, not a complete causal explanation of retraining dynamics (Appendices B, G.1).
- **Analyst inference—supervision scope:** Opposed responses are affirmative compliance templates, not demonstrated substantive answers; reduced abstention does not establish improved answering ability (Appendix I).

## Open research questions

1. **Can finer safety targets reduce over-refusal?** The aggregate target is only a proposed explanation for XSTest degradation (§6). Compare aggregate and category-specific targets at matched rewriting budgets.
2. **When do rankings transfer reliably?** Transfer varies by model, ranking extreme, and training stage (Appendix F). Test reciprocal transfers across model families, measuring behavioral effects and attribution cost.
3. **Does rewriting work beyond refusal-like behaviors?** Clear rewriting targets constrain the current framework (§7). Repeat the selector–intervention comparison on a behavior requiring substantive, instruction-specific responses.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and qualitative findings are clearly described, but numerical verification is limited.

Material omissions include table bodies, plotted effect sizes and uncertainty values, training hyperparameters from Table 6, exact safety scores, judge-validation evidence, and complete template/query contents. Appendix I claims a code/template release, but the supplied text provides no project release link.
