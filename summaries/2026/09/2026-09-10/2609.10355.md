# Why Is Video Still So Expensive? A Survey of Inference-Efficiency Mechanisms in Video and Audiovisual LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10355) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10355)


## One-sentence takeaway
This survey finds that video-aware compression can preserve near-baseline QA accuracy under specific shared-host protocols, but token savings alone do not establish end-to-end efficiency because encoding, selection, audio processing, and cache costs also matter (§§IV–VI).

## Problem and motivation
VideoLLM inference becomes expensive as frame count, spatial resolution, and multimodal context grow, restricting real-time and resource-constrained deployment. The authors identify fragmented evaluation as a central obstacle: methods optimize different pipeline stages and report incompatible input budgets, cost boundaries, and hardware measurements (§I).

## Approach and contributions
This is a literature synthesis, with no new inference algorithm or survey-run benchmark established in the supplied text.

- **Video-specific taxonomy:** Organizes mechanisms by input selection, encoder computation, connector compression, and LLM execution/state, relating temporal coverage to downstream costs. The authors explicitly build on earlier pipeline taxonomies (§§I, IV-A).
- **Comparison curation:** Retains 125 papers through August 2026, using keyword search and citation snowballing; separates shared-host comparisons from heterogeneous operating points (§II-A; Tables II–VII).
- **Audiovisual synthesis and evaluation agenda:** Examines audio compression and audio-guided selection, identifies missing cost accounting and modality ablations, and announces a maintained literature repository (Abstract; §V).

## Evidence and main results

All empirical results below are literature-reported comparisons assembled by the survey.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Frame selection helps unevenly across benchmarks. | On LLaVA-Video-7B with 64 frames, TSPO reports LongVideoBench accuracy of 63.9 versus uniform sampling’s 58.9, but Video-MME accuracy of 65.5 versus 64.4. These are fixed-frame comparisons, not demonstrated net speedups. | Table II; §IV-B3 |
| Temporal compression better preserves accuracy at aggressive retention in one controlled comparison. | On frozen LLaVA-OneVision-7B, 32 frames, LMMs-Eval, at 10% token retention: HoliTom retains 99.1% of the baseline mean accuracy at 6.9% relative LLM-prefill FLOPs; PruneVid retains 96.9% at 8.3%; VisionZip retains 91.6% at 8.3%. Mean covers MVBench, EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, and Video-MME without subtitles; baseline mean is 58.4. | Table V |
| Joint reduction can substantially lower prefill computation while retaining QA performance. | In HieraVid’s LLaVA-Video-7B rerun, HieraVid uses 24.5% of baseline prefill FLOPs. Accuracy is 58.3/79.9/59.2/62.3/70.8 versus 60.4/80.2/59.4/64.1/71.4 on MVBench, NExT-QA, EgoSchema, and Video-MME without/with subtitles, respectively. It also compresses before the LLM, preventing decoder-only attribution. | Table VII; §IV-E6 |
| Compression location determines which costs can fall. | Post-encoder selection has already paid encoding costs; encoder-only savings do not establish downstream savings, and separate mechanism speedups cannot simply be multiplied. | §§IV-C4, IV-D, IV-E6 |
| Audiovisual efficiency remains weakly isolated. | Authors report frequent omission of audio-encoder cost and uncommon modality ablations; no surveyed method reports energy measurements. | §§IV-A, V |

These comparisons support substantial compression benefits under named hosts and protocols. They do not establish a universal retention threshold, deployment speedup, or ranking across architectures, tasks, and hardware.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ scope limits:** Selection is representative rather than exhaustive; analyzer-centric and hybrid systems are excluded, and quantitative comparisons emphasize a limited backbone-size range (§§II-A, III-A).
- **Authors’ comparison caveats:** Training data, input coverage, baseline reproductions, evaluation views, and FLOP boundaries differ. Encoder recognition results cannot be transferred numerically to VideoLLM QA (§§IV-A, IV-C4; Tables V–VI).
- **Authors’ evidence gaps:** Evaluations concentrate on aggregate multiple-choice QA; the comparison tables contain no captioning, retrieval, or grounding metrics. Among the highlighted joint-stage methods, only HoliTom reports an ablation separating stages (§V).
- **Analyst inference:** Small accuracy differences cannot be treated as statistically reliable rankings because the supplied comparison tables provide no uncertainty estimates.
- **Coverage limitation:** The supplied source was truncated; this analysis does not imply complete paper or supplementary-material coverage.

## Open research questions

1. **Which stage combination delivers the best total savings?** Joint gains are rarely separated, and accounting boundaries differ (§V). **Next experiment:** Factorially ablate selection, encoder compression, connector reduction, and cache compression on one frozen host, measuring full-pipeline costs.
2. **Do QA-preserving budgets preserve fine temporal evidence?** Captioning and grounding are absent from the comparison tables (§V). **Next experiment:** Reuse a validated compression budget on caption generation and temporal localization, reporting results by video domain.
3. **When should audio guide visual compression?** Audio can be informative, irrelevant, or conflicting, while modality ablations remain sparse (§V). **Next experiment:** Compare fixed and learned audiovisual allocation on modality-specific and conflicting examples, counting encoder and allocator costs.
