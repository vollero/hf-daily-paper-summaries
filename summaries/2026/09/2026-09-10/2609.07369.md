# DF26: We Cannot Tell Fake From Real Anymore

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.07369) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07369)


## One-sentence takeaway
DF26 exposes substantial weaknesses in visual detection of modern generated public-speaking videos, although successful generator-specific detection and retraining qualify the paper’s broad “near chance” framing.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that benchmarks centered on face manipulation inadequately test detectors against modern generators that synthesize entire scenes. DF26 targets single-person public-speaking videos relevant to disinformation, combining controlled scene matching with known generator provenance to examine generalization beyond legacy training data (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 271 real and 2,420 synthetic videos across direct-to-camera recordings, official statements, and studio interviews, generated using seven systems. Open-source systems cover text-to-video and image-to-video; commercial systems cover text-to-video only (§§3–5).
- **Construction protocol and assets:** Filter source videos, normalize them to five seconds at 1280×720 using H.264, manually curate scenarios, and generate matched synthetic clips using Gemini-derived descriptions or source first frames. The authors provide metadata and prompts under controlled access and state that pipeline code is available (§§3–5, 8).
- **Evaluation:** Test existing frame-based and temporal detectors without fine-tuning, conduct a human comparison against CelebDF++ and DeepSpeak v2, and separately retrain GenD-PE on an open-source subset (§6). The contribution is a benchmark and empirical analysis, not a new detector architecture.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Legacy-trained detectors degrade sharply | Table 5 reports video-level AUROC falling from 94.3% on CDFv3 to 48.2% on DF26 for DFD-FCG, and from 92.3% to 61.6% for PwTF-DVD; corresponding DF26 EERs are 51.9% and 40.8%. Checkpoints were trained on FF++, without DF26 fine-tuning. | §6.1, Table 5 |
| Detection remains above chance for the strongest aggregate model | GenD-PE leads Table 5 with 69.7% pooled DF26 AUROC and 35.9% EER; Table 6 separately reports 72.7% macro-averaged AUROC. | Tables 5–6 |
| Generator differences are substantial | PwTF-DVD obtains 92.9% AUROC on Wan 2.2 T2V versus 41.9% on HunyuanVideo 1.5 I2V. Video-bootstrap 95% CIs are [90.5, 94.9] and [37.1, 46.6], respectively. | Tables 6, S3 |
| Commercial subsets are harder for both temporal detectors | Open-source versus closed-source AUROC: 57.4% versus 34.5% for DFD-FCG; 70.5% versus 48.3% for PwTF-DVD. These subsets differ in mode and scenario coverage. | Table 3; §§5, 6.4 |
| Humans recognize fewer DF26 fakes | Across 232 sessions using silent five-second clips, fake-class accuracy is 52.6% on DF26, 74.5% on CDFv3, and 69.8% on DSv2. Real-class accuracy is 76.0%, 75.5%, and 72.8%, respectively. | §6.5, Table 7; §S2 |
| Human and detector difficulty diverge | LTX I2V receives 27 correct fake judgments among 107 responses: 25.2% accuracy. On that generator, PwTF-DVD, GenD-PE, and DFD-HR achieve 79.5%, 81.5%, and 83.8% AUROC. These are different metrics and evaluation populations. | §S1, Table S1; Table 6 |
| Modern-generator training can transfer | Training GenD-PE on HunyuanVideo I2V official statements yields 96.8%, 95.6%, and 93.1% AUROC on Grok, Veo, and Wan 2.6, versus 66.5%, 76.6%, and 88.3% for FF++ training. Official statements are excluded from evaluation. | §6.3, Table 8 |

The evidence supports failure of many tested checkpoints under this benchmark’s distribution shift, not universal inability to detect synthetic videos. Human fake-class accuracy is not overall accuracy. Moreover, supplementary Table S2 reports different pooled temporal results—49.8%/49.9% AUROC/EER for DFD-FCG and 63.1%/41.0% for PwTF-DVD—without reconciling them with Table 5.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Small dataset and visual-only evaluation exclude audio realism, speech quality, and audiovisual synchronization (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Commercial-versus-open comparisons are diagnostic, not causal; model family, processing, quality, and generation mode can confound them (§6.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Scenario and provenance are entangled: official statements mostly originate from TalkingCelebs and lack commercial generations. Scenario differences therefore cannot cleanly isolate semantic effects (Table 4; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Human-study generalization is limited by volunteer students and unrecorded participant identities. Session independence cannot be established, and human confidence intervals are not supplied (§6.5).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** §3.1 says prompts derive from the first frame, whereas Stage D describes four frames. The supplied text also does not explain how three response options become binary accuracy or reconcile the pooled detector results.
- **Analyst inference:** Temporal confidence intervals resample videos without stated grouping by shared real-source clip; dependence among matched generations may affect uncertainty estimates (§S4).

## Open research questions
- **Can generator effects be separated from scenario and mode effects?** Existing subsets are unbalanced. Evaluate generators on matched source clips, scenarios, and generation modes wherever permitted.
- **How broadly does modern-generator retraining transfer?** Table 8 covers one detector and a narrow training scenario. Repeat source-disjoint training across detector families and hold out entire generators and scenarios.
- **Do human difficulty findings generalize across populations?** Participant identities and human uncertainty estimates are unavailable. Run a preregistered study with identifiable independent sessions, broader recruitment, and explicit binary-response scoring.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark design and broad performance degradation are well supported, but precise aggregate interpretation is weakened by inconsistent tables.

Material missing information includes reconciliation of pooled scores, exact prompt-frame selection, complete generation and retraining configurations, source/identity separation details, unique participant counts, human uncertainty estimates, and explicit response-label aggregation rules.
