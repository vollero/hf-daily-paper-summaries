# SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09113) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across 20 SAE feature-discovery tasks, agents approach the curated expert reference on activation selectivity but fall substantially behind on steering, with expert evaluation-set reuse and judge inconsistencies complicating interpretation.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a gap in evaluating whether agents can conduct hypothesis-driven mechanistic interpretability research: designing experiments, distinguishing concepts from superficial correlations, and selecting features that causally influence generation. They motivate this capability as a potential component of internal model auditing in autonomous AI development, although the benchmark does not test an actual self-improvement loop (§§1, 7).

## Approach and contributions
- **Discovery benchmark:** Twenty concept–layer tasks covering 17 concepts in Gemma-2-9B-IT, using Gemma Scope residual-stream SAEs at layers 9 and 20, each with 131,072 features. Agents write probes, retrieve and test candidates through `probe_sae`, and submit one feature; expert identities and evaluation texts are withheld (§§3.1–3.2; Appendix A.1).
- **Evaluation framework:** Expert-relative activation rank, positive-versus-control activation selectivity, and generation steering beyond baseline and norm-matched random controls. Overall averages these three scores; preservation and degeneration are reported separately (§3.3).
- **Comparative evidence:** Ten model–harness configurations, three investigations per task, plus analyses linking probe design, candidate selection, and generated outputs (§§4–5). The contribution is the benchmark and analysis, rather than a new SAE training algorithm.

Scores below average all 20 tasks; leaderboard uncertainties are standard deviations across three runs.

| Strong induction can accompany damaged output | Real-estate feature 84434 has Target Effect **0.944** and **52.5%** degeneration; feature 17219 has **0.900** and **32.5%**. Both have preservation **1.100/4**. | Table 10 |
| Repeated discovery remains variable | Grok’s mean Steering is **31.47**; retrospectively selecting its best of three investigations per task yields **46.00**. This is an oracle comparison, not a demonstrated selection policy. | Table 29 |

These results support feature discovery and intervention capabilities within the supplied environment, but neither general autonomous scientific competence nor improved alignment. Agents discover through activation queries; steering is evaluated after submission (§3.2). Consequently, the steering gap measures selection quality under activation-only feedback, rather than agents’ ability to perform interactive causal experimentation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** One base model, two SAE layers, predefined concepts, and single-feature discovery limit generalization. Fixed references, fixed text suites, and automated judging also restrict validity (§7).
- **Analyst inference—reference advantage:** Evaluation texts helped construct Expert features but were unavailable to agents. Expert performance therefore is not an equally held-out comparison, despite the authors describing it as an objective reference (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—measurement concerns:** Table 26 assigns full preservation to six- and eleven-line outputs requested to have four lines. Steering itself excludes preservation and degeneration penalties. Rank can reach 200, whereas the other components stop at 100, so equal arithmetic weights do not imply equal possible influence (§3.3).
- **Analyst inference—comparison confounds:** Harnesses differ, search budgets are agent-selected, and failed attempts are replaced by retries. No controlled harness or search-strategy ablation establishes that observed differences arise from model capability or hypothesis-testing quality alone (§4.1; Appendix A.3).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Section 5.4’s wording associates successful Portuguese selections with Expert feature 41424, but Tables 6 and 16 identify different features for Sol and Opus 4.8. Section 4.1 describes unconstrained reasoning, while Appendix A.3 specifies high reasoning settings and a timeout.

## Open research questions
- **Does causal feedback improve discovery?** Agents cannot test steering during search. Compare activation-only access against steering-enabled access under matched resource budgets.
- **How much does reference construction affect the gap?** Expert selection reused evaluation texts. Rebuild references on separate construction data and score all submissions on newly held-out suites.
- **Can induction and instruction preservation improve together?** Strong steering sometimes coincides with degeneration. Compare current selection against a preservation-aware objective using human-validated ratings.
- **Does performance transfer beyond this setup?** The benchmark fixes the model and restricts discovery to individual features. Repeat matched concepts across additional models, layers, and multi-feature interventions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial protocols and numerical evidence, but asymmetric reference construction and rating inconsistencies weaken broader conclusions.

Material gaps include human judge validation, judge agreement statistics, retry counts and costs, controlled harness comparisons, an independently held-out Expert evaluation, and the calibration fallback when no tested scale passes. The announced code and underlying archive were not inspected.
