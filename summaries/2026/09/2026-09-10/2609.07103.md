# Revisiting Complete Reasoning Traces for Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.07103) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07103)


## One-sentence takeaway
Endpoint-based supervised fine-tuning improves average reasoning accuracy in several tested settings by removing middle reasoning segments, but the evidence does not establish that models internally reconstruct missing steps.

## Problem and motivation
Machine-generated reasoning traces can contain lengthy detours, repetition, and backtracking. The authors question whether learning complete traces is necessary for effective post-training, and identify a gap in understanding which trajectory regions provide useful supervision. Their focus is segment position and content, rather than shortening every trace indiscriminately (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Endpoint-based SFT (E-SFT):** Split reasoning traces at double newlines, retain the first and last steps, and delete the middle. The default retains 100 steps at each end for s1K-1.1 and 200 for OpenThoughts3, targeting approximately 20% fewer tokens across each dataset—not a fixed fraction per example (§§4.2, C.3).
- **Segment-level evidence:** Attention analysis, answer regeneration after removal, answer perplexity after replacement, and content labeling examine the different roles of early, middle, and late reasoning (§§4.1, B).
- **Extensions to other objectives:** GRPO and on-policy distillation exclude middle tokens from the training objective while retaining them in context. This differs materially from deleting tokens during SFT (§4.4).
- **Inference-time extension:** After generating the full reasoning trace, shorten its middle before generating the final answer; this saves downstream computation, not initial reasoning generation (Appendix D).

The central contribution is an empirical reassessment and simple training recipe; the authors explicitly frame it this way rather than as a sophisticated new filtering technique (§4.3).

These results support endpoint retention as a useful post-training heuristic in the evaluated settings. They do not establish universal middle-step redundancy: answer similarity measures agreement with the original output, perplexity measures confidence, and attention patterns alone do not demonstrate causal necessity or correctness.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Gains depend on initial model capability and training data. Weaker models can benefit little or lose performance; excessive truncation hurts, and no principled cutoff rule is provided (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated:** Double newlines are approximate semantic boundaries, and functional step labels do not determine whether a step is redundant (§§B.2–B.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Internal reconstruction remains a hypothesis. Retained endings already contain conclusions, so preserved answer quality cannot independently demonstrate recovery of missing derivations (§4.1; Table A).
- **Analyst inference:** Lower training perplexity on a modified token distribution does not by itself establish a better optimization endpoint (Figure 6).
- **Analyst inference:** Three sampled evaluations per instance do not establish training-run robustness; confidence intervals and independent training-seed variation are not reported (§C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** The reasoning-quality judge measures repetition and flow against original s1K traces. “Equivalent” means order-dependent preferences, and the reported comparisons are not a direct E-SFT-versus-SFT correctness test (§§5, C.6).
- **Comparison ambiguity:** Table C labels its Llama comparator “Base,” although surrounding prose frames the experiment as an SFT comparison. This weakens interpretation of that cross-family result (§C.4).

## Open research questions

- **Does position or actual redundancy explain the gains?** Existing labeling does not assess redundancy directly. Next experiment: compare length-matched deletion of expert-labeled essential and redundant steps at different positions.
- **Does E-SFT recover missing reasoning or exploit retained conclusions?** Current diagnostics do not distinguish these mechanisms. Next experiment: retain endpoint structure while removing answer-bearing suffix content, then assess derivation validity and accuracy.
- **How should truncation depend on capability and data?** Appendix A shows sensitivity without a principled selection rule. Next experiment: sweep cutoff, model capability, and dataset size with repeated training seeds and matched compute.
- **Do masking gains persist through longer training?** GRPO and OPD are tested for only 100 steps. Next experiment: extend training and compare learning curves, final accuracy, and token usage across seeds (§C.7).
