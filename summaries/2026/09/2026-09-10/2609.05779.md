# Diffs vs. Whole Files: An Empirical Comparison of Iterative Edit-Based and Direct Generation for Flutter/Dart Code Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05779) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05779)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across two small Flutter/Dart code models, direct whole-file generation substantially outperformed feedback-free iterative edits, while diff wins correlated with shorter trajectories—a finding that supports, but does not causally establish, the authors’ “task locality” explanation.

## Problem and motivation
Diffs can reduce generated text and express localized changes, but their usefulness as a **training target** remains uncertain. The paper compares whole-file and edit-sequence training within a shared Flutter/Dart task pool, addressing confounds in comparisons involving different models, datasets, and edit formats (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Within-backbone comparison:** Train direct and steps variants of Rainbow-Pony-100M and Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B. Direct outputs a complete file; steps emits search/replace actions with a completion marker and a maximum of 20 steps, without compiler/test feedback between edits (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Failure and quality analysis:** Evaluate static-analysis success, reference likelihood, character similarity, and blinded GPT-4.1 ratings; compare identical task IDs and subsets passing static analysis under both regimes (§§3.2–3.4).
- **Locality hypothesis:** Relate diff wins to shorter generated trajectories and to refactoring and error-handling categories. This is an empirical association and proposed explanation, not a tested routing method (§§4.6–4.7).
- **Released assets:** The authors report releasing training datasets, checkpoints, evaluation outputs, and the training/evaluation harness (§8).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Direct substantially improves static-analysis success | Reported `dart_pass` advantage: **45.5 percentage points** for Rainbow-Pony on **1,789 tasks**, and **39.9 points** for Qwen on **1,792 tasks**, using greedy decoding. Absolute rates are absent from the retrieved table content. | §§3.2, 4.1; Table 2 |
| Most steps failures occur after normal completion | Approximately **84%** of Rainbow-Pony and **70%** of Qwen steps failures occur in trajectories ending with `done`. | §4.2; Tables 3–4 |
| Ambiguous-target fallback is associated with failure | Among normally completed trajectories, fallback-affected rows account for roughly **65%** and **71%** of failures for Rainbow-Pony and Qwen, respectively. | §4.3; Table 5 |

| Judge preference persists among jointly passing outputs | On **390 Rainbow-Pony** and **688 Qwen** paired tasks passing static analysis under both regimes, direct scores higher on all three **1–5** judge dimensions; all six comparisons reportedly have **p<0.001**, using Welch’s t-test. Means are unavailable. | §4.5; Table 7 |
| Diff wins favor shorter trajectories | Steps judge wins have shorter trajectories across both backbones and all three dimensions; all six reported tests have **p<0.001**. Refactoring and error-handling categories also have the lowest mean step counts according to the prose. | §§4.6.3–4.7; Tables 11–12 |

These results support a direct-generation advantage **under this training and evaluation setup**. They do not establish runtime correctness, a causal effect of spatial locality, or the superiority of direct generation in feedback-driven agents. The authors also caution that reference likelihood is mismatched to steps generation and character similarity may reward textual preservation (§4.6.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated training confounds:** Steps uses roughly ten times the fine-tuning data tokens; Qwen-direct has an unintended learning-rate schedule. Neither confound is resolved experimentally (§6).
- **Author-stated scope limits:** One language/framework domain, two small backbones, greedy single-sample decoding, no intermediate execution feedback, and a first-occurrence ambiguity heuristic (§6).
- **Author-stated evaluation limits:** Judge scores lack human validation; category wins have small counts and no category-level significance tests (§6).
- **Analyst inference:** Generated trajectory length can reflect model behavior or task difficulty, not just required spatial locality. The reported correlations therefore do not isolate the proposed mechanism (§§4.6–4.7).
- **Analyst inference:** Fallback/failure associations do not measure the benefit of replacing the fallback policy; a controlled intervention is missing (§§4.3, 6).
- **Evidence ambiguity:** Section 4.5’s assertion that “roughly half” the raw static-analysis gap represents residual quality differences is not supported by a clear decomposition in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **Does the ranking survive controlled training budgets and schedules?** Both are confounded here (§6). Retrain both regimes across matched token budgets with corrected schedules.
- **How much would improved edit application or feedback help?** Neither intervention is tested (§6). Compare first-occurrence fallback, ambiguity rejection, and compiler-feedback variants.
- **Can locality guide format selection before generation?** Current evidence relies on observed trajectories (§§4.6–4.7). Annotate required edit locations independently, then test a router against both fixed regimes.
- **Do the quality findings survive behavioral and human evaluation?** Static analysis and an unvalidated judge cannot settle this (§§3.2, 6). Add task-specific execution tests and blinded human ratings.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The prose consistently supports the main ranking, but the supplied retrieval omits table bodies and figure contents, preventing verification of absolute rates, confidence intervals, score means, and subgroup counts.

Other material gaps include omitted evaluation/checkpoint identifiers in §8, detailed split and decomposition procedures, measured inference token/latency costs, and experimental validation of the claimed locality mechanism.
