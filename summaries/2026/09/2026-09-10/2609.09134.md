# Co-Evolving Harnesses and Models: On-Policy Correction Helps Weaker Models Catch Up Where Imitation Fails

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09134) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09134)


## One-sentence takeaway
Across seven enterprise tasks, localized expert correction modestly improves Qwen’s mean success under an evolved harness, whereas full-trajectory expert imitation substantially degrades it (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
An agent’s performance depends on both its model weights and its harness: prompts, tools, execution hooks, and context management. The authors investigate whether lightweight fine-tuning adds to harness optimization or disrupts behavior that the harness already supports, focusing on affordable adaptation to enterprise tasks (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Cross-model harness evaluation:** Evolve task-specific harnesses with a weaker model using GEPA-style search, then evaluate a stronger Gemini expert in those harnesses to measure remaining model-side headroom (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Interference diagnosis:** Compare expert-trajectory LoRA-SFT under baseline and evolved harnesses. Failure classifications and case studies support the authors’ explanation that imitation changes planning behavior and weakens model–harness fit (§3.3; Appendices C–D).
- **Localized correction pipeline:** An automated MLE agent identifies a failing turn in a student rollout; the expert rewrites that turn while surrounding steps remain unchanged. LoRA-SFT uses these corrected trajectories plus student successes. Three correction candidates are sampled per turn and selected by a quality judge (§3.4; Appendix B).

The distinctive contribution is testing expert supervision in model-specific evolved harnesses; the paper acknowledges prior on-policy correction methods (§2). Repeated harness–model co-evolution is proposed, rather than demonstrated over multiple rounds.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Harness optimization produces the largest improvement | Qwen mean test success across seven tasks: **29.2% → 78.0%**, +48.8 percentage points; results averaged over three runs. | Table 1, rows 1–2 |
| The expert benefits on average from the Qwen-evolved harness | Gemini mean test success: **84.4% → 93.6%** across the same tasks; Qwen achieves 78.0% under that evolved harness. | Table 1, rows 2–4 |
| Full-trajectory imitation harms evolved-harness performance | Qwen mean test success: **78.0% → 63.1%**, −14.9 points; all seven tasks regress, with reported drops of 4.2–29.9 points. | Table 1, rows 2, 5 |
| Baseline-harness imitation helps on average | Qwen mean test success: **29.2% → 35.5%**, +6.3 points; individual tasks do not all improve. | Table 1, rows 1, 6 |
| Regression also appears in another student family | Gemma Webarena success: **46.7%** with baseline harness, **55.6%** with evolved harness, **41.1%** after expert imitation. | §3.3.2 |
| Correction adds a modest gain | Qwen mean test success: **78.0 ± 0.97% → 79.7 ± 0.67%**; mean ± SEM over three runs. Five tasks improve; payroll and budget decline by 0.4 and 0.6 points. | Table 1, rows 2, 7 |
| Failure labels support the planning hypothesis | Planning defects constitute **1.1%**, **14.6%**, and **1.8%** of hard failures for evolved-harness base, imitation, and correction respectively. | Table 3 |
| Domain-recipe usage increases after imitation | Share of rollouts invoking the domain-computation recipe rises from **30.8% to 76.1%**. | Table 2 |

The comparisons establish strong imitation regression in the tested Qwen setting and a smaller average correction benefit. They support, but do not causally prove, the authors’ planning-fit explanation. Recipe usage is not proof of knowledge mastery, and category shares among failures do not establish absolute error-rate reductions.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Correction leaves a substantial expert–student gap; the authors suggest lightweight LoRA capacity as a possible explanation (§3.4). Environment modifications also limit direct comparison with the inherited benchmark’s earlier results (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Gemma replication covers only Webarena and imitation. Correction generalization across model families and repeated co-evolution remains untested in the supplied results.
- **Analyst inference—causality:** The comparison changes trajectory source, edit extent, and data selection together. No matched-data ablation isolates on-policy states from localized editing or judge selection.
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Failure labels use an LLM judge without reported human validation or agreement estimates. Formal significance testing for the modest correction gain is absent.
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Claims of matching or improving every task exceed Table 1: correction slightly declines on two tasks, and Gemini’s evolved harness underperforms its baseline on payroll and budget. Table 2 also shows most non-recipe adoption rates declining after imitation. The overall failure rates in §3.4 are not reconciled with Table 1’s mean success rates.

## Open research questions
- **Which correction component drives improvement?** Current comparisons conflate student-state supervision and minimal editing. Next experiment: match training tokens and examples while comparing single-turn edits, expert continuations, and full trajectories.
- **Does repeated co-evolution compound gains?** Figure 1 outlines a loop without multi-round results. Next experiment: alternate harness search and correction across several rounds under a fixed total budget.
- **Does correction generalize beyond Qwen?** The second-family experiment tests imitation regression only. Next experiment: evaluate correction across the full task suite with Gemma and report task-level uncertainty.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The main performance comparisons are explicit; mechanism and broader generalization are less secure.

Missing details include task split sizes, imitation dataset sizes and mixture ratios, complete correction-data accounting, failure-localization and quality-judge specifications, downstream consistency checks after editing a turn, model-selection criteria for reported best LoRA configurations, end-to-end costs, and public code or checkpoint availability.
