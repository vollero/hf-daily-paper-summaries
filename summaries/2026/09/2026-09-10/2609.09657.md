# RESCUE-BENCH: Towards Relation-Aware Multi-Party Emotional Support Conversation Systems

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09657) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09657)


## One-sentence takeaway
RESCUE-BENCH evaluates six components of emotional support in couple and family conversations, finding limited agreement with annotated relation patterns and support strategies across ten zero-shot LLMs, without establishing real-world support effectiveness (§5; Appendix B).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify a gap between individual-focused emotional support and support for interconnected recipients: helping one person can affect others and their relationships. They argue that existing AI benchmarks insufficiently evaluate evolving interpersonal viewpoints, tensions, intervention timing, and recipient selection (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task formulation:** Relation-aware emotional support combines individual emotional states, directed interpersonal states, and group relation patterns with decisions about when, whom, and how to support (§3).
- **Benchmark and annotations:** RESCUE-BENCH contains 191 clips—174 couple and 17 family—with 7,079 annotated turns and 1,064.8 video minutes. Gemini-3.1-Pro supplies preliminary multimodal annotations, reviewed and revised by three PhD-level annotators (§4; Table 3; Appendix G).
- **Evaluation framework:** Six tasks cover emotion recognition (ER), viewpoint prediction (VP), relation pattern prediction (RPP), intervention timing (ITP), support targets (STP), and support strategies (SSP). Ten LLMs receive common zero-shot prompts (§§3.2, 5.1; Appendix H.2).

This is primarily a benchmark contribution. The sequential group-welfare objective is conceptual and is neither instantiated nor optimized (Appendix B).

## Evidence and main results
All model results below use the shared zero-shot evaluation.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Timing and emotion receive comparatively strong scores | Across ten models, mean ITP F1 is **82.62%** and mean ER score is **4.05/5**. ITP candidates are balanced within samples; ER averages judged emotion similarity and an intensity-matching score. | Table 11; Appendices A.5, C |
| Relation-pattern classification remains weak | Mean RPP accuracy is **40.45%**; DeepSeek-V4-Pro leads at **45.60%** across both scenarios. | Table 5; Table 11 |
| Viewpoints score below emotions | Mean VP judge score is **3.58/5**, versus ER **4.05/5**; these scores have different constructions, so the comparison is descriptive. | Table 11; Appendices A.5–A.6 |
| Strategy prediction is harder than target prediction under the benchmark | Mean STP Recall@1/MRR is **64.34%/78.35%**, versus SSP **31.88%/44.96%**. Both rank three candidates; SSP receives the known support target. DeepSeek-V4-Pro leads SSP at **37.26%/52.00%**. | Tables 5, 11; Appendices A.2–A.3 |
| Scenario effects differ by task | Mean STP Recall@1 falls from **66.88%** for couples to **54.87%** for families; SSP rises from **30.54%** to **36.83%**, using scenario-specific strategy taxonomies. | Table 11; Appendix D.2 |
| Rare or transitional patterns are difficult | Couple RPP per-label mean accuracy is **77.38%** for pursue-withdraw but **2.23%** for mixed-transition. Their label shares are **38.7%** and **6.3%**, respectively. | Appendix E.2; Figure 6 |
| Judge agreement is supported on a limited sample | On **200** sampled predictions, split equally between ER and VP, human–LLM exact agreement is **79.5%**, with **0.235 MAE**; each item receives one human rating. | Appendix F; Table 12 |

These results establish agreement with the benchmark’s annotations. They do not establish optimal interventions or improved group well-being: timing labels derive from recorded therapist entries, and support labels describe recorded interventions (Appendix C). The authors’ interpretation that relational reasoning is difficult is consistent with the results, but the evaluation does not isolate it causally from label imbalance, task design, or input differences.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Documentary editing, participant selection, demographics, and cultural context may introduce bias; relational judgments are subjective; labels are long-tailed; source-media restrictions limit multimodal reproducibility (Limitations, p. 9).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation scope:** Known targets in SSP and separate component tasks leave error propagation through a complete support system untested (Appendices A.3, B).
- **Analyst inference—selection:** Excluding unclear relation/strategy labels narrows evaluation to interpretable cases; balancing ITP changes its relationship to naturally occurring intervention frequencies (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—reliability:** Human–judge agreement measures similarity to reference annotations, not annotation truth or therapeutic value. The described human evaluation does not measure inter-rater agreement because each subset has one evaluator (Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference—causality:** Non-monotonic accuracy across dialogue positions does not by itself distinguish temporal-tracking failures from changing label composition; no controlled context or modality ablation is reported in the supplied text (§5.3; Figures 4–5).

## Open research questions
- **Does explicit relational memory improve prediction?** Position-based analyses suggest tracking difficulties but do not isolate them. Compare identical LLMs with and without updated participant-relation summaries, holding context budgets fixed (§5.3).
- **How much do audiovisual cues contribute?** Annotation is multimodal, but per-model evaluation inputs and modality effects remain unclear. Compare transcript-only, textual cue descriptions, and direct audiovisual inputs on identical instances (§3.3; Appendix A).
- **Are alternative strategies unfairly penalized?** SSP matches one annotated next intervention, which does not establish a uniquely appropriate action. Have multiple experts rate acceptable strategy sets and compare these scores with existing rankings (Appendices A.3, C).
- **Do benchmark gains transfer beyond these sources?** Documentary sampling and the small family subset limit generalization. Evaluate on independently collected, appropriately consented interactions with broader demographic and cultural coverage (Table 3; Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Task definitions and reported scores are well supported, but causal and practical conclusions are limited.

The supplied source was **truncated**, including an omission within the annotation prompt; this analysis does not imply complete coverage. Material unresolved information includes exact per-model input modalities and context assembly, decoding settings, annotation agreement and revision rates, statistical uncertainty for model comparisons, and confirmed release contents. The abstract says resources are released, whereas the Ethics Statement describes a planned release.
