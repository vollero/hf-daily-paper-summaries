# StochBench: A Domain-Specific Benchmark for Stochastic Processes in Lean

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09264) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09264)


## One-sentence takeaway
StochBench introduces 450 graduate stochastic-processes theorem targets in Lean 4, with an Opus 4.8-based agent proving 34.9% under a 15-minute per-target limit, although most targets assume properties that limit what successful proofs establish about the original problems (§§3–4, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that competition mathematics and broad textbook benchmarks obscure provers’ strengths and weaknesses within individual disciplines. Stochastic processes also requires infrastructure missing from the chosen Mathlib environment, complicating faithful formalization. StochBench prioritizes depth within this domain while explicitly documenting differences in formalization scope (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Domain-focused corpus:** 450 informal–formal pairs across eight topics, combining original problems with textbook and MIT course material. Human-written definitions and hypotheses were translated into Lean statements with model assistance and checked for elaboration in Lean 4.30.0 (§3).
- **Shared representations and scope labels:** Reusable stochastic-process definitions support 114 direct targets and 336 abstracted targets. Direct targets use Mathlib or shared definitions; abstracted targets assume required properties, sometimes including intermediate results (§3, Table 1).
- **Baseline and released assets:** The authors release theorem targets, shared definitions, and baseline proof attempts. Their compiler-guided agent uses Lean feedback, library search, and proof revision, with results broken down by topic and representation (§§3–4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus is predominantly abstracted | 450 targets: 114 direct and 336 abstracted, spanning eight topics. | §3, Table 1 |
| Baseline solves roughly one-third | 157/450 clean proofs, 34.9%; one Opus 4.8-based agent run per target, counting recorded proving times ≤900 seconds. | §4, Table 1 |
| Direct targets have higher observed success | Direct: 79/114, 69.3%; abstracted: 78/336, 23.2%, under the same baseline protocol. These are unmatched problem groups. | Table 1 |
| Topic performance varies substantially | Martingales/stopping: 58/94, 61.7%; renewal processes: 2/41, 4.9%, under the baseline protocol. | Table 1 |
| Abstracted targets can require substantial supporting proofs | Q361 develops nine auxiliary theorems in a separate run lasting more than 30 minutes, outside the baseline protocol. | Appendix A.1, Figure 2 |
| Failures have multiple sources | Qualitative inspection identifies proof-search failures, missing lemmas/difficult interfaces, and formalization defects involving missing assumptions; category counts are absent. | §4 |

A clean proof is accepted by Lean without `sorry`, `sorryAx`, or additional admitted facts, as checked by Lean comparator (§4). The reported results establish success on the stated formal targets under one protocol. They do not establish complete correspondence with source mathematics, a causal effect of abstraction, or superiority over other provers. The extended Q361 example illustrates proof structure rather than aggregate gains from additional time.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Curation, faithfulness review, and topic/scope labels depend on human judgment; classification terminology is not rigorously defined (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Elaboration checks typing, while proof checking establishes consequences of hypotheses. Neither alone validates correspondence with the informal problem; further peer review is needed (§3).
- **Scope limitation:** Supplied properties can remove substantive work. For example, first-step equations do not themselves establish correspondence with pathwise expected hitting times (§3, Appendix A.1).
- **Evaluation limitation:** One agent, one budget, and one run per target provide no model comparison or repeated-run variability estimate. Representation comparisons are confounded by problem and library-support differences (§4).
- **Analyst inference:** Using the same model family for formalization assistance and proving could introduce representation affinity; the paper provides no cross-model experiment to assess this (§4).

## Open research questions
1. **How much does representation affect proof success?** Existing class comparisons use different problems. Next experiment: construct matched direct and abstracted versions and evaluate them under identical budgets (Table 1, §4).
2. **How prevalent are semantic defects?** Review found missing assumptions but reports no counts. Next experiment: independently audit a stratified sample for source correspondence and assumption adequacy (§§3–4).
3. **How sensitive are results to agent and time budget?** The baseline has one configuration, while Q361 succeeds in a separate longer run. Next experiment: compare multiple agents and budgets with repeated runs (§4, Appendix A.1).
4. **Would reusable intermediate lemmas improve difficult topics?** Inspection identifies missing infrastructure, and Q361 requires coordinated auxiliary results. Next experiment: compare unchanged targets with and without a curated supporting-lemma library (§4, Appendix A.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported corpus composition and baseline summary; **medium** in broader conclusions about domain-level proving ability.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes the exact Mathlib revision, complete baseline prompts and model settings, per-target timing and cost distributions, quantified defect categories, independent faithfulness-audit results, repeated-run variability, and controlled model or representation comparisons.
