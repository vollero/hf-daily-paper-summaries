# AgenticGen: Reward-Guided Agentic Video Generation for Advertising

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09187) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09187)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgenticGen optimizes advertising strategy selection and executable video drafts using business and quality rewards, with reported TikTok A/B lifts over supervised fine-tuning of 2.72% CTR, 2.63% CVR, and 9.61% advertiser value (§5.3, Table 6).

## Problem and motivation
Realistic video synthesis does not determine which product materials, creative strategies, or narratives will produce effective advertisements. The authors identify a missing learning loop between advertising delivery outcomes and the reasoning decisions that shape videos. Direct online reinforcement learning is difficult because reliable feedback takes time and is affected by delivery bias and variable user behavior (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Two trainable reasoning stages:** A Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking policy selects feasible strategies from an expert catalog, then produces structured drafts executed by video generation and rendering tools. Strategy families cover asset editing, reference-guided generation, and cross-asset remixing (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Complementary reward construction:** Randomized, impression-balanced delivery supports within-product preference comparisons. A Bradley–Terry reward model learns business preferences; a separate rubric judge learns expert quality preferences from unanimously labeled human comparisons (§§3.2, 4.1–4.2).
- **Sequential policy optimization:** DPO trains both stages on delivery-derived preferences. GRPO then alternates between strategy optimization using global performance priors and overlap with local winners, and draft optimization using combined business and rubric rewards (§§4.3–4.4; Appendix B).

The contribution is this integrated advertising workflow and feedback design. The authors claim a first formulation of reward-guided agentic advertising video generation; the supplied text does not independently establish that priority.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Pairwise business reward modeling improves accuracy | On the impression-balanced validation set, Bradley–Terry training achieves **60.85%**, versus **52.92%** for pointwise classification: **7.93 percentage points** higher. | §5.2.1, Table 1 |
| Additional modalities improve reward prediction | Impression-balanced accuracy rises from **56.55%** with video features to **58.43%** with audio, **59.36%** with storyline features, and **60.85%** with ad-specific features. | §5.2.1, Table 2 |
| Rubric fine-tuning improves human preference alignment | Against off-the-shelf Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, accuracy increases from **55.40% to 69.50%** on human-labeled validation pairs and **49.20% to 53.30%** on impression-balanced pairs. | §5.2.2, Table 3 |
| DPO improves held-out preference ranking | SFT → DPO accuracy: selection **49.72% → 56.48%**; draft **50.64% → 58.34%**. SFT uses sequence likelihood; DPO uses policy/reference likelihood ratios. | §5.2.3, Table 4; Appendix C |
| Fused GRPO rewards balance the two evaluators | Generated videos compared with same-prompt SFT videos: fusion achieves **60.52% performance / 55.86% rubric** win rates, versus **61.04% / 51.74%** for performance-only and **50.78% / 60.24%** for rubric-only training. | §5.2.4, Table 5 |
| Online gains follow both system integration and optimization | TikTok impression-balanced A/B tests report relative CTR/CVR/Advv lifts of **3.48% / 2.30% / 9.83%** for SFT over the manually configured Pre-Agent pipeline, and **2.72% / 2.63% / 9.61%** for DPO+GRPO over SFT. | §5.3, Table 6 |

These results support improved preference alignment and reported business performance in the tested deployment. Offline GRPO evaluation uses the reward models that guide optimization, so it does not independently establish human-perceived quality. Online results provide separate business evidence, but missing uncertainty estimates prevent assessing statistical precision. Advertiser value is bid-weighted conversion value; its lift cannot be attributed to CTR and CVR alone (§5.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-identified:** Business reward accuracy remains moderate; the authors suggest missing user behavior and user–ad interaction features contribute (§5.2.1). They also identify weak source materials as a constraint (§5.3).
- **Author-disclosed reproducibility limit:** Commercial sensitivity prevents publication of the complete strategy catalog (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation dependence:** Reward-model win rates can favor the objectives optimized during GRPO; no independent post-optimization human assessment or direct clickbait/defect-rate result is reported (§5.2.4).
- **Analyst inference—limited attribution:** Online tests combine DPO and GRPO. They do not isolate either stage, either draft reward, or global versus local strategy rewards (§5.3).
- **Analyst inference—selection and generalization:** Unanimous human labels exclude ambiguous pairs, while evaluation remains within TikTok and its expert criteria. Product-disjoint or temporal holdout guarantees are not specified (§§4.2, 5.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison interpretation:** SFT and DPO preference accuracy use different scoring rules, limiting interpretation as a direct comparison of generated-video quality (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
1. **Does fused reward training improve independently judged quality?** Current GRPO evidence relies on learned judges. Next experiment: blinded human evaluation of SFT, single-reward, and fused-reward outputs, including ambiguous cases and explicit defect labels.
2. **Which optimization components cause the online lift?** The online comparison bundles DPO and GRPO. Next experiment: controlled A/B ablations separating DPO, selection GRPO, draft GRPO, and reward components.
3. **How robust are gains to new products and weak materials?** Transfer is not tested explicitly, and source quality is an acknowledged constraint. Next experiment: product-disjoint, time-separated evaluation stratified by source-material quality and bid level.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly describes the method and reported results, but leaves material validation details unavailable: online sample sizes, test duration, randomization unit, confidence intervals, absolute business metrics, category/audience breakdowns, detailed holdout separation, compute and latency costs, and independent final-output quality judgments. Public code, weights, and dataset availability are not established.
