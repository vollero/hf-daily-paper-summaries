# WearableQA: A Benchmark for Health Reasoning over Real-World Wearable Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05405) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05405)


## One-sentence takeaway
WearableQA evaluates reasoning over real users’ longitudinal wearable records, finding substantial computational weaknesses in LLMs and a large accuracy gain from Python access in the tested GPT-5.4 setting (Tables 1, 3).

## Problem and motivation
Interpreting wearable histories requires both computing patterns from noisy measurements and interpreting their physiological significance. The authors identify a gap between static medical-knowledge benchmarks or synthetic time-series evaluations and reasoning over real individuals’ longitudinal records, including blood biomarkers and demographics (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 4,084 ten-option questions derived from 200 users, with daily wearable measurements, blood panels, demographics, and cohort-reference statistics; the stated evaluation context includes up to 500 days of history (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Construction framework:** Literature-supported relationships and statistically screened population patterns define reasoning objectives. Reusable computation primitives then derive labels from individual measurements; distractor checks, model review, human verification, and shortcut audits filter questions (§§3.3–3.5).
- **Diagnostic taxonomy:** Sixteen question types cross data versus health reasoning with single- versus cross-signal reasoning. Grounding source is a separate annotation (§3.2).
- **Evaluation:** Fourteen LLMs are compared under chain-of-thought and direct-answer prompting, supplemented by input-format, tool-access, and context-removal experiments (§4).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Wide variation, substantial remaining errors | Default CoT overall accuracy: Gemini-3.1-Pro **72.9%**, Claude-Opus-4.6 **60.2%**, Llama-3.2-3B **19.6%**; ten-option chance baseline **10%**. Best open-weight model, Gemma-4-26B-A4B, scores **42.5%**. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Data reasoning is usually harder | GPT-5.4 scores **45.0% data / 63.5% health**; Gemini-3.1-Pro is the sole reversal, at **75.4% / 67.8%**, under CoT. | Table 1 |
| Cross-signal difficulty is model-dependent | GPT-5.4 scores **59.1% single / 45.7% cross**, whereas Gemini-3.1-Pro scores **72.4% / 73.2%**, under CoT. | Table 1 |
| Strong overall models retain task-specific weaknesses | Gemini-3.1-Pro scores **94.4%** on excursion counting but **35.9%** on recovery time and **26.1%** on cross-signal prediction, under CoT. | Table 6 |
| CoT gains vary markedly | Overall DA → CoT accuracy: Claude-Opus-4.6 **40.6% → 60.2%**; Gemini-3.1-Pro **71.3% → 72.9%**; Gemma-3-4B **20.6% → 19.8%**. | Table 7 |
| Python access outperforms format changes in the tested model | GPT-5.4 overall accuracy: row baseline **51.2%**, CSV **51.5%**, Markdown plus statistics **51.9%**, fixed-representation Python agent **71.3%**, multi-view agent **69.7%**. | Table 3 |
| Wearable measurements materially support answers | For Claude-Opus-4.6, withholding all time series reduces overall accuracy **60.2% → 17.3%**, while demographics, blood panels, and cohort references remain. | Table 4 |
| Familiar associations are easier for some models | GPT-5.4 scores **63.2%** on definitional pairs versus **15.0%** on empirical pairs, with relationship-strength bands matched; groups contain **102** and **29** questions respectively. | Table 2; Appendix A.2 |

These results establish differences in benchmark answer accuracy and support the importance of computational access and wearable context. They do not establish clinical effectiveness, causal physiological relationships, or general tool-use benefits across models. The definitional-pair comparison is consistent with reliance on priors, but does not directly identify models’ internal reasoning.

## Limitations and validity risks
The paper has no dedicated limitations section. The following distinguish explicit design boundaries from analyst assessments:

- **Author-described boundaries:** Ambiguous or undefined cases are discarded, population patterns must pass stability gates, and study-specific thresholds are avoided because they may not transfer across cohorts (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference—selection:** These filters may underrepresent weak, ambiguous, or unstable patterns encountered in deployment. Users were also selected for high taxonomy coverage (§3.1).
- **Analyst inference—clinical validity:** Deterministic labels establish consistency with construction rules; observational grounding and rule-derived interpretations do not independently validate diagnoses or recommendations (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation breadth:** Tool and representation experiments cover GPT-5.4; context removal covers Claude-Opus-4.6. Their generality across models is untested here (Tables 3–4).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Table 4 reports unchanged overall accuracy after history removal despite reduced history-dependent accuracy and nearly unchanged window-dependent accuracy. Its uncertainty differs from Table 1 for the same full-context score. Figure 10 also shows 528 days of released history despite the stated 500-day context limit; the distinction is unexplained.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Multiple questions share users, but no user-clustered uncertainty analysis is described. Human-review coverage and agreement are also unspecified (§§3.5, 4.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does Python access help consistently across models?** Only GPT-5.4 receives the reported tool evaluation. Repeat matched tool/no-tool experiments across model families with fixed computational budgets.
- **Do filtered patterns generalize to less curated records?** Construction rejects ambiguous cases and selects users for coverage. Evaluate on independently sampled users, including missing and borderline measurements.
- **Are health labels clinically reliable beyond construction rules?** No independent clinical-outcome validation is reported. Compare labels with blinded clinician adjudication and, where available, observed outcomes.
- **What drives recovery-time and cross-signal prediction failures?** Per-task scores reveal weaknesses without isolating their causes. Supply verified intermediate calculations and test whether interpretation errors persist.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the benchmark design and principal accuracy comparisons, but unresolved reporting inconsistencies limit interpretation.

Material missing information includes detailed cohort provenance and device composition, readable demographic distributions, missing-data handling, full labeling rules and literature cards, human-audit statistics, agent prompts and resource budgets, exact model versions, and explanations for the context-ablation and uncertainty discrepancies. Dataset links are supplied, but their contents are not established by the paper text.
