# The Price of Sparsity: Sufficient Conditions for Sparse Recovery using Sparse and Sparsified Measurements

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.01809) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01809)


## One-sentence takeaway
The authors derive sufficient sample sizes for approximate binary support recovery under sparse Gaussian measurements and post-hoc design sparsification, while leaving exact-recovery equivalence and efficient algorithms unresolved (Theorems 1 and 3; Remark 2.1).

## Problem and motivation
The paper studies recovery of an unknown binary signal with \(s\) nonzeros in dimension \(p\) from noisy linear observations. Sparse measurement matrices reduce storage and matrix-operation costs but can require more observations; the authors address sufficient recovery conditions complementary to existing necessary bounds (§1.1).

They also consider dense observations whose design matrix is subsequently sparsified: can recovery still succeed using the retained entries and rescaled responses? This introduces a mismatch between the design used to generate observations and the design used for estimation (§1.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Sparse-measurement guarantees:** Analyze maximum-likelihood estimation over binary vectors with exactly \(s\) nonzeros. Conditional Gaussian calculations, Chernoff bounds, and a union bound yield sufficient sample sizes in the high-SNR regime \(ds/p\to\infty\), for both sublinear and proportional signal sparsity (Theorem 1; §4).
- **Explicit sparsity cost:** Compare the resulting threshold scale with dense measurements and prior necessary bounds. The comparison distinguishes impossibility of uniform exact recovery from successful vanishing fractional error (Corollary 2; Remark 2.1).
- **Active-sparsification guarantee:** Independently retain each dense Gaussian design entry with probability \(\psi=d/p\), rescale responses to \(\widetilde Y=\psi Y\), and minimize squared residuals over binary supports. A shrunken Chernoff parameter controls exceptional masks and supports a sufficient sample-size scale of \(p/\psi^2\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\psi\) (Theorem 3; Lemmas 5.2–5.3).

## Evidence and main results
Let \(e=\lvert\widehat S\triangle S^\star\rvert/(2s)\), and let \(h\) denote binary entropy.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sublinear sparse-measurement recovery | For \(s=o(p)\), \(d=o(p)\), \(ds/p\to\infty\), fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), \(n\ge(1+\varepsilon)\frac{2s\log(p/s)}{\log(ds/p)+\log(\delta/(2\sigma^2))}\) ensures \(\Pr(e<\delta)\to1\). | Theorem 1(1) |
| Proportional signal recovery | For \(s=\alpha p\), fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and the same measurement assumptions, \(n\ge(1+\varepsilon)\frac{2h(\alpha)p}{\log d+\log(\delta\alpha/(2\sigma^2))}\) ensures \(\Pr(e<\delta)\to1\). | Theorem 1(2) |
| Sampling cost relative to dense measurements | In the sublinear high-SNR setting, the ratio of stated threshold scales is \(\Gamma=\log s/\log(ds/p)\). Recovery notions in the accompanying necessary and sufficient statements differ. | Eq. (10); Remark 2.1 |
| Active-sparsification recovery | For fixed \(\alpha,\delta,\varepsilon\) and sufficiently small fixed \(\psi>0\), \(s=\alpha p\), \(d=\psi p\), and \(n\ge(1+\varepsilon)\frac{2h(\alpha)p}{\log(1+\delta\psi^2/[(1-\psi)(2-\delta(1-\psi))])}\) ensure \(\Pr(e<\delta)\to1\). The threshold expression scales as \(\Theta(p/\psi^2)\) for small \(\psi\). | Theorem 3; Eqs. (14)–(15) |
| Cheaper matrix operations | In Example 2.2’s proportional-signal, slowly growing subpolynomial-\(d\) setting, multiplication costs change from \(\Theta(p^2/\log p)\) for dense measurements to \(\Theta(pd/\log d)\) for sparse measurements, at their respective sampling scales. | Example 2.2; §4.3 |

These are analytical results, with no empirical accuracy or runtime measurements. They establish sufficient conditions for the specified estimators, not practical polynomial-time recovery. The active-sparsification bound has no matching necessity result, and Corollary 2 does not establish an all-or-nothing transition under a shared error criterion.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Sparse-measurement sufficiency requires high SNR; lower-SNR regimes and an all-or-nothing strengthening remain open (§6; Remark 2.1).
- **Author-stated:** Computing the MLE is exponential-time in general. Reduced matrix-operation cost therefore does not establish efficient recovery (§2.2).
- **Scope:** Guarantees assume binary signals, known \(s\), independent Gaussian/Bernoulli designs, and fixed noise variance. Broader signal amplitudes are motivated intuitively rather than covered by the stated theorems (§1.1; §§2.1, 3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Theorem 3 fixes \(\psi\) before taking \(p\to\infty\); its small-\(\psi\) expression does not establish a guarantee for arbitrary sequences \(\psi(p)\to0\).
- **Source ambiguity:** The extracted sparsification-budget expression appears as \(\Theta(p/n)\), whereas algebraically inverting Eq. (15) gives a boundary proportional to \(\sqrt{p/n}\). Square-root formatting is degraded elsewhere in the supplied text, so a rendering loss cannot be distinguished from an original inconsistency (§§1.2, 3.2).
- **Source inconsistency:** §1.1 states \(\operatorname{Var}(X_{ij})=1\), but Definition 2.1 implies \(d/p\); the explicit definition and SNR formula agree with each other.

## Open research questions
- **Does a shared-error all-or-nothing threshold hold?** Remark 2.1 leaves the lower/upper recovery-criterion gap unresolved. Next experiment: enumerate supports at manageable dimensions and track both exact recovery and fractional error across the proposed threshold.
- **Can efficient algorithms tolerate stronger measurement sparsity?** The paper supplies MLE guarantees beyond the cited Lasso setting but no algorithmic result (§§2.2, 6). Next experiment: compare Lasso and exhaustive MLE on identical sparse Gaussian instances while varying \(d\).
- **What happens when active retention vanishes with dimension?** Theorem 3 covers fixed retention, while §6 conjectures impossibility for \(d=o(p)\). Next experiment: vary decreasing retention sequences and growing sample sizes, measuring fractional error to test where the fixed-retention prediction fails.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The main theorem statements and scope are explicit, but mathematical rendering limits detailed verification. Missing information includes empirical validation, runtime benchmarks, a reported code release, matching active-sparsification lower bounds, and guarantees for vanishing retention or general nonbinary signals.
