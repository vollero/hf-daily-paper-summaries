# Scores Alone Do Not Prove Discovery: The Discovery Certification Protocol for Auditing AI Research Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09219) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09219)


## One-sentence takeaway
DCP separates useful performance, recovery without the target’s research history, and feedback benefit through executable audits, with two controlled demonstrations supporting scoped decisions rather than historical novelty claims (§§3–5; Appendix D).

## Problem and motivation
A strong final score does not establish that an agent’s experimental history was necessary: another agent might reach the same outcome from starting information and public sources. Conversely, experimental feedback might improve success even when alternative routes exist. The authors identify a need to evaluate utility, recoverability, and feedback effects separately (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Outcome-level recovery protocol:** Preregister information, resources, validity rules, and evaluation. Gate 1 checks useful improvement on sealed data. Gate 2 gives matched challengers starting information and all captured Web content, withholds the target history, and accepts any valid method reaching the recovery threshold (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Two explicit decisions:** Core requires adequate controls, zero qualified recoveries, and a finite-sample upper bound on recovery probability for one fresh registered episode. Any qualified recovery vetoes Core. Optional Evidence adds a randomized truthful-versus-neutral feedback effect from a shared checkpoint, with independent null calibration and a registered effect margin (§§3.4–3.5).
- **Executable audit infrastructure:** Task adapters, frozen evidence bundles, a reusable collection harness, and a deterministic offline verifier support replay across controlled cases. Reported outcomes are local verifier decisions; formal issuance additionally requires independent provenance verification and countersigning (§3.7; Reproducibility Statement; Appendix D).

These author-reported results demonstrate the protocol’s decisions under the specified tasks, models, information, and budgets. They do not establish impossibility of recovery, historical priority, or feedback necessity for the selected target run. Offline replay checks recorded numerical decisions; the supplied text does not establish independent formal certification (Appendices A.3, D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Recovery bounds apply to one fresh episode from the registered distribution. Larger search campaigns have different recovery probabilities (§3.4; Appendix A.3).
- **Author-stated causal boundary:** Feedback effects are conditional on a checkpoint and specified policies; null calibration applies only to its registered task family (§3.5).
- **Author-stated provenance boundary:** Released records set `formal certificate issued=false`; independent verification and countersigning remain additional requirements (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference:** Constructed information deficits and a deterministic virtual catalyst environment leave generalization to open-ended, noisy scientific work untested (§§4.1–4.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Different models are used for the two complete domains, so these results cannot isolate model effects from task effects. The separate Web-disabled SQLite audit is not the additional Web-withholding intervention required to estimate Web contribution (§§3.6, 4.1; Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions
- **Does DCP remain practical in noisy research environments?** Complete demonstrations use controlled deterministic evaluations. Next experiment: preregister an audit with noisy measurements and sampled sealed evaluation.
- **How stable are Core decisions across challenger budgets and models?** Bounds are explicitly scope-specific. Next experiment: register a model-by-budget sweep against the same frozen outcome and information packet.
- **How sensitive is Evidence to neutral-policy and checkpoint choices?** Current effects and calibration are conditional on those choices. Next experiment: preregister multiple checkpoints and neutral policies, each with independent null calibration.
