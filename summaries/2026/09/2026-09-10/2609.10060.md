# Reference-Based Bias Detection in LLMs via Relative Representations of Hidden States

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10060) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10060)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors’ reference-relative hidden-state metric tracks output-level bias changes in most tested fine-tuning settings at lower evaluation cost, but remains a model-dependent proxy rather than evidence of safety (§§4–6).

## Problem and motivation

Output-based bias audits require benchmark prompts or judge models and may miss internal changes not apparent in sampled generations. The authors investigate whether changes in hidden-state associations can cheaply flag bias introduced by fine-tuning. Their identified gap is comparing these associations across models whose representation geometry has changed (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Reference-based metric:** Average final-layer token states into sentence embeddings, then represent each sentence by its cosine similarities to shared anchor sentences. Each model embeds the anchors independently; no cross-model mapping is fitted (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Representational Bias Shift, ΔB:** Use Euclidean distances in anchor-relative space to measure each target group’s association with positive versus negative sentences. Subtract the reference model’s association score from the audited model’s score; negative ΔB indicates movement toward negative attributes (§§3.3–3.4).
- **Controlled validation:** Compare ΔB with behavioral benchmarks across Llama, Mistral, and Gemma under full and LoRA fine-tuning, including interpolations between unharmful and harmful checkpoints. Test representation baselines and sensitivity to sentence construction, anchors, pooling, and training seeds (§§4.1–4.5).

The contribution combines existing relative representations and SEAT-style associations into comparative auditing; neither underlying technique is presented as newly invented. The authors report releasing auditing code and sentence sets, excluding harmful checkpoints (Ethical Considerations).

These results support an association between ΔB and benchmark-measured behavioral changes within the constructed checkpoint spectra. They do not establish causality, universal detection, or unbiased behavior when ΔB is small. ## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** English templates cover coarse, single-axis social groups. Other languages, intersectional identities, unnamed harms, larger models, and naturally occurring fine-tuning remain untested (Limitations).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** ΔB requires a meaningful reference and cannot certify either model as unbiased. Context, token position, and layer selection may affect reliability; optimizing ΔB need not improve outputs (Limitations; Ethical Considerations).
- **Unresolved architecture effects:** Gemma is weaker, particularly under LoRA. The authors suggest scale, tokenization, and final-layer geometry as explanations but do not isolate them (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—dependent observations:** Correlations pool groups and checkpoints derived from shared fine-tuned endpoints. The text does not describe dependence-aware significance testing or held-out checkpoint validation (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation ambiguity:** Llama’s WildGuardMix RR AUC is 0.89 in §4.2 but 0.964 in §4.5’s baseline comparison. The supplied text does not clearly reconcile the settings. WildGuardMix group assignment also uses ChatGPT 5.2 without reported mapping-validation results (Appendix B).

## Open research questions

- **Does ΔB generalize to ordinary adaptation?** Current shifts come from deliberately constructed harmful/unharmful training and merging. Next experiment: evaluate held-out domain and instruction fine-tunes using prespecified audit thresholds (Limitations; Appendix C).
- **What explains Gemma’s weaker signal?** Scale and architecture explanations remain uncontrolled. Next experiment: compare multiple sizes within families while sweeping layers and LoRA ranks (§4.2).
- **Can the method cover multilingual and intersectional harms?** Existing sentence sets exclude these cases. Next experiment: construct corresponding target and attribute sets and validate against matching behavioral evaluations (Limitations).
- **Can ΔB reliably provide an early warning?** Monitoring during training is proposed, not demonstrated. Next experiment: track ΔB and output bias throughout training and test whether ΔB predicts later deterioration (§5).
