# Programmable World Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-10.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10540) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10540)


## One-sentence takeaway
Programmable World Model separates executable world state from video rendering, achieving 94% Count Accuracy and 98% State Accuracy on CombatStateBench, although these metrics assess coarse visual agreement rather than correct entity-specific interactions (§5.2, Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify three gaps in interactive video world models: limited entity-level control, unreliable persistence of off-screen and nonvisual state, and insufficient support for executable interaction rules. Visually plausible responses alone cannot ensure that inventories, character deaths, or event conditions remain consistent over subsequent interactions (§1).

Their proposed solution makes a lightweight engine responsible for world facts and transitions, while a video model generates their visual realization.

## Approach and contributions
- **Programmable state and rendering interface.** A coding agent combines a detected initial 3D layout with natural-language instructions to define entities, attributes, relations, and executable rules. The engine advances this canonical state. State-augmented 3D oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) provide compact geometry; a deterministic compiler projects them into identity, semantic, and camera-relative motion maps (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Conditioned video renderer.** A trainable Structured Spatial ControlNet conditions a frozen LingBot-World-v1 backbone. Chunk-autoregressive generation uses temporal history and geometry-aligned spatial memory adopted from AlayaWorld. Fine geometry, articulation, and appearance remain the renderer’s responsibility (§4.4). The contribution is the integrated control framework; the backbone and spatial-memory mechanism are inherited.
- **Automatic supervision pipeline.** ViPE, Qwen3-VL, SAM3, and WildDet3D recover camera geometry, semantic categories, persistent tracks, and OBB trajectories from videos to construct training controls (§4.5).
- **CombatStateBench.** The authors introduce controlled combat sequences with synchronized engine states and rendering annotations to evaluate visible alive-character counts and death-state realization (§5.1). Public release is not established in the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results

All numerical results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher visible-count agreement | On 50 CombatStateBench clips, Count Accuracy is **94.00%**, versus **40.75%** for LingBot-World-V2 and **32.00%** for YUME. Exact count matching uses 400 sampled frames, eight per clip, judged by Qwen3.6-27B against engine counts. | §5.2, Eq. 17, Table 1 |
| Higher death-state realization | State Accuracy is **98.00%**, versus **8.00%** and **58.00%**, respectively. Evaluation covers 50 death events, with three post-transition frames per event; success requires a dead character in at least one frame. | §5.2, Eq. 18, Table 1 |
| Best reported video-quality scores | On the same 50 clips, PWM / LingBot-World-V2 / YUME score: Imaging Quality **67.62 / 67.46 / 64.10%**; Subject Consistency **94.74 / 81.87 / 92.35%**; Background Consistency **96.98 / 91.89 / 93.63%**; Temporal Stability **99.00 / 96.85 / 98.76%**. | §5.2, Table 1 |
| Qualitative breadth and extended generation | Selected examples show novel minotaur appearances, previously unseen entities revealed by camera rotation, racing, mixed humans and vehicles, and an **897-frame** autoregressive sequence. These are qualitative demonstrations without corresponding quantitative breakdowns. | §5.3, Fig. 5 |

The results support stronger coarse state-to-video agreement for the complete system under this benchmark. Baselines receive the same initial observations and transitions through native prompt switching, while PWM receives structured spatial controls (§5.2). Consequently, the comparison does not isolate the effects of the engine, representation, renderer adaptation, or memory. It also does not establish correct victim identity, precise interaction localization, or durable death-state persistence.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-explicit metric limits:** Count and State Accuracy are permissive global measures. State Accuracy does not identify which character died or where, and neither metric directly evaluates instance correspondence (§5.2).
- **Author-explicit representation boundary:** OBBs leave articulation, deformation, and secondary dynamics to the generative model; detailed geometric control is outside this representation’s scope (§2).
- **Analyst inference—limited causal attribution:** No component ablations or controlled representation comparisons are reported. The observed gains cannot establish which design choices are necessary (§5).
- **Analyst inference—restricted validation:** Quantitative evaluation covers verifier-filtered short combat scenarios. Other domains and long-horizon behavior receive qualitative demonstrations; reliability of arbitrary programmed rules, inventory logic, and multiplayer behavior is not quantitatively established (§§5.1–5.3).
- **Analyst inference—measurement and preprocessing uncertainty:** The text reports neither human validation of the VLM judge nor annotation-error sensitivity, uncertainty estimates, or repeated-run variability (§§4.5, 5.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Which components drive state fidelity?** No ablations separate explicit state, identity/semantic/direction controls, and memory. A next experiment could remove each component under a fixed backbone and training budget.
2. **Does the correct entity remain dead through occlusion and revisitation?** Current State Accuracy only checks for any dead character in sampled frames. Test identity-specific death persistence across controlled camera departures and returns, with human-validated annotations.
3. **How reliably does language become correct executable behavior?** The agent’s program correctness is not separately evaluated. Test generated programs against specified inventory, ownership, trigger, and objective transitions before evaluating their rendered consequences.
4. **How robust is the pipeline to imperfect geometry?** Training controls depend on estimated cameras, tracks, and OBBs, while benchmark sequences pass consistency checks. Perturb those annotations systematically and measure changes in state agreement and video quality.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and reported benchmark outcomes are clearly described; broader reliability remains less established.

Material missing information includes training-data volume and split details, optimization settings and compute, inference latency, coding-agent configuration and program validation, benchmark clip durations and verifier thresholds, component ablations, judge calibration, statistical uncertainty, and code/model/data release availability.
