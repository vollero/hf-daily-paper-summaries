# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-09-02

## Executive summary

- Persistent and long-horizon agents dominate the day: papers address GUI operation, year-long commerce, multi-day software development, lifecycle control, reusable evidence, protocol integrity, and memory safety.
- World-action models take several distinct forms. H3-World (`2609.01560`) binds textual actions to video intervals; ZimaBlue (`2609.00188`) couples video pre-training with robot control; Qwen-Drive (`2609.00111`) adds explicit perception and trajectory-planning modules.
- Several evaluations expose gaps hidden by headline success: drone proximity does not guarantee a valid completion declaration (`2609.01404`), nominal imitation success does not ensure speed robustness (`2609.01453`), and partial desktop progress rarely becomes exact completion (`2609.00028`).
- Persistent state is both an asset and an attack surface. Safin-1 (`2609.00092`) stores a detachable safety capability in recurrent state, while EAL-BENCH (`2609.01836`) shows ordinary memory updates can create false authorization that executors then propagate.
- Resource-aware training appears at architectural and supervision levels: SMELT (`2609.01343`) reports fitted compute savings under approximately matched budgets, while Switch Distillation (`2609.01532`) improves reasoning under a fixed token budget with a small factual-recall cost.
- Automated judges remain useful but conditional. AgentJudgeBench (`2608.26623`) finds difficulty-dependent alignment loss in tool workflows; DramaChain Bench (`2609.00646`) finds strong model-level ranking agreement but weaker item-level video judgment.
- Evidence organization can trade upfront work for repeated-query savings: Hi-Q (`2608.30468`) adaptively decomposes unresolved questions, EM²Mem (`2609.00551`) builds event-centered video memory, and agentic data cracking (`2608.31082`) incrementally structures opened documents.
- The collection also includes a conceptual persistent-agent architecture (`2608.30478`) and two system-dynamics papers (`2609.00137`, `2608.27509`); their claims should not be read as experimentally demonstrated agent gains or empirical forecasts.

## Main results worth noting

- **Persistent-memory causality:** In EAL-BENCH (`2609.01836`), naturally erroneous typed memories led to unauthorized actions in 205/208 trials, or 98.6%; replacing only the memory with oracle-exact state yielded 0/208. This is strong causal evidence within synthetic closed-world authorization tasks, not a prevalence estimate for deployments.

- **Temporal robustness:** In ParcelStow (`2609.01453`), ACT-A and the expert each achieved 100% success at nominal speed, but at the fastest demonstrated speed \(r=2\), ACT-A reached 53% versus the expert’s 84%; the paired 31-point gap had a 95% bootstrap interval of [18, 44].

- **Protocol versus physical competence:** In DroneCATS (`2609.01404`), Qwen3.5-9B came within 5 m in 90% of 20 approaching episodes but achieved only 35% declaration-based success. This supports a protocol-compliance diagnosis, though no intervention isolates termination handling.

- **Long-horizon GUI limitations:** UI-Venus-2-27B (`2609.00028`) reported strong web scores—93.4% on refreshed WebVoyager and 78.3% on Online-Mind2Web—but only 2.8% Binary Accuracy and 13.2% Partial Score on 108 OSWorld 2.0 tasks with a 150-step budget.

- **World-action scaling:** ZimaBlue (`2609.00188`) reports 36.1%→77.8% task-macro zero-shot success across cumulative data configurations on 12 held-out Franka tasks. The 60K→120K-hour change improved perturbed success from 35.0% to 57.5%, but data quantity and composition were not separated.

- **Driving evaluation:** Qwen-Drive (`2609.00111`) reached 90.7 NAVSIM v1.1 PDMS using one trajectory and 91.4 with oracle best-of-six selection. In 916 AlpaSim scenarios, RL reduced off-road rate from 24% to 12% but lowered progress from 54% to 48% and raised all-event close encounters from 38% to 41%.

- **Compute-matched recurrence:** SMELT (`2609.01343`) reports fitted CE Gains of 6.8–10.0% at \(10^{20}\) FLOPs and 14.7–18.0% at \(10^{21}\), across three sparsity levels. These are fitted compute savings within the measured regime, not demonstrated wall-clock savings.

- **Token-budgeted distillation:** Under 60.4B mid-training tokens, Switch Distillation (`2609.01532`) scored 44.7% reasoning and 29.3% factual recall with a 7B teacher, versus 26.1% and 30.3% for NTP. The finding is a favorable reasoning–recall trade-off, not a strict improvement on both metrics.

- **Localized RL credit:** CE-GRPO (`2608.30457`) averaged 76.04% across nine geometry benchmarks, compared with 72.61% for trajectory-level GRPO. Its margin increased with benchmark-level event count, but that relationship is correlational and based on nine benchmarks.

- **Selective adaptation:** CASTER (`2609.00374`) changed the average effect of affine transport across 307 admissible cells from −3.35 to +1.69 top-1 points using its shipped gate; every observed transport losing more than 10 points was rejected. The certificate did not reliably rank benefit and performed poorly when transferred to Tent.

- **Evidence retrieval:** Hi-Q (`2608.30468`) averaged 52.3 EM/64.0 F1 across three full-corpus 1,000-question subsets versus IRCoT’s 37.2/45.8. On 279 triggered, annotation-clean MuSiQue cases, all-gold evidence coverage rose from 7.9% at root retrieval to 57.7% across all leaves.

- **Reuse economics:** EM²Mem (`2609.00551`) reduced EgoLifeQA inference tokens by 63.66% and average latency from 459.00 to 98.21 seconds per query, but including construction it used 104.50M tokens versus 102.58M for the comparator over 500 queries; estimated token break-even was about 536 queries. Agentic data cracking (`2608.31082`) reduced warmed FanOutQA mean cost from $0.26 to $0.12, while judged accuracy was statistically indistinguishable at 42% versus 43%, \(p=0.39\).

- **Judge reliability:** AgentJudgeBench (`2608.26623`) found all 30 generator–judge pairs degraded from easy to hard, with hard reference-free alignment clustering around 77–82%. DramaChain Bench (`2609.00646`) found mean model-level PLCC/SRCC of 0.918/0.881, but video-stage per-item agreement remained only 0.79–0.85× its human baseline.

- **Realistic software requests:** RealSWE (`2608.27831`) reports a 6.4-point average resolution drop across seven models when detailed benchmark descriptions were replaced by information-sparse variants. Removing Desired Behavior from bug-fix requests caused an average 8.0-point loss across four models, while style-only contrasts were not significant.

- **Continual software development:** HoH@3 (`2609.01481`) improved every reported model–benchmark aggregate over one-pass Vanilla; on GameCraft with Codex/GPT-5.5, Overall rose from 49.58 to 71.52. Single-run conditions and per-task regressions preclude a claim of uniformly monotonic improvement.

- **Theory results:** Recursive Criticality (`2609.00137`) derives a local transition at \(R_{\mathrm{AI}}=1\) under its assumptions. Democratic Dominance (`2608.27509`) derives equilibrium and oscillation conditions for a political-support model. Neither paper empirically estimates its central parameters or validates its scenario trajectories as forecasts.

## Open research directions

1. **Repair agent protocols independently of task competence** — motivated by `2609.01404`, `2609.00621`, and `2609.00028`. Compare prompt-only actions with typed constrained decoding and a separately trained termination head while holding navigation or GUI policy fixed; report semantic success, protocol validity, false completion, and latency.

2. **Evaluate persistent-state safety under realistic memory systems** — motivated by `2609.00092`, `2609.01836`, and `2608.30478`. Introduce retrieval, concurrent writers, revocations, state tampering, and rollback into a benchmark with immutable authorization ground truth; measure both unauthorized action and legitimate-use retention.

3. **Separate video scale from video composition in robot transfer** — motivated by `2609.00188`. Train equal-hour mixtures that vary embodiment relevance, interaction density, source diversity, and curation, then evaluate multiple genuinely unseen physical robots with repeated trials.

4. **Test temporal robustness beyond one manipulation setup** — motivated by `2609.01453`. Run matched expert–learner speed curves across several tasks, policies, chunk lengths, replanning rates, sensing modalities, and hardware platforms; require nominal-performance matching before comparing robustness.

5. **Measure real efficiency rather than arithmetic proxies alone** — motivated by `2609.01343`, `2609.01532`, `2609.00551`, and `2608.31082`. Report wall-clock time, energy, accelerator utilization, memory traffic, teacher cost, construction cost, and break-even curves under cold, warm, and streaming workloads.

6. **Determine when retrieval gains improve final answers** — motivated by `2608.30468`, `2609.00551`, and `2608.31082`. Compare binary versus graded evidence controllers over text and event-centered video memories, with preregistered retrieval coverage, calibrated abstention, answer accuracy, and total cost.

7. **Audit automated judges at item level before using them as rewards** — motivated by `2608.26623`, `2609.00646`, and `2608.28612`. Use multi-annotator references, equivalent-tool and partial-order cases, multiple judge families, and controlled agent-training runs to test whether judge compression or anchoring changes learned behavior.

8. **Factorially isolate structured-credit mechanisms** — motivated by `2608.30457` and `2609.00768`. Hold compute and training data fixed while varying typed events, shared-prefix branching, error-memory diagnosis, confidence filtering, and ordinary trajectory groups; audit whether selected states are genuinely causal.

9. **Make continual coding gains statistically and operationally credible** — motivated by `2609.01481` and `2608.27831`. Repeat runs with fixed model/harness combinations, vary request sparsity, permit bounded clarification, and track hidden-test gains, regressions, stopping decisions, token cost, and issue reopening over longer horizons.

10. **Empirically identify—or reject—feedback-dynamics models** — motivated by `2609.00137` and `2608.27509`. Fit the proposed models and simpler delay-free or autoregressive baselines to named longitudinal data, use out-of-sample prediction, and report parameter identifiability and sensitivity rather than illustrative trajectories alone.

## Coverage and uncertainty

All 30 supplied current papers were analyzed and appear in the taxonomy or singleton note and in “Papers at a glance.” Taxonomy, same-day connections, and historical-connection stages were available, but their assignments were treated as editorial proposals and retained only where supported by the corresponding analyses.

The collection process reported unavailable Hugging Face Markdown for 23 current IDs: `2609.01591`, `2609.00111`, `2609.01343`, `2609.00028`, `2609.00188`, `2608.30468`, `2609.01404`, `2609.01607`, `2609.00092`, `2609.00768`, `2609.01481`, `2609.00551`, `2608.30478`, `2609.00621`, `2608.30730`, `2608.30457`, `2609.01836`, `2609.00968`, `2609.00374`, `2609.00137`, `2609.01532`, and `2609.01453`. Full or partial analyses were nevertheless supplied for each, so none was dropped.

Explicitly truncated current analyses include StudentSim (`2609.01591`, 11,450 omitted characters), the corporate request-mix paper (`2609.01572`, 15,650 omitted), and E-Commerce Bench (`2608.30730`, 65,034 omitted). Several other analyses note absent rendered table cells or incomplete implementation/release details, especially `2608.26623`, `2608.28612`, and `2608.27831`.

Cross-day edges were checked against cached analyses for both endpoints. No retained historical edge relies only on a listing summary. Historical records are service-truncated, so even supported cross-day links should be treated as medium-to-high-confidence topical comparisons, not claims of citation, influence, priority, or chronological dependence.

The lowest-confidence conclusions concern:

- qualitative or example-based generalization in H3-World (`2609.01560`);
- causal attribution among changing data mixtures in ZimaBlue (`2609.00188`);
- conceptual effectiveness of Pera (`2608.30478`);
- population-level human preference for `2608.28612`;
- real-world validity of the two system-dynamics papers (`2609.00137`, `2608.27509`);
- adjacent rankings based on small cells, single runs, absent training variance, or incompatible protocols.
