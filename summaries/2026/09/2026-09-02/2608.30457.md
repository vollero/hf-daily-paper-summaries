# Learning Where Outcomes Change:Credit-Addressable Reasoning for Multimodal Geometry

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30457) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30457)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors combine executable, event-structured geometry reasoning with shared-prefix reinforcement learning, reporting 76.04% mean accuracy across nine benchmarks—3.43 points above trajectory-level GRPO—with larger gains on longer reasoning chains.

## Problem and motivation

Multimodal geometry requires models to extract diagram relations accurately and preserve them through multi-step deductions. The authors identify two linked gaps: free-form traces hide decisive visual and geometric choices, while trajectory-level reinforcement learning assigns one terminal signal across the whole response and cannot isolate which choice changed the outcome (Introduction, pp. 1–2; Fig. 1).

A six-model MathVerse study motivates executable code as a reasoning representation: diagram-plus-external-code input outperformed diagram-only and code-only input for every tested model. However, self-generated code lagged externally generated code by 11.7–16.6 points for the three open models, making code-generation reliability a key bottleneck (Section 3; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Credit-addressable reasoning:** The paper proposes aligning inference-time semantic units with the units at which alternatives are compared and learning credit is assigned (Sections 1 and 7).

- **Code-CoT:** A VLM retains the original diagram while generating line-addressable executable Matplotlib code, a plan, and typed `think`, `reference`, `auxiliary`, and `coordinate` events. Protocol checkers validate structure, execution, references, and actions (Sections 4.1–4.3; Appendix A.1).

- **CE-GRPO:** Structural priors and type-normalized entropy select an event boundary. The method fixes the image, question, and pre-event prefix, samples complete alternative continuations, and calculates group-relative advantages from their terminal rewards. The shared prefix is excluded from the policy loss, localizing updates to the regenerated event and its consequences (Section 4.4; Fig. 3).

- **Training without process labels:** Ordinary trajectory groups and shared-prefix groups are mixed 1:1 under the same programmatic reward and clipped GRPO objective; no learned value model, process annotations, external geometry solver, or test-time branching is used (Sections 4.3–4.4; Appendix A.3–A.5).

These results establish that the complete Code-CoT/CE-GRPO system performed better than the reported in-house baselines across this geometry suite, and that structured selection and code training correlate with better validity, perception fidelity, and long-chain performance. They do not establish that localized credit alone causes every gain: Code-CoT representation, SFT data, mixed ordinary/shared-prefix training, reward shaping, and selector design operate together. The event-count result is a nine-benchmark correlation rather than a controlled manipulation of reasoning length.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated ambiguity:** The external-code versus self-generated-code study does not separate visual perception errors from errors translating correct perceptions into code (Section 3).

- **Selector inefficiency:** A selected event yields no learning signal when all sampled continuations receive the same reward; the authors characterize such selection as computationally wasteful (Section 4.4).

- **Protocol dependence:** Deterministic event boundaries and localized branching rely on traces conforming to the bespoke Code-CoT format, including at least two valid action events (Appendix A.1).

- **Analyst inference — causal attribution:** The selector ablation supports the combined selector, but the supplied text does not report a full factorial ablation separating event representation, shared-prefix branching, mixed ordinary RL, action-validity reward, and data construction.

- **Analyst inference — evaluation scope:** All nine benchmarks concern geometry, so the conclusion that the framework is broadly applicable to other structured reasoning tasks is proposed rather than empirically demonstrated (Sections 5.1 and 7).

- **Analyst inference — judge validity:** Main answer assessment uses two proprietary model judges, and the perception-fidelity study also depends on proprietary extraction and judging. Repeated assessment until agreement does not provide human-validated judge error rates (Appendix B.1; Section 6).

- **Analyst inference — compute comparability:** CE-GRPO training used 64×A100-80GB, whereas reported trainable baselines used eight accelerators, and trajectory-level GRPO used eight rather than four rollouts per prompt. The text does not provide compute-normalized comparisons (Table 12).

- **Analyst inference — statistical coverage:** Significance is reported for the event-count analysis, but confidence intervals or multi-seed variability are not supplied for the main accuracy results.

## Open research questions

1. **Does event-localized credit generalize beyond geometry?** Only geometry and multimodal-math benchmarks were evaluated. A next experiment could apply the same typed-event/shared-prefix design to another task with explicit intermediate operations and compare it with trajectory-level GRPO.

2. **Which component produces the improvement?** Representation, reward shaping, event selection, branching, and mixed training are intertwined. A factorial ablation could independently remove typed events, shared-prefix groups, ordinary groups, and action-validity rewards while holding data and compute fixed.

3. **How sensitive is CE-GRPO to prefix freshness and branching budget?** The 3,270 prefixes are collected once from the initial SFT model, and training uses four rollouts per prompt. Experiments could periodically refresh prefixes and vary candidates and rollout counts while measuring accuracy and tokens per critical event.

4. **Can code errors be diagnosed more precisely?** The authors’ controlled study cannot distinguish perception failure from code-translation failure. Human-annotated diagram facts and gold executable programs could separately evaluate visual extraction, code transcription, execution, and downstream reasoning.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s described method and reported results because the supplied text includes the main paper and methodological appendices.

Material missing information includes multi-seed variance for headline results, confidence intervals for benchmark accuracy, human validation of model-based judging, compute-normalized comparisons, training time and total token cost, broader non-geometry evaluation, and confirmed release contents or licensing for code, data, prefixes, and checkpoints.
