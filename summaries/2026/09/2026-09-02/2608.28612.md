# InternReviewer &amp; InternAdvocate: Objective Reward and Evaluation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning in Peer Review and Rebuttal

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28612) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28612)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors train retrieval-enabled 30B agents for peer review and rebuttal using rule-based citation, format, tool-use, and reference-alignment rewards, reporting much stronger grounding than the base model but mixed human-preference results against GPT-5.2.

## Problem and motivation

Automated scholarly reviewing must combine technical reasoning with verifiable evidence, yet ordinary LLMs may fabricate references or unsupported flaws. The authors argue that existing approaches are limited either by training-free reliance on proprietary models, SFT that imitates noisy human reviews without learning tool use, or RL rewards based on unstable subjective LLM judges. Their target gap is an objective, scalable training and evaluation framework for evidence-grounded peer reviews and author rebuttals (Sections 1–2; Appendix B).

## Approach and contributions

- **Specialized agents:** InternReviewer generates structured reviews from anonymized manuscripts; InternAdvocate produces point-by-point rebuttals from manuscripts and initial critiques (Sections 3.2–3.3).
- **Large scholarly-interaction corpus:** The authors collect 184,857 review–rebuttal pairs and manuscripts from ICLR, NeurIPS, and ICML, then use 72K training and 1K held-out instances after downsampling (Section 3.2; Section 4.1).
- **Retrieval-integrated agentic RL:** Starting from Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 without an SFT cold start, the agents learn interleaved search and generation using GSPO (Sections 3.1, 3.3.4; Appendix A.1).
- **Objective reward:** The decomposed reward combines POLAR-7B reference alignment, structural compliance, bounded tool use, and citation verification against actual retrieval logs (Section 3.3.3, Equations 1–8).
- **Time-aware local search:** A 593,092-paper arXiv index supplies hierarchically summarized evidence under role-specific temporal cutoffs (Section 3.3.2).
- **Evaluation protocol:** The work combines lexical and semantic similarity, structural/tool-use behavior, citation grounding, and human pairwise preference while documenting instability in LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation (Sections 4.2, 4.5; Appendix B).

These results support the claim that the proposed training substantially improves compliance, prescribed tool behavior, citation grounding, and human preference relative to its Qwen backbone in this benchmark. They do not establish superiority to every frontier model: GPT-5.2 wins the review human-preference comparison and both Reranker evaluations. Nor do they establish review correctness, downstream editorial benefit, or generalization beyond the sampled computer-science venues.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Long, mathematical, or figure-heavy papers stress MinerU parsing and the model context window; evidence retrieval is restricted to arXiv; occasional fabricated or misattributed citations remain; broader multi-agent reviewer–author interaction is not tested (Section 5).
- **Author-stated:** References and appendices are removed from manuscript inputs, potentially excluding evidence needed to assess novelty, derivations, or supplementary experiments (Section 3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** POLAR is both a training reward and a principal evaluation metric, so leading POLAR results are not fully independent evidence of general quality. The distinct Reranker metric partially mitigates this concern but ranks GPT-5.2 first (Sections 3.3.3, 4.2–4.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Tool and format scores largely encode compliance with hand-designed target ranges, not whether retrieval was necessary or whether the resulting critique was correct (Equations 3–4).
- **Analyst inference:** The text provides no reward-component ablation, so improvements cannot be attributed separately to POLAR, retrieval constraints, citation penalties, or GSPO.
- **Analyst inference:** Human-study sample size, annotator expertise, agreement, statistical uncertainty, and exact protocol details are missing. Win rates such as 0.85 therefore cannot be interpreted reliably as population-level superiority.
- **Analyst inference:** The conclusion’s claim that the agents surpass GPT-5 under human preference conflicts with the reported review result, where GPT-5.2 scores 0.90 and InternReviewer 0.85 (Section 4.5 versus Section 5).
- **Analyst inference:** The held-out split is described as random, leaving temporal leakage and near-duplicate manuscript/discussion contamination unclear, despite time-aware retrieval safeguards (Section 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which reward components drive substantive quality rather than compliance?** No component ablation is reported. Train matched variants removing POLAR, tool, format, and citation rewards individually, then evaluate with blinded experts and an independent factuality audit.
2. **Does the framework generalize across time, venues, and disciplines?** Evaluation is drawn from ICLR, NeurIPS, and ICML data with a random split. Test on future conference cycles and non-CS venues using strictly temporal, venue-disjoint splits.
3. **Are retrieved citations merely valid, or do they genuinely support each claim?** Log matching verifies that a cited record was retrieved, but the supplied text does not establish entailment between evidence and assertions. Add claim-level expert annotation or retrieval-evidence entailment evaluation.
4. **Does automated feedback improve real papers and decisions?** Preference scores do not measure author usefulness or editorial correctness. Run a prospective study measuring revision quality, reviewer workload, decision calibration, and identified-error validity.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text gives detailed methods and selected numerical findings, but the actual cells of Tables 1–6 are absent. Material missing information includes complete metric values, human-study sample size and annotator qualifications, uncertainty or significance tests, dataset split and deduplication details, reward ablations, compute cost, model/checkpoint and dataset release status, error-category analysis, and independent verification of citation support rather than retrieval-log presence.
