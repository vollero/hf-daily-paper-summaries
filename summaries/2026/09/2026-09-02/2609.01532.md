# Knowledge Distillation During Mid-Training Favors Reasoning over Factual Recall

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01532) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01532)


## One-sentence takeaway

Standard knowledge distillation improves reasoning but slows factual acquisition during language-model mid-training, while the proposed entropy-routed Switch Distillation largely preserves recall and retains substantial reasoning gains after post-training.

## Problem and motivation

Mid-training continues self-supervised learning on a relatively small, curated corpus, making efficient use of each token important. Although logit-based knowledge distillation is established in pre- and post-training, its behavior during mid-training was unclear.

The authors identify a stage-specific gap: standard forward- or reverse-KL distillation can improve reasoning while reducing factual recall relative to next-token prediction (NTP), unlike moderate forward-KL distillation during pre-training. They call this the reasoning–recall tradeoff (§1, Figure 2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Controlled stage comparison.** The authors compare NTP, forward-KL distillation, and reverse-KL distillation across pre-training, mid-training, and subsequent post-training, varying teacher size and distillation strength (§2.2, §3).

2. **Mechanistic account.** They connect the tradeoff to three observations: teachers are less confident on knowledge-intensive tokens; students learn lower-entropy facts earlier; and KL objectives weaken the gold-token learning signal as teacher entropy rises (§4, Figures 3–5).

3. **Switch Distillation.** At every batch, the method routes the lowest-entropy \(q\%\) of tokens to reverse-KL distillation and the remainder to cross-entropy. The default is \(q=20\%\); KD and CE losses are normalized separately (§5, Equation 6; Algorithm 1; Appendix B.2).

4. **Empirical validation.** Switch Distillation is compared with NTP, standard forward/reverse KD, and token-routing KD using 7B and 13B teachers, including evaluation after a four-stage post-training pipeline (§6, Tables 1–3).

The experiments support the authors’ claim that uniform KD can interact poorly with an already knowledgeable student and that teacher entropy is a useful routing signal in the tested settings. They do not establish that entropy is the causal mechanism in every model family, that Switch Distillation is optimal among learned or richer routing methods, or that the gains extend to large student models or substantially different mid-training recipes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Controlled experiments center on the OLMo-2 recipe and relatively small 1B students. Broader validation is constrained by the scarcity of model families releasing intermediate checkpoints, data mixtures, and complete recipes (Appendix A.1).

- **Author-stated:** The effects of student scale, teacher–student capacity gap, and other experimental choices are not exhaustively ablated because of compute cost (Appendix A.1).

- **Author-stated:** Switch Distillation uses a simple fixed entropy router; learned or more expressive routing strategies might identify useful teacher supervision better (Appendix A.1).

- **Analyst inference:** Generalization beyond OLMo-2 has limited direct outcome evidence: the supplied text reports one smaller SmolLM experiment, while broader cross-family analyses test entropy asymmetry rather than full Switch Distillation training (Appendices D.1–D.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The main comparison fixes FKD and RKD at \(\alpha=0.5\) and tunes the Switch Distillation routing threshold over only 10%, 20%, and 30%; this does not exhaust the competing methods’ hyperparameter spaces (§6.1, Appendix B.2).

- **Analyst inference:** Significance markers are reported per task against the strongest competing baseline, but confidence intervals or significance tests for the headline macro-average improvements are not provided in the supplied text (Tables 1–2).

- **Analyst inference:** Online KD still requires teacher forward passes. The claim of negligible added computation applies to entropy routing beyond standard KD, not to KD relative to NTP (§5, Appendix C.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the tradeoff persist for larger students?** The principal students are 1B parameters. A next experiment could repeat the token-matched stage comparison with several larger students while controlling the teacher–student capacity ratio.

2. **Can a learned router outperform predictive entropy?** The paper tests several fixed alternatives but no learned policy. A next experiment could train a lightweight router to predict per-token KD benefit and compare it with entropy routing under equal compute.

3. **When exactly does KD stop Pareto-improving recall?** The authors hypothesize a transition during late pre-training but evaluate only selected stage endpoints. A checkpoint-dense experiment could measure reasoning and recall frontiers throughout late pre-training and mid-training.

4. **How robust are gains across corpora and domain mixtures?** Full Switch Distillation results cover OLMo-2/Dolmino and one SmolLM setup. A factorial experiment varying the procedural-to-knowledge-intensive mixture could test whether entropy routing remains beneficial as corpus composition changes.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high.** The supplied text includes the main paper, limitations, experimental details, ablations, and supplementary analyses.
