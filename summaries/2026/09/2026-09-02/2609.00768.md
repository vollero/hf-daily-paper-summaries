# DiagEvo: Diagnosis-Guided Self-Evolution via Hierarchical Error Memory

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00768) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00768)


## One-sentence takeaway

DiagEvo turns recurring failures from label-free LLM self-play into a structured curriculum, improving nine-benchmark mean accuracy across three tested solvers while revealing that gains peak after five rounds rather than continuing indefinitely.

## Problem and motivation

Self-play can improve language-model reasoning without continued human labeling, but generated questions may drift away from the solver’s competence boundary: they can become longer, unclear, too easy, or too difficult, while unreliable majority-vote pseudo-labels reinforce errors. Existing label-free methods estimate difficulty or diversity but do not identify why failures recur; guided alternatives depend on external examples, documents, difficulty labels, or teachers. The authors seek an endogenous curriculum signal derived solely from the solver’s self-play history (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Hierarchical error-cause memory:** A frozen LLM diagnostician compares a response agreeing with the solver’s majority pseudo-label against one disagreeing with it, extracts their earliest transferable reasoning divergence, deduplicates causes, and organizes them under skill nodes (Section 3.3; Figure 2).

- **Dynamic curriculum scheduling:** Causes are marked `Active` or `Mastered`. Active causes are sampled according to their current-episode failure frequency, while a frequency-dependent schedule mixes targeted generation with free exploration. Mastered siblings may be combined with Active causes through cross-state stitching (Section 3.1, Equation 1).

- **State maintenance:** High mean self-consistency on targeted questions promotes a cause to Mastered; newly attributed failures reactivate it and start a fresh frequency episode (Section 3.3; Appendix D, Equation 9).

- **Double-confidence filtering:** Solver training retains intermediate-consistency questions only when the leading answer also has a sufficiently clear vote advantage over the runner-up, reducing unstable pseudo-labels (Section 3.2, Equation 2).

- **Empirical evaluation:** The method is tested with three solver models, three diagnostician sizes, nine reasoning benchmarks, component ablations, seven-round dynamics, and runtime measurements (Section 4; Appendices E–H).

These results support the authors’ claim that failure-guided curricula, memory-state management, and confidence filtering each improve this particular self-play setup. Three-run means and standard deviations are reported for individual DiagEvo benchmark scores, but not for aggregate columns or baselines (Table 1). The evidence does not establish ground-truth correctness of the training pseudo-labels, superiority outside the tested models and benchmarks, or sustained improvement beyond the selected round-5 checkpoint. Some baseline scores were imported from DARC, although the authors report close reproduction checks for base models and R-Zero (Appendix F, Table 7).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Confidence filters measure agreement, not correctness; a consistently shared error may still pass both constraints (Appendix A).

- **Author-stated:** The training horizon is fixed in advance, and performance declines after its round-5 peak, motivating an adaptive stopping rule (Appendix A; Section 4.4).

- **Author-stated:** Curriculum construction is tested only on mathematical questions. General benchmarks assess transfer, not direct self-evolution in other domains or long, multi-turn interactions (Appendix A).

- **Analyst inference:** Promotion to `Mastered` is based on self-consistency rather than verified accuracy, so confidently wrong behavior could alter both state transitions and subsequent sampling (Appendix D).

- **Analyst inference:** The oracle-agreement analysis is not fully independent of model-based judgment: GPT-5 majority voting supplies oracle answers, and human inspection covers 50 sampled questions per round rather than the full set (Appendix H).

- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons mix locally reproduced scores with values taken from DARC. Aggregate reproduction differences are small, but this is weaker than rerunning every baseline under identical infrastructure (Section 4.1; Appendix F).

- **Analyst inference:** Diagnostician decisions—extraction, deduplication, node assignment, and merging—receive no reported direct accuracy evaluation. Scale comparisons show downstream robustness, but do not quantify memory-label quality (Section 3.3; Appendix J).

- **Analyst inference:** Resource evidence is limited to wall-clock shares on eight H200 GPUs; total elapsed time, token consumption, and memory footprint are not reported in the supplied text (Appendix E).

## Open research questions

1. **Can cause mastery be verified rather than inferred from self-consistency?** Shared errors can be confidently repeated. A next experiment could compare current promotion against verifier-, execution-, or adversarial-consistency-based promotion and measure both state accuracy and downstream performance.

2. **Can the training horizon be selected online?** Performance peaks at round 5 and then declines. A next experiment could stop or pause training based on changes in Active causes, reactivation rates, and held-out self-consistency, compared against fixed-round schedules.

3. **Does diagnosis-guided self-play work beyond mathematics?** The paper only constructs mathematical curricula. A next experiment could apply the same loop to multi-turn tool use or another domain with long trajectories and evaluate direct in-domain learning rather than transfer.

4. **How sensitive is the method to diagnostic errors?** The paper varies diagnostician scale but does not directly audit extracted causes or hierarchy assignments. A next experiment could human-label a stratified sample of extraction, deduplication, routing, and state-transition decisions and correlate their correctness with training gains.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method, reported comparisons, and stated limitations because the supplied text includes the main paper and detailed appendices.

Materially unavailable or unestablished information includes public code or checkpoint availability, total training time and token/compute cost, solver-training-set retention rates, full aggregate uncertainty and baseline variance, direct human evaluation of diagnostic-memory quality, and evaluation of curricula constructed outside mathematics.
