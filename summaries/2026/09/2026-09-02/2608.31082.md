# Token-Efficient Data Reasoning Agents via Adaptive Structuring of Unstructured Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31082) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31082)


## One-sentence takeaway

Agentic data cracking incrementally extracts reusable, evidence-backed structure from documents already opened by an LLM agent, reducing mean FanOutQA cost by 53% with no statistically significant change in judged accuracy.

## Problem and motivation

Complex questions over unstructured corpora require agents to open many documents, extract scattered facts, and assemble intermediate structures. This produces strong answers but repeatedly incurs large prefill costs—up to one million tokens and nearly $1 for a FanOutQA question in the authors’ experiments (Sections 1–2).

The authors identify a gap between expensive document-based reasoning and cheap querying over structured data: an ideal manually structured store was 28× cheaper on a ten-question FanOutQA sample (Figure 1). Structuring an entire corpus beforehand is impractical because useful entities, attributes, and relations are not known until queries arrive, and exhaustive extraction could require unbounded decoding.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Agentic data cracking:** Whenever the answer agent must open a document, a parallel cracking sub-agent reuses the loaded context and extracts additional grounded structure that may benefit related future queries. Documents are never opened solely for cracking (Section 3; Figure 2).

2. **Reusable cracked-object representation:** Extracted facts are stored as extended RDF-style edges containing subject, relation, object, cardinality, optional unit, and source evidence. A catalogue exposes available subject–relation pairs, while raw-document access remains the fallback on misses (Section 3).

3. **Adaptive and speculative extraction:** Queries determine which documents and semantic neighborhoods to structure, but extraction extends beyond the current requested fact under a fixed decode budget (Section 3).

4. **Cross-query evaluation:** The authors augment FanOutQA with one human-verified related predecessor question per test question and add a longer Hitchcock-centered investigation to measure savings from warmed structured stores (Section 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Mean prefill fell from 189K to 87K tokens | FanOutQA, Claude-Haiku-4.5, one related predecessor question per test question | Section 4, Figure 3(a) |
| Mean cost fell from $0.26 to $0.12 (53%) | Same FanOutQA setting; cracking overhead included | Section 4, Figures 1 and 3(b) |
| Judged accuracy was statistically indistinguishable | LLM-as-judge: 42% with cracking versus 43% baseline, \(p=0.39\); word-match accuracy fell by two points | Section 4, Figure 3(c) |
| Median cost fell from $0.246 to $0.072 | FanOutQA; independently sorted distributions; 3.4× ratio of medians | Section 4, Figure 3(d) |
| Savings varied substantially by question | Paired ratios: 9× cheaper at the 10th percentile, but 1.24× baseline cost at the 90th percentile; cracking saved cost on three quarters of questions | Section 4, Figure 3(d) |
| Mean case-study prefill fell from 565K to 161K tokens | Hitchcock investigation with 20 preceding questions and 10 test questions | Section 4, Figure 3(a) |
| Mean case-study cost fell from $0.81 to $0.27 | Same Hitchcock setting; test questions had two-to-four times FanOutQA’s fan-out | Section 4, Figure 3(b) |
| Cracking added 12% overhead and produced about 150 objects | FanOutQA, 4K-token cracking decode budget per question across forks | Section 4 |
| Ideal structure was 28× cheaper | Ten sampled FanOutQA questions, manually extracted required facts, agent queried the resulting database | Sections 1 and 4, Figure 1(right) |

The results establish substantial cost and token reductions in two workloads when related earlier questions warm the cracked-object store. FanOutQA judged accuracy did not differ significantly under the reported test, though word-match accuracy declined slightly.

They do not establish equivalent savings for cold-start, unrelated, private-enterprise, dynamic, or very long-running workloads. The 28× result measures an ideal manually constructed store on only ten sampled questions, not the achieved end-to-end system.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The system assumes a static corpus; edits would require invalidating or incrementally refreshing affected objects (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** Its constrained reads do not support the full SQL space, including direct joins, aggregation, counting, and multi-hop queries (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** FanOutQA naturally lacks the cross-query locality the method targets, so the authors synthetically add related predecessor questions (Sections 4 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** Evaluation uses public Wikipedia content that may be present in model training; private, organization-specific documents were not tested (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** A fixed cracking budget sometimes wastes work: at the reported 90th percentile, cracking costs 1.24× the baseline. Smarter allocation remains future work (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization is uncertain because the main experiments use one model, Claude-Haiku-4.5, and the text reports no cross-model evaluation.
- **Analyst inference:** The longer-workload evidence is narrow: the case study contains ten evaluated questions centered on one cinema topic.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text gives no ablation isolating catalogue lookup, speculative extraction, normalization, cardinality handling, or KV-cache reuse.
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is limited because code availability, full prompts, generated-question data, human-verification protocol, and repeated-run variance are not provided in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **How do savings evolve over genuinely long workloads?** The evaluation provides only one predecessor per FanOutQA item and 20 warm-up questions in the case study. A next experiment could replay months of timestamped queries and plot cumulative extraction cost, coverage, accuracy, and savings.

2. **How robust is cracking under document updates?** The current implementation assumes a static corpus. A next experiment could introduce controlled revisions and compare full invalidation, provenance-based invalidation, and incremental re-cracking.

3. **Can cracking budgets be allocated more selectively?** Fixed 4K-token budgets create overhead on queries with little reuse. A next experiment could compare the fixed policy against document- or query-specific predictors of future reuse.

4. **Can richer structured operations improve efficiency without harming reliability?** The interface omits joins and aggregation. A next experiment could add progressively broader query operators and measure correctness, fallback frequency, cost, and unsupported-query failures.
