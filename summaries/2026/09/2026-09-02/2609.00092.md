# Safin-1: Safety from Within through Memory-Native State Evolution

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00092) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00092)


## One-sentence takeaway

Safin-1 turns checkpointed recurrent states into routable long-term memory and adds a detachable, persistent Safety State that improves average jailbreak resistance over a frozen backbone with less over-refusal than matched LoRA, though evidence is limited to two Qwen3.5 configurations and English safety tests.

## Problem and motivation

Recurrent language models compress history into a fixed-size current state, so earlier information may become irrecoverable after attenuation or overwriting; retaining multiple states then creates an efficient addressing problem. Separately, safety is usually encoded through backbone updates, adapters, prefixes, or inference-time interventions rather than the model’s native memory computation. The authors ask whether a unified state interface can provide both selective historical recall and persistent, context-sensitive safety specialization (Sections 1–2, pp. 3–7).

## Approach and contributions

- **MARCH state routing:** Periodically checkpoint cumulative recurrent states as “anchors,” derive content-aware routing keys from them, and let each token retrieve relevant anchors or choose a learned null candidate. Retrieved states are added to the unchanged current-state path (Sections 2.1–2.2; Figure 2).
- **Persistent capability states:** Place learned, sequence-independent states in the same candidate bank as contextual anchors. The first implementation, Safety State, is optimized while the language-model backbone remains frozen and can subsequently be attached or removed (Section 2.3).
- **Efficient implementation:** A fused producer–reader avoids materializing dense routing and per-candidate readout tensors; Top-4 routing limits costly matrix-valued reads (Section 2.4).
- **Multi-scale validation:** The authors test MARCH in controlled 0.8B pretraining across three recurrent update rules, then train Safin-1 variants at 4B and 35B-A3B and compare Safety State against training-matched rank-8 LoRA (Sections 3–4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| MARCH improves controlled long-context performance | At 0.8B after matched 50B-token pretraining, GDN+MARCH raises average NIAH from **31.58 to 46.43**, LongBench from **11.9 to 14.9**, and real-world retrieval from **20.5** for the stronger GDN baseline to **23.3**. | Tables 2–4, pp. 10–11 |
| Benefits extend across recurrent backbones | MARCH improves NIAH in **11/12** backbone–length settings across GDN, KDA, and GDN2; their NIAH averages change from **31.58→46.43**, **31.64→41.38**, and **40.52→44.80**, respectively. | Section 3.3; Table 4 |
| Scaled Safin-1 improves aggregate capability | Across ten capability benchmarks, the macro-average rises from **66.79 to 69.20** at 4B and **76.25 to 78.35** at 35B-A3B; AIME 2025 Avg@64 rises **55.57→63.59** and **71.88→80.10**. Models receive matched 50B-token CPT and 30B-token SFT. | Section 1, p. 4; Table 8 |
| Safety State reduces aggregate jailbreak success | On five jailbreak benchmarks, average ASR falls **6.65%→3.84%** at 4B and **5.89%→2.81%** at 35B-A3B—reported as relative reductions of **42.3%** and **52.3%**. | Table 9; Section 6 conclusion |
| Safety State outperforms matched LoRA on safety–over-refusal | At 4B, Safety State versus LoRA yields **3.84% vs 5.33% ASR** and **9.00% vs 13.60% ORR**; at 35B-A3B, **2.81% vs 3.04% ASR** and **19.60% vs 27.60% ORR**. Both use the same 5,745 examples and 37 updates. | Sections 4.2.1–4.2.2; Tables 9 and 13 |
| Capability retention is mixed at larger scale | Mean retention over MMLU-Pro, MATH, AIME 2025, and MBPP is **82.07→78.64** for the 35B-A3B base versus Safety State; Safety State trails LoRA on MMLU-Pro and MBPP but leads it on MATH and AIME. | Table 9; Section 4.2.2 |
| Sparse routing improves long-sequence efficiency | At 128K tokens, Top-4 MARCH reportedly more than doubles dense-MARCH training throughput and cuts core forward–backward runtime by roughly one order of magnitude. | Section 3.4; Figure 4 |

These results support the authors’ claim that routed recurrent-state access can improve recall and selected capabilities under matched training, and that persistent Safety State offers a better aggregate safety–over-refusal trade-off than their rank-8 LoRA control. They do not establish universal robustness, protection against adaptive attacks, statistical significance, or superiority to the wider set of safety-alignment methods. Safety State also depends on external supervised examples, despite safety being represented internally.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- The state bank grows with context length; sparse routing reduces read cost but not storage, and fixed-interval anchoring can retain redundant states (Section 6).
- Large-scale tests cover only two Qwen3.5 configurations under one CPT–SFT pipeline (Section 6).
- Safety specialization uses a small corpus—1,915 targets expanded to 5,745 examples—and a limited English benchmark suite; multilingual, adaptive, and state-tampering attacks are absent (Sections 4.2.1 and 6).
- Capability retention at 35B-A3B is task-dependent, and reliable composition of multiple persistent states remains untested (Sections 4.2.2 and 6).
- Detachability introduces an integrity risk: the Safety State or routing path could be removed, substituted, or suppressed (Section 6).

**Analyst inferences:**

- Because no confidence intervals, repeated runs, or significance tests are reported in the supplied text, the stability of modest gains cannot be judged.
- LoRA coverage differs by scale and is only one rank/configuration; therefore the comparison does not establish dominance over LoRA generally (Section 4.2.1).
- The routing ablation shows Top-4 reduces NIAH average from **51.33 to 44.85** relative to dense routing in the 0.8B setting, so scaled-model efficiency comes with an incompletely characterized retrieval trade-off (Table 7).
- No direct ablation isolates whether scaled gains arise from anchoring, routing, added parameters, or interactions with continued training beyond the controlled small-scale analysis.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the state bank remain bounded without losing recall?** Storage still grows with context. Test learned anchor creation, consolidation, and eviction against fixed 512-token anchoring across retrieval quality, memory use, and throughput.
2. **How robust is Safety State to adaptive or integrity attacks?** The paper omits adaptive, multilingual, and state-tampering evaluations. Attack the persistent state and router through suppression, substitution, and adversarial context while measuring ASR, ORR, and capability retention.
3. **Can multiple capability states compose safely?** Only one Safety State is studied. Train separate safety, domain, and style states, then attach them jointly and measure routing conflicts, interference, and compositional generalization.
4. **Does the approach generalize beyond Qwen3.5 hybrids?** Large-scale evidence uses two related backbones. Run matched trials on independently designed recurrent or hybrid families, including repeated seeds and statistical uncertainty.
