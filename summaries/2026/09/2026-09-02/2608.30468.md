# Hi-Q: Hierarchical Evidence-guided Query Refinement for Multi-Hop Question Answering

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30468) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30468)


## One-sentence takeaway

Hi-Q improves multi-hop QA by testing whether each query is answerable from retrieved evidence and recursively decomposing only unresolved queries into dependency-ordered subqueries.

## Problem and motivation

Multi-hop questions compress several dependent information needs into one coarse query, while corpus evidence is often distributed across fine-grained passages. The authors call this mismatch **retrievable granularity discovery**: determining the query unit that is both retrievable and answerable.

They argue that existing approaches do not explicitly solve this problem: single retrieval can match isolated terms, graph RAG imposes query-independent corpus structure, iterative retrieval can propagate an unsupported intermediate query, and preplanned decomposition may fragment an already answerable question (Introduction, pp. 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Evidence-conditioned control:** A resolution operator rewrites the current query using accumulated history, retrieves top-\(k\) passages, and asks a reader for an answer. A non-null answer stops refinement; a null answer permits expansion (Sections 3.1–3.2, pp. 3–5).

- **Dependency-preserving hierarchical decomposition:** Each unresolved node is split into exactly two subqueries. The prerequisite branch is solved first, and its answer, evidence, and context are added to the history before the dependent branch runs (Section 3.3; Algorithm 1, pp. 5–6, 14).

- **Semantic coverage verification:** A verifier checks whether the two children preserve the parent query’s constraints and may repair the split once; an invalid repaired split becomes non-decomposable (Section 3.3, p. 6).

- **Cost-sensitive formulation:** The authors characterize STOP versus EXPAND as a threshold on the probability that the current evidence state is unresolved. They explicitly do not claim that node-wise decisions produce a globally optimal tree (Section 3.2; Appendix B, pp. 5, 15–16).

- **Full-corpus evaluation:** Hi-Q is evaluated on three benchmarks using corpora of up to 5,233,235 passages, alongside controlled supporting/distractor pools, component ablations, signal diagnostics, cost matching, and reader/retriever substitutions (Section 4 and Appendices F–I).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that evidence-conditioned, dependency-aware refinement improves accuracy under the tested English passage-QA settings, including full corpora. Ablations support the importance of hierarchy, dependency ordering, and selective triggering, though the triggering-policy ablation uses only 100 questions.

The evidence does **not** establish global optimality of the generated trees, generalization beyond the listed datasets and modalities, or independence from LLM-module errors. It also does not provide a full-corpus PropRAG comparison on HotpotQA.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Evaluation covers English, Wikipedia-derived, passage-based short-answer QA; long-form, multilingual, domain-specific, structured, and multimodal QA are untested (Appendix N, pp. 21–22).
- The modeled dependencies are acyclic and resolvable sequentially. Mutually dependent prerequisites and branches requiring joint optimization are outside scope (Appendix N).
- Resolution, decomposition, verification, and synthesis all depend on LLM reliability. False abstention can cause unnecessary expansion, while unsupported answers can stop refinement prematurely (Appendix N).
- The binary state cannot represent partial evidence, conflicting evidence, or multiple competing bridge entities (Appendix N).
- Node-wise thresholding is not claimed to construct a globally optimal query tree (Section 3.2; Appendix B).
- Maximum depth four bounds cost but can return unresolved branches; worst-case execution permits 31 visited nodes, although the measured mean is 5.7 LLM calls (Section 4.1; Appendix A).
- The learned controller experiment changes only the root decision, not every recursive routing decision (Appendix C).
- PropRAG is absent from HotpotQA-full because estimated graph-construction API costs exceeded the authors’ budget (Section 4.1).

**Analyst inferences**

- Signal-quality estimates are uncertain because the manual trigger taxonomy uses 100 cases and the adversarial FAR test only 50.
- Some comparisons have limited equivalence: quoted controlled baselines and adapted agent baselines do not all reproduce their original native systems, although the authors standardize reader, retriever, subsets, and metric protocol where applicable.
- The always-decompose ablation’s 100-question samples are weaker evidence than the main 1,000-question evaluations.
- The full paper text gives prompts and pseudocode, but it does not itself establish that executable code, indexes, evaluation predictions, or constructed diagnostic datasets were publicly released.
- Temperature-zero decoding removes sampling variation, but reported bootstrap intervals measure question-population uncertainty rather than variability across models, prompts, or runs (Section 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Can routing represent partial and conflicting support rather than a binary answer/null decision?**  
   This remains open because the authors identify the binary state as a limitation. A next experiment could compare binary routing with a calibrated multi-state entailment controller on incomplete, conflicting, and multi-candidate evidence sets.

2. **How well does Hi-Q handle cyclic or jointly dependent reasoning?**  
   Such dependencies are explicitly outside the current state model. A suitable experiment would create or adopt a benchmark with cyclic and parallel prerequisites and compare sequential binary execution with joint search.

3. **Does the method transfer beyond English Wikipedia passage QA?**  
   The reported evidence does not cover multilingual, domain-specific, structured, multimodal, or long-form settings. A next experiment could hold the reader and retrieval budget fixed while evaluating one representative benchmark from each modality.

4. **Can a learned controller improve recursive decisions without increasing cost?**  
   The reported learned estimator applies only at the root and increases LLM calls without significant accuracy gains. Training and calibrating a shared controller across every tree depth, followed by cost–accuracy evaluation, would test the broader possibility.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated method, evaluated settings, and reported results because the supplied text includes the main tables, ablations, appendices, algorithm, prompts, and limitations.

Material information not established by the supplied text includes executable-code availability, artifact licensing, exact sampled question identifiers or random seeds, released diagnostic datasets and predictions, complete infrastructure costs beyond reader API usage, and independent reproduction of the results.
