# Adapting Without Gradients: Affine Statistics Transport and What Its Certificate Can Tell You

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00374) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00374)


## One-sentence takeaway

CASTER enables gradient-free test-time adaptation of frozen vision models by transporting source class statistics with a batch-estimated affine map and using a mechanism-specific certificate to reject unsupported transports, improving the average effect from −3.35 to +1.69 top-1 points across 307 evaluation cells.

## Problem and motivation

Most test-time adaptation (TTA) methods update model parameters, requiring backward computation, mutable weights, and optimizer state. These requirements may be incompatible with inference-only accelerators, third-party or frozen models, and memory-constrained deployments; moreover, the evaluated BatchNorm-only Tent and EATA configurations become inert on LayerNorm backbones (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

The authors address the stricter setting where the learned network must remain entirely frozen. They identify two gaps: a need for lightweight analytical adaptation using only frozen features, and a need to determine when such adaptation is sufficiently supported by the target batch rather than applying it unconditionally.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Affine statistics transport:** CASTER builds a discriminative source subspace, stores per-class means, covariances, and priors, and estimates a shrinkage-stabilized class-shared affine map from each unlabeled target batch. It transports the source Gaussian class distributions and classifies with a Gaussian discriminant, without gradients, optimizer state, or a source feature bank (Sec. 3.1, Eqs. 1–5).

2. **Adaptive dimensionality and covariance estimation:** The active subspace is limited by both class count and target batch size. CASTER switches from a full to a diagonal affine map when \(B<2k\), reducing the map’s degrees of freedom in undersampled batches (Sec. 3 and Sec. 3.1).

3. **Transportability certificate:** A dimensionless residual-to-margin score compares inverse-transported pseudo-class centroid residuals with the minimum source-class separation in a shared Mahalanobis geometry. Transport is used only if this score and minimum coverage conditions pass; otherwise CASTER returns the frozen-head prediction (Sec. 3.2, Eqs. 6–9).

4. **Empirical characterization of the certificate:** The authors distinguish detecting catastrophic affine transport from ranking its likely benefit. Their evidence supports the former but not the latter, and shows that the certificate does not transfer effectively to Tent (Secs. 4.3 and 4.7).

5. **Evaluation and verification:** The study covers frozen-feature estimation, corruption robustness, safety gating, and computational cost. The authors state that aggregates are regenerated from raw logs by a fail-closed verification script (Contributions, p. 2; Sec. 4.1).

The evidence establishes strong empirical performance for CASTER’s frozen-feature estimator, useful rejection of observed catastrophic affine transports, and favorable cost in the single controlled hardware setting reported. It does not establish a formal safety guarantee, perfect separation between safe and unsafe transport, reliable ranking of adaptation benefit, or applicability of the certificate to mechanisms that modify the representation.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** CASTER cannot repair information or class separation absent from the frozen representation (Sec. 5).
- **Author-stated:** A single class-shared affine map may be inappropriate for class-dependent shifts, small batches, or imbalanced class composition (Sec. 5).
- **Author-stated:** The certificate is an empirical diagnostic, not a guarantee over arbitrary target distributions; harmful and benign score ranges overlap (Secs. 4.3 and 5).
- **Author-stated:** The certificate does not rank benefit within non-degenerate regimes and is specific to CASTER’s transport geometry (Secs. 4.3, 4.7, and 5).
- **Author-stated:** CASTER is not universally superior to gradient-based TTA where backward computation and parameter updates are available (Sec. 5).
- **Author-stated:** Moment storage is not always smaller than \(k\)-NN; Flowers-102 reverses the storage advantage because \(k_0=101\) while \(k\)-NN stores only 1,020 training examples (Sec. 4.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The controlled efficiency result is narrow because Table 3 covers one corruption, severity, backbone, and unspecified device; broader hardware generalization is not established.
- **Analyst inference:** Although thresholds are global and a broad interval performs similarly in the pooled study, the supplied text does not provide dataset-wise threshold sensitivity or uncertainty for the 307-cell aggregate.
- **Analyst inference:** The text specifies the method mathematically and reports released code, but does not expose all hyperparameter values, hardware details, or complete per-cell results needed to reproduce every aggregate from this excerpt alone.

## Open research questions

1. **Can class-dependent or mixture transports outperform a single shared affine map safely?** The shared-map assumption can fail under class-dependent shifts (Sec. 5). A next experiment could compare global, clustered, and class-conditional transports while controlling batch size and pseudo-class coverage.

2. **Can a certificate separate beneficial from merely non-catastrophic transport?** The current score detects severe failures but is positively correlated with transport gain in the non-degenerate regime (Sec. 4.3). A next experiment could train or derive a separate utility estimator and evaluate calibration on held-out corruption types without changing the safety gate.

3. **How should transport work under sparse or imbalanced target batches?** ImageNet-C shows failure when 64 samples must cover 1,000 classes, and class composition can be confounded with shift (Secs. 4.6 and 5). A next experiment could accumulate moments across batches or use balanced memory and measure the trade-off between adaptation delay, accuracy, and catastrophic harm.

4. **What mechanism-aware certificates can monitor representation-updating TTA?** The pre-update CASTER statistic rejects most useful Tent updates because Tent changes the representation (Sec. 4.7). A next experiment could evaluate a post-update or predicted-update certificate that explicitly measures Tent’s resulting representation while accounting for its additional computation.
