# Harness-of-Harness: Multi-Day Autonomous Software Development with Continual Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-02.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01481) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01481)


## One-sentence takeaway

Harness-of-Harness improves three fixed coding-agent configurations by repeatedly planning bounded increments, developing from the current artifact, and independently testing a frozen candidate, though the evidence comes mostly from single-run benchmark trials and one game-development case study.

## Problem and motivation

The paper targets autonomous software development from high-level requirements without ongoing human guidance. Unlike bounded coding tasks, this requires agents to preserve earlier decisions and verified behavior, coordinate interdependent components, incorporate test evidence, and continue making coherent progress over long trajectories.

The authors identify three central gaps: limited continuity across coding-agent invocations, difficulty selecting useful increments without falling into repetitive repair or uncontrolled expansion, and unreliable acceptance when the implementing agent also judges completion (Sections 1 and 3.1–3.4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Iterative three-role orchestration:** HoH repeatedly invokes a Project Planner, Developer, and independent QA Tester using the same fixed harness–model configuration (Algorithm 1; Sections 3.2 and 3.4).
- **Dual cross-loop state:** It carries both the evolving artifact and structured execution evidence, allowing later plans to account for unresolved gaps and verified behavior (Section 3.3).
- **Bounded, locally complete increments:** Each plan selects a small coherent capability, specifies preservation constraints, and defines observable validation conditions (Sections 3.4.1 and A.2).
- **Evidence-grounded acceptance:** The Developer performs local baseline–change–retest cycles, while a read-only QA role independently applies scenario-specific white-box and black-box checks to a frozen artifact (Sections 3.4.2–3.4.3).
- **Runtime-enforced role contracts:** Read/write permissions, input visibility, output schemas, and candidate identity are deterministically controlled without prescribing the agents’ internal workflow (Figure 3; Sections 3.4 and A.1–A.5).
- **Evaluation and released artifacts:** The authors test three harness–model pairs on three benchmarks, conduct cross-loop ablations, and report a traceable 70-loop FPS-development case with code and selected development records (Sections 4–5 and B.9).

The aggregate evidence supports the authors’ claim that HoH generally improves final artifact quality over the corresponding standalone harnesses and that planning updates, evidence feedback, and artifact continuity all matter in the tested setting. The pass-controlled experiment strengthens the case that gains are not merely caused by repeating development.

It does not establish monotonic improvement per task: the supplementary task tables contain regressions, including several negative Vanilla-to-HoH@3 changes for OpenCode and Pi (Tables 15–16), despite the main text’s statement that HoH@3 wins in every reported aggregate category. Nor does it establish reliable completion of arbitrary real-world systems, since the long-running evidence is one game case that retained 16 unresolved issues.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Future work is needed across more game types and non-game software systems (Section 6).
- **Single generation per condition:** Every task–condition score comes from one valid run, so stochastic variability is not measured across repeated generations (Section B.2; Table 7).
- **Generation controls:** The clients expose no common reproducible generation seed; temperature and top-\(p\) use provider defaults (Section B.2).
- **Benchmark subsets:** GameCraft-Bench uses 45 of 140 tasks, while FrontierSWE uses 15 of 17 because two tasks were incompatible with the environment (Sections 4.1 and B.4).
- **Limited ablation breadth:** Ablations cover only GameCraft-Bench with Codex/GPT-5.5 and three iterations, so their conclusions may not transfer to other harnesses, tasks, or longer runs (Section 4.3; Table 3).
- **Resource comparability:** Token accounting may include cached reads and differs by provider, precluding direct cross-provider cost comparison (Sections 4.1 and B.7).
- **Incomplete release:** Raw runs, task data, benchmark repositories, analysis records, environment files, and hidden evaluator contents are excluded from the accompanying package (Section B.9).
- **Analyst inference—case-study validity:** The single Fusepoint trajectory demonstrates feasibility but cannot isolate HoH’s causal effect because no matched long-running baseline is reported; its 16 unresolved issues also complicate the description of the artifact as “complete” (Sections 5.1–5.2).
- **Analyst inference—summary statistic ambiguity:** The abstract’s 52.25% average relative gain and 82.86% maximum gain are not explicitly derived in the supplied text, making their aggregation basis unclear.

## Open research questions

1. **How robust are the gains to generation variance?** Only one valid run is reported per task and condition. A plausible experiment is to repeat each condition with multiple independent generations and report paired confidence intervals and failure rates.

2. **Which HoH mechanisms generalize beyond game construction?** Ablations are restricted to GameCraft-Bench and one model–harness pair. The same factorial ablations could be run across FrontierSWE and ProgramBench for all three configurations.

3. **Does improvement persist without eventual regressions over much longer horizons?** FrontierSWE dominance peaks at iteration 9 rather than 10, and Fusepoint reopened 17 issues. A next experiment could track task-level quality, regression frequency, and cost across substantially more iterations with stopping criteria.

4. **How does HoH compare with other structured long-horizon workflows?** The controlled baseline is primarily standalone or continued Vanilla execution. A useful experiment would compare against alternative role-based or evidence-driven agent workflows under identical models, tools, passes, and token budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the method, reported benchmark aggregates, ablations, and stated experimental protocol because the supplied text includes the full paper and supplement.

Material gaps remain: derivation of the abstract’s relative-gain statistics; ProgramBench task count and detailed results; repeated-run uncertainty for aggregate comparisons; a matched baseline for the 70-loop case; complete Fusepoint acceptance results and player-experience results; raw execution artifacts; and evaluator implementation details beyond the supplied descriptions.
