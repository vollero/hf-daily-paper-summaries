# Softmax Reparameterization for Output-Head Quantization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31291) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31291)


## One-sentence takeaway
Selecting a softmax-equivalent output head before quantization substantially improves fidelity in several sensitive language models while preserving packed execution, but gains depend on the model, quantizer, and evaluation distribution (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Large vocabulary output projections require substantial weight reads during decoding, yet quantization recipes often retain these heads at higher precision because their errors directly affect next-token probabilities. The authors identify a mismatch: weight or logit reconstruction objectives do not fully reflect softmax’s sensitivity to relative logits, and equivalent full-precision heads can quantize differently (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Quantizer-specific representative selection:** Compute the vocabulary-row mean \(\mu\), form \(W_t=W-t\mathbf{1}\mu^\top\), and select \(t\) by source-to-quantized-head validation KL. The scalar grid includes the original head and fixed mean-centering; the decoder remains frozen. The contribution is the post-training selection procedure, not softmax invariance or centering themselves (§§2, 5).
- **Inference compatibility:** Fold the shift into quantized weights for compatible logit paths. For nonlinear paths such as soft-capping, restore the removed common offset before the nonlinearity using a rank-one correction (§2.3; Appendix B).
- **Empirical and mechanistic evidence:** Evaluate RTN, activation-weighted MSE, and full-Hessian GPTQ; demonstrate packed Phi execution; and show that lower KL can accompany greater logit reconstruction error but lower Fisher-weighted error (§§3–4).
- **Exploratory extension:** Search separate coefficients for hidden-channel groups. This enlarges the equivalent-head family, with additional fidelity gains under RTN/AW-MSE and mixed GPTQ results (Appendix J).

These results support improved source-distribution fidelity in the tested settings. The latency benefit comes from head compression; shifting preserves that benefit. They do not establish universal transfer, downstream task improvements, or equivalent performance with quantized decoders. Absolute results from different numerical and evaluation protocols should not be pooled.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope limits:** Selection guarantees only non-increasing validation KL within the searched grid and quantizer; it does not guarantee test improvement or an optimal representative (§2.2).
- **Evaluation reuse:** Main test articles were previously encountered during exploration. Untouched replication covers Phi and BLOOM; small main-matrix differences lack per-article uncertainty (Appendix A.1).
- **Transfer and provenance:** Multilingual regressions occur. Some transfer heads were reconstructed after temporary artifacts were lost, so coefficients—not every historical tensor—were frozen (Appendix C).
- **Deployment limits:** GPTQ latency and packed nonlinear-correction overhead were unmeasured. Keeping Phi’s BF16 embedding while adding its packed output copy increases resident weights **7.17→7.47 GiB** (§3.3; Appendix G).
- **Extension limits:** The nine-model grouped search receives more validation evaluations than scalar search; GPTQ outcomes are mixed. Matched-budget evidence is limited to the separate SmolLM3 pilot (Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference:** Fidelity metrics and short-context probes do not establish broad generation quality. Bootstrap intervals conditional on fixed heads do not measure calibration-refitting variability (§3; Appendix C.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can selection avoid multilingual regressions?** English-selected BLOOM heads regress on some languages. Compare English-only, multilingual-mixture, and worst-language validation objectives on untouched multilingual data (Appendix C.2).
- **Do gains survive decoder quantization?** Evaluations keep decoders in BF16. Repeat matched head searches on quantized-decoder states and measure both KL and task performance (§3).
- **When is grouped search worth its extra capacity?** Broad results confound capacity with search budget, while matched-budget evidence covers one model. Run equal-budget scalar/grouped comparisons across families, including GPTQ, on fresh test sets (Appendix J).
- **What is the deployment cost of nonlinear correction?** Gemma’s correction is tested only in dense probes. Implement packed correction and measure latency, memory, and fidelity across batches (Appendix B).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported method and within-setting findings; broader applicability remains uncertain.

Material gaps include verified public artifact availability, several reproduction hashes/revisions absent from the supplied text, missing raw baseline cells in rendered Table 7, GPTQ deployment timing, packed nonlinear/grouped timing, shifted-head temperature controls, and downstream task evaluations.
