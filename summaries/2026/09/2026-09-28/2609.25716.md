# FoMo: Forking Moment in Generative Trajectory as a Perceptual Distance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25716) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25716)


## One-sentence takeaway
FoMo uses diffusion corruption levels to label image-pair perceptual distances without human annotation, improving average IQA ranking performance across four benchmarks while retaining generator-dependent distortion blind spots (Tables 1, 4; Appendix H).

## Problem and motivation
Reference-based image quality assessment (IQA) needs distances that agree with human perception across references and distortion types. The authors argue that absolute human ratings are expensive and inconsistent, while conventional two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) supervision supplies relative preferences within a shared reference rather than directly supervising cross-reference rankings (§1).

The proposed gap is therefore scalable, reference-grounded supervision that supports comparisons across image pairs.

## Approach and contributions
- **Automatic distance-label generation:** Corrupt a reference image at a sampled diffusion timestep, then denoise it into a variant. The interpolation factor \(t\) becomes its distance label; greater corruption implies greater expected perceptual difference. The main dataset contains 480k pairs, equally divided between ImageNet and FLUX-generated references (§§4.1, 5.1).
- **Human grounding of the proxy:** Single-reference ranking and cross-reference preference studies test whether timestep ordering matches perceived similarity (§3).
- **Cross-reference ranking supervision:** A RankNet-style binary cross-entropy objective compares all image-pair distances within a batch, including pairs with different references. Existing CNN and Transformer backbones learn the metric; diffusion is needed for data generation, not inference (§4.2; Appendix C.2).

The contribution is the diffusion-derived supervision pipeline and its validation, rather than a new diffusion architecture or the original ranking-loss formulation.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

These results support useful ordinal supervision and transfer to the tested IQA benchmarks. They do not establish a universally calibrated perceptual scale, superiority for every backbone or distortion, or consistent dominance over released metrics. The human studies directly validate generated variants; large-scale agreement with existing metrics is supporting proxy evidence, not additional human validation.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coverage limitation:** Generated distortions may omit unfamiliar artificial corruptions. Appendix H identifies additive noise, localized corruption, and photometric shifts as coverage gaps; Appendix I proposes domain-specific generators without testing that remedy.
- **Author-stated bias risk:** Learned metrics may inherit perceptual biases from the generator (Appendix K).
- **Analyst inference—ordinal versus absolute validity:** Human ranking agreement and RankBCE principally establish ordering. Calling labels “calibrated distances” goes beyond demonstrated absolute-distance calibration (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison attribution:** Table 1 changes both training data and objective. Table 2 partially separates them, but does not fully isolate dataset size, diversity, augmentation, and label quality.
- **Reporting ambiguities:** §3.2 reports 69.4% majority agreement within a supposedly 50-item bin, leaving its denominator unclear. The generator comparison cites a DINOv3 human baseline of 0.508 that is not supported by Table 1. Some FoMo PLCC entries also differ between Tables 8 and 10 without explanation.
- **Reproducibility gap:** Code availability is asserted, but the supplied text does not establish released training pairs/checkpoints or fully specify generation prompts, guidance, and scheduling settings.

## Open research questions
1. **Can explicit distortion coverage repair failures?** Missing corruption families remain a stated weakness. Compare FoMo with a matched-budget mixture containing noise, localized corruption, and photometric changes, reporting per-distortion performance.
2. **Can near-tie supervision be improved?** Human agreement drops for nearby timesteps. Test uncertainty-aware ranking weights or repeated generation per reference/timestep against the existing hard ordering.
3. **Does timestep calibration transfer across generators and domains?** Generator performance varies, and direct human grounding is limited to the studied samples. Run matched cross-reference human studies across generators and held-out image domains.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial experiments and ablations, supporting the core ranking-supervision result; broader calibration claims and reporting inconsistencies reduce certainty.

Material missing information includes detailed participant sampling, confidence intervals for human-study outcomes, complete generation settings and costs, confirmed dataset/checkpoint availability, and reconciliations of the numerical discrepancies above.
