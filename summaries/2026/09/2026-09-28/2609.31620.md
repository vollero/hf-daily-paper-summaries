# FuseReg: Regularizing Layer Fusion Mitigates the Reconstruction-Generation Gap in Representation Autoencoders

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31620) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31620)


## One-sentence takeaway
FuseReg trains representation autoencoders on random normalized subsets of frozen encoder layers, improving fusion robustness and unguided ImageNet-256 generation, although generator regularization helps inconsistently across scales and metrics (Tables 1–2, D.1).

## Problem and motivation
An RAE’s pixel decoder and diffusion generator share a latent assembled from encoder layers, but favor different information: shallow features aid reconstruction, while deeper fusions can be easier to generate. Fixed fusion binds both stages to one choice, and decoders can fail when that choice changes (§1, Table 1).

The authors identify dependence on a particular layer composition as an addressable part of this reconstruction–generation gap. Their aim is to make downstream models robust across compositions while keeping the encoder frozen.

## Approach and contributions
- **Randomized fusion:** Independently sample a nonempty subset of layer-normalized encoder features for each example and average the retained features. Use the full-layer mean by default at inference (§3.1–3.2).
- **Separate regularization for two stages:** Train the decoder to reconstruct pixels from subset means; train the DiT on noisy subset means while targeting the full-layer mean. Tune decoder and generator drop rates independently (§3.3).
- **Mechanistic analysis:** Prove exact latent mean preservation and covariance proportional to cross-layer disagreement. For homogeneous linear predictors with squared loss, derive an explicit disagreement penalty. Random subset weights have second moments that deterministic global weights cannot match; this is not a general performance guarantee (§4, §F).
- **Controlled empirical tests:** Evaluate fusion robustness, decoder replacement with fixed generated latents, and joint regularization across generator scales and frozen encoder families (§5, §E).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher reconstruction PSNR does not guarantee better rFID | On 50k ImageNet-256 images with DINOv3-L, FuseReg at decoder rate 0.95 improves full-fusion PSNR from the official RAEv2 baseline’s **27.04 to 27.52 dB**, but worsens rFID from **0.18 to 0.42**. | Table 1 |
| One decoder handles reduced fusions | In the same evaluation, FuseReg achieves **23.77 dB** with the seven-layer fusion versus **22.58 dB** for its specialized RAEv2 decoder; single-layer ℓ11 reaches **25.13 dB**, versus **17.50/14.31 dB** for the fixed seven-/23-layer decoders. | Table 1 |
| Decoder replacement improves native unguided generation | With the RAEv2 DiT-XL generator and 50k sampled latents fixed, full-fusion gFID falls **3.01 → 2.21** at decoder rate 0.95. Internal-guidance gFID at scale 1.78 remains **1.25**. | §5.2, Table D.1 |
| Large shifted-fusion gains measure transfer robustness | With fixed seven-layer generator latents, replacing the reproduced **full-fusion-trained** decoder lowers unguided gFID **27.73 → 1.92**. This is not a comparison against a matched seven-layer decoder. | Table D.1, §D.1 |
| Joint regularization benefits DiT-Base | Unguided ImageNet-256, 40 epochs, 50k samples: baseline gFID **13.96**; decoder-only **12.96**; generator-only **12.09**; joint **9.93**, using decoder/generator rates 0.9/0.7. | Table 2a |
| DiT-XL favors decoder-only regularization for gFID | Under the same joint-grid protocol, baseline gFID **2.91** improves to the grid minimum **2.38** at decoder/generator rates 0.95/0. No jointly regularized entry beats this. | Table 2c, §D.2 |
| Generation gains extend across encoders | Unguided ImageNet-256 with 40-epoch DiT-Base, 50k samples and 50 Euler steps: SigLIP2-L gFID **13.77 → 8.26** at rates 0.9/0.6; EUPE-B **12.17 → 8.00** at rates 0.9/0.3. | Table E.2 |

The fixed-latent experiments directly support a benefit from changing decoder training. Joint-training evidence supports complementary gains on DiT-Base, but not universal superiority of regularizing both stages. Appendix D.2 explicitly treats complementarity as a point-estimate comparison, not a statistical interaction test. Baselines differ between official-checkpoint comparisons, decoder swaps, and training grids; results should be compared within their respective protocols (§B.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation is limited to ImageNet-256; higher resolutions, other domains, and longer schedules remain untested. Preferred rates depend on scale, metric, encoder, and guidance (§7).
- **Author-stated theoretical boundary:** Exact objective penalties require homogeneous linear predictors and squared loss. They do not establish nonlinear prediction invariance, guided-trajectory behavior, or super-additivity (§F.6).
- **Analyst inference—metric trade-offs:** Robustness and PSNR gains do not imply uniformly better reconstruction distributions. Full-fusion rFID worsens in Table 1, and full-fusion fidelity can decline for the additional encoders (Table E.1).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** Baselines retain their stated fusion scales, while FuseReg uses normalized means; no separate scale-matched ablation is supplied (§B.1). The deterministic-fusion theory also excludes input-dependent routing (§F.4).
- **Analyst inference—coverage and inputs:** Positive probability for every subset does not guarantee uniformly strong performance: the loss bound weakens for rare subsets (§F.3). Some “single-layer” probes retain a last-layer classifier surrogate, so they do not isolate that layer as the sole information source (Figure 4, Table E.1).

## Open research questions
- **Does generator regularization help larger models after longer training?** DiT-XL’s best gFID uses no generator regularization. Repeat matched rate sweeps over longer schedules and multiple seeds (§6, Table 2c).
- **Can guidance and FuseReg be calibrated jointly?** Guided and unguided metrics favor different rates. Sweep guidance strength alongside both drop rates and measure disagreement between the full and intermediate heads (§6, Table D.2).
- **How much robustness comes specifically from randomized composition?** Scale conventions differ, and some probes preserve a classifier surrogate. Compare scale-matched deterministic fusion and random-subset training, with and without that surrogate (§B.1, Figure 4).
- **Does robustness transfer beyond ImageNet-256?** Additional encoders are evaluated on the same dataset and resolution. Test higher-resolution reconstruction and generation on another image domain (§7, §E).
