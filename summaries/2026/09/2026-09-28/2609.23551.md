# Paragraph Boundaries Are Not White Space:Compression Depth as the Signature of Hierarchical Structure

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23551) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23551)


## One-sentence takeaway
In small Transformers with explicit hierarchical positional channels, genuine paragraph labels produce deeper attention compression than density-matched random labels in two of three corpora, while the third comparison remains unresolved (§5, Table 3).

## Problem and motivation
Reading-order distance cannot uniquely specify paragraph structure: identical token sequences can have different segmentations. The paper asks whether changing an explicit paragraph coordinate changes attention independently of token content and distance—and whether that response distinguishes meaningful boundaries from arbitrary positional labels. The objective is to characterize attention, rather than improve generation or downstream performance (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled hierarchical representation:** hRoPE assigns paragraph, sentence, and token coordinates separate rotary channel groups. Fake merges and splits change only the paragraph coordinate, holding tokens and lower-level coordinates fixed. Controls include ordinary RoPE, sentence/token-only channels, and density-matched random paragraph labels resampled during training (§3).
- **Compression-depth measurement:** The estimator compares attention at exactly matched token distances, then pools contrasts and fits a quadratic response over paragraph displacement. Its vertex value, \(U^*\), measures compression depth; more negative values indicate deeper compression. Exact matching corrects a distance-binning artifact that previously reversed WikiText-2’s estimated sign (§5.1; Appendix A.6).
- **Structural characterization and explanatory probes:** Real-versus-random comparisons distinguish compression’s presence from its depth. Eight corpus statistics and paragraph-level regressions test whether lexical persistence, length, or embedding coherence account for the observed variation (§§5–6).

## Evidence and main results
All main models use 8 layers, 8 heads, width 512, a 1,024-token context, 5,000 training steps, and three seeds per corpus (§3.4).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Paragraph-coordinate interventions affect attention | Anchor-normalized merge/split effects: WikiText-2 −1.300/+0.841; OpenWebText −1.302/+0.914; Code −3.769/+3.373. All reported document-bootstrap 95% intervals exclude zero; paragraph-blind controls are unchanged by construction. | §4.2, Table 1 |
| Genuine structure gives deeper compression in two corpora | \(U^*\), hRoPE versus random: Code −0.772 versus −0.238, paired gap 95% CI [−0.578, −0.498]; WikiText-2 −0.481 versus −0.220, CI [−0.341, −0.171]. Exact-distance estimator, at least 2,000 pairs per cell, quadratic vertex depth. | §5.2, Table 3 |
| OpenWebText’s depth gap is unresolved | hRoPE −0.305 versus random −0.326; paired gap 95% CI [−0.059, +0.093], under the same protocol. Both models compress. | §5.2, Table 3 |
| Trained weights respond to coordinate content | Inference-only substitution yields validation-loss ordering real < periodic < random < constant in every seed and corpus. Mean real/constant losses: Code 2.383/2.720, WikiText-2 5.411/5.758, OpenWebText 5.622/5.922 nats. Seed-level evidence is directional. | §4.3, Table 2 |
| Corpus statistics provide incomplete correspondence | Aggregate embedding coherence matches the descriptive depth ranking and separates two of three corpus pairs under its own variance estimate; coherence half-life and local lexical persistence also match nominal ordering but separate no pairs. | §6.3, Table 4 |
| Adjacent coherence predicts shallower paragraph-level compression | Conditional standardized coherence slopes are +0.015/+0.013/+0.017 for Code/WikiText-2/OpenWebText, all reported \(p<0.01\); pairwise raw-slope differences have \(|z|\leq0.95\). Regression controls length, diversity, and position at paragraph displacement one. | §6.4, Tables 5, 9 |

The interventions establish causal sensitivity to the supplied coordinate in these architectures. They do not establish paragraph understanding in ordinary Transformers or downstream benefits. Compression alone is insufficient evidence of genuine structure.

The authors’ statement that no corpus statistic “reproduces” the ordering needs qualification: several match the point-estimate ranking, but none distinguishes every corpus pair under its reported uncertainty. Likewise, undetected slope differences support compatibility with a common slope, not proof of universality.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Three fixed corpora, small models, three training seeds, corpus-specific paragraph definitions, and no downstream evaluation limit generalization. Document bootstrap intervals do not establish population-level cross-corpus ordering (§7; Appendix B).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** WikiText-2’s compression location is non-identifiable and its depth varies with the sampling threshold; Code’s location depends on polynomial degree. The estimator cannot support the periodic control’s depth comparison because its coordinate is strongly coupled to token distance (§5.1; Appendices A.6–A.7, C).
- **Author-stated explanatory limits:** Paragraph regressions are correlational and measure displacement-one responses, rather than the corpus-level vertex depth. Softmax-based competing mechanisms remain untested (§7; Appendices A.10, A.15).
- **Analyst inference—regression uncertainty:** Classical homoskedastic WLS errors do not explicitly account for shared documents or boundary pairs counted at both paragraph endpoints. Document-clustered inference could change the reported significance (Appendix A.10).
- **Analyst observation—reporting inconsistency:** Appendix A.10 says Code’s well lies near displacement one, whereas Table 10 reports approximately 5.5 for the quadratic fit and 3.4 for the cubic fit. The distinction between paragraph-level and vertex-depth measurements therefore deserves care.

## Open research questions
1. **Is OpenWebText’s unresolved gap a true null or limited power?** The paper cannot distinguish these explanations. Repeat matched real/random training with more seeds and documents.
2. **What causes compression depth?** Tested corpus summaries provide incomplete correspondence, and the additive-process argument excludes only a restricted mechanism. Fit and intervene on the proposed softmax-competitive alternative.
3. **Does the effect persist and matter at scale?** Production-scale models and downstream tasks are untested. Repeat the coordinate interventions in larger models and measure tasks requiring cross-paragraph context.
4. **Is the coherence slope robust to dependence and measurement choices?** Existing evidence uses classical WLS and displacement-one responses. Re-estimate with document-clustered uncertainty and additional paragraph displacements.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed protocols, controls, and uncertainty estimates, supporting the narrow real-versus-random finding.

Material missing information includes code/checkpoint release locations, Python corpus provenance, exact rotary channel allocations and calibrated bases, the numerical calibration safety margin, a fully specified random-label sampler, document-clustered regression checks, and larger-scale or downstream results.
