# Enhancing Photogrammetric Digital Surface Models with Pretrained Diffusion Models and Multimodal Conditioning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31199) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31199)


## One-sentence takeaway

A pruned Stable Diffusion 3 model conditioned on calibrated stereo elevation and RGB imagery reduces Dense Urban RMSE from 6.00 to 3.45 m across eight French test cities and from 4.16 to 2.77 m in held-out Bordeaux, with gains dependent on surface type and input compatibility (Table 6).

## Problem and motivation

Satellite stereo-photogrammetry produces economical digital surface models (DSMs), but noise, matching errors, and missing elevations limit their quality; aerial LiDAR provides more accurate references at greater cost. The authors target local correction of dense stereo DSMs, distinguishing this task from prior void filling, super-resolution, and sparse-depth completion work (§1, Table 1).

The task assumes an already vertically calibrated DSM and compatibility between the surfaces represented by DSM and LiDAR. It does not address arbitrary vertical offsets or verified physical changes between acquisitions (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Patch-wise physical-value normalization:** Normalize elevations using a patch offset and scale, weight the flow-matching loss by squared scale, and restore predictions to meters. Conditional training and inference derive statistics from valid input DSM pixels (§2.2).
- **Image-only pretrained backbone:** Remove SD3’s text stream while retaining pretrained image weights. Elevation patches are replicated across RGB channels for the existing VAE; scale and offset receive separate embeddings (§2.3).
- **Multimodal refinement:** Separate DSM and RGB ControlNets inject summed residuals into the frozen adapted backbone. RGB conditioning is trained first, then frozen while DSM conditioning is trained; the paper compares this complete configuration with DSM-only conditioning (§§2.3.2, 2.5).

The contributions are adaptations and their evaluation for elevation refinement; the underlying flow-matching and ControlNet mechanisms are adopted from prior work.

## Evidence and main results

Headline elevation metrics use common finite LiDAR/DSM pixels in retained test patches and average learned-model metrics over 20 inference seeds.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong urban refinement within training cities | Dense Urban RMSE: calibrated DSM **6.00 m**, DSM-only **3.85 m**, DSM+RGB **3.45 m**; test comprises 6,385 patches across eight cities. | Table 6; Table A4 |
| Urban improvement transfers to Bordeaux | Dense Urban RMSE: **4.16 → 3.10 → 2.77 m**, respectively; 3,209 patches from the held-out city. | Table 6; Table A4 |
| Improvement is not universal | Bordeaux Vegetation MAE worsens from **1.69 to 1.75 m** with DSM+RGB; RMSE changes only from **3.12 to 3.10 m**, with the paired spatial interval including zero. | Table 6; §3.3, Figure 6 |
| RGB helps secondary void recovery | DSM+RGB has lower mean RMSE than DSM-only in every evaluated land-cover group in both tests; only original DSM voids with valid LiDAR are evaluated. | Table A7 |
| High-error inputs reveal an RGB exception | On 4,046 RGB-supported, P90-excluded patches, both models improve every group over calibrated DSM; Croplands RMSE is **2.064 m** for DSM-only versus **2.240 m** for DSM+RGB, averaged over 20 seeds. | Table A6 |
| Pretraining improves generative feature matching | FD DINOv2: **169.4** pretrained versus **692.8** best scratch learning rate, using 50,000 generated and reference samples each after the reported backbone training. This is not meter-space accuracy. | Table 5; Table A2 |
| VAE introduces measurable distortion | LiDAR-normalized encode–decode diagnostic over 9,594 test patches: **0.272 m MAE**, **0.573 m RMSE**. | §3.2 |
| Pruning improves benchmark throughput | With two ControlNets at batch 32: **0.82 versus 0.58 samples/s** for pruned versus unpruned models on one A100 80 GB, using synthetic inputs, 50 denoising steps, and VAE decoding. | Table A3 |

These results establish improvements over the calibrated input and DSM-only configuration within the tested protocol. They do not establish superiority over competing enhancement methods or isolate each architectural contribution. Spatial bootstrap intervals and inference-seed variation are reported separately; neither measures variability across independently trained models (§§3.3–4).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Calibration and selection dependence:** LiDAR supports both vertical calibration and target-dependent P90 filtering, including in Bordeaux. Surface compatibility is assumed rather than directly verified; filtering alters land-cover composition (§2.4, Appendix C).
- **Restricted transfer evidence:** Bordeaux is the sole geographically held-out city and shares the sensor and processing setting. In-context training and test patches can touch without buffers (§4).
- **Incomplete attribution:** There are no controlled comparisons of alternative fusion, training order, or joint training; no repeated training runs; and no task-matched conventional or supervised enhancement baselines (§§2.5, 4). A controlled normalization ablation is not reported.
- **Reference and representation uncertainty:** LiDAR void interpolation, residual misalignment, coarse land-cover labels, and VAE distortion complicate interpretation. Tile continuity and performance on bridges, high-rises, and steep terrain remain untested (§§3.2, 4).
- **Analyst inference:** The RGB comparison cannot isolate the effect of adding RGB alone because the complete multimodal recipe also adds a separate training stage using a broader LiDAR–RGB dataset (§2.5, Table A4).
- **Reproducibility:** Implementation, weights, and source patches are unavailable publicly; redistribution is restricted (Appendix F).

## Open research questions

1. **Does refinement generalize beyond this acquisition pipeline?** Only one same-setting held-out city is tested. Evaluate spatially buffered, independently calibrated datasets from other regions, sensors, and stereo pipelines.
2. **Which components drive downstream accuracy?** Feature-distance results do not isolate conditional elevation benefits. Run matched-budget, repeated-training ablations of pretraining, normalization, pruning, and RGB training.
3. **Can refinement preserve genuine surface changes?** Compatibility is assumed, and seasonal/building changes complicate evaluation. Test verified unchanged and changed areas separately, measuring elevation-change preservation.
4. **How sensitive are results to target-dependent curation?** The filtering threshold and LiDAR filling rule were not ablated. Retrain and evaluate across filtering choices, including unfiltered inputs and measured-only LiDAR reference support.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** in the reported within-protocol findings; broader generalization and component-level attribution remain uncertain.

Material unavailable evidence includes independent training replicates, task-matched external baselines, controlled component and curation ablations, cross-sensor/country evaluations, end-to-end deployment timing, tile-continuity tests, and public code, weights, and source evaluation data.
