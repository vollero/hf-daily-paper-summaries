# Not All Ranks Are Equal: Budget-Aware LoRA Merging Across Tasks

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22237) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22237)


## One-sentence takeaway
Net Utility selects LoRA singular directions using a data-free benefit–interference score, improving average normalized accuracy over uniform allocation in the tested vision and NLI settings, with some language regressions and unresolved theoretical inconsistencies.

## Problem and motivation
Merging task-specific LoRAs into one adapter avoids inference-time swapping, but a limited rank budget forces information loss. The authors argue that uniform allocation across layers—and sometimes tasks—wastes capacity because useful directions and cross-task interference vary. Their target is offline merging under a fixed component budget without task data or forward passes (Introduction; Methodology, pp. 1–4).

## Approach and contributions
- **Direction-level scoring:** Decompose each adapter into singular components and score normalized source-task preservation minus interference with other tasks. Input geometry is approximated from adapter weights, with a Gram-based formulation and a heuristic row-space alternative (Eq. 8; Appendix A.4).
- **Budget allocation:** Pool scores across tasks and adapted layers, retaining the highest positive values up to the global budget. This permits variable ranks, non-prefix selections, and unused capacity. Selection is exact for the stated separable objective, not the original model-output objective (Eq. 9).
- **Empirical integration:** Apply allocation to TA, TSV, DARE, TIES, and Iso-C combinations in full, Core, and KnOTS spaces. Crucially, DARE and TIES retain equal per-layer budgets; their experiments do not test global redistribution across layers (Experiments, p. 4).

The contribution is an allocation criterion layered onto existing merging operators, rather than a new backbone or benchmark.

## Evidence and main results
Values below are normalized accuracy on the tables’ percentage scale; improvements are percentage-point changes, not raw task-accuracy gains.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Overall reported gains | Authors report average improvements of +2.1 points for seven vision tasks and +2.2 for six NLI tasks across merging methods/spaces | Abstract; Conclusion |
| Strongest tabulated vision gain | ViT-B/32, full-space DARE, R=64: 63.5 → 67.3, reported +3.8; averages over seven tasks | Table 1 |
| Strong language improvement | Qwen3-4B, TSV, R=16: 76.8 → 80.3, +3.5 in all three spaces; averages over six NLI tasks | Table 2 |
| Gains are not universal | NLI, KnOTS-space DARE, R=32: 70.9 → 70.5, −0.4 | Table 2 |
| Allocation also helps Iso-C combinations | R=16, full space: reported gains of +1.2/+1.3 for vision and +0.5/+1.0 for NLI with TSV/TA | Table 3 |
| Leaving capacity unused can help | Vision TA, R=112: positive-only selection retains 61.5 directions per module on average and scores 66.94 versus 63.75 when spending the full budget, +3.19 | Table 4 |
| Selected directions often violate prefix truncation | Vision: 88% of selected sets are non-prefix at every tested budget; language: 64.5% at R=64 | Analysis, p. 6 |
| Difficulty interpretation is correlational | Seven MATH topic adapters: Pearson r=−0.78 between mean utility and adapter-minus-base accuracy | Fig. 5 |

These results support allocation as a useful intervention under the tested component budgets. They do not establish measured deployment speedups, universal gains, or that uniform allocation is the dominant cause of the remaining gap to individual adapters. Task-average reporting also leaves individual-task trade-offs unresolved.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The derivation covers additive merging; transfer to nonlinear operators is empirical. Validation-based tuning of λ is proposed as potentially beneficial but not demonstrated (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—derivation problem:** Appendix A.2’s stated substitution into Young’s inequality gives an interference coefficient of **M−1+λ**, rather than the **λ** shown in Eq. 7. Additionally, dropping only the source-residual coefficient changes the trade-off unless λ is reparameterized. The supplied derivation therefore does not establish the claimed bound as written.
- **Analyst inference—limited attribution:** No reported ablation cleanly separates interference scoring, non-prefix selection, task allocation, and layer allocation. Vision uses the heuristic geometry; a controlled comparison of geometry choices is absent.
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Main evaluations use one backbone per modality, and language merging is restricted to NLI. No repeated-run uncertainty, per-task outcome tables, or measured runtime/memory comparisons are supplied.
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** Table 4 reports 45% unspent capacity at full budget, while its caption says over half is unspent and the prose says only 48% is spent. Some displayed deltas also differ from subtraction of rounded endpoints.
- **Analyst inference—difficulty claim:** Adapter-minus-base improvement does not independently establish intrinsic task difficulty; Fig. 5 supports an association across seven topics, not the proposed explanation.

## Open research questions
1. **Which allocation mechanism drives the gains?** The components are not isolated experimentally. Compare magnitude-only, interference-aware prefix, non-prefix local, and global selection at matched budgets.
2. **Can the theoretical guarantee be repaired?** The bound and coefficient handling are inconsistent. Derive corrected coefficients and compare the resulting selections and preservation losses against the published score.
3. **Do component budgets translate into deployment benefits?** No serving measurements are reported. Benchmark latency, memory, and realized ranks for uniform and variable-rank merged adapters.
4. **Does utility predict independently measured difficulty?** The evidence uses adapter improvement as a proxy. Repeat the topic analysis with independent difficulty labels and multiple training seeds.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The algorithm and aggregate comparisons are explicit, but theoretical and reporting inconsistencies limit stronger conclusions.

Material missing information includes per-task raw accuracies, uncertainty estimates, exact baseline tuning settings, classification-head merging details, complete MATH training details, measured serving costs, and confirmation of a release of the proposed implementation or trained adapters.
