# LastOPD: Taming Collapse in Latent On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28845) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28845)


## One-sentence takeaway
LastOPD combines last-layer latent alignment with a short crossfade into token-level distillation, improving mathematical reasoning in two tested cross-size Qwen3 pairs while avoiding the collapse observed under sustained latent-only supervision.

## Problem and motivation
Token-level on-policy distillation trains a student on its own responses using a teacher’s next-token distributions. Latent supervision adds hidden-state targets, but the authors find that sustained alignment can improve its own similarity metric while severely degrading task accuracy.

The identified gap is whether hidden-state alignment remains beneficial when teacher and student layers differ in depth, width, and computational role—and when that supervision should stop (§§1, 4). The authors hypothesize that early alignment transfers useful information, whereas continued matching imposes teacher representations the student cannot use effectively; this mechanism is not established causally.

## Approach and contributions
- **Failure diagnosis:** Track latent-only collapse alongside improving alignment, and examine layerwise readouts, linear probes, and activation-dominated similarity metrics. Masking large activation dimensions and remapping layers using raw CKA do not prevent collapse (§4).
- **Last-layer alignment:** Match normalized, post-normalization states immediately before the language-model heads using a trainable student-to-teacher MLP adapter. This removes intermediate-layer pairing (§5).
- **Short crossfade:** On student-generated responses, linearly decrease latent supervision while increasing reverse top-16 token-level distillation over the first 10 optimizer steps. Continue with token-only supervision afterward; discard the adapter at inference (§5).

These results support the recipe within the tested cross-size settings and demonstrate that the reported alignment metrics are unreliable performance proxies. They do not establish a universal collapse mechanism, broad superiority across model lineages, or a wall-clock speedup.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-disclosed:** Each main-table cell represents one training run; no across-seed uncertainty is reported (§6.1).
- **Author-disclosed:** MATH-500 informed diagnostics and the crossfade-window choice. The other seven benchmarks provide evidence independent of that choice (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains narrow: experiments cover mathematical tasks, two cross-size pairs sharing one student, and one same-lineage control. The control does not favor LastOPD on MATH-500 (Table 1).
- **Analyst inference:** Layer-role mismatch is a plausible explanation, but probes and similarity measurements do not isolate it causally. Arithmetic readouts also cover small diagnostic tasks; the first-readable-layer analysis excludes positions never read out (Appendix B; Figure 6).
- **Comparison caveat disclosed in Table 3:** Reversed-projector and Gram-loss variants also retain both losses throughout training. Their scores therefore do not isolate loss-form effects relative to full LastOPD.
- **Analyst inference:** No reported all-layer crossfade with a matched trainable adapter cleanly separates the benefits of last-layer placement, adapter design, and scheduling.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Appendix C.6’s rollout-category percentages do not sum to a complete partition; Figure 10 notes exclusions for short answers, but the denominator handling remains unclear.

## Open research questions
- **Can the handoff adapt to model compatibility?** The same-lineage control favors continued latent supervision. Next experiment: compare fixed and adaptive schedules across additional pairs, choosing schedules on separate validation data.
- **Which component prevents collapse?** Existing comparisons do not fully disentangle alignment location, projector design, and duration. Next experiment: run a factorial ablation holding adapter capacity and loss weights fixed.
- **Does the benefit survive broader replication and compute accounting?** Main comparisons are single-run, math-focused, and report step efficiency. Next experiment: repeat across seeds and non-math tasks while measuring wall-clock time and peak memory.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text clearly supports the reported recipe and within-setting gains; causal interpretation and broader generalization remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes across-seed variance, hardware and wall-clock costs, exact adapter architecture, complete optimizer settings, and detailed J-Lens fitting procedures. The authors link code, but its contents and reproducibility were not verified from the supplied text.
