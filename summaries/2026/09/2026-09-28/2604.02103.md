# BoundInk: Boundary-Aware Online Handwriting Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.02103) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02103)


## One-sentence takeaway
BoundInk explicitly models adjacent-character transitions and sentence context, improving handwriting connectivity, spacing, and trajectory similarity in three matched baseline comparisons, with broader generalization and long-sequence reliability still unresolved (Table 3; §5).

## Problem and motivation
Online handwriting generation must reproduce pen trajectories and pen states, including writer-specific joins, spacing, and alignment. The authors argue that existing sentence generators learn these boundary behaviors implicitly, allowing recognizable glyphs to coexist with unnatural transitions; global trajectory distances can also miss these defects (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Explicit local boundary generation:** A bigram-aware sliding-window Transformer generates each character using its predecessor’s trajectory. Separate character-identity embeddings and gated sentence context control content and context-dependent transitions, while handwriting-image references supply writer and glyph style (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Boundary-aware training:** A cumulative glyph→bigram→sentence curriculum combines trajectory mixture-density prediction, explicit cursive-continuation labels, style losses, and vertical-drift regularization (§3.4; Appendix A).
- **Targeted evaluation:** Connectivity and Spacing Metrics (CSM) measure boundary-level cursive F1, cursive-rate agreement (CRE), inter-character gap similarity (KGS), and word-spacing similarity (SSS), complementing DTW (§4.1; Appendix F).

The sequence-reference extension in Appendix D is exploratory and separate from the main image-reference model.

The evidence supports gains within each matched protocol, reinforced by component ablations and perceptual judgments. It does not establish a unified cross-baseline ranking, generalization beyond the tested scripts, or uniform improvement across geometric criteria. Human percentages describe criterion-wise judgments, not independent participants or sentences.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Broader scripts, unseen character combinations, scarce or mismatched references, decoding efficiency, and accumulated autoregressive errors remain future work (§5).
- **Comparison limits:** Baselines are selected for compatibility and reproducible inference; BoundInk is retrained separately for each protocol (§4.1). **Analyst inference:** these comparisons do not constitute an exhaustive common-setting benchmark.
- **Metric comparability:** Baselines lacking explicit cursive states use a distance threshold, while BoundInk uses predicted states directly (Appendix F.1). **Analyst inference:** threshold sensitivity could affect connectivity comparisons; no sensitivity study is supplied.
- **Metric interpretation:** KGS penalizes even correctly matched overlapping characters and discards negative-gap magnitude (Appendix F.2). **Analyst inference:** it incompletely measures overlap fidelity.
- **Ablation gaps:** Reported main ablations test local decoding, context fusion, and character identity, but do not separately isolate curriculum, cursive supervision, or VDL (§4.2).
- **Exploratory extension:** Authors explicitly acknowledge confounded changes, intermediate checkpoints, segmentation-conditioned selection, and worse full-set DTW for V2 (Appendix D.1).
- **Human-study scope:** Participants were Korean adults evaluating rendered images; broader populations and temporal writing realism are not established (Appendix E.1).

## Open research questions
- **Do local transitions generalize to unseen combinations?** This remains future work (§5). Hold out character pairs while retaining their individual glyphs, then evaluate CSM, DTW, and content accuracy.
- **Which training components cause the gains?** Curriculum, cursive labels, and VDL lack isolated ablations. Run matched-budget factorial experiments with these components independently enabled.
- **Can sequence references reduce drift without worsening trajectory fidelity?** Appendix D shows a confounded stability–DTW tradeoff. Compare reference conditioning and auxiliary supervision separately at converged checkpoints on the full test set.
- **How robust are CSM rankings?** Baseline state conversion and overlap penalties introduce evaluation choices (Appendix F). Sweep those choices and test agreement with blinded boundary annotations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides substantial methodological and quantitative evidence, but causal attribution and generalization remain limited.

Material missing information includes public code/checkpoint/data-release status, numerical OCR results, uncertainty or repeated-seed results for the main comparisons, detailed counts of excluded benchmark samples, and inference latency. Some implementation descriptions also need reconciliation: Appendix A.2’s bigram-VDL schedule and prose disagree about its Stage 3 activation, while §4.1’s generic aggregation description differs from Appendix F’s metric-specific definitions.
