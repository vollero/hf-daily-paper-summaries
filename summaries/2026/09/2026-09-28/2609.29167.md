# IndicBankBench: Evaluating Safety and Reliability of Language Model Assistants in Indian Retail Banking

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29167) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29167)


## One-sentence takeaway
IndicBankBench finds that eleven evaluated models complete only 43.7–58.2% of its synthetic banking cases successfully on all three trials, exposing substantial gaps between occasional success and consistent performance (Table 5).

## Problem and motivation
Banking assistants must combine customer records, conversation history, and tool actions correctly. A fluent final answer can conceal stale evidence, incorrect account selection, missing confirmation, or an erroneous write. The authors identify a gap in aggregate evaluation: it can obscure both the location of failures and inconsistency across repeated runs (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 799 synthetic cases across five operational domains, a capability/refusal domain, and twenty primary axes. Cases combine authenticated customer context, scripted conversations, and deterministic mock tools; 204 require writes (§3; Table 2).
- **Staged evaluation:** Safety, action/tool use, and response adequacy independently gate success; advisory quality does not affect the verdict. Most safety and all action checks are deterministic, with a narrow resolver for ambiguous confirmation and an LLM judge for semantic response adequacy (§3.3; Table 3).
- **Reliability and diagnostics:** Three trials per case distinguish strict **pass³**—all trials succeed—from **pass@3**—at least one succeeds. The authors also provide first-failure diagnostics and a descriptive failure taxonomy (§§4–6).
- **Released assets:** The authors report releasing cases, mock tools, prompts, and evaluation code; code uses MIT licensing and case data CC BY 4.0 (Appendix A.1).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strict reliability remains limited | Across eleven models and 799 cases, pass³ ranges from 349/799 (43.7%, Gemma 4 E4B IT) to 465/799 (58.2%, DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813); three trials at temperature 0.7. | §4; Table 5 |
| Occasional success exceeds consistency | Pass@3 exceeds pass³ by 10.8–21.4 percentage points across models. GLM-5.3-Flash achieves 74.3% pass@3 versus 56.8% pass³. | Table 5; Figure 3 |
| No clear winner among leaders | The five leading models span 56.8–58.2% pass³. All ten paired bootstrap intervals include zero; exact McNemar tests are nonsignificant before and after Holm adjustment. | §5.1; Table 9 |
| Incorrect customer claims are difficult | On 142 wrong-information cases, no model exceeds 40.1% pass³; this is the lowest-scoring task axis for seven of eleven models. | §5.2; Table 11 |
| Response and execution checks detect different failures | Of 26,291 trajectories with both decisions available, 3,390 (12.9%) pass safety/action but fail response; 1,497 (5.7%) show the reverse. | §5.2; Appendix B.2 |
| Semantic grading has measurable uncertainty | Judge–human response agreement is 31/38 decidable audit cases (81.6%; bootstrap 95% CI 68.4–92.1%), from a blinded 40-transcript sample. | Appendix A.4; Table 8 |

These results support complementary execution and response checks and show inconsistent success under the reported protocol. They do not establish production reliability, equivalence among leading models, or the effectiveness of any proposed mitigation. The authors explicitly caution that case-based confidence intervals do not estimate performance on live banking traffic (Appendix B.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** English-only synthetic cases and deterministic mocks omit many production policies, operational failures, languages, accessibility needs, and authorization/fraud systems (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Three trials provide limited reliability estimation; endpoint behavior and noncomparable provider reasoning controls can affect results (§8; Table 4).
- **Author-stated:** The judge audit is small and excludes three later-added models. Possible evaluator-family bias remains unresolved (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Case snapshots differ in fictional profile text despite reportedly equivalent semantics. Seventy-six trajectories lack saved ambiguous-confirmation results for cross-stage analysis (§4; Appendix B.2).
- **Analyst inference:** First-failure counts cannot identify the full distribution of co-occurring errors. Likewise, the taxonomy’s separate screening sample does not establish pattern prevalence in the headline evaluation (§§5.2, 6.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Rankings depend partly on authored case weighting: equal task-axis weighting swaps two adjacent model pairs (§5.2).

## Open research questions
- **How sensitive are scores to evaluator choice?** The small audit and possible family bias leave this unresolved. Next experiment: rescore a larger stratified sample with independent judges and blinded human adjudication.
- **Do targeted interventions improve both action and response reliability?** The paper diagnoses failures but does not test remedies. Next experiment: compare a fixed baseline against evidence-refresh and confirmation-management interventions using unchanged cases and grading.
- **How well do findings transfer beyond the benchmark?** English scripts and deterministic mocks limit external validity. Next experiment: evaluate matched multilingual cases and environments with controlled tool failures, then compare failure patterns.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in summarizing the reported benchmark and results; **medium** in fine-grained model comparisons and evaluator validity.
