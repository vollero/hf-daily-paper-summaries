# TrackEverything: Long Horizon Dense Tracking via De-Duplicating 3D Scene Representations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30222) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30222)


## One-sentence takeaway
TrackEverything combines persistent voxelized scene tracks with selective trajectory decoding to enable dense tracking over long videos, achieving competitive benchmark accuracy while remaining dependent on geometry quality and potentially irreversible track merges (§§3–5).

## Problem and motivation
Dense tracking repeatedly represents the same surfaces in successive frames, increasing memory and computation. The authors identify a gap between sparse trackers that support long sequences and dense trackers restricted to first-frame points or short clips. Their objective is to track newly revealed surfaces throughout a video, with active representation size governed by observed scene geometry rather than repeated observations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Persistent scene de-duplication:** Process video in temporal windows, advance points to a shared boundary timestep, then voxelize and mean-pool co-located tracks. New observations join this persistent world-coordinate representation (§§3.1, 3.3).
- **Endpoints before trajectories:** Refine destination positions and classify points as static or dynamic; decode within-window trajectories only for dynamic points (§3.2).
- **3D WAFT:** Sample source and projected target image features to guide iterative refinement without constructing dense 4D correlation volumes (§3.2.1).
- **Supporting infrastructure:** Record merge lineage and spatial residuals for historical trajectory export; provide an optional early merge of static points. The authors also describe PointOdyssey annotation corrections intended for release with their code (§§A.2, A.4–A.5).

The method consumes RGB video, externally supplied world-coordinate geometry, and camera calibration for projection. Its contribution is tracking and representation efficiency; downstream robotics or foundation-model benefits are proposed rather than evaluated.

These results support an efficiency advantage and competitive sampled-point accuracy. They do not establish uniformly superior tracking: D4RT scores higher overall on short clips, and some baselines lead on individual datasets or metrics. TAPVid-3D evaluates sparse annotations, while synthetic dense evaluations and visualizations provide complementary evidence. Geometry, training data, model size, and tracking scope differ across several comparisons (§4.1; §A.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Memory still grows during exploration of new scenes; geometry errors affect tracking; spatial mean-pooling can permanently merge distinct surfaces (§5).
- **Author-stated:** Dynamic points classified as static remain stationary for that window, although classification is reconsidered later (§A.4).
- **Evaluation scope:** PointOdyssey scalability experiments involve unequal training exposure across methods, explicitly acknowledged by the authors. Corrected annotations still contain noise, and uniform application of corrections across baseline evaluations is not fully specified (§4.3; §A.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Sparse benchmark queries cannot fully validate dense merge fidelity because explicit query points are exempt from voxelization (§§3.4, 4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Reconstructing historical tracks with stored constant offsets does not demonstrate recovery of independent motion after an erroneous merge; this limits the interpretation of “exact inverse unvoxelization” (§A.4 versus §5).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** The main text cites strong classifier F1 values without distinguishing classes; §A.1 identifies PointOdyssey dynamic F1 as **78.5%** and static F1 as **92.5%** for full-length evaluation.

## Open research questions
- **Can merging remain reversible?** Permanent collapse is an acknowledged failure mode. Compare mean-pooling against appearance-aware or reversible merging on close-contact scenes, measuring identity preservation and memory (§5).
- **Can memory remain bounded during continuous exploration?** New geometry still accumulates. Test spatial eviction and later reactivation on extended exploration with revisits (§5).
- **How robust is selective decoding to subtle or intermittent motion?** False-static predictions suppress motion within a window. Sweep motion thresholds and dynamic-scene proportions, measuring dynamic-point accuracy and runtime (§A.4).
- **Does long-horizon dense accuracy transfer to real scenes?** Real-world quantitative evaluation uses sparse labels. Evaluate dense tracks on controlled, sensor-grounded long captures, separately scoring merged and protected query points (§4.1; §A.1).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text includes detailed methods, ablations, and supplementary results, but leaves material gaps:

- Confirmed availability of code, weights, and dataset corrections.
- Repeated-run uncertainty estimates.
- Precise settings for some ablations and geometry used in the full-length PointOdyssey result.
- Host-memory and exported-history storage costs.
- Complete loss specifications, including the confidence-loss term introduced in §A.4.
- Quantitative long-horizon real-world dense correspondence and merge-error evaluation.
