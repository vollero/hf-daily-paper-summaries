# Disaggregated Quantization: Specializing LLM Prefill and Decode

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26333) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26333)


## One-sentence takeaway

Phase-specific quantization and trained prefill weights improve low-bit model accuracy, while SSD-streamed prefill achieves a reported 1.78× time-to-first-token speedup for Qwen3.8-27B at 8K context without additional device weight residency (Table 5; §3.2).

## Problem and motivation

Prefill benefits from hardware-native low-precision computation; batch-one decode benefits from compact weights that reduce memory traffic. Applying the same quantization pathway to both phases can sacrifice accuracy or speed. The authors address how to jointly optimize these different representations, preserve their cache compatibility, and accommodate an additional prefill checkpoint on a single device (§§1–2).

The paper acknowledges prior phase-specific precision and prefill adaptation work; its claimed contribution is a unified training and serving approach combining native low-precision prefill with compressed weight-only decode (§4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Quantization-aware distillation with disaggregation (QADD):** Uses instruction-training masks to route prompt and assistant tokens through different linear-layer pathways. Response-only teacher distillation trains both pathways through prompt cache representations in one forward-backward pass (§2.1; Appendix A.1).
- **Format and weight specialization:** Format disaggregation retains shared weights but removes activation quantization during decode. Full disaggregation trains separate NVFP4 prefill weights alongside low-bit decode weights (§§2.3–2.4).
- **Frozen-decoder prefillers:** Trains a prefiller around an existing quantized decoder without updating that decoder or requiring its original quantization pipeline (§2.6).
- **Offloaded disaggregated prefill (ODP):** Streams prefill blocks from SSD using two buffers borrowed from temporarily unused decode-weight memory, overlapping loading with computation and restoring displaced weights before generation (§2.5).

## Evidence and main results

Here, **DH** averages GSM8K, MATH-500 and MMLU-Pro, including both reasoning modes for Qwen 3; **PH** averages RULER tasks at 4K–32K contexts. Core results average late checkpoints and model sizes within each family (§3.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Format disaggregation improves accuracy with unchanged weight storage | NVFP4 DH accuracy: Qwen 3 **61.8→63.7%**; Gemma 3 **51.9→55.0%**. Prefill timings remain unchanged; batch-one decode speedups over BF16 increase **2.86→2.93×** on Qwen3-8B and **3.18→3.27×** on Gemma3-12B. | Table 1; Appendix C.2 |
| Separate prefill weights substantially improve nominal 2-bit decoding | Against 2-bit weight-only: Qwen 3 DH **38.4→45.5%**, PH **64.1→76.6%**; Gemma 3 DH **33.7→38.2%**, PH **52.4→61.3%**. | Table 1 |
| ODP retains most prefill acceleration | At 16K context on DGX Spark, streamed transformer-stack prefill is **1.47×** faster than BF16 for Qwen3-8B and **1.58×** for Gemma3-12B, without extra device weight residency. | Tables 1, 10; Appendix C.1 |
| A trained prefiller rescues an extremely compressed frozen decoder | Qwen3.8-27B IQ1_S: MMLU-Pro **29.04→61.54%**; MMMU-Pro **24.39→59.65%**. One evaluation per arm after text-only prefiller training. | Table 5; Appendix A.5 |
| Practical TTFT improves for sufficiently long prompts | Qwen3.8-27B IQ1_S in llama.cpp on DGX Spark: **12.27→6.90 seconds**, or **1.78×**, at 8K context; ODP is slower for short prompts. | §3.2; Figure 1; Appendix C.1 |
| Large-model PTQ gains are modest and non-universal | Format disaggregation improves **11/13** model–benchmark point estimates; **6** gains meet per-comparison **p<0.05**, with no significant degradations, across models up to **2.8T** parameters. No multiple-testing correction. | §3.3; Table 2; Appendix A.5 |

The evidence supports useful accuracy–latency tradeoffs, especially for highly compressed decoders and longer prompts. It does not establish universal improvements: some higher-bit prefiller configurations lose accuracy, and unchanged per-token decode computation does not guarantee unchanged total generation cost (Tables 5, 8).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated scope:** Highly batched serving, multi-turn conversations and agentic behavior are untested. Rebuilding assistant-token caches through prefill may differ from retaining decode-produced caches (§5). ODP is less attractive for short prompts and MoE models (§2.5).
- **Timing qualifications:** Core prefill measurements exclude several operations outside the transformer stack. Weight-only prefill uses BF16 as its timing reference; LUT prefill timings omit conversion overhead, and non-disaggregated LUT decode overhead is approximated rather than measured with complete autocast (Appendices B.3, C.1).
- **Evaluation backend:** The 27B accuracy experiments use GGUF weights dequantized to BF16 on GB300. Encoded model sizes and native llama.cpp timing therefore describe different execution settings (Appendix A.5).
- **Statistical limits:** Core error bars measure checkpoint variation within one training run, not independent training seeds. Main 27B results have no repeat averaging; PTQ significance is uncorrected for multiple comparisons (Appendix A.5).
- **Training and attribution:** Full disaggregation adds checkpoint storage and optimizer state. The authors acknowledge that their extra-parameter ablation does not isolate weight specialization from higher prefill precision; frozen-decoder RTN comparisons provide partial evidence that training itself matters (Appendices A.1, B.1, B.4).
- **Analyst observation:** §3.2 reports a 5.3-point Qwen LUT2 PH gain over non-disaggregated inference, whereas Table 1 implies **15.3 points** from **61.3→76.6%**. The table is used above.
- **Data validity:** The 27B training corpus has limited detected MMLU-Pro overlap; the authors explicitly do not establish general freedom from benchmark contamination (Appendix A.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Does DQ remain reliable across cache policies in multi-turn use?** This is explicitly untested (§5). Compare retained, rebuilt and partially reused caches on identical conversations.
2. **Where does ODP remain beneficial across hardware and concurrency?** Measurements focus on DGX Spark and batch-one decode. Sweep SSD bandwidth, prompt length and serving concurrency while measuring full-request latency and memory.
3. **Can one prefiller serve several quantized decoders reliably?** Cross-decoder transfer is mixed, and the training-matched prefiller is not always best (Table 7). Compare decoder-specific training with training against a mixture of frozen decoders.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Detailed methods and tables support the main findings, but generalization is constrained by timing approximations, narrow serving conditions and limited replication.

Material missing information includes independent-seed QADD results, repeated main 27B evaluations, multi-turn and high-concurrency measurements, complete LUT autocast timings, and direct URLs or version identifiers for the claimed released artifacts.
