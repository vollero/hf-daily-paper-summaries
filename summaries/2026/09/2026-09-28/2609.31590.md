# AgentWorld: Benchmarking Long-Horizon Collaboration of Multi-agent LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31590) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31590)


## One-sentence takeaway
AgentWorld introduces a collaboration benchmark and action-contribution metric, with Gemini 3 Flash achieving the highest main-set success rate at 52.0%, although the results do not fully isolate collaboration from planning and resource constraints (§4, Table 2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that existing benchmarks emphasize short interactions, competitive settings, or low-level control, making sustained collaboration difficult to measure. AgentWorld targets joint planning, communication, and resource sharing among agents with complementary capabilities and no access to teammates’ internal states (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and environment:** A Kaetram-based MMORPG sandbox provides structured text observations and 13 high-level tools. The benchmark contains 100 human-designed tasks and 100 augmented variants, with 3–20 agents and round budgets of 25–55. Agents act sequentially and coordinate through explicit messages (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Collaboration metric:** Causal Collaboration Effectiveness (CCE) traces backward from success actions using LLM judgments, then divides contributing actions by total actions. Main results assign failed tasks zero CCE; Python verifiers separately measure success and checkpoint completion (§3.5, Appendix F).
- **Evaluation and assets:** Model comparisons, communication baselines, design ablations, and judge agreement analyses accompany an author-reported release of the simulator, task definitions, verifiers, evaluation scripts, and annotation platform (§§3–4, Appendix C).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Main-set performance remains limited | On 100 main tasks, SR / PSR / mean CCE: Gemini 52.0% / 71.5% / 0.320; Claude Haiku 45.0% / 62.7% / 0.294; GPT-5 Mini 36.0% / 62.6% / 0.206; DeepSeek 20.0% / 43.5% / 0.125. | Table 2 |
| Augmentation reduces primary-model success | On 100 augmented tasks, SR falls to 24.0%, 26.0%, 21.0%, and 10.0%, respectively, for those same models. | Table 2 |
| Communication access improves reported success | Gemini SR: no communication 22.9%; human shared plan without communication 17.6%; vanilla team 52.0%; oracle communication 60.0%. Exact baseline task coverage is unspecified. | Table 3; §4.4 |
| Success-conditioned efficiency changes the leader | Mean CCE on successful tasks is 0.653 for Claude versus 0.609 for Gemini. | §4.3 |
| CCE has partial human and cross-judge support | One annotator agrees with GPT-4.1 on 69/84 labels across 11 tasks: 82% agreement, κ = 0.64. The four primary models retain their CCE ranking under three judges. | §4.3; Table 6 |
| Documentation and available rounds matter | On a 35-task Gemini subset, SR is 54.3% in the full setting, 29.4% without documentation, and 37.1% when the round budget is shortened from 50 to 10. | Table 4; §4.4 |
| Stale messages dominate categorized communication errors | Among 61 categorized errors in Gemini traces from 100 tasks, 23 are stale or redundant (37.7%); this is not a percentage of all messages. | §4.5; Table 5 |

These results support difficulty under the tested protocol and benefits from communication access. **Analyst interpretation:** mean CCE is a task-averaged, success-weighted measure; 0.320 does not establish that exactly 32% of all pooled actions were useful. Likewise, cross-model chat counts do not establish an optimal communication rate, and incomplete checkpoints alone do not locate failures specifically at late-stage coordination.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-acknowledged:** Inclusive causal labeling makes CCE an intended upper bound on useful-action contribution. Three repeated runs on one model and one subset do not establish broader stability or statistical significance (§§4.3–4.4).
- **Analyst inference—metric validity:** CCE combines success and action efficiency without directly measuring balanced participation or requiring cross-agent dependencies. Appendix F introduces per-agent contribution, but aggregate results do not report it.
- **Analyst inference—horizon and scope:** The abstract and Table 1 emphasize “50+” rounds, whereas detailed specifications give maximum budgets of 25–55; successful examples finish much earlier. Generalization to asynchronous teams or professional tasks is untested (§3.4; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference—comparison clarity:** The single-agent baseline consolidates roles and items, changing the capability allocation. Its lower score supports an advantage for the tested team setup, not universal impossibility of single-agent solutions (§4.4).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Main-text CCE assigns every failed task zero, while Appendix F specifies zero when no success criteria are met. Pairwise tracing permits same-round dependencies, but the supplied roundwise prompt references later rounds; handling of these dependencies is unclear.
- **Limited validation:** Human CCE validation uses one annotator; detailed augmentation validation samples only 10% of variants. Additional Table 2 models use an augmented v1 split, and Gemma results are provisional (§4.3; Appendix D; Table 2).

## Open research questions
- **Does CCE capture collaboration beyond task efficiency?** Participation and cross-agent dependence are not directly reflected in its scalar score. Compare teams with matched success and action counts but different dependency structures against multiple human raters.
- **Which communication policy improves outcomes?** Chat-volume findings are observational. Randomize communication budgets and progress-reporting protocols while holding model, tasks, and action budgets fixed.
- **Do failures persist when resources are matched?** Shorter horizons and missing documentation substantially affect success. Compare team and consolidated-agent setups under matched total action budgets, then vary sequential versus asynchronous execution.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark and reported scores are clearly described, but causal interpretations and comparison details remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes baseline task coverage, comprehensive model-version and inference settings, token/cost budgets, multi-model uncertainty estimates, exact CCE pruning/window parameters, resolution of CCE procedural inconsistencies, and executable assets beyond the supplied excerpts.
