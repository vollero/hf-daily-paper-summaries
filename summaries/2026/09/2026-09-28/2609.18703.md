# RayOrch: Programming and Executing Lineage-Controlled Multi-Grain Dataflows for Foundation-Model Data Preparation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18703) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18703)


## One-sentence takeaway
RayOrch makes parent–child structure explicit in distributed data pipelines, enabling cross-parent batching and independent ordered reconstruction while reporting lower document-processing times than the evaluated Ray Data and Daft implementations (§§4, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Document and video preparation repeatedly expands sources into variable numbers of pages, regions, or clips. Efficient GPU batches mix children from different parents, but reconstruction still requires correct ownership, order, and completion tracking.

The authors identify a representation gap: coarse source-level jobs hide parallelism, while flat records leave structural bookkeeping to applications. Their evaluated flat pipelines also introduce global regrouping barriers that delay completed parents (§§1–2, Table 1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Explicit structural programming model:** Compiler-validated expansion/gather pairs describe finite, acyclic hierarchies. `F.expand` creates ordered children; `F.reduce` reconstructs each parent from surviving results (§§4.1–4.2).
- **Runtime-owned lineage and scheduling:** The engine maintains child membership, immutable ordinals, and terminal outcomes. Per-Call FIFO queues batch ready work across parents while allowing each completed parent to advance independently (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Commit and failure semantics:** Generation checks reject stale or duplicate execution reports. Typed parent-scoped failures suppress undispatched siblings for one Call and parent; in-flight siblings cannot commit, while unrelated parents continue (§4.4).
- **Ray-based implementation:** Persistent actor pools execute model stages, while the driver manages structural state. Adapters cover MinerU, Docling, and a Qwen2.5-VL video pipeline (§5).

The contribution is the integrated structural execution contract, rather than a new parsing or vision-language model.

| Video scaling advantage is modest over Ray Data | On 27,091 videos / 104,952 clips, scaling from 8 to 64 H20 GPUs gives 7.82× for RayOrch, 7.60× for Ray Data, and 6.24× for Daft; respective 64-GPU processing times are 0.42, 0.43, and 0.53 h. | Table 4, §6.2, Figure 6 |
| Lower Docling E2E time | On 2,000 PDFs and 4 H20 GPUs: 9,489.07 s versus 11,298.75 s for Ray Data and 12,214.70 s for Docling Serve; reported reductions of 16.0% and 22.3%. | §6.2, Table 6 |
| FIFO adds benefit beyond rebatching | On 368 PDFs / 7,072 pages and 4 H20 GPUs, holding lineage, commit, OCR UDF, and batch limit fixed: 634.1 → 579.3 s, an 8.6% reduction. | Table 4, §6.4, Table 7 |
| Typed failures avoid sibling work | On a 45,507-page trace with 4 H20 GPUs and a fixed 50-ms page UDF, poisoning page zero of the 99 largest parents prevents 6,241 sibling executions. Mean wall-time reduction is 14.93% against matched clean RayOrch runs; all expected healthy outputs are preserved. | §6.5, Table 8 |

The results support efficiency gains on the tested implementations. Overlapping stage windows are consistent with the authors’ explanation that local parent completion removes regrouping tails (§6.3, Figure 7). Scaling and cold E2E results come from separate run series and should not be conflated. Failure-study savings compare poisoned runs with clean runs, not equal amounts of successful work. No downstream model-quality improvement is demonstrated.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Stated scope:** Gathers operate within source microbatches; general joins, cross-microbatch windows, and feedback are unsupported (§1).
- **Conditional semantics:** Schedule independence assumes fixed per-Grain outcomes. Byte-identical payloads additionally require UDF invariance to batch shape, input order, and randomness (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** Gains depend partly on the evaluated baselines’ global regrouping designs; the paper does not establish that every alternative implementation requires those barriers (§§1, 6.3).
- **Analyst inference—failure generality:** Failures target the largest parents at their first page using a fixed-cost UDF. Later, randomly distributed, or real model failures could produce different savings (§6.5).
- **Missing validation:** The supplied evaluation does not quantify driver metadata overhead or isolate retry/generation-fencing performance. Main performance comparisons lack reported variability estimates; repeated pairs are specified for the failure study (§6).

## Open research questions
1. **How does lineage-state overhead scale with hierarchy size?** Driver-managed metadata is described without memory or scheduling-overhead measurements. Sweep fan-out, nesting depth, and concurrent microbatch size while measuring driver memory and dispatch latency.
2. **How sensitive are containment savings to failure location?** The experiment poisons first pages of the largest parents. Vary failure position, parent size, and UDF cost, then measure avoided work and healthy-parent latency.
3. **How much advantage remains against incremental parent-aware baselines?** Tested baselines regroup globally. Compare implementations with equivalent local completion semantics, model settings, and resource allocations to separate representation benefits from scheduling effects.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The design and reported measurements are explicit, but broader generalization remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes corpus provenance and access, complete baseline tuning and resource configurations, uncertainty estimates for main timing results, lineage-memory measurements, detailed correctness-test results, and empirical actor-failure recovery tests. Code availability is claimed in the paper but was not independently verified.
