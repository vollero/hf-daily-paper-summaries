# Evidence-Grounded Auditing of Identification Assumptions in Climate-Policy Causal Evaluations

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30867) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30867)


## One-sentence takeaway
ARGUS detects planted identification flaws better than keyword scoring, but its real-paper pilot exposes substantial retrieval failures and excessive severity, leaving climate-policy and expert-review usefulness unvalidated (§§5–7).

## Problem and motivation
Difference-in-differences (DID) evaluations depend on assumptions whose support is difficult to assess across prose, diagnostics, and policy details. Merely finding an event study or robustness check does not establish that it adequately supports identification. The authors address this gap by auditing reported evidence, while explicitly avoiding judgments about the true causal effect (§1).

Climate policy motivates the task because overlapping interventions, staggered implementation, anticipation, spillovers, and measurement changes complicate identification; the real-paper evaluation nevertheless uses general economics papers (Appendices A, H).

## Approach and contributions
- **Operational rubric and auditor:** Eleven dimensions connect assumptions or design requirements to observable implications and expected evidence. ARGUS uses lexical retrieval, an LLM relevance gate, and a separate adequacy judgment, producing evidence-linked risk reports. Failed retrieval yields `unknown`; partial relevance still permits assessment (§3).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Structural deletions and flawed-design rewrites provide known target dimensions without embedding the keyword detector’s negative phrases. Architecture ablations and a shared-evidence control investigate which components explain detection gains (§4; Appendix K).
- **Failure diagnosis and calibration:** A reconciled human pilot distinguishes retrieval failures from excessive severity. A deterministic rule demotes high-risk judgments supported only by weak retrieval; its improvement is explicitly in-sample (§§5.4–5.6).

The evaluated system follows fixed control flow, uses text and captions, and does not exercise its proposed figure-parsing or policy-lookup tools (§3.2).

The strongest controlled evidence supports adequacy scoring over keyword presence on these fixtures. It does not establish population-level architecture superiority, reliable human agreement, causal validity, or climate-policy usefulness. False-alarm estimates repeatedly reuse one clean fixture rather than independent clean papers (Appendices G, K).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated scope limits:** The rubric, injector, and synthetic fixture are co-designed; independently authored flaws, interacting flaws, other causal designs, and dedicated climate-policy evaluations remain untested (Limitations).
- **Authors’ stated reference-label limits:** The pilot has two annotators rather than a DID-methodologist panel, only two high-risk labels, and 23/55 cells assessed through analogues of DID assumptions. Its predominantly medium labels make exact agreement lenient (§5.4; Limitations).
- **Authors’ stated calibration limits:** Rules were fixed before the replacement human labels arrived but developed on the same papers using earlier labels. Leave-one-paper-out analysis checks recurrence, not held-out calibration; cell-level uncertainty ignores nesting within papers (§5.6; Appendices G, J).
- **Authors’ stated retrieval and reproducibility limits:** Visual evidence enters only through captions; retrieval settings were not swept. Earlier run results were not reproduced, with the cause unresolved (Appendices D, F).
- **Analyst inference:** `Unknown` and high-risk labels cannot be interpreted as interchangeable evidence of a deficient paper: the pilot shows human-retrievable evidence behind many abstentions, and weak retrieval frequently produces high-risk judgments (§§5.3–5.6).

## Open research questions
1. **Does ARGUS generalize to its intended domain?** No dedicated climate-policy corpus was evaluated. Next experiment: audit independently selected climate-policy DID papers against blinded methodologist labels.
2. **Can improved retrieval reduce both abstention and excessive severity?** Caption-only retrieval and partial-match assessment leave their contributions unresolved. Next experiment: hold the adequacy judge fixed while comparing current retrieval, expanded text retrieval, and figure/table access using human-marked evidence locations.
3. **Does calibration transfer beyond a medium-heavy pilot?** Existing gains are in-sample. Next experiment: freeze the weak-retrieval rule and evaluate on unseen papers with broader severity coverage, reporting agreement, high-risk discrimination, and paper-level uncertainty.
4. **Can it detect independently constructed and interacting flaws?** Current injections target one dimension within a co-designed fixture. Next experiment: use expert-authored flaws across multiple clean studies, including multi-dimension perturbations.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in this account of the supplied text; confidence in deployment effectiveness remains low.

Material evidence unavailable includes held-out calibration results, dedicated climate-policy validation, expert-review utility measurements, independent multi-fixture flaw tests, and an explanation for historical run discrepancies. The paper reports releasing code, prompts, and data; those external artifacts were not inspected.
