# MOPD-Router: Rethinking Teacher Routing in Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30837) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30837)


## One-sentence takeaway
ExpertAlign routes token-level supervision using alignment between each teacher’s post-training specialization and its correction to the student, achieving the highest overall score in all four tested Qwen3 distillation settings without domain labels (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation transfers specialist capabilities through supervision on student-generated responses. Conventional routing assigns one teacher to an entire response using a prompt’s domain label. The authors identify two problems: mixed training data may lack reliable labels, and a single response may benefit from different specialists at different positions (§1).

The research gap is how to select and weight useful teacher signals at each token without domain labels or a separately trained router.

## Approach and contributions
- **MOPD-Router framework:** Combines per-teacher sampled-token log-probability advantages using context-dependent weights, then applies a clipped policy update. Its plug-in interface accommodates different routing metrics (§§3.1–3.2).
- **ExpertAlign metric:** On the student’s top-16 candidate tokens, compares the teacher’s log-probability shift from its shared pre-RL base with its proposed correction to the student. Teachers passing a positive-alignment gate receive cosine-proportional weights; positions with no retained teacher are skipped (§3.3, Equations 8–11).
- **Controlled empirical analysis:** Compares ExpertAlign with confidence-based Entropy, shared-support discrepancy-based Novelty, uniform aggregation, and label-based baselines. Ablations separately examine teacher selection, weighting, token-level allocation, and cross-domain supervision (§4.3). The authors report releasing the framework’s code (p. 1; §1).

## Evidence and main results
“Overall” is the unweighted mean of nine benchmark metrics: mathematics Avg@8 accuracy, code Pass@1, and instruction-following prompt-level accuracy using five completions per instance (§4.1). Score differences below are percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best overall results on unlabeled data | With three 4B teachers, ExpertAlign scores **38.58** for the 1.7B student and **53.76** for the 4B student; Mean scores 34.49/47.88, Standard MOPD 36.33/48.76, and Open-MOPD 34.02/49.41. Label-based baselines use generated labels. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Best overall results on labeled data | ExpertAlign scores **40.19/54.58** for 1.7B/4B students, versus Standard MOPD 37.52/50.63 and Open-MOPD 39.08/52.37, while ignoring labels. | Table 2 |
| Evaluation uncertainty generally supports gains | Paired item bootstrap with Holm correction finds gains over Mean and Standard MOPD significant in every setting. Against Open-MOPD, labeled 1.7B yields +1.11 points, 95% CI [−0.6, 2.8], adjusted p=.208. | Appendix D.1; Table 9 |
| Training-seed evidence supports the labeled 4B result | Across three seeds, Overall is **54.69±0.12**, versus 50.94±0.34 for Standard MOPD and 52.81±0.38 for Open-MOPD; ± denotes sample standard deviation. | Appendix D.2; Table 10 |
| Selection and weighting both contribute | Positive-alignment selection with uniform weights improves Overall over Mean in all four settings; cosine weighting further improves every domain average in all four. | Table 3 |
| Cross-domain access and token-level allocation help | On labeled data, full ExpertAlign scores 40.19/54.58 versus 37.79/51.83 with domain-restricted teachers and 36.57/52.61 with response-level weights. | Table 4; §4.3.4 |
| Additional supervision has measurable cost | On eight H20 GPUs in labeled same-size training, ExpertAlign requires **716.9 GPU-hours** versus 692.9 for Standard MOPD; token-normalized end-to-end cost is **1.18×**. Response lengths differ. | Table 5; §4.4 |

These comparisons support useful label-free routing within the tested setup. The ablations support contributions from cross-domain supervision and token-dependent weighting, but do not establish that alignment universally measures teacher expertise. The nonsignificant Open-MOPD comparison does not establish statistical equivalence, and the teacher reference combines domain-specific teachers rather than representing one unified model (§4.1; Appendix D).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments use three same-family Qwen3 specialists and two student sizes. Larger or heterogeneous teacher pools remain untested; ExpertAlign relies on a shared pre-RL base (Appendix A).
- **Author-stated design limitation:** Routing metrics follow empirical intuition rather than a unified theoretical framework (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—limited robustness coverage:** Training-seed repeats cover only labeled same-size distillation. Bootstrap intervals in other settings measure evaluation uncertainty conditional on a trained checkpoint (Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—support-size selection:** The authors select top-k using Overall in the labeled 4B setting; a separate tuning set is not specified. Moreover, k=1 switches from cosine to product weighting, so that comparison changes more than support size (Appendix E.4).
- **Analyst inference—comparison and cost boundaries:** Generated baseline labels have imperfect human agreement, although results also hold on originally labeled data. Cost measurements cover one setting and exclude initialization, downloads, validation, and checkpoint I/O (§4.4; Appendices C–D).

## Open research questions
- **Does alignment work across different teacher bases?** The shared-base setup leaves this unresolved. Test heterogeneous specialists using their individual pre-RL checkpoints, as suggested in Appendix A.
- **Does the benefit survive larger teacher pools?** Only three specialists are tested. Expand the pool with overlapping and weaker specialists, measuring accuracy, routing behavior, and token-normalized cost.
- **Can probability-aware alignment reduce sensitivity to tail tokens?** Performance drops at k=32, which the authors attribute to noisy low-probability log-ratios. Compare probability-weighted alignment with fixed-support cosine routing using a separate tuning split (Appendix E.4).
- **How reproducible are gains outside labeled same-size training?** Repeat the remaining settings across training seeds, especially the labeled 1.7B comparison with Open-MOPD (Appendix D).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-setting findings; broader generalization remains unestablished.

Material unavailable information includes heterogeneous or larger-pool results, training-seed variation for the other three settings, a clearly separate support-size tuning split, the precise uncertainty procedure behind Figure 4’s error bars, and independently verified contents of the reported code release.
