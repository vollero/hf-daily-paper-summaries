# SAGE: Mitigating Long-Horizon Reasoning Biases via Topological Guidance

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30192) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30192)


## One-sentence takeaway
SAGE combines algebraic and hyperbolic guidance during LLM post-training to improve reported reasoning accuracy and verified symbolic success, but its guarantees depend on structural assumptions and several headline claims exceed the tabulated evidence.

## Problem and motivation
The authors identify two failures in long reasoning chains: exploration favors locally plausible but unproductive branches, while sparse terminal rewards allow early deviations to accumulate. They argue that existing process supervision is expensive and outcome-based methods leave the structure of successful trajectories implicit (§§1–2).

The intended gap is training useful intermediate guidance without gold reasoning traces or dense correctness labels—not eliminating task-specific structural knowledge.

## Approach and contributions
- **Symbolic Closure Analysis (SCA):** Defines a prefix-closed region of locally admissible trajectories and analyzes feasible-region volume, gradient variance, and reference-policy anchoring under rare terminal rewards. Local admissibility explicitly does not guarantee success (§3; Appendix A).
- **Two structural potentials:** Algebraic sparsification scores how well an operator’s subspace explains the unresolved residual; hyperbolic guidance scores proximity to a target anchor in a Poincaré embedding (§4.1).
- **Training integration:** The potentials reweight candidate reasoning steps and augment rewards in a KL-regularized, group-relative policy update. Symbolic tasks use explicit structure; other tasks use frozen encoders, learned probes, operator taxonomies, and prompt-conditioned anchors. Inference uses the trained policy directly (§4.2; Appendix E).

The contribution is the combined structural framework and its evaluation. The sparse-recovery result is explicitly a standard OMP guarantee, not a new general reasoning guarantee (Appendix C).

The tables support aggregate improvements and complementary contributions from the two potentials. They do not establish universal superiority, resolution of the Andrews–Curtis problem, or a general guarantee of successful long-horizon reasoning. The geometry and anchor controls support the proposed design, but do not uniquely establish the authors’ causal explanation of the two biases.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Non-symbolic residuals, anchors, and subspaces are approximate learned priors; their quality limits transfer. Candidate scoring, probing, and distance computation add training overhead, which is not quantified (Appendices I–J).
- **Theoretical scope:** Feasible-support concentration requires a separating potential and analyzes idealized trajectory reweighting. Local validity can coexist with terminal failure (Proposition 4.2; Appendices A/D). **Analyst inference:** this does not by itself explain elimination of locally valid but unproductive branches or guarantee the implemented finite-candidate sampler’s behavior.
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** The paper alternates between 12 benchmarks/7 models and 13 benchmarks/8 models (§1, §5, checklist). Section 5.2 gives Qwen3.6-27B ARC-C as **58.11%**, whereas Tables 2/5 report **57.11%**.
- **Baseline interpretation:** Section 5.4 says GRPO-PRM improves over GRPO, but Table 4(a) shows improvement only on Lean, with lower Olympiad and BBH-H accuracy.
- **Reproducibility:** Appendix E describes procedures but leaves numerical training settings and parts of anchor/projection construction unspecified. Five-seed variability is supplied for natural reasoning, not the mathematics or AC tables. Comparable rollout budgets do not establish equal total training compute.

## Open research questions
1. **Do structural scores predict eventual success among locally valid trajectories?** Feasibility does not imply success (Appendix A). Next experiment: measure score calibration against verified completion rates, stratified by depth and local validity.
2. **How robust are learned priors outside their training distribution?** Non-symbolic guarantees remain conditional (Appendices E/J). Next experiment: transfer frozen probes and anchors to held-out task families and systematically perturb anchor quality.
3. **What is the gain at equal total compute?** Training overhead is acknowledged but unmeasured (Appendix I). Next experiment: compare SAGE, GRPO, and GRPO-PRM under matched GPU-hours, including candidate generation and auxiliary-model fitting.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and tabulated results are sufficiently detailed for a qualified assessment, but inconsistent reporting limits confidence in broad claims.

Material missing information includes Figure 3’s numerical results, complete training hyperparameters and data splits, precise anchor/projection fitting procedures, mathematics/AC uncertainty estimates, measured training overhead, and independently verified release contents.
