# SLCA-GRPO: Resolving Cross-Segment Credit Misattribution in Tool-Calling RL

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29050) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29050)


## One-sentence takeaway
SLCA-GRPO separately normalizes and routes tool-execution and summary rewards, improving matched GRPO results across three backbones while providing a per-update advantage-isolation guarantee rather than global optimization guarantees (§3.3; Tables 1, 17–18).

## Problem and motivation
Tool-calling agents interleave reasoning and API calls with final natural-language responses. Standard GRPO assigns every generated token an advantage derived from the combined trajectory reward, so a good summary can reinforce poor tool use—or a poor summary can penalize correct execution. The authors call this **cross-segment credit misattribution** and argue that denser rewards alone do not eliminate it when rewards are aggregated before advantage estimation (§§1–2).

The identified gap is structural credit assignment within one policy: separating execution from articulation without additional intermediate-state rollouts or a separate summarizer.

## Approach and contributions
- **Segment-locked estimator:** Environment-token masks identify earlier reasoning/tool segments and the final response block. Tool and summary rewards are normalized separately within the rollout group and applied only to their corresponding tokens in a PPO-style objective (§§3.1–3.5).
- **Formal characterization:** The authors prove that the routed tool-gradient component has no direct dependence on summary rewards within an update. Conditional noise-removal and directional results use a local estimator with explicit assumptions; they do not establish unconditional variance reduction for fully normalized GRPO (App. A.2).
- **Supporting infrastructure and evaluation:** HierR supplies execution scores and judged summary quality; SGLS combines schema validation with frozen LLM response simulation. These support the central estimator contribution, alongside matched comparisons, support controls, reward sweeps, and mocker replacement checks (§3; Apps. C–D).

The strongest evidence is the matched GRPO comparison, which holds initialization, rewards, simulation, data, and rollout budget fixed. Support controls further support blocking summary rewards from tool-token updates, but do not establish that blocking both directions is optimal. Results demonstrate higher reported means, not universal domain-level superiority or statistical significance. Efficiency and gradient-stability claims rely mainly on representative single-run traces rather than measured end-to-end cost savings (Figures 2, 5; Table 5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Clear segment boundaries and sufficiently informative execution rewards are required. Gold-trajectory matching can penalize alternative valid plans, and Toucan Success reuses that reward rather than an independent outcome label (§6).
- **Author-stated:** Summary advantages retain cross-state grouping bias; every tool token still receives one execution advantage. Temporal credit-assignment combinations, larger policies, and direct real-API comparisons remain untested (§6; App. A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The local theoretical result cannot establish globally better optimization; shared parameters permit cross-segment effects across future updates (App. A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Adapted ToolPO/RLTR comparisons are less controlled than the GRPO comparison. ToolPO’s reward sensitivity especially limits conclusions about additive estimators generally (App. C.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Large-tool-space results face survivor selection: at the largest tested candidate size, context filtering excludes substantial portions of the evaluation set, making cross-size comparisons difficult (Tables 13–14, 24).

## Open research questions
- **Is one-way isolation sufficient?** The support control slightly favors allowing execution rewards into summary updates on two benchmarks. Repeat this factorial experiment across backbones with controlled advantage magnitudes and more runs (Table 21).
- **Can structural and temporal credit assignment complement each other?** SLCA does not distinguish successful from failing actions within the tool segment. Test a combined estimator on tasks with delayed, localized tool failures (§6).
- **When does withholding outcome feedback hurt tool learning?** The method assumes execution rewards are sufficiently informative. Vary execution-reward noise and completeness on tasks with multiple valid plans, using independent task outcomes for evaluation (§2; §6).
- **Do benefits persist with real tools at larger scale?** Compare matched simulated and real-API training while measuring task success, latency, and total compute cost; these comparisons are absent (§6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the method description and reported benchmark comparisons; **medium** in the broader mechanism and efficiency conclusions.

Material missing information includes per-run raw results and significance tests, measured total training/inference costs, direct real-API validation, larger-policy experiments, and detailed judge configuration/prompt for the binary execution-reward experiment. Code and dataset links are supplied, but their contents and reproducibility are not established by the paper text alone.
