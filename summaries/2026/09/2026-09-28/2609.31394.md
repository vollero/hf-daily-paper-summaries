# InternW0-Δ: A World Action Model Bridging Predictive Dynamics and Actions with 20K+ Hours of Open Data

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-28.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31394) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31394)


## One-sentence takeaway
InternW0-Δ combines future-supervised predictive representations with action generation without online video rollout, reporting leading aggregate results on several manipulation benchmarks after task-specific post-training, but limited quantitative real-robot validation.

## Problem and motivation
Video prediction can encode scene dynamics, but robot control also needs task-relevant changes, geometry, motion, and instruction grounding. The authors address how to transfer these complementary priors into actions without the inference cost of generating future videos. They also tackle incompatible representations and data quality across robot, human, and UMI demonstrations (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Directed world–action architecture:** A pretrained Wan video expert and an ActionDiT action expert communicate through masked Mixture-of-Transformers attention. Sparse anchor/recent/current observations provide temporal context; a frozen RynnBrain VLM supplies scene-conditioned instruction features (§§3.2–3.6).
- **Causal Imprint:** Learnable tokens predict changes between clean future video latents and align with detached future-video features. Attention routing prevents realized future observations from entering the action pathway; inference reuses cached observed-context and Imprint features (§§3.4, 3.6–3.8).
- **Training-only geometric supervision:** A Track4World teacher supplies geometry and motion descriptors distilled into the video expert. Both teacher and student distillation branch are removed at inference (§3.5).
- **Data and execution recipe:** The authors unify heterogeneous demonstrations into a canonical state/action interface, filter trajectories, and synthesize Ego2Robot data using trajectory-level kinematic checks and visual replacement. They combine pretraining, embodiment-specific post-training, feature caching, compilation, and asynchronous action execution (§§4–6).

The distinctive contribution is this integration and its Causal Imprint interface; the paper explicitly builds on existing video models, distillation teachers, human-to-robot conversion methods, and real-time chunking approaches. Code, weights, recipes, and eligible processed data are primarily described as planned releases, not verified available assets (§§2, 8).

## Evidence and main results
All results below are author-reported.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong LIBERO robustness | After standard LIBERO post-training, LIBERO-Plus success is **92.8%**, versus **91.4%** for Qwen-RobotManip-Context and **84.8%** for Being-H0.7; no adaptation to perturbed evaluation environments. | §7.2.1, Table 9 |
| Strong clean-to-random transfer | Clean-only RoboTwin post-training yields **90.0% Clean2Clean / 71.9% Clean2Random** success, versus **84.7% / 69.4%** for Qwen-RobotManip-Context and **89.4% / 48.7%** for OpenWAM-α. | §7.2.2, Table 10 |
| EBench score leads, success rate does not | Overall **49.2% success / 66.0 score**, versus OpenWAM-α’s **49.4% / 64.7**. Long-horizon results are **49.4% / 76.5**, versus **44.3% / 72.6**. | §7.2.3, Table 11 |
| RoboDojo improves but remains difficult | Average **23.91% success / 30.77 score**, versus **11.92% / 17.18** for OpenWAM-α and **22.48% / 28.97** for the listed GPT-6 Astra policy. | §7.2.4, Table 12 |
| Predictive supervision helps within the ablation setting | Independently trained LIBERO-Plus variants progress from **69.08%** with sparse memory and RynnBrain to **70.80%** with Imprint, **76.45%** with future-feature alignment, and **78.37%** with 4D distillation. | §7.4.1, Table 14 |
| Pretraining helps two physical tasks | Following task-specific post-training, toast-bread success rises from **4/20 to 19/20** and Luminol-reaction success from **0/20 to 19/20**. | §7.3, Table 13 |
| Runtime optimization reduces measured latency | On dexterous-hand deployment using one RTX 5090, mean controller round-trip time falls from **780.5 to 152.8 ms**, a **5.11×** speedup, averaging **50 requests** after warm-up. Separate LIBERO-Plus success measurements change from **92.78% to 92.23%**. | §6.2, Table 8 |
| Corpus volume includes derived data | Retained data comprise **11,302.20 robot hours**, **2,075.42 UMI hours**, **4,061.35 egocentric hours**, and **5,633.77 synthesized robot-hours**; the latter originate from **1,101.55 hours** of successfully converted source coverage. | §4.5, Tables 2–3 |
| External correction is promising in a selected subset | On **five** RoboDojo tasks selected for low standalone performance under seed 0, assistant corrections increase average success from **10.80% to 47.20%**, and score from **13.92 to 52.00**. | §7.5, Table 18 |

The evidence supports competitive benchmark performance, useful auxiliary predictive supervision, and practical action-only inference. It does not establish universal superiority: EBench overall success is slightly below OpenWAM-α, several RoboDojo categories favor other methods, and physical-platform breadth largely rests on demonstrations rather than comprehensive success-rate comparisons. “Zero-shot” robustness here follows benchmark-specific post-training; it is not zero-shot deployment of the pretrained checkpoint.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Egocentric source selection, conversion strategies, mixture ratios, and supervision forms are not systematically explored. Agent-assisted control remains preliminary (§8).
- **Author-stated scope:** Data-source ablations primarily evaluate gripper manipulation and downstream success; the authors caution against extending conclusions to dexterity or representation quality (§7.4.2).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Cumulative component ablations show conditional improvements but do not isolate all interactions. Their final performance differs from the headline model, and the precise relationship between these training regimes is insufficiently explained (§7.4, Tables 9, 14, 16).
- **Analyst inference—comparison uncertainty:** Baseline results come from papers or public benchmark reports under reportedly matching protocols, rather than a common retraining exercise. Main simulation tables lack uncertainty estimates and detailed seed/trial accounting (§7.1).
- **Analyst inference—physical validity:** Quantitative pretraining comparisons cover only the two tasks in Table 13. Water-level generalization is illustrated through successful examples without an aggregate evaluation; adaptation to other embodiments requires separate post-training (§7.3).
- **Analyst inference—data scale:** Synthesized robot-hours count multiple embodiment conversions of source footage. The corpus total therefore does not represent equally many unique hours of physical interaction (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—reproducibility:** Release commitments are conditional on licensing. The supplied text also leaves implementation ambiguities, including the loss-weight prose versus Table 4 and how the reported larger hand interface maps into canonical hand slots (Table 1; §§5.1, 7.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Do Imprint and 4D supervision retain their gains at full pretraining scale?** Existing component ablations use a setting whose relationship to headline training is unclear. Run matched full-scale removals of each objective, with repeated seeds and physical-task evaluation.
2. **When does human data improve dexterous control?** Current source ablations emphasize grippers and do not establish scaling behavior. Compare raw Ego, Ego2Robot, and robot-only mixtures under matched compute and source coverage on dexterous tasks.
3. **How robust is asynchronous deployment beyond average latency?** The paper reports mean round-trip time and selected continuity traces. Measure latency tails, missed replanning deadlines, and task success under controlled transport and scheduling delays.
4. **Does external correction generalize beyond selected failures?** The assistant study selects low-performing tasks and does not systematically examine invocation policies. Evaluate all RoboDojo tasks across seeds, reporting correction frequency, latency, cost, and success.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text documents the architecture and reported outcomes extensively, but supports weaker conclusions about causal attribution and broad physical generalization.

Material missing information includes uncertainty and seed accounting for main results; exact EBench/RoboDojo score definitions; full ablation training budgets; quantitative results across all physical tasks; inference integration-step count and latency tails; cross-source/evaluation overlap auditing; and confirmation of released artifacts and their licenses.
