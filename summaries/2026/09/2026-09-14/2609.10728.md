# Towards a Deterministic Math Solver for Clinical Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10728) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10728)


## One-sentence takeaway
With formulas, gold variables and whole notes supplied, generated Python improves clinical-calculation accuracy over direct arithmetic for Qwen2.5-32B-AWQ on the full benchmark, but the advantage is uncertain at 7B and on audit-filtered subsets (Tables 2, 12).

## Problem and motivation
Clinical calculation requires choosing a formula, extracting patient variables and executing arithmetic correctly. Hand-written calculators provide reliable execution but require individual implementation and maintenance; a partial library must abstain outside its coverage. The authors investigate whether case-specific code generation can expand coverage while isolating the benefit of execution under matched inputs (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Program-Solve interface:** The model translates a supplied formula and gold variables into Python; a restricted local subprocess executes it. The executor contains no calculator-specific functions or constants (§2).
- **Controlled evaluation:** Compare generated programs with direct arithmetic under identical formula, variable and whole-note access, alongside gold-input and extracted-input calculator libraries. The contribution is this clinical comparison, rather than program-aided reasoning itself (§§1–2).
- **Formula audits and failure analysis:** Separately examine clinical appropriateness and completeness of supplied formulas, repair benchmark-specific formula texts, and analyze residual errors and library-first hybrids (§4; Tables 11–23).

## Evidence and main results

Accuracy uses benchmark tolerances: ±5% for decimal outputs and exact matching for integers, dates and gestational-age tuples. Full-set results cover 1,100 cases across 55 calculators and five repeated runs. Intervals below are unadjusted 95% calculator-cluster bootstrap intervals (§2; Appendix A.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Matched execution benefit depends on checkpoint | Program-Solve versus arithmetic: 7B **75.31% vs 72.02%**, paired **+3.29 percentage points [−3.49, 10.38]**; 32B-AWQ **90.53% vs 83.47%**, **+7.05 [0.47, 14.60]**. Same formula, gold variables and whole note. | Tables 1–2 |
| Library advantage is reliability within coverage | Gold-Solve achieves **100%** on its 440 supported cases, versus Program-Solve’s **84.18%/98.64%** at 7B/32B; abstention elsewhere makes library full-set accuracy **40.00%**. | Table 1 |
| Removing supplied inputs sharply reduces accuracy | Blind Program-Solve reaches **28.04%/44.71%** at 7B/32B on the full set. Formula and gold-variable access are removed together. | §3.2; Table 1 |
| Other families do not establish a general execution advantage | Full-set program-minus-arithmetic gaps: Mistral **−7.62 points [−16.42, 1.40]**; Phi **+4.15 [−3.56, 12.22]**. Both cluster intervals include zero. | Tables 1, 7 |
| Clinical-audit filtering weakens the headline comparison | On 39 calculators with no identified issue, program-minus-arithmetic gaps are **−0.69 [−7.59, 6.54]** at 7B and **+4.36 [−2.44, 12.49]** at 32B. | Table 12 |
| Gold-input hybrids improve point estimates | Library-first with program fallback reaches **81.64%/91.07%**, versus **78.56%/86.89%** with arithmetic fallback; paired fallback gaps **+3.07 [−2.20, 9.15] / +4.18 [−1.60, 10.96]** remain uncertain. | Table 23 |
| Formula quality is a separate problem | Clinical audit flags **16/55** calculators; completeness audit finds **28/55** supplied texts wrong or incomplete, including **10** unable to reproduce benchmark answers before repair. | Table 11; Tables 21–22 |

The evidence supports a checkpoint-specific execution benefit under supplied, corrected inputs. It does not establish clinical correctness, end-to-end reliability, equivalence when intervals cross zero, or superiority to a fully implemented calculator library. **Analyst inference:** Table 12 materially qualifies the full-set 32B result: although filtered estimates remain compatible with the headline interval, their own intervals include zero.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Input dependence:** Formula recall and variable extraction change together in the blind arm, preventing attribution of its decline to either component (§3.2).
- **Comparator scope:** The library is an opportunistic selection without point-based scores; full-set gains largely reflect coverage (§2).
- **Benchmark validity:** Gold answers are calculator outputs, not independent validation of clinical appropriateness or program logic. The clinical audit is preliminary and single-annotator (§4; Table 11).
- **Evaluation adaptation:** Syntax prompts were selected on the test split. Formula corrections used benchmark worked solutions; unseen-formula generalization remains untested (§4; Tables 21–22).
- **Deployment and reproducibility:** English/US conventions dominate; serving costs are unmeasured. The executor lacks container or syscall isolation. Runs repeat greedy decoding without passing seeds to the server, and the 7B vLLM version is unrecorded (§4; Appendix A.1; Table 16).
- **Analyst inference—hybrid ambiguity:** Extract-first program fallback performs substantially worse than arithmetic fallback, but fallback input access is not clearly specified enough to reconcile this with the main supplied-input program results (Tables 15, 23).
- **Analyst inference—determinism boundary:** Deterministic execution does not imply deterministic generation or correct formula translation; repeated-run scoring disagreements remain (Table 24).

## Open research questions
1. **What predicts benefit from execution?** Checkpoints show mixed results, and scale is not isolated. Next experiment: compare additional checkpoints with matched precision, prompts and output budgets.
2. **How much error comes from formula selection versus extraction?** The blind comparison changes both. Next experiment: independently vary formula availability and gold-variable availability.
3. **Does the benefit survive independent formula and locale changes?** Corrected benchmark formulas and English/US conventions dominate. Next experiment: evaluate held-out calculators with independently verified formulas and equivalent notes using different units and date formats.
4. **When should a hybrid abstain?** Broader coverage introduces wrong answers, and no validated trust threshold is supplied. Next experiment: measure error-versus-coverage curves for routing and abstention rules on held-out cases.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark-level summary; clinical and broader deployment conclusions remain unsupported.

Material information unavailable or unresolved includes verbatim prompts and source code within the supplied text, clear Extract-first fallback input protocols, serving latency and cost, adversarial executor testing, independent clinical validation, and evaluation on unseen formulas or non-English settings.
