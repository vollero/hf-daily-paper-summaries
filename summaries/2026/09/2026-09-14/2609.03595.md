# How Far Can Synthetic Data Take Thai OCR?

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03595) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03595)


## One-sentence takeaway

Synthetic reconstruction substantially improves Thai OCR, but transfer depends on typography, handwriting glyphs, and training granularity, with persistent gaps on difficult pages and real handwriting (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation

Thai OCR lacks reliable labeled documents, while extraction and pseudo-labeling can corrupt characters and reading order. The authors identify a gap in prior synthetic-data work: generation pipelines change several properties together, obscuring which properties actually support transfer to real documents (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Controllable reconstruction:** Erase source text and render known labels into its original regions, controlling background context, typography, spatial arrangement, source domain, and handwriting glyph source. Thai shaping and fit checks accommodate translated text (§2).
- **Controlled transfer experiments:** Use Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct to compare page-level and crop-level training, synthetic versus real-image supervision, and typeface versus real-glyph handwriting (§3).
- **Adapted OCR model:** Train all parameters of the 0.9B PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 recognizer on 45,723 synthetic pages to produce Wayu-Paxa-OCR-Zero, with PP-DocLayoutV3 supplying inference regions (§4.1).

The final model uses English source pages, but its handwriting assets include real Thai glyphs and iApp crops; “synthetic-only” does not mean independence from real Thai handwriting data (§4.1; Appendix D).

## Evidence and main results

All CER values below are percentages; lower is better. “Median” means median page CER, while the reported mean is character-weighted. All scores use prediction projection before scoring.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Background context has limited benefit; typography and layout matter | Across Qwen page/crop settings and three internal sets, removing non-text context changes median CER by at most 1.62 percentage points. Removing font diversity, then spatial layout, progressively worsens handwriting. | §3.2, Table 2 discussion |
| Source-domain preference reverses with training granularity | On printed Heldout, out-of-domain → in-domain reconstruction changes median CER from **5.07 → 1.82** for page training, but **5.52 → 15.59** for crop training. | §§3.3–3.4, Table 3 discussion |
| Reconstruction approaches real-image supervision on typical printed pages | Qwen page training, Heldout: in-domain synthetic versus Real Thai (Print) gives median CER **1.82 vs 1.31**, but mean CER **16.20 vs 9.79**. | §3.5, Table 4 discussion |
| Real glyphs improve on handwriting typefaces | Handwriting / Easy Handwriting median CER: page training **38.65 / 34.84 → 37.91 / 30.66**; crop training **42.44 / 40.64 → 39.97 / 35.66**. Real print-plus-handwriting page supervision reaches **26.05 / 20.79**. | §3.6, Table 5 discussion |
| Final adaptation substantially improves its base | PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 → Wayu-Paxa-OCR-Zero median CER: Heldout **6.64 → 1.24**; Handwriting **74.87 → 20.55**; Easy Handwriting **73.74 → 14.18**. Authors report both aggregates improve on all five evaluation sets. | §4.2, Table 7 discussion |
| Competitiveness varies by comparator and benchmark | Authors report outperforming Typhoon OCR v1 7B on all five sets. Against Typhoon OCR 1.5 2B, median CER is **20.55 vs 19.36** on Handwriting and **4.86 vs 5.81** on SEA-DocBench’s clean Thai subset; Wayu trails on the other three sets. | §4.2, Table 7 caption/discussion |

The fixed-base comparison supports the effectiveness of the complete adaptation recipe. It does not isolate each final data addition. Comparisons across independently trained systems establish reported competitiveness, not superiority of synthetic supervision itself. The reconstruction-versus-real comparison uses pseudo-labeled real images, rather than established human-corrected ground truth (Appendix B).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The source-domain reversal remains unexplained; synthetic supervision retains severe-error and handwriting gaps; larger glyph banks and broader appearance coverage remain untested (§§3.4–3.6, 5).
- **Analyst inference—reference quality:** Internal references are Azure OCR outputs normalized by Gemini 2.5 Flash; human auditing is not described. Easy Handwriting is selected using agreement between proprietary recognizers, potentially favoring machine-readable handwriting (§3.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference—metric scope:** Projection discards unclaimed output, removes whitespace and formatting, and assembles matched spans in reference order. These scores therefore do not fully establish raw-output fidelity or reading-order accuracy. Appendix C also caps page CER, qualifying §3.1’s simpler description of mean CER.
- **Analyst inference—synthetic-only boundary:** Glyph construction uses real Thai training images and OCR-label alignment. The claim excludes real Thai page-level training supervision, but does not establish a pipeline free of real Thai labels or visual evidence (§4.1; Appendix D).
- **Internal inconsistency:** Appendix D reports 5,953 glyphs across 76 classes while stating a maximum of 60 per class; those quantities cannot all hold.
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Sequential property removals do not establish every interaction, and no separate ablations of the final recipe’s added forms, pages, and crops are supplied (§§3.2, 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Why does in-domain reconstruction hurt crop training?** The authors leave the reversal unresolved. Next experiment: match source-domain data volume and crop geometry, then compare annotated versus detected inference crops (§3.4).
2. **Can richer handwriting assets close the remaining gap?** The bank is limited and independently samples characters without writer consistency. Next experiment: vary bank size and writer-consistent sampling under a fixed training budget (§3.6; Appendix D).
3. **Do gains persist under independently audited evaluation?** Internal labels are machine-generated and scoring uses projection. Next experiment: human-correct a stratified subset and compare projected CER with raw transcription and reading-order evaluation (Appendices B–C).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied prose supports the main trends, but table bodies are absent despite captions and surrounding discussion.

Material missing information includes complete results and data mixtures, repeated-run uncertainty, human reference audits, exact controlled-condition training counts, translation implementation, compute costs, release links, and clarification of the glyph-bank count inconsistency.
