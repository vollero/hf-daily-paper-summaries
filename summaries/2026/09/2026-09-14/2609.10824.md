# Studying Without a Syllabus: Task-Agnostic Environment Preprocessing

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10824) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10824)


## One-sentence takeaway
Open-ended agents can prepare reusable artifacts without downstream task examples, achieving the highest mean reward on five of six benchmarks, but gains depend on environment and do not reliably increase with study budget (Table 1; §6).

## Problem and motivation
Agents entering unfamiliar environments must discover where information lives and how tools work. Existing adaptation methods typically require task examples or trajectories, while task-agnostic alternatives prescribe a fixed preparation strategy. The authors investigate whether an agent can choose its own preparation strategy from the environment alone (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Formal setting:** A studying system inspects an environment before receiving downstream tasks and adds reusable files for a solver with fixed weights and harness implementation. Real task instances, traces, labels, and evaluation feedback are excluded (§3).
- **Open-ended preparation:** META-AGENT chooses what to explore and produce, including navigation aids, scripts, and procedural guidance. Its archive-assisted variant can invoke and combine exploratory study, PREPING synthetic practice, and CORPUS2SKILL corpus compilation (§4).
- **Comparative evaluation:** The paper compares these variants with fixed workflows and no preparation, examining reward, preparation cost, test-time sampling, and artifact behavior (§§5–7). The contribution is adaptive preparation across heterogeneous environments; PREPING and CORPUS2SKILL are existing methods.

## Evidence and main results

All primary evaluations use Claude Haiku 4.5 with Claude Code. Studied scores average three independent artifact iterations, each evaluated with three rollouts per task (§5).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Meta-agents lead on most benchmarks | Highest Avg@3 on five of six: unaided wins OfficeQA and AppWorld; archive-assisted wins Harvey LAB, DABStep, and APEX-Agents. Archive-assisted improves over NOSTUDY everywhere. | Table 1 |
| Corpus specialization remains valuable | BCP-G Avg@3: CORPUS2SKILL **0.471 ± 0.015**, archive-assisted **0.286 ± 0.021**, NOSTUDY **0.197 ± 0.007**. | Table 1 |
| Archive benefits are uneven | DABStep Avg@3 rises from unaided **0.400 ± 0.006** to archive-assisted **0.529 ± 0.097**; AppWorld falls from **0.688 ± 0.057** to **0.665 ± 0.042**. | Table 1 |
| More study compute does not reliably help | Across the **$1–$50** sweep, OfficeQA and APEX show no sustained increase. Harvey LAB dense criterion pass rate rises **0.271→0.348** unaided and **0.249→0.332** archive-assisted. | §6, RQ3; Figure 2 |
| Preparation reduces required test-time sampling | To exceed the strongest studied single-rollout score, oracle-selected NOSTUDY requires **8/2/3/5/2/4** rollouts on BCP-G/OfficeQA/Harvey LAB/DABStep/APEX/AppWorld. Authors estimate **1.6–5.5×** task-time inference cost, excluding preparation. | §6, RQ4; Figure 3; Table 4 |
| Preparation costs differ substantially | On BCP-G, mean preparation cost is **$0.68 ± $0.19** unaided, **$97.87 ± $112.73** archive-assisted, and **$127.65 ± $110.56** for CORPUS2SKILL. | Table 5 |

These results establish improvements for the tested configurations, not superiority at matched total cost. Studied-score uncertainty is sample SD across artifact iterations; NOSTUDY uses population SD over subsets of its repeated evaluations, so the error bars are not equivalent. Best@K assumes oracle selection, and total savings require amortizing preparation (§5; Tables 1–2; §6).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** One solver, one harness, and models from the same family leave cross-solver transfer untested. One archive and no non-agentic workflow selector prevent isolating the benefit of agentic selection, composition, or individual entries (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Budget scaling measures the current agent’s budget-management policy. Dollar costs depend on provider pricing; primary comparisons lack a shared study budget (§§5, 8).
- **Measurement limits:** Exploration and artifact-use analyses are observational. Stratum analyses use a smaller NOSTUDY subset and lack multiple-comparison adjustment; qualitative examples are not seed-paired (Appendices B.1, C.1–C.3).
- **Analyst inference—interface confounding:** CORPUS2SKILL’s navigation prompt restricts direct corpus search, whereas archive bundles retain native access. Performance differences may therefore reflect delivery constraints as well as preparation quality (Appendix A.3.2).
- **Analyst inference—protocol ambiguity:** The archive prompt excludes “answer-bearing files,” yet DABStep explicitly exposes answer-bearing references; environmental histories also support task anticipation. The supplied text does not fully reconcile these boundaries (§5; Appendices A.3.2, B.3).
- Artifacts can misdirect search; the APEX example documents omission of essential input files (Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does agentic workflow composition outperform simple selection?** This control is absent. Compare the meta-agent with an environment-description classifier using the same archive and matched budgets (§8).
2. **Do artifacts transfer across solvers?** Only one frozen solver is evaluated. Reuse identical bundles across different model families and harnesses (§8).
3. **Can better budget control produce reliable scaling?** Existing sweeps are mostly flat. Compare explicit cost tracking and staged exploration against prompt-only budget guidance (§8).
4. **Can completeness checks prevent harmful guidance?** The examples show incomplete artifacts narrowing search. Randomize artifact verification and fallback-search instructions, measuring reward and added cost (Appendix C.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported comparisons; **medium** in generalization and causal explanations.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes full rendered benchmark-specific prompts, raw trajectories and artifacts, complete stratum counts and intervals, matched-total-cost comparisons, and cross-solver evaluations. The paper points to implementation and analysis records, but their contents were not supplied.
