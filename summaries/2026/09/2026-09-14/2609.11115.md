# Benchmark Radar: A Living Database and Search Engine for AI Benchmarks and Evaluation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11115) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11115)


## One-sentence takeaway
Benchmark Radar integrates benchmark discovery, searchable source records, and traceable score histories, but its evaluation establishes catalog coverage rather than retrieval effectiveness or reliable cross-benchmark comparisons.

## Problem and motivation
Benchmark selection requires finding evaluations, locating datasets and code, and checking the conditions behind reported scores across scattered sources. The authors identify a gap between existing catalogs and the combined workflow of discovering new benchmarks, tracking later use, and inspecting supporting evidence (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Integrated evidence catalog:** Preserves source-specific benchmark records, including entries without scores, and connects related records through reviewed identity links; daily discovery histories remain a separate population (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Explainable retrieval and shared access:** Uses BM25F lexical ranking with bounded name/phrase boosts and exposes matching terms and score components. Web, CLI, and HTTP interfaces share record IDs; versioned, checksum-verified downloads support repeatable offline queries (§3.4).
- **Full-catalog audit:** Measures artifact links, documentation, model coverage, score scales, and date evidence without excluding incomplete records. Released analysis scripts and exports support reproducing the census (§3.5; Appendix F).

The contribution is chiefly system integration and evidence auditing; the paper does not demonstrate a new retrieval algorithm or superior search performance.

These findings establish the contents and missing evidence of the audited catalog. They do not establish exhaustive benchmark coverage, improved retrieval, benchmark suitability, or catalog-wide saturation. Counts of source records also cannot be read as counts of distinct underlying tests.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated coverage limits:** Request failures, collection caps, missing identifiers, and differing snapshot dates affect coverage. Registry inputs remain August snapshots despite the September discovery cutoff (§6; Appendix F).
- **Author-stated retrieval limits:** Lexical matching can miss paraphrases and renamed tasks. The arXiv route misses later versions of papers first submitted before the collection window; related-work searches missed relevant earlier studies (§6).
- **Author-stated measurement limits:** Comparable scales alone do not establish comparable versions, prompts, tools, attempts, evaluators, or evaluation dates (§3.5; Appendix B).
- **Author-stated classification gap:** Discovery-track capability classification remains unvalidated; the CI null extractor assigns no capability levels to 5,863 tracks (§6).
- **Analyst observation:** §4.2 reports 128 agentic-facet records in the Agentic & Tool Use primary class, whereas Figure 5 lists 135 records in that class. The text does not explain whether this reflects differing facet membership or an inconsistency.
- **Analyst inference:** Identifier-based linking may undercount overlap across discovery sources because the authors explicitly acknowledge missing or inconsistent identifiers (§4.3).

## Open research questions
1. **Does retrieval improve benchmark selection?** Effectiveness is unmeasured; compare BM25F, semantic retrieval, and ordinary web search using reviewed relevance judgments and timed selection tasks (§6).
2. **How complete and accurate is artifact linking?** Identifier gaps leave cross-source overlap uncertain; manually audit a stratified artifact sample for missed and incorrect links (§4.3).
3. **Which score histories support saturation analysis?** Scale and date eligibility do not establish matched protocols; curate a subset with verified settings and reporting dates, then compare its trends with the displayed histories (Appendix B).
4. **How reliable are the classifications?** Capability labeling remains unvalidated; independently annotate catalog domains and discovery-track capabilities, then measure agreement and errors (§4.2; §6).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the descriptive summary; **medium** in conclusions about practical usefulness.

Material missing evidence includes retrieval relevance judgments, controlled user comparisons, precision/recall or time-saving measurements, validated classification accuracy, comprehensive protocol comparability, and actual evaluation dates. The supplied text describes reproducibility artifacts but does not provide their full contents or independent verification.
