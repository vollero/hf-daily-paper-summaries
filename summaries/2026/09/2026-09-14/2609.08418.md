# Feyospace-v1: How the Cyber Mercury Seven Trained Frontier Cyber Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08418) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08418)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that execution-audited, long-context supervised fine-tuning improves three open-weight cyber agents on vulnerability reproduction and CTF tasks, while full exploit capability remains limited and multi-turn misuse resistance worsens (§5.2, §5.5).

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses the difficulty of producing reliable training data for cyber agents: tasks need reproducible environments, executable verification, coherent multi-turn supervision, and affordable teacher access. The authors argue that these bottlenecks constrain independent teams more directly than model scale, and that existing evaluation environments do not supply sufficient training-scale supervision (§1, §3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Environment-grounded data pipeline.** Constructs resettable coding, vulnerability, CTF, Linux-kernel, exploit, firmware, and physical-device tasks. Execution checks and layered evidence audits produce **164,269 retained trajectories**, distinguishing environments from potentially multiple trajectories per environment (§3, Table 9).
- **Agentic SFT recipe.** Independently fine-tunes three Qwen checkpoints, preserving message roles and causal tool ordering. Training masks context tokens, weights reasoning below answers and tool calls, and packs trajectories with isolated attention into contexts of up to **262,144 tokens**. No subsequent RL stage is used (§4).
- **Capability and supervision methods.** PSBreakup proposes prompt-conditioned, reverse-KL distillation to recover cyber behavior while preserving other domains; Kreator rewrites local expert interventions into self-contained teacher trajectories (§2.4–2.5).
- **Exploratory systems, with important exclusions.** Choulea analyzes recovered reasoning but supplies **no direct SFT targets**. SkyReal’s account-based sampling system and Hongzwang’s refusal-bypass system were **excluded from the final training pipeline** (§2.1–2.3). Thus, the abstract’s five-system framing should not be read as five experimentally validated contributors to the final gains.
- **Planned assets.** The authors promise training trajectories, a diagnostic dataset with serialization errors, and a development subset of CyberGym; release availability is not established in the supplied text (§1, §4.2, §5.1).

## Evidence and main results

All results below are author-reported; **pp** denotes percentage points.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| CyberGym improves across all three checkpoints | Full 1,507-task suite, shared Claude Code setup: s0 **9.75% → 51.29%** (+41.54 pp); s1 **54.51% → 63.24%** (+8.73 pp); s2 **29.07% → 50.07%** (+21.00 pp), relative to their respective starting checkpoints. | §5.2, Fig. 10 |
| Interactive CTF performance improves | Pooled CyBench, XBOW, and NYU CTF success: s0 **33.23% → 46.23%**; s1 **45.51% → 52.21%**; s2 **28.31% → 40.09%**, using shared serving and agent settings. | §5.2, Fig. 11(a) |
| Expert-rewrite compatibility matters in a small internal comparison | On **55 internal V8 environments**, averaging **3.6 expert turns**, verified exploits number **27** with an external rewriter, **32** with teacher self-rewrite, and **31** with student-voice rewriting. These are trajectory-generation results, not downstream student gains. | §2.5, Table 8 |
| Full exploit capability remains unresolved | No main Feyospace checkpoint reaches ExploitBench T2 generic primitives or T1 full control. Separate EXP-only SFT of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next raises capability coverage **12.96% → 19.05%**, reaching T3 on two V8 cases, but neither T2 nor T1. | §5.2 |
| Reasoning becomes shorter on shared successes | Across **65 tasks solved by all six base/post-trained models**, mean reasoning tokens fall **13.2%, 17.7%, and 16.3%** for s1, s0, and s2 respectively; turn counts do not uniformly decrease. | §5.4, Table 13 |
| Multi-turn misuse robustness deteriorates | On the reconstructed **500-task Poly-Guard Cyber subset**, multi-turn attack success rises from **76.6% → 86.8%** for s0, **88.0% → 92.2%** for s1, and **82.4% → 96.4%** for s2; lower is better. | §5.5, Fig. 12 |

The paired evaluations support improvements from the **complete post-training recipe** under the reported harness. They do not isolate individual components or establish general frontier leadership: several reference models score higher on CyberGym, and closed-model comparisons use different evaluation arrangements (§5.2).

The abstract’s average improvements of “23.76%” and “10.49%” correspond to **percentage-point gains**, as clarified by the paired results, rather than relative percentage improvements.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Authors’ stated boundaries:** recovered reasoning was excluded because of cost and data-quality concerns; Kreator’s V8 set is internal and unreleased; full exploit control remains unresolved; and safety regresses without a separate alignment stage (§2.1, §2.5, §5.2, §5.5).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** no controlled component ablation establishes the contributions of auditing, expert rewriting, mixture composition, or reasoning weighting. PSBreakup lacks quantitative recovery-versus-retention results, and its exact contribution to final teacher data is unclear (§2.4, §4–5).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** coding-repository exclusions and kernel prefix screening are described, but comprehensive train–evaluation overlap measurements across the security corpus are absent. Hardware data also lack a dedicated downstream evaluation (§3, §5).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** repeated-run uncertainty is unreported; pooled CTF results omit suite-specific denominators and most per-suite scores. Reasoning-length findings condition on shared successes (§5).
- **Reproducibility and economics:** several extraction methods are withheld, promised artifacts are not confirmed released, and Appendix A excludes later institution-supported SFT costs. The full program’s cost efficiency therefore cannot be assessed.
- **Proxy limitation:** “self-exile” measures a model judge’s exclusion decision, not actual provider retention or training-data use (§5.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Which training components drive the gains?** Component effects remain unisolated. Run matched-budget ablations of auditing, expert-rewritten data, and reasoning-token weighting.
2. **Does PSBreakup selectively recover capability?** The paper gives an objective but no quantitative recovery/retention comparison. Compare it with ordinary target-domain SFT and prompt elicitation on held-out cyber and retained-domain tasks.
3. **Can stronger exploit supervision reach full control on unseen tasks?** Current results stop at target-specific primitives. Evaluate EXP-supervised models on held-out projects and vulnerability families with repeatable end-to-end verification.
4. **Can alignment recover misuse resistance while retaining capability?** Multi-turn attack success rises after SFT. Apply a separate alignment stage and jointly re-evaluate capability benchmarks and adaptive safety attacks.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The text provides detailed construction procedures and paired benchmark results, but causal and reproducibility evidence is incomplete.

Material missing information includes confirmed artifact availability, full training costs and hardware accounting, teacher/category mixture proportions, quantitative PSBreakup results, comprehensive contamination measurements, repeated-run uncertainty, CTF pooling denominators, and separate ExploitGym results.
