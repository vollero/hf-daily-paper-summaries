# ActionSplice: In-Flight Action Editing for Interactive World Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08230) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08230)


## One-sentence takeaway
ActionSplice learns to correct an interrupted video sampler’s state for a revised action, improving agreement with rollback references on two frozen world models without replaying completed sampling steps (§§3–4).

## Problem and motivation
Chunk-autoregressive world models typically hold one action fixed while generating a chunk. A mid-generation update therefore either waits, changes conditioning while retaining a stale state, or requires costly rollback. The authors identify same-step state correction as the missing capability needed for responsive control within the active chunk (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Counterfactual State Transport (CST):** Train a residual corrector on matched old-action and revised-action states sharing the initial state, history, solver step, and stochastic inputs. Resume the frozen sampler from the corrected state (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Two intervention types:** CST-R retargets the entire chunk; CST-T masks correction to a temporal suffix. Its training reference clamps the prefix to the old-action trajectory (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Backbone-specific implementations and evaluation:** Separate correctors edit minWM clean predictions or HY-WM1.5 Euler states. Recurrent training and matched evaluations cover action transitions, receipt steps, temporal boundaries, and repeated updates (§§3.4–4.1; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-chunk fidelity improves | At receipt step 2 of 4, across 30 held-out prompts, CST-R versus condition swapping reduces rollback-relative LPIPS from 0.3155 to 0.1214 on minWM and 0.3446 to 0.0831 on HY-WM1.5: reported reductions of 61.5% and 75.9%. | Table 1; §4.2 |
| Suffix editing improves fidelity and latency versus waiting | Across 90 prompt-boundary groups at receipt step 2, CST-T versus condition swapping reduces suffix LPIPS from 0.2868 to 0.1258 on minWM and 0.2731 to 0.0615 on HY-WM1.5. Pixel-ready speedups over waiting are 2.73× and 1.69×, respectively. | Table 2 |
| Human annotations support responsiveness | On HY-WM1.5, CST-R versus condition swapping achieves 94.7% versus 84.2% action following and 1.05 versus 8.89 mean stale frames; CST-T achieves 100.0% versus 86.7% and 0.40 versus 3.73. Caption reports 30 CST-R and 90 CST-T matched groups. | Table 3 |
| Global video similarity improves in the reported benchmark | Under HY-WorldPlay, CST-R achieves 25.66 PSNR, 0.6902 SSIM, and 0.1337 LPIPS versus the original rollout, compared with Light Interaction’s reported 24.81, 0.6500, and 0.1788. | Table 4 |
| Late edits remain harder | On HY-WM1.5 CST-R, rollback-relative LPIPS rises from 0.0643 at receipt step 1 to 0.2694 at step 3, while pixel-ready latency falls from 7578.8 to 6026.6 ms on the same 30 held-out prompts. | Appendix B.1, Table 7 |
| Pairing and masking matter | Removing CST-T’s hard mask raises prefix LPIPS from 0.0412 to 0.0604; unmatched supervision raises rollback-relative LPIPS from 0.0615 to 0.7719 under the full-model evaluation protocol. | Table 6 |
| Repeated updates retain an advantage | Authors report CST-R remains closer to sequential rollback than condition swapping on every plotted metric across five interruptions, after training on three-interruption trajectories. Exact curve values are unavailable in the text. | §4.6; Figure 3 |

These results support improved rollback agreement and action responsiveness within the tested settings. Rollback is a model-generated reference, so agreement does not establish physical correctness. Speedups are relative to waiting: HY-WM1.5 CST-R is slower than condition swapping in pixel-ready latency, at 7442.2 versus 7052.1 ms (Table 1). Published camera-following comparisons use different request protocols and explicitly do not support a direct ranking (§4.5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-reported scope:** Receipt-step performance deteriorates for late updates; yaw-right generalization is shown only qualitatively (Appendix B.1; Figure 5).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Two backbones, four-step samplers, three training actions, and one seed per prompt leave broader control spaces and stochastic robustness untested (§4.1; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—prefix guarantee is local:** The hard mask preserves transport coordinates at receipt time; it does not establish exact preservation of final decoded prefix frames (§3.4; nonzero prefix LPIPS in Table 2).
- **Analyst inference—selection and reproducibility concerns:** Checkpoints minimize held-out transport NMSE, but a separate final test split is not described. Pilot-checkpoint initialization is reported without its training provenance (Appendix A.3).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Human annotation denominators, annotator counts, and agreement are unspecified. Table 5 camera errors differ from the receipt-step-2 values in Table 7 without a clear reconciliation. Confidence intervals are absent.
- **Missing component evidence:** Table 6 tests pairing and masking, but does not isolate recurrent training, auxiliary losses, or pilot initialization. Appendix A.3’s backbone-specific state/residual objectives also require reconciliation with the core residual objective in §3.4.

## Open research questions
- **Can late edits recover early-edit fidelity?** Table 7 shows degradation near sampling completion. Compare late-step-specialized correctors against the existing corrector under matched compute budgets.
- **Does control generalization extend beyond the training actions?** Unseen yaw-right control has only qualitative evidence. Evaluate unseen directions and action combinations across multiple seeds.
- **How stable are longer intervention sequences?** Recurrent evaluation reaches five updates on HY-WM1.5 CST-R. Test longer sequences on both variants and backbones, including an ablation without recurrent training.
- **Can decoded prefixes be preserved more reliably?** Masking guarantees only receipt-state preservation. Compare final prefix drift with and without continued prefix constraints during remaining sampling.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the central method and reported comparisons, but broader validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes accessible release URLs, pilot-training details, a clearly separate final test set, annotator statistics, uncertainty estimates, precise definitions of some error metrics, full corrector dimensions and training cost, and numerical repeated-update curves.
