# Building and Evaluating Fixed-Voice Thai TTS from Synthetic Speech

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03502) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03502)


## One-sentence takeaway
An 82M-parameter Thai–English TTS student trained on filtered synthetic speech improves its teacher’s Thai CER and pause precision but loses accuracy on difficult expressions, highlighting the importance of training-text coverage (§4, Table 8).

## Problem and motivation
Compact fixed-voice TTS usually requires a speaker-specific corpus, while large voice-cloning models impose greater inference demands. The authors investigate generating the student’s training speech from short voice references instead (§1).

The gap is both practical and evaluative: teacher mistakes become training targets, filtering can remove difficult examples, and sentence-level character error rate (CER) can conceal mispronounced names, code-switching errors, and misplaced pauses (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Synthetic-data recipe:** Generate speech with OmniVoice using twelve frozen, designed voice references; verbalize ambiguous text; filter pronunciation, pauses, rate, and duration; and re-render eligible failures to preserve coverage. Train a Kokoro/StyleTTS2 student on the resulting corpus (§2).
- **Thai–English adaptation:** Combine TLTK and Misaki phoneme frontends, add a Thai low-tone embedding, and reuse pretrained model components. The final bilingual corpus contains 54.35 hours of synthetic speech (§§2.2, 4.4).
- **Diagnostic evaluation:** Introduce targeted keyword and allowed-pause-position evaluations alongside CER, speaker similarity, and speaking rate; report recipe ablations and inference-only frontend improvements (§§3–4).
- **Teacher and dialect studies:** Measure oracle best-of-K recovery and explore Isan adaptation from a short reference. The authors report releasing Wayu-Paxa-TTS-Edge and Thai-TTS-Eval (Abstract; §§4.5–5; Appendix A.1).

The contribution is primarily the pipeline, evaluation framework, and empirical analysis; the acoustic backbone is reused.

These results support the tested recipe’s usefulness under the paper’s automated measurements. They do not establish human-perceived naturalness, measured on-device efficiency, or that a practical selector and retrained student would realize the oracle sampling gains. Lower PPER must be interpreted alongside the student’s lower pause frequency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ stated limitations:** Pause scoring depends on segmentation, forced alignment, and handling verbalized or code-switched spans. The detector remains approximate; comparisons under the same instrument are more reliable than absolute rates (§6; Appendix A.2).
- **Authors’ stated limitation:** Isan CER does not establish naturalness or dialect fidelity (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference:** ASR-based keyword matching measures transcript recovery rather than directly verified pronunciation. The paper itself notes that autoregressive ASR can recover intended words despite acoustic errors (§§2.1.3, 3.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Cumulative recipe ablations change multiple conditions across the sequence, including corpus size. They support the combined recipe but do not fully isolate filtering, coverage, and data-volume effects (Table 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Pause masks are text-model-generated, and aligner validation uses early Kokoro-generated clips rather than listener annotations. Human validity remains incompletely established (§3.2; Appendix A.2).
- **Comparison caveats:** Gemini speaker similarity uses different voice references, and its parameter count is explicitly a literature-derived proxy, not a measured TTS model size (Table 8).

## Open research questions
- **Can practical selection deliver the oracle gains?** Best-of-K establishes availability of correct teacher outputs, not deployable selection quality. Next experiment: compare automatic selectors under matched generation budgets, then train students on their selections (§5.1).
- **Does coverage preservation independently explain resampling’s benefit?** Corpus duration also changes in the cumulative ablation. Next experiment: match retained hours and text distributions while varying rejection and resampling policies (Table 5).
- **Do automated improvements match listener judgments?** Naturalness and Isan fidelity remain unverified. Next experiment: blinded native-listener assessments of pronunciation, phrasing, identity, and dialect fidelity (§4.5; Appendix A.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides detailed methods, result tables, and scorer constants, but broader conclusions depend heavily on automated evaluation.

Material missing information includes human listening results, uncertainty estimates and repeated-run variability, measured device latency/memory/energy, generation and training costs, and a demonstrated student benefit from oracle sampling. The text also leaves the Table 7 starting accuracy difference from Table 5 and the meaning of Table 11’s “Unresolved” column insufficiently explained.
