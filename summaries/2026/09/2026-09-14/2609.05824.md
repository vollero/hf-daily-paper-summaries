# Beyond Top-k Skill Retrieval: Diversity-Aware Skill Routing for LLM Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05824) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05824)


## One-sentence takeaway
DSR reports modest improvements in multi-skill retrieval coverage by combining relevance scores with query-residual diversity, but limited evaluation and unequal output lengths weaken broader claims (§§4.1–4.3).

## Problem and motivation
Independent relevance ranking can fill an agent’s limited context with overlapping skills while missing complementary capabilities. The authors identify a second difficulty: raw similarity can reflect either functional redundancy or useful shared relevance to a task, so indiscriminate diversification may discard necessary skills (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Set selection for skill routing:** DSR keeps the existing retriever and relevance model, then uses a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) to favor relevant, mutually non-redundant subsets (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Query-residual kernel:** Its central methodological contribution subtracts the query-aligned component of each skill embedding, blends the residual with the original embedding, and computes diversity from the normalized result (§3.3).
- **Practical inference and evaluation:** Greedy selection uses incremental Cholesky updates; selected skills are finally sorted by relevance. Experiments use 50 retrieved candidates and residual mixing coefficient 0.85 (§§3.4, 4.1).

The contribution is a selection method and its evaluation; the paper explicitly does not introduce a new benchmark or skill representation (§2).

## Evidence and main results
Results below are author-reported, averaged across Easy and Hard tiers under §4.1’s default protocol. The benchmark has 75 queries, including 51 requiring two to five skills.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Modest overall gains at a shared cutoff | SkillRouter → DSR: Recall@20 **0.754 → 0.768**; Full Coverage@20 **0.560 → 0.573**; same retrieval and relevance models. | §4.2, referring to Table 1 |
| Larger multi-skill gains | SkillRouter → DSR: Recall@20 **0.704 → 0.739**; Full Coverage@20 **0.458 → 0.492**. | §4.2, Table 1 discussion |
| Longer outputs recover more targets, with an unequal-length comparison | Overall Recall@50 **0.754 → 0.808** and Full Coverage@50 **0.560 → 0.633**; SkillRouter actually returns only 20 skills, versus DSR’s 50. | §§4.1–4.2 |
| Residual kernel improves over raw cosine diversity | With reranker quality, multi-skill Full Coverage@10 **0.254 → 0.441** when replacing the standard cosine kernel. | §4.3, Table 2 discussion |
| Relevance quality remains important | With the residual kernel, embedding → reranker quality increases overall Recall@10 **0.618 → 0.711** and multi-skill Full Coverage@10 **0.237 → 0.441**. | §4.3, Table 2 discussion |
| Another comparison reports a different DSR coverage value | Multi-skill Recall@10: SkillRouter **0.659**, DSR **0.668**; Full Coverage@10: **0.424**, **0.432**. DSR’s coverage differs from §4.3’s **0.441**, without an explicit explanation. | §4.4, Table 3 discussion |
| Small reported change in first-correct-skill ranking | SkillRouter → DSR MRR@10: overall **0.788 → 0.784**; multi-skill **0.792 → 0.788**. | Appendix A |

The shared-cutoff comparisons support improved target-skill recovery in this setting. They do not establish statistical reliability, downstream task completion, or actual reductions in context tokens.

**Analyst inference:** At output size 50, selecting all 50 retrieved candidates and sorting them by relevance leaves no subset-selection advantage under the stated algorithm. Consequently, the @50 results cannot isolate a diversity benefit over an equally long pointwise output (§§3.4, 4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Few evaluation queries; required skills missed during retrieval cannot be recovered; coverage does not measure agent execution; DPP selection adds deployment overhead (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** No confidence intervals or significance tests are supplied, limiting interpretation of small gains.
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text contains table captions but no table bodies. Narrative results therefore cannot be fully cross-checked, including the differing multi-skill Full Coverage@10 values.
- **Analyst inference:** No residual-weight or candidate-size sensitivity study, measured latency, or experimental comparison with MMR is supplied. The results establish an advantage over the reported raw-cosine ablation, not over diversity methods generally.

## Open research questions
1. **Does improved coverage increase completed tasks?** Execution is untested. Run the same agent with pointwise and DSR shortlists under matched token budgets and measure task completion.
2. **Does DSR help under equal output lengths?** The @50 comparator is truncated. Compare both methods at identical candidate and output sizes, including pointwise top-50.
3. **How stable are the gains and kernel settings?** Query coverage is limited and sensitivity is unreported. Evaluate a larger held-out query set while sweeping residual weight and candidate size, reporting uncertainty and latency.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and several numerical comparisons are explicit, but the evidence supports a narrow conclusion.

Material missing information includes table bodies, an explanation for inconsistent coverage values, statistical uncertainty, tier-specific results, hyperparameter-selection procedures, latency measurements, downstream execution results, and a code-release location.
