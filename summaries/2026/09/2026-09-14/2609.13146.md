# SNAP3D: Physically Grounded 3D Parts for Assembly from a Single Image

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13146) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13146)


## One-sentence takeaway
SNAP3D repairs generated part geometry and optimizes explicit connectors, achieving 95% simulated stability on 100 curated assets with ground-contacting parts pinned, while demonstrating manual assembly on six printed objects (Table 1; §D).

## Problem and motivation
Visually plausible 3D parts can overlap, lack attachments, or separate under gravity. The authors identify a gap between semantic part generation and physical fabrication: existing generators emphasize appearance, while prior fabrication approaches often assume compatible geometry or change the semantic decomposition (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
SNAP3D post-processes Hunyuan3D reconstructions decomposed by XPart; it does not train a new image-to-3D generator (§3.1).

- **Geometry repair:** Assign overlapping volume to one neighboring part, trim the other, and constrain excessive erosion while preserving meaningful boundaries (§3.1; §B).
- **Contact and connector construction:** Recover a graph from nearby, opposing surfaces and introduce complementary peg-and-socket connectors with geometric interlocks (§3.2).
- **Simulation-guided refinement:** Localize unstable components and use the Cross-Entropy Method to search connector position, direction, radius, and insertion length, rejecting geometrically infeasible candidates (§3.3).
- **Physical evaluation:** Combine geometric fidelity and penetration metrics with validity checks, gravity rollouts, additional loading tests, and fabrication demonstrations (§4.1; §§A–D).

The contribution is this integrated treatment of generated semantic parts and their connections. The authors’ claim to be first is not independently established by the supplied text.

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Large improvement in simulated stability | On 100 curated HY3D-Bench assets: SNAP3D **95% stable**, versus XPart **0%**, OmniPart **2%**, and PartCrafter **1%**; falls were **3%, 64%, 55%, 89%**, respectively. Gravity rollout: **2 s**, supports pinned; stability requires valid intersection-free geometry, displacement below **15 grid cells** and rotation below **10°**. | Table 1; §§A–B |
| Lower displacement and penetration | Same benchmark: mean worst-part Shift **5.6 cells**, versus **73.5/33.4/98.1** for XPart/OmniPart/PartCrafter. SNAP3D IVR **<0.01%**, IPR **0.5%**, versus XPart **4.86%**, **31.8%**. | Table 1; §B |
| Small fidelity cost relative to its input baseline | SNAP3D versus XPart: CD **0.98 vs 0.85** in reported ×10⁻² units; F1 **94.8% vs 95.9%**; p-CD **1.72 vs 1.67**; p-F1 **80.6% vs 81.3%**. Surviving annotation matches cover **74.3% vs 74.9%** of annotated parts. | Table 1; §B |
| Connectors provide most of the stability gain | Cumulative stages on the same inputs: baseline **0%**, geometry editing **22%**, initialized connectors **86%**, full optimization **95%** stable. | Table 2 |
| Search choices contribute | Stability drops from **95%** to **92%** without direction search, **90%** without scale or position search, **88%** without multi-basin initialization, and **89%** without full covariance. | Tables 3, 6 |
| Some robustness beyond optimized loading | Among the **95 gravity-stable assemblies**, hold rates were **88.4%** under reversed gravity and **96.8%** under three-axis shaking. Shaking used **0.5% of bounding-box diagonal** amplitude at **2 Hz**, for **3.5 s**, followed by **1.5 s** settling. | §C; Table 5 |
| Physical assembly demonstrated | All **six** printed examples were manually assembled using generated connectors and reportedly survived manual handling and shaking; printed in PLA at **15 cm** longest dimension. | §D; Fig. 9 |

These results support improved compatibility and retention under the specified simulation protocol. Invalid initial assemblies remain in the full benchmark denominator and cannot count as stable (§A); consequently, stability measures both initial validity and subsequent retention. It does not establish that every baseline physically collapsed.

The printed examples demonstrate feasibility, but do not establish a benchmark-wide fabrication success rate or general insertion feasibility.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Rigid-body simulation omits printed-material deformation and preload; high-fidelity contact makes optimization costly (§5; §D).
- **Author-stated:** Open generated shells, thin interfaces, and detached components can defeat geometry repair or connector placement (§4.2; §E).
- **Author-stated:** Assembly order is unmodeled. A connector valid in the assembled configuration may be blocked during insertion (§E).
- **Analyst inference:** Pinned supports limit conclusions about freely standing objects and whole-object tipping. Additional loading tests also retain pinned parts (§4.1; §C).
- **Analyst inference:** Generalization remains bounded by curated data exclusions and quantitative evaluation using only XPart as the pipeline input (§4.1; §A).
- **Comparison caveats:** Part metrics exclude weakly matched parts; qualitative baseline sequences use Genesis while SNAP3D uses IPC. Quantitative evaluation uses the stated IPC protocol (§§B–C).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Invalid-case counts and their treatment in reported Shift and falls are not fully specified; stress-test baseline subset sizes are absent (§§A, C).

## Open research questions
- **Can all generated joints be inserted?** Assembly sequencing is explicitly excluded (§E). Next experiment: plan collision-free insertion sequences and physically test predicted feasible and blocked cases.
- **How reliable is transfer across materials and scales?** Validation covers one printing material and size setting (§D). Next experiment: vary material, clearance, and object scale, measuring insertion force and retention.
- **Does refinement generalize across generators?** Quantitative refinement uses XPart outputs (§4.1). Next experiment: apply the same pipeline to OmniPart and PartCrafter, separating failures by input geometry and contact quality.
- **Can simulation cost be reduced without losing stability?** The authors identify contact simulation as costly (§5). Next experiment: compare rollout budgets or surrogate-assisted search, reporting runtime alongside stability.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported benchmark findings; **medium** in broader physical-assembly generalization.

Material missing information includes runtime and hardware, uncertainty across repeated searches, per-method invalid simulation counts, full simulator material/contact settings, quantitative physical retention tests, and explicit code or evaluation-subset release status.
