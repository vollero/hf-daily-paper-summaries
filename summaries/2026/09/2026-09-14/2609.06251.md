# MobileVLA-R1 2.0: RL-Enhanced Reasoning for Mobile Robot Control

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06251) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06251)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors report that a learned reasoning-to-action decoder combined with offline reinforcement learning improves mobile robot control, including G1 task completion without G1-specific policy training, while relying on predefined robot controllers (§§IV–V).

## Problem and motivation

Mobile robots must translate language and spatial reasoning into reliable locomotion and manipulation under uncertain observations and long task sequences. The authors identify an insufficiently developed interface between explicit reasoning and executable control: generating a rationale alone does not ensure useful physical actions (§I).

## Approach and contributions

- **Learned reasoning-to-action interface:** The principal methodological extension replaces MobileVLA-R1’s deterministic text parsing with cross-attention over observation and reasoning states. Separate queries predict planar velocities and a discrete behavior primitive (§§I, IV-C).
- **Structured supervision and offline RL:** MobileVLA-CoT contains 134K episode-, step-, and navigation-level annotations synthesized from R2R, RxR, and QUARD. Supervised alignment precedes GRPO, which rewards command similarity, behavior correctness, and output formatting. The decoder remains frozen during GRPO; training uses fixed data without environment interaction (§§III–IV).
- **Controller-mediated embodiment transfer:** The policy predicts task-level actions; robot-specific controllers execute them. The extended evaluation introduces G1 manipulation without G1 trajectories or policy fine-tuning, together with decoder ablations and deployment diagnostics. G1 reaching, grasping, and related movements use fixed controller routines (§§I, IV-C, V-C).

The manuscript presents reasoning supervision and RL as parts of the overall framework; its stated extensions over the earlier version center on learned decoding, the action interface, and expanded evaluation (§I).

## Evidence and main results

All numbers below are author-reported in the supplied prose; table bodies are absent.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Improved R2R-CE navigation | Val-unseen SR **69.8**, SPL **66.9**; gains of **1.5** and **1.7 percentage points** over MobileVLA-R1. | §V-B; Table II |
| Improved RxR-CE navigation | Val-unseen SR **73.1**, SPL **68.5**, nDTW **77.6**; gains of **1.6**, **1.7**, and **1.5 points** over MobileVLA-R1. | §V-B; Table II |

| Deployment requires remote inference | Hybrid end-to-end latency **205–245 ms**, approximately **4.1–4.9 Hz** high-level decisions; includes communication and command handoff. | §VI |

The comparisons support improved performance within the reported settings, and ablations support contributions from reasoning supervision and decoding. They do not establish general embodiment adaptation, learned joint-level manipulation, or the faithfulness of generated explanations. GRPO optimizes action-label agreement and formatting rather than measured online task returns (§IV-E).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** A finite task-level action space cannot directly express dexterous contact dynamics or continuous whole-body motion; broader morphology and environment transfer remains untested (§VII).
- **Author-stated:** Remote inference depends on network connectivity and exceeds current onboard memory capacity (§VI).
- **Analyst inference:** G1 results establish transfer of high-level behavior selection through existing controllers; they cannot isolate newly learned manipulation capability from controller capability (§§IV-C, V-C).
- **Analyst inference:** Reported reward-component studies use deterministic parsing, limiting direct evidence that the same reward balance is optimal with the new decoder (§V-D; Appendix A).
- **Evidence gap:** Missing table bodies, success-rate uncertainty estimates, and repeated-run statistics limit assessment of comparison fairness and statistical reliability.

## Open research questions

1. **Do reasoning contents causally guide actions?** Conditioning ablations help, but rationale faithfulness is not tested. Next experiment: replace or perturb reasoning while holding observations fixed and measure action changes and task success.
2. **How broadly does controller-mediated transfer generalize?** G1 is the demonstrated unseen training embodiment. Next experiment: evaluate the frozen policy on additional robots using documented, matched primitive interfaces (§VII).
3. **Can richer actions reduce manipulation failures?** Grasping and execution failures remain prominent. Next experiment: compare finite primitives with parameterized manipulation skills under identical G1 tasks (§§V-C, VII).
4. **Can inference run onboard without losing performance?** Current deployment requires a remote GPU. Next experiment: evaluate compressed models for onboard latency, memory use, and closed-loop success (§VI).
