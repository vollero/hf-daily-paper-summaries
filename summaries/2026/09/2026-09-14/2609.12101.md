# Competence-Gated Pooling of Language Models and Priors for Event Forecasting

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.12101) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12101)


## One-sentence takeaway
Outcome-trained, domain-specific pooling improves binary forecasts over external and global-pooling baselines, but does not significantly beat a strong contextual stacker or improve the official market subset (§7).

## Problem and motivation
A language model can forecast accurately yet add little beyond an existing market, crowd, or statistical forecast. The authors target **relative competence**: whether model predictions improve the forecast already available. They distinguish replacing an external source from complementing its errors through pooling (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Comparative formulation and theory:** Under Brier loss, derive when model disagreement provides complementary information and an exact identity for the benefit of domain-specific versus global affine weights (§4, Theorem 1).
- **Competence gate:** Fit ridge-regularized source weights subject to summing to one, using resolved outcomes within each source domain. Shrink domain estimates toward global weights, clip pooled probabilities, and apply isotonic recalibration. Signed weights permit error correction (§5).
- **Empirical evaluation:** Compare routing, global pooling, contextual stacking, and confidence-based selection using five models. Model forecasts use question text, background, and resolution criteria without retrieval or access to external probabilities (§§6–7).

The authors explicitly acknowledge that context-dependent pooling itself is established; their claimed contribution combines the comparative target, closed-form gate, and routing identity (§2).

These results support domain adaptation within the evaluated historical setting, including improvement against a stronger structured prior. They do not establish superiority over contextual stacking, universal benefits from adding models, or prospective reliability.

**Analyst interpretation:** The routing theorem is exact for optimal affine pools. Its empirical comparison involves estimated weights, shrinkage, clipping, recalibration, and a simplex comparator; numerical proximity therefore supports correspondence in scale, not an exact validation of the deployed pipeline.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Historical contamination cannot be eliminated; retrieval-free forecasting does not represent a complete forecasting agent (§8).
- **Author-stated:** Unseen-domain transfer is uncertain because training requires resolved outcomes. Source labels are coarse, and historical competence may change (§8; Impact Statement).
- **Author-stated:** Smaller structured sources are underpowered; time-series results depend on series matching, available historical vintages, and ARIMA assumptions (§§7–8).
- **Author-stated:** Confidence conclusions cover four Qwen models; sharpness and sampling agreement cover only Qwen3-8B. Latency, token usage, and provider costs are not evaluated (§8).
- **Analyst inference:** Supplementary material is repeatedly referenced but absent here. Consequently, chronological controls, alternative partitions, external-probability exposure, and the unrecalibrated gate cannot be assessed in detail.
- **Analyst inference:** Reported significance against the global simplex pool does not fully isolate domain routing from weight constraints: the main gate permits signed weights, while that comparator does not (§§5–7).

## Open research questions
1. **Does competence routing remain useful prospectively?** Historical contamination and changing competence remain unresolved (§8). Freeze the gate before future questions and evaluate chronological performance and calibration.
2. **Can question-level features improve routing?** Current domains are source labels (§8). Compare source-only gating with horizon, disagreement, and prior-uncertainty features under identical held-out evaluation.
3. **How much gain comes from routing versus signed weights and recalibration?** The main comparisons vary these components, and detailed ablations are unavailable. Run a matched global/domain × affine/simplex × calibrated/uncalibrated comparison.
4. **Can richer uncertainty signals predict relative advantage?** Confidence coverage is narrow (§8). Compare learned and internal uncertainty signals with outcome-based competence across additional model families using the same comparative target.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The main method and numerical findings are explicit, but their full validation is unavailable.

Missing material includes supplementary results and construction details, hyperparameter values, complete domain weights and sample counts, exact market-subset Brier scores, full confidence-elicitation and abstention protocols, and confirmation of released code or data.
