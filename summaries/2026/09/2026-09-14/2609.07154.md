# Ambient @ EgoLongQA 2026: Distilling Long-Video perception into a Sub-2B Model

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.07154) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07154)


## One-sentence takeaway
The paper reports that distilling correct junior-perception traces into a vocabulary-pruned 1.9985B vision-language model raises held-out EgoLongQA accuracy from 27.1% to 81.4% and wins the ≤2B test division with 0.8279 accuracy (Tables 3–5).

## Problem and motivation
EgoLongQA asks four-option questions about roughly ten-minute egocentric videos, often requiring reasoning about events relative to an anchor event. The challenge’s ≤2B limit includes the vision tower, making the authors’ large, tool-using video agent unsuitable for this division (§1).

The authors investigate whether its single-pass perception component can provide effective supervision for a small model. They also address an oversized backbone and answer-position bias that can inflate validation accuracy (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Component-level trace distillation:** Train a Qwen3.5 student on 2,936 traces covering 605 videos, retaining correct answers with unanimous agreement across three junior passes. The student receives sampled video frames and a question, then generates a description and structured answer without the senior agent’s tool loop (§§2.1–2.3).
- **Synthetic supervision and prior correction:** Generate 943 questions over 408 Ego4D videos, filtering questions answerable without video and those lacking visible evidence. Balance answer positions and evaluate with shuffled options to reduce reliance on answer-letter priors (§§2.4–2.5; Fig. 2).
- **Vocabulary pruning:** Reduce the tied embedding vocabulary from 248,320 to 143,469 rows, bringing the checkpoint from 2.2132B to 1.9985B parameters. The authors report unchanged retained-row logits and identical generation on their held-out items (§2.6).
- **Efficient adaptation:** Use one-epoch LoRA training, extra loss weight on answer-choice tokens, and higher visual resolution at inference (§2.5). These are implementation choices; the central contribution is their integration with component-level distillation.

The reported results support strong task-specific gains from trace distillation and an additional external-evaluation benefit from synthetic augmentation. They do not isolate the effects of teacher selection, unanimity filtering, description supervision, or answer-token weighting. The leaderboard results are reproduced from the supplied paper, without independent verification.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Internal inconsistencies:** Table 2’s caption calls its final model “synthetic-only,” but the row specifies distillation plus synthetic. Section 3 gives a 65% single-pass baseline for a 27B model, whereas Table 4 lists 14.3%. The frame-budget arithmetic is also inconsistent.
- **Unresolved headline comparison:** The abstract’s “89%” of agent accuracy and “1.1%” of its parameters lack a clearly reconciled comparison; §1 instead reports the agent at the same 81.4% as the distilled student.
- **Protocol ambiguity:** The claimed absence of input mismatch is not fully supported: Table 1 includes 400-frame teacher traces, while student inference uses 100 frames. Details of training-option permutation and trace adaptation are incomplete.
- **Author-reported negative result:** GRPO produced no reproducible held-out gain. The authors suspect insufficient unseen, in-distribution rollout data; this explanation is not experimentally established (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** High accuracy on unanimous junior cases does not establish that junior supervision is superior to senior supervision, because the senior receives harder cases (§2.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Pruning equivalence on retained rows and tested generations does not establish unrestricted generative equivalence. Handling of removed input tokens is insufficiently described (§2.6).

## Open research questions
1. **Which trace component causes the gain?** No controlled junior-versus-senior or filtering ablation is reported. Compare matched-size training sets using junior traces, senior-derived answers, and unfiltered junior traces.
2. **Does synthetic augmentation improve visual reasoning or mainly answer-position robustness?** Table 2 changes both accuracy and letter frequency. Evaluate balanced and unbalanced synthetic augmentation with repeated option permutations and video-masked controls.
3. **Why does the frame-budget effect differ by model size?** The small- and large-model observations use different settings (§4.2). Sweep frame count across model sizes with matched questions, resolution, and decoding.
4. **How broadly does pruning preserve behavior?** Equivalence is tested on a limited held-out set. Compare original and pruned checkpoints on prompts containing removed tokens and on outputs requiring rare vocabulary.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The central results are explicit, but contradictory labels and comparisons weaken interpretation.

Material missing information includes exact split and question counts, uncertainty estimates, training seeds and learning rate, detailed external-set construction, hardware and latency methodology, removed-token handling, component ablations, and verified release contents.
