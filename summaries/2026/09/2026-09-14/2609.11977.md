# Occamy-1.0: Open Pareto-frontier 35B Intelligence for Co-work

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11977) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11977)


## One-sentence takeaway
Occamy-1.0 improves stateful agent execution over its Qwen3.6-35B-A3B starting checkpoint, with lower measured Claw-Eval resource use and competitive aggregate performance under a shared token-pricing proxy, although individual training components’ contributions remain incompletely isolated (§6).

## Problem and motivation
Long workflows accumulate inference costs while requiring reliable state tracking, tool use, recovery, and artifact verification. The authors identify a gap between strong standalone reasoning and sustained execution across changing environments and compacted conversation histories; they target useful end-to-end capability at a self-hostable model scale (§1).

## Approach and contributions
The contribution is principally an integrated training system and resulting checkpoint; HDPO, SAO, context-compaction RL, and model soups are credited to prior work.

- **Execution-grounded data:** Environment-first and capability-first synthesis produce executable task contracts with public inputs, persistent state, tools, and private graders. Reference and negative executions test feasibility and grader discrimination (§3; Appendix A).
- **Replayable infrastructure:** Harness adapters preserve exact sampled tokens, environment state, delegation lineage, and history-rewrite boundaries. Only sampled policy outputs receive training loss; rewritten context remains conditioning (§4).
- **Specialization and consolidation:** A long-horizon Marathon checkpoint receives SFT and HDPO; a broader Sprint checkpoint receives SFT. Uniform parameter averaging combines them, followed by SAO with trainable context compaction (§5; Appendix C).
- **Reported releases:** Model weights, a training-data subset, and Dressage infrastructure with training recipes; deployment-specific components may be omitted (abstract; §4.5).

These results support checkpoint-level improvements under the reported environments. They do not establish universal frontier-model parity, statistically significant small ranking differences, or actual deployment total cost. The authors’ Pareto claim depends on the selected models, four benchmarks, normalization, and frozen pricing proxy.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated gaps:** Timeout robustness, native browser/visual interaction, and joint learning across subagents and multiple objectives remain unfinished (§8).
- **Causal attribution — analyst inference:** SFT, merging, and RL all affect the final checkpoint. Selected intermediate results are provided, but controlled quantitative ablations isolating synthesis routes, harness diversity, replay, and compaction are absent; SFT-ablation lessons are qualitative (§§6.4–7.3).
- **Comparison boundaries:** GDPval uses an independently selected cohort and judge panel; VitaBench changes simulator/judge settings; some coding baselines are externally reported. These constrain comparisons with outside leaderboards (Table 5; Appendix B.3).
- **Reporting uncertainty:** No confidence intervals accompany main results. Claw-Eval’s “one attempt per task” description does not clearly reconcile with Table 6’s repeated attempts, and pass3 aggregation is not fully specified (Appendix B.3; Table 6).
- **Audit scope:** Four confirmed hacking trajectories among 4,352 audited trajectories came from one task. This supports a localized observed defect, but does not establish absence of broader exploitative behavior; uncertain cases remain subject to review (§7.4).
- **Reproducibility:** Only a data subset is released, detailed mixture accounting remains internal, and deployment services may be missing (§4.5; Appendix A.7).

## Open research questions
1. **Which components produce the gains?** Existing ablations do not isolate the full recipe. Next experiment: compute-matched variants removing each synthesis route, harness diversity, merging, or final RL.
2. **Does continuity training transfer to unfamiliar harnesses?** Controlled held-out harness results are not reported. Next experiment: evaluate unseen compaction policies and tool-schema compositions on fixed tasks.
3. **Can subagent learning improve complete workflows?** Current RL excludes other trajectories in the episode from direct policy loss (§5.2). Next experiment: compare current training with joint parent–child optimization under equal rollout budgets.
4. **How stable is the cost frontier?** Costs depend on pricing and execution settings (§6.6; Appendix B.2). Next experiment: vary workload weights, cache conditions, concurrency, and measured self-hosting costs.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the reported system design and benchmark comparisons, but not strong causal or universal cost claims.

Material missing information includes per-expert data allocation, complete RL task counts and mixture weights, detailed Marathon/HDPO training configuration, quantitative component ablations, statistical uncertainty, a reconciled Claw-Eval repetition protocol, and complete deployment/training-cost accounting.
