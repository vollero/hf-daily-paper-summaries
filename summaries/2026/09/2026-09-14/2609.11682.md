# COBRA-Skills: Contextual Bandit-Guided Evolution for Agent Skill Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-14.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11682) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11682)


## One-sentence takeaway

COBRA-Skills combines bandit-guided candidate selection with trajectory-grounded skill evolution, achieving the highest average scores across six benchmarks for three target models at 55–58% lower reported optimization cost than SkillOpt (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation

Reusable textual skills can improve agent behavior, but evaluating candidate skills requires expensive task executions, while repeated trajectory analysis and rewriting add teaching-model costs. The authors identify a need to allocate evaluations selectively and reuse execution evidence efficiently when task data and optimization budgets are limited (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Optimization formulation:** Treat textual skills as candidates in a budgeted sequential search with an evolving candidate population; the objective is performance on unseen tasks from the same distribution (§2).
- **Bandit-guided selection and retention:** Represent skills using fixed embeddings, predict rewards with a small neural network, and add a LinearUCB-style exploration bonus. The combined score determines both which skill to evaluate and which candidates to retain (§3.2).
- **Evidence-grounded evolution:** Periodically replace low-priority candidates through regeneration from no-skill trajectories, mutation from the selected skill’s execution feedback, and crossover using strong and weak skills. New candidates inherit no reward; the final output is the evaluated skill with the highest mean observed reward (§3.3; Algorithm 1).

The claimed methodological contribution is this coupling of adaptive evaluation, population retention, and evidence-based evolution—not the individual bandit or evolutionary components (§6.2).

These results support stronger **average** performance and lower token-priced optimization cost under the reported configurations. They do not establish superiority on every benchmark, statistical significance of every difference, or that bandit selection alone causes the savings. Target-model token use exceeds SkillOpt for Qwen and Gemma; reduced teaching-model usage drives the cost advantage (Table 2).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-reported comparison constraint:** OfficeQA was excluded because the authors could not reliably reproduce SkillOpt’s reported results (Appendix A.3).
- **Analyst inference—unequal optimization protocols:** COBRA-Skills uses 50 unique examples per benchmark, while baselines use benchmark-dependent larger pools and different optimization procedures. The results compare complete configurations, without isolating performance at identical data and monetary budgets (Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference—limited mechanism isolation:** Ablations cover Qwen under the native harness. They do not separately test each evolution operator, neural prediction versus exploration, or scheduling. The no-evolution variant also changes the exploration coefficient (Table 4; Appendix A.1).
- **Analyst inference—restricted generalization evidence:** Self-teaching and external-harness experiments use Qwen only. The formal objective assumes the same task distribution, and target reasoning is disabled (§2; §§4.1, 5.2).
- **Analyst inference—cost and uncertainty scope:** Reported costs price target/teacher tokens; embedding computation, predictor training, and wall-clock overhead are not quantified. Three-run standard errors are provided, but formal significance tests are absent (Appendix B.1; Tables 1–4).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the advantage persist with matched resources?** Current baseline data pools and procedures differ. Run all optimizers on identical examples under matched token-cost budgets (Appendix A.2).
2. **Which evolution mechanisms matter most?** Existing ablations remove evolution as a whole. Remove regeneration, mutation, and crossover individually while holding evaluation and generation budgets fixed (§5.1).
3. **How broadly does self-teaching work?** It is tested only with Qwen. Repeat teacher-versus-self comparisons for the other target models, measuring both task scores and costs (§5.2).
4. **Can selection anticipate transfer failures?** Cross-model transfer has exceptions. Test transfer-aware candidate scoring on held-out recipient models and compare it with source-model-only selection (§5.3).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the method description and reported comparisons well; causal attribution and broader generalization remain less certain.

Material missing information includes matched-budget comparisons, operator-level ablations, formal significance tests, exact self-teaching cost totals, non-token computational overhead, and the contents of the claimed code release.
