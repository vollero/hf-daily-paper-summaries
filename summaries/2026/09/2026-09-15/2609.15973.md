# Discovery Foundation Models: Toward Open-Ended Discovery Intelligence

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15973) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15973)


## One-sentence takeaway

The paper proposes Discovery Foundation Models as systems that can revise the structure of a research program, instantiates the concept in the Zetema architecture, and grounds part of the framework through an existing therapeutic peptide discovery case, while leaving general cross-task capability untested.

## Problem and motivation

Most foundation models operate after humans have specified the question, representation, objective, tools, and evaluator. The authors argue that open-ended discovery also requires recognizing valuable unknowns, repairing malformed questions, constructing representations, choosing discriminating interventions, attributing failures, and revising the research process itself (§§1–2.3).

This matters when additional search within a fixed task cannot resolve missing variables, non-identifying measurements, evaluator mismatch, or observationally equivalent mechanisms. The identified gap is therefore a lack of systems and evaluations centered on revisable research structure and evidence-driven change, rather than final-answer performance alone (§§2.1–2.3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Capability definition:** The authors define a DFM as a general-purpose model system with seven coupled capabilities: problem discovery, problem formulation, representation construction, hypothesis formation, intervention design, evidence-grounded revision, and continual improvement across tasks (§§3.1–3.2, Eq. 3).

2. **Discovery Process:** They formalize discovery as transitions over an explicit, revisable research state. Evidence may cause revision of the problem, representation, hypotheses, intervention plan, or uncertainty rather than merely selecting a final answer (§4, Eqs. 5–9).

3. **Zetema architecture:** Zetema combines explicit research state, branching and rollback, provenance, a Research World Model, verification and action gates, Dry-Lab/Wet-Lab interfaces, and validated cross-task Discovery Skill memory (§5, Eqs. 10–15).

4. **Training and evaluation proposal:** The paper specifies a compositional training objective spanning action imitation, state-transition learning, preferences, verification, world modeling, reinforcement learning, and skill retrieval (§6, Eqs. 16–25). Evaluation separates current validated knowledge progress from improvement on future discovery tasks (§7, Eq. 27).

5. **Physical case study:** The authors map GALILEO, an existing therapeutic peptide discovery system, onto parts of the DFM framework. Its laboratory results reportedly altered later target beliefs, assays, mechanism hypotheses, and design policies (§5.7, Fig. 5). The paper explicitly says this does not demonstrate a full general-purpose DFM.

| General DFM performance is not empirically established | The training procedure and evaluation protocol are formulations; no trained Zetema model, benchmark scores, ablations, or baseline comparisons are reported | §§6–7; Algorithm 1 |

The evidence supports the narrower author claim that a real therapeutic workflow can contain an intervention–evidence–revision loop. It does not establish that Zetema has been implemented, that the proposed training recipe works, or that Discovery Skills transfer across unrelated tasks or domains. The two \(IC_{50}\) values come from different target and organoid settings, so the supplied text does not justify a direct comparative efficacy claim.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** GALILEO operates within a substantially structured problem domain, molecular modality, experimental interface, and objective; it does not demonstrate a general-purpose DFM or broad cross-domain transfer (§5.7).

- **Author-stated limitation:** The framework does not claim that general autonomous scientific discovery has been solved or that every component should use one architecture (§10).

- **Author-stated risk:** World-model errors can systematically suppress unusual interventions or favor actions consistent with a misspecified model (§5.4).

- **Author-stated risk:** Persistent skills and recursive updates can create self-reinforcing selection, evaluator drift, negative transfer, or narrowing of explored strategies (§§6.5, 9.4).

- **Analyst inference:** Much of the paper is a conceptual and formal specification. No implementation details, model checkpoint, training corpus, experimental budget, benchmark execution, or quantitative Zetema result are provided.

- **Analyst inference:** The supplied GALILEO evidence lacks sample sizes, uncertainty intervals, statistical tests, full controls, and numerical baseline comparisons. This prevents assessment of robustness from this paper alone.

- **Analyst inference:** The proposed training objective contains many components and weights, but the text provides no ablation showing which components are necessary or whether their signals can be measured reliably (§6.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The GALILEO case is presented as empirical grounding for the framework rather than a controlled evaluation of Zetema; consequently, it cannot isolate the contribution of explicit state, gating, skill memory, or other proposed modules.

## Open research questions

1. **Can the proposed discovery operations improve performance beyond strong scientific agents?**  
   This remains open because no Zetema-versus-baseline experiment is reported. A next experiment could compare Zetema with a resource-matched tool-using agent on hidden-variable and misframed-problem tasks, measuring identifying interventions and validated state revisions.

2. **Do Discovery Skills transfer independently of facts, prompts, and extra compute?**  
   The paper proposes controls but reports no transfer results. A next experiment could evaluate no memory, fact-only memory, full-trajectory retrieval, and Discovery Skill Memory on held-out mechanisms under identical retrieval and compute budgets.

3. **Which architectural components produce the claimed behavior?**  
   No ablation evidence is supplied. A next experiment could remove explicit research state, the Research World Model, action gating, or rollback individually and measure failure attribution, resource use, and response to counterevidence.

4. **Does the GALILEO-derived design rule generalize beyond its optimization setting?**  
   Transfer to external workflows is asserted qualitatively, without metrics in the supplied text. A next experiment could preregister evaluation of the Amphiphilic Balance Grammar on held-out target families against matched peptide-design baselines.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s proposed framework and its stated scope; **medium** for interpreting empirical strength because the case-study reporting is incomplete.

Material missing information includes:

- A Zetema implementation or trained model.
- Benchmark results and quantitative baseline comparisons.
- Ablations of system and training components.
- Training data, model sizes, compute, and hyperparameters.
- GALILEO sample sizes, statistical uncertainty, complete controls, and raw measurements.
- Quantitative evidence for Amphiphilic Balance Grammar transfer.
- Independent replication of the therapeutic findings.
- A released DFM dataset, benchmark, or executable evaluation suite.
