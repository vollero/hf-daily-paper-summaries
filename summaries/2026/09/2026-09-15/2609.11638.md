# Vidu S2: Real-Time Interactive, Editable, and Spatial Video Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11638) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11638)


## One-sentence takeaway

Vidu S2 combines real-time 720p interactive avatar generation and streaming video editing, using Self-Replay Forcing, frame-aligned conditioning, and an optimized serving stack to achieve leading reported benchmark results, while its spatial-video extension remains qualitatively evaluated.

## Problem and motivation

Most video diffusion systems generate complete videos offline, preventing users from changing instructions or references during generation. The authors target interactive settings such as digital characters, live streams, games, and headset experiences, where outputs must respond continuously and with low latency (Section 1).

Their previous Vidu S1 system was limited to 540p, fixed references, mostly talking-head motion, and no editing of incoming streams. Vidu S2 addresses these gaps through higher-resolution avatar generation, dynamic references, broader body-motion control, real-time editing, and exploratory stereoscopic output (Sections 1 and 4).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Vidu S2-Avatar:** An audio-visual Diffusion Transformer for reference-conditioned, segment-wise streaming generation. It supports 720p output at a claimed 25–42 FPS, dynamic reference images, and motions such as dancing (Sections 1 and 2.2).

2. **Self-Replay Forcing:** The student first generates a detached autoregressive trajectory, which is re-noised and replayed through one differentiable causal pass. This exposes training to inference-time histories while allowing gradients across replayed segment boundaries (Section 2.2, Equation 4).

3. **Instruction and preference optimization:** Segment-level temporally ordered captions, bidirectional-to-causal training, diffusion DPO, and Streaming Negative-aware Fine-Tuning target instruction adherence, motion quality, and robustness to accumulated errors (Sections 2.1–2.2).

4. **Streaming video editing:** Vidu S2-Editing performs style transfer, virtual try-on, subject replacement, and background replacement. Frame-aligned attention restricts each target frame to its corresponding source frame while making the reference image available throughout the sequence (Sections 3.1–3.2).

5. **Efficient inference:** Hybrid efficient attention, blockwise W8A8 GEMM, fused kernels, CUDA Graphs, quantized multi-GPU communication, and shared scheduling across model modules are used to reduce latency and GPU demand (Sections 2.3 and 3.3).

6. **Spatial-video exploration:** Monocular outputs are converted into synchronized stereo views using stabilized depth and disparity-based warping; existing stereo streams can instead be edited jointly as horizontally concatenated views (Section 4).

These results support strong relative performance across the selected public benchmarks and internal human evaluations. They do not isolate which training or serving component causes each gain, demonstrate generalization beyond the evaluated tasks, or quantitatively validate spatial depth quality, headset comfort, end-to-end latency, cost, or resource requirements.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** Spatial video requires higher resolution and lower end-to-end latency than ordinary monocular streaming; headset passthrough latency can desynchronize head motion and display output (Section 4.3).

- **Author-stated limitation:** The current spatial system is fixed-view. Panoramic spatial video that supports head movement is left as future work (Section 4.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Spatial-video evidence is qualitative only. Figure 13 shows stereo examples, but no depth, stereo consistency, latency, comfort, or user-study results are reported.

- **Analyst inference:** The paper provides no component ablations for Self-Replay Forcing, the refiner, preference optimization, captioning changes, frame-aligned attention, or individual inference optimizations. Their separate contributions therefore cannot be quantified from the supplied text.

- **Analyst inference:** Internal benchmarks may have limited external validity. Although the protocol describes 20 trained evaluators, randomization, and retained records, the internal prompts, samples, system versions, and annotations are not supplied (Sections 5.1.1 and 5.1.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is constrained because model sizes, training compute, optimization settings, dataset licensing and composition details, precise hardware configurations, latency measurements, and code or weight availability are not reported in the retrieved text.

- **Analyst inference:** The claim of supporting more than 50 styles is not accompanied by per-style results or a clearly specified evaluation protocol (Section 3.1).

## Open research questions

1. **Which parts of Self-Replay Forcing produce the reported long-horizon gains?**  
   The paper provides no ablation against Self-Forcing, replay without cross-block gradients, or alternative replay lengths. A controlled comparison on StreamAV-Bench could vary these elements while measuring SC, BC, quality, and collapse rate over longer streams.

2. **How well does spatial output preserve binocular and temporal consistency?**  
   Only qualitative stereo examples are provided. A next experiment could measure disparity errors, temporal depth flicker, stereo comfort, and motion-to-photon latency on both monocular conversion and joint stereo editing.

3. **What is the quality–latency–hardware tradeoff?**  
   The paper claims 25–42 FPS but does not report hardware-specific latency or component costs. Profiling the backbone, refiner, VAE stages, communication, and agent loop across GPU counts would clarify practical deployment requirements.

4. **How robust are dynamic references and editing under repeated or conflicting updates?**  
   The benchmark covers runtime updates, but the supplied results do not separately quantify reference switching or conflicting instructions. A stress test could issue rapid object, clothing, identity, and background changes and measure response delay, state retention, and unintended edits.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated architecture, tasks, benchmark scores, and comparison protocols because the complete retrieved text includes methods and Tables 1–4. **Confidence is medium** for broad claims of real-time practicality and spatial-video quality because supporting measurements are incomplete.

Material missing information includes:

- Model parameter counts, training schedules, and compute requirements.
- Exact inference hardware, GPU count, batch size, latency, memory, and cost for the 25–42 FPS claim.
- Ablations for the proposed training, data, and serving components.
- Quantitative spatial-video evaluation.
- Detailed construction and public availability of internal benchmarks.
- Statistical uncertainty or significance tests for human evaluations.
- Availability and licensing of model weights, code, and training datasets.
