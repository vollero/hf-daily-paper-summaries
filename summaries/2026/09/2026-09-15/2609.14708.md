# Lightning Weave: Improving the Accuracy-Efficiency Frontier of Reasoning Models through Capability Composition

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14708) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14708)


## One-sentence takeaway
Lightning Weave combines cached policy changes from accuracy- and efficiency-oriented specialists, improving average accuracy while reducing response tokens across five tested students, with guarantees limited to targets on cached states (Table 1; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
Reasoning models can spend many tokens exploring solutions, but shortening their reasoning can reduce accuracy. The authors ask whether independently trained specialists can contribute complementary improvements to one student.

Their identified technical gap is that naively reusing cached policy-shift supervision can produce an incorrect regularization gradient as the student changes, pushing it beyond the intended target (§§1, 3.2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Compose policy changes:** Represent each capability by the token-probability log-ratio between a specialist and its checkpoint before post-training. Combine weighted, aligned shifts at shared student-generated states; this builds on existing policy-shift distillation (§3.3).
- **Correct cached supervision:** Tilt the initial student distribution using the combined shift, then train toward that explicit target. The full-distribution formulation has a unique local target and zero gradient when the student matches it (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Make composition reusable:** Cache anchor scores once, then train students with different composition weights without live anchors. The implementation uses 16 candidate tokens plus a residual outcome and a squared log-residual loss—not the full-distribution KL analyzed theoretically (Appendix B.4).

The principal experiments combine Klear’s accuracy-oriented shift with DECS’s efficiency-oriented shift. Mathematics and code use separately trained checkpoints (Appendices B.1–B.2).

These results support improved empirical trade-offs within the tested settings, rather than universal dominance on both metrics. Some individual benchmarks use more tokens after composition. The authors’ broader state-of-the-art claim is supported only by the reported comparison set; token savings do not establish deployment latency savings.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments cover models below 10B; matched pre/post anchors may be unavailable; efficiency gains remain smaller than those of methods optimized exclusively for efficiency (Appendix E).
- **Author-stated theoretical limits:** Guarantees apply to local targets on cached states, not sequence-level optimality, optimizer convergence, coverage of later student states, or improved task outcomes. The implemented surrogate does not inherit full-KL objective equivalence (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Each configuration is trained once. Repeated decoding does not measure training-seed variability, and confidence intervals are not reported (Appendix B.8).
- **Analyst inference—approximation sensitivity:** No reported ablation isolates candidate count, zero-shift residual treatment, or alignment masking. Their contribution to performance remains unresolved (Appendix B.4; §4.5).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation boundaries:** Separate math/code checkpoints do not demonstrate a single model retaining both domains. Each math benchmark contains only 30 problems; additional Table 8 code results use Pass@1 and should not be directly combined with the main Avg@4 results (Appendices B.2, B.5, C.2).

## Open research questions
- **Does refreshing cached states improve transfer after substantial policy change?** Fixed-state guarantees leave this unresolved; compare a fixed cache with periodic refresh under matched total compute (Appendix A).
- **How much do finite-candidate approximations matter?** The practical loss differs from the theoretical objective; vary candidate count and compare against full-vocabulary targets on a tractable student (Appendix B.4).
- **Do gains extend to larger models and tool use?** Both remain outside the demonstrated scope; test matched anchor compositions on larger students and agentic benchmarks (Appendix E).
- **Can one checkpoint retain the math and code gains together?** Training is domain-separated; compare joint-domain training against the separate checkpoints with matched data and updates (Appendix B.2).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the description of the method and reported results; **medium** in robustness and broader generalization.

Material missing information includes training-seed variance, confidence intervals, deployment latency, CPU target-construction cost, approximation ablations, and a released code artifact. The supplied text promises code release but does not establish its availability.
