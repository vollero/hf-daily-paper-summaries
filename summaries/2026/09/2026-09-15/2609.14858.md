# Dream-RSI: Recursive Self-Improvement through Evolving Worlds

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14858) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14858)


## One-sentence takeaway

Dream-RSI reuses recorded discovery trees to refine an agent’s exploration policy offline, and the authors report better discovery quality or lower agent-call cost across eight algorithm, mathematics, and GPU-kernel tasks.

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon agent discovery may require thousands of proposal and evaluation cycles, making exploration strategy a major cost driver. The authors identify two limitations of existing approaches: fixed exploration policies cannot learn from accumulated experience, while online policy optimization requires expensive, delayed feedback from full discovery rollouts.

Their proposed gap is an inexpensive way to evaluate alternative exploration policies before deploying them online. They argue that completed discovery histories contain enough recorded decisions and outcomes to simulate alternative traversals of the observed search space (Sections 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Discovery history as a replay simulator.** Each online rollout is stored as a tree of workspace states, attempts, diagnostics, scores, and parent-child relationships. Alternative policies can reveal recorded branches in different orders and batches without rerunning the coding agent or evaluator (Sections 2–3; Figure 2).

2. **Programmable exploration layer.** A lightweight policy controls branch selection, parallel batches, and stopping while the discovery agent, evaluator, and execution interface remain fixed (Section 3).

3. **Recursive policy-improvement loop.** Dream-RSI alternates between online exploration, simulator construction, and LLM-driven revisions of executable exploration-policy code. The best policy by average replay score is redeployed to collect another discovery tree (Section 3; Figure 1).

4. **Quality, cost, and parallelism objective.** Replay scoring combines the best discovered score, a penalty for represented generation-evaluation requests, and a bonus for useful batching (Section 3, Equation 1).

5. **Cross-domain evaluation.** The framework is tested on eight tasks covering Lasso solver discovery, three mathematical optimization problems, and four KernelBench workloads (Section 4).

These results support the authors’ claim that replay-guided policy updates can improve the quality-cost trade-off in the tested settings. They do not establish that replay-selected policies will always improve online: the formal non-degradation result applies only to average replay score on the fixed historical trees, not unseen stochastic rollouts (Section 3). The supplied text also does not report uncertainty estimates or statistical tests.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Source truncation:** The supplied source omits 14,699 characters from the middle of Appendix C. This analysis therefore does not cover the complete paper text or complete solver implementation.

- **Author-defined replay boundary:** Replay can traverse only outcomes already present in historical trees and cannot estimate results for unrecorded actions or branches (Section 3). This limits policy evaluation to the realized search space.

- **Replay improvement is an in-sample guarantee:** Policy selection ensures a non-decreasing average replay score on the fixed history, but the paper does not claim a corresponding guarantee for the next online rollout (Section 3).

- **Analyst inference — uncertain robustness:** No repeated-run statistics, confidence intervals, or significance tests are visible in the supplied experiments. Stochastic variability therefore cannot be assessed.

- **Analyst inference — external-baseline comparability:** Several task-specific baselines use different models and budgets. Those comparisons show achieved results and resource counts, but do not isolate the contribution of Dream-RSI as cleanly as the controlled Recursive Fixed Exploration comparison.

- **Analyst inference — limited ablation coverage:** The supplied analysis compares interactive replay with semantic prompt guidance on ConvDiv, but does not provide component ablations for the replay objective’s quality, cost, and parallelism terms.

- **Analyst inference — incomplete reproduction detail:** Prompts and the Lasso program are provided, but the supplied text does not fully specify hardware, run-to-run protocol, all hyperparameter values such as \(\beta_1,\beta_2,K_1,K_2,M\), or the identity and settings of the policy-development model.

## Open research questions

1. **How well does replay score predict unseen online performance?** The formal guarantee is confined to stored histories. A next experiment could measure correlations between candidate policies’ replay rankings and their results across multiple fresh online rollouts.

2. **Which replay-objective terms drive the gains?** The paper combines quality, request cost, and parallelism, but the supplied text contains no term-by-term ablation. An experiment could remove or sweep each term while holding trees and policy-development budgets fixed.

3. **How much simulator diversity is required?** Policies are evaluated against an expanding pool of historical trees, but sample efficiency as a function of pool size is not reported. A controlled study could train on different numbers and types of trees and test on held-out discovery runs.

4. **Do improvements persist across agents and hardware environments?** Results use two Gemini variants for Lasso and Gemini-3.1 Pro for the mathematics and kernel tasks. Testing additional coding agents and GPU platforms would assess whether learned exploration behavior transfers beyond these settings.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The main method, controlled baseline, metrics, and headline results are described clearly, but coverage is incomplete because the source was truncated.

Material unavailable or unclear in the supplied text includes the omitted portion of Appendix C, full experimental hardware details, variance across independent runs, statistical testing, complete replay hyperparameters, policy-development model configuration, per-task generation counts for the mathematics experiments, exact values underlying Figure 5, and detailed ablations of the replay objective and recursive-loop components.
