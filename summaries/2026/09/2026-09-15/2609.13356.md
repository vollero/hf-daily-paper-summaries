# ZGCM-1: A Fully Open and Extremely Efficient Foundation Model for Math and Agentic Search

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13356) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13356)


## One-sentence takeaway

ZGCM-1 is an openly released 7.39B model whose hybrid attention, progressive long-context training, dense action supervision, and mixed post-training deliver strong mathematical and tool-based performance, although several efficiency and capability claims rely on estimated or narrowly scoped comparisons.

## Problem and motivation

The authors target two barriers to research on reasoning and agents: frontier models often require prohibitive compute, and open-weight releases commonly omit training data recipes, curricula, intermediate checkpoints, and telemetry. They argue that a compact model can partly offset limited stored knowledge through deliberate reasoning and external tool use, provided it can train and operate efficiently over long contexts (Section 1, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **Efficient long-context architecture:** A 7.39B decoder-only Transformer interleaves 27 gated 128-token sliding-window layers with five global-attention layers, supporting 256K tokens at a 5:1 local-to-global ratio (Section 2.1, Figures 3–4).
- **Full training recipe:** General pre-training is followed by 600.51B tokens of mid-training with staged 16K, 64K, and 256K contexts. Interaction traces are also reformulated as state-conditioned action examples for denser supervision (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figures 5–7).
- **System and optimization co-design:** Hybrid FP8 computation, Muon, TWEO outlier suppression, and hybrid attention are combined to reduce training time and memory use (Section 3.1.3).
- **Calibrated post-training:** The pipeline uses quality-filtered mixed think/no-think SFT, joint general-agent training, and GRPO over mathematics, code, and general tasks (Section 4).
- **Evaluation and reusable assets:** The work evaluates reasoning, coding, knowledge, instruction following, web research, and binary analysis; it reports releasing weights from multiple stages, checkpoints, code, data recipes, logs, and evaluation harnesses (Sections 5 and 7.1).
- **AI-assisted model development:** Agents participated in data processing, cluster operations, experiments, evaluation, and deployment, with contributor-rated autonomy varying substantially by task (Section 6).

| Long-context compute and memory improve substantially | Against full attention on the same 7B backbone, SWA 5:1 is 3.94× faster at 256K. At batch 1 in BF16, KV cache is 5.0 GiB versus 32.0 GiB, a 6.4× reduction. | Sections 2.2–2.3; Figures 4b–c |

The evidence supports strong long-context systems efficiency and competitive performance on selected mathematics, web-research, and binary-analysis evaluations. It does not establish uniform superiority over other 7B–8B models: Table 2 contains substantial deficits in knowledge, coding, and instruction following. It also does not isolate the causal contribution of the full training recipe, because several reported gains combine multiple interventions or use estimated multiplicative factors.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** The 7.39B parameter scale limits closed-book factual recall; external search only helps where tools are available (Section 7.2).
- **Author-stated:** Heavy reasoning supervision can increase verbosity and weaken strict instruction adherence (Sections 4.1.1 and 7.2).
- **Author-stated:** General repository engineering and terminal agency remain immature, with modest completion rates (Section 7.2; Appendices C.3–C.4).
- **Author-stated:** Agent behavior is brittle to schema changes, noisy observations, tool-format deviations, latency, and unstable environment feedback (Section 7.2).
- **Author-stated:** Web results depend on search date, providers, extraction, summarization, judging, and retry policy (Appendix C.1).
- **Analyst inference:** The 4.2× time-to-loss result is a product of component estimates rather than one directly reported end-to-end controlled run, limiting causal confidence (Section 3.1.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons are not fully uniform: external benchmark values come from cited releases, some models use fewer runs, and web baselines were not all evaluated through the same harness (Section 5.1; Appendix F).
- **Analyst inference:** Binary Function Search reports only a 50-task sample from a much larger pool, so uncertainty across projects and task types is not quantified (Section 5.3; Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The mixed think/no-think comparison is observational and may differ in tokens, data, checkpoint history, and optimization schedule (Appendix B.4).

## Open research questions

1. **Which component actually drives the reported 4.2× training improvement?** The paper combines separately estimated factors. A matched end-to-end ablation could add SWA, FP8/TWEO, Muon, and normalization choices one at a time under identical hardware and target loss.

2. **Does MDP-style state-action supervision improve tool use beyond full-trajectory training?** The paper describes the transformation but gives no isolated downstream ablation. A controlled experiment could compare identical trajectory pools with full-sequence, state-action, and combined supervision.

3. **How well does agentic performance transfer across protocols and noisy environments?** The authors identify schema and environment brittleness. An experiment could perturb tool schemas, observation formatting, latency, and missing results across web, terminal, and binary tasks.

4. **Do Binary Function Search results persist across the full validated pool?** The reported evaluation uses 50 tasks from ten projects. A larger project-stratified evaluation with confidence intervals could test robustness across compilers, architectures, project families, and function types.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the paper’s stated methods, benchmark settings, and reported values because the supplied text includes the main paper and appendices.

Material gaps remain: exact compute expenditure and training duration; full per-stage data manifests and immutable artifact revisions; statistical uncertainty for most benchmark comparisons; a direct end-to-end validation of the 4.2× claim; isolated ablations for MDP supervision, joint agent/general SFT, and mixed RL; and full-scale Binary Function Search results.
