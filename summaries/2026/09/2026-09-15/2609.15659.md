# Kaininja: Extending Native 3D Generators to the Part Level

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15659) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15659)


## One-sentence takeaway
KaiNinja adapts TRELLIS.2 to generate separate part meshes from one image, improving geometry and in-corpus part metrics, but losing to the strongest segmentation cascade on external HY3D-Bench part metrics (Tables 1–5).

## Problem and motivation
Native image-to-3D generators produce fused meshes, whereas editing, rigging, and simulation require separate components. Segmentation cascades add cost and depend on segmentation accuracy. The authors identify a representation bottleneck: O-Voxel stores one surface sheet per voxel, so coincident contact surfaces cannot both survive in a single volume (§§1, 3.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Dual-volume O-Voxel packing:** Adapt PartPacker’s existing packing principle to TRELLIS.2’s sparse voxel representation. A contact graph assigns touching parts to different volumes; non-bipartite graphs require merging parts. Connected components recover parts while retaining the representation’s support for open surfaces and materials (§3.1).
- **Stage-specific pretrained adaptation:** The layout flow uses separately fitted streams linked by zero-initialized attention, followed by warmup and joint fine-tuning. The refinement flow retains the backbone architecture, changes attention scope between volumes, and is subsequently fine-tuned; the autoencoders remain frozen (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Mixed-source part supervision:** Training combines CAD, artist-made, and agent-authored assets. The authors claim the first use of agent-authored assets to train a 3D generative model; this historical claim is not independently established by the supplied text (§3.4).
- **Segmentation-free extraction:** Occupancy merging, component grouping, fragment attachment, and geometric cleanup produce the final part meshes. No external segmenter is required, but post-processing materially affects decomposition quality (§3.5; Table 7).

## Evidence and main results
Here, **W** denotes whole-object metrics and **P** denotes matched-part metrics; lower Chamfer distance (CD) is better.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Best in-corpus scores across all reported metrics | On 986 commonly completed held-out objects, KaiNinja versus Hunyuan3D-2.1 + X-Part: CD_W **0.0186 vs 0.0314**; F1_P at 0.05 **0.692 vs 0.597**; mIoU_P **0.521 vs 0.489**. | Table 1 |
| Improvement beyond same-corpus backbone fine-tuning | Versus fine-tuned TRELLIS.2@512: CD_W **0.0186 vs 0.0300** and F1_W at 0.05 **0.919 vs 0.830**. Authors report approximately **2.5×** generation cost. | Table 2; §4.2 |
| Gains on sources outside fine-tuning | On 95 Sketchfab/GitHub assets, strict F1_W **0.901 vs 0.826** for both X-Part cascades; strict F1_P **0.753 vs 0.717** for the closest competitor, TRELLIS.2 + X-Part. KaiNinja does not lead mIoU_P. | Table 4 |
| External benchmark reverses part ranking | On 200 HY3D-Bench objects, KaiNinja versus Hunyuan3D-2.1 + X-Part: strict F1_W **0.724 vs 0.699**, but strict F1_P **0.505 vs 0.555** and mIoU_P **0.356 vs 0.412**. | Table 5 |
| More volumes improve intermediate part fidelity | Stage-1 occupancy, 986 scored objects: three versus two volumes gives strict F1_P **0.795 vs 0.779**. Across 1,000 objects, three volumes fail on **13**, versus **0** for two. | §4.5; Table 6 |
| Post-processing matters more than overlap loss for final metrics | On 977 commonly completed objects, with overlap loss, full post-processing versus none raises strict F1_P **0.675 → 0.692** and reduces mean components **14.06 → 5.43**, against **5.85** ground-truth parts. Without overlap loss, the full pipeline scores **0.695**. | Table 7 |

The evidence supports strong geometry gains and improved part recovery under the curated evaluation convention. It does **not** establish universally superior decomposition: HY3D-Bench favors another method. The authors attribute this reversal to annotation granularity, but no granularity-controlled experiment establishes that explanation. Likewise, the same-corpus comparison supports the complete extension, not packing alone as the cause of improvement.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Dense non-bipartite contacts force part merging. Generation can undersegment, collapse to one volume, or duplicate surfaces; thin shells can evade overlap cleanup. Decoder holes require geometric cleanup (§5; Appendix B).
- **Author-stated:** Decomposition varies with random seed and depends on training annotations. Sketchfab/GitHub evaluation is outside fine-tuning sources but potentially overlaps backbone pretraining (§§4.3, 5).
- **Analyst inference:** Claims of unrestricted part count describe the architecture, not demonstrated scaling: training filters retain objects with 2–32 parts (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Baseline comparisons combine differences in representation, backbone, and training data. There are no reported ablations isolating agent-authored data or the fit/merge/warmup schedule (§§4.2, 4.5).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Some prose values differ from tables. Timing discusses decimation despite the setup saying it was disabled; Table 6’s scored subset is not fully reconciled with its failure counts (§§4.1–4.2, 4.5). Main results lack reported uncertainty across seeds.

## Open research questions
- **Can decomposition granularity be controlled?** HY3D-Bench reverses part rankings. Test granularity-conditioned generation against coarse and fine annotations of the same objects (§4.3).
- **Can additional volumes improve final meshes reliably?** The three-volume experiment measures only layout occupancy and increases failures. Evaluate complete three-volume generation, including runtime and contact-complexity breakdowns (§4.5).
- **What does agent-authored supervision contribute?** Its inclusion is demonstrated, but its incremental benefit is not. Run matched-size training ablations with and without Articraft data (§3.4).
- **Which adaptation steps cause the gains?** The inheritance rationale lacks direct schedule ablations. Compare joint training with and without per-volume fitting and frozen warmup under matched compute (§3.3).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and comparative tables are detailed, but causal attribution and some reporting details remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes per-volume fitting hyperparameters, total training compute, several filtering and cleanup specifications, main-result confidence intervals and seed variability, verified release contents, and quantitative material-quality or downstream editing/rigging/simulation evaluations.
