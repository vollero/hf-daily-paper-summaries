# Not All Prompts Are Equal: Exploration-Guided Prompt Scaffolding for Multimodal Reinforcement Post-Training

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15051) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15051)


## One-sentence takeaway
Answer-aware teacher scaffolding of prompts selected using rollout rewards improves multimodal GRPO results across two training datasets and two model sizes, but the experiments do not isolate the benefits of scoring, filtering, and teacher guidance.

## Problem and motivation
Standard online RL allocates rollouts to prompts that may already be solved consistently or remain too difficult to produce useful reward variation. The authors argue that prompt usefulness changes with the policy, motivating continuous adaptation of training inputs rather than a fixed prompt distribution or teacher-output imitation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Exploration Potential Score (EPS):** A prompt score derived from a local KL-regularized improvement objective: softmax-weighted rollout reward minus mean rollout reward. It reuses GRPO samples without requiring additional scoring rollouts; the authors describe it as a ranking proxy, not a prediction of future learning (§2.2; Appendix B).
- **Teacher-generated scaffolding:** Low-scoring prompts receive hints conditioned on student reasoning. The implemented teacher, Qwen-VL-Max, also receives the reference answer and is instructed to conceal the final answer (§4.1; Appendix C).
- **Dynamic training pool:** An asynchronous worker rewrites prompts, periodically reinserts scaffolded versions, and supports reconsideration of reserved originals. Students continue learning from their own GRPO rollouts (§3; Appendix A).

The tables support consistent improvements over the reported GRPO baseline under teacher-assisted training. They do not establish statistical reliability, equal-total-compute efficiency, or which component causes the gains. The authors explicitly describe their component analyses as incomplete causal disentanglement (§4.3).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** EPS is an imperfect finite-sample utility proxy; scaffolding requires reference answers in this implementation; experiments concentrate on mathematical and visual reasoning with verifiable rewards (Limitations).
- **Analyst finding—EPS inconsistency:** Contrary to §3.1 and Appendix B.6.1, the stated estimator is nonnegative in exact arithmetic for any sampled reward vector: softmax weighting favors larger rewards. For finite positive temperature, it is zero when all sampled rewards are equal. Thus, the default zero threshold effectively detects uniform-reward groups; negative values cannot be explained by sampling noise alone. This undermines the stated threshold rationale, though it does not invalidate the accuracy tables.
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** No matched comparison isolates EPS against simpler reward-variation selection, random rewriting, or equally funded teacher guidance. Figure 5 alone cannot establish improvement beyond filtering.
- **Analyst inference—efficiency and supervision:** Asynchronous execution does not eliminate teacher cost. Teacher inference, reserve rescoring, wall-clock savings, and answer-leakage rates are not quantified (§3.3; Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference—diagnostic ambiguity:** Figure 4 reports advantage values near numerical zero without a clear aggregation definition. Because Eq. (3) centers rewards within each group, these plots need clarification before supporting stronger-update claims.
- **Analyst inference—external validity:** Results cover one model family and two sizes, without reported seeds or uncertainty estimates. Comparing Geometry3K with MMK12 also changes dataset composition, so it does not isolate training-data scale (§4.2).

## Open research questions
1. **Does EPS outperform simpler selection rules?** Its independent value is unresolved. Compare EPS with reward variance and uniform-reward filtering while matching teacher calls and rollout budgets.
2. **Can scaffolding work without reference answers?** The evaluated implementation is answer-aware. Compare answer-aware and answer-free teachers, including audits of hint correctness and answer leakage.
3. **Are gains cost-effective?** Total training costs are unreported. Measure accuracy against student-plus-teacher compute and elapsed time under fixed budgets.
4. **Does the method generalize beyond the evaluated setting?** Broader tasks and non-verifiable rewards remain untested. Repeat controlled comparisons across additional model families and reward types.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Reported accuracy gains are clear, but causal attribution and efficiency remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes repeated-run uncertainty, teacher-call and token costs, refresh/reserve schedules, exact reward construction, leakage audits, checkpoint-selection details, and confirmed code/model availability. Appendix C also shows a single-response teacher template without the explicit reward/EPS inputs described in §3.2, leaving the exact implemented teacher context unclear.
