# BVB: Benchmarking Agentic Video Understanding via Programmatic Reconstruction in Blender

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15478) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15478)


## One-sentence takeaway
BVB evaluates video understanding through executable Blender reconstructions, finding that its strongest tested agent achieves 88.6 perceptual similarity but preserves only 53.7% of judge-verifiable source answers (Table 1).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that video question answering can reward answer priors or isolated-frame recognition without demonstrating coherent scene understanding. Reconstructing object placement, camera motion, and temporal order provides a more demanding test, requiring agents to express their interpretation as an executable scene. BVB addresses the lack of standardized evaluation for holistic reconstruction from real videos without external assets or repeated human guidance (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark and harness:** BVB repurposes 288 indoor egocentric scenes and 5,130 questions from VSI-Bench. Mini-BVB lets agents inspect frames and execute Blender code in a shared sandbox, normally under a $3 per-scene API budget. Agents build primitive-based geometry and an animated camera, producing editable `.blend` files (§§2.1, 3.1; Appendix A).
- **Two-axis evaluation:** Dual VQA (DV) measures answer retention on questions a fixed judge answers correctly from the source. Latent Similarity (LS) averages layout and global clip-feature cosine similarities from frozen V-JEPA 2.1 representations. Overall combines the two using a square-root mean; unrenderable outputs receive zero on both axes (§2.2; Appendices E–F).
- **Comparative evidence:** The paper evaluates 51 zero-shot configurations across ten model families, analyzes reasoning effort and cost, and compares automatic metrics with blinded human rankings (§§3–4).

The contributions are the reconstruction benchmark, controlled protocol, and evaluation study; the source videos, question annotations, and feature encoder are reused assets.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |

| Best reconstruction still loses substantial answer retention | Across 288 scenes, GPT-6-Astra-high scores 70.07 Overall, 88.6 LS, and 53.7% DV: 981 of 1,827 source-correct answers retained. | Table 1; §4.1 |
| Overall leadership is clearer than semantic leadership | Astra versus GPT-5.6-Sol-xhigh: Overall difference +2.58, 95% paired scene-bootstrap interval [0.72, 4.45]; DV difference +1.97 points, interval [−0.91, 4.79], using 10,000 resamples. | Appendix C, Table 4 |
| Counts and temporal order are particularly weak | Mean DV across 51 configurations: object size 63.0%, route planning 58.5%, object count 29.3%, appearance order 16.1%. | §4.2; Table 5 |
| Human preference aligns mainly with LS | Fifteen raters evaluated five configurations across a pool of 24 scenes. Scene-model Spearman correlations: LS 0.83 over 120 observations; DV 0.11 over 110. Aggregate human ordering matches Overall exactly, ρ=1.00. | Table 2; Appendix J |
| Effort helps perceptual similarity more consistently | Across four GPT effort ladders, mean within-family Spearman correlation with effort is 0.97 for LS, 0.57 for DV, and 0.88 for Overall. | Appendix H, Table 6 |
| Lower-cost configurations remain competitive | Astra: 70.07 Overall at $1.258/scene; Sol-xhigh: 67.49 at $0.778; GLM-5.3-Flash-xhigh: 63.96 at $0.024. Costs cover Stage-1 API use and exclude evaluation. | Table 3; Appendix K |

These results establish meaningful differences under the paper’s reconstruction protocol. They do not establish that DV measures all semantic content: its denominator contains only questions the judge answered correctly on the source. Likewise, LS is a feature-similarity score, not a percentage of visual correctness, so its numerical gap from DV should not be interpreted as a calibrated difference between two accuracies.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Evaluation covers indoor egocentric videos from three sources. Outdoor environments and interactive editing remain untested (Appendix L).
- **Author-stated confounding:** Performance depends on coding skill and Blender familiarity as well as video understanding. Both metrics inspect rendered videos rather than underlying geometry (Appendix L).
- **Judge dependence:** The judge answers only 35.6% of source questions correctly. **Analyst inference:** DV therefore measures a selected, unevenly weighted subset; object-size questions contribute much more than appearance-order questions to pooled retention (§3.1; Appendix E).
- **Limited human validation:** The authors explicitly describe scene-model observations as clustered and correlations as descriptive. Only five configurations are covered, excluding the leaderboard leader (Appendix J; Table 2).
- **Effort and budget interpretation:** The authors caution that effort settings are provider-specific and larger budgets might improve results. **Analyst inference:** The proposed explanation that extra reasoning refines appearance rather than verifies facts is not established by a targeted intervention (§4.4; Appendices H, L).
- **Unmeasured stochastic variation:** The supplied uncertainty analysis resamples scenes; repeated independent reconstruction runs are not reported. **Analyst inference:** Those intervals do not establish run-to-run stability (Appendix C).

## Open research questions
- **Can explicit factual checking improve retention?** Additional effort favors LS more consistently than DV. Compare the existing harness with an object-inventory and camera-coverage verification stage under matched budgets (§4.4).
- **How sensitive are rankings to the judge and frame sampling?** DV uses one fixed judge and sampled frames. Repeat scoring with multiple judges, denser sampling, and human-verified answers on a stratified subset (Appendix E).
- **Does render agreement imply correct 3D structure?** Neither axis directly evaluates geometry. Test reconstructions on calibrated scenes with held-out viewpoints and geometric measurements (Appendix L).
- **How much failure comes from understanding versus implementation?** Blender proficiency is an acknowledged confound. Compare free-form coding with a structured scene specification translated into Blender by a fixed renderer (Appendix L).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in summarizing the supplied protocol and reported findings; broader claims about video understanding remain conditional on the evaluation design.

Material information unavailable includes repeated-run variability, alternative-judge sensitivity, targeted factual-verification ablations, direct geometry validation, complete evaluation costs, and human validation beyond the five sampled configurations. The text claims released artifacts, but usable project and repository URLs are not exposed in the supplied extraction.
