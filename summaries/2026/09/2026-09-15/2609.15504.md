# How Lossless Is Lossless Speculative Decoding? The Role of Numerical Precision in Orthrus

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15504) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15504)


## One-sentence takeaway

Orthrus exactly matches its autoregressive reference on all 1,190 tested prompts in FP32, but only 45% and 43% with two model variants in BF16, showing that its practical losslessness depends on numerical precision.

## Problem and motivation

Orthrus accelerates language model inference by proposing multiple tokens in parallel and validating them against a frozen autoregressive backbone. Although its consensus mechanism is claimed to preserve the backbone’s exact output, finite precision computation may change token selections and cause subsequent generation trajectories to diverge.

The authors identify two evaluation gaps: losslessness claims may omit the numerical precision under which equivalence is tested, and downstream benchmark scores cannot establish token by token trajectory equivalence (Sections 1 and 5).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Independent reproduction:** The authors implement Orthrus training and inference independently and compare their model with the released Orthrus-Qwen3-1.7B checkpoint (Sections 1–2).
2. **On-policy distillation:** They train the diffusion component on 4,113,358 prompt–response samples generated greedily by the frozen Qwen3-1.7B backbone, so training responses follow the backbone’s own trajectories (Section 2.1).
3. **Direct losslessness test:** They compare complete Orthrus and autoregressive token trajectories over 1,190 prompts from 12 domains under BF16 and FP32 inference (Sections 2.3, 3, and 5).
4. **Divergence analysis:** Logistic regression tests whether exact matching is associated with reference-model conditional perplexity after controlling for response length and domain (Section 3).
5. **Separate utility evaluation:** They evaluate downstream performance on GSM8K, HumanEval, and IFEval and distinguish task scores from exact trajectory equivalence (Section 4).

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Exact BF16 matching is limited. | Across 1,190 prompts, 45% of trajectories exactly matched for the released checkpoint and 43% for the independently trained variant; inference used BF16, eager attention, greedy decoding, and at most 128 new tokens. | Table 3; Sections 3 and 6 |
| Higher reference-model perplexity predicts lower matching probability. | Released checkpoint: coefficient on log conditional perplexity −8.10, 95% CI [−10.50, −5.71], \(p=3\times10^{-11}\). Independent model: −10.92, 95% CI [−13.60, −8.24], \(p=1.3\times10^{-15}\). Regressions control for response length and domain. | Tables 5–6; Section 3 |
| FP32 restores exact matching in this evaluation. | Both Orthrus variants matched their autoregressive references on all 1,190 prompts when models, prompts, and decoding procedures were retained and inference precision changed to FP32. | Table 8; Section 5 |
| The independent model usually has higher parallel-decoding efficiency. | Its mean Tokens Per Forward was higher in 10 of 12 domains; the released checkpoint was higher in two. Numerical TPF values are absent from the supplied text. | Table 2; Section 2.3 |
| Divergence does not produce systematic benchmark degradation. | On GSM8K, HumanEval, and IFEval, the independent model had higher point estimates than Qwen3 on all three tasks, but the authors explicitly state that uncertainty does not support statistically significant improvements. Numerical scores are absent. | Table 7; Section 4 |

The evidence establishes precision-dependent trajectory equivalence for the two tested Orthrus variants, software and hardware configuration, greedy-decoding procedure, and prompt set. It also establishes an adjusted statistical association between conditional perplexity and matching.

It does not establish that FP32 guarantees equivalence universally, that perplexity causes divergence, or that BF16 divergence improves downstream performance. The reported benchmark point estimates are not statistically significant, and the text does not provide their numerical values.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** The experiments do not identify which layer or computational operation causes the BF16 deviations (Section 5).
- **Author-stated caution:** Higher downstream point estimates should not be interpreted as statistically significant improvements (Section 4).
- **Analyst inference:** Precision is varied only between BF16 and FP32; other formats, mixed-precision policies, attention implementations, and hardware are not evaluated in the supplied text.
- **Analyst inference:** The trajectory study is restricted to two Qwen3-1.7B-based Orthrus variants, greedy decoding, 1,190 prompts, and responses capped at 128 new tokens, limiting generalization to other backbones and generation settings.
- **Analyst inference:** FP32 matching on all tested prompts is finite empirical evidence rather than a proof of exact equivalence.
- **Analyst inference:** The source reports no intervention or localization experiment that could separate individual numerical mechanisms.
- **Analyst inference:** Reproducibility is limited because the supplied text does not establish public availability of the independent implementation, training corpus, or evaluation prompts.

## Open research questions

1. **Which computation first causes BF16 divergence?** Section 5 leaves the responsible stage unidentified. A next experiment could record and compare layer outputs, logits, KV-cache states, and token margins at the first divergent step under BF16 and FP32.
2. **How does losslessness vary across numerical formats and hardware?** Only BF16 and FP32 are compared in the stated configuration. The same fixed prompt suite could be run with FP16, mixed precision, and multiple GPU architectures and attention implementations.
3. **Do token-margin or uncertainty measures explain divergence better than sequence perplexity?** The paper finds an association with response-conditional perplexity but does not establish a mechanism. A token-level analysis could model divergence from top-two logit margins and local entropy immediately before the first mismatch.
4. **Does the FP32 result generalize to larger models and longer generations?** The study uses 1.7B-parameter models and at most 128 new tokens. A factorial evaluation across model sizes, context lengths, and generation lengths could estimate how mismatch risk scales.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported conclusions because the supplied text gives the main settings, sample size, match rates, regression estimates, confidence intervals, and precision comparison.

Material missing information includes the numerical contents of Tables 1–5 and 7–8, domain names and domain-level match rates, exact TPF values, downstream benchmark scores and uncertainty estimates, prompt-suite provenance, release status of code and data, FP32 runtime and memory costs, random-seed details, and token-level localization of the numerical divergence.
