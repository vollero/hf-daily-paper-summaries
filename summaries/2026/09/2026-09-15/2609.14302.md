# E2A-Bench: Benchmarking Evidence-to-Action Reliability in Financial Chart Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-15.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14302) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14302)


## One-sentence takeaway
E2A-Bench shows that low unsupported-claim rates can coexist with little directional action, but its coverage-aware rankings measure rule-defined evidence consistency rather than investment quality.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that claim-level hallucination scores and aggregate task accuracy miss whether chart evidence remains consistent through a model’s rationale, confidence, and recommendation. They identify two gaps: evaluations can reward avoiding BUY/SELL decisions, and they can overlook inconsistencies between otherwise plausible response components (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** 969 queries covering 323 HS300 constituents, each represented as text-only OHLCV data, a chart, and combined input. Ten deterministic anchors per stock window provide 3,230 reference facts without assigning a correct trading action (§§3.2–3.3; Table 3).
- **Scoring protocol:** Pattern-based parsing measures unsupported claims (UCR), explicit rationale–action contradictions (RCI), and high-confidence directional actions without strong signals (ECI). Net Decision Reliability combines directional coverage with the complements of the three directional-conditioned error rates (§3.4, Eq. 11).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Twenty models reveal coverage-dependent rankings, model-specific modality effects, and BUY-side shifts across three base–financial-fine-tune pairs (§4). This is a benchmark and evaluation contribution, not a new trained model.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest hallucination score does not yield highest NDR | Across the trimodal benchmark, Gemma-4-E2B-It has the lowest UCRall, **39.9%**, but **6.4%** directional coverage and **5.1% NDR**. Qwen2.5-VL-3B achieves the highest NDR, **32.1%**, with **82.5%** coverage and **53.1% UCRall**. | Table 4 |
| Chart input has the highest mean baseline NDR | Across 20 models: chart-only **19.2%**, text-only **14.5%**, multimodal **15.6%**; individual models prefer different modalities. | §4.3.1; Fig. 4 |
| Financial adaptation shifts action distributions | Three strict base–fine-tune pairs show BUY:SELL amplification of **4.21–4.68×**, while trimodal NDR changes are **+1.5, −6.8, and −7.2 percentage points**. | §4.3.2; Fig. 5 |
| Scorer agrees reasonably with professional judgments | Against human-consensus labels, F1 is **90.0% UCR**, **85.9% RCI**, and **91.8% ECI**; four annotators provide agreement estimates. | §A.5; Table 8 |
| Holistic validation is promising but small | On **30 stratified responses**, NDR correlates with mean human reliability ratings at **ρ=0.466, p=0.009**, versus **ρ=0.453** for coverage alone. | §A.5 |
| Verification-induced coverage collapse is asserted but insufficiently documented | Authors report reduced unsupported claims and negative mean ΔNDR under chart-only oracle-aided verification; intervention procedures and quantitative results are absent from the supplied text. | Abstract; §1; §5 |

The evidence supports separating coverage from factual grounding and reporting several diagnostics. It does not establish correct trading decisions, profitability, or general superiority outside this benchmark. The human pilot does not establish that NDR’s small correlation advantage over coverage alone is statistically meaningful.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** A fixed-date HS300 snapshot does not test other market regimes, asset classes, or trading conventions. Pattern detectors may miss paraphrases and nuanced reasoning (Limitations).
- **Author-stated causal boundary:** Incomplete disclosure of fine-tuning data and objectives prevents a causal explanation of BUY-side shifts (§4.3.2).
- **Analyst inference—coverage preference:** Main NDR rewards any BUY/SELL coverage and gives HOLD/UNCERTAIN no coverage credit. Without action ground truth, it cannot determine whether abstention was appropriate (§3.4).
- **Analyst inference—scoring blind spots:** Unmatched claims escape verification. A directional response with no matched claims could receive UCR=0 without losing coverage; neutral/unknown rationales also avoid RCI penalties. ECI checks only a narrow weak-evidence condition, not general confidence calibration (§§A.3, B).
- **Analyst inference—aggregation:** Multiplying aggregate component averages does not directly measure the fraction of responses satisfying every reliability condition jointly (Eq. 11).
- **Specification and reproducibility gaps:** Table 7 marks MA cross as an ECI signal, but Table 5 omits it. Evaluations run once; scorer perturbations do not establish generation stability. The abstract advertises code/data, while §A.7 promises release upon acceptance.

## Open research questions
- **Does the ranking survive market changes?** The fixed snapshot leaves this unresolved. Repeat the benchmark across dates, regimes, and asset classes.
- **Does NDR distinguish justified action from justified abstention?** No correct-action labels are provided. Collect blinded expert judgments explicitly allowing appropriate HOLD/UNCERTAIN decisions and compare coverage-sensitive metrics.
- **Can verification improve grounding without suppressing action?** The claimed intervention lacks supporting detail. Compare baseline, self-verification, and oracle verification on matched queries, reporting coverage and all chain diagnostics.
- **How sensitive are rankings to parsing and signal rules?** Current detectors leave claims unmatched, and signal specifications conflict. Re-score with adjudicated semantic parsing and alternative strong-signal definitions.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The benchmark and principal leaderboard findings are clearly described, but their broader interpretation is constrained.

Material missing information includes detailed verification-intervention results, component-validation sample sizes and consensus procedures, complete parser rules, generation-run variability, fine-tuning corpus details, and confirmed artifact availability.
