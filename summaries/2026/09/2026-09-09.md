# Hugging Face Daily Papers — 2026-09-09

## Executive summary

- **Agent improvement is measurable, but sustained self-improvement remains unproven.** NeoHorse-1 improves its reported benchmark averages after one update cycle; Procedural Graphs wins 19 of 24 evaluated settings against each setting’s strongest baseline. Neither establishes reliable improvement across repeated generations or changing interfaces (`2609.08183`, `2609.09153`, `2609.04280`).
- **Reward-driven learning still needs an initial foothold.** OPRD improves checkpoint-averaged reasoning results in controlled Qwen3 transfers, but cannot amplify zero verifier gradients. Environment scaffolding improves selected benchmark averages yet fails to produce positive rewards on AppWorld (`2609.08798`, `2609.08404`).
- **World–action training helps manipulation, with important transfer boundaries.** OpenWAM’s controlled mixed-data study improves randomized-scene success; GE-Act’s real-robot results improve with co-training scale. Visual robustness, compute-matched scaling, and isolated cross-embodiment transfer remain unresolved (`2609.07398`, `2609.05588`).
- **Task completion can overstate instruction understanding.** RoboSPA exposes spatial, procedural, and memory failures despite task-specific fine-tuning. Greek robot-policy experiments show that high success can persist under wrong instructions (`2609.05324`, `2609.07470`).
- **Efficiency gains require complete accounting.** Online draft co-training reports end-to-end training acceleration; BeaconKV and CoVeR exchange some accuracy for lower inference costs. Graph Machine reduces estimated arithmetic while its H100 prototype trains slower (`2609.07108`, `2609.04971`, `2609.08345`, `2609.02881`).
- **Geometry prediction benefits from attention to decoded outputs.** Marigold V2 and TransNormal-2 improve reported depth-boundary and surface-normal measurements, respectively. Their strongest claims remain bounded by ranking restrictions, material-specific evaluation, and incomplete component attribution (`2609.08084`, `2609.06665`).
- **Interactive and generative quality remains multidimensional.** Played-speech feedback improves interruption anchoring but weakens some turn-management rates; Mask Forcing improves video quality while sometimes reducing motion; MovieGrid improves shot coverage but sacrifices resolution when extending duration at fixed token budget (`2609.05592`, `2609.09123`, `2609.06373`).
- **Deployment-facing evidence is mixed.** The trading telemetry establishes no directional edge, MOLE monitors miss substantial completed harm under limited review budgets, and NOAH’s intervention forecasts remain sensitive to outcome scoring. These findings support narrower conclusions than profitability, prevention, or causal treatment-effect claims (`2609.05663`, `2609.06966`, `2609.09140`).

## Main results worth noting

The following findings stand out for their controls, explicit trade-offs, or diagnostic value. They are not ranked across incomparable metrics.

- **Controlled data mixing improves manipulation transfer (`2609.07398`).** At the same 600-hour pretraining budget, OpenWAM’s ego–robot co-training raises Clean2Random randomized-scene success from **14.50% to 26.62%**, while clean-scene success changes **87.00%→87.68%**. This supports the tested mixture’s transfer value; it does not isolate every property of its sources.

- **Teacher-guided gradients improve learning trajectories, with a clear failure condition (`2609.08798`).** Four 4B-Base specialists consolidated into an 8B-Base student yield checkpoint-averaged reported Pass@1 **58.77**, versus **47.68** for Mix-RL, over student updates 60–300. In a separate 8B-Base→1.7B-Base Knights & Knaves setting, OPRD remains below **20% Pass@1**, while OPD reaches **40.5%**. Useful verifier gradients are a substantive boundary.

- **Greek transfer survives explicit null controls, but is partial (`2609.07470`).** On LIBERO-90 with **20 trials/task/seed and three training seeds**, bilingual-policy Greek success averages **27.4%**, versus **20.5%** under wrong Greek instructions. Each seed’s **6.7–7.1-point** margin has a task-bootstrap interval excluding zero. English success remains **62.5%**; the experiment covers trained goals and machine-produced commands.

- **Played-output feedback has a large matched effect (`2609.05592`).** AnchorSpeech accuracy rises **7.8%→73.0%** with shared backbone, training data, TTS, and inference configuration. The same paper reports lower Respond/Resume rates on Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5. Missing table bodies and ordered-content testing limit broader conclusions.

- **Boundary quality and global depth accuracy can move differently (`2609.08084`).** In the Qwen-based SinkLoss comparison, mean AbsRel across five datasets changes **4.10→4.12**, while HyperSim SEE3 improves **0.449→0.352**. The result supports a boundary-sensitive trade-off, not universal metric improvement or exact real-world edge localization.

- **Inference savings carry measured accuracy costs (`2609.04971`, `2609.08345`).** BeaconKV’s Qwen3-4B/A100 setting reduces peak memory **77.0→13.3 GB** and raises throughput **82.3→356.4 tokens/s**, while LiveCodeBench accuracy falls **54.4%→51.1%**. Separately, CoVeR’s ScanQA/LLaVA-OV-7B setting at 9% token retention reduces latency **0.497→0.174 seconds** while EM@1 falls **28.2→27.1**. These are distinct workloads, not a speedup ranking.

- **End-to-end training acceleration is stronger evidence than operator speed alone (`2609.07108`).** Qwen3-8B with DFlash reports **2.23× rollout** and **1.88× end-to-end training** speedups over the baseline without speculation/co-training. Qwen3.5-122B-A10B with DFlash reports **1.72×** and **1.35×**, respectively. A frozen-draft control is still needed to isolate online adaptation.

- **Data losses can misrank downstream utility (`2609.06652`).** In three-seed early Wan pretraining, Mixed achieves VBench Total **60.64±0.83**, versus Selected **59.74±0.55** and Rejected **59.42±1.06**, despite different training/validation-loss preferences. Mixed leads each matched seed, but encounters more distinct clips; this is a practical recipe result rather than an isolated quality-filter effect.

- **Useful representations do not establish superior absolute measurement (`2609.08108`).** On real GPJATK under preferred views, GaitXFormer’s average correlation is **0.84**, versus synthetic-trained STT’s **0.82**; the paired difference is **0.013 [−0.008, 0.035]**. GaitXFormer cadence MAE is **9.97 steps/min**, versus **4.21** for STT†. Correlation leadership is insufficient for a measurement-quality claim.

- **Operational scoring changes conclusions (`2609.06966`, `2609.09140`).** MOLE’s best audit-only monitor retrieves **53.3%** of positives within ten daily reviews on the GPT-5.3 single-day test—**2,106 account-days, 45 positives**. NOAH’s simulated saline-minus-Ringer’s mortality contrast changes from **+8.86 points** among explicit-outcome runs to **+4.30 points** over all runs in **1,085 sepsis patients**. The former measures constrained detection; the latter exposes scoring sensitivity in statistical forecasts, not causal treatment effects.

## Open research directions

These are editorial priorities derived from explicit limitations in the supplied analyses. The experiments are proposals, not reported results.

1. **Test improvement across repeated updates and changing harnesses.** NeoHorse validates one cycle, Procedural Graphs uses noisy validation-gated search, and EVOHARNESSBENCH exposes retention/adaptation trade-offs (`2609.08183`, `2609.09153`, `2609.04280`). Run repeated updates with fixed compute, frozen controls, and fresh earlier/new-task cohorts; report only selected artifacts’ held-out performance and cumulative cost.

2. **Find the minimum supervision needed to bootstrap sparse-reward learning.** OPRD’s zero-gradient failure, AppWorld’s absent positive rewards, and StudyBench’s solution-informed guidance motivate controlled supervision comparisons (`2609.08798`, `2609.08404`, `2609.00787`). Compare short SFT/OPD warm-ups, environment guidance, and solution-free retrieval at equal total compute, measuring first-success frequency and subsequent unaided transfer.

3. **Separate robot grounding, memory, and control.** Wrong-instruction success and hardest-level procedural failures leave their contributions entangled (`2609.05324`, `2609.07470`, `2609.05588`). Cross correct/wrong commands, shared-scene goals, oracle target identification, and explicit memory/progress inputs while holding the controller fixed; measure target choice, partial progress, completion, and native-speaker phrasing robustness.

4. **Isolate which embodied data improves transfer.** OpenWAM’s mixtures and GE-Act’s scaling alter multiple data properties; CosmoH2G shows hand-scale sensitivity (`2609.07398`, `2609.05588`, `2609.07498`). Hold target-embodiment exposure and compute constant while varying action-free video, other embodiments, appearance diversity, and hand-scale coverage; test unseen conditions with unchanged evaluation protocols.

5. **Measure efficiency at preserved task quality and complete cost.** Draft co-training lacks a frozen-draft control; sparse addressing, weight compression, and demand coding have incomplete deployment benefits (`2609.07108`, `2609.02881`, `2510.08999`, `2609.06008`). Compare time-to-target quality, complete serialized storage, memory, and inference costs—including sparse kernels, masks, posterior samples, bootstrap, and required model state where applicable.

6. **Test whether latent and geometric signals are causally useful.** CVRR offers interventions, while compressed reasoning, expertise patches, value geometry, and impossibility probes leave behavioral specificity unresolved (`2609.06746`, `2609.07821`, `2609.07139`, `2609.06289`, `2608.29109`). Use content-controlled state replacement and necessity tests, then score full responses for correction, appropriate adaptation, collateral value changes, and erroneous abstention.

7. **Evaluate interaction as a joint timing-and-correctness problem.** Gander loses task accuracy through the full stack; Self-Listening trades anchoring against turn management; AuK relies on prompt enhancement (`2609.08977`, `2609.05592`, `2609.08936`). Separately substitute verified transcripts, oracle delegation, playback-aligned text, and waveform feedback; measure completed tasks, recovery accuracy, interruption latency, and human listening judgments.

8. **Validate visual gains beyond aggregate similarity.** Depth/normal boundaries, video motion, shot coverage, and edited-region preservation retain unresolved measurement or attribution issues (`2609.08084`, `2609.06665`, `2609.09123`, `2609.06373`, `2609.04942`). Use task-specific geometric references and independent human assessments; run matched component ablations and measure boundary displacement, motion preservation, ordered events, and unedited-region drift separately.

9. **Convert monitoring proposals into prospective prevention evidence.** MOLE lacks intervention results, Counter-Swarm lacks measured discovery benefits, and ReactVAU’s sparse-stream efficiency is projected (`2609.06966`, `2609.06140`, `2609.07941`). For agent traces, compare account-local and cross-execution monitoring under matched review budgets, then randomize response policies. Separately evaluate video monitoring on extended benign-heavy streams with actual trigger rates, false alarms, latency, and evidence-grounded explanations.

10. **Validate deployment claims with independent outcomes.** Trading protection results are largely paper-based; gait references are incomplete; NOAH lacks external prospective validation (`2609.05663`, `2609.08108`, `2609.09140`). Use prospective controlled execution tests, independent synchronized gait measurements, and external clinical cohorts with fixed prediction landmarks. Report failures, unresolved outcomes, calibration, and complete denominators alongside headline performance.
