# Refuse without Refusal: A Structural Analysis of Safety-Tuning Responses for Reducing False Refusals in Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04714) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04714)


## One-sentence takeaway
Removing boilerplate refusal statements from safety-training responses improves benign-query compliance across the tested models, but safety equivalence and the proposed shortcut-learning mechanism remain less firmly established than the behavioral improvement.

## Problem and motivation
Safety-tuned models can reject harmless requests containing superficially risky language. The authors investigate whether the **structure of safety-training responses** contributes to this over-refusal, addressing a data-level gap left by methods that modify inference-time behavior (§§1–3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Controlled response decomposition:** Separate refusal statements from explanatory rationales, then vary statement position, component inclusion, and rationale specificity while preserving requests and minimizing other edits (§3).
- **Rationale-Only supervision:** Fine-tune base models using 1,024 Alpaca examples plus 256 safety examples processed by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. Request-specific rationales explicitly identify the action and its risks (§4).
- **Structural and mechanistic probes:** Test neutral prefixes, varied refusal wording, keyword-conditioned statements, first-token entropy, and token ablations. These support the authors’ interpretation that templates encourage superficial associations, without uniquely establishing that mechanism (§6).
- **Applicability and controls:** Examine model and dataset scaling, short rationales, reasoning-first prompting, URIAL in-context learning, and compatibility with Self-CD and SCANS (§7; Appendices G–K).

## Evidence and main results

CR denotes compliance rate: higher is better on benign benchmarks and lower on harmful benchmarks. Comparisons below use Rationale-Only versus Statement and Rationale unless stated otherwise.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Substantial benign-compliance gains | Llama-3.1-8B: XSTest-Safe CR **0.71 vs 0.39**, OKTest **0.60 vs 0.41**. Mistral-7B: **0.75 vs 0.49**, **0.77 vs 0.55**, respectively. | Table 2 |
| Direct harmful compliance sometimes increases | Llama: AdvBench **0.02 vs 0.02**, MaliciousInstruct **0.06 vs 0.02**. Mistral: **0.04 vs 0.01**, **0.06 vs 0.00**, respectively. | Table 2 |
| Request specificity improves responsiveness with a safety tradeoff | Llama Request-Specific versus Generic: XSTest-Safe/OKTest CR **0.80/0.72 vs 0.66/0.60**; AdvBench/MaliciousInstruct **0.04/0.05 vs 0.03/0.02**. | Table 2 |
| Evaluated jailbreak safety is broadly comparable | Reported average harmful CR across four benchmarks: Llama **0.20 vs 0.21**; Mistral **0.15 vs 0.16**. | Table 4; §6.1 |
| Neutral templates also reduce benign compliance | Llama Prefix and Rationale yields XSTest-Safe/OKTest CR **0.44/0.46**, versus **0.71/0.60** for Rationale-Only. | Table 6 |
| Benefits extend to the tested ICL configuration | Llama URIAL: XSTest-Safe/OKTest CR **0.81/0.84 vs 0.52/0.65**; harmful CR **0.06/0.06 vs 0.04/0.03**. | Table 8 |
| Multiple controls preserve the main trend | Rationale-Only retains its benign-compliance advantage across additional model sizes and safety-data sizes; short-rationale and reasoning-first controls do not eliminate the component gap. | Tables 36–38, 40 |
| General-capability preservation is uneven | Llama’s average exact-match score is **53.25 vs 54.08**, but MMLU is **46.53 vs 52.34**; Qwen GSM8K, using eight-shot CoT, is **69.22 vs 76.11**. | Table 25 |
| Judge validation supports—but does not settle—measurement validity | On Llama Rationale-Only benign outputs, automatic versus human agreement is **κ = 0.81**; GPT-5.1 judging retains the main component trend. | Appendix C; Tables 14–17 |

The evidence supports response structure as an experimentally manipulable contributor to measured over-refusal. It does **not** establish universal safety preservation, uniform capability preservation, or a unique internal explanation. The benign-compliance metric also penalizes disclaimers even when the requested information follows, so its gains combine changes in substantive responsiveness and response style.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Resource constraints limit scaling; instruction-tuned comparisons are difficult to interpret because alignment pipelines differ. Qwen fails reliably functioning URIAL evaluation, and Surgical mitigation cannot be applied under its required selection criterion (Limitations; Appendices G, J).
- **Analyst inference—measurement alignment:** Table 48 automatically treats any disclaimer or ethical caution as refusal, even alongside a substantive answer. This directly favors the style encouraged by Rationale-Only and is stricter than the human instructions in Table 16.
- **Analyst inference—uncertainty:** Main compliance and capability tables lack repeated-training variability or confidence intervals. “Comparable safety” is therefore descriptive, not a demonstrated equivalence result.
- **Analyst inference—mechanistic limits:** First-token entropy reflects response-opening regularity as well as possible intent discrimination. Keyword interventions also produce off-target declines, weakening claims of exclusively keyword-specific effects (Tables 27, 31).
- **Analyst inference—curation and validation:** Generation prompts permit creating missing explanations, leaving semantic preservation imperfectly verified. Human validation covers one model/component condition with nonoverlapping annotations; detailed validation data are withheld (Appendix C; Ethics Statement; Tables 41, 43).

## Open research questions
- **How much improvement survives style-neutral judging?** The benign judge penalizes disclaimers categorically. Next experiment: blindly annotate substantive task fulfillment and refusal wording separately across all response conditions.
- **Can benign gains be retained at matched harmful compliance?** Several conditions increase direct harmful CR. Next experiment: sweep safety-data mixtures and rationale specificity over repeated seeds, comparing models at matched harmful-compliance levels.
- **Does the intervention transfer to established alignment pipelines?** Controlled experiments primarily tune base models. Next experiment: apply paired response transformations within a shared instruction-tuned training pipeline.
- **Do superficial associations explain the full effect?** Entropy and attribution are suggestive but not decisive. Next experiment: independently vary prefix regularity and risky-word/intent correlations, then evaluate held-out paraphrases and keywords.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides extensive controlled comparisons and supporting appendices, but causal interpretation and safety equivalence remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes repeated-run variability, complete evaluation-subset specifications, detailed semantic-preservation audits, attribution-annotation selection and agreement procedures, and independently verifiable release contents. Detailed human-validation data are explicitly withheld.
