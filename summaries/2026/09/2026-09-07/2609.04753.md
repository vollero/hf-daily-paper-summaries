# Beneath the Surface of Chains-of-Thought: A Mechanistic Interpretation of Reasoning Operations in LLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04753) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04753)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors find that annotated reasoning operations are decodable from LLM hidden states, especially in middle layers, with evidence beyond tested lexical and positional cues and context-masking effects on operation alignment, but no demonstration that these representations control reasoning success (§§3.2–3.5, 6).

## Problem and motivation

Reasoning traces contain distinct functions—such as decomposition, recall, and deduction—but their textual organization does not establish corresponding internal computation. The authors investigate whether these recurring operations share hidden-state geometry across contexts, connecting research on textual reasoning structure with representation analysis (§§1, 4).

## Approach and contributions

- **Operation-level probing framework:** GPT-5 annotates reasoning spans using a Pólya-inspired taxonomy; eight frequent operations enter the main analysis. Hidden states undergo normalization, PCA to 128 dimensions, and supervised LDA to seven dimensions. Training-set centroid differences define operation vectors evaluated using held-out one-vs-rest AUROC/AUPRC (§§2, 3.1).
- **Controlled representational analysis:** The contribution is applying this framework across models, layers, and span positions, with lexical, positional, PCA-only, cross-task, and erroneous-execution controls—not a new dimensionality-reduction algorithm (§§3.2–3.5; Appendices C–D).
- **Context and token dynamics:** Within-span variance, shared-token comparisons, and fixed-sequence attention masking examine how operation signals become distributed and depend on preceding context (§§3.3–3.4). Code and project materials are announced in the abstract; their contents are not established by the supplied text.

Directions still use training labels. | Appendix D.1, Table E |
| Signals spread across spans and contextualize identical tokens | Within-span score variance decreases toward middle layers; shared-token occurrences separate by surrounding operation in the learned probe space. | Preceding context affects operation alignment | Masking the preceding **30 tokens** from the target span’s first token while fixing the sequence reduces reasoning alignment: aggregate **Δ = −0.4356, p < 0.0001**. These results support recoverable information about **textually annotated functions**, robustness to the tested confounds, and a causal effect of context removal on probe scores. They do not establish discrete cognitive modules, exhaustive removal of linguistic confounds, or improved answer correctness through operation-vector intervention.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated annotation uncertainty:** GPT-5 matches the human reference on 64/84 spans, reported as 76.2%; human validation tests labels rather than boundaries. Labels approximate textual functions, not latent cognitive states (§6; Table N).
- **Split inconsistency:** Several controls describe question-level splits, but Appendix F.1.1 explicitly reports splitting trace instances, with 14/608 Qwen3 questions appearing in both sets. **Analyst inference:** main held-out results cannot be interpreted as strictly unseen-question evaluation.
- **Restricted scope and selection:** Main analyses retain judge-approved correct responses; correctness uses Qwen3.6-27B and a substring-based acceptance rule. Generation has no fixed seed. The authors acknowledge limited tasks and models and no demonstrated behavioral improvement (§6; Appendix F.2).
- **Intervention comparability:** §3.4 warns that masking variants differ in lengths and eligible samples, while Appendix B.1 nevertheless interprets their magnitude ordering. **Analyst inference:** context dependence is better supported than claims about which context type contributes most.
- **Aggregate reporting ambiguity:** Table M’s per-operation layer-averaged values do not straightforwardly reconcile with Table 2’s macro values. Their aggregation or setting difference is not clearly explained.
- **Statistical scope:** Bootstrap resampling operates on spans, and the error comparison pairs layer-level metrics (Appendices D.5, C.3). **Analyst inference:** these analyses do not directly quantify uncertainty across independent training runs or question clusters.

## Open research questions

1. **Does separability persist under strict question separation?** Reported overlap leaves this unresolved; repeat fitting and evaluation with question-disjoint splits and question-clustered uncertainty estimates.
2. **Do operation directions causally influence reasoning behavior?** Masking changes alignment, not demonstrated problem-solving performance; test targeted activation interventions against matched random directions and measure operation choice and answer accuracy.
3. **How dependent are findings on annotation and window selection?** Boundary validation is absent, and entropy-centered windows are used; compare independently human-segmented spans with random, centered, and full-span representations.
4. **How specific is the geometry to reasoning operations?** The literary control differs in domain and eligible samples; compare reasoning and discourse functions within matched texts, span lengths, label budgets, and intervention eligibility.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** Multiple controls support the descriptive result, but annotation uncertainty and reporting inconsistencies constrain stronger mechanistic conclusions.

Material gaps include exact figure-only peak values, intervention sample counts and layer details, reconciliation of aggregate tables and split descriptions, a clearly reproduced full GPT-5 annotation prompt, and confirmation of which traces, labels, and fitted probes are included in the announced release.
