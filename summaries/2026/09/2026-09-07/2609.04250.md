# Motion-Omni: End-to-End Joint Speech and Full-Body Motion for Spoken Dialogue

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04250) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04250)


## One-sentence takeaway

Motion-Omni generates conversational speech and full-body motion from shared speech-generation states, reporting a 5.4× offline speedup over its same-audio LOM teacher cascade with near-teacher motion metrics on SwDA-500 (§§4.4–4.5).

## Problem and motivation

Conversational avatars need speech coordinated with facial expressions, hands, and body movement. The authors identify two costs of generating speech first and motion afterward: a separate audio-to-motion inference pass and no motion supervision reaching the dialogue or speech parameters. Their goal is native full-body output without sacrificing spoken-dialogue ability; they also identify shortages of consistent-voice training pairs and controlled open-ended evaluation (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Integrated generation:** A speech projector, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone, Speech Generator, and four part-specific motion decoders produce speech and SMPL-X/FLAME motion. Motion queries use speech-token embeddings interpolated from 12.5 Hz to 30 Hz and attend to Speech Generator hidden states. The contribution combines native full-body output with joint adaptation; the gated fusion mechanism and frozen LOM codebooks are reused components (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Training and supervision:** Four training stages progressively introduce speech, motion, and joint dialogue objectives. A replaceable teacher labels consistent-voice responses; reconstruction error and beat correlation rank supervision for curriculum learning. The reported corpus contains 422,856 pseudo-labeled pairs totaling 1,402 hours (§§1, 3.3–3.4).
- **Evaluation assets:** The authors report releasing SwDA-500 and a protocol combining matched generated audio, shared rendering, automatic metrics, human preferences, and offline latency. Their “first public protocol” characterization is an author novelty claim (§§1, 4.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|

| Strong descriptive motion scores | SwDA-500: BC **7.59**, reported in **×10⁻¹ units**, and unscaled diversity **13.67**, highest among evaluated systems without LOM at inference. | Low speech transcription error | Seed-TTS-Eval English, **1,088 samples**: Whisper-large-v3 corpus WER **2.62%**, reported lowest among compared omni-modal systems; some dedicated TTS systems score lower. | §4.3 |
| Spoken-dialogue capability remains measurable | VoiceBench Overall **47.63**, versus LLaMA-Omni **41.12**, Ex-Omni **43.57**, Mini-Omni2 **33.49**, and Moshi **29.51**; authors caution that pretraining and objectives differ. | §4.2; Appendix E |
| Exploratory human comparisons favor MO over EMAGE | Same-audio pooled wins/ties/losses: **45/10/20** against EMAGE and **25/27/23** against LOM, across three dimensions, with **25 pairs per arm** and **four annotators**. | §4.4.2; Appendix D |
| Joint training helps alignment in the reported pilot | Freezing the Speech Generator led to higher plateaued motion loss and visible misalignment; joint updates improved both. No numerical effect size is supplied. | §3.3 |
| Video judging failed in a small calibration | On **13 same-audio pairs**, the video judge matched one human’s aggregate ranking on **4/13 (31%)**, Cohen’s κ **0.079**. | Appendix H |

The matched-audio results support an efficient motion pathway under controlled speech. Teacher-reference FGD and facial errors establish agreement with LOM, not fidelity to captured human movement. The human results do not establish reliable preference over LOM, and cross-system VoiceBench scores do not isolate the effect of motion training.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Motion is constrained by LOM pseudo-labels and frozen codebooks. Only one backbone instance is trained; English-only, fixed-voice training lacks explicit identity or emotion conditioning (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Generation is offline. The reported RTF does not establish streaming responsiveness or time to first output (§4.5; Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Automatic motion metrics imperfectly reflect perception. The exploratory study uses four young-adult male annotators and lacks a larger non-author study (§4.4.2; Limitations).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied freezing pilot directly supports adapting the Speech Generator, but does not separately establish that LLM adaptation is necessary. Progressive curriculum improvements likewise do not isolate ranking from additional training (§3.3; Appendix G).
- **Analyst inference:** The explanation that conversational training causes preference over EMAGE remains a hypothesis without a controlled domain-matching experiment (§4.4.2).

## Open research questions

1. **Which shared parameters need motion gradients?** The freezing pilot does not isolate each module’s contribution. Compare frozen versus trainable LLM and Speech Generator configurations under identical data and compute (§3.3).
2. **Does teacher fidelity translate into human motion quality?** References are synthetic and human evidence is exploratory. Test matched-audio outputs with a larger, diverse non-author panel and real-motion evaluation assets (§4.4; Limitations).
3. **Can the approach support streaming interaction?** It currently consumes complete utterances. Evaluate a chunked variant using time to first speech/motion, interruptions, and synchronization quality (Limitations).
4. **How portable is the recipe?** Only one teacher/backbone and English training are tested. Repeat training with another teacher and multilingual data, measuring speech quality and culturally appropriate motion (Limitations).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and latency comparison are clearly described, but several quantitative claims cannot be checked fully from the supplied extraction.

Material gaps include most table bodies—particularly detailed motion comparisons, stage configurations, and ablations—GPU model, stochastic-run uncertainty, human agreement values, and a controlled before/after dialogue-retention comparison. The abstract’s “within 2%” and conclusion’s “about 1%” teacher-proximity summaries cannot be reconciled precisely without the missing metric rows. Release availability is author-reported; judge-calibration artifacts are described as planned.
