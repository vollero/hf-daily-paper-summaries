# To See a World in a Living Context: Unified Indoor-Outdoor Urban World Generation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05879) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05879)


## One-sentence takeaway
HoloWorld links text-generated urban exteriors to building-specific, footprint-constrained interiors through an evolving hierarchical context, with reported improvements in exterior quality and indoor–outdoor coherence on 3×3-block cities (Experiments).

## Problem and motivation
Urban exterior and indoor scene generators usually operate independently, leaving interiors disconnected from their buildings’ function, appearance, and geometry. The authors target coherent worlds for immersive environments and embodied simulation, where movement across spatial scales requires consistent spaces (Introduction). The central gap is explicit building-to-interior correspondence, beyond simply supporting both scene types.

## Approach and contributions
- **Unified generation task:** Represent a world as urban blocks plus interiors associated with selected, stably identified building instances. The authors claim novelty for this unified formulation; priority is not independently established here (Problem Formulation, Eq. 1; Introduction).
- **Evolving cross-scale context:** Maintain world-, block-, and building-level semantic, visual, and geometric information. Task-specific generators retrieve relevant context, and a validation gate controls updates. Exterior blocks condition on previously generated neighbors (Cross-Scale World Context Representation, Eqs. 2–5).
- **Building-grounded interiors:** Associate exterior image regions with 3D building instances and footprints, then generate functional programs, assets, room layouts, and object placements within those footprints. Exterior appearance supplies design cues, not observations of hidden interiors (Building Instance Association; Building-Level Context-Driven Indoor Generation, Eqs. 6–8).

The implementation combines GPT-5.4 reasoning, GPT-Image-2 image generation/editing, and Meshy image-to-3D conversion; these are dependencies rather than new models contributed by the paper (Experimental Setup).

AQS uses a 1–10 scale; RDR measures pairwise preference. Human evaluation involves 20 experts (Evaluation Metrics). These findings support judged coherence and footprint alignment in the tested setting. They do not establish physical usability, large-scale robustness, or statistical significance. The abstract’s **7.68% average AQS improvement over the stated strongest baseline** cannot be independently checked without the table values and aggregation details.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Interiors are single-floor; vertical structures are not explicitly modeled. Richer structural constraints and interactive physical validation remain future work (Conclusion).
- **Analyst inference—confounded context ablation:** Static context removes updates, building-level localization, and the actual footprint, substituting a rectangle. Its degradation supports the combined configuration, but does not isolate dynamic updating itself (Ablation Studies).
- **Analyst inference—restricted coverage:** All experiments use 3×3 grids, and indoor evaluation samples valid buildings after association. Larger-grid performance and association failures are therefore not established (Experimental Setup).
- **Analyst inference—comparison limits:** TRELLIS tests independent paired-text generation; missing spatial baseline metrics prevent a comparative claim about geometric superiority over another footprint-conditioned method (Table 2 caption).
- **Analyst inference—measurement limits:** High footprint IoU measures envelope agreement, not traversability or physical correctness. Sample counts, uncertainty estimates, and detailed evaluator procedures are unavailable (Evaluation Metrics; Conclusion).

## Open research questions
- **Which context components drive improvement?** The static ablation changes several conditions together. Next experiment: vary context updates, building localization, and footprint constraints independently.
- **Does coherence persist at larger scales?** Evaluation uses only 3×3 cities. Next experiment: increase grid size while measuring continuity, accumulated failures, runtime, and cost.
- **Can generated worlds support physical navigation across floors and building boundaries?** Vertical modeling and physical validation remain future work. Next experiment: extend to multi-floor buildings and test connectivity, collisions, and indoor–outdoor navigation in simulation.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported result directions are clear, but quantitative scrutiny is limited by missing table bodies.

Material missing information includes city/building sample counts and prompt sets; run variability and statistical uncertainty; RDR aggregation and detailed judging protocols; validation-gate criteria; instance-association failure rates; runtime and cost; implementation details and release status; and the reason full-model correspondence scores differ between the main comparison and ablation settings.
