# Unfold The World: Factorize 4D Properties in Reinforcing Spatial Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03729) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03729)


## One-sentence takeaway
FactoSR combines spatial supervised fine-tuning with geometry- and motion-based reinforcement rewards, improving Qwen3-VL-8B’s reported VSI-Bench and All-Angles-Bench scores by 5.9 percentage points each, without establishing a general 4D world model (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that VLMs trained on image projections often substitute appearance heuristics for reasoning about depth, cross-view identity, and motion. They identify a gap in spatial training based on single-view supervision or final-answer rewards: neither explicitly enforces geometric and temporal consistency (§1). Better spatial reasoning could support navigation and robotics, although the experiments evaluate benchmark answers rather than deployed systems.

## Approach and contributions
- **Progressive spatial training:** Qwen3-VL undergoes short-answer spatial/general SFT, then structured “Anchor-Transfer-Verify” reasoning supervision, followed by revised GRPO reinforcement learning (§4.1).
- **Factorized rewards—the central methodological contribution:** XY rewards score reprojection accuracy with visibility gating; Z rewards score relative depth ordering using Kendall correlation after matching predicted and ground-truth 3D boxes; T rewards require correct answers to both forward and inverse camera-motion questions. These combine with answer accuracy under a strict output-format gate (§4.2).
- **Training data and models:** The authors describe a newly constructed 1.2M-sample spatial dataset within an 8.2M-sample SFT collection, plus 32K RL samples, and evaluate FactoSR-SFT and FactoSR-RL checkpoints (§5.1; Tables 1–2). A code link is supplied, but dataset and checkpoint release availability is not established.

The tables support improved benchmark performance for this training pipeline and backbone. Ablation prose suggests rewards contribute beyond SFT, but incomplete tables limit independent assessment of the comparisons. Answer scores and selected reasoning examples do not establish faithful internal geometric reconstruction or a general world model.

Two reporting discrepancies matter: the abstract/introduction claim a 4.5% All-Angles gain, whereas Tables 1–2 show a 5.9-point gain over the base model; §5.3’s unqualified claim of best spatial-average performance conflicts with Gemini’s higher Table 2 score.

## Limitations and validity risks
The supplied text contains no dedicated author limitations discussion.

- **Analyst inference—scope:** Only one trained backbone and size are evaluated. Benchmark QA and qualitative route examples do not demonstrate closed-loop navigation or broader physical interaction (§§5.2–5.5).
- **Analyst inference—supervision dependence:** XY requires depth and camera geometry; Z requires annotated 3D boxes. Robustness to inaccurate annotations is not reported (§4.2).
- **Analyst inference—temporal interpretation:** T checks paired answer correctness for reversible motion; this does not by itself verify temporal causality or intermediate reasoning (§4.2).
- **Evidence gaps:** Full ablation results, repeated-run uncertainty, and referenced appendices are unavailable. Reward weights, geometric thresholds, compute costs, and train–test overlap checks are not specified in the supplied text.

## Open research questions
- **How robust are the rewards to imperfect geometry?** Clean geometric inputs underpin XY/Z, but noise sensitivity is untested. Perturb depth, camera poses, and boxes during training and measure downstream accuracy.
- **Does T teach motion reasoning beyond paired-answer consistency?** Current evidence combines inverse QA and selected examples. Test unfamiliar motion compositions and longer sequences with independently scored intermediate states.
- **How portable is the decomposition?** Results cover one backbone. Repeat matched-data, matched-compute SFT, vanilla GRPO, and factorized-reward experiments across model families and sizes.
