# RISE: Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05295) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05295)


## One-sentence takeaway
RISE distills teachers extrapolated from a model’s own reward-guided training trajectory, improving tested math, STEM, and agentic results over GRPO while accelerating code training toward comparable final accuracy (§4.2).

## Problem and motivation
Outcome rewards provide sequence-level supervision without identifying useful individual tokens. Distillation supplies token-level targets, but the authors argue that external teachers suffer from distribution mismatch and privileged-context self-teachers depend on limited in-context learning capabilities. RISE addresses the resulting teacher-quality bottleneck using checkpoints already produced during training (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Trajectory-derived teacher:** After an RLVR update, extrapolate beyond the current checkpoint along its displacement from a trailing anchor. The teacher requires neither an external model nor privileged conditioning (§3.1).
- **Two implementations:** Weight-space extrapolation materializes teacher parameters; logit-space extrapolation constructs a normalized geometric mixture of anchor and current-policy distributions. Both support token-level distillation (§3.1, Algorithms 1–2).
- **Recursive training loop:** Alternate GRPO and distillation, refreshing the teacher each iteration and reusing the same rollouts. Practical training uses Jensen–Shannon divergence, top-K distributions with a tail bucket, a decreasing extrapolation scale, and model-dependent anchor smoothing (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Mechanism analysis:** Ablations test reward grounding, distillation, anchors, schedules, and extra optimization. The theory provides conditional suboptimality bounds under idealized trajectory assumptions, rather than a general convergence guarantee (§4.3–4.4; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results

Math Avg aggregates six benchmarks using their specified avg@N protocols; results below are reported accuracies unless otherwise indicated.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Strong math improvements | OLMo3-7B-Instruct-SFT/OpenR1: logit RISE Math Avg **56.4% vs. 47.6%** GRPO; AIME’24 avg@16 **46.9% vs. 30.2%**. | Table 1; Appendix B.2 |
| Gains persist across seeds | DAPOMath, three-seed mean ± SD: Qwen3-8B weight RISE Math Avg **62.4±0.2% vs. 60.1±0.2%** GRPO; Qwen3-1.7B logit RISE **49.6±0.4% vs. 45.1±0.4%**. | Table C.8 |
| Mixed-domain improvements | Qwen3-4B-Base, mixed math/STEM: weight RISE Math Avg **44.8% vs. 40.2%**, STEM Avg **47.5% vs. 45.5%** GRPO. | Table 2 |
| Code benefit is chiefly faster learning | Qwen3-8B-Base/Skywork-OR1-Code: authors report logit RISE reaches GRPO’s final HumanEval+ avg@4 accuracy at **step 50 versus step 90**; final accuracy is comparable. | §4.2; Figure 4 |
| Weight RISE improves agent outcomes | Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct: ALFWorld success **84.4% vs. 75.0%** GRPO; WebShop perfect-reward success **74.2% vs. 63.3%**. | Table 3; Appendix B.2 |
| Gains exceed an extra GRPO pass | Matching gradient-pass count and sampling cost: Qwen3-8B weight RISE Math Avg **62.7% vs. 60.5%** GRPO-2×; Qwen3-1.7B logit RISE **50.2% vs. 47.3%**. | Table C.7 |
| Both phases matter in tested ablations | Qwen3-8B without RLVR reaches **2.4% MATH-500 avg@4** within **60 steps**; directly adopting extrapolated weights yields **60.3% Math Avg**, versus **60.0%** GRPO and **62.7%** weight RISE. | §4.3; Figure 5; Table C.3 |
| Sampling reuse has a compute cost | No additional rollout sampling; per-iteration wall time is approximately **1.6× GRPO at Qwen3-8B** and **1.3× at Qwen3-1.7B**. | §4.4 |

The evidence supports improvements within these training configurations, particularly mathematical reasoning. It does not establish universal superiority: code ends at comparable accuracy, logit RISE lowers WebShop score, and weight RISE trails some baselines on Qwen3-1.7B-Base (Table 3; Table C.1). Gradient-pass matching also does not establish equal wall-clock efficiency.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Extrapolation depends on sufficiently well-behaved trajectories; detecting unreliable directions remains unresolved. Reward hacking could be amplified (§5).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Multi-seed evidence covers selected Qwen math configurations, while external-teacher comparisons are excluded. Broader robustness and comparative teacher quality remain untested (§4.1; Table C.8).
- **Analyst inference—theory/practice gap:** The bounds assume linear trajectories, use reverse KL, and concern bounds rather than guaranteed realized improvement. The stated inequality relating JSD to KL does not by itself justify transferring a KL-controlled guarantee to JSD optimization (§3.2; Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference—approximation and coverage:** No quantitative full-vocabulary comparison establishes negligible top-K bias. The reported pass@N bootstraps existing sampled completions, so improved scores do not independently prove discovery of fundamentally new solution strategies (Appendices A.4, B.2).
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Weight-space top-K selection is described differently in Appendix A.4 versus §3.2 and Algorithm 2, which specify student-selected indices.

## Open research questions
- **Can extrapolation be adapted automatically?** Anchor preferences differ across model families and safe scales narrow during training. Compare an online reliability-based scale/anchor controller against fixed schedules (§4.4).
- **How robust is RISE to imperfect rewards?** Reward-bias amplification is acknowledged but not tested. Introduce controlled verifier errors and measure true task success separately from training reward (§5).
- **Does the implemented approximation preserve the proposed mechanism?** JSD and top-K introduce gaps from the analysis. Compare full-vocabulary KL, truncated KL, and truncated JSD at matched compute (§3.2; Appendix A.4).
- **Does RISE improve capability per wall-clock budget?** Existing controls match gradient passes. Compare time-to-target accuracy and final performance under equal elapsed-time budgets (§4.4).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the empirical pattern and algorithm, but stronger causal and theoretical claims remain uncertain.

Material missing information includes released code/checkpoints, GPU models and absolute runtimes, complete agentic hyperparameters and evaluation episode counts, numerical code-result tables, full-vocabulary approximation measurements, and multi-seed results outside the selected math settings.
