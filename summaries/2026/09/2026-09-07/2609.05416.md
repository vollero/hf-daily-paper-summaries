# WorldSculpt: Generating Compositional Worlds from Grounded Videos

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05416) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05416)


## One-sentence takeaway

WorldSculpt adapts an object-level generative prior to posed, masked multi-view observations, producing separate meshes for densely cluttered static scenes without scene-level training and outperforming ShapeR on UE-MeshyScene (§§3–4, Table 4).

## Problem and motivation

Cluttered scenes require both plausible completion of occluded geometry and individually editable objects. The authors identify a gap between geometry-based methods that produce fused, incomplete scenes and compositional generative methods demonstrated mainly on simpler layouts. Separate object meshes would support content authoring, simulation, gaming, AR/VR, and robotics (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **Grounded object generation:** Each object receives an anchor-aligned canonical cube derived from its localization box and a selected view. Crop-aware camera projections align observations; generated meshes are transformed into a shared world frame without joint object optimization (§§3.1–3.4).
- **Multi-view adaptation of Pixal3D:** DINOv3 features are lifted into canonical voxels and combined through a permutation-invariant learned aggregator. Zero-initialized projections and LoRA adapt the pretrained geometry stages while retaining frozen base parameters (§3.2).
- **Object-only training for scene inference:** Training on TexVerse individual objects uses variable view counts and progressively stronger occlusion, pose, segmentation, and resolution degradations. The resulting model is applied to scenes without scene-level training (§§3.3–3.5).
- **UE-MeshyScene benchmark:** The authors introduce six rendered environments with 93–701 objects each, totaling 2,299 objects and 5,964 frames, with complete per-object geometry and annotations (§4.1, Table 1).

The contribution is this combined adaptation and evaluation framework; Pixal3D, DINOv3, LoRA, and the IBR-style aggregation family are existing components.

## Evidence and main results

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Additional views improve heavily occluded object recovery | On 500 Toys4k objects at 75% per-view occlusion, WorldSculpt F@.05 rises from 0.631 with one view to 0.929 with 16; 16-view TRELLIS scores 0.484. Canonical evaluation uses ground-truth unit-sphere normalization and ICP. | §4.2, Table 2 |
| Clean single-view performance does not uniformly beat the original prior | At zero occlusion with one view, CD-ℓ₂ is 2.59 for WorldSculpt versus 2.17 for Pixal3D, in ×10⁻³ units; lower is better. Same canonical protocol. | Table 2 |
| Better geometry and placement on controlled scenes | WorldSculpt versus ShapeR CD-ℓ₂: 0.28 versus 1.26 on HouseCat6D; 0.61 versus 8.38 on Toys4k-Scene, in ×10⁻³ units. World-frame evaluation has no per-object ICP and normalizes distances by ground-truth box diagonal. | §4.1, Table 3 |
| Better large-scene reconstruction than the evaluated baseline | Across six UE-MeshyScene environments, F-Score@.05 is 0.951 versus ShapeR’s 0.813 under the world-frame, no-ICP protocol. WorldSculpt leads all reported metrics. | §4.4, Table 4 |
| Learned fusion helps, but is not universally superior | UE-MeshyScene F@.05: IBR 0.951 versus mean fusion 0.944; HouseCat6D: 0.995 versus 0.996. Both use the same world-frame, no-ICP protocol. | §4.5, Table 6 |
| Generated-world conversion is demonstrated qualitatively | A Marble 3DGS scene is converted using rendered RGB/depth, SAM3 tracks, estimated boxes, and unchanged WorldSculpt inference, without additional training. | §4.6, Figure 7 |

These results support multi-view geometry generation and transfer from individual-object training to the evaluated scenes. They do not establish universal superiority, dense real-world capture reliability, or practical computational scalability. The abstract mentions HY-World 2.0, but the supplied application experiment documents only Marble.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Large camera, mask, or localization errors can degrade results; low light and motion artifacts are not explicitly modeled. Outputs lack texture/materials, and moving or deforming objects are unsupported (§4.7).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Dense-scene quantitative evidence comes from synthetic UE-MeshyScene; the real-world HouseCat6D evaluation uses comparatively sparse, mildly occluded tabletop scenes (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference—attribution:** Fusion is ablated, but the text provides no separate ablations of the augmentation curriculum, canonicalization, or LoRA. Their individual contributions therefore remain unresolved (§4.5).
- **Analyst inference—comparison scope:** UE-MeshyScene compares only against ShapeR. Exact scene inference view budgets and baseline configurations are insufficiently specified to assess matching of input information (§§3.4, 4.3–4.4).
- **Analyst inference—downstream validity:** Independent mesh generation and geometric metrics do not establish collision-free composition or successful physics simulation; neither is evaluated (§§3.4, 4.1).

## Open research questions

1. **How robust is the full pipeline to estimated inputs in dense real captures?** Upstream failures are acknowledged but not systematically quantified. Next experiment: compare annotated versus estimated poses, masks, and boxes, with controlled error sweeps.
2. **Which adaptation components drive occlusion robustness?** Only fusion receives a component ablation. Next experiment: independently remove augmentation types, the curriculum, and LoRA under matched training and view budgets.
3. **Can independently generated objects form physically usable scenes?** Current evaluation measures geometry and placement. Next experiment: measure interpenetration, support consistency, and simulation stability before and after scene-level refinement.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The method and reported metric trends are clear; broader validity and reproducibility are less established.

Missing information includes runtime, hardware and peak memory; exact scene view budgets; detailed baseline configurations; scene evaluation subset sizes for HouseCat6D and Toys4k-Scene; augmentation magnitudes; statistical uncertainty; training/evaluation asset-overlap checks; and concrete code, checkpoint, and benchmark access details.
