# Enoki: Efficient Multi-Level Hallucination Detection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00581) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00581)


## One-sentence takeaway
Enoki uses text-anchored relational facts to combine factual verification with hallucination localization, reporting stronger entity/span results and faster lightweight variants, although missing result tables limit assessment of its headline gains.

## Problem and motivation
Claim-level detectors provide interpretable factual judgments, while span-level detectors identify unsupported text. Connecting these outputs typically requires additional alignment and costly LLM calls, introducing opportunities for error propagation. The authors seek a shared representation that supports both verification and localization efficiently (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared factual representation:** Extract subject–predicate–object triples anchored to answer text, verify them against reference evidence, and project unsupported arguments back to spans. Incremental refinements isolate newly introduced unsupported information (§3).
- **Three extraction backends:** Enoki-LLM extends CycleOIE prompting; Enoki-Rule uses dependency rules developed through gated agent-assisted refinement; Enoki-Encoder adapts OpenIE6’s iterative grid labeling with ModernBERT-large and permutation-invariant Hungarian matching (§3.1). These are adaptations of existing components within the proposed framework.
- **EnokiQA:** English Wikipedia QA data with 3,990 automatically labeled examples and 19,594 unlabeled examples, aligning triple verification with span annotations. The encoder learns from Enoki-LLM extractions on the development split (§§4–5.1).

Verification uses maximum entailment across overlapping evidence chunks; experimental explicit-verification pipelines share ModernBERT-large-nli (§§3, 5).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Extraction omissions constrain recall; non-nested or interacting errors weaken incremental projection; cross-sentence references remain partially handled; verifier calibration affects final judgments (Limitations).
- **Analyst inference—metric sensitivity:** A small prediction anywhere inside a broad gold span can receive containment credit, even without isolating the actual error. Boundary-sensitive metrics and human assessment would complement Span Coverage F1 (Appendix E).
- **Analyst inference—supervision dependence:** EnokiQA annotations and encoder supervision share Enoki-LLM extraction, potentially transmitting its omissions and granularity preferences (§§4–5.1). Human span agreement provides only limited reassurance.
- **Analyst inference—scope:** English Wikipedia construction and English-restricted span evaluations leave broader linguistic generalization unestablished (§§4, 5.3).
- **Reporting inconsistencies:** §5.1 describes 5,474 development sentences with 36,865 triples, whereas Appendix H.2 describes a 58k-sentence EnokiQA validation corpus; Table 13’s average of 250.6 triples per development answer also needs reconciliation with the training total. Filtering or different data versions may explain this, but the text does not specify.

## Open research questions
- **Does improved containment reflect better error localization?** Exact-boundary quality is not established. Compare rankings under Span Coverage F1, character overlap, and human judgments of minimal unsupported spans.
- **How much quality is lost through automatic supervision?** Enoki-Encoder inherits LLM extractions, and automatic span agreement is imperfect. Train matched models with automatic versus independently corrected triples and evaluate on human annotations.
- **Can discourse-aware extraction improve reliability?** Optional coreference has inconsistent effects. Compare sentence-wise extraction with document-aware extraction on examples annotated for coreference, ellipsis, and attribution.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method is clearly described, but quantitative confidence is limited by absent bodies of most results tables. Material missing information includes full baseline scores, uncertainty estimates, hardware and batching details, training hyperparameters, actual prompt contents, generator identities, and reconciliation of development-data statistics. Code and dataset availability is asserted in the supplied text but was not independently checked.
