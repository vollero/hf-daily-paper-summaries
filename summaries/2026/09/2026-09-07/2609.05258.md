# Ask Before You Optimize: Dynamic Pre-Formulation Clarification for Interactive Optimization

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05258) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05258)


## One-sentence takeaway
InterOPT improves recovery of missing optimization requirements in choice-based clarification, but requires substantially more questions and trails the strongest free-form baseline on the authors’ OR-Clarify benchmark.

## Problem and motivation
Incomplete business requests can leave objectives, constraints, or modeling assumptions unspecified. Silently choosing defaults can change the resulting optimization problem. The authors argue that existing evaluations largely assess formulation from complete specifications, leaving requirement recovery and readiness to model insufficiently tested (§§1–3).

The proposed task asks whether unresolved business facts could change the formulation and, if so, which clarification questions should precede modeling.

## Approach and contributions
- **Task definition:** Formulation completeness means that all plausible business completions consistent with the public information induce the same formulation structure (§3).
- **OR-Clarify:** A construction and evaluation framework that masks source-supported requirements while preserving business context and numerical inputs. Its benchmark contains 100 cases and 178 hidden slots, with fact-bounded simulated users and semantic recovery rubrics. It supports free-form and choice-based interaction (§4).
- **InterOPT:** A training-free, two-stage policy. Dynamic Gap Search maintains a public-evidence gap ledger; Gap-Guided Action Search generates three candidate questions and selects one, while independently deciding whether to stop. Choice questions include three generated options plus a fixed free-form correction option (§§5–6.2).

The ledger marks queried gaps as **Asked**, not resolved; neither an empty ledger nor remaining open entries determines readiness (§§5.1–5.3).

## Evidence and main results
Unless stated otherwise, comparisons use DeepSeek V4 Pro, 100 cases, five runs per case, and a maximum of 20 turns. Core Exact requires recovery of every P0/P1 slot and covers 94 eligible cases; All-Slot Exact covers all cases. Partial matches receive no exact credit (§§4.3, 6.1).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Choice recovery improves at substantial interaction cost | InterOPT vs. MC-D: Core Exact **0.675 vs. 0.506**; All-Slot Exact **0.638 vs. 0.474**; silent assumptions/run **0.366 vs. 0.692**; average atomic questions **10.036 vs. 2.350**. | Table 2 |
| InterOPT does not lead free-form recovery | Open InterOPT vs. adapted ORPilot: Core Exact **0.538 vs. 0.583**; All-Slot Exact **0.492 vs. 0.546**; silent assumptions/run **0.560 vs. 0.268**; average atomic questions **4.824 vs. 4.918**. | Table 2 |
| Both stages contribute to Choice endpoint recovery | Core Exact: full InterOPT **0.675**, without Stage 1 **0.564**, without Stage 2 **0.615**. Average atomic questions also differ: **10.036**, **3.456**, and **4.860**, respectively. | Table 3 |
| Open action selection reduces questioning, with mixed behavioral effects | Full InterOPT vs. without Stage 2: Core Exact **0.538 vs. 0.506**, average atomic questions **4.824 vs. 9.502**, but silent assumptions/run **0.560 vs. 0.254**. | Table 3 |
| Premature readiness remains frequent | Open InterOPT declares readiness in **98.8%** of runs; the stopping audit marks **203/500 runs (40.6%)** premature. | §6.6, pp. 13–14 |

These results establish a Choice endpoint recovery advantage under this harness, alongside a clear interaction-cost trade-off. Figure 4 provides question-budget comparisons with case-level bootstrap intervals, but the supplied text does not provide numerical curve values establishing a uniform budget-matched advantage. The evaluation does not establish downstream formulation correctness, solution quality, or effectiveness with real users.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Question efficiency, stopping calibration, residual silent assumptions, and benchmark expansion remain unresolved; gains do not transfer uniformly to free-form clarification (§7).
- **Analyst inference—generalization:** Main method comparisons use one tested model. Table 1 compares other models only under base protocols, leaving InterOPT’s cross-model gains unestablished (§§6.1–6.3).
- **Analyst inference—benchmark realism:** Deterministic masking and fact-bounded simulated answers may differ from naturally incomplete requests and uncertain human responses. Source-domain composition and real-user validation are not reported (§4).
- **Analyst inference—component attribution:** Ablations change architecture and interaction length together; removing Stage 2 also relocates the readiness decision. They do not isolate individual mechanisms at equal question or inference budgets (§6.5).
- **Analyst inference—completeness proxy:** Hidden-slot recovery measures annotated omissions, not exhaustive equivalence across all plausible formulations. Queried ledger entries also need not be resolved (§§3–5).
- **Comparison scope:** Prior-system baselines are task-aligned adapters, excluding their downstream tools and solver workflows (§6.2.2).

## Open research questions
- **Can readiness decisions track confirmed resolution?** Asked gaps are not necessarily resolved, and premature stopping remains common. Compare explicit resolution tracking against the existing ledger under matched question budgets.
- **How much Choice improvement survives equal resource budgets?** Endpoint gains accompany longer interactions, while inference costs are unreported. Compare policies at fixed atomic-question, token, and latency budgets.
- **Does clarification improve downstream models with real users?** Evaluation ends before formulation and uses simulated responses. Test naturally incomplete requests with human users, then independently assess generated formulations.
- **Do gains generalize across models and task distributions?** Full InterOPT comparisons primarily use one model and one source collection. Repeat the method comparisons across tested models and held-out OR domains.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the reported within-benchmark comparisons; broader effectiveness remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes source-task provenance and domain breakdown, full prompts, simulator/judge model identities, human-audit procedures and agreement, numerical budget-matched results, inference costs, and downstream or real-user evaluation. Code and dataset links are supplied, but their contents were not inspected.
