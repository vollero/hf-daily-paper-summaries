# Training-Free Speech-Centric Omni Understanding with Frozen VLMs

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04242) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04242)


## One-sentence takeaway
TFO routes speech transcripts into frozen VLMs without additional training, improving reported audio-only averages across every tested pairing while remaining weaker on non-speech acoustics and adding transcription latency (Tables 2, 8).

## Problem and motivation
The authors ask whether every VLM needs a separately trained audio pathway to understand speech alongside visual inputs. They argue that native omni training is expensive, backbone-dependent, and potentially harmful to existing capabilities. The identified evaluation gap is a systematic comparison against frozen VLMs supplied with speech transcripts, including tests of capability preservation (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Training-free integration:** Whisper-large-v3-turbo supplies confidence-filtered transcripts and optional segment timestamps through the existing language interface; the complete VLM remains frozen. The default threshold is 0.65, and audio context is omitted when no reliable speech remains. Optional CosyVoice3 speech output is outside the evaluated understanding pipeline (§3; Appendix C).
- **Comparative study:** Five pairings across Qwen2.5, MiniCPM4.5, VILA, and Qwen3 cover a reported 56 core datasets and 21 CoVoST2 languages, including visual, reasoning, medical QA, and grounding preservation tests (§4.1; Appendix A).
- **Boundary analysis:** Transcript, timestamp, filtering, auxiliary-audio, and latency experiments examine where linguistic evidence suffices and where acoustic information is lost (§§4.4–4.5; Appendix D).

The authors acknowledge prior transcript-based model composition; their principal claimed novelty is the systematic matched control and preservation analysis (§2.1).

## Evidence and main results

Numerical averages below are **reported values**, subject to the aggregation concern noted afterward.

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| AV performance is competitive, not uniformly better | Reported percentage-score averages, native → TFO: Qwen2.5-3B **43.2→46.2**, Qwen2.5-7B **45.8→48.0**, VILA **46.7→47.1**, MiniCPM **54.6→52.4**, Qwen3 **54.9→54.1**. Tasks use option accuracy or semantic correctness. | Table 1; Appendix E |
| Audio-only aggregate improves in every pairing | Across nine tasks, reported average gains are **1.5, 1.4, 4.0, 13.5, 1.4 points** for Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, MiniCPM, VILA, Qwen3. These averages mix accuracy measures and MELD weighted F1. | Table 2; Appendix E |
| Translation gains survive an alternative metric | MiniCPM CoVoST2 macro-average across 21 languages rises **45.6→64.0** in thresholded translation accuracy and **33.6→46.3** in mean sentence-level BLEU-1. Both metrics improve on average in every pairing. | Tables 3, 13; Appendix E |
| Preservation is broad but has exceptions | Image and medical QA averages improve in every pairing; video averages improve except MiniCPM. Coding/math averages improve except NVILA. Grounding generally improves, with model-dependent RefCOCO results. | Tables 4–7 |
| Acoustic verification remains weaker | Qwen3 AVHBench higher-is-better scores: AV matching **61.57→61.25**; video-driven audio hallucination **75.72→68.30**; audio-driven video hallucination **77.64→86.80**. MMAR exact-match accuracy declines across every pairing. | Tables 2, 8 |
| Timestamps provide targeted gains | Qwen2.5-VL-3B with versus without timestamps: AVUT-Gemini option accuracy **58.9 vs 56.6**; Daily-Omni **60.6 vs 59.2**. | Tables 9, 11; Appendix E |
| ASR increases inference time | On AVMeme, Qwen3 native inference is **2.39 s**; TFO reports **0.74 s ASR + 2.34 s VLM**. Experiments use MI210 GPUs. | Table 8; Appendix B |

The evidence supports transcript routing as a useful speech-centric baseline and shows advantages over several native checkpoints. It does not isolate native training as the cause of capability differences. Coding scores are LLM-judged solution correctness, **not execution-based pass@1**; transcription scores are judged content coverage, **not WER/CER** (Appendix E).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Transcripts discard music, environmental sounds, vocal tone, and other acoustic evidence. Auxiliary textual descriptions offer inconsistent gains, while sequential ASR adds latency (§4.4; Appendix D).
- **Analyst inference—causality:** Existing checkpoint comparisons do not control training histories. “Capability drift” is a plausible interpretation rather than an isolated causal result (§§4.1–4.3).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** Each model is evaluated once; uncertainty estimates are absent. Custom judges and heterogeneous aggregate metrics limit comparisons with standard benchmark reports (Appendices B, E).
- **Analyst inference—scope:** Component ablations focus on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Their generality across backbones is untested, and failed textual concatenation does not establish that all textual acoustic representations are inadequate (Tables 11–12).
- **Reporting ambiguities:** Table 1’s displayed averages do not reconcile with Appendix E’s stated exclusion of diagnostic subsets. MiniCPM CoVoST2 is 45.5 in Table 2 but 45.6 in Table 3. Appendix D discusses CoVoST2 ablation values absent from Table 12.
- **Implementation ambiguity:** §3 describes post-transcription segment filtering, whereas Appendix D describes confidence gating before transcription; their relationship and confidence definition are unclear.

## Open research questions
- **How much depends on ASR quality?** No alternative ASR or reference-transcript comparison is reported. Compare Whisper, another ASR front-end, and reference transcripts across languages and noisy inputs.
- **Can acoustic evidence be added without degrading reasoning?** Simple auxiliary-text concatenation is inconsistent. Compare timestamped, query-selected acoustic descriptions with direct concatenation and a dedicated acoustic pathway on MMAR and AVHBench.
- **Does native adaptation itself cause capability loss?** Checkpoint comparisons leave training differences unresolved. Run controlled adaptations from one initial VLM with matched data and compute, then evaluate both speech gains and original capabilities.
- **Do gains persist under standard scoring?** Several tasks use custom correctness measures. Re-evaluate generated code through execution and speech recognition through WER/CER alongside the reported judges.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The method and broad performance pattern are well documented, but aggregation inconsistencies and custom evaluation weaken precise quantitative conclusions.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes the actual evaluation code and full judge prompts, per-task sample counts, uncertainty estimates, complete visual preprocessing settings, a precise confidence definition, and sufficient timing details for independent latency reproduction. Appendix B announces a future toolkit release; Appendix E claims supplementary code is included, but that code is not supplied here.
