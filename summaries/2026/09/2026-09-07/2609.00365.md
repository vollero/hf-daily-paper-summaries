# Dr. Claw: An AI Scientist Workspace for Vibe Research

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00365) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00365)


## One-sentence takeaway
Dr. Claw integrates existing coding agents into a persistent, human-supervised research workspace, with a small pilot suggesting better research-component coverage but not establishing greater scientific correctness or statistically reliable improvement.

## Problem and motivation
The authors argue that capable coding agents leave research planning, execution, verification, and writing fragmented across tools, making decisions and artifacts difficult to inspect or recover. Their proposed “Vibe Research” workflow keeps humans responsible for direction and acceptance while agents execute tasks within a continuous, recorded process (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Research orchestration:** A dependency-aware Task Graph, Artifact Store, Decision Log, and Execution Trace preserve workflow state through a Plan–Execute–Verify–Write-back loop (§§3.1–3.3).
- **Reusable capabilities and executor integration:** The workspace wraps existing coding agents, attaches stage-specific skills to tasks, and supports executor switching, permissions, and retry/revise/handoff operations. It reports 58 stage-mapped skills and 171 catalogue skills; recommendations do not guarantee invocation (§§3.4–3.5; Appendix B.2).
- **Integration and evaluation:** The contribution is the combination of research-specific state, human control, and existing executors—not a new model or individually novel orchestration primitive. Evidence comprises a matched-executor pilot, a recovery demonstration, and a retrospective human study (§2.1; Table 1; §5).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Higher observed completeness, with substantial uncertainty | Three medical tasks, one run per condition per task; same Codex/gpt-5.4 backend and `danger-full-access/approval-never` profile. Mean fraction of 21 components: **0.952 Dr. Claw vs 0.873 bare**; two wins and one tie. Difference **+0.079**, reported 95% bootstrap CI **[−0.00, +0.14]**, including zero. | §5.1; Fig. 4 |
| Differences concern research hygiene | Across those tasks, pooled pass rates, bare → Dr. Claw: limitations **0.33 → 1.00**, subgroup analysis **0.33 → 1.00**, related-work citations **0.00 → 0.67**. Both conditions passed the reported modeling elements at **1.00**. | §5.1; Fig. 5 |
| Structured process records are available by design | Dr. Claw persisted task graphs, traces, decision logs, and claim–evidence organization; the bare agent did not produce these workspace-specific objects. Authors explicitly reject treating their presence as a comparative performance score. | §5.1; Fig. 6 |
| Recovery demonstrated in one project | Following an induced wrong-path error in a Derm7pt mini-project, Dr. Claw recovered to reported accuracy **0.892**, retaining all **5** pre-existing files across **23** tool events. No matched bare-agent recovery run was conducted. | §5.2; Fig. 7 |
| Human-study associations favor the workspace | **Seven AI PhD researchers**, three stages, live Dr. Claw logs versus retrospective No-AI and Web/Desktop-AI controls. Experience favored Dr. Claw against both controls in every stage: Holm-corrected pairwise **p = 0.0469**. Time-band comparisons reached **p = 0.0469** against both controls in Experiment and Publication; output-quality and switching comparisons were less consistently significant. | §5.3; Appendix A.1–A.3 |

The pilot supports a directional improvement in component coverage and demonstrates persistent records and one recovery path. It does not establish scientific correctness, component-specific causation, superior recovery versus another system, or format-neutral traceability superiority. The human study establishes exploratory associations under unequal data-collection methods.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The automated pilot is underpowered, covers only medical tasks, and bundles skills, instructions, task graphs, and state management without ablations. The control is a bare executor rather than another orchestration framework (Limitations).
- **Author-stated:** Completeness measures presence, not correctness. Skill execution is best-effort; the reference audit failed to activate on the tied task (§5.1).
- **Author-stated:** The human study risks recall bias, familiarity differences, and subjective ratings; Experiment timing excludes model runtime (Appendix A).
- **Analyst inference:** Runtime efficiency cannot be assessed quantitatively: the text says Dr. Claw is slower and calls the overhead bounded, but supplies no elapsed-time measurements for the automated comparison (§5.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Audit-state counts are ambiguous: §5.1 reports both a mean of 17 tracked tasks and a mean of 14 task-graph nodes without explaining their relationship.

## Open research questions
- **Which components produce the coverage gains?** Bundled evaluation leaves attribution unresolved. Next experiment: compare bare, skills-only, orchestration-only, and full-system conditions across repeated runs.
- **Does increased coverage improve scientific correctness?** The rubric checks component presence. Next experiment: blinded expert assessment of analyses, citations, and claim–evidence consistency.
- **Can verified skill execution improve reliability?** Suggested skills sometimes fail to run. Next experiment: compare recommendation-only dispatch with execution verification, measuring omissions and added runtime.
- **Does the workflow generalize beyond medical tasks?** Cross-domain transfer remains untested. Next experiment: evaluate matched research tasks in additional fields, including human intervention effort and structural friction.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and reported pilot outcomes are clear; efficacy and generalizability remain uncertain.

Material information unavailable in the supplied text includes the complete scoring rules and exact prompts, clinical-note dataset details, repeated-run outcomes, automated runtime costs, expert correctness assessments, human-study raw observations and rater agreement, and exact replication revisions/configurations. The text provides a repository link and replication checklist but does not establish that all evaluation artifacts are released.
