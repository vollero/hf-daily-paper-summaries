# Iris: Climbing to the Search Frontier

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-07.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04304) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04304)


## One-sentence takeaway
Iris combines synthetic multi-hop search tasks with alternating supervised and reinforcement learning, reporting strong size-matched benchmark results while showing that inference-time context management substantially affects scores (Tables 1–2).

## Problem and motivation
Search agents must retrieve and combine evidence over long interactions. The authors identify two gaps: ordinary web questions often lack sufficient difficulty for training, and benchmark comparisons can conflate learned search ability with inference-time context management (CM), which extends effective search beyond a filled context window (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Task construction:** Build entity graphs from seed pages and hyperlinks, generate multi-hop questions, and replace named clues with descriptive references. Retain questions only when a reference model fails without evidence but succeeds with the graph supplied (§2).
- **Filtered supervision:** Collect teacher ReAct trajectories using SEARCH and SCRAPE. Filter whole trajectories for correctness, repetition, and search depth; then mask poor assistant turns using a judge rubric induced from sampled critiques (§3.1).
- **SFT–RL climbing:** Alternate supervised fine-tuning with live-search RL. Feed successful, relatively short trajectories from questions with low rollout success rates into subsequent supervised rounds. Resume interrupted RL trajectories from cached prefixes with truncated importance sampling; serve the reward judge and page summarizer inside the cluster (§§3.2–3.3).
- **Paired evaluation:** Evaluate two MoE agents with and without CM using a single ReAct agent, without sub-agents or test-time verification. The contribution is the integrated recipe and its results; the paper acknowledges precedents for graph-based synthesis and CM (§1; §4).

The results support strong performance among the listed size-matched systems and substantial benefits from CM. They do not isolate which training components cause the gains. The controlled CM comparison is clearer than cross-system comparisons, whose harnesses are not established as identical. Reported evaluation uses pass@1 and benchmark-specific LLM judging (§4.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ acknowledged scope:** Evaluation emphasizes established search benchmarks; broader realistic search scenarios remain future work. Retry adds substantial cost, and gaps to frontier systems remain (§§4.2–4.3).
- **Analyst inference—causal attribution:** No component ablations isolate task abstraction, fine filtering, climbing, or partial-rollout reuse. Their individual contributions remain unresolved (§§3–4).
- **Analyst inference—measurement:** No uncertainty estimates, repeated-run results, or quantitative inference-cost accounting accompany Tables 1–2. Small score differences therefore lack demonstrated statistical or cost-adjusted significance.
- **Analyst inference—verification limits:** Reference-model success with supplied evidence does not independently establish unique answers. Likewise, blocking benchmark-hosting Hugging Face pages does not establish absence of all contamination (§2.3; §4.1).
- **Annotation concern:** Appendix A presents one alleged BrowseComp-ZH ground-truth error. This supports investigating annotation quality, but does not establish a benchmark-wide error rate.

## Open research questions
- **Which training components matter most?** The integrated recipe lacks component ablations. Run matched-budget experiments removing anchor abstraction, turn masking, and climbing separately.
- **How much CM benefit survives equal compute budgets?** Retry improves scores but its cost is unquantified. Compare strategies under fixed total tokens, tool calls, and latency.
- **Does search training reliably transfer beyond browsing?** Section 5 gives only qualitative transfer claims. Evaluate matched base and search-trained teachers on the named non-search benchmarks with explicit distillation controls.
- **What limits BrowseComp-ZH performance?** Tied scores and one disputed annotation do not identify the bottleneck. Audit failed items independently and rerun evaluation with adjudicated references.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The methods and benchmark tables are explicit, but causal and reproducibility evidence is incomplete.

Missing details include training-set sizes and mixture proportions, teacher/reference identities, synthesis and filtering thresholds, RL hyperparameters and climb counts, actual maximum turn and retry budgets, CM trigger thresholds, compute costs, uncertainty estimates, and quantitative transfer results. Model and pipeline release is promised, not established as completed in the supplied text.
