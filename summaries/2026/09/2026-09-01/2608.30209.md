# DICS: Exploring Data Intrinsic Consistency for Visual Instruction Selection

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30209) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30209)


## One-sentence takeaway

DICS selects visual-instruction samples by combining model-derived image–response and instruction–response consistency scores with diversity-aware sampling, matching or exceeding full-data tuning with substantially smaller subsets in the reported experiments.

## Problem and motivation

Visual instruction datasets are growing faster than they can be economically curated and trained on. Existing subset-selection methods emphasize global diversity, representativeness, influence, or heuristic quality, but often ignore whether each sample’s image, instruction, and response are internally coherent. The authors argue that this omission can retain samples answerable from language priors, irrelevant responses, and hallucinated content; text-only consistency metrics and simple image–text similarity do not directly measure whether visual information is necessary (Sections 1, 2.2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Data Intrinsic Consistency (DIC):** A self-scoring metric defined as `min(VIC, RIC)`, requiring both forms of consistency to be high (Sections 3.1–3.2; Eq. 6).
- **Visual Information Consistency (VIC):** Measures the response-loss reduction obtained by conditioning on the image rather than the instruction alone (Eq. 1–3). POS-based weights emphasize content tokens.
- **Response Information Consistency (RIC):** Uses inverse prediction to measure how much the response helps reconstruct the instruction given the image (Eq. 4–5; Appendix A.3).
- **Budget-adaptive selection:** DICS ranks samples by DIC and combines the ranking with Farthest Point Sampling (FPS). Below 50%, FPS operates within a high-DIC candidate pool; at 50%, DICS takes the top half; above 50%, it retains a high-DIC core and uses FPS to rescue diverse remaining samples (Section 4; Algorithms 1–2).
- **Scale and transfer evaluation:** The method is tested across LLaVA-1.5-665K, Vision-FLAN-186K, and the authors’ 6M-sample DICS-6M corpus, including cross-architecture selection and training (Sections 5.1–5.3).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that DICS improves data efficiency across the tested datasets and architectures, with especially strong results on LLaVA-1.5-665K and DICS-6M. It does not establish that the scores are architecture-independent in general, that DICS-6M is intrinsically superior to the official InternVL training corpus, or that the aggregate gains transfer beyond the reported image–text models, training recipes, and benchmarks. The human correlations also indicate alignment with perceived quality, but only weak-to-moderate ranking agreement.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** DIC currently covers image–text data, excluding video and audio (Section 6).
- **Author-stated:** The optimal subset size is not automatically determined and varies across datasets and metrics (Sections 5.2 and 6).
- **Author-stated:** Low-DIC samples are discarded rather than repaired (Section 6).
- **Analyst inference:** DIC scores depend on a particular warmed-up selector, prompts, POS tagger, and model losses. Cross-architecture results are encouraging but cover only the reported LLaVA and Qwen settings (Section 5.1; Tables 3 and 11).
- **Analyst inference:** Text-only samples are assigned a fixed DIC of 1.0, so their quality is not assessed using an equivalent consistency test (Section 5.1; Appendix A.2).
- **Analyst inference:** DICS-6M is assembled following InternVL’s protocol, but the supplied text does not provide a controlled same-data comparison against the official 21.7M training mixture; the 94.52% comparison therefore combines differences in both volume and corpus composition (Table 4; Appendix B.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Human validation uses 750 stratified examples and reports correlations but not annotator count, agreement, sampling uncertainty, or detailed annotation procedure (Appendix C.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 8 describes the low-ratio pool as “top-k,” whereas Section 4 and Algorithm 1 specify top-`2k`; this textual inconsistency could impede reproduction if the algorithm is not treated as authoritative.
- **Analyst inference:** The attention-map evidence relies on an empirically chosen layer and is qualitative, so it does not independently demonstrate improved causal visual grounding (Appendix C.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can the budget be chosen automatically?** Performance peaks at different ratios, and the authors identify automatic subset sizing as future work. A next experiment could estimate a stopping point from held-out DIC distributions and validate it across all three datasets.

2. **How well does intrinsic consistency extend beyond images?** Video and audio are explicitly excluded. A next experiment could define modality-removal loss contrasts for video–text or audio–text instruction samples and compare them with modality-specific corruption tests.

3. **Can low-DIC samples be repaired instead of removed?** The paper only filters them. A next experiment could regenerate or edit the suspected inconsistent component, rescore the repaired sample, and compare training with repaired, discarded, and original data.

4. **How selector-dependent are DIC rankings?** Transfer is tested across two 7B selectors and one larger target, but broader invariance is unestablished. A next experiment could measure rank correlation and downstream subset performance across several selector sizes and architecture families under identical data and budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported results because the supplied text includes equations, algorithms, detailed tables, ablations, repeated runs, and appendices.

Material missing or unclear information includes the complete training hyperparameters beyond the summarized settings, exact data licenses and deduplication procedures for DICS-6M, annotator details and agreement statistics, uncertainty for most benchmark-level comparisons, per-ratio numerical values underlying Figure 1, and access to the promised released models, dataset, and executable code state.
