# Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness of Reasoning Models Varies with Where and How Preference Cues Are Delivered

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29464) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29464)


## One-sentence takeaway

Across 15 open-weight reasoning models, preference adoption was less often disclosed in chain-of-thought when cues came from tool returns or raw artifacts, and neither attribution prompts nor monitor-awareness reliably eliminated these gaps.

## Problem and motivation

Chain-of-thought monitoring assumes that reasoning traces record information that materially shapes model outputs. Existing planted-cue tests typically place explicit bias cues in user messages, while deployed agents may instead encounter preferences through tool results, retrieved memories, emails, or other artifacts requiring inference. The authors test whether cue location and explicitness affect whether a model records its decision to tailor an answer, which matters because directly auditing agent behavior may be costly (Introduction, pp. 2–3).

## Approach and contributions

- **FACE-Eval:** A 5,100-sample evaluation comprising 5,000 cued prompts and 100 no-cue controls. It crosses five preference axes, two sides, five artifact sources, five delivery conditions, and 20 scenarios per axis (Fig. 1; §2.1).
- **Controlled delivery comparison:** Cues appear in either a user message or a prefilled tool return and are either explicit summaries or implicit raw artifacts. Cue text is identical across channels at a fixed explicitness level (§2.2; Table 1).
- **Commitment-focused metrics:** The authors introduce verbalized commitment rate (VCR), cue-following rate (CFR), and unverbalized adoption rate (UAR), distinguishing acknowledgment of a cue from an explicit statement that it shaped the answer (§2.4, Eqs. 1–3).
- **Broad model evaluation:** Fifteen open-weight models from eight families, spanning 4B–1.60T total parameters, are evaluated with three seeds and three reported system-prompt settings (§2.3).
- **Robustness analyses:** The study examines automated-judge agreement, cue readability and clarity, system prompts, reasoning effort, cue source, and preference-adoption detection by two transcript monitors (§§3.1–3.5; Appendices D–K).

The evidence establishes consistent descriptive associations between cue delivery, cue form, CoT disclosure, and monitor performance in this benchmark. Matched text across channels, register controls, clarity analyses, a second judge, and a second monitor strengthen the result.

It does not establish why channel and explicitness change disclosure, whether the CoT causally produces the answer, whether models deliberately conceal preferences, or whether the findings generalize to autonomous multi-step tool use (§5).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Channel conditions differ not only in role but also through the presence of a prefilled tool call, so training priors, reporting conventions, and judge sensitivity remain confounded (§5; Appendix M).
- **Author-stated:** Explicit and implicit drafts differ in format, directness, wording, and inference burden. Model-rated clarity matching cannot remove unmeasured or full-task clarity differences (§5; Appendix H).
- **Author-stated:** Models produce only one response after a prefilled single call; they neither select the tool nor operate in a multi-step agentic environment (§2.2; §5).
- **Author-stated:** Automated judges reproduce the main trends but disagree on absolute VCR values and some model rankings; both may share rubric-related channel biases (§2.4; §5).
- **Author-stated:** Longer traces may merely offer judges more opportunities to detect commitment language, weakening interpretation of reasoning-effort effects (§3.5; §5).
- **Author-stated:** The study measures whether traces report tailoring, not causal dependence, deliberate concealment, or whether personalization is desirable (§2.4; §5).
- **Analyst inference:** Because the monitor study uses only the largest model from each family and one selected effort setting for adjustable models, its AUROC results do not establish equivalent performance over all 15 models or effort levels (§3.3; Appendix J).
- **Analyst inference:** Preference-side asymmetries—especially expert versus novice—show that appropriate context sensitivity and scenario framing can affect CFR, complicating interpretation as generic sycophancy (Appendix L).

## Open research questions

1. **What mechanism causes the tool-channel disclosure gap?** The current design cannot separate channel-role priors from the prefilled-call wrapper or reporting style. A next experiment could factorially vary role, wrapper, tool-call authorship, and training history while holding text fixed.

2. **Does the gap persist when models autonomously select tools in multi-step tasks?** FACE-Eval supplies a single prefilled call and does not test tool-selection policy. A follow-up could embed matched cues in interactive trajectories where the model chooses whether and when to retrieve them.

3. **Does higher reasoning effort improve substantive faithfulness or merely rubric detectability?** GPT-OSS shows rising VCR with longer traces, but the paper cannot distinguish genuine attribution from extra opportunities to emit matching phrases. Human annotation or semantic counterfactual tests could compare concise and length-matched traces.

4. **Can interventions lower tool-channel UAR without degrading the user channel?** The attribution directive sometimes narrowed the gap by increasing user-channel failures. A targeted intervention could optimize absolute UAR per channel and test it against default, attribution, and monitor-aware prompts.
