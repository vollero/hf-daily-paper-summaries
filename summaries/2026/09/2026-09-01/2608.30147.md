# CAST: Critique-Aware Supervision for Training Reliable Long-Horizon Tool-Calling Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30147) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30147)


## One-sentence takeaway

CAST turns trajectory-level success signals into step-level critique supervision, enabling smaller fine-tuned tool-calling agents to improve repeated-run reliability and, with a learned critic, transfer some gains beyond the training domain.

## Problem and motivation

Long-horizon agents can fail irreversibly after one incorrect tool call, policy violation, or hallucinated argument, while sparse trajectory rewards do not identify the responsible action. The authors argue that repeated-run reliability is distinct from single-run success and that existing prompt-based critics, post-hoc self-correction, and actor–critic training do not adequately supervise why an intermediate action is invalid under the information available at that step (Sections 1–3).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Action-level supervision from sparse outcomes.** CAST repeatedly samples trajectories and uses a multi-agent verifier to label each proposed action as valid or invalid with a structured rationale (Sections 3.2–3.3; Figure 2).

2. **Specialized learned critic.** CAST-Critic is trained jointly for rationale generation and binary verification, considering only the history, current observation, candidate action, tools, and domain constraints available at that step (Sections 3.1 and 3.4).

3. **Critique-aware policy training.** The critic intercepts and revises proposed actions during a second collection phase; successful critique-enriched trajectories are then used to supervised-fine-tune CAST-Policy, which can run alone or with the critic at inference (Section 3.5).

4. **Cross-domain evaluation.** Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B variants are trained only on Retail and evaluated on Retail, Airline, Telecom, and Telehealth using single-run success and repeated-run reliability (Sections 4.1–4.2).

The evidence supports meaningful improvements over same-size base and RFT models on in-domain Retail reliability, plus strong critic-guided results in several held-out settings. It does not establish uniform out-of-domain improvement: several model/domain combinations regress, notably Telehealth for the 8B actor–critic configuration. Nor does it establish real-world safety, because evaluation uses simulated customer-service benchmarks and task reward rather than deployment outcomes.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Training is supervised rather than on-policy, so the policy does not directly learn correction strategies through its own exploration with critic feedback (Limitations, page 10).
- **Author-stated:** Verification judges the current action rather than explicitly predicting downstream consequences (Limitations, page 10).
- **Author-demonstrated failure:** CAST-Critic can misread argument roles and approve an invalid consequential action (Appendix F.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Domain transfer is mixed rather than consistently positive; Table 1 contains held-out cases where CAST variants underperform RFT or base models.
- **Analyst inference:** All training data comes from one Retail split and one teacher-policy/user-simulator pipeline, limiting evidence about robustness to other annotation sources and environments (Section 4.1; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text does not report uncertainty intervals or statistical significance, making the stability of small percentage-point differences unclear.
- **Reporting ambiguity:** Retail is listed as 114 tasks in Table 2, 115 tasks in Appendix C, and 500 training tasks in Section 4.1; these may refer to different splits, but the distinction is not fully clarified.
- **Reproducibility risk:** Prompts and hyperparameters are supplied, but public availability of code, generated annotations, and trained checkpoints is not established in the retrieved text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can on-policy optimization make policies use critiques more effectively?** The current policy is trained only on successful filtered trajectories. A next experiment could compare CAST-SFT against reinforcement learning using CAST-Critic as a feedback model on identical training tasks and budgets.

2. **Would forward-looking critiques prevent locally plausible but harmful actions?** CAST verifies present-step validity without modeling downstream states. A controlled ablation could train critics with versus without predicted consequences and evaluate irreversible-action errors separately.

3. **How robust is CAST to critic annotation errors?** Appendix F.4 shows an incorrect approval, but aggregate sensitivity to noisy labels is not reported. An experiment could inject controlled label and rationale corruption and measure pass^k, false approvals, and unnecessary revisions.

4. **Does the method generalize beyond Retail-derived supervision and customer service?** Training uses only τ-Bench Retail. A next experiment could train separately on each domain, test all cross-domain directions, and include non-customer-service tool environments.

## Confidence and missing information
