# Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31046) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046)


## One-sentence takeaway

The authors argue that on-policy distillation gains arise largely from suppressing student-sampled low-probability tokens rather than transferring teacher knowledge, and introduce OPSA, a teacher- and reward-free entropy-adaptive method that outperforms OPD and other tested baselines.

## Problem and motivation

On-policy distillation (OPD) gives language-model policies dense, token-level advantages, but its teacher must score student-generated prefixes that are off-policy for the teacher. The authors ask whether these signals reliably transfer teacher knowledge and what actually causes OPD’s improvements.

They identify two gaps: teacher-signal reliability had not been adequately quantified on verifiable answer tokens, and the respective effects of token selection and advantage assignment remained entangled. This matters because OPD requires white-box teacher logits and a shared vocabulary, while self-distillation variants still require hints and may leak information (§1–§2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Teacher-noise diagnosis:** The paper defines noise as an advantage sign that conflicts with final-answer correctness and measures it using verified `\boxed{}` answer tokens from student trajectories (§2.2).

2. **Mechanism isolation:** Filtering experiments, log-probability-based token selection, randomized advantages, and fixed-sign controls test whether OPD depends on clean teacher supervision, high-probability tokens, or teacher-specific advantage values (§2.3–§3.2).

3. **OPSA:** On-Policy Self-Adaptation updates the lowest-20%-log-probability sampled tokens with negative advantages whose magnitude increases with token entropy. It uses only the student’s probabilities and requires no teacher, labels, reference answers, or verifiable rewards (§4.1–§4.2, Eq. 5).

4. **Mechanistic and empirical analysis:** The authors claim OPSA suppresses sampled tail tokens while redistributing mass among competing head tokens, and evaluate it across three Qwen models, mathematical reasoning, code generation, and general question answering (§4.3–§5).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that, in these configurations, clean teacher-specific advantages are not necessary for substantial improvement and that entropy-conditioned negative updates are effective. It does not prove that teacher knowledge never contributes to OPD, establish a general causal theory for all OPD formulations, or show scalability beyond the tested models and tasks. Several mechanistic conclusions rely on training curves and illustrative probability analyses rather than formal guarantees.

## Limitations and validity risks

The authors explicitly limit the study to models of at most 9B parameters, leaving larger dense and mixture-of-experts models untested. They also warn that OPSA mainly redistributes existing probability mass, so it may help less when a heavily post-trained model already has a very sharp, low-entropy distribution. Its ability to expand the policy’s exploration frontier is uncertain, with relatively modest thinking-mode Pass@k gains (Appendix A).

Author-stated and design-specific risks include:

- Most training and evaluation uses Qwen3/Qwen3.5 models in non-thinking mode, limiting model-family and decoding-regime coverage (§5.1).
- Training is math-centered on DAPO-17k; MBPP+ and GPQA-Diamond are evaluation-only transfer tests (§5.1).
- Noise is operationalized only at verifiable final-answer tokens. This does not measure whether intermediate reasoning-token advantages are semantically correct (§2.2).
- Best checkpoints are selected using validation Avg@4, which may favor methods with volatile training curves; validation-set composition is not described in the supplied text (Appendix B.1).

Analyst inference: no uncertainty intervals, repeated-seed results, or significance tests are reported, so the stability of numerical differences across runs is unclear. The reflective-fork ablation depends on a manually specified nine-word set (Appendix B.3), making the proposed reflective-reasoning mechanism suggestive rather than comprehensive. The paper also does not report a direct ablation of entropy estimation quality or alternative token-ranking criteria beyond the tested logp fractions.

## Open research questions

1. **Does OPSA scale beyond 9B and to mixture-of-experts models?** This is explicitly untested due to compute limits. A next experiment could apply an identical training and evaluation protocol to larger dense and MoE policies while measuring accuracy, entropy allocation, and training cost.

2. **When does genuine teacher knowledge matter in OPD?** The experiments show teacher-free equivalence in selected settings, not that all OPD gains are teacher-independent. A factorial experiment could vary teacher–student capability gap, prefix distribution mismatch, and advantage sign/magnitude while controlling the updated token set.

3. **Can OPSA expand rather than merely reshape the exploration frontier?** Appendix A acknowledges this uncertainty. A next experiment could combine OPSA with GRPO or another outcome-reward method and compare discovery of solutions absent from the base policy’s sampled support.

4. **Is entropy the optimal uncertainty signal for assigning negative advantages?** The paper compares positive, zero, and negative entropy correlation but not other uncertainty measures. A controlled study could compare entropy with margin, rank, calibration error, or rollout disagreement under matched gradient budgets.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the reported methods, settings, and tabulated results; **medium** for the broad causal interpretation that OPD generally improves through self-reshaping rather than distillation.

Material missing information includes random-seed counts, error bars or significance tests, validation-set details, exact final values for several curve-only ablations, behavior on models above 9B or non-Qwen architectures, and whether code or trained checkpoints are actually released.
