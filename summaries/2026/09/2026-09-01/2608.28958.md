# CoVA-SFT: A Large-Scale Dataset for Chain of Visual Abstractions

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28958) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28958)


## One-sentence takeaway

CoVA-SFT provides 51,904 self-corrected, interleaved text–visual reasoning trajectories, and its fine-tuned baseline more than doubles the best interleaved baseline’s average on the accompanying in-domain benchmark, but remains substantially behind the strongest text-only baseline overall.

## Problem and motivation

Text-only chain-of-thought must serialize spatial, structural, and relational state into prose, which the authors argue is inefficient for naturally visual problems. Existing visual-reasoning resources are described as limited in scale, domain diversity, sequential depth, or focused on images already present in the input. The identified gap is a large, multi-step dataset for teaching multimodal models to construct and maintain visual workspaces from purely textual problems, together with a reproducible evaluation set (Introduction, pp. 1–2; Table 1).

## Approach and contributions

- **CoVA-SFT dataset:** 51,904 trajectories containing 222,046 visual or multimodal reasoning steps across 17 tasks and five abstraction families: Table, Graph, Layout, Game, and Math (Sections 1–2, pp. 2–3; Figure 2).
- **Agentic synthesis pipeline:** Claude 4.5 Sonnet formulates a rationale and question–answer pair, generates stepwise Matplotlib renderings, receives each image back as context, and checks structural consistency with redraws when errors are detected (Section 2, p. 3; Figure 3).
- **CoVA-Bench:** 1,700 held-out examples—100 per task—from the same 17 tasks and five families, selected for multi-step logical reasoning and visual-state maintenance (Section 2, p. 3).
- **Latent-workspace training baseline:** Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking is fine-tuned with autoregressive text loss plus cosine alignment of predicted and target latent visual tokens, using 128 visual tokens per image (Section 3, pp. 3–4; Table E.1).
- **Detailed task assets:** The corpus combines procedurally generated problems and existing open-source datasets; source repositories are documented in Table B.1, while original material is slated for CC BY 4.0 (Appendix B/D, pp. 7, 15).

The evidence establishes that the dataset supplies a learnable signal and that its latent-workspace baseline substantially outperforms the tested external-rendering interleaved methods on an in-domain benchmark. It does not establish that visual abstractions are generally superior to text-only reasoning: the baseline loses overall and in four of five families. Nor does it establish out-of-domain transfer, causal benefit from individual pipeline stages, or robustness under alternative judges.

## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations**

- Generated traces depend on Claude 4.5 Sonnet; subtle hallucinations or rendering-code errors may survive verification and enter the training distribution (Limitations, p. 5).
- Visual coverage is restricted to static, schematic representations such as tables, graphs, grids, and coordinate plots. Open-world spatial simulations, continuous environments, and robotics are absent (Limitations, p. 5).
- Joint text/visual training increases memory, tuning, context-window, and inference-compute requirements; some trajectories contain eight or more sequential visual states (Limitations, p. 5).
- Models trained on residual errors may reproduce them or become overconfident, so the authors advise against high-stakes use without further validation (Appendix D, p. 7).

**Analyst inferences supported by the text**

- **In-domain validity:** CoVA-Bench is drawn from the same 17 tasks and five families as training, so the reported results do not demonstrate transfer to unseen task types (Section 2, p. 3).
- **Missing causal ablations:** No supplied experiment isolates the effects of self-correction, rationale generation, latent visual supervision, visual-token budget, or individual data sources.
- **Judge dependence:** Headline scores rely on Gemini-2.5-flash because answers have heterogeneous formats, but no agreement study or comparison with exact-match/human grading is supplied (Appendix A, p. 7).
- **Uneven effectiveness:** Large per-task swings—including zero performance on Chess and Sudoku—make the aggregate family averages an incomplete picture (Table B.2, p. 16).
- **Data-quality concern visible in examples:** Some supplied trajectories appear to rely directly on known answers or contain internally confused visual descriptions, consistent with the authors’ warning that logic and rendering errors may remain (Figure C.1, pp. 8–10).

## Open research questions

1. **Does the approach generalize to unseen task families?**  
   This remains open because CoVA-Bench uses the same 17 tasks as training. A next experiment could hold out entire tasks or abstraction families during SFT and evaluate zero-shot transfer.

2. **Which pipeline component produces the gains?**  
   The paper supplies no component ablation. A factorial experiment could remove rationale formulation, image feedback, verification/redrawing, or latent-token loss separately while holding training volume fixed.

3. **When should a model use visual rather than textual intermediate state?**  
   Graph gains contrast with severe Math losses, but the paper tests no routing mechanism. A plausible experiment would train a policy to choose text, visual, or hybrid reasoning per problem and compare accuracy and compute.

4. **How sensitive are results to data errors and evaluation method?**  
   The authors acknowledge residual synthetic errors, while headline scoring uses one LLM judge. A next study could manually audit a stratified sample, inject controlled corruption during training, and compare exact, human, and multiple-judge evaluation.
