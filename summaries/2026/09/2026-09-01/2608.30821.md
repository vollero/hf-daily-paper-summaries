# Lucida: Parse, Generate, and Place for Composable Real-to-Sim Scene Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30821) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30821)


## One-sentence takeaway

Lucida reconstructs cluttered indoor captures as editable, complete 3D object assets by consolidating multi-view evidence, generating objects amodally, and using a closed-loop VLM policy to refine each asset’s 9-DoF placement.

## Problem and motivation

Composable real-to-sim modeling must recover both complete individual objects and their observed spatial arrangement for simulation, embodied AI, AR/VR, and content creation. Existing parse–generate–place pipelines assume precise instance geometry, unobstructed object views, or close agreement between generated assets and observations—conditions rarely met in cluttered, occluded captures. Monolithic reconstructions are not independently editable, while CAD-based systems are limited by library coverage (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

The authors’ central premise is that precision should be achieved through final closed-loop placement rather than required from early parsing and generation stages.

## Approach and contributions

1. **Evidence-centered parsing:** Geometry-aware keyframes, full-sequence instance consolidation, and relation-aware refinement produce a scene graph. Each node contains multi-view observations, masks or boxes, partial point clouds, a representative 3D box, and a referring description (Sec. 2.1, Eq. 1).

2. **Amodal asset generation:** Reliable complementary views are organized with Set-of-Mark prompting and a VLM; an image-editing model synthesizes an isolated, occlusion-free view, which an image-to-3D model converts into a complete asset (Sec. 2.2).

3. **GizmoAct placement:** A VLM treats 9-DoF alignment—rotation, translation, and anisotropic scale—as multi-turn GUI interaction. It observes rendered feedback, applies incremental gizmo-frame edits, can perform coarse axis permutations, and decides when to stop (Secs. 2.3.1–2.3.3; Figs. 3–4).

4. **Interactive policy training:** GizmoAct is supervised on synthetic expert trajectories with injected errors and recovery demonstrations, then optimized with GRPO on online rollouts (Secs. 2.3.4–2.3.5).

5. **R2S evaluation asset:** The authors introduce real-world R2S-Scene and R2S-Object splits for scene reconstruction/detection and object-pose evaluation, respectively (Secs. 3.1–3.3). Release or public availability is not stated.

These results support the authors’ claim that evidence consolidation and closed-loop pose refinement improve the evaluated detection, alignment, and reconstruction metrics. They do not establish generalization to outdoor environments, unposed captures, unseen sensor conditions, or downstream robot-policy performance. Some full-system comparisons also use different evidence: Lucida receives per-instance multi-view evidence, whereas SceneGen and SAM 3D receive a reference image and masks (Sec. 3.3; Fig. 8).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Objects still missing after parsing cannot be recovered by generation or grounding (Conclusion, p. 21).
- **Author-stated:** Agentic refinement applies only to final placement, not the complete parse–generate–place pipeline (Conclusion).
- **Author-stated:** The best pose initializer depends on input-geometry accuracy (Sec. 3.2; Table 3).
- **Author-stated:** Symmetric objects are excluded from RL because the rotation reward lacks symmetry annotations (Sec. 2.3.5).
- **Analyst inference:** Full-system baseline comparability is limited because Lucida uses multi-view evidence while SceneGen and SAM 3D receive a single reference image and masks (Sec. 3.3).
- **Analyst inference:** Pose datasets are modest—199 CA-1M, 125 R2S-Object, and 60 ADT objects—and include manual mask filtering or pose annotation, so broader scalability is not demonstrated (Sec. 3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** The paper does not provide an ablation isolating the amodal generation design, such as multi-view completion versus a single-view generator.
- **Analyst inference:** Runtime, compute cost, model sizes, training-set sizes, and failure rates from invalid or non-completing interaction trajectories are not reported in the supplied text.

## Open research questions

1. **Can missing instances be recovered after parsing?** The current downstream stages cannot restore omitted objects. A next experiment could add a reconstruction-coverage critic that revisits the full video after composition and measure recall and scene F-Score.

2. **Can closed-loop reasoning improve generation as well as placement?** Only placement is agentic. An experiment could iteratively render generated assets into observed views, request regeneration or editing when discrepancies remain, and evaluate object CD/F-Score.

3. **How robust is Lucida without reliable poses or depth?** The method assumes posed captures and commonly uses RGB-D or estimated depth. Testing jointly estimated camera poses and monocular depth under controlled corruption would quantify sensitivity.

4. **Does geometric improvement benefit embodied tasks?** Evaluation stops at reconstruction metrics. A plausible experiment would compare manipulation or navigation policy success in Lucida replicas against baseline reconstructions and the corresponding real environments.
