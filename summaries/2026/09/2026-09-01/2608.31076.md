# Learning to Evaluate Before Improving: Automatic Rubric Induction for Automatic Research Agents

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31076) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31076)


## One-sentence takeaway

AutoSciRub turns underspecified scientific tasks into literature- and data-grounded executable rubrics, then uses criterion-level verification and revision to improve research-agent outputs across two benchmarks, albeit with added and uncontrolled compute.

## Problem and motivation

Open-ended research instructions often omit necessary analyses, methods, evidence, and success criteria, allowing agents to produce plausible reports that miss key experiments or contain unsupported conclusions (Introduction; Figure 1). Expert rubrics make such requirements explicit but require substantial domain expertise and are difficult to scale. Existing automatic rubrics may lack scientific or task-data grounding and are generally used only for post-hoc scoring rather than guiding execution and revision (Introduction, pp. 1–2).

## Approach and contributions

1. **Automatic Rubric Induction:** AutoSciRub decomposes an instruction into atomic goals, grounds them using scientific literature, inspects task-visible data and constraints, and synthesizes executable criteria specifying analyses, metrics, comparisons, artifacts, and satisfaction conditions (Sections 3.1–3.2; Figure 2).

2. **Evaluation-first agent control:** The rubric guides initial execution; a verifier then checks each criterion, describes evidence gaps, and directs up to three targeted revision rounds with adaptive stopping (Section 3.3; Appendix A.3).

3. **Cross-model and cross-harness evaluation:** The method is tested on all 40 ResearchClawBench tasks and a fixed random 20-task subset of AstaBench, covering multiple models and agent harnesses (Section 4.1; Tables 1, 4–5).

4. **Rubric and revision analyses:** The paper measures rubric quality, conducts a cumulative stage-wise ablation, and compares rubric-guided revision against generic self-refinement (Section 4.3–4.4; Tables 2–3; Figures 4–6).

The evidence supports the authors’ claim that AutoSciRub improves evaluator-assessed output quality across the tested model–harness configurations and that structured feedback contributes more than generic rewriting in the studied setup. It does not establish compute efficiency, statistical significance, human-rated scientific correctness, or generalization beyond the two benchmark samples and evaluator models used.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated limitation:** Rubric induction improved specificity and verifiability but reduced scientific-core coverage from 3.35 to 3.07. The authors conclude that high-level scientific framing remains dependent on the backbone model and that grounding may elaborate an off-target direction (Section 4.4; Figure 4).

- **Author-stated resource limitation:** Vanilla and AutoSciRub runs were not compute-matched; there was no shared cap on tokens, tool calls, runtime, or monetary cost. The evaluation therefore measures output quality rather than budget-normalized efficiency (Appendix A.1).

- **Author-stated control mismatch:** Guided and rubric-free revision were neither strictly compute-matched nor workspace-identical. Guided revision used separate verification calls and retained code/output state, whereas self-refinement combined review and revision and began from copied reports and figures (Appendix A.3).

- **Analyst inference:** Scores depend on LLM judges—GPT-5.1, MiniMax-M3, and MiniMax-M3-based rubric-quality judgments—and the supplied text reports no human validation or judge-sensitivity analysis. Consequently, metric gains may not map directly to scientific validity (Section 4.1; Appendix A.1–A.2).

- **Analyst inference:** AstaBench evidence is limited to one seeded 20-task sample from its 40-task Easy test set, so the reported 16.78-point average may not represent the full benchmark or harder tasks (Appendix A.1; Table 4).

- **Analyst inference:** Each task–configuration pair was executed once; only evaluator scoring was repeated three times. The paper therefore does not quantify variance from stochastic agent execution or provide significance tests (Appendix A.1).

- **Analyst inference:** The verifier’s `overall_pass` is an LLM judgment, not a deterministic aggregation of item checks, and lacks a structured “not applicable” state or schema-validation retry. Early stopping may therefore be sensitive to verifier inconsistency (Appendix A.3).

## Open research questions

1. **Can rubric induction recover a missing or incorrect scientific core?** Core coverage declined after grounding, so an experiment could add independent hypothesis-level critics or multiple candidate skeletons and measure core coverage and final-report quality (Figure 4).

2. **Do gains persist under equal resource budgets?** AutoSciRub adds model, retrieval, verification, and revision calls without compute matching. A next experiment should compare methods under fixed token, tool-call, time, and monetary budgets (Appendix A.1).

3. **Are improvements robust to evaluator choice and human review?** Current conclusions rely on LLM judges. A blinded expert study and cross-judge evaluation could measure agreement, factual correctness, and sensitivity of rankings (Section 4.1; Appendix A.1–A.2).

4. **How well does the method generalize beyond the sampled tasks?** AstaBench uses only 20 Easy tasks, while ResearchClawBench contains 40 tasks derived from target papers. Testing full, harder, and independently constructed task sets would probe broader validity (Appendix A.1).

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: high** for the described method and reported benchmark results because the supplied text includes the complete method, tables, appendices, and evaluation protocols.

Material missing information includes statistical uncertainty and significance tests, execution-level variance across repeated agent runs, human evaluation, total compute/cost measurements, cross-evaluator robustness, performance on the full AstaBench test set or harder splits, and an ablation separating literature grounding, data exploration, and criterion synthesis from one another.
