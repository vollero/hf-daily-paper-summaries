# SHAPE of Chain-of-Thought in Math Reasoning

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28600) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28600)


## One-sentence takeaway

SHAPE characterizes mathematical chain-of-thought by its heuristic actions and evolving semantic spaces, finding that these features better predict correctness than conventional CoT statistics and that heuristic-guided RL training improves performance, although the supplied text omits the latter experiment’s numerical results.

## Problem and motivation

Final-answer accuracy and common CoT descriptors—length, revision markers, generic reasoning episodes, or graph structure—provide limited insight into how an LLM mathematically interprets and reorganizes a problem. The authors identify a gap in tracking both the model’s current mathematical framing and the purposeful actions it takes within that framing, which matters for diagnosing failures and improving post-training (§1).

## Approach and contributions

- **SHAPE framework:** Represents each CoT as a sequence of mathematical heuristics and trajectory-relative semantic spaces, drawing on mathematics-education theory (§2.1; Appendix A).
- **Automated annotation:** Segments CoTs into content units, assigns multi-label heuristics, and tracks semantic spaces using `New`, `Maintain`, and `Return` decisions (Figure 2; §2.2).
- **Structural metrics:** Defines entropy-based effective numbers of semantic spaces and transitions, a transition ratio, and heuristic-frequency distributions (§2.3, Equations 1–2).
- **Diagnostic findings:** Tests whether SHAPE features predict correctness, distinguishes successful from diffuse or repetitive reasoning, and analyzes strategy changes under problem perturbations and RL post-training (§§3–4).
- **Training intervention:** Introduces HA-Plan-GRPO, which exposes Qwen3-1.7B-Base to eleven mathematical heuristics during planning-guided RLVR rollouts (§5; Appendix F).

These results support the authors’ claim that heuristic annotations carry useful process-level information and that post-training is associated with narrower heuristic distributions. They establish associations between semantic-space patterns and correctness, not a causal effect of those patterns. The reported HA-Plan-GRPO comparison suggests that heuristic prompting contributes beyond generic planning under the tested setup, but the missing Table 5 values, variance estimates, and significance tests prevent judging the effect size or robustness.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** SHAPE analyzes observable verbalized CoT rather than internal computation, and validation is limited to mathematical benchmarks (§7).
- **Author-stated:** Semantic spaces have no objective gold standard; their tracking prompt was calibrated through iterative manual review of 48 trajectories (Appendix C).
- **Author-stated:** Heuristic annotation is interpretive. The gold set uses consensus adjudication rather than independent agreement as its defining reference (Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** The 48-trajectory gold set covers only six seed problems, two variants, and four models, so annotation validation may not represent the broader evaluation distribution (§2.2; Appendix B).
- **Analyst inference:** Rare heuristic classes have less stable annotator estimates, and the supplied text omits the actual class-wise scores from Tables 8–9 (Appendix C).
- **Analyst inference:** Comparisons involving hidden or unavailable reasoning traces may not be directly equivalent to full-trace models; the text notes this model grouping but does not explain trace comparability (§3.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Table 5’s missing numerical results and absent uncertainty or significance reporting make the training claim difficult to assess.
- **Analyst inference:** HA-Plan-GRPO changes a complex rollout prompt, including explicit heuristic selection and justification, so the experiment does not isolate which prompt component causes the reported gain (Appendix F).

## Open research questions

1. **Do SHAPE labels correspond to causally important reasoning operations?** The paper establishes predictive associations from observable CoT but not causal relevance. A next experiment could intervene on selected heuristic steps and measure whether correctness changes.

2. **How reliable is semantic-space tracking across annotators and model families?** No objective semantic-space gold standard is provided. A blinded comparison among human experts and several tracking models on a larger, independently sampled set could quantify stability.

3. **Can heuristic-guided RL preserve or expand strategic diversity while improving accuracy?** The paper reports mode-seeking after RL and better performance from heuristic prompting, but does not report whether HA-Plan-GRPO reverses diversity loss. A next experiment could jointly measure accuracy, Density/Coverage, and semantic-space metrics before and after training.

4. **Does SHAPE generalize beyond mathematics?** Validation is explicitly confined to mathematical benchmarks. Domain-specific taxonomies and adjudicated evaluations on scientific or program-reasoning traces would test transfer.

## Confidence and missing information

**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text provides the main methodology, settings, and several quantitative findings, but omits the numerical contents of Tables 1–3, 5, and 8–11 despite summarizing some of them in prose. Material missing information includes HA-Plan-GRPO scores and uncertainty, complete perturbation results, annotator F1/kappa values, per-model structural statistics, full training hyperparameters, reward and verifier details, and statistical testing for the training comparison.
