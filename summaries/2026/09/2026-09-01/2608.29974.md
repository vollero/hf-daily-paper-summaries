# SpanCalib-VLM: Calibrated Hallucination Span Detection in Vision-Language Models

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29974) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29974)


## One-sentence takeaway

SpanCalib-VLM combines a calibrated multimodal sequence tagger with a generative span proposer, achieving Pearson correlation 0.413 and overall IoU 0.391 on the 379-sample English validation split, though hallucinated-span localization remains weak.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses character-level localization of hallucinated content in vision-language model responses together with calibrated confidence estimation. This matters because generative VLMs reportedly localize spans relatively well but are slow and overconfident, whereas discriminative taggers are faster and better calibrated but tend to miss or truncate hallucinated spans. The authors identify a gap in jointly optimizing localization and uncertainty for the SHROOM-Visions task (§1–§2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Multimodal sequence tagger:** XLM-RoBERTa-Large text representations are fused through cross-attention with projected SigLIP image patches. Separate heads predict hallucination labels, continuous annotator-consensus probabilities, and five error categories (§3.2; Figure 1).

- **Direct calibration supervision:** A composite objective combines binary cross-entropy, MSE against human consensus scores, and auxiliary category classification (§3.2, Equation 2).

- **Generative span proposal:** A Qwen3.5-4B VLM is supervised-fine-tuned on SHROOM-Visions to emit structured JSON spans (§3.3).

- **Union-Calibrated Fusion:** Character-level scores combine tagger probabilities and the generative model’s binary proposal mask with validation-selected weights of 0.55 and 0.45 (§3.4, Equation 3).

- **Released artifacts:** The authors state that model weights and code are publicly available, but the supplied text contains no repository location (Abstract).

These results support the authors’ claim that the two subsystems offer complementary localization and calibration behavior on SHROOM-Visions, and the ablation summary supports the importance of direct MSE calibration supervision. They do not establish generalization to other benchmarks, model families, domains, or longer responses. ## Limitations and validity risks

**Author-stated limitations:**

- Complex but correct wording, idioms, and rare accurate details can produce false positives, especially in the tagger (§7).
- The 512-token limit makes hallucinations after truncation undetectable (§7).
- Hallucinated-span IoU remains only 0.196 despite stronger aggregate and clean-response results (§7).
- Fusion weights and thresholds were optimized primarily on English despite multilingual training (§7).
- Fusion cannot recover a span missed by both constituent systems (§7).

**Analyst inferences:**

- The high clean-response IoU of 0.913 may make overall IoU 0.391 look stronger than performance on actual hallucinated spans; per-class prevalence is unavailable, so the magnitude of this effect cannot be quantified.
- Validation-set weight and threshold selection may overstate English validation performance. The text mentions a blind test set but does not expose its numerical table contents.
- The proposed explanation that Chinese gains arise from character–token alignment is presented without a described causal ablation (§5.2).
- No uncertainty intervals, multiple-run variation, or statistical tests are reported in the supplied text.
- Reproducibility is incomplete here because the claimed public code and weights are not linked, and the full configuration and result tables were not retrieved as data cells.

## Open research questions

1. **Can hallucinated-span boundary accuracy improve without sacrificing calibration?** Hallucinated IoU remains 0.196. A next experiment could compare boundary-aware losses, character-level decoders, and span refinement heads while holding the calibration objective fixed.

2. **Does multilingual tuning require language-specific fusion parameters?** Current parameters were optimized mainly on English. A multilingual nested-validation study could compare global, per-language, and dynamically predicted thresholds and weights.

3. **How much does visual grounding actually contribute?** Removing the vision tower reportedly changes IoU by only 0.003. Controlled tests stratified by whether detection genuinely requires image evidence could compare text-only, frozen-vision, and fully trained fusion variants.

4. **Can the system handle long responses and jointly missed spans?** Truncation and shared subsystem misses impose hard recall limits. Sliding-window tagging, long-context encoders, and an independent retrieval or verification stage could be evaluated on length-stratified examples.
