# GenFirst: Generation Before Reconstruction for Stable End-to-End Latent Generative Modeling

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29335) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29335)


## One-sentence takeaway
GenFirst stabilizes joint VAE–prior training by explicitly preserving posterior entropy, then prioritizes generation before reconstruction refinement to learn more modelable latent spaces while retaining usable image detail.

## Problem and motivation
Conventional latent generators train a reconstruction-focused VAE first and then freeze it, although such latents may be unnecessarily dispersed and difficult for a generative prior to model. Direct joint training could let generation shape the representation, but naïve implementations suffer latent collapse and a conflict between generation-friendly compression and faithful reconstruction.

The authors attribute collapse to a prior–entropy imbalance: reconstruction and the learned prior shrink the posterior, while the entropy component of the conventionally tiny KL term is too weak to maintain uncertainty. They further identify asymmetric optimization dynamics—reconstruction learns quickly from direct supervision, whereas distribution modeling takes longer—arguing that fixed or smoothly balanced objectives can anchor training prematurely to reconstruction-oriented latents (Sections 3.1–3.3, Figures 2–5).

## Approach and contributions

- **Entropy-preserving end-to-end objective:** The VAE encoder, decoder, and generative prior are jointly optimized, with posterior entropy separated from prior fitting and explicitly weighted to prevent variance and mean collapse (Equation 6; Section 3.1).

- **GenFirst schedule:** Training first uses stronger prior and entropy weights so generation shapes the latent geometry, then lowers both weights to give reconstruction greater relative influence and recover detail (Equation 12; Section 4.3).

- **Multiple prior families:** The framework is instantiated with an exact-likelihood continuous autoregressive prior, EAR, and flow-matching SiT/MMDiT priors, including an additional small KL term for SiT latent-scale control (Sections 4.1–4.2).

- **Broader latent-space extensions:** The authors experiment with shared visual latents for generation, reconstruction, and representation learning, plus bidirectional continuous text–image latent modeling (Section 5.4, Figures 9–10).

The evidence supports the authors’ claims that entropy preservation avoids the observed numerical collapse, staged training improves the tested generation–reconstruction operating point, and the resulting latents can benefit several prior architectures and tasks. It does not establish that the prior–entropy imbalance is the unique cause of collapse, that GenFirst is optimal beyond the tested schedules and model families, or that improvements generalize beyond the reported image and text–image settings. Some headline EiT results also include REPA, decoder fine-tuning, guidance, and a long prior-only phase, so they should not be attributed to GenFirst alone (Appendix A; Section C.1).

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** GenFirst alleviates but does not eliminate the generation–reconstruction trade-off; end-to-end VAEs still lose reconstruction fidelity, including lower PSNR (Section 7; Table 12).

- **Author-stated:** Guidance for continuous autoregressive models does not scale with the unguided gains, limiting EAR’s CFG performance (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Text-to-image training uses substantially less data than major large-scale systems, leaving scaling behavior unresolved (Section 7).

- **Author-stated:** Fine tuning of SiT’s two-stage prior weights was curtailed by computational constraints (Appendix A, Table 13).

- **Analyst inference:** The comparison against constant, cosine, and PI weighting covers only a small set of schedules and a simple hyperparameter search, so it cannot establish that optimization order is universally more important than adaptive weighting (Section 3.3; Section C.2).

- **Analyst inference:** The best EiT pipeline combines end-to-end training with REPA, a prior-only stage, and—for one ImageNet-256 result—10K decoder-tuning steps. Table 14 shows these components are complementary, but their combination complicates attribution of final scores solely to GenFirst.

- **Analyst inference:** The supplied text lists detailed configurations and a project page but does not explicitly confirm public release of code, checkpoints, training mixtures, or evaluation artifacts, limiting independent reproducibility assessment.

## Open research questions

1. **Can reconstruction fidelity be recovered without making the latent distribution harder to model?** The remaining PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS degradation shows that GenFirst does not remove the trade-off. A next experiment could add a late decoder-only or information-preservation phase and measure both frozen-prior gFID and reconstruction metrics.

2. **Is explicit entropy preservation sufficient across other posterior and prior families?** Evidence covers Gaussian VAEs with continuous AR and flow-matching priors. Testing discrete, hierarchical, or non-Gaussian latent models while tracking posterior variance, mean diversity, and training stability would probe generality.

3. **Which component drives the convergence gains?** GenFirst, REPA, decoder fine-tuning, and prior-only retraining coexist in parts of the EiT pipeline. A factorial ablation under matched compute could isolate each component and their interactions.

4. **How does the method scale with text–image data and compute?** The authors identify their smaller corpus as a limitation. Scaling dataset size and training budget while holding model architecture fixed could determine whether GenFirst’s advantage persists or grows.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** for the method description and reported within-paper results because the supplied text includes the complete main paper, tables, and implementation appendix.

Material gaps include explicit confirmation of code/checkpoint availability, exact composition and size of the text-to-image mixture, uncertainty estimates or repeated-run variance, detailed compute/hardware costs, and broader evaluation beyond image and text–image modalities.
