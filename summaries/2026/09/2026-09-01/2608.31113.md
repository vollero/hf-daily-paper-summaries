# BLARM: Animating 3D Objects from Video via Blending Latent Rigid Motion Primitives

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-01.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31113) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31113)


## One-sentence takeaway

BLARM animates a static 3D mesh from monocular video by blending learned, time-varying rigid transformations with fixed per-vertex skinning weights, outperforming three feed-forward mesh-animation baselines across all reported geometry and appearance metrics on ActionBench and Motion80.

## Problem and motivation

The paper addresses video-driven 3D mesh animation: given a monocular video and a canonical static mesh, predict a temporally coherent mesh sequence that follows the observed motion. This matters because meshes are editable and compatible with established rendering, simulation, and animation pipelines, while monocular video is easier to obtain than hand-authored animation.

The authors identify a gap between two existing approaches: geometry-derived rigs provide compact control but may not fit sequence-specific motion, whereas direct per-vertex prediction is expressive but high-dimensional, difficult to interpret, and weakly biased toward coherent part motion (Introduction; Section 2).

## Approach and contributions

- **Latent rigid-motion representation:** BLARM decodes a compact set of motion-conditioned latents into time-varying rigid transformations. Time-invariant, non-negative skinning weights blend the non-root transformations before applying a global root transform through linear blend skinning (Section 3.1, Eq. 1).

- **Geometry-and-video architecture:** Learnable queries compress frozen TripoSG mesh features into geometry-aware tokens. These attend to frame-wise frozen DINOv3 features, interact through within-frame spatial attention, and maintain temporal coherence through per-component temporal attention (Section 3.2, Eqs. 2–6; Figure 1).

- **Unsupervised skinning-weight prediction:** Vertex geometry and frozen PartField semantic features attend to temporally pooled motion latents to predict weights without skeleton, cage, part-label, or skinning supervision (Section 3.2, Eq. 7).

- **Structure-inducing training:** Point-trajectory reconstruction is combined with entropy regularization and motion-aware contrastive learning, encouraging sparse weights and similar assignments for vertices with similar ground-truth motion (Section 3.3, Eqs. 8–11).

The evidence establishes strong benchmark performance against the three selected feed-forward, rig-free mesh-animation baselines, including geometric fidelity, rendered appearance, temporal metrics, and one runtime setting. The ablations support the value of the latent representation and show that PartField features, entropy regularization, and contrastive learning are jointly useful.

It does not establish superiority over optimization-based, rigged, or other untested method families. Consistent4D appearance scores are also less conclusive than the primary evaluations because reference geometry and camera parameters are unavailable, and comparisons are made between multi-view renders and the input video.

## Limitations and validity risks

- **Author-stated:** Visually similar neighboring regions may receive incorrect component assignments and become entangled even when they should move independently (Section 5; Appendix B.4; Figure 7).

- **Author-stated:** The canonical mesh topology must be capable of supporting the target motion; otherwise deformation artifacts can occur (Section 5).

- **Analyst inference:** Generalization evidence is limited to two ground-truth-mesh benchmarks and a 12-video in-the-wild split. Broader object categories, motion types, and severe domain shifts are not separately quantified in the supplied text.

- **Analyst inference:** Longer videos are handled autoregressively from 16-frame windows, but the paper does not report how errors or component identities evolve as sequence length increases (Appendix C).

- **Analyst inference:** The method depends on frozen TripoSG, DINOv3, and PartField encoders. Table 3 ablates PartField, but the supplied text provides no corresponding ablations for the shape or video backbone.

- **Analyst inference:** Training uses approximately 10,000 Objaverse shapes and requires about 1.5 days on eight NVIDIA H200 GPUs for 1,000 epochs. Code, checkpoints, random-seed variability, and statistical uncertainty are not reported in the supplied text (Section 4; Appendix C).

## Open research questions

1. **Can assignments separate adjacent, visually similar parts more reliably?** Figure 7 shows that such parts can become entangled. A next experiment could replace or augment PartField features with explicit boundary-aware geometry cues and measure local assignment errors plus CD-Motion.

2. **How stable is autoregressive animation over long videos?** The model processes 16 frames at a time, but long-horizon drift is not quantified. Evaluate increasing video lengths with per-window and cumulative CD-Motion, component-identity consistency, and boundary discontinuity metrics.

3. **Can the rigid components handle strongly non-rigid deformation?** LBS of rigid transformations imposes a structured deformation subspace whose limits are not isolated. Compare LBS with dual-quaternion skinning or residual non-rigid components on a benchmark stratified by deformation type.

4. **How much do frozen pretrained encoders drive the gains?** Only PartField is directly ablated. Controlled swaps or removal of TripoSG, DINOv3, and PartField—holding capacity and training data fixed—could identify which pretrained representations are essential.
