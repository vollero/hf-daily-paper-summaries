# Unifying Conformal Language Tasks with In-Context Ensembles

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03005) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03005)


## One-sentence takeaway
Conformal Relevance averages four LLM scorers with differently selected demonstrations to improve sentence selection across seven benchmarks while preserving marginal recall coverage under exchangeability, although conciseness gains are not universal at stricter coverage targets (§§3–5; Table 18).

## Problem and motivation
Extractive language tasks must retain relevant content while discarding unnecessary sentences. Conformal calibration guarantees a specified recall level with high probability, but the scoring function determines how much content can be removed. The authors identify task-specific prompt engineering as a labor-intensive bottleneck and propose demonstrating relevance through labeled examples instead (§§1–2).

## Approach and contributions
- **Shared relevance-scoring pipeline:** Ens4 averages sentence scores from four LLM calls, each given two labeled demonstrations, then calibrates a threshold on a separate labeled set. The guarantee concerns retaining at least recall fraction β with probability at least 1−α (§4; Algorithm 1).
- **Diverse demonstration selection:** The four strategies combine semantic anchoring with DPP selection, DPP selection over positive-minus-negative embedding directions, BM25 retrieval, and random sampling. Their combination—not the underlying retrieval primitives—is the proposed scoring design (§4.3).
- **Recall-oriented ensemble theory:** The authors characterize when two scorers’ complementary errors raise the minimum positive score above both individual minima and bound improvement from an additional scorer by O(1/K). Extensions cover partial recall (§3; Appendix C).
- **Cross-task evaluation:** A fixed ensemble configuration is evaluated across seven datasets, with controls for demonstration count, labeled supervision, stochastic ensembling, and scoring model (§5; Appendix I).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Better relevance ranking | With Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, Ens4 beats ICL0 and oracle Best Single on all seven datasets; reported paired improvement CIs exclude zero. HotpotQA MAP is 0.839 versus 0.749 for both baselines. | Table 3; §5.2 |
| Coverage tracks its target | With β=0.8 and 100 calibration examples, empirical coverage averaged over 400 random calibration/test splits stays within one percentage point of target across the tested α sweep. | §5.4; Figure 1a |
| More concise sets at the main operating point | At α=0.20, β=0.8, Ens4 improves sentence-removal fraction on every dataset: SubSumE 0.768 versus 0.228; HotpotQA 0.868 versus 0.724 for ICL0. | Table 18 |
| Stricter coverage can reverse gains | At α=0.05, β=0.8, Evidence Inference removal fraction falls to 0.168 versus ICL0’s 0.305. | Table 18 |
| Label-only comparator sometimes wins | A logistic classifier trained on pool and calibration labels beats Ens4 on ECTSum MAP, 0.615 versus 0.516; Ens4 wins on the other six datasets. | Appendix I.3; Table 9 |
| Task hints can be essential | Removing the hint reduces PhysioNet Ens4 MAP from 0.879 to 0.549. | Table 14 |
| Additional inference has substantial cost | Four scorer calls replace one; on 150-sample serial latency measurements, SubSumE takes 45.19 versus 2.51 seconds/sample, including provider retries. | Appendix H; Table 6 |

The evidence supports stronger sentence ranking and often smaller calibrated selections in the evaluated settings. It does not establish downstream answer quality, successful redaction, conditional coverage, or universal conciseness improvement. The theoretical bound concerns positive-score order statistics; translating these into smaller sets also depends on irrelevant-sentence scores (§3.1).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** The method needs 150–440 labeled examples per task, stable relevance criteria, additional LLM inference, and sufficiently informative positive demonstrations. Hosted-model variation limits exact replication; coverage is marginal rather than subgroup-specific (p. 10).
- **Analyst assessment—mechanism:** Retrieval-diversity controls support the proposed explanation but do not fully isolate it. The format-by-ensemble factorial ablation is explicitly deferred, and matching call counts does not match input-token budgets (Appendices E.1, H, I.4).
- **Analyst assessment—reporting consistency:** Table 3’s SubSumE delta does not match its displayed MAP values; Table 8 reverses the SubSumE and Evidence Inference Ens4 values relative to the main table; several Table 12 deltas disagree with its displayed means. Exact effect sizes from these entries require clarification.
- **Analyst assessment—implementation ambiguity:** Appendix E.2’s stated windowing trigger would include several datasets it explicitly says are not windowed, given Table 2’s statistics.
- **Analyst assessment—scope:** Evaluation uses binary sentence selection, including reformulated tasks. Six ICL0 prompts were written without empirical tuning, limiting conclusions about stronger prompt-engineered alternatives (Appendices D.1, G).

## Open research questions
- **Can gains survive distribution or intent shifts?** Exchangeability and stable relevance are assumed. Test temporal or held-out-intent shifts, measuring coverage and removal fraction before and after recalibration.
- **Can fewer calls retain the gains?** Ensemble-size experiments stop at combinations of the existing scorers. Test an independently trained adaptive selector or aggregator against Ens4 at matched token budgets.
- **How much does demonstration formatting explain complementarity?** The full interaction ablation remains unrun. Cross retrieval strategy with contrastive, positive-only, and fully labeled formats under matched context lengths.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The supplied text supports the overall method and directional findings, but numerical and implementation inconsistencies weaken confidence in precise reproduction.

Material missing or unresolved information includes corrected table entries, an unambiguous windowing rule, exact seed values and several retrieval settings, tabulated results for all claimed GPT-5.6-terra datasets, and downstream or subgroup evaluations. Code availability is author-reported and was not independently checked.
