# Safety for Whom? Boundary-Aware Self-Distillation for Controlled LLM Safety Refusal

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04482) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04482)


## One-sentence takeaway
On synthetic political prompts, benign boundary-pair training substantially reduces false refusals while modestly lowering harmful-prompt refusal, showing why broader safety gains must be evaluated alongside legitimate access (§6.3).

## Problem and motivation
Deployments may need to refuse manipulative political requests while answering factual questions about the same topic. The authors argue that broad safety taxonomies and aggregate harmfulness scores miss this internal boundary: apparent safety improvements can reflect excessive refusal. Self-generated training also drops prompts that fail to elicit verified refusals, leaving incomplete supervision (§§1, 3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Boundary formulation and evaluation:** Define a deployment-specific harmful subset within a topic and construct paired prompts sharing a topic anchor but requesting different intents. Evaluate harmful and benign sides separately (§§3.2, 4.2).
- **Coverage repair:** Extend a ThinkSafe-derived single-shot recipe with transferable neutral refusals (*Graft*), progressively stronger retry steering (*Escalate*), or both (§4.1).
- **Controlled data composition:** Combine hierarchical political prompt generation with target-model compliance responses, benign boundary examples, and *FakeHarm* prompts whose dangerous-looking wording has benign meaning (§4.2).
- **Offline training:** Apply cross-entropy to harmful refusal traces and forward-KL preservation against the frozen base model on benign examples, using LoRA (§4.3; Appendix A).

The distinctive contribution is the combination of coverage repair, targeted benign compensation, and paired boundary measurement; self-generated refusal tuning itself is inherited from prior work (§5).

These results support improved precision on the constructed boundary and a safety–usability trade-off. They do not establish real-world political neutrality, general capability preservation, or a universally superior coverage strategy. The authors also acknowledge that response-source comparisons change verification and have incomplete prompt overlap, preventing causal isolation (§§4.2, 6.2).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated scope:** Experiments concern politics, predominantly one model and one LoRA configuration. Religion data exists but has no completed domain evaluation (Limitations; Appendix H.2).
- **Judge dependence:** WildGuard participates in construction and evaluation; no independent multi-annotator validation is reported. Evaluation parse failures become non-refusals without separate logging (Appendices E.5, G.4).
- **Incomplete uncertainty and provenance:** Only selected recipes receive two-seed checks; no confidence intervals are provided. Near-duplicate auditing is incomplete, model revisions are unpinned, and repair builds are not fully aligned (Appendices A.1, B.5, E.6, F.7).
- **Metric coverage:** Many complementary refusal scores are missing. Aggregation averages available benchmark fields, and the reported judge-correlation analysis assumes common benchmark composition (Appendices E.2, E.6, F.8).
- **Internal inconsistency:** §6.2 says FakeHarm reduces unsafe responses relative to refusal-only training, whereas Fig. 5’s caption says it raises them from near zero; the supplied text does not resolve this discrepancy.
- **Analyst inference:** Matching epochs does not match training compute because mixtures contain different numbers of examples; composition effects may partly include additional training exposure (Appendix B.4).
- **Analyst inference:** Non-refusal alone does not demonstrate accurate or useful civic answers; the reported evaluation lacks a factual-answer quality measure (Table 9).

## Open research questions
- **Does boundary precision transfer beyond synthetic politics?** Religion remains unevaluated. Next experiment: train and test its boundary pairs alongside independently authored political prompts.
- **Which part of SC2 produces the benefit?** Response source and verification change together. Next experiment: hold prompts and token budgets fixed while crossing response source with verification.
- **Do guard-measured improvements preserve useful answers?** Independent annotation and answer-quality evaluation are absent. Next experiment: blinded human assessment of refusal appropriateness, factual accuracy, and usefulness.
- **Does coverage repair help because it retains difficult examples?** Build provenance and training exposure are imperfectly matched. Next experiment: compare strategies on one preserved prompt pool under equal token and generation budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** Numerical boundary effects are clearly reported, but broader interpretation is limited by synthetic evaluation, automated judging, and imperfect controls.

Material missing information includes completed artifact releases, pinned model revisions, full duplicate audits, independent human validation, general capability measurements, comprehensive uncertainty estimates, complete complementary judge scores, verified runtime costs, and a resolution of the FakeHarm reporting inconsistency.
