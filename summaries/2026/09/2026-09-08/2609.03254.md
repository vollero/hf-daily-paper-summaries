# What Else Needs Fixing? Exploring Cost-Effective Test-Time Compute for Revision Propagation in Artifacts Generated Through Conversation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03254) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03254)


## One-sentence takeaway
On RevPropBench’s synthetic conversational JSON revision tasks, selecting whole patches from parallel samples improves completion more consistently than merging edits, with three-sample medoid selection offering a favorable cost–latency trade-off (§§4.1–4.2; Appendix A).

## Problem and motivation
A local revision can require changes elsewhere in an artifact—for example, changing a trip date may shift dependent activities. Those dependencies may exist only in the conversation that produced the artifact. The author identifies this as a gap relative to revision settings with pre-existing dependency structures, and asks whether additional inference computation can improve reliability cost-effectively (§1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Benchmark:** RevPropBench contains 150 synthetic, human-reviewed samples across 50 scenarios, nine domains, and artifact sizes of 10, 50, and 100 elements. A scenario-level split reserves 30 samples for development and 120 for testing (§§2–3).
- **Comparative evaluation:** Six LLMs are evaluated using nine methods: artifact/history context baselines; sequential reflection; OR, AND, and majority merging; medoid selection; and same-model LLM selection. MED selects the candidate artifact with minimum average leaf-level disagreement; SELECT asks the LLM to choose a complete candidate (§3; Appendix D).
- **Reusable assets:** The author reports releasing benchmark instances, code, and a sampling/annotation tool. The principal novelty is the conversational revision setting and its evaluation, rather than the underlying sampling or reflection families (§1).

Completion measures whether the resulting artifact satisfies the gold annotation, with supported matching rules and optional edits—not literal patch-string equality (§§2.3–2.4; Appendix C.2).

## Evidence and main results
| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| History improves completion | On 120 test samples averaged over five runs, J / H / J+H score 90.7 / 92.7 / 93.0% for gpt-5.4-mini and 81.7 / 86.7 / 90.3% for qwen3.5-122b. | §4.1; Figure 4 |
| Whole-candidate selection is consistently effective | Against J+H completion of 68.3–93.0%, five-call SELECT gains 3.3–12.5 percentage points across six models; MED gains 1.8–7.7 points. SELECT uses four candidates plus one selection call; MED uses five candidates. | §3; Figure 4 |
| Unanimity merging suppresses necessary edits | Five-call AND loses 13.2–21.3 percentage points versus J+H across the six models. Missed edits dominate its failures; OR introduces substantial over-editing. | §4.1; Figures 4–5 |
| Small sampling budgets provide the recommended trade-off | Post-hoc analysis favors SELECT with three or four total calls, or MED with three calls, for average gain per additional normalized cost. The practical recommendation is three candidates plus selection, or three-candidate MED. | §4.2; Figure 6 |
| MED adds less latency | Three-call MED takes 1.12–1.36× J+H latency across models, versus 2.13–5.62× for four-call SELECT, under the reported API/GPU deployments. | Appendix A; Table 1 |

These results establish gains within this benchmark and deployment setup. The conclusion reports a **2.2–9.7%** improvement for recommended three-sample selection, but does not tabulate its model/method breakdown; Figure 4 instead reports five-call settings. The evidence does not establish effectiveness on real conversations or other editing modalities.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Conversations are synthetic, dependencies are deliberately deterministic, coverage is limited, and GPT-generated samples may favor GPT models. Strong baseline performance also creates saturation risk (Limitations, pp. 7–8).
- **Analyst inference:** One annotator reviewed and double-checked the gold patches with LLM assistance; independent inter-annotator reliability remains unestablished (Appendix C.2).
- **Analyst inference:** Aggregate results do not reveal which domains, artifact sizes, or propagation patterns drive improvements. Five-seed standard deviations are shown, but significance tests are not reported (§4.1).
- **Analyst inference:** Cost recommendations depend on specified token prices and a post-hoc budget analysis. Latency depends on deployment; neither guarantees the same ranking under other prices or hardware (§4.2; Appendices A–B).

## Open research questions
- **Do gains transfer to human conversations?** Synthetic, deterministic data leaves this open. Next experiment: evaluate naturally occurring revisions with independently annotated acceptable outcomes.
- **Which dependency structures defeat selection?** Results lack performance breakdowns by pattern or size. Next experiment: stratify completion and errors by propagation pattern, chain depth, and artifact size.
- **Is candidate generation or selection the bottleneck?** No oracle-selection comparison is reported. Next experiment: compare MED and SELECT with an oracle choosing any correct sampled candidate at matched budgets.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: high** in the benchmark-specific findings; external validity remains uncertain.

Material missing information includes independent annotation agreement, statistical significance tests, stratified performance, oracle-selection results, and tabulated per-model accuracy for the recommended three-sample settings. Asset availability is reported by the paper and was not independently verified.
