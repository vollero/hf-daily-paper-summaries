# ENEAS: Embedding-guided Neural Ensemble for Adaptive Segmentation

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03756) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03756)


## One-sentence takeaway
ENEAS combines text-initialized memory tracking with selective semantic verification, reporting substantially better person-versus-statue discrimination than SAM 3 on one church capture, but only small tracking-benchmark gains and non-real-time inference (Tables 1–3, 6).

## Problem and motivation
Text-guided segmentation can follow distractors when a target disappears, fragment objects during close-ups, and mistake statues or paintings for people. The authors identify insufficient identity and semantic verification as the gap. Their motivating application is preprocessing images for 3D reconstruction: removing a sculpture mistakenly classified as a visitor damages the asset being reconstructed (§§1, 4.1).

## Approach and contributions
- **Text initialization for existing memory tracking:** Florence-2 grounds a description on a reference frame, initializing SeC to propagate the target mask. The contribution is the text interface; concept memory and tracking robustness are inherited from SeC (§3.1).
- **Selective verification for category discovery:** Florence-2 proposes candidates; SigLIP 2 scores crops using prompt ensembles. Two thresholds accept, reject, or route candidates to a Qwen3-VL judge. The judge masks neighboring candidates and returns a constrained binary verdict; accepted regions receive SAM masks (§3.2).
- **Integrated framework and empirical study:** Both modes share grounding and segmentation components. Experiments examine semantic filtering, uncertainty thresholds, and judge size, principally on Church Statues (§§4–5). The novelty is the integration and conditional verification design, rather than new backbone models.

## Evidence and main results

All values below are author-reported. Latencies use a single NVIDIA L4 with 24 GB VRAM and include loading and CPU preprocessing (§4.3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger semantic precision, with lower recall than SAM 3 | Church Statues, “person”: precision/recall/F1 (%) = **11.1/83.7/19.5** for SAM 3, **94.7/73.5/82.8** for ENEAS-2B, and **97.5/79.6/87.6** for ENEAS-4B. | §4.6, Table 1 |
| Verification improves over embedding-only filtering | Church Statues: strict embedding threshold >0.80 gives precision/recall/F1 **94.3/67.4/78.6%**; ENEAS-2B with uncertainty interval 0.10–0.90 gives **94.7/73.5/82.8%**. | §5.1, Table 4 |
| Small external tracking gains | SA-V Test subsample, instance tracking: ENEAS/SAM 3 HOTA **26.70/26.51%**, AssA **90.77/90.18%**, TETA **17.86/16.65%**; LocA favors SAM 3, **87.86/89.22%**. | §4.6, Table 2 |
| Small discovery-benchmark gains, mixed components | Same subsample, discovery: ENEAS/SAM 3 HOTA **9.23/9.19%**, TETA **9.53/9.10%**; DetA **6.16/6.21%** and LocA **74.25/75.38%** favor SAM 3. | §4.6, Table 3 |
| Selective judging outperforms judging every candidate | Church Statues, 2B: robust interval 0.10–0.90 yields **82.8% F1**, **3.29 s/frame**, **78.1%** VLM activation; VLM-only yields **79.1% F1**, **5.05 s/frame**, **100%** activation. | §5.2, Table 5 |
| Larger judge trades latency for accuracy | Church Statues, robust configuration: 2B gives **82.8% F1 at 3.29 s/frame**; 4B gives **87.6% at 5.02 s/frame**. | §5.4, Table 6 |
| Faster operation in a different scene | Moving Boxes, acceptance threshold 0.40: **98.0% F1**, **zero false positives**, **1.14 s/frame**, **27.0%** VLM activation. | §5.3 |
| Qualitative tracking robustness | Blue Painting examples show empty masks during absence and whole-object masks during close-ups compared with Grounded SAM and SAM 3. | §4.4, Fig. 2 |

The evidence supports a substantial semantic-filtering benefit on Church Statues and a configurable accuracy–latency trade-off. It does not establish broad superiority across categories, statistically reliable external-benchmark gains, or improved downstream reconstruction quality. Moving Boxes changes both scene and thresholds, so its speed difference does not isolate either factor.

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Author-stated:** Missed proposals cannot be recovered; distant, occluded, or context-free crops remain difficult. Dense scenes increase verification cost. Discovery lacks persistent identities and confidence scores, and robust inference is not real time (p. 15).
- **Analyst inference—limited generalization evidence:** Semantic ablations and threshold selection concentrate on the same church capture; no independent threshold-validation split is described (§5).
- **Analyst inference—attribution gaps:** Prompt ensembles, native-aspect-ratio encoding, neighbor masking, and reasoning suppression lack separate quantitative ablations. The “over-reasoning” explanation for VLM-only degradation remains an author hypothesis (§§4.2, 5.2).
- **Analyst inference—evaluation ambiguity:** Discovery association metrics are reported despite the stated absence of persistent discovery identities; the evaluator mapping is unspecified. Unordered-collection support also lacks a dedicated order-perturbation experiment (§4.6; p. 15).

## Open research questions
1. **Do verification thresholds transfer across domains?** Most calibration evidence comes from Church Statues. Fix thresholds there and evaluate independently annotated scenes containing different categories and look-alikes.
2. **Which verification components drive the gains?** Their individual effects are not quantified. Ablate prompt ensembles, masking, aspect-ratio handling, and judge prompting while holding proposals fixed.
3. **Can discovery gain persistent identities without losing semantic precision?** The authors explicitly leave temporal linking open. Add memory-based association and evaluate both semantic F1 and association metrics on the same sequences.
4. **Do better masks improve reconstruction?** Reconstruction motivates the method but is not evaluated downstream. Compare reconstructions produced with SAM 3 masks, ENEAS masks, and manual masks.

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium.** The architecture and reported tables are clear; generalization and reproducibility are less established.

Missing information includes dataset sizes, SA-V subsample selection and split counts, Church Statues matching criteria, statistical uncertainty, exact prompt templates and checkpoint configurations, discovery identity handling in evaluation, and independent threshold validation. Code/model availability is asserted but unverified; dataset and annotation release is not established.
