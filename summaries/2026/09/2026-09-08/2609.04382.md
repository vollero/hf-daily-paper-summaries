# Privacy Failure in Split-LLM Training, The Returned Gradient Nullifies the Decoys

> [← Daily summary](../2026-09-08.md) · [Relationship graph](relationships.md) · [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04382) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04382)


## One-sentence takeaway
In one split-LLM implementation, returned gradients exactly reveal which activation rows are decoys, defeating their concealment despite a passing forward-channel privacy check; clipping and noise suppress the tested leakage but do not establish general privacy (§5).

## Problem and motivation
A trusted node keeps private text and computes the training loss while an untrusted cloud processes protected activations. The original privacy evaluation tested only outgoing activations, overlooking the output gradients subsequently sent to the cloud. Because decoys do not contribute to the loss, their gradients are exactly zero, exposing the real/decoy partition (§§1–2).

The authors identify an implementation defect and an evaluation omission—not the first discovery of gradient leakage. Their broader concern is that a privacy gate can pass while an observable channel remains untested (§3).

## Approach and contributions
- **Verified mechanism:** Inspect returned-gradient support to identify loss-bearing rows, then measure bounded frequent-token inference separately from structural disclosure (§5.1).
- **Calibrated case-study evaluation:** Combine injected token leaks, shuffled-label controls, a fixed probe family, and a predeclared gate. Training seeds are independent units; within-run uncertainty clusters by frame (§§3–4).
- **Utility-qualified confirmation and mitigation:** Test a shallower configuration that meets the utility budget, then add per-row gradient clipping and Gaussian noise (§5.3).
- **Traceable evidence:** Provide an artifact index distinguishing committed summaries and code from unavailable raw captures. The authors explicitly disclaim a generally validated new methodology (§9; Appendix A).

## Evidence and main results
Here, **pp** means percentage points above a constant guess of the most frequent evaluation token. The privacy gate fails when the maximum Bonferroni-adjusted Wilson upper-bound excess exceeds **+1.0 pp**; this differs from a paired point effect (§3).

| Finding | Evidence / setting | Location in paper |
|---|---|---|
| Exact structural disclosure | Main configuration: all **4,096/4,096 frames per seed**, across nine seeds, reveal the partition; row agreement **1.000**. Frames contain 32 real and 48 decoy rows. | §5.1, Table 8 |
| Modest content inference | Frequent-token gradient-arm paired effects span **+0.6456 to +1.5008 pp** across nine seeds; shuffled controls remain at floor. Reported pooled effect: **+0.92 pp**, 95% interval **[0.74, 1.09]**. | Table 8, §5.3 |
| Forward and utility passes miss joint-view leakage | Four-layer, 2,000-step runs: all **6/6** open-gradient cells across two WikiText-2 corpora pass forward privacy and utility checks, but joint-view gate statistics reach **+1.435 to +2.180 pp**. Utility increases are **0.054–0.092 nats**, below the **0.35-nat** budget. | Table 9, §5.3 |
| Gradient mitigation suppresses measured leakage | Three private-corpus seeds: clipping at **C=0.01**, Gaussian noise **σ=0.35C**; exact partition recovery falls to **0/1,024 frames per run**, joint gates to **+0.476–0.481 pp**, with approximately **0.01 nats** additional block-held-out loss. | Tables 1, 9; §5.3 |
| Calibration exposes limited sensitivity | At injection amplitude **1.0**, token top-1 first breaks the gate at **0.06 coverage**, with point excess **+0.98 pp**; one calibration run spans 19 doses. | Figure 3, Tables 4–5 |
| Longer exposure does not strengthen this probe | Single-seed main-setting comparison: paired advantage **+0.9302 pp at 40k steps** versus **+0.9066 pp at 100k steps**. | §6, Table 10 |

The strongest evidence concerns deterministic partition disclosure. Content results establish limited frequent-token discrimination, not sequence or held-out-text reconstruction. Mitigation results apply to the implemented probes and settings; passing their gate is not a general privacy guarantee (§§5, 8).

## Limitations and validity risks
- **Authors’ scope:** One implementation, with a small surrogate replacing the delegated middle computation. Utility-qualified evidence consists of short diagnostics on the 0.6B model; the main configuration fails its utility gate. Both tested corpora are WikiText-2 variants (§2, §5.3, §8).
- **Unmeasured attacks:** Accumulated history, remote state, timing, membership/property inference, and response-side recovery remain unmeasured; active perturbation is not evaluated at mitigation-run scale (§8).
- **Evaluation constraints:** Depth/width sweeps use one seed, with some depth–budget confounding. The forward comparison in Table 8 lacks its own shuffled control. Deep-probe sensitivity selects epochs on evaluation data (§4.2, Table 8, §6).
- **Data and reproducibility:** Evaluation uses sequential blocks despite a protocol specifying document-level separation. Raw prediction tensors, full transcript payloads, and the internal scorer reimplementation are unavailable in the release (§8; Appendix A).
- **Analyst observations:** Table 9 reports seed-52 paired excess **+2.333 pp** above its purported upper-bound gate excess **+2.180 pp**, without explaining the discrepancy. Section 5.3(a) says gradient-only attacks remain at guessing level across all six cells, while the subsequent paragraph reports public-corpus gate breaks. These inconsistencies warrant clarification before interpreting exact statistics.

## Open research questions
- **Does mitigation withstand accumulated observations?** History and remote-state attacks are unmeasured. Next experiment: train temporal attackers on repeated observations from mitigated runs (§8).
- **Does the privacy–utility tradeoff persist through convergence?** Utility-qualified runs are short diagnostics. Next experiment: longer four-layer training with document-held-out evaluation and the same attack protocol (§5.3, §8).
- **Can stronger attackers recover sequences or bypass noise?** Sequence reconstruction is untested, and implemented cross-Gram probes read at floor. Next experiment: adapt sequence-reconstruction and learned cross-tensor attacks with representation-matched controls (§5.3, §8).

## Confidence and missing information
**Confidence: medium overall**, with high confidence in the described zero-support mechanism.

Material gaps include raw captures for independent verification, clarification of inconsistent statistics, convergence-scale mitigation evidence, document-independent utility estimates, and measurements for the declared untested attack families.
